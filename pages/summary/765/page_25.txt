This survey paper discusses the recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving using deep learning techniques. It highlights various methodologies and models that have been developed to enhance vehicle control, navigation, and decision-making processes in self-driving cars. Key contributions include:

1. **Conditional Imitation Learning** - Explored by Codevilla et al., this approach focuses on training models to drive by imitating human behavior under various conditions.

2. **Controllable Imitative Reinforcement Learning** - Introduced by Liang et al., this framework allows for flexible planning and control based on visual inputs.

3. **Flow-based Learning** - Wang et al. proposed FlowDriveNet, which utilizes image optical flow and LiDAR point flows to learn driving policies.

4. **Multi-future Prediction** - The Lookout model by Cui et al. emphasizes planning for diverse future scenarios in driving situations.

5. **Interpretable Motion Planning** - Zeng et al. presented an end-to-end neural motion planner that enhances the interpretability of its decisions.

6. **Behavioral Cloning Framework** - Bain and Sammut's foundational work on behavioral cloning remains relevant as it forms the basis for many imitation learning approaches in autonomous driving.

7. **Reinforcement Learning Techniques** - Various studies, including those by Sutton and Barto, have explored reinforcement learning frameworks that adapt to dynamic driving environments.

8. **Simulation and Data Augmentation** - Research on realistic simulation environments, like CARLA, and data augmentation techniques for training models using synthetic data has been pivotal in improving the robustness of autonomous systems.

The paper synthesizes these advancements and underscores the importance of integrating perception, prediction, and planning in autonomous driving systems to achieve a higher level of safety and efficiency. It concludes by suggesting future directions for research, particularly in interpretability, robustness, and real-world application of these techniques.
