The paper surveys recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving using deep learning, focusing on various aspects such as evaluation metrics, datasets, simulators, and future research directions. It highlights the importance of specific metrics for assessing infractions, including collisions and traffic rule violations, and discusses the dynamic testing adaptability provided by closed-loop evaluation, despite challenges like setup complexity and domain gap.

The quality and richness of datasets are crucial for training end-to-end models, with a summary of available self-driving datasets categorized by sensor modalities such as camera, LiDAR, and GNSS. The paper emphasizes that weather conditions significantly affect model performance, citing datasets like ApolloScape that cover various weather scenarios.

Advanced software simulators are essential for testing and refining autonomous driving systems in controlled environments, preventing the high costs and restrictions of real-world testing. The paper compares popular simulation platforms such as CARLA, LGSVL, Gazebo, and MATLAB/Simulink, noting their capabilities in simulating different driving conditions and supporting model generalization.

Future research directions are outlined, focusing on improving learning robustness through reinforcement and imitation learning, enhancing safety by accurately predicting vehicle behaviors, and advancing model explainability to address interpretability challenges. Additionally, the potential of collaborative perception systems is discussed, which allow vehicles to communicate and share environmental observations, improving situational awareness.

Overall, the paper presents an organized overview of the current state of end-to-end autonomous driving research, identifying key areas for further exploration and development.
