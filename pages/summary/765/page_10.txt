The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving utilizing deep learning techniques, particularly focusing on imitation learning and its variations. The primary aim is to develop a policy that maps driving states to actions as closely as possible to an expert policy by leveraging large-scale expert driving datasets.

Key concepts include:

1. **Imitation Learning**: This encompasses methods such as Behavioral Cloning (BC), Direct Policy Learning (DPL), and Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL). Imitation learning trains models to mimic expert behavior in diverse driving scenarios.

2. **Behavioral Cloning (BC)**: BC treats state-action pairs from expert data as independent examples, aiming to minimize imitation loss and learn a driving model that can generalize from the training data. However, it faces challenges like distribution shift when real-world observations diverge from those in the training dataset.

3. **Direct Policy Learning (DPL)**: DPL seeks to learn an optimal driving policy directly from sensor inputs. It enhances training by integrating expert evaluations during runtime, allowing the agent to adapt and discover new driving strategies. However, DPL requires continuous expert access, which can be costly.

4. **Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)**: IRL aims to infer the underlying reward functions from expert demonstrations, allowing for the replication of expert behavior. While effective in teaching various driving styles, IRL is often computationally intensive and may struggle with stability and convergence.

5. **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: RL offers a robust alternative to imitation learning by maximizing cumulative rewards through interaction with the environment. It is better at handling novel situations that differ from the training dataset, making it a promising approach for autonomous driving.

The paper highlights the strengths and weaknesses of each method, emphasizing the importance of effectively addressing the distribution shift problem in autonomous driving systems.
