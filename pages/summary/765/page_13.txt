The paper surveys recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving systems utilizing deep learning techniques. It presents a comprehensive overview of various methods, categorized by their year of publication and the specific environments in which they operate, such as CARLA and NuScenes. Key input modalities include RGB images, LiDAR, and radar, while output modalities typically encompass control signals for steering, throttle, and braking.

The study highlights multiple approaches, including imitation learning, reinforcement learning, and model-based methods, emphasizing their learning frameworks and evaluation metrics. Several methods demonstrate significant results in terms of safety and performance, with metrics such as the success rate, collision rates, and driving scores provided for comparison.

For instance, the MMFN model integrates multi-modal data for enhanced situational awareness, while CADRE employs a cascade of deep reinforcement learning strategies to optimize control signals. Other notable methods like LookOut and MP3 focus on trajectory prediction and planning, incorporating safety and comfort into their cost functions.

The results indicate improvements in safety metrics, with some models achieving high success rates in various driving scenarios. The paper also discusses the importance of explainability and safety in autonomous driving systems, underscoring the need for interpretable models that can adapt to complex driving environments. Overall, the survey encapsulates the dynamic landscape of deep learning applications in autonomous driving, highlighting both the technological advancements and the ongoing challenges in ensuring safety and reliability.
