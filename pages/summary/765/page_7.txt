The paper surveys recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving utilizing deep learning, focusing on various input modalities and their integration. 

Camera-based methods have demonstrated significant potential, with examples like Toromanoff et al. winning the CARLA 2019 challenge using vision-based approaches. Techniques employing monocular and stereo vision have been effective for image-to-control driving, with several studies using camera-only modalities to generate driving commands through imitation learning.

LiDAR sensors are highlighted for their robustness against varying lighting conditions and their ability to provide detailed spatial information through PointClouds. The paper discusses the use of LiDAR for feature mapping and localization, emphasizing its role in enhancing driving performance through depth and semantic information.

Multi-modal approaches are presented as superior to single-modality techniques, combining data from various sensors. The paper categorizes fusion methods into early, mid, and late fusion, detailing how different studies have successfully integrated multi-sensor inputs to improve driving policies and performance.

Navigational inputs are derived from route planners and navigation commands, with various strategies proposed to leverage these inputs for driving guidance. The paper mentions techniques that utilize GPS coordinates and discrete navigation directives to inform control actions.

Regarding outputs, the paper explains that end-to-end systems typically predict control commands, waypoints, or trajectories. Several authors have explored auto-regressive networks for waypoint prediction, which are then translated into low-level controls using methods like Model Predictive Control and Proportional Integral Derivative controllers.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of diverse input modalities and their effective integration in advancing autonomous driving technology through deep learning.
