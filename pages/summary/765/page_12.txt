The paper surveys recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving using deep learning, highlighting various methods and their performance metrics. It provides a comprehensive overview of methods that utilize different input modalities such as multi-camera images and LiDAR, and their corresponding output modalities, which include control commands like steering and acceleration.

Key methodologies discussed include multi-task learning, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning, with a focus on safety and explainability. For example, the PAD method from 2023 uses multi-task learning to achieve the lowest collision rate and L2 error, while ReasonNet emphasizes attention maps to enhance decision-making in driving scenarios.

The results from various studies are presented, showcasing metrics such as minimum Average Distance Error (ADE), Final Distance Error (FDE), and Intersection over Union (IoU). Notably, methods like Think Twice and Scaling Vision-based demonstrate strong performance in anticipating future motions to avoid collisions.

The survey also emphasizes the importance of safety in autonomous driving, with several methods incorporating safety-critical evaluations and explainable AI features. For instance, the Safety-Enhanced Autonomous Driving method utilizes intermediate interpretable features to ensure safe actions.

Overall, the paper underscores the rapid development in autonomous driving technologies, driven by deep learning, and highlights the trade-offs between performance, safety, and explainability in these systems.
