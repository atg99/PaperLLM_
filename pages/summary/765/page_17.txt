The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving, particularly focusing on the use of deep learning techniques. Key mechanisms, such as incorporating top intentions, are highlighted to enhance the system's ability to detect and respond to unexpected traffic situations, thereby reducing accident risks. 

Testing methodologies like adversarial attacks are employed to evaluate the robustness of control signals, while the End-to-End testing oracle assesses correct control decisions in varied scenarios. Metamorphic testing ensures consistency in steering angles across diverse conditions, and differential testing reveals inconsistencies among different deep neural network models. The model-based oracle utilizes a probabilistic model to predict potential risks in real-world scenarios.

Safety metrics, including Time to Collision and Crash Potential Index, quantitatively evaluate the performance of autonomous driving systems, offering objective comparisons and identifying areas for improvement.

Explainability is crucial for user trust in autonomous vehicles, encompassing interpretability and completeness. The paper categorizes explanation approaches into local and global explanations. Local explanations clarify the rationale behind model predictions, while global explanations aim to offer a comprehensive understanding of model behavior. The research identifies a gap in global explanation studies within end-to-end autonomous driving, indicating a need for further exploration.

Local explanation methods, particularly post-hoc saliency techniques, are discussed as they reveal which input regions significantly impact model outputs. These methods focus on the perception aspect of driving architecture, with various approaches introduced to visualize input effects on decisions. However, challenges remain in evaluating the effectiveness of attention mechanisms used in these methods.
