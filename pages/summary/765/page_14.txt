This survey paper reviews recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving systems that utilize deep learning techniques. It highlights various methods implemented in simulated environments such as CARLA and real-world scenarios, focusing on the input and output modalities, learning frameworks, evaluation metrics, explainability, and safety results.

Key advancements include:

1. **Behavior Cloning and Imitation Learning**: Many methods employ behavior cloning or imitation learning to replicate human driving behaviors. For instance, the method by Learning Situational Driving utilizes a front-facing camera to generate longitudinal and lateral control values, achieving a NoCrash score under specific conditions.

2. **Conditional Imitation Learning**: Approaches like SAM leverage conditional imitation learning for predicting stop intentions, which significantly aids in avoiding hazardous situations. Such methods report high performance in terms of NoCrash metrics after training on substantial datasets.

3. **Multi-task Learning**: The Multi-task Learning with Future States for Vision method showcases the effectiveness of combining multiple tasks, including localization, to enhance safety during driving.

4. **Interpretable Neural Motion Planning**: The End-to-end interpretable neural motion planner emphasizes the importance of interpretability in planning by generating cost volumes and future trajectories, which allow for safer driving decision-making.

5. **Safety Metrics and Evaluation**: The paper discusses various metrics used for evaluating safety and performance, including collision rates, traffic violation rates, and average displacement errors. These metrics help in assessing the models under different traffic conditions and scenarios.

Overall, the review underscores the importance of advancing safety and interpretability in autonomous driving systems, as well as the growing reliance on deep learning techniques to enhance performance in diverse driving environments.
