The paper provides a comprehensive survey on recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving using deep learning. It highlights the evaluation methods for autonomous driving systems, focusing on both open-loop and closed-loop evaluations. Open-loop evaluations utilize benchmark datasets like KITTI and nuScenes to measure deviations from expert actions, employing metrics such as MinADE, MinFDE, and collision rates. In contrast, closed-loop evaluations assess the system's performance in real-world or simulated environments, allowing for independent driving and learning of safe maneuvers.

The paper also presents a comparison of various state-of-the-art methods on the CARLA public leaderboard, which evaluates autonomous driving systems in unpredictable scenarios. Key metrics used for evaluation include Route Completion (RC), Infraction Score (IS), and Driving Score (DS), which together provide insights into the system's performance in completing routes while minimizing infractions. The leaderboard ranks submissions according to their performance, with top entries like ReasonNet and InterFuser demonstrating high effectiveness in driving tasks. Overall, the survey encapsulates the progress in autonomous driving technologies and the methodologies used for their assessment.
