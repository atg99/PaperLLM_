This paper surveys recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving using deep learning techniques. It highlights key research contributions and methodologies, including policy pre-training through self-supervised geometric modeling, model-based imitation learning for urban environments, and addressing causal confusion in imitation learning.

The survey discusses simulation-based reinforcement learning approaches that bridge the gap between virtual training environments and real-world applications. It emphasizes the need for robust testing mechanisms, such as automated testing of deep neural networks, to ensure safety and reliability in autonomous systems.

Furthermore, the paper explores various strategies for enhancing interpretability and explainability in deep learning models, crucial for building trust in autonomous vehicles. It covers advancements in visual explanations, rule extraction, and causal learning, which contribute to understanding decision-making processes in neural networks.

The survey also reviews datasets and software architectures relevant to autonomous driving, underscoring the importance of comprehensive data collection and integration for effective model training. Overall, the paper encapsulates the dynamic landscape of deep learning in autonomous driving, providing insights into methodologies, challenges, and future directions in the field.
