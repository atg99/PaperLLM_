The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving using deep learning, highlighting the differences between end-to-end and modular pipelines. End-to-end systems generate control signals directly from sensory input, bypassing sub-tasks related to perception and planning. This approach allows for continuous learning similar to human driving behavior. The initial end-to-end driving model, Pomerleau's Alvinn, utilized a three-layer sensorimotor network to determine vehicle direction.

End-to-end driving relies on various sensory inputs, primarily from cameras, LiDAR, navigation commands, and vehicle dynamics. It can produce different motion types, such as acceleration and steering, and may include additional outputs for safe maneuvering. Two primary training methodologies are discussed: Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Imitation Learning (IL). RL focuses on maximizing rewards through environmental interaction, while IL aims to replicate human driving by learning from expert demonstrations. However, IL faces challenges in covering all driving scenarios, whereas RL, though less data-efficient, allows for real-time exploration.

The section concludes by emphasizing the importance of input modalities in end-to-end systems, which include cameras for visual insights and LiDAR for accurate three-dimensional mapping. The paper provides a comprehensive overview of the current state and methodologies in end-to-end autonomous driving, summarizing key concepts and challenges in the field.
