The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving using deep learning, focusing on the integration of reinforcement learning (RL) and human expertise in training driving agents. Key methodologies highlighted include various RL models such as Deep Q-Networks (DQN), Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), and policy optimization methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Notably, the work of Liang et al. introduced a successful RL approach that surpassed traditional modular pipelines, while Chen et al. utilized tabular-RL for expert policy learning.

The paper emphasizes the rising interest in Human-In-The-Loop (HITL) approaches, which incorporate expert demonstrations to enhance policy learning. Techniques such as EGPO and HACO are showcased for their ability to guide agents in hazardous situations, ensuring safety during training. The Roach framework is mentioned for its capability to translate visual data into actionable driving commands, facilitating both imitation and reinforcement learning.

Additionally, the paper addresses the challenge of domain adaptation from simulated to real-world scenarios. It highlights the H-Divergence framework for bridging the domain gap through adversarial learning and proposes strategies for aligning virtual and real data. Various methods, including PlaceNet and GeoSim, are described for augmenting synthetic environments to improve model training. The use of virtual LiDAR data and techniques like CycleGAN for style transfer is also explored, indicating a comprehensive approach to overcoming the disparities between simulation and real-world applications in autonomous driving.
