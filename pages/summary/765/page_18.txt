The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving systems leveraging deep learning, particularly focusing on explainability methods. It highlights two primary types of explanations: counterfactual and global.

Counterfactual explanations address the 'what' question by modifying input features to explore how these changes affect model predictions. For instance, they can identify which changes in a traffic scenario might prevent a vehicle from stopping, such as the presence of a red light. Techniques such as causal inference are employed to identify risky objects, while auxiliary outputs like occupancy maps help provide context for counterfactual reasoning regarding potential failures.

Global explanations, on the other hand, aim to elucidate the overall behavior of a model. This can involve model translation, where an interpretable model mimics the input-output relationships of a complex self-driving model, or explaining representations, which examines the information captured by the model's structures. Methods here include evaluating neuron activation through input patterns and partitioning input spaces based on neuron coverage to identify potential hazards.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of explainability in enhancing the safety and reliability of autonomous driving systems, making complex deep learning models more interpretable and manageable.
