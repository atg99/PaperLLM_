The paper investigates the impact of annotator identification on model performance in various datasets. It introduces a framework where each example is associated with a unique annotator ID, which enhances the predictive capability of the model across datasets such as OPENBOOK QA, COMMONSENSE QA, and MNLI. Results indicate that incorporating the annotator ID leads to significant performance improvements, as shown by the p-values calculated via statistical tests.

The study further explores the model's ability to recognize annotators based solely on the input data, without explicit ID exposure. By fine-tuning a BERT-base model to predict annotator IDs from examples, the results reveal that models perform better in recognizing annotators who contribute a larger number of examples. The F1-scores for recognition vary across datasets, with the model achieving notable success in COMMONSENSE QA and OPENBOOK QA while struggling with MNLI.

Lastly, the paper examines the generalization capabilities of models to new annotators by creating training and development sets with disjoint annotator groups. The evaluation of these splits demonstrates the model's performance when encountering unfamiliar annotators, reinforcing the importance of annotator information in enhancing predictive accuracy in downstream tasks. Overall, the findings underscore the utility of annotator identification for improving model performance and the feasibility of recognizing annotators based on the input data alone.
