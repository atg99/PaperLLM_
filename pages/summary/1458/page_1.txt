The paper "Are We Modeling the Task or the Annotator? An Investigation of Annotator Bias in Natural Language Understanding Datasets" by Mor Geva, Yoav Goldberg, and Jonathan Berant examines the impact of annotator bias in natural language understanding (NLU) datasets generated through crowdsourcing. The authors note that the prevalent practice of using a small group of high-quality workers to generate a large volume of data raises concerns about the diversity of the dataset and the generalization capabilities of models trained on it.

Through a series of experiments involving three NLU datasets—MNLI, OPENBOOK QA, and COMMONSENSE QA—the authors demonstrate that incorporating annotator identifiers as input features enhances model performance. They find that models can identify prolific annotators, suggesting that these biases are learned during training. Moreover, the models often struggle to generalize to examples from annotators not included in the training set, indicating that the reliance on a limited pool of annotators can lead to overfitting.

The findings emphasize the need for monitoring annotator bias during dataset creation and advocate for separating the training and test sets by using different annotators. This approach aims to improve model robustness and ensure better generalization to unseen data. Overall, the study highlights the importance of addressing annotator bias to enhance the reliability of NLU models.
