This study examines the impact of crowdsourcing practices on annotator bias in natural language understanding (NLU) datasets, particularly how this bias may lead to an over-estimation of model performance. The research demonstrates that high-quality annotator information can enhance model performance and that the language used by annotators often reflects their identity. The study evaluated the generalization ability of models on unseen annotators across three NLU datasets, revealing annotator bias in two cases. This bias is attributed to the skewed distribution of annotators, where a small number contribute most examples, increasing susceptibility to biases.

The findings underscore the importance of monitoring annotator bias in crowdsourced text generation tasks. It is recommended that model performance be assessed on new annotators during the data collection process. Additionally, to mitigate annotator bias, the study suggests separating annotators in the training set from those in the test set.

The research received support from various funding sources and acknowledges contributions from specific individuals and datasets, indicating a collaborative effort in advancing understanding of annotator bias in NLU.
