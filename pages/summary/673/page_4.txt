This paper discusses the application of neural networks for sentiment analysis in under-resourced languages, specifically focusing on Roman Urdu. It highlights the challenges associated with sentiment analysis in this dialect, emphasizing the necessity of lexical normalization. The authors reference work by Sharf and Rehman, who developed a phonetic normalization algorithm using data from various sources, leading to an 80% accuracy in discourse-based sentiment analysis.

Several studies are reviewed, showcasing the development of datasets and methodologies for analyzing sentiments in Roman Urdu. One notable study analyzed a bilingual Twitter dataset related to general elections, employing a three-phase technique involving topic and language classification, lexicon building, and sentiment evaluation using a bilingual lexicon. Other research efforts included the construction of sentiment lexicons, classification of tweets by political affiliation, and the use of various classifiers such as Naive Bayes and Decision Trees for sentiment classification.

The paper also addresses the lack of standardized lexicons for Roman Urdu and the presence of spelling variations that complicate natural language processing tasks. It concludes by noting the significant challenges posed by lexical inconsistencies, which affect applications like word segmentation and machine translation in Roman Urdu. Overall, the work underscores the advancements made in sentiment analysis for Roman Urdu while highlighting ongoing challenges and the need for further research.
