The paper discusses the performance of different models for sentiment analysis of Roman Urdu, particularly focusing on a rule-based model, an N-gram model, and a deep learning model (RCNN). 

In section 5.2, the rule-based model was evaluated using the RUSA-19 Corpus and the UCL dataset. The results indicated that the RUSA-19 Corpus achieved better accuracy in binary classification (0.544) compared to tertiary classification (0.497). However, the RCNN model outperformed the rule-based approach across all evaluation metrics, primarily because the rule-based model did not incorporate semantic information, relying solely on lexicon-based word analysis.

Section 5.3 presents the N-gram model, which utilized character N-grams of varying lengths (2-10 characters) analyzed through the Naive Bayes algorithm. The character-based N-gram model yielded inferior results compared to the RCNN model, with the 2-gram features showing the best performance in binary classification (0.743 for RUSA-19 and 0.755 for UCL). Tertiary classification results were comparatively lower, with UCL achieving an accuracy of 0.531.

Figures 4 and 5 compare the performance of the models across accuracy, precision, recall, and F-measure for both datasets and classification types, consistently showing that the RCNN model significantly outperforms both the rule-based and N-gram models.

In the concluding remarks, the paper highlights the potential of deep learning in sentiment analysis for low-resource languages like Roman Urdu. It notes that while traditional lexical approaches require extensive feature engineering, the proposed deep learning model automates feature extraction effectively, achieving high accuracy. The study suggests that future research should focus on enhancing deep learning techniques for improved results and mentions that the publicly available data corpus can serve as a benchmark for further studies in this domain.
