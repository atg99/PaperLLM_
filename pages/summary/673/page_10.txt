The paper discusses the evaluation of a proposed RCNN model for binary and tertiary classification tasks in the context of sentiment analysis, specifically focusing on Roman Urdu datasets. Accuracy is defined as the ratio of correctly classified subjects to the total number of subjects, with precision and recall as additional performance metrics. The F-Measure combines precision and recall, providing a harmonic mean of the two.

In the results section, the RCNN model is compared against baseline models, including a Rule-based approach and a character N-gram model. The findings indicate that the RCNN model outperforms both baseline models, with the highest accuracy of 0.751 for binary classification on the RUSA-19 Corpus and an accuracy of 0.713 for tertiary classification. The results highlight that binary classification yields better performance metrics compared to tertiary classification, which is attributed to various factors such as the number of classes and the length of reviews.

The training accuracy curves show that the RCNN model stabilizes after initial training, suggesting its efficiency in handling low-resource languages like Roman Urdu by preserving contextual information and reducing noise. Overall, the study emphasizes the effectiveness of the RCNN model in sentiment analysis tasks and provides a comparative analysis with other classification approaches.
