The paper discusses the significance of sentiment analysis, particularly in the context of Roman Urdu, a language that lacks adequate resources for natural language processing. The anonymity of the internet has led to an increase in hostile social media content, making sentiment analysis crucial for understanding user behavior and emotions. Companies and politicians are increasingly relying on sentiment analysis to gauge public opinion and reputation through social media analytics.

Despite the prevalence of sentiment analysis tools in languages like English, Roman Urdu remains resource-poor. Roman Urdu, which uses the Roman script for Urdu, is widely utilized on social media platforms in South Asia. This paper emphasizes the necessity of sentiment analysis for Roman Urdu to facilitate comprehension of opinions and emotions expressed in this language.

The authors present the RUSA-19 Corpus, a manually annotated dataset comprising 10,021 sentences across various genres, including sports, food, politics, software, and drama. Each sentence is classified into three categories: positive, negative, or neutral. A deep learning model is also developed to demonstrate the efficiency of the RUSA-19 Corpus in sentiment analysis tasks, with accompanying Python code provided for further research.

The paper is structured into six sections, including a literature review that examines related research and previous corpora efforts in sentiment analysis. The methodology for corpus generation and experimental results of sentiment classification models are also discussed, culminating in a conclusion that highlights the potential of the RUSA-19 Corpus to advance research in sentiment analysis for under-resourced languages like Roman Urdu.
