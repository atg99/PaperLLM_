The paper discusses various sentiment analysis systems assessed through SemEval competitions, which provide benchmark corpora and methodologies in English and Arabic dialects. The competitions involve distinct tasks categorized by types of reviews or tweets, with varying dataset sizes each year. For instance, the 2013 SemEval utilized Twitter and SMS datasets, while subsequent years expanded upon these with more structured datasets across multiple subtasks.

The paper highlights sentiment analysis research across languages including Korean, German, Indonesian, and Italian. Notable corpora include KOSAC for Korean sentiment analysis, a German consumer feedback corpus from Amazon reviews, a large Indonesian tweets corpus, and an Italian corpus focused on film sentiment analysis, each annotated for specific sentiment categories.

Different techniques employed for sentiment analysis are discussed, ranging from unsupervised rule-based methods to supervised machine learning approaches such as Support Vector Machines and ensemble methods like Random Forests. Advanced methods like Long Short Term Memory networks and word embeddings are also mentioned as effective for capturing sentiment in text.

Several unsupervised and machine learning approaches are outlined, including Turney’s method for film genre analysis and various neural network strategies for sentiment classification across different languages. The paper concludes by noting the ongoing research in under-resourced languages like Hindi, Thai, and Bengali, indicating a growing interest and progress in sentiment analysis beyond major languages. However, it suggests a gap in comprehensive studies for these under-resourced languages.
