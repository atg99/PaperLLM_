The paper outlines guidelines for classifying sentences into positive, negative, and neutral sentiment categories in the context of sentiment analysis of the RUSA-19 Corpus, which consists of Roman Urdu reviews. 

For positive classifications, sentences are deemed positive if they contain approval agreements, illocutionary acts such as praise or congratulations, or express positive sentiments through aspect terms. 

Negative classifications are determined by the presence of negative sentiment terms, direct disagreements, terms indicating penalties or bans, and negation of positive adjectives. Additionally, sentences with more negative terms than positive ones are classified as negative.

Neutral sentences are identified by their factual nature, shared thoughts, and the presence of uncertain expressions like "maybe." Sentences exhibiting both positive and negative sentiments are also classified as neutral.

The RUSA-19 Corpus was manually annotated by three annotators familiar with Urdu and Roman Urdu, achieving an Inter-Annotator Agreement of 70.72% and a Cohen's Kappa score of 0.557, indicating moderate agreement. The corpus contains 10,021 reviews, categorized into 3,778 positive, 2,941 negative, and 3,302 neutral reviews, with an average review length of 15 words. Examples illustrate the distinctions among the three sentiment classes.
