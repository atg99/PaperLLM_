The document discusses the sentiment analysis of a newly developed corpus, termed the RUSA-19 Corpus, containing 10,021 Roman Urdu sentences across five genres. The sentiment distribution within the corpus reveals 38% positive, 29% negative, and 33% neutral reviews, indicating a balanced class distribution. The paper highlights the limitations of existing datasets, such as the UCL Roman Urdu dataset, which while larger, lacks genre diversity and is primarily focused on binary classification.

The authors detail their experimental methodology, which includes both binary and tertiary classification tasks. The binary classification focuses on distinguishing between positive and negative sentiments, while the tertiary classification incorporates neutral sentiments as well. The RUSA-19 Corpus is compared against the UCL dataset, which consists of 20,228 sentences categorized similarly.

For classification, the authors implemented various approaches: a rule-based method using a lexicon of 500 positive and 500 negative words, an N-gram model, and a Recurrent Convolutional Neural Network (RCNN). The rule-based method evaluates sentiment based on the proportion of positive and negative words in a sentence. The N-gram model employs character-based sequences to extract information, while the RCNN addresses limitations of traditional neural networks by incorporating contextual understanding across sentences.

Overall, the study aims to enhance sentiment analysis capabilities for Roman Urdu texts by leveraging a diverse corpus and multiple classification techniques.
