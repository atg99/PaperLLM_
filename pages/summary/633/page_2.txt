The paper discusses various fusion strategies for multi-modal information, categorizing them into early fusion, late fusion, and collaborative fusion. Early fusion involves integrating multi-view information for processing by specific models, while late fusion processes each view independently before combining outputs. Collaborative fusion, as proposed by Wang et al., emphasizes intuitive collaboration among modalities, enhancing consensus essential for multi-modal data analytics across applications like classification and retrieval.

With advancements in web technology and big data, deep neural networks have gained prominence for modeling complex relationships in high-dimensional data. This has led to the emergence of deep multi-modal architectures, which consist of multiple deep networks corresponding to different modalities. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) represent another interaction method, fostering collaboration through competition to address tasks more effectively.

The paper further examines deep multi-modal learning, highlighting its effectiveness in clustering and classification tasks. Key contributions include Zhang et al.'s latent multi-view subspace clustering method, which integrates multiple views into a latent representation, and Huang et al.'s multi-view spectral clustering network that optimizes view consistency. Additionally, Hu et al. introduced a multi-view metric learning method, later extended to a deep network-based approach. Li et al. proposed a probabilistic hierarchical model for multi-view classification, demonstrating the significant impact of deep multi-modal models on fundamental problems.
