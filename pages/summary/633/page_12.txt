The paper discusses various datasets used for cross-modal retrieval, highlighting their characteristics, such as the number of images and types of data (e.g., visible, near-infrared, thermal). Notable datasets include CUHK-PEDES, which contains 40,206 pedestrian images with corresponding text, and Flickr30K, which features 31,783 image-text pairs.

The author categorizes deep cross-modal retrieval methods into two main types: real-valued representation learning and binary code or hashing learning. Real-valued representation learning focuses on creating a shared subspace for measuring semantic similarities, while hashing learning aims to develop efficient representations for quick search and storage through Hamming distance.

In the realm of real-valued representation learning, several key approaches are highlighted. For instance, Deep-CCA, an advancement of canonical-correlation analysis using deep neural networks, enables complex transformations across modalities. Additionally, Corr-AE employs autoencoders to derive cross-modal representations by minimizing both intra-modal and inter-modal learning errors. Other methods, such as the regularized deep convolutional network (RE-CNN) and Deep-SM, utilize deep learning techniques to enhance cross-modal semantic mapping and retrieval effectiveness. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the advancements in deep learning techniques for improving cross-modal retrieval, addressing challenges in semantic representation and correlation.
