The paper discusses various advancements in deep cross-modal hashing models aimed at enhancing data retrieval across different modalities, specifically focusing on both supervised and unsupervised approaches. 

In the supervised category, Jiang et al. introduced the Deep Cross-Modal Hashing (DCMH) model, enhancing feature learning and hash-code generation. Tu et al. proposed the DCHUC model, which learns unified hash codes for cross-modal instances. Other notable models include Cao et al.'s collective deep quantization, Jin et al.'s deep semantic-preserving ordinal hashing (DSPOH), and Xie et al.'s Multi-Task Consistency Preserving Adversarial Hashing (CPAH), which emphasizes semantic consistency through advanced modules. Liong et al. developed CMDVH and CM-DVStH, focusing on non-linear transformations and structural layers to improve cross-modal correlations. Liu et al. introduced RDCMH, leveraging semi-supervised semantic ranking, while Li et al. presented a self-supervised adversarial hashing (SSAH) approach to enhance representation consistency. Deng et al. and Chen et al. contributed models that utilized triplet labels and a tri-stage framework to optimize cross-modal retrieval.

In the unsupervised domain, Li et al. developed the Deep Binary Reconstruction (DBRC) network for learning binary codes directly. Wu et al. introduced the UDCMH framework that combines deep learning with matrix decomposition for hash code generation. Zhang et al. proposed the unsupervised multi-pathway generative adversarial hashing model (MGAH), which focuses on the manifold structure of multi-modal data. Wang et al. introduced the Unsupervised Deep Cross-modal Hashing with Virtual Label Regression (UDCH-VLR), a unified framework integrating deep hash function training with virtual label learning.

The results of these models were summarized in tables comparing their performance on benchmark datasets such as IAPRTC-12, MIRFlickr-25k, and NUS-WIDE for image-to-text and text-to-image retrieval, revealing common hash code lengths of 16, 32, and 64 bits. The MIRFlickr-25k dataset comprises 25,000 images across 24 semantically independent categories.
