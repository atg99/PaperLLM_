The paper presents a survey on the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) across various tasks, specifically focusing on classification and clustering. It summarizes several studies, detailing the datasets used, whether the approach is single-view or multi-view, and provides a brief description of each method.

In the classification domain, methods such as those by [101] and [17] highlight advancements in semi-supervised learning and image-to-image translation, respectively. For instance, [101] emphasizes new structural features and training procedures within the GAN framework to generate visually realistic images, while [17] introduces StarGAN, noted for producing higher quality images across multiple domains. Other approaches, like [21] and [26], focus on semi-supervised conditional generative modeling and the adversarially learned inference model, respectively, enhancing the understanding of latent spaces.

Clustering techniques are also explored, with [153] aiming to represent incomplete multi-view data through GANs, capturing hidden information via reconstruction. Studies like [53] and [87] demonstrate methods for learning cycle-consistent mappings and enabling clustering in latent space, respectively. The paper further discusses models that learn clustering structures while inferring incomplete views, emphasizing the versatility of GANs in handling diverse data representations.

Overall, the survey captures the collaborative and competitive aspects of GAN applications, showcasing their potential in multi-modal data analytics through effective fusion of different data types and methodologies.
