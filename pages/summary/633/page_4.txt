In this section, the paper discusses various deep multi-view learning methods that have been effectively utilized in multimedia analytics, specifically for image retrieval, representation, and recognition. 

For image retrieval, methods such as a multi-view 3D object retrieval approach leverage a deep embedding network guided by triplet and classification losses to generate comprehensive descriptions for 3D objects. Additionally, deep multi-modal hashing models transform feature descriptors into a similarity-preserving Hamming space, facilitating large-scale retrieval.

In terms of deep image representation, multi-view learning techniques using deep auto-encoders have been introduced. Notably, SkeletonNet utilizes a hybrid deep network for unsupervised multi-view subspace learning through skeleton embedding, while Bimodal Correlative Deep Autoencoder constructs a fashion semantic space to capture correlations between visual features and fashion styles.

The paper also highlights the application of deep multi-view learning in image recognition. Approaches like Deep Multi-Modal Face Representation employ multiple CNNs to extract and concatenate features, followed by dimension reduction via auto-encoders. Coupled Deep Learning enhances the relevance of projection matrices for heterogeneous face matching. Other notable methods include a 3D fingerprint segmentation network that integrates shape and texture information, and a multi-scale CNN architecture addressing variable label numbers.

Moreover, Multi-Modal CNN for Multi-Instance Multi-Label generates multi-modal instances from images and text, facilitating multi-label classification. The end-to-end approach proposed by Jia et al. incorporates a neuron-wise correlation-maximizing regularizer to improve classification performance through feature concatenation from individual views. Lastly, the Deep Explicit Attentive Multi-View Learning Model is introduced, emphasizing the potential of deep multi-view learning in various multimedia applications.
