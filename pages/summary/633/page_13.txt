The paper discusses the performance of various deep multi-modal data analytics methods in the context of image-to-text (I2T) and text-to-image (T2I) retrieval tasks, as summarized in Table 5. The methods evaluated include Deep-CCA, Corr-AE, RE-CNN, Deep-SM, CCL, CMDN, GIN, GXN, DLA-CMR, and others, with results presented for three datasets: Wikipedia, NUS-WIDE, and Pascal Sentence.

The results indicate that retrieval performance, measured by the mean Average Precision (mAP) score, varies significantly across different datasets. Notably, the performance on the Wikipedia dataset is generally lower compared to NUS-WIDE and Pascal Sentence. This discrepancy is attributed to the abstract nature of categories in Wikipedia, which include terms like "history" and "art & architecture," leading to more confusing feature representations. In contrast, the more concrete categories in NUS-WIDE and Pascal Sentence, such as "bicycle" and "dog," allow for clearer and more discriminative features.

The paper also highlights advancements in cross-modal correlation learning, such as the CCL model, which integrates intra-modality and inter-modality correlations during feature learning. Other methods discussed include CMDN, which combines inter-media and intra-media representations, GIN, which utilizes graph representations for semantic relations, and GXN, which integrates generative models for comprehensive semantic capture. Additionally, DLA-CMR employs dictionary learning and adversarial techniques to enhance feature discrimination.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of developing methods that can effectively learn and represent discriminative features from cross-modal data, particularly in varied semantic contexts. The paper suggests a growing interest in cross-modal hashing techniques as a complement to real-valued representation learning.
