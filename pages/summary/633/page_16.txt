The paper discusses advancements in deep cross-modal learning, particularly focusing on methods that enhance retrieval tasks across different modalities such as images and text. The performance of various approaches is highlighted, with CM-DVStH achieving the highest mean average precision (mAP) for image queries, while CDQ shows competitive results for text queries across two datasets. 

Several deep learning methods for multimedia applications are introduced, including a neural network by Sarfraz et al. that captures non-linear mappings while preserving identity information and Aytar et al.'s cross-modal scene dataset with an optimized neural network for shared representation learning. Zheng et al. propose a hetero-manifold regularization method for hash functions and multi-modal representations, demonstrating effectiveness in cross-camera re-identification and age recognition tasks. Other notable contributions include Kim et al.'s robust descriptor against image deformation and Zhang et al.'s loss functions aimed at improving image-text matching.

The paper also explores deep cross-modal learning using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), noting that cross-modal retrieval is a particularly active area of research. Various GAN-based methods are presented, such as ACMR, which generates modality-invariant representations, and CM-GANs, which explore both inter- and intra-modality correlations. Noteworthy is the Unsupervised Coupled Cycle GAN, which excels in generating reliable hash codes for cross-modal retrieval. Additionally, TANSS utilizes self-supervised learning for zero-shot retrieval, while UCAL employs adversarial learning to enhance modality correlations. Overall, the advancements in deep cross-modal learning highlight the potential for improved multimedia retrieval applications.
