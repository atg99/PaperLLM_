The paper discusses the concept of spatial-temporal multi-modal collaboration in the context of path selection within a road network. It presents a scenario where two candidates, representing two modalities, aim to reach their destinations while avoiding obstacles, such as being trapped by bombs. The authors highlight the challenges posed by complexity and uncertainty in large-scale networks and propose that information sharing between the modalities can help mitigate these issues. 

Key points include the importance of determining when and where to collaborate, as timing can significantly affect the candidates’ ability to navigate effectively. The paper emphasizes the need for robust deep multi-modal models capable of generating powerful feature representations to facilitate effective collaboration. 

In the conclusion, the authors provide an overview of current advancements in deep multi-modal data analytics and stress the necessity of developing methods for effective multi-modal collaboration, including adversarial approaches, to integrate complementary information for improved outcomes.
