The paper discusses the architecture of generative adversarial networks (GANs) applied to multi-view clustering. It describes the process where each view, denoted as Xi, is processed by an encoder Ei to produce a deep representation Zi. This representation is then fed into a generator Gi, which acts as both a decoder and generator, aiming to fool a discriminator Di in a min-max rivalry game. The representations from different views are combined to form a multi-view representation Z for clustering purposes.

The study utilizes various datasets, including handwritten numerals, a two-view database of drosophila embryos, the MNIST dataset of handwritten digits, the Columbia Consumer Video dataset, and a YouTube dataset, each offering diverse features for analysis. 

Furthermore, the paper highlights the effectiveness of deep generative adversarial networks in various multi-view applications, such as cross-view joint distribution matching and action recognition. It cites the work of Du et al. who developed a multi-view adversarially learned inference model, MALI, to facilitate cross-domain joint inference.
