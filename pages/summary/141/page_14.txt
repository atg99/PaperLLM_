The paper investigates the relationship between the average loss of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) model during image generation and the number of training epochs. It presents data indicating that as the number of epochs increases, the average loss decreases, albeit at a diminishing rate. For instance, the average loss drops from 1259.3 at 1 epoch to 968.24 after 50 epochs, suggesting that while the model fits well to the training dataset from game maps, the improvement in loss becomes less significant with additional epochs. The findings, supported by both graphical and statistical analyses, indicate that 50 epochs is a reasonable training duration for the VAE model.

Additionally, the study examines the time required for training the VAE model, highlighting a linear relationship between training time and the number of epochs, which was corroborated by statistical analysis showing a high R2 value of 0.979. This suggests that the time complexity of the VAE model is O(n), where n represents the size of the dataset. The results emphasize the importance of both average loss and processing time when considering the scalability of the VAE model for larger datasets in future applications.
