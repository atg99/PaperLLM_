The paper discusses the implementation of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) model for generating video game maps, specifically focusing on the Arknights game. It details the preprocessing steps, including the scaling factor and the number of epochs set for model training. A scaling factor of 0.2 was employed, and 50 epochs were chosen to balance model accuracy with computational feasibility.

Statistical analyses were carried out, applying least-square fitting to linear relationships and observing trends in non-linear data. The coefficient of determination (R2) was used to assess the accuracy of these fits. The study aimed to identify convergence trends in average information loss and recognition accuracy relative to the number of epochs, providing insights for future game design.

Results from the VAE model showed that the generated game maps retained features of the original images, although the output images lacked sharpness due to noise from the training process. The paper suggests using sharpening filters to enhance image clarity. The findings serve as valuable references for game designers in creating new levels and characters.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of the VAE model in generating useful outputs while also addressing the need for further image enhancement techniques.
