The paper discusses a stochastic process for generating outputs using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) model, focusing on the encoding and decoding of data samples. The process involves two key steps: first, the prior distribution of the data sample X is encoded to generate a hidden variable z, and second, a data point or sample xi is derived from the conditional distribution p(X|zi). The decoding process utilizes samples zi from a standard normal distribution to establish a mapping that outputs the distribution of zi corresponding to X.

Assuming that X follows an isotropic multivariate Gaussian distribution, the paper formulates the relationship in terms of parameters m and s², leading to the equation pq(X|zi). The model employs Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) to estimate the parameters based on the observed dataset, as detailed in the formulation of Equation (3). Given the high dimensionality of X, a sufficient number of samples zi must be considered for accurate estimates of pq(X).

To refine the estimation, the posterior distribution pq(z|xi) is introduced using Bayes' formula, allowing for the computation of the posterior based on the input data. The AutoEncoding Variational Bayesian (AEVB) algorithm is then applied to optimize the encoder and the approximate posterior distribution qf(z|xi). The paper concludes with a step-by-step outline of the VAE model, emphasizing the use of neural networks to derive parameters of the Gaussian distribution that z obeys, illustrated through an example involving images of orange cats.
