The paper discusses the application of the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) model for data clustering, highlighting its dual role as both a generative model and a feature extractor. The VAE comprises an encoder and a decoder, allowing it to approximate the distribution of latent variables as a standard normal distribution. The study utilized the MNIST dataset of handwritten numbers to demonstrate the VAE's clustering capabilities without external supervision.

Figures presented show sampled numbers and the corresponding outputs generated by the VAE after clustering. The VAE performed effectively in classifying different data types and generating images that reflect the clustered datasets. The study conducted 50 epochs and reported an average recognition accuracy of 85.4% across ten experiments, with a training accuracy of approximately 83.7%. Additionally, the paper notes that generating colored images would require significantly more computer memory due to the need for a 3-dimensional array to capture RGB pixel values. The results underscore the VAE's potential for generating new datasets and images through effective data clustering.
