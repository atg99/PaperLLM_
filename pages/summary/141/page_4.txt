The paper discusses the application of the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) model, originally proposed by Kingma and Welling, across various fields including image generation, data classification, and anomaly detection. Notable applications include a weather prediction model by Vuyyuru et al. combining VAE with Multilayer Perceptrons, and an anomaly detection approach for office temperature by Lin et al. using LSTM and VAE. Bao et al. demonstrated a combination of Convolutional Variational Autoencoder with GANs for generating gender-specific human photos. These examples highlight the VAE's versatility and practical utility.

The authors propose that by implementing adequate data processing, fine-tuning model parameters, and minimizing loss functions during training, the VAE model can assist game developers in generating game functions and level maps, thus improving the efficiency of game design.

The structure of the paper is outlined as follows: Section 2 presents a flowchart detailing the VAE model's application and the datasets used. Section 3 covers the mathematical theories and statistical properties of the VAE model. Section 4 includes numerical experiments and statistical analyses. Section 5 addresses the study's limitations and future research directions, followed by a conclusion in Section 6.

In the methodology, raw data from games and databases undergoes preprocessing and scaling before being used for model training. The VAE model's variational component is optimized to achieve the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), with a focus on minimizing the loss function to enhance model training efficiency. The study analyzes relationships between average loss figures and epochs, time complexity relative to dataset sizes, and the impact of scaling factors, with results presented in graphical and tabular formats.

Three specific datasets were utilized for training the VAE model, chosen for their relevance to game design, humanistic character representation, and practical applications in image processing and data clustering.
