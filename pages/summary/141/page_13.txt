The paper discusses the application of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) model in generating new game maps based on original images from the game Arknights. The results indicate that the VAE model effectively retains the characteristics of the original images while allowing for the creation of new images through a mixing process. However, it was noted that the sharpness of the generated images was insufficient due to noise introduced during training. To enhance image quality, the authors recommend using a Laplacian sharpening filter or Sobel gradient to improve edge visibility.

Following the generation of output images, the paper also presents numerical experiments aimed at exploring the statistical properties of the VAE model. The learning curves illustrated in the paper show a clear relationship between the average loss during the image generation process and the number of training epochs. As expected, the average loss decreased with an increasing number of epochs, although the rate of decrease diminished over time. Specifically, the average loss decreased from 1259.3 at 1 epoch to 968.24 after 50 epochs, indicating that 50 epochs is a reasonable training duration for this dataset. Overall, the findings support the suitability of the VAE model for generating game maps, providing valuable references for game designers in developing new levels and features.
