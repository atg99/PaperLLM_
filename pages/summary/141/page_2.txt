The paper discusses the increasing integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) in various fields, particularly in video game design. The emergence of generative models has enabled innovative applications, such as the use of conditional adversarial neural networks for generating city maps and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for creating images based on textual descriptions. This shift towards "computational creativity" in video game design is becoming a key focus for developers aiming to reduce production costs and implement new working procedures.

Historically, video games began with simple designs, exemplified by the first video game, Spacewar!, created in 1962. This game marked a significant milestone in gaming history, influencing computing resource advancements and the evolution of different game genres. The development of the first home console, Magnavox Odyssey, and arcade games like Computer Space and Pong followed in the early 1970s. Despite technological progress, there was initially a lack of standardized genre classifications. However, games can be categorized into genres such as action, first-person shooters, sports and racing, and simulation games.

As technology advanced, the popularity of arcades and consoles declined, giving way to games designed for personal computers and mobile devices. Modern games are characterized by their multi-genre nature and compatibility across various platforms, such as PC, smartphones, and consoles. 

Today's video games consist of three primary components: the program, which establishes the game's structure and logic; gameplay, which dictates player interactions and game dynamics; and artistic elements, encompassing visual and auditory design elements. This comprehensive approach highlights the complexity and creativity involved in contemporary game development.
