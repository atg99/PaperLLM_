This paper discusses the datasets used in a study involving Variational Autoencoders (VAE) and outlines the methodologies for preprocessing these datasets. The datasets include characters from the Konachan site, which hosts over 60,000 anime and manga wallpapers, and the Modified National Institute of Standards and Technology (MNIST) database, which contains binary images of handwritten digits. The MNIST dataset, created in 1998, is divided into a training set and a test set, providing pre-labeled data essential for testing clustering effects.

The paper details the steps taken in the VAE model's construction, emphasizing the importance of data preprocessing. Initially, raw images were compressed using a scaling factor to optimize memory usage and enhance training speed. The images were then decolorized to maintain color contrasts while being converted to grayscale due to computational limitations. The authors ensured that texture and pixel properties were preserved to evaluate the VAE model's effectiveness accurately. The study utilized an Intel Xeon CPU E5-2670 v3 setup with a 64-bit operating system and 128 GB RAM for processing.

Additionally, for the Arknights game maps dataset, which contains only class labels, each of the 180 available images was replicated 10 times to create a total of 1,800 images for the VAE model input. Overall, the study aims to optimize VAE performance through careful dataset selection and preprocessing techniques.
