This paper discusses a novel approach to sequence modeling in automatic speech recognition (ASR) using Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) integrated with self-attention mechanisms, as opposed to traditional recurrent and convolutional models. CTC facilitates the modeling of sequence distributions by marginalizing over all paths corresponding to an output, allowing for efficient training and prediction through dynamic programming.

The authors highlight the limitations of recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly bidirectional long short-term memory units (BLSTMs), due to their computational expense. They also note that while convolutional layers can reduce operations, they still face challenges with sequential processing. This leads to the exploration of self-attention layers, which provide a richer contextual representation by allowing each position to attend to all others, thus overcoming the bottleneck of sequential operations.

The proposed Self-Attention Network for CTC (SAN-CTC) framework consists of several stages: downsampling the input, embedding token positions, and projecting the final representation to logits over an intermediate alphabet. The self-attention layer operates through multi-head attention, enabling the model to capture long-range dependencies with reduced complexity compared to RNNs and convolutional layers.

In conclusion, the introduction of self-attention layers within the CTC framework offers a promising alternative to traditional methods in ASR, potentially improving efficiency and performance in processing sequences.
