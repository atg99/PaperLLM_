The paper presents an analysis of attention mechanisms in self-attentional models for automatic speech recognition (ASR), specifically focusing on character and phoneme models using the WSJ and LibriSpeech datasets. Figure 2 illustrates the average attention weights across different layers and heads, showing variations in standard deviation and attention magnitudes. It notes that the position embedding's contribution is minor, with lossy downsampling maintaining character error rate (CER) but degrading word error rate (WER).

The study highlights a gradual expansion of context across layers, with character labels exhibiting both forward and backward attention heads, suggesting that characters are more context-dependent than phonemes. The paper further evaluates the performance of various models on the LibriSpeech dataset, noting that the fully self-attentional CTC (Connectionist Temporal Classification) model achieved a state-of-the-art test-clean CER of 2.8%. The results indicate that minor label smoothing negatively impacted performance.

The comparison of different architectures reveals that the proposed models outperform previous end-to-end systems, with the CTC model achieving competitive results even without a language model (LM). The paper also explores the effects of using byte pair encoding (BPE) subwords as targets, which resulted in a CER of 7.4% and a WER of 5.2% with a subword-based LM. Overall, the findings support the efficacy of self-attention mechanisms in ASR while emphasizing the importance of context in character-based models.
