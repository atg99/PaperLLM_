The paper presents SAN-CTC, a self-attention network designed for connectionist temporal classification (CTC) in speech recognition. Building on the success of self-attention mechanisms in natural language processing, the authors explore their application in end-to-end speech recognition architectures. CTC has emerged as a popular approach for sequence transduction tasks, allowing for alignment-free processing, which is particularly beneficial for automatic speech recognition (ASR).

The authors argue that existing encoder-decoder models often face challenges due to the lack of enforced monotonicity and the necessity for vast amounts of training data. In contrast, SAN-CTC utilizes a fully self-attentional architecture, enabling it to predict tokens in parallel during inference, thus improving efficiency. The proposed model demonstrates rapid training and superior performance compared to existing CTC models and many encoder-decoder systems, achieving character error rates (CERs) of 4.7% on the Wall Street Journal dataset and 2.8% on the LibriSpeech test-clean dataset, with minimal training time and resources.

The study also investigates various design choices, such as position and downsampling techniques, and examines how different label alphabets (character, phoneme, subword) influence the performance and the behavior of attention heads within the model. Overall, the findings suggest that SAN-CTC is a promising approach for advancing state-of-the-art ASR systems, offering practical insights for future research.
