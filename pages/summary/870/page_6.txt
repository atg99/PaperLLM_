The referenced works encompass a range of advancements in speech recognition technologies, particularly focusing on sequence-to-sequence models and self-attentional mechanisms. Notable contributions include the exploration of self-attentional acoustic models by Sperber et al. and the application of Transformer architectures for syllable-based speech recognition in Mandarin by Zhou et al. Furthermore, Zhou and colleagues also investigated multilingual end-to-end recognition for low-resource languages.

Prabhavalkar et al. provided a comparative analysis of various sequence-to-sequence models, while Das et al. advanced connectionist temporal classification through attention modeling. Zhou et al. enhanced end-to-end recognition via policy learning, and the Deep Speech framework, introduced by Hannun et al., emphasized scaling end-to-end systems.

The integration of convolutional and recurrent neural networks was highlighted by Sainath et al., and Zenkel et al. examined the use of subword and crossword units in connectionist temporal classification models. The Kaldi toolkit, discussed by Povey et al., remains a significant resource in the field.

Bahdanau et al. presented an attention-based approach for large vocabulary speech recognition, while Zeghidour et al. focused on recognizing speech directly from raw waveforms. Other notable studies include the optimal completion distillation for sequence learning by Sabour et al., and the LibriSpeech corpus developed by Panayotov et al., which is crucial for automatic speech recognition research.

The Mozilla Foundation documented efforts to achieve a word error rate of less than 10%, and Liang et al. worked on learning noise-invariant representations for robust recognition. Lastly, Sriram et al. proposed a method for training sequence-to-sequence models alongside language models, showcasing the ongoing evolution of speech recognition technologies.
