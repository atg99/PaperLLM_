The paper discusses the principles of uniform affine quantization, specifically in the context of neural network accelerators for quantized inference. It defines quantization through three key parameters: scale factors, zero-point, and bit-width. The scale factor determines the step size of the quantizer, while the zero-point ensures accurate mapping of real zero to the integer grid, which is crucial for avoiding quantization errors during operations like zero padding or ReLU.

The quantization operation begins with mapping a real-valued vector to an unsigned integer grid, where the values are clamped within a specified range to prevent overflow and underflow. The operation involves a de-quantization step to approximate the original real-valued input. The quantization function is defined as a combination of these steps, allowing for the determination of quantization grid limits.

The paper also addresses the trade-offs involved in choosing quantization parameters, particularly between clipping and rounding errors. Increasing the scale factor can reduce clipping errors but may lead to higher rounding errors. Additionally, it introduces symmetric uniform quantization, which simplifies the process by setting the zero-point to zero, thus reducing computational overhead but limiting the mapping flexibility between integer and floating-point domains. The paper promises further exploration of these concepts in subsequent sections.
