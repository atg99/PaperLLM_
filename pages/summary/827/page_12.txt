The paper evaluates the impact of cross-layer equalization (CLE) and bias absorption on the performance of MobileNetV2 when quantized to INT8, compared to its original FP32 model. The results, presented in Table 3, show that the original model achieves an accuracy of 71.72% at FP32 and 0.12% at INT8. Implementing CLE slightly reduces FP32 performance to 71.70% but significantly improves INT8 performance to 69.91%. Absorbing high biases leads to a further decline in FP32 accuracy to 71.57%, while enhancing INT8 accuracy to 70.92%. The per-channel quantization method achieves 71.72% at FP32 and 70.65% at INT8.

The authors reference work by Nagel et al. (2019), who propose equalizing dynamic ranges across consecutive layers for optimal weight equalization. They also discuss the method of Meller et al. (2019), which introduces a scaling factor considering intermediate activation tensors but lacks a proof of optimality. Additionally, Nagel et al. suggest a procedure for absorbing high biases into subsequent layers to minimize discrepancies in dynamic ranges.

The paper's experiments indicate that while CLE and bias absorption result in a slight decrease in FP32 performance, they significantly enhance quantized performance. Specifically, combining CLE and bias absorption with per-tensor quantization yields superior results compared to per-channel quantization. Furthermore, the paper addresses the common issue of biased quantization error, which can shift the expected outputs of the original and quantized models.
