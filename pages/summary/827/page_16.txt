The paper outlines a standard pipeline for post-training quantization (PTQ) aimed at optimizing model performance in computer vision and natural language processing tasks. The proposed pipeline, illustrated in figure 8, includes essential steps such as cross-layer equalization (CLE), adding quantizers, setting weight ranges, applying AdaRound, performing bias correction, and determining activation ranges.

The pipeline begins with CLE, which enhances the full precision model's compatibility with quantization, particularly benefiting models with depth-wise separable layers. Following this, quantizers are selected based on the target hardware, with recommendations for symmetric quantizers for weights and asymmetric for activations. Weight range settings utilize layer-wise mean squared error (MSE) criteria, with per-channel quantization preferred in some cases.

If a small calibration dataset is available, AdaRound is employed to refine weight rounding, crucial for achieving low-bit quantization. In the absence of such data, analytical bias correction is utilized for networks using batch normalization. The pipeline concludes with the setting of quantization ranges for activation tensors, which can be achieved through MSE criteria or a data-free approach using batch normalization.

The experimental results demonstrate that the PTQ pipeline maintains competitive performance across various models. Specifically, 8-bit quantization of weights and activations results in only a marginal accuracy loss (within 0.7%) compared to floating-point representations across different tasks, including semantic segmentation, object detection, and natural language understanding. The findings indicate the efficacy of the proposed PTQ steps in practical applications.
