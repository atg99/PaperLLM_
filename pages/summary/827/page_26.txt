The referenced works focus on the quantization of neural networks, addressing the challenges of limited numerical precision and the associated biases. Esser et al. introduce learned step size quantization, which optimizes quantization parameters to enhance performance. Finkelstein et al. discuss combating quantization bias through bias adjustments. Gupta et al. explore deep learning with restricted numerical precision, emphasizing the trade-offs in model performance.

Horowitz highlights the energy challenges in computing, relevant for efficient neural network deployment. Howard et al. present MobileNets, designed for mobile vision applications, showcasing efficient convolutional neural networks. Ioffe and Szegedy's batch normalization technique accelerates training by mitigating internal covariate shifts.

Jacob et al. focus on quantization and training neural networks for integer-arithmetic-only inference, while Jain et al. propose trained uniform quantization for fixed-point hardware. Krishnamoorthi provides a comprehensive overview of quantizing deep convolutional networks for efficient inference.

Meller et al. investigate recovering quantization error through weight factorization. Nagel et al. introduce data-free quantization methods and adaptive rounding techniques for post-training quantization, improving accuracy without extensive data requirements.

Nascimento et al. present DSConv, an efficient convolution operator, while Ramachandran et al. search for optimal activation functions. Finally, Rouhani et al. discuss advancements in narrow precision inferencing at scale, presenting significant developments in quantization strategies for neural networks. These studies collectively highlight the importance of quantization in enhancing neural network efficiency and performance across various applications.
