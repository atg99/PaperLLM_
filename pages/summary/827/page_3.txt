The paper discusses the transition from floating-point to fixed-point arithmetic in digital computations, particularly in the context of neural network accelerators. It highlights that the cost of digital arithmetic scales with the number of bits used and that fixed-point addition is generally more efficient than floating-point addition. 

To facilitate this transition, the authors propose a method for converting floating-point vectors into integer representations. This involves expressing a floating-point vector as a product of a scalar and an integer vector. The quantization process allows for the accumulation of operations in a more efficient fixed-point format, where separate scale factors for weights and activations are utilized to minimize quantization errors.

The paper emphasizes the need for higher bit-width accumulators (typically 32-bits) to avoid overflow during calculations, as the results of multiple products are accumulated. After computation, the activations stored in these accumulators are quantized back to a lower bit-width, such as INT8, to optimize data transfer and prepare for subsequent layers in the neural network.

Furthermore, the authors introduce uniform affine quantization as the quantization scheme used in this study, noting its prevalence and efficiency in implementing fixed-point arithmetic.
