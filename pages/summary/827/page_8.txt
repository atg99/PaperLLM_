The paper discusses the challenges and considerations involved in quantizing neural networks, particularly focusing on the addition and concatenation of branches with differing quantization parameters. It highlights the necessity of a requantization step when these branches do not share the same quantization grids, and suggests that optimizing the network to have shared quantization parameters can mitigate this issue.

In practical terms, the paper emphasizes the importance of quantization choices, including the quantization scheme, granularity, and bit-width. It specifically notes that a homogeneous bit-width is considered for weights and activations, as it is more universally supported by hardware. The paper contrasts symmetric and asymmetric quantization, pointing out that while asymmetric quantization is more expressive, it can introduce computational overhead due to the need for additional data-dependent computations during inference.

The discussion extends to per-tensor versus per-channel quantization, with per-tensor quantization being the conventional standard. However, per-channel quantization is recognized for its potential to improve accuracy, especially when weight distributions vary across channels. The implementation of per-channel quantization is noted to be more complex for activations compared to weights.

Finally, the paper introduces post-training quantization (PTQ) as a method to convert a pre-trained FP32 network into a fixed-point network without retraining, thereby streamlining the quantization process.
