This paper discusses the impact of batch normalization (BN) folding on quantization-aware training (QAT) for neural networks, specifically focusing on the ResNet18 and MobileNetV2 architectures. The results presented in Table 7 demonstrate various configurations of BN integration during QAT, highlighting the effects of different bit-widths and BN strategies on accuracy. 

The study emphasizes the importance of simulating inference behavior during QAT by incorporating BN folding. Ignoring this effect can lead to significant accuracy drops when transitioning from training to deployment, especially with per-tensor quantization. The paper proposes a static folding method, which re-parametrizes weights by incorporating BN scaling and offset into linear layer parameters, proving to be effective for models that have already converged. 

In contrast, an alternative method involving double forward passes for updating running statistics during QAT is noted to be more complex and computationally intensive, yet results show that static folding often yields comparable or superior performance.

Furthermore, the paper explores per-channel quantization, which enhances accuracy by allowing for the absorption of BN scaling into per-channel scale factors. This approach retains the BN layer during training, merging scaling factors into quantization parameters post-training. Overall, the findings indicate that careful consideration of BN folding strategies is crucial for optimizing accuracy in quantized neural networks.
