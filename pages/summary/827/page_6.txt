This paper discusses the process of quantized inference on devices, specifically focusing on convolutional layers. It presents two approaches to simulate quantized inference: one using actual fixed-point operations on dedicated hardware and another using floating-point operations on general-purpose hardware. The simulation approach is highlighted as an easier method for testing various quantization options and allows for GPU acceleration in quantization-aware training.

The paper explains how inputs such as biases, weights, and activations are represented in fixed-point format during on-device inference, while simulations utilize floating-point formats. To bridge this gap, quantizer blocks are introduced in the compute graph to mimic quantization effects. These blocks implement quantization functions defined by parameters like scale factor, zero-point, and bit-width.

Additionally, the paper discusses batch normalization folding, a technique that integrates batch normalization operations into adjacent linear layers, thereby reducing computational overhead and minimizing data movement. This process enhances efficiency during on-device inference by eliminating the need for separate batch normalization calculations.

Overall, the document emphasizes the importance of accurately simulating quantized inference for effective deployment in devices while addressing performance optimization techniques such as batch normalization folding.
