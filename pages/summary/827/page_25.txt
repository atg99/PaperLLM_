Deep learning has become essential in various applications, necessitating fast and power-efficient neural network inference. Neural network quantization is a key method for achieving these goals by converting floating-point representations to fixed-point formats, thereby reducing energy consumption and latency during inference. This paper discusses two main quantization approaches: Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) and Quantization-Aware Training (QAT).

PTQ involves converting pre-trained FP32 networks to fixed-point without retraining, providing a low-effort solution that maintains high accuracy. The proposed PTQ pipeline achieves 8-bit quantization with accuracy within 1% of floating-point results across various models and tasks. Additionally, it demonstrates that many networks can be effectively quantized to 4-bit weights with minimal accuracy loss. A debugging workflow is introduced to address potential issues in quantizing new networks.

QAT, on the other hand, simulates quantization noise during training, leading to better performance than PTQ and allowing for more aggressive activation quantization. The authors present a standard QAT pipeline that includes batch normalization folding, revealing that static folding outperforms more complex methods. With this pipeline, 4-bit quantization of weights and, in some cases, activations can be achieved with only slight accuracy reductions.

The choice between PTQ and QAT hinges on the specific accuracy and power requirements of applications. Both methods are crucial for enhancing model efficiency, and the authors aim for their proposed pipelines to facilitate the deployment of high-performing quantized models with reduced effort and time.
