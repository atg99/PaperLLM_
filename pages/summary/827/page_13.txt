The paper discusses the challenges and solutions associated with quantization in neural networks, particularly focusing on the bias introduced by quantization errors in depth-wise separable layers. The primary issue identified is the clipping error, which can lead to a shift in the expected output distribution. Several studies have proposed methods to address this shift, including empirical and analytic bias correction techniques.

The expected output distribution is derived by considering both the weight and quantization errors. The biased error resulting from quantization can be corrected by subtracting it from the output, thereby aligning the output distribution with the original. The empirical bias correction method utilizes a calibration dataset to compute the correction term by comparing activations from quantized and full precision models. In contrast, the analytic bias correction method, introduced by Nagel et al., leverages batch normalization statistics to estimate the expected input distribution without requiring additional data.

Experiments demonstrate that applying analytic bias correction significantly enhances the performance of quantized models, achieving over 50% improvement. When combined with a technique known as CLE, the models can reach near full precision performance without additional data.

The paper also introduces the AdaRound strategy, which challenges the conventional rounding-to-nearest method for quantizing neural network weights. While rounding-to-nearest minimizes mean squared error, it may not be the most effective approach for optimizing quantization. The authors suggest that alternative strategies, such as AdaRound, could yield better performance outcomes by taking into account the specific characteristics of the network and quantization grid.
