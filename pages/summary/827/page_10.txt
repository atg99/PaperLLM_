The paper presents an ablation study on different methods for range setting in activation quantizers while maintaining weights in FP32 format, focusing on the impact on ImageNet validation accuracy. The study compares various bit-widths (A8, A6, A4) across two models: ResNet18 and MobileNetV2. Results indicate that while MSE and min-max approaches perform similarly at higher bit-widths, MSE outperforms min-max at lower bit-widths. Additionally, combining MSE with cross-entropy for the last layer, referred to as MSE + Xent, yields superior performance, particularly at lower bit-widths.

The paper emphasizes the importance of cross-entropy in quantizing logits, as it prioritizes preserving the order of significant logits over less important ones, contrasting with MSE's equal weighting approach. Furthermore, it discusses batch normalization's role in determining activation quantizer parameters, suggesting that learned shift and scale parameters can effectively guide range settings.

A section on cross-layer equalization highlights issues with quantization error due to the variability in tensor magnitudes, particularly in depth-wise separable layers. The authors note that batch normalization folding exacerbates this issue, leading to imbalances in weights across output channels, which is critical for per-tensor quantization but less so for per-channel quantization.

Overall, the study provides insights into optimizing quantization methods to improve model accuracy while addressing challenges related to tensor magnitudes and the importance of proper loss functions in the quantization process.
