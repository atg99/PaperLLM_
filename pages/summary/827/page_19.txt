The paper discusses techniques for effectively quantizing neural networks to improve performance while minimizing accuracy loss. Key strategies include:

1. **Per-Channel Quantization:** This method addresses uneven weight distribution by quantizing weights on a per-channel basis, enhancing model performance.

2. **Bias Correction and AdaRound:** If calibration data is available, these techniques can be applied to refine quantization and correct biases in weight distributions.

3. **Activation Quantization:** To mitigate quantization errors in activations, the paper suggests experimenting with various range setting methods and adjusting the Channel-wise Linear Expansion (CLE) to account for activation ranges, as the standard CLE may lead to uneven activation distributions.

4. **Per-Layer Analysis:** If global quantization does not restore acceptable accuracy, each quantizer can be analyzed individually. By setting each quantizer to the target bit-width sequentially while keeping others at 32 bits, the impact of quantization can be assessed.

5. **Visualization of Layers:** Visualizing the tensor distribution at different granularities helps identify tensors sensitive to quantization, allowing for targeted adjustments such as custom range settings or increasing bit-width for specific quantizers.

6. **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT):** When post-training quantization techniques fall short, particularly for low-bit quantization (e.g., 4-bit), QAT is recommended. This method simulates quantization noise during training, enabling the model to learn more optimal solutions. However, it comes with challenges such as increased training time, the necessity for labeled data, and hyperparameter tuning.

7. **Gradient Approximation for Backward Path:** The paper highlights the difficulty of back-propagating through quantization due to the undefined gradient of rounding operations. The straight-through estimator (STE) is proposed as a solution, approximating the gradient of the rounding operation to facilitate gradient-based training.

In conclusion, the paper outlines a comprehensive approach to quantization, emphasizing the importance of both post-training techniques and QAT to achieve optimal model performance while addressing the challenges posed by low-bit quantization.
