During inference, batch normalization is represented as an affine transformation of the output x. The process involves using the mean and variance, calculated as an exponential moving average during training, alongside learned affine parameters for each channel. When batch normalization is applied after a linear layer, it can be integrated with the linear transformation, simplifying the computations.

In practical implementations, particularly in quantized accelerators, the activation function typically follows the linear operation. To enhance efficiency, many hardware designs perform non-linear transformations before requantization, thereby eliminating the need to store and reload activation values. Simple non-linearities like ReLU can be easily managed within the requantization framework, while more complex functions such as sigmoid or Swish may necessitate additional quantization steps, potentially impacting model accuracy.

Furthermore, the handling of other neural network layers varies based on hardware specifics. For instance, max pooling does not require activation quantization as input and output values align on the same quantization grid. Conversely, average pooling necessitates a quantization step due to the nature of integer averages. Element-wise addition, while straightforward, poses challenges in simulation due to the requirement for matching quantization ranges of the inputs. Overall, proper quantization simulation is crucial for achieving optimal performance in neural network implementations.
