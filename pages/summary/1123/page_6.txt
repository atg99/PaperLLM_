The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in task scheduling within Cloud-Fog computing environments. It highlights the limitations of existing studies, such as those by Gu et al. and Bitam et al., which either fail to generalize across Cloud-Fog scenarios or only focus on small datasets without considering Cloud data center associations. The research emphasizes the significant differences in processing capabilities and resource costs between Cloud and Fog nodes, suggesting that traditional scheduling algorithms may not be effective in hybrid environments.

The main contribution of this study is the introduction of the TCaS algorithm, an evolutionary strategy designed to optimize the trade-off between execution time and processing cost in task scheduling. The paper also compares TCaS with other evolutionary algorithms, including Particle Swarm Optimization and Bee Life Algorithm, as well as the Round Robin method to showcase its efficiency.

The proposed system architecture involves a Fog broker that coordinates task scheduling between Fog nodes and Cloud nodes. Mobile users send requests to the nearest Fog node, which forwards them to the Fog broker. The broker decomposes jobs into tasks, estimates resource needs, and employs the TCaS algorithm to assign tasks to the appropriate nodes. The results are processed and returned to the Fog broker, which combines them and relays the final response to the user. This structured approach aims to enhance the performance and efficiency of task execution in Cloud-Fog computing environments.
