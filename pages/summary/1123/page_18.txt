The paper presents a study on task scheduling for IoT applications within a Cloud-Fog computing environment, introducing the TCaS algorithm, which is based on evolutionary strategies. The results indicate that TCaS significantly outperformed three other methods—MPSO, BLA, and RR—in both Fog and Cloud-Fog systems, achieving better trade-offs between execution time and cost. Specifically, TCaS demonstrated an average improvement of 11.04% over MPSO, 15.11% over BLA, and 44.17% over RR across various task sets, particularly in reducing makespan.

The study explored the influence of the balance coefficient 'a' on performance metrics. When 'a' was set to 0, the monetary cost was maximized, but the makespan was excessively long at 1827 seconds due to task centralization on low-cost nodes. Adjusting 'a' to 0.5 improved makespan by 5.09 times with a 1.20 times increase in total cost. As 'a' approached 1, there was a slight reduction in scheduling length and a marginal increase in cost, indicating a focus on execution time optimization. This flexibility allows users to prioritize either performance or cost efficiency based on their requirements.

Future work aims to enhance the scheduling algorithms further, particularly evolutionary algorithms, and to address additional objectives such as energy consumption and resource optimization. Practical constraints like budget and deadlines will also be integrated into future studies.
