The paper discusses the integration of Fog computing with the Internet of Things (IoT), highlighting its potential to provide uninterrupted services even with sporadic cloud connectivity. It outlines the security challenges faced by IoT devices and how Fog systems can enhance security by acting as proxies, managing security credentials, executing security protocols, monitoring device security, and identifying threats using contextual information.

The paper reviews various survey articles that examine Fog computing's applications, particularly in healthcare and other scenarios as outlined by Shi et al. and Yi et al. The architecture proposed includes central Fog services within a software-defined resource management layer, which allows for Cloud-based middleware to monitor and orchestrate Fog cells while preventing autonomous actions.

The integration of IoT with Fog computing is further explored by Bonomi et al., who present a hierarchically distributed architecture tested in applications like intelligent traffic systems. Yousefpour et al. focus on minimizing service delays in IoT-Fog-Cloud applications through a delay-minimizing policy that optimizes communication between Fog nodes.

Security and privacy concerns arising from this integration are discussed by Lee et al., who emphasize the necessity of establishing a secure Fog computing environment. Hong et al. introduce a Mobile Fog architecture that enables data processing across devices, enhancing IoT application performance. Finally, Mahmud et al. provide a taxonomy of the Fog computing landscape, differentiating it from other computing paradigms and examining associated challenges and metrics.

The paper concludes with a mention of task scheduling algorithms relevant to Fog computing, summarizing past studies and their contributions to addressing scheduling problems in this emerging field.
