The paper presents a comparative analysis of the proposed TCaS algorithm, which is based on Genetic Algorithm principles, against other evolutionary algorithms: Modified Particle Swarm Optimization (MPSO) and Bee Life Algorithm (BLA), as well as a traditional scheduling method, Round Robin (RR). The BLA, inspired by bee behaviors such as reproduction and food foraging, operates by evaluating a population of individuals, selecting a queen bee, and utilizing drones and worker bees for optimization through crossover and mutation processes.

The experimental setup utilized an Intel Core i7-4710MQ processor with 8 GB of memory, running the iFogSim simulator on Windows 10 Professional. The parameters for each algorithm were standardized, with 30 running times, a population size of 100 for TCaS and MPSO, and 69100 for BLA, along with a crossover rate of 90% and a mutation rate of 1%. The balance coefficient in the optimization process was set to a=0.5, indicating equal priority for makespan and total cost.

Two scenarios were examined: the first involved task scheduling within a Fog environment consisting of 10 interconnected Fog nodes, tasked with executing between 40 and 500 tasks. Each algorithm's performance was evaluated over 30 iterations with 500 generations to derive average results, allowing for a comprehensive analysis of the scheduling efficiency across different algorithms.
