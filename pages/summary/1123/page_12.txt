The paper discusses an evolutionary algorithm, specifically a modified Genetic Algorithm (GA) termed TCaS, which incorporates various selection and mutation strategies to optimize the process of generating new populations. 

The selection strategy emphasizes the Darwinian principle of survival of the fittest, where individuals with higher fitness values are more likely to be selected as parents for the next generation. Following a two-point crossover operation, offspring are evaluated based on their fitness compared to their parents. If an offspring has a better fitness value, it is retained for the next generation; otherwise, the parent remains in the population. This greedy selection approach helps maintain population diversity, ensuring that less fit individuals are not rapidly eliminated, allowing for exploration of new solution areas.

Additionally, a one-point mutation operator is applied to introduce variation in the population after crossover. This mutation involves randomly altering a gene in an individual, which helps to avoid local optima and enhances the search for optimal solutions.

Key modifications introduced in the TCaS algorithm include a unique parental selection process where the first parent is any individual in the population and the second is chosen using the roulette wheel method. The greedy selection strategy ensures that only superior offspring are passed to the next generation, promoting the retention of advantageous genetic traits while allowing for continued exploration of the solution space.
