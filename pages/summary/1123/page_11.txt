4.2.5. Mutation Operator
The mutation operator introduces random changes to the genes in a chromosome to maintain genetic diversity and prevent premature convergence. A mutation rate is defined, and for each gene in a chromosome, a random decision is made to alter its value. This ensures that new traits can emerge in the population, further exploring the solution space.

4.3. Algorithm Steps
The proposed TCaS algorithm follows a systematic approach: 
1. Initialize the population randomly.
2. Evaluate the fitness of each individual using the defined fitness function.
3. Select individuals based on their fitness for reproduction.
4. Apply crossover and mutation operators to create a new generation.
5. Repeat the evaluation and selection process until a stopping criterion is met, such as a maximum number of generations or a satisfactory level of fitness.

4.4. Conclusion
The TCaS algorithm effectively addresses the task scheduling problem in Cloud-Fog computing environments by leveraging genetic algorithm principles. Its chromosome encoding, population initialization, and genetic operators contribute to discovering optimal scheduling solutions while ensuring diversity and adaptability within the population. This approach aims to enhance efficiency and reduce costs in task management across distributed computing systems.
