The paper summarizes various task scheduling algorithms for Fog computing, highlighting their ideas, improvements, criteria, and limitations. Key contributions include Huynh Thi Thanh Binh's genetic algorithm focused on execution time and processing cost, albeit limited to small datasets. Bitam et al.'s bee life algorithm also targets execution time and memory but is restricted to Fog environments. Gu et al. introduced a two-phase linear programming heuristic aimed at reducing communication costs, yet it lacks computational offloading capability.

Deng et al. emphasized minimizing power consumption and latency using nonlinear integers and the Hungarian method, but their approach is challenging to implement in Fog computing due to its centralized nature. Guo et al. addressed power consumption and latency in edge Clouds but relied on a simplistic model. Ningning et al. proposed a graph-based load balancing mechanism, which, while effective, struggles with dynamic load balancing. Oueis et al. improved user experience through load balancing but face complexity issues as the Fog infrastructure scales.

Abdi et al. compared modified particle swarm optimization with PSO and GA, achieving better task completion times, yet their work is limited to Cloud environments. Overall, the studies reflect a range of methodologies and outcomes, with many facing challenges related to scalability, dataset size, and applicability to dynamic Fog computing environments.
