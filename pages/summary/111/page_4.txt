This paper provides a comprehensive overview and comparative analysis of deep learning models, focusing on two primary architectures: Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs).

MLPs, the foundational architecture for deep learning, operate under a supervised learning framework. They consist of three layers: an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. Each layer is fully connected, allowing for comprehensive interaction between neurons. The input layer normalizes data, hidden layers process inputs, and the output layer generates predictions. The activation function, represented by a non-linear mapping in the model, is crucial for determining the output based on the weighted input and bias.

CNNs are recognized for their effectiveness in various applications such as object detection, speech recognition, computer vision, and time series prediction. They utilize a feedforward structure that includes a classifier and a feature extractor, enabling automatic feature extraction and minimal preprocessing. This architecture is inspired by human visual perception and consists of key components: convolutional layers, pooling layers, fully connected layers, and activation functions. The CNN design allows it to learn features directly from data without manual intervention, enhancing its efficiency and performance in processing tasks.

Figures included illustrate the structure of the MLP and the operational pipeline of a CNN, emphasizing their respective processes in handling input data and generating outputs.
