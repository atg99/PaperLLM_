The paper references a series of influential works in the field of deep learning and computer vision, highlighting advancements in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Key contributions include the introduction of novel activation functions, such as Gaussian Error Linear Units (GELUs), and significant architectures like AlexNet, VGG, and Inception, which have shaped image classification and recognition tasks. The paper discusses the development of deeper and more efficient networks, including Residual Networks (ResNets) and DenseNet, which address issues of vanishing gradients and model scalability. Additionally, it covers lightweight models like SqueezeNet and MobileNet, designed for mobile applications, and strategies like depthwise separable convolutions in Xception. Recent advancements, such as EfficientNet and its successor EfficientNetV2, focus on optimizing model performance and training efficiency. The findings underscore the evolution of hybrid deep learning models and their applications, particularly in human activity recognition.
