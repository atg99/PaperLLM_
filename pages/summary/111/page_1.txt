This review article provides a comprehensive overview of various deep learning models within the context of artificial intelligence and machine learning. It highlights the advancements of deep learning as a powerful subset of machine learning, particularly in handling unstructured and large datasets across multiple domains such as speech recognition, healthcare, and autonomous vehicles. The authors detail several deep learning architectures, including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN), Transformers, Kolmogorov-Arnold networks (KAN), Generative Models, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), and Deep Transfer Learning.

The paper examines the structure, applications, advantages, and limitations of each model, providing a critical analysis of their effectiveness. Performance evaluations are conducted using three publicly available datasets: IMDB, ARAS, and Fruit-360. Specifically, the authors compare six notable models—CNN, RNN, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Bidirectional LSTM, Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), and Bidirectional GRU—using the IMDB and ARAS datasets. They also assess the performance of eight CNN-based models, including VGG, Inception, ResNet, and others, for image classification tasks with the Fruit-360 dataset.

Overall, the article serves as a valuable resource for understanding the diverse landscape of deep learning models and their applications, while also addressing the challenges inherent in developing effective models for real-world problems.
