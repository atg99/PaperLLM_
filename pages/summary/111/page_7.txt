The paper discusses various activation functions used in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), highlighting the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) as a predominant choice due to its effectiveness in addressing the vanishing gradient problem during training. ReLU is mathematically defined as f(x)=max(0,x), which allows CNNs to learn complex features by deactivating negative inputs while preserving positive values, thus preventing neuron saturation.

In contrast, the Sigmoid function, defined as f(x)=1/(1+e^(-x)), is noted for its S-shape and ability to map real numbers to a range between 0 and 1, making it suitable for binary classification. However, its tendency to saturate can impede training, leading to the vanishing gradient issue in deep networks.

The paper also mentions various CNN architectures, including AlexNet, VGG, Inception, ResNet, and others, which are utilized in diverse fields such as natural language processing and image analysis. Each variant demonstrates distinct learning capabilities and performance suited for specific applications.
