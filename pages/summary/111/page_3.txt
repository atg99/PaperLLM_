The paper discusses deep learning (DL), highlighting its ability to learn hierarchical data representations through architectures with multiple hidden layers. The rise of high-performance computing has contributed to the growing popularity of deep learning, particularly using deep neural networks. In deep learning, data is processed through several layers, where initial layers extract low-level features that are progressively combined by subsequent layers to create a comprehensive representation.

Unlike traditional machine learning, which relies on a sequential process involving pre-processing and meticulous feature selection, deep learning allows for simultaneous learning and classification. This automated feature learning capability enhances efficiency across various tasks. The paper categorizes deep learning methods into discriminative (supervised) and generative (unsupervised) approaches, with prominent examples including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) for discriminative models, and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Auto-Encoders (AEs) for generative models.

The section on supervised deep learning models focuses on algorithms designed for classification tasks that model posterior distributions of classes based on observable data. Common supervised models include Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), CNN, RNN, Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN), and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN), each offering distinct advantages in pattern classification. The paper promises a comprehensive survey of these deep learning models in subsequent sections.
