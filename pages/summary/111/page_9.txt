The paper discusses the architecture and functionality of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, specifically focusing on their internal mechanisms and extensions. Figure 10 illustrates the high-level architecture and the inner workings of an LSTM unit. The update equations for the LSTM unit, as expressed in Equation (5), detail how the hidden state and cell state are computed using activation functions and gating mechanisms. The gating operations, represented as g, utilize a sigmoid activation function to produce weights for the input, output, and forget gates.

While standard LSTMs perform well in sequential tasks, they may struggle with more complex input structures. To address this, a tree-structured LSTM, referred to as S-LSTM, is introduced. S-LSTM enhances performance in challenging sequential modeling tasks but with increased computational complexity.

The paper also covers Bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM), an extension of the LSTM architecture that processes data in both forward and backward directions. This dual processing allows Bi-LSTM to capture temporal dependencies more effectively by considering both past and future contexts. The structure of Bi-LSTM consists of two parallel LSTM layers, improving the model's understanding of sequences.
