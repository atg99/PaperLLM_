The paper discusses the architecture of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and their enhancements, particularly focusing on the final memory state and the role of gates in controlling information flow. The final memory state, denoted as ℎ𝑡, is computed through a combination of the previous hidden state and the candidate activation, as expressed in Equation (9). The update gate, represented by 𝑧𝑡, balances these two components. An output gate, 𝑜𝑡, further regulates the flow of information to the output, calculated using the current memory state and typically employing an activation function like the sigmoid function. The GRU offers a simpler alternative to Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, requiring fewer tensor operations and enabling faster training; however, the selection between GRU and LSTM is contingent upon the specific application and problem characteristics.

Additionally, the paper introduces the Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU), which enhances the conventional GRU by processing input sequences in both forward and backward directions. This is achieved through two parallel GRU layers, allowing the model to integrate contextual information from both the past and future, thereby improving performance in sequential modeling tasks. The structural layout of the Bi-GRU is illustrated, demonstrating its dual processing capability.
