The references listed include various studies and techniques addressing the challenge of class imbalance in machine learning, particularly in classification tasks. Key contributions involve different sampling methods such as oversampling, undersampling, and cost-sensitive learning to improve model performance on imbalanced datasets.

Tang et al. (2008) discuss SVM modeling for imbalanced classification, while Barua et al. (2012) introduce the Majority Weighted Minority Oversampling Technique (MWMOTE). Bellinger et al. (2020) present a framework for extreme imbalance classification, emphasizing the SWIM approach that incorporates majority class sampling.

Other notable methods include Reverse-SMOTE, which integrates reverse nearest neighbors for oversampling (Das et al., 2020), and Constrained Oversampling aimed at reducing noise in imbalanced datasets (Liu et al., 2020). Tarawneh et al. (2020) propose Smotefuna, utilizing the furthest neighbor algorithm, while Liu et al. (2008) explore exploratory undersampling techniques.

Additional studies cover inverse random undersampling (Tahir et al., 2012), a novel undersampling approach for medical diagnosis (Babar and Ade, 2016), and discussions on multi-class cost-sensitive learning (Zhou and Liu, 2010; Ling and Sheng, 2008). Seliya et al. (2021) provide a literature review on one-class classification applications in big data, and Spelmen and Porkodi (2018) review methods for handling imbalanced data.

Overall, these works contribute to a deeper understanding of strategies to effectively manage class imbalance, enhancing classification accuracy in diverse applications.
