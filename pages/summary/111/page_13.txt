The paper discusses the architecture of Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) and addresses challenges related to network depth, such as vanishing gradients. To tackle these issues, TCNs utilize dilated convolutions, which expand the receptive field exponentially without significantly increasing computational complexity. This is achieved by inserting gaps between weights in the convolutional kernel, allowing for efficient processing of large time series data. The mathematical formulation of dilated convolution is presented, highlighting how increasing the dilation rate enhances the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the importance of residual connections in TCNs. These connections enable better data flow across layers by providing shortcut paths, allowing the network to learn residual functions instead of full transformations. This technique is particularly beneficial for training very deep networks, mitigating the risk of gradient decay or vanishing gradients. Overall, the combination of dilated convolutions and residual connections enhances the expressiveness and performance of TCNs in handling complex time series data.
