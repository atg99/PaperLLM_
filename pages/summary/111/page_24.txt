The paper provides a comprehensive overview of different strategies within deep transfer learning (DTL), focusing on three primary approaches: instance-based, mapping-based, and network-based (model-based) deep transfer learning.

Instance-based DTL selects a subset of instances from the source domain and assigns weight values to enhance the training set for the target domain. Notable algorithms in this realm include TaskTrAdaBoost and TrAdaBoost.R2.

Mapping-based DTL aims to project instances from both domains into a new data space where they share similarities, facilitating unified training of deep neural networks. Methods such as Extend MMD and MK-MMD exemplify this approach.

Network-based DTL is characterized by the reuse of segments from pre-trained networks, including their architecture and parameters, to adapt to the target domain. This method is particularly effective for domain adaptation, even when significant differences exist between source and target data. It typically involves steps like pre-training, freezing, fine-tuning, and adding new layers. Freezing retains fixed parameters from the pre-trained model, while fine-tuning initializes parameters with pre-trained values, enhancing the learning process.

A notable advancement in network-based DTL is the introduction of Progressive Neural Networks (PNNs). This approach freezes a pre-trained model and adds new layers for training on target data, illustrating a learning process akin to human skill acquisition, where new abilities build upon existing knowledge. Each new task encountered leads to the construction of a new model, reflecting the incremental nature of learning.
