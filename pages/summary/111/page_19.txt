The Transformer architecture, introduced by Vaswani et al. in 2017 for machine translation, has become a key model in deep learning, particularly in natural language processing. It operates as a self-attention encoder-decoder structure. The encoder is composed of a stack of identical layers, each featuring two sublayers: a multi-head self-attention mechanism and a position-wise fully connected feed-forward network. Normalizing layers and residual connections link the inputs and outputs of the attention module. The decoder, which generates the output sequence from the encoder's representation, also consists of identical layers but includes an additional sublayer for multi-head attention over the encoder's output.

Transformers offer a significant advantage over traditional RNN-based Seq2Seq models by allowing parallel training, which enhances computational efficiency and reduces the training time. The multi-head self-attention mechanism within the Transformer improves the model's ability to capture dependencies between elements in a sequence. It aggregates information from various tokens, enabling a better understanding of contextual relationships. This mechanism utilizes a query, key-value pairs, and an output to form an attention function, with outputs computed as weighted sums of values based on the compatibility between the query and its corresponding key.
