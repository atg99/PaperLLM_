Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are a specialized class of deep learning models that utilize internal memory to capture sequential dependencies in data. Unlike traditional neural networks, which treat inputs independently, RNNs consider the order of inputs, making them ideal for tasks such as natural language processing, video classification, and speech recognition. RNNs apply the same operation across a series of inputs, allowing current computations to depend on previous ones, which is crucial for understanding context in sequences.

Despite their strengths, standard RNNs face limitations due to their short-term memory, which hampers their ability to retain information over long sequences. To address this challenge, several advanced RNN variants have been developed, including Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, bidirectional LSTMs, Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), and bidirectional GRUs.

LSTM networks, introduced in 1997 and further refined in 2013, are particularly effective at managing long-term dependencies. They consist of an architecture that includes three gates: the input gate, which updates the internal state; the forget gate, which determines the retention of the previous internal state; and the output gate, which controls the influence of the internal state on the output. This structure enhances the model's capacity to maintain and utilize information over extended sequences, making LSTMs a preferred choice in various applications.
