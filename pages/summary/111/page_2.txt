This paper provides a comprehensive overview and comparative analysis of deep learning models, emphasizing their advancements and applications in various fields such as computer vision, text analysis, video games, medical sciences, and cybersecurity. Deep learning, a subset of machine learning, has shown superior performance over traditional methods due to key advancements, including the availability of large datasets and improvements in hardware technologies like GPUs and FPGAs, which have reduced training times and enhanced computational capabilities.

The structure of deep learning models is inspired by the human nervous system, utilizing interconnected layers of processing units. Each unit processes inputs by applying weights, contributing to the model's overall function. The paper is organized into several sections, beginning with a fundamental definition of deep learning and progressing through various model types, including supervised models (e.g., Multi-Layer Perceptron, Convolutional Neural Networks), generative models (e.g., Autoencoders, GANs), and advanced architectures like Transformers.

Additionally, the paper discusses Deep Reinforcement Learning, Deep Transfer Learning, hybrid deep learning principles, and the challenges faced in the field, along with potential solutions. Performance evaluations of different models are conducted using three datasets, leading to insights on future research directions. The paper concludes with a summary of findings and implications for the future of deep learning.
