The paper provides an extensive overview of deep learning models, focusing on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Deep Belief Networks (DBNs). GANs utilize a dual architecture comprising a discriminator and a generator, where backpropagation updates both components to enhance the discriminator's ability to identify real images and the generator's skill in creating realistic synthetic images. CNNs typically serve as discriminators, while deconvolutional neural networks function as generative networks. The applications of GANs are diverse, spanning image blending, 3D object generation, face aging, medical applications, steganography, image manipulation, text transfer, language and speech synthesis, traffic control, and video generation. Numerous specialized models have emerged from the GAN framework, including Laplacian GAN, Coupled GAN, Wasserstein GAN, and StyleGAN, among others.

In contrast, the Deep Belief Network (DBN) is a generative model designed for unsupervised learning, effectively uncovering patterns in large datasets. DBNs consist of multiple layers of hidden units that excel in feature extraction from unlabeled data, demonstrating greater resistance to overfitting compared to discriminative models. The architecture of a DBN relies on a stack of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), which are trained layer by layer. Each RBM consists of a hidden layer for latent representations and a visible layer for observable data features. Through iterative training, DBNs develop joint probability distributions that capture the underlying data structure. The paper includes a schematic representation of a DBN model for clarity.
