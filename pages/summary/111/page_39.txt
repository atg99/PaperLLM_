The paper presents a deep learning approach for fruit image classification using a dataset of 55,244 images across 81 fruit classes. The methodology involves passing images through an input layer, followed by feature extraction using one of eight models: VGG, Inception, ResNet, InceptionResNet, Xception, MobileNet, DenseNet, or NASNet. A Global Average Pooling layer reduces data dimensionality, and a dropout layer mitigates overfitting. The output is classified using a fully connected layer with a Softmax function.

For the experiments, a subset of 60 fruit classes was utilized, consisting of 28,484 training images and 9,558 testing images. All models were trained under consistent parameters, including 20 epochs, a batch size of 512, a dropout rate of 0.2, and the Adam optimizer, with pre-training on the ImageNet dataset.

DenseNet and MobileNet emerged as the top performers, with MobileNet offering a notable advantage in training time while maintaining high accuracy. The Xception model also demonstrated strong performance with a shorter training duration than DenseNet. The findings suggest that MobileNet is a particularly effective choice for fruit image classification due to its efficiency and accuracy. Additionally, accuracy and loss diagrams were utilized to further assess model performance during training.
