The paper discusses significant challenges faced by deep learning models, particularly focusing on insufficient and imbalanced data. 

Insufficient data is a major hurdle as deep learning models typically require large datasets for optimal performance. To tackle this issue, the paper suggests three strategies: 

1. **Transfer Learning**: Utilizing pre-trained model components in new models.
2. **Data Augmentation**: Enhancing the model's generalization capabilities through various techniques such as geometric transformations and generative data augmentation.
3. **Simulated Data**: Creating additional training data through simulations based on an understanding of the underlying processes.

Imbalanced data is another prevalent challenge, where one class significantly outnumbers another in the dataset. The paper outlines three main approaches to address this:

1. **Data-Level Techniques**: Adjusting the training set by oversampling the minority class or undersampling the majority class. Various methods like SMOTE and its variants are highlighted.
2. **Algorithm-Level Techniques**: Modifying learning algorithms to reduce bias towards the majority class, with methods such as Cost-Sensitive Learning and One-Class Learning.
3. **Hybrid Techniques**: Combining data-level and algorithm-level approaches to achieve better balance.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the necessity of addressing these challenges to enhance the effectiveness of deep learning models in real-world applications.
