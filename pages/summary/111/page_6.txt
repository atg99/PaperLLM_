The paper discusses the structure and function of Fully Connected (FC) layers and activation functions in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The FC layer, positioned at the end of a CNN architecture, connects every neuron to all neurons in the preceding layer, serving as the classifier that enables the network to make predictions based on the flattened feature maps from previous layers.

Activation functions are essential for introducing non-linearity into CNNs, allowing them to model complex patterns and relationships in data. Without these functions, CNNs would be limited to linear operations, hindering their ability to accurately represent non-linear behaviors common in real-world applications. The paper illustrates how activation functions transform input signals into outputs, emphasizing the importance of various types, including Sigmoid, Tanh, and Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) functions.

Saturating nonlinearities, such as Sigmoid and Tanh, can lead to issues when inputs are extreme, prompting the development of alternative nonlinearities to mitigate these effects. These alternatives include various forms of ReLU, such as Leaky ReLU, Parametric ReLU, and Exponential Linear Units, among others. The paper highlights the significance of these activation functions in enhancing the performance of CNNs across diverse applications by enabling the recognition of intricate patterns and delivering accurate predictions.
