The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of deep learning models, focusing on their performance across different datasets. Preprocessing tasks such as data cleaning and segmentation have resulted in normalized and balanced data, leading to consistent outcomes. The Transformer and Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) models outperformed others in the ARAS dataset, which contains spatial and temporal sequences of sensor events. The Transformer achieved the highest scores in accuracy, recall, and F1-score, while the Bi-LSTM model excelled in precision. Additionally, the Transformer demonstrated efficient training times, second only to CNN, and stabilized earlier in accuracy and loss curves compared to other models. Overall, the Transformer model emerged as the most effective choice for recognizing human activities based on sensor data due to its balance of accuracy, training efficiency, and consistency.

In the methodology section focused on the Fruit-360 dataset, the paper highlights the limitations of recurrent models for image classification tasks, emphasizing the superior performance of CNN-based models. Eight CNN variants—VGG, Inception, ResNet, InceptionResNet, Xception, MobileNet, DenseNet, and NASNet—were analyzed using deep transfer learning techniques to enhance classification accuracy. A structural overview of these models is provided, illustrating their evaluation on the Fruit-360 dataset.
