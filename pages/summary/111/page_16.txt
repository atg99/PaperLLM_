The paper provides a comprehensive overview of generative deep learning models, emphasizing their role in unsupervised learning, particularly within computer vision. It discusses the evolution of generative models (GMs) that utilize training data from unknown distributions to create new samples that mimic real data distributions. Key models mentioned include Autoencoders, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), and Deep Belief Networks (DBNs).

Focusing on Autoencoders, the paper explains that they are neural networks designed to reconstruct input data by learning meaningful representations in an unsupervised manner. An Autoencoder consists of two main components: an encoder that maps input data to a feature space, and a decoder that reconstructs the data from this feature space. Modern Autoencoders are represented as stochastic functions, enhancing their application in various tasks such as generative modeling, dimensionality reduction, feature extraction, anomaly detection, and denoising.

The paper categorizes Autoencoders into Regularized Autoencoders, which aid in classification tasks, and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), which serve as generative models using probabilistic distributions for data generation. VAEs are particularly noted for their framework that combines an encoder and a decoder, facilitating deep latent-variable modeling and inference. Overall, the paper highlights the significance of generative models in advancing unsupervised learning methodologies.
