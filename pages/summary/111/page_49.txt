This section references a variety of significant works in the fields of autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs). The foundational paper by Kingma and Welling introduces variational autoencoders, providing a framework for probabilistic graphical models. Ng discusses sparse autoencoders, which emphasize feature learning. Rifai et al. present higher-order contractive autoencoders, enhancing robustness in feature extraction. Vincent et al. explore denoising autoencoders, highlighting their ability to learn strong representations from corrupted data.

In the domain of GANs, Liu and Tuzel's work on coupled generative adversarial networks expands the capabilities of GANs by enabling joint distribution learning. Wang et al. introduce evolutionary GANs, which incorporate evolutionary strategies for improved performance. Aggarwal et al. provide an overview of GANs, detailing their theoretical underpinnings and applications. Notable applications of GANs include image compositing, face aging, synthetic brain image generation, and high-capacity image steganography.

Further advancements include SC-GAN for neuroimaging synthesis, Text-adaptive GANs for image manipulation through natural language, and GE-GAN for traffic state estimation. Each of these studies contributes to the understanding and application of deep learning techniques in various fields, showcasing the versatility and potential of autoencoders and GANs in modern artificial intelligence.
