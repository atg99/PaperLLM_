The paper presents a systematic study of network architecture design choices aimed at enhancing the efficiency of object detection. It introduces a weighted bidirectional feature network and a customized compound scaling method, forming the basis for a new family of detectors called EfficientDet. This approach demonstrates improved accuracy and efficiency compared to prior models across various resource constraints.

Key findings include the comparison between softmax-based and fast normalized feature fusion methods. The fast normalized fusion achieves similar accuracy to softmax while offering a speedup of 1.26x to 1.31x on GPUs, indicating a more efficient processing capability. The analysis of normalized weights during training reveals that different features contribute unequally to the fusion process, with rapid changes in weights observed.

Furthermore, the paper discusses compound scaling, which jointly scales multiple dimensions of the network architecture, achieving better efficiency and accuracy compared to alternative methods that focus on scaling a single dimension. The results indicate that EfficientDet models achieve state-of-the-art performance with significantly fewer parameters and FLOPs than existing object detection and semantic segmentation models.

In conclusion, the study highlights the effectiveness of designed architecture and scaling methods in optimizing object detection tasks, paving the way for future advancements in the field. Acknowledgments are given to contributors who assisted with infrastructure and discussions.
