The paper discusses a novel approach to feature fusion in object detection through a method called BiFPN (Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network). This method introduces learnable weights that can vary in dimensionality—scalar, vector, or tensor—allowing for flexible feature scaling. While scalar weights can lead to training instability due to their unbounded nature, the authors implement weight normalization to maintain stability.

To fuse features efficiently, the paper critiques traditional softmax-based fusion, which, despite its effectiveness, introduces latency on GPU hardware. As a solution, the authors propose a fast normalized fusion method that uses a rectified linear unit (ReLU) to ensure non-negativity of weights and a small epsilon value to prevent numerical instability. This approach achieves comparable accuracy to softmax fusion while being significantly faster.

The BiFPN architecture utilizes both top-down and bottom-up connections for feature fusion, employing depthwise separable convolutions and batch normalization to enhance efficiency. The resulting detection models, termed EfficientDet, leverage pretrained EfficientNets as their backbone and integrate the BiFPN for feature extraction and prediction.

Additionally, the paper introduces a compound scaling method for EfficientDet, which optimally adjusts the model's dimensions—backbone network, BiFPN, and prediction networks—using a compound coefficient to balance accuracy and efficiency. This method allows for simultaneous scaling of multiple dimensions, addressing challenges posed by traditional scaling techniques that often focus on a singular aspect.

Overall, the EfficientDet framework represents a significant advancement in object detection, combining efficient feature fusion with a robust scaling strategy to accommodate various resource constraints.
