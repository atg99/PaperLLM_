The paper discusses advancements in object detection, particularly focusing on multi-scale feature fusion and model scaling. It highlights the limitations of previous methods, which often treated input features equally during fusion, disregarding their varying contributions due to differences in resolution. To overcome this, the authors introduce the Weighted Bi-directional Feature Pyramid Network (BiFPN), which incorporates learnable weights to assess the importance of different features and enables repeated top-down and bottom-up multi-scale feature fusion.

In addressing model scaling, the paper critiques the traditional reliance on larger backbone networks and image sizes for improving accuracy. Instead, it proposes a compound scaling method that optimally increases the resolution, depth, and width of all components of the detection network, including the backbone and the box/class prediction networks. The authors also note the efficiency of EfficientNets, which they combine with BiFPN and their scaling method to create a new family of object detectors called EfficientDet. This new architecture significantly outperforms existing detectors in terms of accuracy while using fewer parameters and floating-point operations per second (FLOPs).

The paper presents performance comparisons on the COCO dataset, demonstrating that EfficientDet achieves state-of-the-art results, including a notable reduction in FLOPs compared to other models like YOLOv3 and RetinaNet. Additionally, it shows that EfficientDet can achieve high mean Intersection over Union (mIOU) accuracy in semantic segmentation tasks with fewer FLOPs than competing models.

The related work section categorizes existing object detectors into one-stage and two-stage models, emphasizing the trend toward one-stage detectors due to their simplicity and efficiency. The paper also reviews previous efforts in multi-scale feature representation, highlighting various methodologies that aim to enhance the processing of features at different scales.

In the BiFPN section, the authors formalize the multi-scale feature fusion problem and outline their approach, which focuses on efficient bidirectional cross-scale connections and weighted feature fusion to enhance the performance of object detection systems.
