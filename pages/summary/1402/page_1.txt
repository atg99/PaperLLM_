In the paper "EfficientDet: Scalable and Efficient Object Detection," the authors Mingxing Tan, Ruoming Pang, and Quoc V. Le from Google Research present a comprehensive study on enhancing the efficiency of neural network architectures for object detection. They introduce key optimizations, including a weighted bi-directional feature pyramid network (BiFPN) for rapid multi-scale feature fusion and a compound scaling method that uniformly adjusts the resolution, depth, and width of backbone, feature networks, and box/class prediction networks.

The proposed EfficientDet family of object detectors leverages these optimizations alongside EfficientNet backbones, achieving superior efficiency compared to previous models across a range of resource constraints. Notably, their EfficientDet-D7 model achieves state-of-the-art performance with 52.2 average precision (AP) on the COCO test dataset, utilizing only 52 million parameters and 325 billion floating point operations (FLOPs), making it significantly smaller and less computationally intensive than prior models, which often required 30 times more FLOPs.

The authors highlight the importance of model efficiency in real-world applications, where large model sizes and high computational demands can hinder deployment in settings like robotics and autonomous vehicles. They address the challenge of creating a scalable detection architecture that balances higher accuracy with improved efficiency, aiming to meet diverse resource requirements from mobile devices to data centers. Their work sets a new standard in the field of object detection by providing a scalable solution that maintains high performance while being resource-efficient.
