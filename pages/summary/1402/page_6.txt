The paper presents an evaluation of the EfﬁcientDet models on the COCO dataset, detailing their performance metrics including average precision (AP), parameters, floating point operations (FLOPs), and inference latency across various configurations. The results indicate that EfﬁcientDet-D7 achieves a state-of-the-art performance with an AP of 52.2 on the test-dev set and 51.8 on the validation set, while requiring fewer parameters and FLOPs compared to previous models like AmoebaNet. 

The authors emphasize the efficiency of EfﬁcientDet models, showing that they are significantly faster on both GPU and CPU compared to other detectors, achieving up to 3.2 times faster GPU inference and 8.1 times faster CPU inference. This efficiency is attributed to the use of a consistent anchoring strategy and training methods that do not rely on complex setups.

In addition to object detection, the paper explores the application of EfﬁcientDet to semantic segmentation. A modified version of the EfﬁcientDet-D4 model is evaluated, demonstrating competitive performance with a mean Intersection over Union (mIOU) of 81.74% while maintaining a low parameter count and FLOPs compared to traditional models like DeepLabV3+. 

Overall, the findings highlight the versatility and efficiency of the EfﬁcientDet architecture in various computer vision tasks.
