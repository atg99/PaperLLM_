The paper presents EfficientDet, an architecture for object detection that utilizes EfficientNet as the backbone network and BiFPN for feature extraction, alongside a shared class and box prediction network. The architecture is scalable, with configurations D0 to D7 defined by a compound coefficient φ that adjusts input size, number of channels, and layers for BiFPN and the prediction networks.

Table 1 outlines the scaling configurations, indicating how depth and input resolution increase with φ. The model's input resolution must be divisible by 128, with resolutions linearly increasing based on φ. The scaling method, while heuristic and potentially non-optimal, is shown to significantly enhance efficiency compared to traditional single-dimension scaling methods.

In the evaluation section, EfficientDet is tested on the COCO 2017 dataset, utilizing a training regimen with SGD optimizer, synchronized batch normalization, swish activation, focal loss, and multi-resolution cropping. The results, summarized in Table 2, indicate that EfficientDet outperforms existing object detectors in terms of efficiency, being significantly smaller and requiring fewer FLOPs while maintaining competitive accuracy. For instance, EfficientDet-D0 matches YOLOv3 accuracy with 28 times fewer FLOPs, while EfficientDet-D1 achieves similar accuracy to RetinaNet and Mask-RCNN with substantially fewer parameters and FLOPs. Overall, EfficientDet demonstrates a balance of performance and efficiency across various accuracy regimes.
