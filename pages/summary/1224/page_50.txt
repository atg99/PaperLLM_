The study examines the differences in preferences between male and female students regarding the recommendation of OCEM to others, as well as the availability and quality of teaching resources and canteen facilities. The results show that the mean scores for the availability of teaching resources are similar for both genders, with male students scoring an average of 3.07 and female students also at 3.07, indicating no significant difference. However, for canteen facilities, female students had a higher mean score of 3.44 compared to male students' 3.00, with statistical significance suggesting a notable difference in preferences.

Additionally, a Chi-square test was performed to assess the association between gender and the likelihood of recommending OCEM to others. The results revealed that a significant majority of female students (153 out of 180) intended to recommend OCEM, compared to 36 out of 57 male students. The Chi-square value was calculated at 12.787 with a significance level of 0.001, leading to the rejection of the null hypothesis. This indicates a statistically significant association between gender and the preference to recommend OCEM for enrollment, highlighting that female students are more inclined to recommend the institution than their male counterparts.
