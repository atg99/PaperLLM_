The study evaluates customer satisfaction at Buddha Air, revealing that 165 customers expressed satisfaction while 11 indicated dissatisfaction. A predictive model demonstrated a 98.20 percent accuracy in forecasting both satisfaction and dissatisfaction, with an overall correct prediction rate of 92.40 percent. The analysis identified a significant negative correlation between ticket price fluctuations and customer satisfaction (p < 0.05), suggesting that a one-unit increase in price fluctuation could decrease satisfaction by approximately 0.559 times, consistent with previous research on the impact of price on customer satisfaction.

Additionally, a negative association was found between strict flight schedules, lengthy security processes, and customer satisfaction, supporting earlier studies. The linear regression analysis explored the relationship between customer satisfaction and categorical variables such as gender and reasons for choosing Buddha Air. The results indicated a weak positive correlation (R = 0.274) and explained only 7.50 percent of the variance in customer satisfaction, suggesting limited predictive power of the regression model. The Durbin-Watson statistic indicated no first-order linear auto-correlation in the data. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of price stability and efficient service delivery in enhancing customer satisfaction at Buddha Air.
