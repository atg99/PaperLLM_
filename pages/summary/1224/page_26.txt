The paper presents an analysis of the seven most cited articles in educational technology, focusing on citation counts and the themes of each article. The most cited article, published in 2013 in the Journal of Computing in Higher Education (JCE), is "Gamifying learning experience: Practical implications and outcomes," which has been cited 801 times, representing 27% of the total citations among the highlighted articles. Other notable articles include "Current status, opportunities and challenges of augmented reality in education," also from JCE, cited 606 times, and "Flipping the classroom and instructional technology integration in a college-level information system spreadsheet course," from the Journal of Educational Technology Research and Development (JETRD), cited 506 times.

The analysis indicates that JCE is a prominent journal in this field, demonstrating a significant number of citations for articles published between 2013 and 2017. The research themes predominantly focus on massive open online courses (MOOCs) and educational technology, emphasizing the importance of integrating Information and Communication Technology (ICT) in teaching and learning for quality education. 

Different research methods were employed across the articles, including mixed methods, longitudinal surveys, qualitative interviews, and quantitative approaches. Notably, review methods were frequently used, highlighting a trend in educational technology research. The findings align with previous studies, such as those by Abramovich et al., which noted that older articles tend to receive more citations than newer publications. Overall, the review underscores the evolving landscape of educational technology research and the significance of various methodologies and instruments in generating impactful studies.
