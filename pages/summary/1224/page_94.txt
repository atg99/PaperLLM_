This study focuses on customer satisfaction at Buddha Airlines, based in Lalitpur, Nepal, with specific emphasis on service quality, pricing, customer management, and employee behavior. The primary objective is to evaluate customer satisfaction levels concerning these factors, while previous research highlights the significance of customer relationship management (CRM) in enhancing satisfaction. CRM is described as a strategic approach aimed at fostering valuable relationships with key customers, thereby improving shareholder value.

The paper asserts that satisfied customers are essential for business success, while dissatisfaction can lead to risks and loss of clientele, particularly in the competitive airline industry. To remain viable, airlines must prioritize customer satisfaction across all company functions, not just in customer service departments. The findings from this research are expected to aid management in enhancing service quality, as prior studies indicate a significant number of customers are dissatisfied with airline services, prompting them to seek alternative transportation options.

The research employs a quantitative methodology, specifically a survey method, to collect data from a larger respondent pool, facilitating generalizable results. Ethical approval was secured from Buddha Air, and permissions were granted for data collection. A structured questionnaire, based on prior studies, was piloted with five participants to gauge its effectiveness. The target population consisted of 500 individuals, with a sample of 185 respondents, achieving a response rate of 84.09%. Among these respondents, 105 were female and 80 were male, providing a diverse demographic for the study.
