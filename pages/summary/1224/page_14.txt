This paper explores the impact of maternal international migration (MIM) on left-behind children (LBGs) regarding their education, health, and psychosocial development. Through qualitative interviews, it was found that while some LBGs expressed a lack of support from their immediate family, one interviewee noted positive contributions to her development from her immediate parents. The qualitative findings reveal a predominance of negative experiences among LBGs, with five out of six interviewees indicating insufficient support after their mother's migration.

The quantitative analysis employed a Wholesome Binary Logistic Regression Model (WLRM) to examine the consequences of MIM on LBGs, identifying twelve significant indicators derived from survey data. However, only three indicators were statistically significant: the use of social media and outdoor activities, sound psychosocial feelings, and family support. The model's findings indicate that family support and psychosocial well-being play crucial roles in the overall development of LBGs, as evidenced by their respective significance levels in the analysis.

The results underscore the complex dynamics faced by LBGs in the absence of their mothers, highlighting both the potential for positive influences from immediate family and the prevalent challenges that can hinder their growth. The study emphasizes the necessity for greater awareness and support systems for LBGs to foster their development in the context of maternal migration.
