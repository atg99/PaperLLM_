The paper discusses a study focused on identifying underlying constructs related to student preferences and motives for recommending enrollment at OCEM. The methodology included Principal Component Analysis (PCA) followed by Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) with Varimax rotation to refine the identified components. Key metrics such as Eigenvalues, scree plots, and theoretical interpretability guided the decision on the number of factors, with a factor loading cut-off of 0.40. 

To address research questions (RQs), descriptive statistics and cross-tabulations were used to analyze the relationship between students' preferences and personal variables (RQ1), as well as their motives for recommending OCEM (RQ2). The impact of actual college facility experiences on recommendation preferences, controlling for various demographic factors (RQ3), was assessed using Binary Logistic Regression, alongside Chi-square and Student t-Tests to examine associations between categorical variables.

In the results section, preliminary analyses provided mean, standard deviation, reliabilities, and p-values for twelve subscales related to student satisfaction. These subscales were categorized into three groups based on mean values. Notable findings included a mean of 2.04 for classroom facilities, indicating student disagreement regarding the sufficiency of furniture and classroom quality. Other subscales reflected varying levels of satisfaction, with significant p-values indicating differences in perceptions of faculty support and technological facilities. Overall, the study highlights the importance of these factors in shaping student experiences and recommendations.
