The reviewed journal article in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences focuses on the discrepancies between the expectations of companies and the educational practices of business schools in relation to MBA graduates. It aims to address three key questions: the expectations companies have for MBA graduates, the perspectives of scholars on MBA program organizers, and the best strategies for business schools to align with current global demands.

The article is structured into four main sections: an introduction, a literature review, methodology, and a discussion with conclusions. It highlights that MBA graduates prioritize self-improvement, career development, and the enhancement of business skills, alongside networking opportunities and cultural experiences. Despite significant investment in MBA programs, there is a noted trend of graduates seeking opportunities abroad, indicating a mismatch between the skills taught and market needs.

The literature review reveals that many business schools struggle to meet corporate expectations, leading to dissatisfaction with graduates' competencies. Companies are particularly concerned about the lack of essential soft skills, alongside the need for functional and technical skills. The article argues that while business schools aim to cultivate a well-rounded skill set in future leaders, there is a gap in developing critical leadership dimensions such as communication, interpersonal skills, and the ability to integrate various types of knowledge.

This disconnect has led companies to seek alternatives to traditional MBA graduates, as evidenced by hiring practices at prominent firms that include non-MBA candidates. The article underscores the urgent need for business schools to adapt their curricula to better prepare graduates for the evolving demands of the global business landscape.
