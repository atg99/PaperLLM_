The paper discusses the prevalence and assessment of Internet Addiction (IA) among adolescents, focusing on various studies and a specific research design involving students at OCEM College. Previous research indicated that a notable portion of online problem users exhibit symptoms of addiction, particularly loss of control and conflict. A cross-sectional survey among high school students in Connecticut found a prevalence of approximately 4% of IA, with no significant gender differences. Another study in Guangzhou reported that the majority of adolescents were normal internet users, with only a small percentage classified as moderately or severely addicted.

The current research was structured as a descriptive, analytical, and cross-tab study involving 169 students from grades 11 and 12, aged 15 to 19 years. Inclusion criteria focused on students within this age range who were willing to participate, while those outside this range or absent were excluded. The data collection process involved obtaining permission from authorities, pre-testing, and ensuring informed consent from participants and their parents when necessary. A structured self-administered questionnaire was utilized, which included demographic and internet use factors, along with assessments for IA and associated variables.

The findings from a similar survey indicated a 5.3% prevalence of IA among secondary school students, with a male predominance and a correlation between IA and lower academic performance. The study underscores the importance of understanding IA in adolescents, considering its potential impacts on their health and academic success.
