The study presented in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences explores the adoption of home automation technologies in Nepal. Notably, while only 6.5% of respondents reported a willingness to install these technologies themselves, a significant 64.5% indicated they would prefer to hire professionals for installation. Additionally, 11.3% would seek expert advice, and another 6.5% would opt for custom services from companies.

The survey also highlights key barriers to adoption, with 53.2% of respondents citing cost as the primary deterrent, describing the products as "too expensive." Other concerns included the perceived need for excessive attention (13.7%) and difficulties in management (13.7%). Issues such as lack of complete reliability and flexibility were not viewed as major obstacles by the respondents. The findings reflect the challenges faced in promoting home automation technologies in a developing context like Nepal, where economic factors play a significant role in consumer decision-making.
