The study investigates the differences in male and female students' intentions to recommend enrollment at OCEM. Results indicate that male students scored significantly higher than female students in the subscales of student-centered activities and lifelong academic skills, suggesting a greater inclination among males to recommend OCEM to their kith and kin. Specifically, male students had a mean score of 2.36 (SD = 0.87) compared to females' 2.06 (SD = 0.71) in student-centered activities, and 2.33 (SD = 0.60) versus 2.12 (SD = 0.56) in lifelong academic skills, both with statistically significant differences. 

Other subscales, such as strong faculty management, standard lecturers, better lab and library facilities, college physical facilities, proactive faculty support, better transportation system, college infrastructure, and strong security environment did not show statistically significant differences between genders. 

Additionally, a chi-square test was performed to analyze the relationship between students' locations and their recommendation intentions. Among the 204 sampled students, varying numbers from different locations expressed positive intentions to recommend OCEM, with the highest from campus periphery and Eastern Chitwan. The results highlight gender differences in recommendation intentions and suggest that location may also influence these intentions.
