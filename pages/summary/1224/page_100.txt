The study presented in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences investigates the relationship between employee behavior and customer satisfaction in the context of Buddha Air Service. The analysis employs a Principal Component Model that identifies four principal components (PCs) related to employee motivation, customer-centered strategies, pilot behavior, and service facilities, accounting for variances of 34.88%, 12.85%, 10%, and 9% respectively, with a KMO value of 0.721 indicating adequate sampling.

Mean values from customer feedback on various employee behavior subscales suggest overall dissatisfaction with service quality, employee mistakes, pilot behavior, and service facilities, with average scores below the midpoint on a scale. Specifically, the mean scores for the subscales were 2.41, 2.74, 2.66, and 2.55.

Binary logistic regression analysis shows a significant relationship between employee behavior and customer satisfaction, as indicated by an Omnibus Test result (Chi-Square = 14.844, p = 0.005). The model is a better fit compared to the base model, with Cox and Snell R2 at 7.700% and Nagelkerke R2 at 16.80%. The Hosmer and Lemeshow Test supports this model fit (p = 0.119).

The regression results indicate that service quality and employee behavior, as well as employee mistakes, significantly impact customer satisfaction, with odds ratios of 0.568 and 0.523 respectively. Other factors, such as pilot behavior and employee cooperation, as well as competent employees and service facilities, did not show significant effects on customer satisfaction. Overall, the findings underline the importance of enhancing employee behavior and service quality to improve customer satisfaction in the airline industry.
