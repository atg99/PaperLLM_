The survey discussed in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences suggests that home automation technology has significant potential for future adoption. Key findings indicate that while many respondents recognize the benefits of home automation, concerns about high costs, maintenance difficulties, and false alarm rates hinder purchases. Notably, only 6% of participants expressed a reluctance to buy products made in Nepal, reflecting a positive attitude towards local manufacturing.

Cost emerged as a critical factor influencing 47.6% of respondents, with 53.2% believing that expense would deter them from purchasing automation products. Interestingly, only 13.7% anticipated management challenges with these systems. A substantial 41.9% indicated that transparency regarding investment and costs would encourage adoption, compared to 29.8% who prioritized affordability.

The study recommends strategies to enhance customer relations and promote home automation, including regular maintenance, discounts, warranties, user-friendly interfaces, security measures, and flexible payment options. The expectation is that as awareness of home automation grows, so will its market potential and revenue.
