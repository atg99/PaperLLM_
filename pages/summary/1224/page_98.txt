The paper discusses a study on customer perceptions and experiences regarding the pricing of Buddha Air tickets and their overall satisfaction. A survey was conducted, focusing on price fluctuations, comparisons with other airlines, and online ticketing experiences. The analysis utilized a Principal Component Model, which identified two principal components (PCs): the price of the ticket and the nature of ticket price, explaining 30.37% and 14.85% of the variance, respectively.

The findings indicated that customer satisfaction was low, with mean values of 2.49 and 2.93 for the two subscales, suggesting customers did not perceive Buddha Air's ticket prices as competitive or stable. Binary logistic regression analysis revealed significant results (Chi-Square = 9.295, p = .010), indicating that fluctuations in ticket prices negatively impacted customer satisfaction. The model showed that the perception of ticket prices being similar to competitors was not a strong predictor of satisfaction, with an Exp(B) value of 0.857. Conversely, the fluctuation in ticket prices was a significant factor, with an Exp(B) of 0.474, suggesting that price stability is crucial for customer satisfaction. Overall, the study highlights the need for Buddha Air to address pricing strategies to improve customer perceptions and satisfaction.
