The study conducted in Chitwan District investigates the effects of maternal international migration (MIM) on left-behind girls (LBGs) concerning their education, health, and psychosocial development. Utilizing a mixed-methods approach, the research involved a survey of 237 LBGs and qualitative interviews with six participants. The findings reveal both positive and negative consequences of MIM, with twelve significant indicators identified (p < 0.05). Notably, there is a significant association between MIM consequences and factors such as social media use, outdoor activities, positive psychosocial feelings, and family support.

The study highlights the need for local governments to develop child-friendly policies and protect child rights, especially in the context of the adverse conditions faced by LBGs in Nepal. The linear regression model used in the analysis indicates statistical significance (p < 0.038), confirming that the model effectively predicts the outcome variables. The paper calls for further research focused on the rights and development of children, emphasizing the diversity of conditions affecting LBGs globally.

Future research plans include expanding the sample size to 2,000 respondents and conducting additional qualitative interviews and focus group discussions, targeting various stakeholders such as healthcare professionals and social workers. The study contributes to the ongoing discourse on the implications of MIM for vulnerable populations and underscores the necessity for comprehensive investigations into the well-being of left-behind children.
