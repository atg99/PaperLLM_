The paper discusses the prevalence of Internet Addiction (IA) among students, highlighting a significant increase in internet users globally from 1999 to 2016, with notable disparities across regions. A cross-sectional descriptive study involving 236 science students found that 50.8% exhibited mild addiction, 40.7% moderate addiction, and 1.3% severe addiction, indicating a high prevalence of IA.

The literature review examines multiple studies, including a large-scale investigation in China with 13,723 students, which identified 11.7% prevalence of IA alongside significant physical and psychological symptoms. Another study involving 17,599 students revealed that problematic internet use (PIU) was particularly prominent among males and was linked to various psychosomatic symptoms and lower life satisfaction scores. Additionally, a study in Baguio City showed significant differences in IA based on gender and school type, while a survey of British adolescents highlighted a prevalence of 5.290% among younger males.

Overall, the findings underscore the health risks associated with excessive internet use and the need for further research to understand the underlying factors contributing to IA among adolescents.
