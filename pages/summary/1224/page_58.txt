The paper discusses customer satisfaction as a crucial metric for business performance, emphasizing its subjective nature and its rare use in stakeholder performance measurement. It outlines key factors influencing customer satisfaction in supermarkets, specifically focusing on product price and quality.

Price is highlighted as a significant determinant of satisfaction, with its impact varying across supermarket formats. Research indicates that customers with large shopping baskets prefer stores with everyday low pricing, while those with smaller baskets favor high-low pricing strategies. Additionally, low prices are noted as the second most important characteristic for supermarket shoppers, following store location.

Product quality emerges as another critical factor, with studies showing it to be a primary criterion for customer choice in grocery shopping. Improvements in product quality have a modest positive effect on satisfaction, whereas declines in quality can significantly reduce it. Specialty store customers particularly value product quality, ranking it higher than customers of mass merchandisers or department stores.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of understanding how pricing strategies and product quality contribute to customer satisfaction in the supermarket sector, influencing company profits and strategic decisions.
