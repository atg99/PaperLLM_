The paper discusses the key factors influencing the development of leadership skills in MBA graduates, emphasizing the importance of stakeholder determination, a clear vision from program directors, and commitment to cultivating dynamic leaders. It identifies significant challenges faced by business schools, including issues related to pedagogy, instructional methods, resources, and the relevance of course content to global leadership skills. 

The study highlights criticisms of MBA programs for not adequately teaching essential leadership skills. It suggests future research should address limitations in practical skill development, explore ways to enhance leadership capabilities in a competitive business environment, and focus on the integrative thinking abilities of MBA graduates compared to undergraduates from other fields. Additionally, it calls for a balance between practical and theoretical skills in MBA curricula and stresses the need for ongoing examination and reform of these programs to align with the expectations of companies and the demands of the global market.

The review is based on twenty articles, which may limit the reliability of findings due to potential gaps in contemporary data. It also acknowledges the absence of a specific selection guideline for the articles reviewed, highlighting the need for further research in pedagogical development and policy reform in MBA programs. Overall, the paper underlines the necessity of adapting MBA education to better prepare students for leadership roles in the evolving business landscape.
