This collection of references encompasses a range of topics related to management education, gender dynamics in self-employment, leadership development, and the effectiveness of business schools. 

Marlow and Carter (2004) explore the intersection of professional status and gender disadvantage in self-employment, highlighting the challenges women face in this domain. Mintzberg and Gosling (2002) argue for a more global perspective in management education, suggesting that borders should not limit the learning experiences of future leaders.

Montgomery (2005) discusses contemporary issues in Asian management education, while Petriglieri et al. (2011) emphasize the importance of personalizing management learning to foster leadership development. Pfeffer and Fong (2002) critically assess the success of business schools, questioning their effectiveness in preparing students for real-world challenges.

Podsakoff et al. (2003) provide a comprehensive review of common method biases in behavioral research, offering remedies for more reliable outcomes. Roeser and Peck (2009) focus on self-regulated learning from a contemplative standpoint, advocating for awareness and motivation in education.

Shepherd et al. (2008) examine MBA admission criteria in relation to fostering an entrepreneurial mindset in diverse cultural contexts, while Steiner and Watson (2006) critique the service-learning component in business education, pointing out gaps in values integration.

Torraco (2005) offers guidelines for writing integrative literature reviews, contributing to the academic discourse on research synthesis. Tverdola and Tverdola (2018) discuss leadership development tools in a volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous (VUCA) world, stressing the need for adaptive leadership strategies.

Lastly, Waddock and Lozano (2013) share lessons from holistic management education programs, and Ibeh et al. (2008) assess the focus of top-rated business schools on educating women for global management roles, highlighting the ongoing need for gender inclusivity in management training. 

Overall, these works collectively underscore the evolving landscape of management education and the imperative to address gender disparities, global perspectives, and practical relevance in curricula.
