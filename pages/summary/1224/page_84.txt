The paper discusses the critical role of information and communication technology (ICT) in the strategic decision-making processes within educational organizations. It highlights that the quality, sources, and utilization of information are often overlooked in strategic management, as indicated by Citroen and others. The advent of ICT has transformed how college executives operate, enhancing forecasting accuracy and streamlining decision-making through improved communication. 

The paper points out that the Internet serves as a vital decision-support tool, positively influencing rational decision-making practices among college leaders. It emphasizes the necessity of reliable and accessible information for making informed strategic decisions, particularly in an environment characterized by high costs and competitive pressures. The reliance on both internal and external sources for information is underscored, with executives often consulting staff or commissioning studies when additional expertise is needed.

Finally, the paper concludes that the quality of information is paramount, as sound strategic decisions can only be made based on accurate and comprehensive information. This highlights the ongoing challenge faced by college executives in ensuring the quality of information provided to decision-makers.
