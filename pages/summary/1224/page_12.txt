This research study utilized a mixed-method design, incorporating both hypothesis testing and generating, focusing on left behind girls (LBGs) aged 10-16 in the Chitwan District of Nepal. Data collection involved engaging with key informants and parents, along with a sample of 237 LBGs selected from private and public institutions, including schools and NGOs. Multiple communication methods were employed to reach the participants.

The analysis involved content analysis and descriptive statistics, including the Factors Reduction Method to condense variables, followed by Logistic Regression via Principal Component Analysis to explore relationships between dependent and independent variables. Reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s Alpha. Ethical considerations aligned with the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child were strictly followed, ensuring participant confidentiality and informed consent.

Results indicated that roughly 45% of respondents were under 12 years old, with a response rate of 95%. The analysis identified new Principal Components, with descriptive statistics revealing mean values and standard deviations. Binary Logistic Regression assessed the impacts of migration on LBGs’ education, health, and psychosocial development. Findings suggested that mean values below 3.00 indicated inadequate support from immediate parents in these areas post-migration. Overall, the study highlights significant challenges faced by LBGs, emphasizing the need for improved support systems.
