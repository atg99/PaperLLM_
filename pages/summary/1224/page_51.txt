The paper presents an analysis of the relationship between gender and students' preferences to continue their higher education at the Oxford College of Engineering and Management (OCEM). A Chi-Square test was conducted, revealing that out of 57 male students, 28 intended to continue their education at OCEM while 29 did not. In contrast, among 180 female students, 127 intended to continue and 53 did not. This indicates a significant association between gender and educational preferences at OCEM.

The Chi-Square value was found to be 8.788 with a significance level of 0.004, which is below the threshold of 0.05. This leads to the rejection of the null hypothesis, confirming that gender significantly influences students' preferences regarding further education at private colleges.

Additionally, the paper discusses the application of a Binary Logistic Regression model aimed at identifying significant indicators influencing students' recommendations for their peers to join OCEM. Among twelve measurement scales analyzed, nine were identified as significant predictors of student satisfaction related to these recommendations. The analysis underscores the importance of understanding gender dynamics and other factors in shaping students' educational choices at OCEM.
