The methodology employed involved a comprehensive review of over twenty academic articles from various reputable journals, including the Journal of Higher Education Policy and Management and the Academy of Management Review. The findings revealed significant gaps in the skills taught in MBA programs, particularly in leadership and integrative thinking. Many graduates struggled with applying their knowledge effectively in professional settings, which raises concerns about the relevance of current MBA curricula to real-world demands.

The study highlights that a significant number of MBA graduates are not adequately prepared for the complexities of modern managerial roles. Students often lack both technical and interpersonal skills, contributing to confusion regarding their professional capabilities. Additionally, there is criticism regarding the profit-driven motives of many business schools, which may prioritize financial gain over the educational needs of students.

The implications of this review suggest that MBA program designers must reformulate their curricula to better align with the evolving expectations of global industries. Future research should focus on analyzing more widely cited studies to further investigate the existing knowledge gaps in MBA training and its alignment with industry requirements.

In conclusion, while MBA programs are popular and evolving, there remains a critical need for enhancement to ensure that graduates possess the necessary skills to thrive in the competitive global marketplace.
