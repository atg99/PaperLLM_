The data presented in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences highlights various aspects of internet usage among individuals. Key findings reveal that the majority of respondents use the internet primarily in the evening and night, with significant statistical evidence supporting this trend (p < 0.000).

The analysis indicates that the primary purpose of internet use is for studying, with 120 individuals affirming this purpose (p = 0.024). Online gaming and chatting also show high engagement levels, with 71 and 152 users respectively, both achieving significant p-values (p < 0.000 for gaming and p = 0.001 for chatting). Other activities such as pornography and social networking sites also demonstrate notable usage, with p-values of 0.008 and 0.016 respectively.

Conversely, activities like blogging, downloading movies, reading news, and online shopping show minimal engagement, with p-values indicating no significant interest. Notably, interactions with strangers and the effects on family relationships were significant, with p-values of 0.000 and 0.000 respectively.

The Kruskal Wallis Test results indicate that the experience of using the internet significantly affects behavior, particularly for users with less than 6 months of experience (p = 0.037). Additionally, the average hours of internet use per day show a strong correlation, especially for those using the internet for less than 2 hours, with a p-value of 0.000.

Overall, the findings emphasize the diverse purposes of internet use and its impact on social interactions and relationships.
