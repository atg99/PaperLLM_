The study presented in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences focuses on the impact of maternal out-migration (MIM) on the education, health, and psychosocial development of left-behind groups (LBGs). The model's statistical analysis reveals a -2Log Likelihood value of 232.205, with Cox and Snell R2 at 25.60% and Nagelkerke R2 at 35.20%, indicating a moderate explanation of variance by independent variables. The Hosmer and Lemeshow Test shows a p-value of 0.280, suggesting a good fit for the regression model.

The classification results indicate that among 104 LBGs who reported being affected by their mothers' migration, 29 were predicted not to be affected in terms of education, health, and psychosocial development. Conversely, from 109 LBGs who stated they were not affected, 30 were found to be impacted. The model achieved a prediction accuracy of 71.4% for those claiming to be affected and 73.1% for those claiming not to be affected, with an overall correctness of 72.3%.

Additionally, significant associations were found between social media use, outdoor activities, sound psychosocial feelings, and family support concerning LBGs and MIM, with p-values less than 0.05 and odds ratios indicating positive impacts. Specifically, a unit increase in social media use and outdoor activities predicted a 1.780-fold increase in the impact of MIM, while sound psychosocial feelings and family support predicted increases of 1.972 and 2.459 times, respectively. These findings corroborate previous research by Dhar (2012) and Jensen et al. (2018), reinforcing the positive correlation between these factors and the development outcomes for LBGs.

In the linear regression analysis of categorical variables, the correlation between the ages of LBGs and their recollection of their mothers was assessed, yielding an R value of 0.173 and an R Square of 0.030, suggesting a weak correlation. The results underscore the complexities surrounding the experiences of LBGs in the context of maternal out-migration.
