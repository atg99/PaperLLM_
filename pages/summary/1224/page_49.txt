The study investigates the relationship between students' preferences and gender at OCEM, examining various subscales related to educational facilities and support. The research employs Levene's test to determine whether to use equal or unequal variance t-tests based on the significance of the variances.

Findings indicate that male students' preferences differ significantly from female students in certain areas. For instance, male students showed higher mean scores in faculty support (M = 2.33, p = 0.031) and technological facilities (M = 3.28, p = 0.001), suggesting a greater inclination to recommend OCEM to others, with medium effect sizes observed. Conversely, female students had a higher preference for health facilities (M = 2.64, p = 0.04). Other subscales, such as classroom facilities and emphasis on punctuality, showed no significant differences between genders.

Overall, the results highlight specific areas where male students express stronger preferences, while female students demonstrate higher preferences in health-related aspects, indicating nuanced differences in how genders perceive and recommend OCEM.
