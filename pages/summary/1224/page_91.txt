The study found that school leaders' perceptions regarding the importance of information for rational decision making were accurately predicted. However, the overall results indicated no significant impact of information factors such as value, purity, efficiency, quality, and formats on decision making processes, contradicting previous studies that identified these associations.

The research revealed that private colleges had a statistically higher mean score for the purity of information compared to public colleges, while their mean score for the quality of information was lower. This suggests that private colleges may not prioritize the quality of information in their decision-making processes. Furthermore, the findings indicated that the mean values of all subscales were below the average threshold, underscoring a general lack of perceived impact of information on rational strategic decision making in educational institutions.

The study's limitations include a small sample size and a limited number of survey instruments, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. Despite these limitations, the study provides valuable insights into the relationship between information factors and decision-making processes, suggesting that educational institutions may need to reevaluate their approach to utilizing information in strategic decision making.
