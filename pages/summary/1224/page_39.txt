The referenced works in this paper explore various dimensions of leadership development, education, and management practices. Brungardt reviews research on leadership development, emphasizing the importance of education in shaping effective leaders. Carvan discusses the challenges faced in leadership education amid a volatile and complex environment. Chermack and Passmore highlight the role of journals and databases in enhancing research methodologies within organizations.

Collinson proposes new directions for critical leadership studies, addressing existing dichotomies and dilemmas. Datar, Garvin, and Cullen call for a reevaluation of MBA programs to better prepare future leaders. DeRue and Ashford investigate the social processes that influence leadership identity within organizations. Dragoni et al. focus on the integration of developmental assignments and learning orientation in predicting managerial competencies.

Gapper comments on the detrimental impact of poor management theories on practices, while Hooijberg and Lane advocate for the effective use of multisource feedback in executive education. Ibeh et al. analyze the focus of top business schools on educating women for global management roles. Klimoski and Amos stress the importance of evidence-based education in leadership development.

Lawrence et al. present strategies for developing leadership potential in graduate students through assessment and coaching. Lester et al. conduct a field experiment to assess mentoring's impact on leader efficacy development. Finally, McCall discusses identifying leadership potential in future international executives, and Dubas emphasizes the effective design and assessment of MBA programs through benchmarking. Collectively, these studies contribute to the understanding and advancement of leadership education and development practices.
