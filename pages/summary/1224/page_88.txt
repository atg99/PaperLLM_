The paper presents an analysis of the subscales related to various factors influencing rational strategic decision making (RSDM) in educational institutions. Four main factors—time, content, form, and technology—were evaluated through nine subscales. The data, summarized in Table 2, includes mean values, standard deviations, and Cronbach's Alpha for each subscale, emphasizing the reliability of the measurements.

Findings reveal that respondents generally disagreed with statements regarding the importance of time factors such as currency, relevance, timeliness, and quality of information for RSDM. The purity of information was prioritized most, while the value of information was deemed least important. Similarly, for content factors, respondents showed minimal concern for completeness and relevance, with dissatisfaction noted regarding the quality of information.

In terms of form factors, respondents disagreed with the significance of information detail and organization for RSDM. Among the technology factors, advanced technology adaptation and performance were rated lower than average, indicating a lack of emphasis on these aspects as well.

Overall, the results suggest that educational institutions do not prioritize time, content, and form factors significantly in their strategic decision-making processes, with a clear ranking of priorities established among the various subscales.
