The paper presents an analysis of customer satisfaction regarding the quality of services provided by Buddha Air, based on a sample of 185 customers. The study evaluates three subscales: proper shopping environment and customer management, quality of services, and high-level facilities and security, reporting mean values of 3.41, 3.16, and 3.37 respectively. These values indicate that customers generally agree that the service quality, shopping environment, and security measures are satisfactory.

A binary logistic regression model was employed to assess the relationship between service quality and customer satisfaction. The model demonstrated a significant fit, as indicated by the Omnibus Tests (Chi-Square = 36.273, p = .001) and a good variance explanation (17.80% Cox and Snell R²; 38.80% Nagelkerke R²). The Hosmer and Lemeshow Test confirmed the model's adequacy (p = 0.129). The classification table revealed a high overall accuracy of 90.3% in predicting customer satisfaction.

Notably, the study found a significant negative association between strict flight schedules and security processes and customer satisfaction (p < 0.05, odds ratio = .198). This suggests that as the level of strictness in flight schedules and security increases, customer satisfaction is likely to decrease. This finding aligns with previous research by de Lange et al. (2013), affirming that customers often express dissatisfaction with stringent flight schedules and lengthy security protocols.
