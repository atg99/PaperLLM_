The paper discusses the increasing competition among higher education institutions (HEIs) and their need to adopt market-oriented strategies to attract students. It emphasizes the shift of HEIs towards a business-like service model, focusing on student needs and satisfaction. The primary aim of the study is to analyze the experiences and opinions of students in grades 11 and 12 regarding the academic, managerial, physical, and infrastructure facilities of their institutions, particularly their intention to recommend these institutions to others. Key factors influencing student satisfaction include the college's image, location, quality of facilities and academic programs, and administrative staff.

The secondary objective is to assess students' intentions to continue their education at OCEM, highlighting that student satisfaction is a short-term attitude shaped by their educational experiences. High levels of satisfaction can lead to positive word-of-mouth, which is crucial for institutional reputation and success. The study notes a rising level of student dissatisfaction in Nepalese institutions, posing retention challenges for educational practitioners. It advocates for student-centered marketing strategies to address contemporary student demands and improve satisfaction.

The theoretical model presented links student recommendations to their satisfaction levels, identifying essential indicators such as the quality of administrative staff, college programs, and the external environment. It also outlines various factors affecting student satisfaction, including cultural influences, social class, and personal circumstances. The paper concludes that understanding these dynamics is vital for academic leaders and local governments to enhance educational strategies and meet student needs effectively.
