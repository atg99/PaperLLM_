The study examines the relationship between the ages of left behind girls (LBGs) and their remembrance of their mothers. The findings indicate a weak positive correlation, with an R value of 0.173 and an R² value of 0.030, suggesting that only 3% of the variance in mothers' remembrance can be explained by the ages of the LBGs. The adjusted R² of 0.021 further confirms the minimal explanatory power of the model for predicting mothers' remembrance based on age. The Durbin-Watson statistic of 2.036 indicates no first-order linear auto-correlation in the data.

ANOVA results show statistical significance in the regression model, with a p-value of 0.038, suggesting that the model significantly predicts the level of mothers' remembrance among the LBGs. Coefficient analysis reveals that for LBGs aged 14 to 15, the level of mothers' remembrance decreases slightly, while for those aged 15 to 16, there is a notable increase in remembrance. Specifically, the analysis indicates a decrease of 0.008 for ages 14 to 15 and an increase of 0.153 for ages 15 to 16, with confidence intervals for these estimates provided.
