The paper discusses correlation analysis, which examines the degree of relationship between variables, distinguishing between simple correlation (two variables) and multiple correlation (more than two variables). It emphasizes that correlation does not imply causation. Two primary methods for studying correlation are identified: diagrammatic and mathematical. The diagrammatic method utilizes scatter diagrams, where each data pair is plotted, allowing visual assessment of relationships. A positive correlation occurs when an increase in one variable corresponds with an increase in another, while a negative correlation reflects the opposite trend. The paper references Karl Pearson's method as a widely used mathematical approach for correlation analysis and provides visual examples of different types of correlations, including perfect positive, perfect negative, and no correlation.
