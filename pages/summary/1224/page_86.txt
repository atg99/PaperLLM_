The paper discusses the management of information in the context of rational strategic decision-making (RSDM), focusing on data collection and analysis phases that boards undertake. The study highlights that information is primarily sourced from departments such as Corporate Development and Market Intelligence, with a significant emphasis on internal data collection from various business units. The research identifies a shift in the bottleneck of information management from data searching to data interpretation, indicating that the technical aspects of querying databases have become more accessible.

Using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), the study extracted nine principal components (PCs) from various survey instruments, explaining significant total variances in information management. The first set of PCs emphasizes the values, purity, and efficiency of information, with a total variance of 76.26%. The subsequent surveys yielded additional components related to the importance and quality of information, as well as the formats and perfection of information, along with factors concerning advanced technology in human resources.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of timely and relevant information for effective decision-making, which is crucial for executives in strategic roles. The study utilizes Binary Logistic Regression to explore associations between independent and dependent variables, reinforcing the importance of structured information management in organizational decision-making processes.
