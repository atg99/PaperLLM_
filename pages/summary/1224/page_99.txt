The paper evaluates a model predicting customer satisfaction related to Buddha Air's ticket pricing and service quality. The model demonstrates an improvement over the base model, as indicated by a positive Chi-Square value and a significant reduction in deviance. Key metrics such as -2Log Likelihood, Cox and Snell R2 (4.90%), and Nagelkerke R2 (10.70%) suggest that the model explains a portion of variance based on independent variables. The Hosmer and Lemeshow Test results (p = 0.268) indicate the model fits well.

Out of 185 surveyed customers, the model accurately predicts that 168 customers are satisfied with ticket prices, achieving a 100% accuracy rate for satisfied customers. Overall, the model's correct prediction rate is 90.8%. A significant association is found between ticket price fluctuations and customer satisfaction (p < 0.05), with an odds ratio of 0.474, suggesting that increases in ticket price negatively affect satisfaction.

Additionally, the study explores the impact of employee behavior on customer satisfaction. It examines various aspects such as employee politeness, motivation, entertainment services, and accountability. The analysis utilizes a Varimax rotated principal components matrix, highlighting that polite and motivated employees, satisfactory service quality, and effective use of technology contribute positively to customer satisfaction. The findings align with previous research by Aligholi (2014), reinforcing the negative impact of ticket price fluctuations on customer satisfaction.
