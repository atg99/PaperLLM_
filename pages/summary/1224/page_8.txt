This paper investigates the impact of maternal migration on left-behind girls (LBGs) in Asian countries, focusing on how family dynamics such as sibling relationships, birth order, and gender roles affect their education, health, and psychosocial well-being. 

Existing studies have primarily concentrated on macroeconomic factors, leaving the specific vulnerabilities of LBGs underexplored. The absence of maternal care is linked to increased engagement in antisocial behaviors among LBGs, further exacerbating their developmental challenges. The research highlights that the number of siblings, their ages, and birth order significantly influence the resources available to each child, which in turn affects educational attainment. 

Additionally, societal norms dictate that boys receive more educational opportunities, while girls are often relegated to domestic responsibilities, hindering their educational prospects. The findings suggest that the interplay of familial and societal structures plays a critical role in shaping the outcomes for LBGs, emphasizing the need for more focused research on their unique experiences and challenges.
