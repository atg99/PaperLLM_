The paper discusses the significance of strategic decision-making within educational organizations, emphasizing that it transcends mere computation and involves rational judgment and choices. Strategic decision-making is portrayed as a multifaceted concept that includes planning, positioning, and perspective, all aimed at ensuring organizational sustainability in a competitive landscape.

A rational approach to decision-making is highlighted, where executives are expected to make strategic decisions through a structured process devoid of biases. This involves thorough evaluation of information regarding organizational goals, alternatives, and potential outcomes. However, the reality of decision-making is often complicated by stakeholder interests, negotiation dynamics, and information limitations. Despite these challenges, maintaining an appearance of rationality is crucial for internal trust and external legitimacy.

The role of information in strategic decision-making is addressed, noting that it is often overlooked in management research. While information is essential for understanding the business environment and influences on decision-making, it is frequently regarded as a given rather than a critical factor. The paper underscores the importance of information in generating new knowledge and enhancing organizational processes, further linking it to successful strategic decision-making.
