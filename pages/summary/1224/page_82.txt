This study investigates the impact of information factors on rational decision-making within educational institutions. It highlights the necessity for more data regarding how information technology influences strategic decision-making processes in these settings. Previous research, such as that by Aharoni, Tihanyi, and Connelly (2011), indicates a positive correlation between effective strategic decision-making and the relevant information provided by IT departments. Conversely, Nutt and Wilson (2010) found that poor technological performance negatively impacts strategic decisions.

The paper discusses the micro perspective of strategic decision-making, termed the strategy as practice perspective, which focuses on how college executives think and act. It asserts that strategic decisions are typically grounded in a rational process that involves careful consideration of various factors, including competition and market trends. The use of information is crucial in reducing uncertainty during the decision-making process, yet its role has been underexplored in management research.

The study aims to contribute new insights into the significance of modern information resources as essential prerequisites for structuring strategic decision-making processes. It will analyze actual strategic decisions made by educational executives, emphasizing the role of information in these rational processes rather than the outcomes of the decisions themselves.

In the literature review, the paper defines information as a vital element in organizational activities, emphasizing the need for a comprehensive understanding of how information is created and utilized. It outlines three strategic areas where information plays a critical role in enhancing an organization's capacity for rational decision-making. Nutt and Wilson's (2010) definition of strategic decision-making is reiterated, emphasizing the social aspect of decision-making and the importance of collective and cognitive processes within organizations.
