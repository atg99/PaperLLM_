The paper presents a study on the predictors of customer satisfaction at BBSM using Binary Logistic Regression with a sample of 190 respondents. Key independent variables analyzed include the use of technology and clean environment, better behavior of BBSM employees, customer-centered service, facilities and equipped store technology, varieties of products and quality service, better customer management, and customer facilities at BBSM.

The results indicate that better customer management is a statistically significant predictor of customer satisfaction (p < 0.05, B = 0.438), suggesting that an increase in better customer management can lead to a 1.459 times increase in customer satisfaction when controlling for other variables. The model's overall predictive accuracy is 65.7%, with a high accuracy of 90.2% for predicting satisfied customers, but only 22.2% for dissatisfied ones.

Preliminary tests, including the Omnibus Tests and Hosmer and Lemeshow Test, confirm the model's fit, while the variance explained by the independent variables is 8.30% (Cox and Snell) and 11.60% (Nagelkerke). Notably, no significant relationships were found for several variables, such as customer-centered service and equipped technology, aligning with previous findings by Mithas, Krishnan, and Fornell (2005) that emphasize the positive impact of customer relationship management on customer satisfaction. The study concludes that effective CRM applications enhance customer knowledge and satisfaction, especially when firms share customer-related information with supply chain partners.
