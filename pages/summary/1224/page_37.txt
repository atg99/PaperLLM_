The methodology involved a comprehensive review of ten selected articles from various reputable journals, focusing on the relationship between MBA programs and leadership skills. This theoretical review aimed to synthesize existing knowledge and identify gaps in the literature.

Key findings reveal that many MBA graduates struggle with integrative thinking and often lack the necessary technical and interpersonal skills. The review critiques the current state of MBA programs, suggesting that they prioritize profitability over educational relevance, leading to a disconnect between academic output and career readiness. 

The study identifies seven essential factors for effective leadership development: drives, experiences, awareness, learning ability, leadership traits, capability, and derailment risks. It concludes that business schools must adapt their pedagogical practices to meet the expectations of global companies and better prepare students for real-world challenges. 

Overall, the research underscores the importance of effective teaching methods, self-awareness, and ongoing assessment in cultivating competent leaders capable of thriving in a competitive business landscape.
