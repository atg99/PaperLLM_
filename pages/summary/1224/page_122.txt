The study presented in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences builds upon the findings of Heo et al. (2014), establishing a significant correlation between addictive Internet use and various factors such as age, school grade, and marital status of students. Notably, female students in girls' schools exhibited a higher propensity for Internet addiction compared to their counterparts in coeducational settings. 

The paper discusses the multifaceted impact of Internet use on adolescents, highlighting both its advantages for accessing entertainment, communication, and education, as well as the detrimental effects of addiction on academic performance and familial relationships. The authors emphasize that excessive Internet usage can lead to serious psychosocial issues, including depression, anxiety, and low self-esteem, drawing parallels between Internet addiction and other substance-related addictions.

Despite the absence of formal diagnostic criteria, the study references Young's definition of Internet addiction as an uncontrollable and distressing behavior that disrupts daily life. It categorizes Internet addiction into several types, including cyber-sexual addiction and game addiction. The prevalence of this issue has been noted in both Western and Eastern societies among various age groups, with adolescents being particularly vulnerable due to their developmental stage.

The study suggests that both home and school environments contribute to the risk of Internet addiction, calling for increased awareness and preventive measures from parents and educators. The data presented in Table 2 illustrates associations between Internet addiction and factors such as access to the Internet at home and ownership of gadgets, although the p-values indicate varying levels of significance. Overall, the findings underscore the urgent need for intervention strategies to address the growing concern of Internet addiction among youth.
