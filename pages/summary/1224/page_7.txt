The study conducted by Dr. Basanta Prasad Adhikari investigates the impact of Mother International Migration (MIM) on girls under the age of 16 left behind in Chitwan District, Nepal. Utilizing a mixed methods approach, the research incorporated a survey and qualitative interviews to gather data. A total of 250 questionnaires were distributed, achieving a response rate of over 94.8%, with respondents primarily aged 12 to 16.

Findings reveal a positive correlation between MIM and various negative outcomes, including feelings of loneliness, poor health, negative neighbor attitudes, and an unsupportive home environment. Conversely, a significant negative association was identified between MIM and social attachment, outdoor activities, positive psychosocial feelings, and family support. Approximately 80% of interviewees acknowledged adverse impacts on their education, health, and psychosocial development due to MIM, with around 60% explicitly stating these areas were negatively affected.

The qualitative data reinforced the quantitative findings, providing deeper insights into the consequences of MIM on left-behind girls (LBGs). This study aligns with prior research indicating that the absence of mothers adversely influences the education, health, and psychological welfare of LBGs. The implications highlight the need for policymakers to consider both the positive and negative effects of MIM on this demographic. Overall, the study contributes valuable knowledge regarding the consequences of MIM in the context of Nepalese foreign employment.
