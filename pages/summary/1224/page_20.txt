The paper examines the impacts of parental migration on left-behind children, particularly focusing on girls in various countries including Ethiopia, India, Peru, and Vietnam. It references several studies that explore the complexities of child well-being in the context of parental absence due to migration. Nguyen's research highlights both potential benefits and drawbacks for left-behind girls, suggesting that outcomes can vary significantly based on local circumstances and support systems. 

Additionally, the UNICEF report by Wise and Blair emphasizes the importance of considering child well-being in migration discussions. Wickramage et al. address the mental health and nutritional risks faced by children left behind by international labor migrants, indicating a need for targeted interventions. Yeoh and Lam analyze the broader implications of immigration, while Zhao et al. focus on the long-term psychosocial effects of parental migration in China, identifying factors that either mitigate or exacerbate these impacts. Collectively, these works underscore the need for nuanced understanding and policy considerations regarding the welfare of children affected by parental migration.
