Executives highlighted that trustworthy information is characterized by integrity, robustness, and completeness. Information sourced internally is preferred over external information, which undergoes rigorous scrutiny for credibility before acceptance. 

The research methodology involved surveying a group of college executives to understand how they utilize information to make strategic decisions. A total of 30 surveys were distributed, yielding an 80% response rate with 24 completed questionnaires. The data analysis utilized descriptive statistics, Principal Component Analysis, Student’s t-Test for average differences in decision-making between public and private colleges, and Logistic Regression to examine the relationship between information factors and rational decision-making.

The fieldwork involved 16 executives from various departments across selected colleges in Nawalparasi and Chitwan Districts, focusing on those in strategic management roles. The sample population consisted of 24 executives, predominantly male (79.2%), with a mix of private (58.33%) and public (41.66%) college representatives.

Results indicated a diverse age distribution among respondents, with a significant portion falling into the 40-45 age range. The analysis underscores the critical role of various information factors in facilitating rational decision-making in academic settings.
