The references provided in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences focus on the topic of internet addiction and its associated factors across various populations, particularly adolescents and college students. The studies highlight diverse aspects of internet use patterns, prevalence rates, and the psychological impacts of problematic internet usage. 

Key findings include the association between internet addiction and obesity in children and adolescents, as demonstrated by Bozkurt et al. and Tsitsika et al. Additionally, research by Christakis et al. and Moreno emphasizes the problematic internet usage among college students in the U.S. and its potential psychopathological implications. 

Further, studies from different countries, such as Heo et al. in Korea and Hawi in Lebanon, provide insights into the global prevalence of internet addiction and its correlation with various socio-demographic factors. The references collectively underscore the need for continued research and intervention strategies to address internet addiction among youth, as reflected in the works of Lopez-Fernandez et al. and Wu et al. 

Overall, the literature indicates a growing concern regarding internet addiction and its implications for mental health and academic performance, suggesting the necessity for awareness and preventive measures in educational settings.
