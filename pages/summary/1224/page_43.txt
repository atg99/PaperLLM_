Economic growth increasingly relies on knowledge production, with higher education institutions playing a crucial role in fostering a knowledge-based economy. As quality in higher education becomes paramount, effective degree programs can enhance student satisfaction, knowledge acquisition, and career prospects, ultimately attracting motivated students and securing funding.

To improve student retention, colleges must understand the reasons behind students' decisions to leave programs. Access to higher education is recognized as a human right, necessitating that governments and educational providers ensure both broad access and high-quality educational standards. High-quality education encompasses teaching, research, administrative services, and facilities, with student satisfaction influenced by various factors including academic quality, support systems, and infrastructure.

The primary aim of this study was to investigate students' willingness to recommend private colleges to their peers and their preference for continuing education at these institutions in Nawalpur District, Nepal. Additionally, the study sought to assess student satisfaction related to managerial, support service, administrative, and infrastructure factors impacting their recommendation preferences. The phenomenon of student satisfaction remains a complex and debated issue within the educational sector, particularly given the challenge of high student turnover rates.
