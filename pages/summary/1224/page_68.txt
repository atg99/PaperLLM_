The research employed a quantitative methodology through a survey, with a sample of 204 respondents selected from a population of 440. The gender distribution was 55.88% boys and 41.11% girls, achieving a high response rate of 94.22%. The reliability of the data was verified using Cronbach's Alpha, while an independent sample t-test was conducted to assess differences in satisfaction levels between male and female students.

Previous literature indicates that student satisfaction at secondary schools is influenced by various factors, including the quality of education, school administration, physical environment, and location. The analysis identified key elements contributing to student satisfaction at OCEM, including the provision of lifelong academic skills, qualified lecturers, student-centered activities, effective faculty management, and robust infrastructure such as libraries and laboratories.

The findings reveal a significant relationship between students' likelihood to recommend OCEM for enrollment and factors such as student-centered activities, advanced facilities, and overall college environment, with statistical significance noted (p < 0.05). These insights are valuable for college administrators and policymakers, who can leverage them to enhance student-centered strategies and improve enrollment motivation.

However, the study acknowledges limitations related to sampling methods and the representativeness of the gender distribution, suggesting that caution should be exercised when generalizing the results. 

In conclusion, the research underscores the importance of addressing various factors that contribute to student satisfaction to remain competitive in the higher education landscape.
