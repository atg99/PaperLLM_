The paper discusses factors influencing student satisfaction in higher education, specifically in the context of Nepal. It references Gajic's model, which outlines key components affecting satisfaction: the college program (product), tuition fees (price), internship and job guarantees (placement), college advertising (promotion), faculty and administrative staff (people), program completion processes (process), and college facilities (physical evidence). It emphasizes that enhancing the college program, lowering tuition, strengthening connections with the economic environment, improving college image, academic staff quality, management practices, and facilities are crucial for increasing student satisfaction, as noted by Hanssen and Solvoll.

The research employs a quantitative approach, utilizing a survey method with a sample size of 204 respondents from a target population of 450. The gender distribution included 114 boys (55.88%) and 90 girls (41.11%). The primary research problem focuses on students' intentions to recommend their school to others, with a subproblem exploring their experiences and opinions during grades 11 and 12. The study aims to derive statistically valid insights into student satisfaction and recommendations in the higher education sector.
