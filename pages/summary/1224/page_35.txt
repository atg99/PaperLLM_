The paper discusses key challenges faced by business schools in developing effective leadership programs and addressing gender imbalances in leadership roles. It emphasizes the importance of pedagogy in leadership development, questioning the sufficiency of traditional instructional methods such as case studies, presentations, and group discussions. The authors reference previous studies that suggest creating student ownership through supportive structures, mentoring, self-reflection, and emotional engagement is crucial for effective leadership training.

Additionally, the paper highlights challenges related to faculty training in pedagogy and curriculum design, as many educators lack the necessary skills to teach leadership effectively. Financial constraints have led some schools to adopt cost-saving strategies, raising concerns about their effectiveness in achieving educational goals. The status quo in business education is also critiqued, as it often perpetuates existing systems that hinder innovation and improvement.

Another significant issue addressed is the underrepresentation of female students and leaders in business. The paper notes that while some regions have made strides in gender equality, women still face barriers in attaining leadership positions. Business schools are encouraged to implement initiatives such as scholarships and partnerships with organizations to attract and support female students, thereby addressing the gender imbalance in management roles. Overall, the paper calls for a reevaluation of pedagogical approaches and institutional practices to better prepare future business leaders and promote diversity in leadership.
