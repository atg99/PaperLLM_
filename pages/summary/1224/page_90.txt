The study presented in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences investigates the differences in various subscales related to information quality and its impact on rational strategic decision-making in educational institutions. The analysis reveals that the mean scores for both private and public colleges on several subscales, including the perfectness of information and advanced technology adapted human resources, did not show statistically significant differences, leading to the acceptance of the null hypothesis for these comparisons.

Additionally, a Binary Logistic Regression Model was employed to assess the influence of independent variables, such as the value of information and its purity, on the dependent variable of rational strategic decision-making. The results indicate that none of the nine measurement scales significantly impacted decision-making. The Omnibus Tests suggest that the model fits better than the base model, with significant Chi-Square values. However, despite this model's strength, the independent variables did not yield statistically significant predictors of rational decision-making.

The findings emphasize that while the role of information in decision-making is acknowledged by school leaders, the specific attributes of information assessed in this study did not demonstrate a significant relationship with their decision-making processes. Overall, the study contributes to understanding the dynamics of information quality in educational leadership contexts.
