The study published in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences investigates students' intentions to recommend OCEM to their acquaintances. Out of 210 surveyed students, 174 initially intended to make recommendations, but predictive modeling indicated that only 118 would actually do so. The model accurately predicted recommendations with 91.5% precision, while the accuracy for those not intending to recommend was only 35.2%. Overall, the correctness of the observed data was 71.5%.

The analysis revealed a significant association between students' recommendation intentions and factors such as student-centered activities and college infrastructure, both showing negative impacts on students' motivations. Specifically, strict student-centered activities were linked to dissatisfaction, aligning with Calder's 2013 findings. Additionally, infrastructure issues, including safety and space, were associated with students' reluctance to recommend OCEM, corroborating Weerasinghe and Fernando's 2018 study.

The research utilized a quantitative approach with a high response rate of 94.22%. It concluded that key factors influencing recommendations included academic quality, faculty support, college environment, and infrastructure facilities. Chi-square results demonstrated significant associations between recommendation intentions and various college aspects, particularly emphasizing the importance of student-centered activities and adequate lab and library facilities, which resonates with Mullamaa's 2017 findings.
