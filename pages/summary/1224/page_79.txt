The referenced works encompass a range of studies focused on student satisfaction in higher education, examining factors such as university facilities, service quality, and overall academic life. 

Hanssen and Solvoll (2015) highlight the significance of university facilities in enhancing student satisfaction at a Norwegian institution. Hernadewita et al. (2019) utilize PLS-SEM analysis to explore the relationship between learning services, service quality, and student satisfaction in a polytechnic setting. Hossain and Islam (2012) investigate perceived service quality in the context of Dhaka University Library, while Insch and Sun (2013) assess students' needs and satisfaction with their host city.

Kärnä and Julin (2015) propose a framework for evaluating satisfaction with campus facilities among students and staff. Kreber (2009) discusses the interplay between academics' teacher identities and effective pedagogy. Kwun et al. (2013) examine how campus foodservice attributes influence customer satisfaction and word-of-mouth.

Langstrand et al. (2014) emphasize the importance of quality education in teaching about quality, and Mihanović et al. (2016) explore the correlation between students' satisfaction with faculty and their overall student life and performance. Mukhtar et al. (2015) conduct a comparative analysis of service quality in public and private sector universities.

Mullamaa (2017) emphasizes student-centered teaching approaches, while Nogueira (2018) measures academic life satisfaction among Portuguese students. Prentice et al. (2018) investigate the relationships between education service quality, value, and student behavior. The 2016 study on quality improvement through process management approaches focuses on enhancing university student satisfaction.

Sweeney (2016) presents a predictive model of student satisfaction, and Tucker (2013) discusses the role of student evaluations in improving learning experiences. Lastly, Uprety and Chhetri (2014) examine the influence of college culture on student satisfaction. Collectively, these studies contribute to understanding the multifaceted nature of student satisfaction in higher education contexts.
