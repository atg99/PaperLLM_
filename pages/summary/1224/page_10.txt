The paper discusses the adverse effects experienced by Left Behind Girls (LBGs) globally, particularly those in incomplete family environments. These girls often face neglect, inadequate care, and suffer from poor school performance, health issues, and behavioral problems. Research indicates that LBGs living with younger caretakers are more prone to substance abuse and mental health issues, especially in mother-migrant households. 

Empirical studies highlight that mother migration has more detrimental consequences on LBGs compared to father migration. For instance, school enrollment and overall development are negatively impacted by the absence of mothers, leading to lower educational and health outcomes. LBGs in mother-migrant households exhibit a higher likelihood of engaging in risky behaviors, including unprotected sex and substance abuse, which can lead to increased instances of single motherhood at a young age. 

The findings emphasize the critical role of mothers in the psychosocial development, health, and education of LBGs, suggesting that the lack of maternal presence significantly hampers their overall growth and well-being.
