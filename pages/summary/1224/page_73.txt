The paper discusses various factors influencing student support and academic success within educational institutions, categorized into academic, managerial, physical, and infrastructure factors. 

Academic factors emphasize the importance of standard lecturers, lifelong academic skills, and student-centered activities, with mean values indicating moderate perceptions of their effectiveness. Managerial factors focus on strong faculty management and proactive faculty support, highlighting the significance of faculty engagement in addressing student needs. 

Physical factors assess the adequacy of facilities such as sports, libraries, canteens, and transportation, which contribute to a conducive learning environment. Infrastructure factors evaluate the availability of essential resources, including furniture, clean drinking water, and technologically equipped classrooms.

The study employs Cronbach’s Alpha for reliability analysis of these subscales, confirming the internal consistency of the measures used. The results indicate varying levels of student perception regarding these factors, suggesting areas for potential improvement to enhance the overall educational experience.
