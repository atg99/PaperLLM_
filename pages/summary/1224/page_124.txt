The study published in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences investigates the prevalence and factors associated with internet addiction among adolescents. The findings reveal that 79.9% of respondents were classified as addictive internet users, while only 20.1% were non-addictive. The classification of internet addiction showed 38.5% with mild addiction, 40.8% with moderate addiction, and a mere 0.6% with severe addiction. Notably, adolescents aged 17 years exhibited the highest levels of addiction (34.91%), while those aged 15 years showed the least (1.18%). Males were found to be more addicted (45%) compared to females (34.9%).

The primary purposes for internet use included chatting (89.9%), studying (70.4%), and social networking (74.6%). The study aligns with previous research by Wu et al. (2016), highlighting significant factors influencing internet addiction such as parental control, household income, academic performance, and access to the internet. Furthermore, a negative correlation was identified between internet addiction and social support, along with a positive association with depression.

The discussion emphasizes the concerning prevalence of internet addiction among adolescents, noting its psychological, physical, and social repercussions. It highlights the need for preventive strategies and therapeutic interventions. Key factors influencing internet addiction included sex, educational background, and the purpose of internet use, particularly activities like online gaming and social networking. Additionally, the duration of internet use (over five years) and high daily usage (more than five hours) were significantly linked to higher addiction levels. Monthly internet expenditure also correlated with addiction severity.
