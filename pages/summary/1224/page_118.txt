The study focused on the prevalence of internet addiction among students at Oxford College of Engineering and Management (OCEM) and explored the association between socio-demographic variables and internet usage factors. Utilizing a quantitative approach, the research involved a survey distributed to 169 students, selected through simple random sampling. The findings revealed that 79.9% of participants were classified as internet addicts, with 38.5% exhibiting mild addiction, 40.8% moderate addiction, and a mere 0.6% suffering from severe addiction. The data indicated a strong correlation between internet addiction and various demographic factors.

The implications of this study are significant for parents, educational leaders, and policymakers. It highlights the necessity for awareness programs that address the negative impacts of excessive internet use, emphasizing the importance of developing strategies to mitigate internet addiction among students. The study serves as a reminder of the vital role that the internet plays in contemporary life while also cautioning against its potential risks to physical and mental health.
