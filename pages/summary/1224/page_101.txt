The study published in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences evaluates customer satisfaction concerning employee behavior at Buddha Air. Analysis of 176 customers revealed a high satisfaction rate with employee behavior at 97.60 percent accuracy, while dissatisfaction was noted at 0 percent accuracy among dissatisfied customers. The overall prediction accuracy for customer satisfaction was 88.60 percent.

Statistical results indicated a significant negative association between service quality, employee behavior, and customer satisfaction (p < 0.05). Specifically, an increase in service quality and employee behavior correlates with a predicted decrease in customer satisfaction by approximately 0.568 times. Similarly, the study found that employee mistakes and entertainment facilities negatively impacted customer satisfaction, predicting a decrease of about 0.649 times with increased positive employee behavior and customer-centered strategies.

These findings align with previous research by Kattara, Weheba & El-Said (2008), which identified a positive correlation between service quality, employee behavior, and customer satisfaction. The previous study emphasized that quality service and polite employee behavior significantly enhance customer satisfaction, irrespective of demographic factors.

Additionally, the research focused on Customer Relationship Management (CRM), assessing respondents' perceptions regarding ticket availability, check-in processes, and overall customer management at Buddha Air. CRM is highlighted as a vital business strategy that integrates technologies and processes to meet customer needs. The study outlines an eight-stage CRM life-cycle, emphasizing its importance in enhancing customer satisfaction and loyalty. 

Overall, the research underscores the critical role of employee behavior and service quality in determining customer satisfaction in the airline industry.
