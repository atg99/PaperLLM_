The study presented in the OCEM Journal of Management, Technology & Social Sciences analyzes various aspects of student satisfaction at OCEM. The results indicate that students expressed dissatisfaction across several subscales related to faculty management, teaching standards, student-centered activities, and academic skills. Specifically, "strong faculty management" received a mean score of 2.06, indicating disagreement with faculty's capabilities in managing course schedules and providing support. The "standard lecturers" subscale scored 2.22, showing that students were uncertain about the use of modern technology and cooperative teaching methods.

Further, the "student-centered activities" subscale had a mean of 2.23, reflecting students' concerns about the principal's support and decision-making. The "lifelong academic skills" subscale scored 2.24, indicating dissatisfaction with teaching effectiveness and modern pedagogy. Students were similarly critical of library and laboratory facilities, scoring 2.28, and college environment and physical facilities, with scores of 2.34 and 2.44, respectively.

In contrast, the "best transportation system" subscale scored 2.76, suggesting general agreement on transport punctuality and comfort. The "strong security environment" subscale received a mean of 2.93, indicating satisfaction with security measures. Finally, the "weak college infrastructure facilities" scored 2.92, suggesting that students felt the college buildings were safe and adequately equipped. Overall, the mean values ranged from 2.06 to 2.92, highlighting significant areas for improvement in student satisfaction at OCEM.
