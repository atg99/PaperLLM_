The paper presents descriptive statistics for a study involving 237 respondents, focusing on various subscales related to parental support and psychosocial factors affecting the lives of LBGs (likely referring to a specific demographic group). Mean values, standard deviations (SD), and Cronbach's Alpha coefficients were calculated for each subscale to assess reliability.

Key findings include:

- The subscales related to unmet needs of parental affection, health stress, unsupportive parental roles, poor neighbor behavior, and lack of family support had mean values around 3.00 to 3.10, indicating respondents were undecided about these issues.
- Conversely, respondents generally agreed with statements regarding communication activities, poor health conditions, social isolation, house environment, and adverse psychosocial thinking, with mean values above 3.10. This suggests significant negative impacts on education, health, and psychosocial development due to inadequate support from immediate parents.
- The Cronbach’s Alpha values indicate varying levels of reliability across subscales, with some achieving higher reliability (e.g., adverse psychosocial thinking at .87).

Qualitative insights from interviews highlight the emotional distress experienced by LBGs due to the absence of parental support, particularly from mothers, emphasizing feelings of frustration and longing for understanding and care in educational and health matters. 

Overall, the study underscores the critical need for parental involvement and support in the psychosocial development of LBGs, with implications for improving educational and health outcomes.
