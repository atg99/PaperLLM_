EfficientNet builds upon these advancements by employing a scalable compound scaling method that uniformly scales the depth, width, and resolution of the network. This approach leads to a family of models that can be adapted to various resource constraints without sacrificing accuracy. The EfficientNet-B0 model serves as the foundation for this series, incorporating MobileNetV2's inverted bottleneck and Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) modules to improve feature representation.

In this paper, the authors conduct an empirical analysis of the SE modules' impact on the performance of EfficientNet-B0. By systematically repositioning and removing these modules, the study seeks to understand their contribution to the overall efficacy of the model. The experiments are carried out using the ImageNet dataset to evaluate the variations of EfficientNet-B0 and quantify the performance changes resulting from modifications to the SE modules.

The results are expected to provide insights into the balance between model efficiency and accuracy, contributing to the ongoing discourse on optimizing CNN architectures for practical applications, especially in environments with limited computational resources. The findings may also have implications for future research in the design of lightweight neural networks that retain high performance.
