The paper discusses the architecture and efficiency of EfficientNets, a series of convolutional neural networks optimized for image classification tasks. EfficientNets utilize a structure that includes 1x1 expansion convolutions, depthwise convolutions, and a 1x1 projection layer, with residual connections maintained only when the input and output share the same number of channels and spatial sizes. This design allows for a compact representation while expanding feature space internally, enhancing the expressiveness of nonlinear transformations.

EfficientNets are notable for their balance between accuracy and efficiency, achieved through neural architecture search and the implementation of mobile inverted bottleneck convolutions (MBConv). The family of models, starting with EfficientNet-B0, demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on the ImageNet challenge while minimizing model size and floating-point operations.

The paper also introduces the Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Block, which enhances channel interdependencies in CNNs with minimal computational cost. This module uses a two-layer neural network to generate weights for each channel, allowing the model to adaptively focus on the most relevant features. Incorporating SE modules into architectures like ResNet-50 has shown to yield similar accuracy to ResNet-101 while significantly reducing computational costs.

EfficientNet-B0 is described in detail, highlighting its use of MBConv modules, batch normalization, ReLU activation, and the final classification layer. Overall, the paper emphasizes the innovative aspects of EfficientNets and SE Blocks in improving the efficiency and accuracy of deep learning models for image classification.
