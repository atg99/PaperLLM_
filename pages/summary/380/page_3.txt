The basic structure of the mobile inverted bottleneck convolution is detailed, highlighting its similarity to depthwise separable convolution. The architecture includes a 1x1 convolution for channel expansion, followed by a depthwise convolution, and concludes with another 1x1 convolution to restore the original channel dimensions. The SE module can be positioned after the depthwise convolution or the final 1x1 convolution, with the paper exploring both configurations.

Experiments conducted on the ILSVRC 2012 classification dataset reveal that repositioning or removing SE modules in no-skip blocks results in a minimal accuracy drop of 0.1% with a 3% reduction in parameters. In contrast, similar modifications in skip blocks lead to a 0.7% accuracy decrease and an 8% parameter reduction. When all blocks are altered, the accuracy declines significantly by 1% while parameters decrease by 1.4%. The findings suggest that the SE module's impact diminishes when placed at the end of the architecture, indicating that its effectiveness is contingent upon its position within the block. 

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of architectural choices in deep learning models, particularly regarding the integration of SE modules within mobile inverted bottleneck convolutions.
