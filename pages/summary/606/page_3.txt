The paper discusses the effectiveness of fine-tuning convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in transfer learning, specifically addressing three key questions: the impact of learning reduction in transferred layers, the optimal type of dataset for source tasks, and the performance comparison between ensembles of fine-tuned CNNs and randomly initialized CNNs. The authors demonstrate that a tuned network significantly outperforms a randomly initialized counterpart and counter the claim by Girshick et al. regarding the benefits of halting learning in transferred layers, showing that this can diminish performance.

The study further explores the effects of dataset dissimilarity by categorizing data into man-made and natural subsets. While this approach yielded less improvement, the authors argue that visual dissimilarity is not accurately captured by this classification. They illustrate this with examples of classes that may be visually similar despite differing categories.

To investigate the effects of fine-tuning on CNN ensembles, the authors outline an experimental framework using a dataset that integrates seven fine-grained datasets, each with major and minor classification labels. This aggregation allows for various configurations, including generalist and high-fan networks, which vary in their classification granularity. The paper concludes with a visual representation of the fine-tuning process, demonstrating how a network can be adapted from a source task to a target task by reinitializing certain layers.
