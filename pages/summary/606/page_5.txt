This paper investigates the impact of transfer learning and fine-tuning on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), specifically utilizing a standardized approach to ensure comparability across datasets. A dataset split of 75% for training and 25% for testing is maintained, with a formula used to measure error reduction rates. The study employs a consistent set of hyperparameters across all experiments to isolate the effects of fine-tuning, despite this potentially leading to suboptimal performance for specific datasets.

The foundational architecture used is based on AlexNet, chosen for its recognition and efficiency in training times. Various hyperparameters are defined, including a minibatch size of 256 and a learning rate starting at 0.001, which is halved at specified training intervals. All networks are trained for 200,000 iterations to ensure thoroughness, with model accuracy reported based on validation set performance.

The first experiment focuses on optimizing the learning rate during fine-tuning, testing the theory proposed by Girshick et al. regarding the benefits of reducing the learning rate. By applying a learning rate multiplier to transferred layers, the study finds that completely freezing the initialization results in lower accuracy compared to using a global learning rate. The global learning rate yields the best performance, contradicting Girshick's claim in contexts involving straightforward classification tasks. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of learning rate strategies in enhancing the effectiveness of transfer learning in CNNs.
