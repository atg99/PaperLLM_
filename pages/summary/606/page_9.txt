The study investigates the performance of ensemble models versus individually fine-tuned convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It was found that while ensembles produced fewer errors than individual models in general, the best fine-tuned network surpassed the entire ensemble of scratch networks, which is notable given that deploying an ensemble requires significantly more computational resources. The results showed consistency across major classes, except for the dog subset where the ensemble of scratch networks outperformed the best fine-tuned network, although the ensemble of fine-tuned networks still exceeded the scratch ensemble.

The analysis revealed that high-fan networks, when excluded from the ensemble, led to a decrease in overall accuracy, reinforcing the idea that CNN ensembles yield better accuracy than their individual components. Additionally, the paper challenges the notion proposed by Girshick that reducing learning in fine-tuning is beneficial, indicating that full training on the target dataset is preferable. It was also observed that pretraining on datasets with a diverse range of finely grained images is more advantageous than on smaller, more general datasets. Data similarity between source and target datasets emerged as a crucial factor influencing the benefits of pretraining.

In conclusion, the findings support the effectiveness of fine-tuning and highlight the importance of dataset characteristics in the training process. Future work may focus on optimizing ensemble configurations and exploring additional dataset properties to further enhance model performance.
