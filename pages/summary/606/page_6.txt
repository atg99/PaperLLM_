The paper discusses the optimization of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) through the minimization of the cross-entropy loss function, emphasizing the challenges posed by its nonconvex nature, which can lead to local minima. It suggests that a better initialization can help discover more favorable minima, advocating for a global learning rate for straightforward labeling tasks, especially after weight transfer.

The authors explore the impact of the source dataset used for pretraining on fine-tuning tasks. They conduct experiments using their dataset to assess how different types of source tasks and their visual similarities affect classification accuracy. Five specialist networks, initialized under various conditions, were trained to evaluate performance against a random initialization baseline. The results revealed that all networks improved over random initialization, with varying degrees of benefit depending on the source task.

Particularly, the flower dataset showed significant improvement compared to the bird dataset, despite similar training data sizes. Distant source tasks generally performed worse than visually similar ones, though the high-fan-without initialization excelled for the vegetable network. Notably, the dog network's performance was subpar, with some initializations yielding minimal advantages over random initialization. The sign network demonstrated high performance from random initialization, indicating that it may not be suitable for transfer learning due to its already solved status.

The overall findings suggested that the high-fan initialization was often the most effective, as reflected in the normalized results across different subsets of data. The paper concludes by highlighting the importance of choosing appropriate datasets for pretraining to enhance fine-grained image classification performance.
