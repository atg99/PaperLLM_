The introduction discusses the evolution of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in the context of image classification competitions, particularly highlighting the advancements made since AlexNet. It notes that subsequent architectures, such as VGG and GoogLeNet, have utilized deeper networks with more layers and learnable weights. Despite these advancements, the need for extensive labeled training data remains a challenge due to the complexity of CNN training, which typically employs stochastic gradient descent (SGD). This method, while effective, can be sensitive to initialization and often requires large datasets to reach optimal performance.

To address the issue of limited training data, the paper introduces parameter fine-tuning, a transfer learning technique where a network pre-trained on a different dataset is retrained on a target dataset. This approach has been shown to enhance performance significantly compared to random initialization. However, the authors note a lack of comprehensive research in this area and aim to fill these gaps through their study.

The paper outlines three main areas of investigation: determining the optimal learning rate for transferred layers, analyzing the impact of different source datasets on various target datasets, and comparing ensembles of fine-tuned networks with those initialized randomly. An experimental framework is proposed to systematically explore these variables across multiple datasets, including a unique dataset aggregated from various sources.

The organization of the paper is detailed, with sections dedicated to related work in transfer learning, the experimental framework, and specific investigations into learning rates, source datasets, and ensemble methods, concluding with discussions on findings and future work.
