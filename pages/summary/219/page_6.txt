The paper investigates a proposed projection method aimed at enhancing the gradient norm preservation in convolutional layers of neural networks. The study focuses on a small 3-layer network consisting of convolutional layers followed by ReLU non-linearity. An experiment was conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of the projection by analyzing the gradient norm ratio for varying input and output channels during training on the CIFAR-10 dataset. The results indicate that the proposed projection improves the norm preservation, moving the gradient norm ratios closer to 1, particularly in practical scenarios with larger channel sizes. However, it performs poorly in cases with very small channels due to the violation of assumptions regarding energy distribution in the signal.

Subsequently, the paper presents experiments using Residual Network (ResNet) and the modified Procrustes Residual Network (ProcResNet) on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets. The networks were trained using standard techniques and data augmentation, with results measured by top-1 classification accuracy. Three network architectures were evaluated: ResNet, ProcResNet, and a plain network without skip connections. The proposed regularization method was shown to increase training time marginally while significantly reducing computational overhead compared to traditional regularization methods like SVD.

The initial experiments focused on norm preservation across different network depths, with a particular emphasis on the gradient norm ratios, reinforcing the benefits of the proposed projection in maintaining gradient flow during training. Overall, the findings validate both the theoretical investigation and the practical effectiveness of the proposed modifications.
