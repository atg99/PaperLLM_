The paper investigates the training behavior of various deep neural network architectures, specifically focusing on gradient norm ratios in transition and non-transition blocks during training on the CIFAR10 dataset. It presents findings from an experiment conducted over 100 epochs without learning rate decay, highlighting the differences between Plain networks, ResNet, and ProcResNet architectures.

Key observations include:

1. **Norm-Preservation**: The experiment reveals that while Plain networks initially maintain norm-preservation, they become unstable as training progresses. The gradient norm ratios diverge from 1 due to the lack of skip connections, leading to numerical instability and convergence issues.

2. **Impact of Depth**: As network depth increases, Plain networks struggle with norm-preservation, resulting in optimization difficulties and degraded performance. Conversely, ResNet and ProcResNet architectures, utilizing identity skip connections, demonstrate enhanced norm-preservation even as they grow deeper.

3. **Transition Blocks**: Notably, the analysis of specific transition blocks indicates that even a single problematic block can hinder optimization. For instance, in the comparison between Plain83 and Plain164, the first transition block in Plain83 initially exhibits a high gradient norm ratio but stabilizes, whereas the same block in Plain164 leads to excessive divergence, preventing convergence.

Overall, the study emphasizes the necessity of architectural design, particularly the inclusion of skip connections, to ensure stability and effective training in deeper networks.
