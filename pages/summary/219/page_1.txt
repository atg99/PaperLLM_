This paper explores the role of skip connections in Residual Networks (ResNets) and their impact on network depth and optimization stability. The authors present theoretical results demonstrating that identity skip connections preserve the norm of the gradient during back-propagation, which enhances stability in the optimization process. They argue that as more residual blocks are added, the norm-preservation effect increases, countering the hypothesis that ResNets only mitigate vanishing gradients by shortening the effective gradient path.

The paper also proposes a method to further enhance norm-preservation by regularizing the singular values of the convolution operator, leading to improved learning dynamics and classification performance in ResNets, termed Procrustes ResNets. Empirical evidence supports these theoretical claims, highlighting the significance of identity mappings in maintaining optimization stability while allowing networks to be deeper without performance degradation.

In summary, the findings underscore the importance of skip connections in deep networks, providing insights into training deeper architectures effectively. The proposed modifications could guide future designs for even deeper neural networks.
