The paper discusses modifications to the ResNet architecture, specifically focusing on the transition blocks that change dimensions within the network. The authors highlight the importance of these transition blocks in preserving the norm of back-propagated gradients. They note that the original ResNet transition blocks, which utilize 1x1 convolution with a stride of 2, do not preserve the norm, which could impact the performance of the network.

To address this issue, the authors propose a new type of transition block that employs a norm-preserving convolution layer, denoted as conv*. This layer modifies the convolution kernel to ensure norm preservation by regularizing its singular values, thus maintaining the gradient's norm across layers. The paper outlines the mathematical formulation for the gradients associated with the convolution layer and illustrates how to calculate the expected value of the norm of the gradients.

The central argument is that by ensuring norm preservation throughout the network, particularly in transition blocks, the overall effectiveness of residual networks can be enhanced. The authors provide a framework for setting the singular values to achieve this norm preservation, thereby improving the gradients' consistency during back-propagation. The proposed modifications aim to leverage the benefits of norm preservation, which may contribute to better training outcomes for deep learning models.
