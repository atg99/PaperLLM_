The paper presents a theoretical analysis of residual networks, focusing on the role of identity skip connections in making residual blocks norm-preserving. It highlights how these connections contribute to the stability of the optimization process and enhance the overall performance of the network. The experiments conducted on the CIFAR10 dataset demonstrate that various architectures, including different configurations of convolution layers and proposed regularization techniques, positively impact performance by improving norm preservation in transition blocks. The results indicate that both the architecture changes and regularization strategies enhance the stability and efficiency of the training process. The findings underscore the significance of norm preservation in neural network performance and provide insights into effective design choices for improving model efficiency.
