The paper discusses the properties of residual networks, particularly focusing on their ability to maintain norm preservation during training, which is crucial for avoiding issues such as vanishing or exploding gradients in deep architectures. The central theorem establishes that a mapping can be represented by a sequence of non-linear residual blocks where the gradient norm remains relatively stable through backpropagation, leading to increasingly norm-preserving solutions as the number of layers increases.

The corollary presented indicates that the norm preservation of these residual blocks relies on the norm of the weights. By constraining the optimizer to functions with small norms, the network can ensure norm preservation throughout training. This means that any critical point reached during optimization is norm-preserving, and under certain conditions, these critical points are global minimizers, achieving the same loss value as the global minimum.

The paper also explores linear residual blocks, demonstrating that they maintain norm preservation even more rapidly than non-linear blocks. It concludes that as the depth of the network increases, both types of blocks enhance their norm-preserving properties, facilitating better conditioning and faster convergence.

Additionally, the paper outlines the structure of residual networks, which include convolution layers, fully connected layers, and transition blocks, suggesting a versatile architecture that adapts to different dimensions throughout the processing stages.
