The paper discusses the preservation of norms in convolutional layers during training processes, particularly in deep neural networks like ResNets. It begins by analyzing the expected energy ratio of the output signal and how it relates to the singular values associated with the convolution operator. The authors assume that a significant portion of the energy lies in a specific subspace defined by nonzero singular values, allowing for a simplified approach to ensure norm preservation without singular value decomposition.

The central theorem referenced indicates that singular values of the convolution operator can be derived from the Fourier transform of the convolution kernel slices. This enables the authors to set nonzero singular values to a specific value that ensures norm preservation, using an optimization problem that seeks to minimize the difference between the original and adjusted matrices.

An iterative algorithm is proposed to compute the necessary adjustments efficiently, leveraging matrix multiplications suitable for GPU implementation. The algorithm maintains norm preservation during the gradient descent step of training, with minimal computational overhead, especially as network depth increases.

Additionally, the paper addresses the impact of ReLU nonlinearity, indicating that adjustments to singular values are necessary to account for the expected energy loss due to the ReLU operation. This ensures that the backward signals' norms are also preserved.

Overall, the proposed method enhances the training of deep networks by maintaining norm preservation, which is crucial for effective learning and convergence. The experiments conducted show that the implementation of this approach incurs a minimal increase in training time while significantly benefiting network performance.
