This paper provides a comprehensive overview of the privacy vulnerabilities associated with federated learning (FL) and explores various defensive strategies to counter these threats. With the growing emphasis on data privacy and regulations, FL has emerged as a collaborative machine learning approach that enables model training without sharing raw data. Instead, clients train models locally and send updates to a central server, which aggregates them into a unified model. While FL offers privacy benefits by reducing data exposure to external adversaries, it remains susceptible to attacks from internal adversaries, such as malicious servers.

The authors analyze existing vulnerabilities within FL, detailing specific attack methods that can compromise its privacy protections. They categorize these attacks using a basic taxonomy to facilitate understanding and assessment of the risks involved. The paper also reviews recent defensive strategies aimed at enhancing FL's security, organizing these strategies based on their foundational principles.

Ultimately, the authors conclude that relying on a single defensive measure is insufficient to safeguard against all potential attacks, indicating a need for a multifaceted approach to ensure robust privacy protection in federated learning applications. This analysis serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners considering the use of FL for handling sensitive information.
