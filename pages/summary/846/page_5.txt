The paper discusses various vulnerabilities in federated learning (FL) systems, categorized by the type of attacks they facilitate. It outlines server and client-side vulnerabilities, highlighting methods such as manipulation of client-specific models, access to aggregate models, and influence over gradient updates. The attacks are classified into misclassification and model corruption categories.

Misclassification attacks primarily involve adversarial examples that are crafted to mislead the model during runtime. This is particularly concerning in applications like on-device authentication, where adversaries can exploit the model's deployment to generate misleading inputs.

Model corruption attacks, specifically model poisoning, allow adversaries to directly influence the shared model's weights. Techniques such as inserting backdoors or manipulating model outcomes are discussed. Bagdasaryan et al.'s work exemplifies how a malicious client can declare an inflated number of samples to alter the shared model. Additional strategies include sybil-based attacks, where an adversary can join and leave the client pool to submit multiple poisoned models, and joint-effort model poisoning, which can corrupt models through coordinated user actions.

The paper also touches on data poisoning attacks, noting their inferiority to model poisoning in the context of FL due to the nature of data handling. 

In terms of defensive measures, the paper emphasizes the need for a combination of strategies tailored to counter specific attack types. A table is provided summarizing various defensive techniques and their effectiveness against the identified attacks. The effectiveness of these measures is context-dependent, indicating that careful implementation is critical for achieving robust protection against adversarial threats.
