The paper discusses the vulnerabilities of federated learning (FL) to insider attacks, particularly highlighting the issue of Byzantine clients who can disrupt the integrity of the model updates sent to a centralized server. Various aggregation strategies have been proposed to mitigate these risks. Blanchard et al. introduced Krum, which aims to minimize the influence of outlier updates from malicious clients, providing security against up to n/4 Byzantine workers. El Mhamdi et al. advanced this with the Bulyan aggregation rule, securing against n/3 Byzantine clients. Li et al. proposed a reliability-based weighting strategy to enhance the robustness of updates from heterogeneous data sources. Other notable contributions include Pilluta et al.'s approximate geometric median for federated learning and Hsu et al.'s server-side momentum approach to manage non-IID client datasets.

Additionally, Chang et al. presented the Cronus algorithm, which emphasizes privacy by aggregating predicted labels instead of model updates, thus demonstrating robustness against model poisoning attacks, albeit at a potential accuracy cost.

The related work section reviews existing literature on attack methods and defense strategies in FL, noting a lack of comprehensive studies focused on these aspects. It categorizes various defensive strategies and highlights open problems in FL, particularly concerning privacy vulnerabilities.

The conclusion emphasizes that while FL offers improved data privacy, clients cannot fully trust the centralized server to protect their data from insider threats. The paper provides a matrix evaluating the effectiveness of different defensive methods against specified attack strategies, contributing to the understanding of security in federated learning environments.
