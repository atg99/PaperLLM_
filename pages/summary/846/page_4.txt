In summary, the paper discusses various attack methods in federated learning, categorized by their characteristics such as the attacker's role (active or passive), the model's accessibility (white-box or black-box), and their specific goals. Key methods include:

1. **Loss-function/ReLu Server**: A passive white-box attack focused on sample reconstruction using linear networks with ReLu activation.
2. **DLG/iDLG**: Algorithms that iteratively optimize inputs to reproduce training samples, effective only with small local datasets due to high computational costs.
3. **Model Inversion Attacks (MIA)**: These attacks exploit the output of linear models to infer private input features, relying on brute-force techniques that are computationally infeasible for larger input spaces.
4. **mGAN-AI**: This attack utilizes GANs to reconstruct training samples with the help of model updates from clients and auxiliary datasets, showing effectiveness in compromising client privacy.
5. **GAN**: An active attack method that manipulates the shared model to mislabel generated images, targeting victim clients and potentially revealing private data.

Overall, the paper highlights the vulnerabilities in federated learning systems and the specific conditions under which these attacks can be effectively executed.
