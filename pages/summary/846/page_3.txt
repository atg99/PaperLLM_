3) Membership inference attack: This attack uses the model's output to determine whether a specific sample was part of the training dataset. By analyzing the confidence scores of the model, an adversary can infer membership based on whether the model is more confident about certain samples. This method can be conducted with both black-box and white-box access to the model, making it versatile in its application.

4) Backdoor attacks: These attacks involve manipulating the training process to implant a backdoor in the model. An attacker can introduce specific patterns in the training data that, when encountered during inference, trigger the model to produce desired outputs. This type of attack is particularly concerning as it can compromise the integrity of the model without directly revealing private information.

5) Adversarial examples: These attacks create inputs that are intentionally designed to cause the model to misclassify. By slightly perturbing the input data in a way that is imperceptible to humans, the model can be tricked into making incorrect predictions. This method is applicable across various machine learning architectures and poses a significant challenge for ensuring model robustness.

In summary, the paper categorizes different types of federated learning attacks based on the attacker's goals, including sample reconstruction, information inference, model corruption, and runtime misclassification. Each attack type is further explored with specific methods and their implications for privacy and security within federated learning systems. Understanding these attacks is crucial for developing effective defenses and maintaining the integrity of federated learning.
