This paper discusses various defensive measures against vulnerabilities in federated learning systems, as illustrated in Table III and Figure 1. The effectiveness of these defenses is assessed based on their ability to mitigate specific attack vectors targeting both server and client models.

1. **Gradient Subset**: This approach limits the communication to a subset of gradients, enhancing communication efficiency while reducing the information available to the server. Although it can hinder some attacks, it does not completely prevent adversaries from extracting information from client updates.

2. **Gradient Compression**: Originally intended to improve communication efficiency, lossy compression techniques can also enhance information security. One method involves using an autoencoder to encrypt gradient updates, thereby increasing efficiency and limiting the server's knowledge of the updates. However, the effectiveness of this method against malicious reconstruction remains uncertain.

3. **Dropout**: Typically used to prevent overfitting, dropout introduces randomness to gradient updates, which can reduce the attack surface. While it offers some defense, it may hinder the ability to infer generalized data.

4. **Differential Privacy (DP)**: A robust privacy standard, DP introduces noise to ensure privacy. It has been effectively applied in various machine learning contexts, although its implementation in federated learning is challenging due to varying dataset sizes among clients. Recent advancements focus on applying DP during gradient updates to protect client information, but potential vulnerabilities to server-side attacks persist.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate defensive strategies to safeguard against specific vulnerabilities in federated learning environments, while acknowledging the limitations of each method.
