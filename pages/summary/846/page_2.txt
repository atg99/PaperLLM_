The paper discusses the concept of adversarial attacks within the context of Federated Learning (FL). It outlines the process of FL, where a model is sent from a server to client devices, trained locally, and then aggregated back at the server. The paper identifies two main adversarial goals: extracting private information from clients and manipulating the model's behavior, which can include introducing backdoors or causing misclassifications.

The threat model considers adversaries as insiders, either clients or the server, operating under the assumption that communication is secured with cryptographic measures. It emphasizes the importance of the honest-but-curious server model, which does not access private training data.

The attack surface in FL is divided into vulnerabilities associated with malicious clients and servers. Malicious servers can manipulate the model, control client participation, and differentiate model updates, while malicious clients can access the aggregate model, manipulate training data and process, and influence the model aggregation.

The paper categorizes attacks into client-side and server-side, with the exception of man-in-the-middle attacks. It distinguishes between black-box attacks, where adversaries have limited knowledge of the system, and white-box attacks, where they have complete knowledge except for private data. It notes that clients are vulnerable to white-box attacks in federated averaging, while server-side attacks may vary.

Additionally, attacks are classified as active or passive. Active attacks involve manipulating system parameters and are generally detectable, while passive attacks operate without such manipulation, making them harder to detect and therefore more dangerous. The paper concludes by discussing various attack characteristics and the inherent vulnerabilities in FL systems.
