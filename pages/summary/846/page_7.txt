One approach to robust aggregation is to utilize techniques that identify and filter out outlier model updates that deviate significantly from the expected distribution. This can involve using statistical methods or machine learning algorithms that evaluate the consistency of updates based on the collective behavior of clients. By focusing on the majority of updates and disregarding those that appear suspicious, the server can maintain a more accurate global model.

In addition to this, incorporating cryptographic measures, such as secure multiparty computation or homomorphic encryption, can further enhance the security of model aggregation. These techniques allow for the computation of model updates in a way that protects the individual contributions of clients, ensuring that no sensitive information is disclosed during the aggregation process.

The paper emphasizes the importance of these defensive measures in federated learning, particularly in the context of potential attacks like model inversion or gradient leakage. By employing robust aggregation techniques alongside other privacy-preserving methods, federated learning can achieve a balance between model performance and client privacy.

Overall, the discussion highlights the ongoing challenges in securing federated learning environments and the necessity for continued research into effective strategies that can withstand various attack vectors while still allowing for collaborative model training across distributed clients.
