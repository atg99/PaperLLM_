Lan et al. provide an overview of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) with a focus on their applications in digital image processing within the biomedical informatics field. The authors conducted a literature review by screening relevant abstracts from top conferences and Google Scholar, specifically targeting areas such as medical imaging analysis and bioinformatics.

The paper begins with a brief history of GANs, explaining the distinction between discriminative and generative models in deep learning. Discriminative models focus on modeling the relationship between known and unknown data, while generative models can create new data instances based on learned distributions. Traditional generative models rely on maximum likelihood estimation, which often struggles with high-dimensional data. In contrast, GANs utilize latent codes to represent complex data distributions without the need for Markov chains, enabling them to simulate intricate data relationships effectively.

The authors detail the GAN architecture, which consists of a generator and a discriminator, both typically implemented as deep neural networks. The generator aims to produce data that closely resembles real data distributions, while the discriminator serves as a binary classifier to differentiate between real and generated samples. The training involves minimizing the divergence between the real data distribution and the generated distribution, leveraging a loss function based on binary cross-entropy.

Overall, the paper highlights the potential of GANs in various biomedical applications, such as data augmentation, image conversion, and electronic health record generation, while also addressing the theoretical underpinnings and practical implications of using GANs in this domain.
