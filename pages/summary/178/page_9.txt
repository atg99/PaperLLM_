The paper by Lan et al. discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in biomedical informatics, highlighting various studies that utilize GANs for medical data prediction and analysis. Lietal utilized GANs to predict rare diseases by maximizing the log probability of unlabeled data as real data, achieving a 5% improvement in prediction accuracy over baseline methods. Guan et al. developed medical text GAN (mtGAN) to enhance electronic medical records while addressing privacy and sample imbalance issues. Yang et al. presented a semi-supervised approach using GANs to generate synthetic data for medical decision-making, which improved classification performance for cerebral stroke datasets.

Furthermore, Tang et al. introduced a GAN-based method for automatically retrieving patient questions, showing performance improvements through fine-tuning. Hassouni et al. combined GANs with LSTM to create realistic simulation environments, demonstrating that models trained on GAN-generated data performed similarly to those trained on real data.

The paper emphasizes the challenges in bioinformatics, such as the accessibility of large datasets and the high costs of sequencing, which can lead to imbalanced datasets and poor classification results. GANs have been employed to generate data samples to mitigate these issues. For instance, Ghahramani et al. successfully simulated realistic single-cell RNA sequencing data using WGAN-GP across various cell types, demonstrating the potential of GANs to produce diverse and representative biological data. Overall, the paper illustrates the transformative role of GANs in enhancing data availability and analysis in bioinformatics.
