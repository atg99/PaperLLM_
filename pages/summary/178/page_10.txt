The paper by Lan et al. discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in biomedical informatics, particularly in bioinformatics. It presents a table summarizing various studies that utilize different GAN models on public datasets for tasks such as gene expression analysis, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data generation, and DNA sequence creation.

Key studies include:

1. Ghahramani et al. utilized a Wasserstein-GAN with a gradient penalty loss function on multiple datasets to explore gene expression dynamics during cell differentiation, allowing for the identification of transiently expressed genes.

2. Marouf et al. developed a single-cell GAN (scGAN) to generate realistic scRNA-seq data from a dataset of 68,579 PBMCs, effectively modeling gene regulatory networks and addressing class imbalance through conditional generation.

3. Xu et al. proposed scIGANs for imputing missing scRNA-seq data by generating realistic cell expression profiles, thereby overcoming issues related to technical noise and dropout events.

4. Hong et al. introduced DeepHiC, a GAN that predicts high-resolution Hi-C contact maps from low-coverage sequencing data, enhancing chromatin loop detection.

5. Killoran et al. created a WGAN for generating new DNA sequences, learning important structures from a large dataset of nucleotide sequences.

6. Gupta and Zou's Feedback GAN optimized synthetic gene sequences for specific properties using a feedback-loop architecture, demonstrating the generation of antimicrobial peptide sequences.

7. Anand and Huang employed GANs to generate protein structures, utilizing data from the Protein Data Bank to derive meaningful 3D structures from 2D pairwise distance representations.

The paper highlights the potential of GANs to address various challenges in bioinformatics, including data generation, imputation, and sequence optimization, showcasing their versatility and effectiveness in advancing research in the field.
