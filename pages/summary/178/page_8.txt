The paper by Lan et al. discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in biomedical informatics, highlighting their potential in generating synthetic medical data, which addresses the challenges of limited medical datasets. The authors emphasize that the traditional GANs struggle with discrete medical data, such as disease diagnoses and severity, due to the need for differentiable gradient functions. To overcome these limitations, various modified GAN architectures have been developed.

The paper presents a summary of several studies (Table 3) that utilized GANs in medical data generation. Choi et al. introduced medGAN, which effectively generated synthetic electronic health records (EHR) by employing an autoencoder to manage high-dimensional multi-label discrete variables from datasets like PAMF and MIMIC-III. Baowaly et al. improved upon this with medWGAN and medBGAN, which produced more realistic EHR using MIMIC-III and other datasets.

Yoon et al. explored radialGAN, which utilized auxiliary datasets from different hospitals to enhance prediction performance by better aligning patient distributions. Che et al. applied ehrGAN to longitudinal data for heart failure and diabetes, focusing on improving risk prediction through data augmentation in a semi-supervised learning context.

Esteban et al. introduced RGAN and RCGAN, utilizing LSTM architecture to predict patient criticality based on recorded variables from the Philips eICU database, achieving comparable results to real data through a "Train on Synthetic, Test on Real" approach.

Overall, the paper illustrates the advancement of GANs in generating synthetic medical data, thereby addressing issues of data scarcity and imbalance in medical informatics.
