The paper by Lan et al. discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), specifically Cycle-GAN, in the field of biomedical informatics, focusing on image synthesis between MRI and CT scans. The authors highlight several studies that demonstrate the effectiveness of Cycle-GAN in generating high-quality medical images, even when paired images are limited. For instance, Wolterink et al. showed that Cycle-GAN trained with unpaired images outperformed single GAN models trained with paired images for brain tumor imaging in radiotherapy planning. Hiasa et al. improved edge alignment between MRI and CT images by integrating gradient consistency loss into the Cycle-GAN architecture, resulting in enhanced synthesis and segmentation accuracy.

Huo et al. developed an end-to-end model combining Cycle-GAN and segmentation networks, leading to better segmentation results compared to traditional approaches. Tanner et al. and Jin et al. demonstrated the use of Cycle-GAN for deformable image registration and image synthesis respectively, with the latter achieving low mean absolute error through a combination of paired and unpaired images. Yue et al. proposed a task-driven generative model for X-ray image segmentation, enhancing segmentation results by incorporating losses from a previously trained segmentation network into the Cycle-GAN training process.

Zhang et al. addressed shape consistency issues by replacing discriminators with segmenters in their Cycle-GAN framework, leading to mutual benefits in segmenting cardiovascular volumes. The authors also emphasize the broader implications of GANs in medical informatics, particularly given the rapid growth of medical data from various sources and the challenges related to data security and privacy. The integration of GANs in medical research holds promise for overcoming barriers in data sharing and enhancing the quality of scientific investigations in healthcare.
