the study highlights the challenges of training convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on medical datasets due to the limited availability of images. It emphasizes the significance of transfer learning, where pre-trained CNN weights are adapted for a specific target dataset, a process known as fine-tuning. The research specifically evaluates the fine-tuning efficacy of three prominent CNN architectures—VGG16, VGG19, and InceptionV3—on two histopathology datasets using varying learning rates.

Key findings indicate that fine-tuning the entire network does not yield optimal results for the VGG architectures, with better performance achieved by fine-tuning only the top layers. In contrast, InceptionV3 benefits from fine-tuning the entire network. The study argues that the lower layers of these networks capture general features applicable across datasets, suggesting that freezing these weights can be advantageous.

The results also demonstrate the critical role of learning rates in training efficacy. A learning rate of 0.0001 emerged as the most effective, balancing training time and performance, while higher rates destabilized the training process for shallower architectures like VGGs. Overall, the study concludes that fine-tuning outperforms training from scratch, reinforcing the importance of selecting appropriate learning rates and fine-tuning strategies for better model performance in medical image classification. Comparing these results with other studies proved challenging due to variations in evaluation metrics and datasets used.
