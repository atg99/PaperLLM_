The paper discusses the optimization of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to enhance their performance, particularly regarding overfitting. Key strategies employed include early stopping, which halts training when no accuracy improvement is observed, thus saving computational resources. The best model is saved based on accuracy improvements, and dropout is applied to each neuron to enhance robustness against overfitting by creating variations of the network. Image augmentation is also employed to artificially expand the training dataset through transformations, which is crucial since obtaining a large dataset is often impractical.

The study fine-tunes three CNN architectures—VGG16, VGG19, and InceptionV3—using the publicly available PatchCamelyon dataset, which comprises 220,000 labeled images (60% positive, 40% negative) and 57,458 unlabeled images for performance assessment. The experiments utilized a batch size of 64 and explored three learning rates (10^-3, 10^-4, and 10^-5) with the Adam optimizer. The original fully connected layers were replaced with a dropout layer set at 50% probability to enhance accuracy.

Results indicate that fine-tuning VGG16 with different learning rates yielded similar AUC values for the ROC curve, with the highest performance at a learning rate of 10^-3 for the first block trained. Subsequent block fine-tuning showed improvements, particularly with a learning rate of 10^-4. However, performance decreased when fine-tuning progressed beyond certain blocks, with the entire network fine-tuning yielding lower results than earlier experiments. The study highlights the importance of strategic block-wise fine-tuning in optimizing CNN architectures for better generalizability.
