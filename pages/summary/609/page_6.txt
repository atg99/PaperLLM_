The section on transfer learning discusses the process of utilizing a source dataset, typically large and diverse like ImageNet, to train convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for a different target dataset. Four primary techniques for transfer learning are outlined:

1. **Freezing Weights**: The first technique involves freezing the weights of the source CNN and replacing the original fully connected layers with a new classifier, such as a support vector machine, to leverage the original weights for feature extraction.

2. **Fine-Tuning Top Layers**: The second technique focuses on fine-tuning the top layers of the source CNN while keeping the bottom layers frozen, based on the premise that the lower layers capture generic features applicable across various datasets.

3. **Fine-Tuning Entire Network**: The third technique entails fine-tuning the entire network’s weights with a small learning rate to preserve the source weights, followed by the removal of the last fully connected layers to accommodate the target dataset.

4. **From Scratch**: The fourth technique utilizes the CNN’s original architecture without transferring any weights, initializing them from scratch. This approach aims to benefit from well-established architectures that have shown efficacy on challenging datasets.
