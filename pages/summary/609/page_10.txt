The paper discusses the datasets used for training a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) in a study focused on histopathology classification. It highlights the necessity of large datasets for effective training of deep CNN architectures. Two primary datasets were utilized: the ImageNet dataset and the PatchCamelyon histopathology dataset.

The ImageNet dataset, introduced in 2010 through the ImageNet Large-Scale Visual Recognition Challenge (ILSVRC), contains millions of hand-labeled images across thousands of categories, primarily featuring everyday subjects like animals and objects. This dataset serves as the foundational training set for the CNN.

The PatchCamelyon histopathology dataset, publicly available, consists of 220,000 labeled images and 57,000 unlabeled images, with a class distribution of 60% positive (tumor tissue present) and 40% negative. This dataset is crucial for fine-tuning the CNN's weights learned from ImageNet. It is noteworthy that the dataset is free of duplicates and allows for evaluation via the Kaggle platform, which computes the Area Under the Curve (AUC) of the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of these datasets in enabling accurate training and classification within the realm of computer vision and histopathology.
