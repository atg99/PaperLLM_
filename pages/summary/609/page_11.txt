The paper discusses various performance measures used to evaluate classifiers, particularly in the context of medical image classification. The accuracy metric is highlighted as a common measure, defined as the ratio of true positives and true negatives to the total number of predictions. However, accuracy is noted to be misleading for imbalanced datasets. Alternative measures include sensitivity (true positive rate) and specificity (true negative rate), which focus on the performance of positive and negative classes, respectively. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve is introduced as a combined metric, plotting sensitivity against (1 - specificity), with the area under the curve (AUC) providing a measure of the classifier's ability to distinguish between classes. An AUC of 1 indicates perfect classification, while 0.5 suggests random guessing.

To combat overfitting—a concern in medical image classification—the paper emphasizes the need for robust measures. Overfitting occurs when a model performs well on training data but poorly on test data, highlighting the importance of generalizability. Regularization techniques are discussed as methods to reduce overfitting by increasing training error to decrease testing error. One specific technique mentioned is early stopping, which halts training when validation accuracy does not improve over a set number of epochs, thereby preventing overfitting.
