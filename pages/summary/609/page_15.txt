The paper presents results from experiments using the InceptionV3 architecture and other CNN models, specifically VGG16 and VGG19, focusing on their performance in histopathology image classification. Table 4 summarizes the Area Under the Curve (AUC) results for the InceptionV3 architecture under various fine-tuning conditions and learning rates. The best performance was achieved when fine-tuning all blocks with a learning rate of 10^-4, resulting in an AUC of 0.9058, with the overall maximum AUC of 0.9280 noted for the same architecture when all blocks were fine-tuned at a learning rate of 10^-5.

Table 5 details the outcomes of training the VGG16, VGG19, and InceptionV3 architectures from scratch with different learning rates. It reveals that neither VGG16 nor VGG19 converged satisfactorily at the highest learning rate. The medium learning rate yielded better performance than the lowest rate for VGG16, while VGG19 performed better with the lowest rate. InceptionV3 showed better convergence at lower learning rates, particularly excelling with a medium learning rate.

To validate findings, the authors conducted a second set of experiments on the BreakHis dataset containing 7909 histopathology images. This dataset was divided into training, validation, and testing subsets. The results from fine-tuning the VGG16, VGG19, and InceptionV3 architectures on this dataset are documented in subsequent tables, reinforcing the initial findings from the PatchCamelyon dataset. The early stopping criteria were adjusted to 50 epochs due to the dataset size, ensuring robust training and evaluation.
