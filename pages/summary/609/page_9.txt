This paper discusses the enhancement of histopathology predictive functions using transfer learning from the ImageNet dataset. It introduces two datasets: the histopathology dataset (Dℋ) and the ImageNet dataset (D𝑖), with corresponding learning tasks Kℋ and K𝑖. The authors aim to improve the predictive function of the histopathology dataset by leveraging the knowledge from the ImageNet dataset.

The paper reviews several convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures that have been influential in image classification. It highlights the evolution of CNNs, starting with AlexNet in 2012, followed by VGG, GoogLeNet, ResNet, and DenseNet. The authors focus on three specific architectures: VGG16, VGG19, and InceptionV3, which are commonly used in related competitions and have shown superior performance.

VGG architectures, developed by Oxford’s Visual Geometry Group, consist of VGG16 with 13 convolutional layers and VGG19 with 16 layers, using small filter sizes to reduce parameters. Each architecture has multiple convolutional blocks followed by fully connected layers, with VGG16 having 138 million parameters and VGG19 having 143 million.

The InceptionV3 architecture, introduced in 2014, employs parallel convolutional layers of different sizes to effectively capture various scales of objects, consisting of nine inception modules. This architecture has 23.8 million parameters and allows for efficient memory use and increased model capacity.

Overall, the study aims to determine the effectiveness of fine-tuning these CNN architectures for analyzing the histopathology dataset, contributing to advancements in medical image analysis.
