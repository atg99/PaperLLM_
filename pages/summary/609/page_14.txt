The paper presents a comparative analysis of fine-tuning different convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures, specifically VGG16, VGG19, and InceptionV3, to optimize performance on the PatchCamelyon dataset. The results indicate that the highest area under the curve (AUC) for VGG16 was achieved at 96% by fine-tuning the last three blocks with a learning rate of 10^-4. The performance metrics for VGG16 are detailed in a table, showing varying AUC results based on the learning rate and the specific block fine-tuned.

For the VGG19 architecture, similar fine-tuning strategies were employed, yielding the best AUC through fine-tuning the last three blocks at a learning rate of 10^-4. The results show a consistent trend where lower learning rates generally yielded better performance compared to higher rates.

The InceptionV3 architecture results reveal that fine-tuning the last several blocks while freezing the earlier ones yielded optimal performance, particularly with a learning rate of 10^-4 and 10^-5 when the entire architecture was fine-tuned.

The study emphasizes the effectiveness of the fine-tuning approach in enhancing model performance, particularly when compared to CNNs trained from scratch. The findings suggest that careful selection of learning rates and the specific blocks to fine-tune are critical factors influencing the AUC performance across different CNN architectures.
