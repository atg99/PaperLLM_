The paper presents the results of fine-tuning various convolutional neural network architectures, specifically VGG16, VGG19, and InceptionV3, using the BreakHis dataset. The performance is evaluated based on the area under the curve (AUC) for different learning rates (LR) and blocks being fine-tuned.

For VGG16 (Table 6), the best AUC results were achieved with a learning rate of 10^-5, particularly when fine-tuning the 3rd and 5th blocks. The performance improved with smaller learning rates, with the 4th block fine-tuning yielding notable results as well.

In the case of VGG19 (Table 7), similar trends were observed, where fine-tuning the top layers resulted in optimal performance. Again, a learning rate of 10^-5 produced the best outcomes, particularly when fine-tuning from the 5th to the 3rd block.

InceptionV3's results (Table 8) exhibited a different pattern where fine-tuning all blocks led to an AUC of 85.69% at a learning rate of 10^-3, while performance declined in higher blocks. The overall trend across all architectures indicates that smaller learning rates and selective fine-tuning of the upper layers generally enhance model performance, aligning with findings from the PatchCamelyon dataset.
