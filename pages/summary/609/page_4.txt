The paper examines the impact of fine-tuning convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on the performance of histopathological image classification. It highlights previous studies, including those by Sharma et al. and Ahmad et al., which utilized various CNN architectures such as AlexNet, VGG16, and ResNet on different datasets. Ahmad et al. notably augmented their dataset from 260 to 72,800 images and found that ResNet achieved the highest accuracy at 85%. The paper presents a summary table that consolidates findings from several studies, detailing dataset names, sizes, architectures used, and their best accuracies.

A critical observation from the reviewed literature is that none of the studies explored the block-wise effects of fine-tuning on CNN performance, which this research aims to address. The methodology section outlines the process of fine-tuning weights from the ImageNet dataset to classify histopathological images as normal or abnormal. It formalizes the supervised machine learning process, defining the training dataset and the loss function, which is based on cross-entropy. The study emphasizes the computational expense associated with fine-tuning CNNs and seeks to determine the extent to which fine-tuning should be applied for optimal performance.
