The study found that the combined feature extraction approach yielded a higher accuracy compared to fine-tuning the AlexNet alone, highlighting the potential benefits of utilizing multiple architectures in transfer learning for histopathology image classification.

The paper emphasizes the significance of fine-tuning CNN architectures block-wise rather than as a whole, suggesting that this approach could enhance performance without the extensive computational costs associated with training entire models. The authors aim to evaluate the impact of this methodology across three advanced CNN architectures, employing various learning rates and measuring performance through the AUC of the ROC curve.

In the literature review, several studies are discussed that explore the effectiveness of transfer learning in medical image classification, particularly in histopathology. Different architectures and datasets are analyzed, revealing varying outcomes based on the methods employed. The findings suggest that while transfer learning can significantly improve classification accuracy, specific strategies—such as ensemble models and block-wise fine-tuning—can further optimize performance.

The paper is structured to first provide a literature review, followed by a detailed methodology, results, discussion of findings, and a conclusion, culminating in a comprehensive analysis of the impact of fine-tuning strategies on CNN performance in histopathology.
