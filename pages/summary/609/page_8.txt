This paper discusses the concept of transfer learning, focusing on improving the predictive function for histopathology datasets using knowledge derived from the ImageNet dataset. The source domain (DS) is represented by ImageNet, while the target domain (DT) pertains to the histopathology dataset. The goal is to enhance the target predictive function, denoted as T(.), by leveraging the predictive function S(.) from the source domain.

The paper also reviews several Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures that have been influential in image classification. Notable architectures include AlexNet, which won the ImageNet challenge in 2012, followed by deeper models such as VGG, GoogLeNet, ResNet, and DenseNet. The authors specifically highlight VGG16, VGG19, and InceptionV3 as the architectures chosen for their study, noting their popularity and superior performance in related competitions.

VGG architectures, introduced by the Visual Geometry Group in 2014, are characterized by their use of smaller convolution filters concatenated to reduce the number of parameters. VGG16 consists of 13 convolution layers, while VGG19 has 16. Both architectures utilize five convolution blocks and are equipped with a maximum pooling layer and two fully connected layers for classification. VGG16 has approximately 138 million parameters, whereas VGG19 has around 143 million, making them both deep networks with substantial complexity.
