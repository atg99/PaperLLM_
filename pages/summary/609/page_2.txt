The paper discusses the application of deep learning, specifically convolutional neural networks (CNN), in image classification, particularly within the medical field. Deep learning allows for the automated extraction of image features without manual intervention, making it effective for classifying images into predefined categories. CNNs have demonstrated high accuracy in various medical applications, including diabetic retinopathy, Alzheimer’s disease, and skin lesion detection.

The historical context of CNNs is provided, noting the initial development by LeCun, inspired by earlier researchers, which faced limitations due to dataset size and computational power. The introduction of AlexNet in 2012 marked a significant advancement, achieving a lower error rate in image classification competitions and establishing CNNs as state-of-the-art classifiers.

Training CNNs typically requires large datasets to avoid overfitting, which can be a challenge in certain domains, such as medical imaging. To address this, transfer learning has emerged, allowing the use of pre-trained network weights on different large datasets to enhance training efficiency. The paper highlights two techniques within transfer learning: fine-tuning and feature extraction, with the choice depending on dataset size and similarity to the original dataset.

In this study, fine-tuning was preferred due to the large number of images available and the significant difference between the original ImageNet dataset and the histopathology dataset used. The paper underscores the potential benefits of CNNs in medical image classification while acknowledging the challenges associated with training data availability.
