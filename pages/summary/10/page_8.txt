In music processing, different models such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNNs) have been employed for tasks like chord prediction and tempo estimation. Recent advancements have demonstrated the effectiveness of these models in handling complex tasks, including the prediction of a wide range of chord classes and the estimation of global tempo without relying on traditional onset detection methods. Various architectures have been explored, including the use of log-mel spectrograms and raw audio, but no single approach has emerged as the definitive standard.

For environmental sound analysis, three primary tasks are identified: acoustic scene classification, acoustic event detection, and tagging. Acoustic scene classification involves labeling an entire recording with a scene type, while acoustic event detection focuses on identifying the timing and type of individual sound events. Tagging, on the other hand, predicts the activity of multiple sound classes simultaneously. The paper emphasizes the potential of using multilabel classifiers to improve performance in these tasks by capturing the interactions between overlapping sound classes.

Overall, the study underscores the growing importance of deep learning in audio signal processing and the need for further research to establish standardized methodologies in both music and environmental sound analysis.
