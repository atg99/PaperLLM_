The paper discusses various deep learning models used for audio signal analysis, highlighting their structures and functionalities. It categorizes these models into three main types: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and Sequence-to-Sequence Models.

1. **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**: CNNs process audio input through convolutional layers that apply learnable kernels to extract features. They can handle different input types, such as raw waveforms or spectral features, using 1-D or 2-D convolutions. The architecture typically includes pooling layers to downsample feature maps. While CNNs can achieve large receptive fields through larger kernels or stacking layers, they may become computationally intensive. Dilated convolutions are introduced as an efficient alternative, allowing for large receptive fields without significantly increasing the number of layers. However, optimal architecture design remains largely empirical, relying on validation error for adjustments.

2. **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)**: RNNs address the limitations of CNNs in modeling long-term dependencies by using hidden states to capture temporal information. They can extend their receptive field indefinitely, which is beneficial for sequence modeling. Variants like Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks introduce gating mechanisms to mitigate issues like vanishing gradients, making them suitable for audio synthesis. LSTMs can also be adapted to model audio across both time and frequency, with Frequency LSTMs (F-LSTMs) and Time-Frequency LSTMs (TF-LSTMs) offering advantages over CNNs in certain tasks, although they are less parallelizable.

3. **Sequence-to-Sequence Models**: These models aim to transduce an input sequence directly into an output sequence, proving particularly effective for audio processing tasks such as speech recognition. Traditional systems often break down tasks into sub-tasks, but there is a shift towards end-to-end systems that jointly train acoustic, pronunciation, and language modeling components. This approach simplifies training and decoding processes, as the models are designed to directly predict target sequences without relying on separate components or bootstrapping techniques.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolving capabilities of deep learning models in audio signal processing and the trend towards integrated, end-to-end systems that streamline the training and prediction processes.
