The paper discusses several key aspects of deep learning applications in audio signal processing. It highlights the challenges of transfer learning between different audio tasks, suggesting the need for alternative learning paradigms such as semi-supervised, active, or few-shot learning when transfer learning is ineffective. 

The computational complexity of deep neural networks is addressed, noting that they require significant computational resources and large training datasets, which are not always feasible with conventional CPUs. Instead, specialized processors like GPGPUs and TPUs are recommended for training deep models, while smaller models are necessary for applications with limited resources, such as mobile devices.

The paper also touches on the interpretability and adaptability of deep learning models. It emphasizes the difficulty in understanding the connection between network parameters and specific tasks, advocating for further research to clarify how network neurons relate to target tasks and improve model structures.

Overall, the paper suggests that advancements in understanding deep learning models, along with the development of efficient computational methods, are essential for enhancing audio signal processing applications.
