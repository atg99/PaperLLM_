D. Computational Complexity
Deep learning models in audio processing can be computationally intensive, requiring significant resources for training and inference. The complexity varies depending on the model architecture, with CNNs generally being more efficient than RNNs due to their parallel processing capabilities. However, RNNs are often preferred for tasks involving temporal dependencies. The choice of model impacts not only performance but also the feasibility of deploying these systems in real-time applications. 

E. Interpretability and Adaptability
Interpretability remains a challenge in deep learning for audio processing. Understanding how models make predictions can be critical, especially in applications like medical diagnostics or autonomous systems. Efforts to enhance interpretability often involve visualizing learned features or analyzing decision-making processes. Adaptability to new tasks or domains is also crucial, as many audio applications require models to generalize well from limited training data. 

In conclusion, deep learning has significantly advanced audio processing across various domains, yet challenges remain in features, model selection, data requirements, computational complexity, and interpretability. Ongoing research is needed to address these issues and improve the effectiveness and applicability of deep learning in audio signal processing.
