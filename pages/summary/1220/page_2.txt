The study investigates global trends in sperm count, building on previous findings that indicated a significant decline in sperm concentration and total sperm count among men from North America, Europe, and Australia between 1981 and 2013. This updated meta-analysis focuses on data from all continents, particularly including previously underrepresented regions such as South and Central America, Asia, and Africa.

The researchers conducted a comprehensive search of PubMed/MEDLINE and EMBASE for studies published between 2014 and 2019, ultimately analyzing 44 estimates from 38 studies alongside data from their earlier meta-analysis. This combined analysis encompasses 223 studies with samples collected from 1973 to 2018. The study utilized linear regression and weighted meta-regression to assess trends in sperm count and total sperm count, adjusting for various covariates and exploring differences by fertility status and geographic region.

The results indicate a significant decline in sperm concentration, with a notable decrease of 51.6% among unselected men globally from 1973 to 2018, and an accelerated decline post-2000. The total sperm count also showed a 62.3% decline among unselected men in the same period. The findings highlight that the decline is not only present in traditionally studied regions but is also evident in the previously overlooked continents.

The study underscores the urgent need for research into the causes of this decline and potential interventions to mitigate ongoing disruptions to male reproductive health.
