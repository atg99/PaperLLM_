This paper presents a meta-analysis of data from 223 publications concerning trends in semen quality over time. The analysis reveals a significant decline in sperm concentration (SC) from 1973 to 2018, with an average decrease of 0.93% per year, resulting in an overall decline of 41.5%. The decline was more pronounced among unselected men, with a yearly decrease of 1.23% compared to 0.30% in fertile men. Total sperm count (TSC) also showed a steeper decline among unselected men, while semen volume remained unchanged throughout the study period.

The study employed both simple linear models and meta-regression models to assess the trends. Simple linear models indicated a significant decline in SC (slope of -0.87 million/ml per year), while meta-regression models provided slightly less steep slopes but reinforced the overall findings, particularly highlighting the pronounced decline among unselected men.

The analysis further stratified results by fertility group and geographic region, indicating that the decline in SC was consistent across various populations. Notably, the study emphasizes the importance of these findings for understanding male reproductive health trends and the potential implications for fertility.
