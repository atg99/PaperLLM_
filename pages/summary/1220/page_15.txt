The referenced studies collectively examine various factors influencing semen quality and male fertility. Key findings include the impact of environmental factors, lifestyle choices, and genetic factors on sperm parameters. Research indicates that artificial intelligence can predict semen parameters based on these factors. 

Several studies highlight the importance of specific biomarkers, such as serum anti-Mullerian hormone and inhibin B, in differentiating between fertile and subfertile men. The role of oxidative stress and exposure to endocrine-disrupting chemicals is emphasized, particularly in relation to sperm DNA damage and overall semen quality. 

Geographical variations in semen quality are documented, with notable differences observed across Europe and among various populations in Asia. The decline in semen quality over time is a recurring theme, raising concerns about male reproductive health. 

Additionally, the studies discuss the implications of lifestyle factors, such as pesticide exposure and dietary supplements, on reproductive outcomes. Overall, the body of research underscores the multifaceted nature of male fertility, suggesting that both biological and environmental influences play critical roles.
