The analysis investigates trends in sperm concentration (SC) and total sperm count (TSC) from 1973 to 2018, revealing significant declines across various groups. The overall decline in TSC was observed at a rate of 2.06 million per year, with a notable decrease among unselected men (4.70 million per year) compared to fertile men, who showed no significant decline. The data indicates a 62.3% overall decrease in TSC among unselected men from 335.7 million in 1973 to 126.6 million in 2018. Specific declines were noted in unselected North America–Europe–Australia (NEA) and South/Central America–Asia–Africa (SAA) groups.

Further analysis of recent periods (post-1995 and post-2000) indicated a steeper decline in SC, increasing from 1.16% per year before 2000 to 2.64% thereafter. Sensitivity analyses reinforced the robustness of these findings, suggesting that the decline in sperm parameters is consistent across different models and interactions, adjusted for potential confounders. Overall, the study highlights concerning trends in male reproductive health, particularly among unselected populations.
