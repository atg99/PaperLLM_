The paper presents a stratified meta-regression model analyzing trends in mean sperm concentration (SC) and total sperm count (TSC) among unselected men over various periods from 1972 to 2018. The study includes data from different years, detailing the first and last year of each period, the initial and final SC and TSC values, percentage changes per year, and slopes with confidence intervals.

Key findings include:

1. **Mean Sperm Concentration**:
   - A significant decline in SC was observed across all periods, with the steepest decrease noted for the period starting after 2000, where the slope was -1.73 million/ml/year (95% CI: -3.23 to -0.24).
   - The SC decreased from 101.2 million/ml in 1973 to 49.0 million/ml in 2018 for the >1972 period.

2. **Total Sperm Count**:
   - Similar trends were noted for TSC, with a decline across all periods, particularly a slope of -5.26 million/year for the >2000 period (95% CI: -10.72 to 0.19).
   - The TSC decreased from 335.7 million in 1973 to 126.6 million in 2018 for the >1972 period.

The model was adjusted for various factors such as continents, age, abstinence time, and methods of semen collection and counting. The study emphasizes the need for further investigation into the underlying causes of the observed declines in sperm metrics.
