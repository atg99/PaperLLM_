The study presented in this paper confirms previous findings of a significant decline in sperm count among men from North America, Europe, and Australia between 1973 and 2018, with evidence of a similar trend in unselected men from South/Central America, Africa, and Asia. This decline, which has accelerated since 2000, is increasingly recognized as a major public health concern. The authors highlight the need for further research into the causes of this decline and emphasize the importance of addressing male reproductive health for the survival of human and other species. 

The authors note that there may be selection biases affecting fertility studies, particularly concerning semen quality and the characteristics of men providing samples. They conducted a literature review, acknowledging limitations such as the exclusion of non-English publications and the need for more localized studies to understand trends in specific populations. The call for action by clinicians and scientists in 2018 to recognize decreased male fertility as a public health issue underscores the urgency of the situation. 

The study received support from The Grantham Foundation for the Protection of the Environment, and all authors declared no conflicts of interest. The paper concludes with an appeal for attention from clinicians, scientists, decision-makers, and the public to the ongoing decline in male reproductive health.
