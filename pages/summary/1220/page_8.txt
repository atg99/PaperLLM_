The paper presents meta-regression models analyzing mean sperm concentration (SC) and total sperm count (TSC) over time among unselected men from various continents. The analysis adjusts for potential confounders such as age, abstinence time, and study selection criteria, ensuring a comprehensive assessment of sperm metrics across different periods, including a focus on studies conducted post-2000.

Figure 2 details the models for SC and TSC by collection year, highlighting the adjustments made for confounding factors. The models are weighted by the standard error (SE) of SC and TSC, addressing issues like the method of semen collection, counting methods, and exclusion criteria for participants. 

Figure 3 further examines these metrics with an interaction for continents, categorizing data into regions such as North America, Europe, Australia (NEA) and South/Central America, Asia, Africa (SAA). This interaction allows for a nuanced understanding of how geographic differences may influence sperm metrics.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of rigorous statistical modeling to account for various factors that may affect sperm concentration and count trends over time, providing valuable insights into male reproductive health.
