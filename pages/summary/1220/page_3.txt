This paper presents an updated systematic review and meta-regression analysis of trends in human sperm count from 1981 to 2019, extending previous research that highlighted a significant decline in sperm concentration and total sperm count in North America, Europe, and Australia/New Zealand. The authors conducted a literature search to include studies published between 2014 and 2019, addressing whether similar trends were observable in South America, Africa, and Asia, and whether the decline continued after 2011.

The introduction underscores the growing recognition of male reproductive health issues, including the economic and societal implications of male infertility. Evidence links reduced sperm counts to increased mortality, morbidity, and declines in testosterone levels, along with rising instances of testicular cancer and male genital anomalies. The authors argue that the decline in semen quality represents a crisis, necessitating a robust monitoring system for reproductive health indicators.

The methodology adheres to established guidelines for systematic reviews and meta-analyses. The research team, comprising epidemiologists and andrologists, followed a predefined protocol to ensure best practices. They conducted a comprehensive search in PubMed/MEDLINE and EMBASE for studies reporting primary data on sperm count, applying strict inclusion and exclusion criteria based on fertility status and other factors.

Overall, this analysis aims to fill data gaps identified in prior studies, providing updated insights into the global trends in sperm count and the broader implications for male reproductive health.
