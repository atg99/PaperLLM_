The paper outlines a systematic review and meta-analysis focused on analyzing semen concentration (SC) and total sperm count (TSC) across various studies. The methodology involved a multi-step screening process where initial titles and abstracts were evaluated, followed by full-text reviews to determine eligibility based on specific criteria. Studies with fewer than ten men or those using non-standard methods for sperm assessment were excluded.

Data extraction included detailed statistics on SC and TSC, semen volume, sample sizes, and various covariates such as age, fertility status, and collection methods. Quality control measures were implemented throughout the process, with multiple reviewers involved in screening and data extraction to ensure consistency and accuracy.

Statistical analyses employed linear regression and random-effects meta-regression models to examine trends over time, accounting for potential confounders. The analysis included sensitivity tests to validate findings. 

The systematic review identified 2936 publications, leading to the inclusion of 223 studies with 288 estimates of SC and TSC. The findings contribute to understanding trends in semen quality over time, with implications for male reproductive health.
