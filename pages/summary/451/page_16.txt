The paper discusses the challenges of segmenting medical images, particularly focusing on the performance of U-Net and MultiResUNet in the context of colon endoscopy and dermoscopy images. It highlights the difficulties in distinguishing regions of interest from the background due to vague boundaries, leading to issues of under-segmentation and over-segmentation.

The results show that U-Net struggles significantly with images containing perturbations or non-uniform backgrounds, often resulting in scattered or erroneous segmentations. In contrast, MultiResUNet demonstrates superior performance, managing to segment polyps more effectively even in challenging scenarios. Although MultiResUNet is not flawless, it consistently outperforms U-Net, particularly in cases where the distinction between background and foreground is subtle.

The paper also notes that the initial version of MultiResUNet faced similar issues before enhancements were made, suggesting that the application of additional non-linear operations on encoder feature maps contributes to its robustness against perturbations. Furthermore, the presence of outliers in medical images, such as bright objects that resemble nuclei, complicates segmentation tasks, but MultiResUNet remains more reliable than U-Net in identifying the correct regions of interest.
