This capability highlights the advantages of the MultiResUNet architecture, particularly in complex medical imaging contexts where precise segmentation is critical.

The results from the 5-fold cross-validation indicate that MultiResUNet consistently outperforms U-Net across various modalities, with notable relative improvements in dermoscopy, endoscopy, fluorescence microscopy, and MRI. The only modality where U-Net matched MultiResUNet's performance was in electron microscopy, where the improvement was marginal. 

Additionally, MultiResUNet converges faster than U-Net, achieving better accuracy in fewer epochs due to the beneficial interactions between residual connections and batch normalization. The reduced standard deviation in MultiResUNet's performance across trials suggests increased reliability and robustness compared to U-Net.

Overall, the findings advocate for the adoption of MultiResUNet in medical image segmentation tasks, particularly for images with less distinct boundaries, as it provides enhanced accuracy and efficiency in training.
