The paper discusses the U-Net architecture in the context of medical image segmentation, particularly focusing on the challenge of varying scales of objects such as cell nuclei, organs, and tumors. It highlights that medical images often contain objects of irregular and diverse sizes, exemplified by dermoscopy images of skin lesions. The authors argue that a robust segmentation network must effectively analyze these varying scales, a concern that has not been adequately addressed in medical imaging.

Previous works in deep computer vision, such as those by Serre et al. and the Inception architecture, have tackled this issue by utilizing techniques like fixed Gabor filters and Inception blocks, which employ convolutional layers of different kernel sizes in parallel. This allows the network to capture features at multiple scales, enhancing the analysis of images.

The paper suggests that the U-Net architecture can be improved by integrating Inception-like blocks—specifically, incorporating 3x3 and 7x7 convolution operations alongside the existing 5x5 convolutions. This modification aims to better reconcile features learned from images at different scales. Although strided convolutions were also considered, the results indicated that U-Net with Inception-like blocks outperformed them. The introduction of these additional convolutional layers is expected to significantly improve performance in medical image segmentation tasks.
