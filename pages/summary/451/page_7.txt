The paper discusses the challenges associated with merging features from the encoder and decoder in a neural network, particularly highlighting the semantic gap between lower-level features from the encoder and higher-level features from the decoder. This discrepancy is believed to adversely affect the learning and prediction processes. To address this issue, the authors propose incorporating convolutional layers with residual connections along the shortcut connections between the encoder and decoder. This approach aims to process the encoder features further before merging them with the decoder features, thereby reducing the semantic gap. The introduction of residual connections is noted to facilitate easier learning and has shown promise in medical image analysis. The proposed method, referred to as the 'Res path,' involves passing encoder features through a series of convolutional layers before concatenating them with decoder features, utilizing 3x3 filters for convolution and 1x1 filters for residual connections. This innovative strategy is illustrated and is expected to enhance the effectiveness of feature fusion in deep convolutional networks.
