This paper discusses the optimization of convolutional neural networks to reduce memory requirements while maintaining performance. The authors propose a method to factorize larger convolutional layers, specifically 5x5 and 7x7 filters, into sequences of smaller 3x3 convolutional blocks. This approach allows the outputs from these blocks to approximate the larger convolutions effectively and extract spatial features from various scales. Their experiments indicate that this compact structure yields results comparable to more memory-intensive architectures.

Despite the reduction in memory demand, the authors note that deep networks can still be memory-intensive, particularly when two convolutional layers are sequential, as the number of filters in the first layer impacts memory usage quadratically. To mitigate this, they suggest gradually increasing the number of filters across the three layers and incorporating a residual connection. This new arrangement is termed a 'MultiRes block,' which also includes 1x1 convolutional layers to retain spatial information.

Additionally, the paper highlights the significance of shortcut connections in the U-Net architecture, which allow for the preservation of spatial information lost during pooling operations. However, the authors also acknowledge potential limitations of these skip connections, particularly regarding the semantic gap between corresponding encoder-decoder levels.
