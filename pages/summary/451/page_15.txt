The paper discusses the performance of two segmentation models, U-Net and MultiResUNet, in the context of medical imaging, specifically focusing on polyp segmentation. The validation performance, measured by the Jaccard Index, shows that MultiResUNet consistently outperforms U-Net across multiple epochs, with a smaller standard deviation indicating greater reliability. 

In practical segmentation tasks, both models demonstrate high performance, with U-Net achieving a J.I. of 0.9678 and MultiResUNet slightly better at 0.9697. However, MultiResUNet exhibits superior segmentation capabilities in more challenging scenarios, including under-segmented and over-segmented cases. The paper highlights that MultiResUNet's ability to delineate boundaries is particularly effective in dermoscopic images, which typically have clearer boundaries. This improvement is attributed to the model's use of multiple filter sizes, enhancing pixel-perfect segmentation.

Additionally, the paper asserts that MultiResUNet is more resilient to perturbations caused by noise and artifacts in medical images, which can complicate the segmentation process. Overall, the findings suggest that MultiResUNet is a more robust option for semantic segmentation in medical imaging applications.
