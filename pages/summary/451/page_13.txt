The paper discusses object localization algorithms, focusing on the Jaccard Index as a metric for evaluating segmentation accuracy. The Jaccard Index is defined as the ratio of the intersection and union of two sets, specifically the ground truth binary segmentation mask and the predicted mask. This metric emphasizes precise segmentation while penalizing both under-segmentation and over-segmentation.

The study employs k-Fold Cross Validation, specifically 5-Fold Cross Validation, to assess the effectiveness of the proposed MultiResUNet architecture compared to the baseline U-Net model. In this method, the dataset is divided into mutually exclusive subsets, and the algorithm is tested multiple times, each time using a different subset as the validation set. The best results from these runs are recorded to provide an overall performance estimation.

Results indicate that the MultiResUNet consistently outperforms the U-Net across various medical image datasets, particularly excelling in challenging cases such as Dermoscopy and Endoscopy images. This improvement is significant given the complexity and variability of these images. The proposed model also shows a relative improvement in segmenting Fluorescence Microscopy images, despite having fewer parameters than U-Net. Overall, the findings highlight the advantages of MultiResUNet in medical image segmentation tasks.
