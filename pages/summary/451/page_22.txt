This collection of references highlights significant advancements in deep learning techniques for image segmentation, particularly in medical imaging. 

Badrinarayanan et al. introduced SegNet, a deep convolutional encoder-decoder architecture tailored for image segmentation. Following this, Chen et al. developed DeepLab, which leverages atrous convolution and fully connected conditional random fields to enhance semantic segmentation performance. 

Litjens et al. conducted a comprehensive survey on the application of deep learning in medical image analysis, while Anwar et al. provided a review specifically focusing on convolutional neural networks in this domain. 

Ronneberger et al. presented U-Net, a convolutional network designed for biomedical image segmentation, which has become a cornerstone in the field. Christ et al. advanced this area with a method for automatic liver and lesion segmentation in CT scans using cascaded fully convolutional neural networks.

Further research by Lin et al. compared U-Nets with clustering methods for skin lesion segmentation, while Sirinukunwattana et al. focused on gland segmentation in colon histology images, contributing to the GLAS challenge. Cicek et al. introduced the 3D U-Net, enabling dense volumetric segmentation from sparse annotations. Lastly, Merkow et al. explored dense volume-to-volume vascular boundary detection, showcasing the breadth of applications for these segmentation techniques.

Overall, these works collectively push the boundaries of image segmentation, particularly in the medical field, demonstrating the efficacy of deep learning methodologies.
