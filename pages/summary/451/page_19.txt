The paper discusses the challenges and performance of various datasets used for image segmentation, highlighting the distinct difficulties posed by each. The Murphy Lab Fluorescence Microscopy dataset is noted for its clear contrast between cell nuclei and background, while the CVC-ClinicDB dataset presents challenges due to vague boundaries in colon endoscopy images. The ISIC-2018 dermoscopy dataset suffers from poor contrast, making lesion detection difficult, and the ISBI-2012 electron microscopy dataset faces issues with over-segmentation due to the majority of the image being covered by the region of interest. The BraTS17 MRI dataset introduces a different challenge with its multimodal 3D images.

The authors compare the performance of their proposed MultiResUNet architecture to the classical U-Net. While U-Net performs well on high-quality images, MultiResUNet shows significant improvements in accuracy on complex images with noise and unclear boundaries, achieving relative performance gains of 10.15%, 5.07%, 2.63%, 1.41%, and 0.62% across the five datasets. MultiResUNet provides more reliable segmentation, effectively captures subtle boundaries, and demonstrates resilience against perturbations and outliers. Additionally, the 3D adaptation of MultiResUNet outperforms 3D U-Net.

The paper concludes that MultiResUNet has the potential to succeed U-Net in segmentation tasks. Future research directions include optimizing hyperparameters, testing on diverse medical imaging modalities, and applying domain-specific pre-processing and post-processing methods to enhance performance. The authors believe that integrating expert knowledge into their model could further improve segmentation outcomes.
