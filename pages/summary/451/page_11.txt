The paper presents an overview of various datasets used in medical imaging, detailing the number of images, original resolutions, and input resolutions for different modalities, including fluorescence microscopy, electron microscopy, dermoscopy, endoscopy, and MRI. The datasets vary significantly in size and resolution, reflecting the diversity of medical imaging techniques.

The experiments were conducted using Python and Keras with a TensorFlow backend on a high-performance desktop computer. The proposed architecture, MultiResUNet, is designed to enhance the U-Net architecture for medical image segmentation. The performance of MultiResUNet is compared against the U-Net architecture as a baseline, ensuring a comparable number of parameters. The study also includes a 3D version of the U-Net for 3D image segmentation tasks, achieved by replacing 2D layers with their 3D counterparts.

Table 3 provides a comparison of the number of parameters for the models used in the experiments, showing that both the 2D and 3D versions of MultiResUNet require fewer parameters than their U-Net counterparts. This suggests that the proposed model may offer a more efficient solution for medical image segmentation without compromising performance.
