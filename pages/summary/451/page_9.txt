The paper introduces a novel architecture called MultiResUNet, which enhances traditional U-Net models by substituting pairs of convolutional layers with MultiRes blocks and implementing Respaths instead of standard shortcut connections. The architecture is evaluated on three distinct datasets, each presenting unique challenges. 

1. **Fluorescence Microscopy Images**: The dataset consists of 97 images with 4009 cells, evenly divided between U2OS and NIH3T3 cells. Expert manual segmentation of nuclei highlights the difficulties posed by irregular brightness and debris in the images. The original image resolutions range from 1349x1030 to 1344x1024, but they were resized to 256x256 for processing.

2. **Electron Microscopy Images**: This dataset from the ISBI-2012 2D EM segmentation challenge includes 30 images of the Drosophila larva's ventral nerve cord. The images are affected by slight alignment errors and noise, and like the fluorescence dataset, they were resized to 256x256 due to computational constraints.

3. **Dermoscopy Images**: Sourced from the ISIC-2018 lesion boundary segmentation challenge, this dataset comprises 2594 images of various skin lesions, all annotated by experts. 

Overall, the proposed MultiResUNet architecture aims to improve segmentation performance across these challenging datasets by leveraging advanced structural modifications.
