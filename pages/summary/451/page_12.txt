In the paper, the authors investigate the performance of the MultiResUNet architecture compared to the original U-Net for semantic segmentation tasks. The pre-processing involved resizing input images to fit GPU memory and normalizing pixel values to the range [0, 1]. No domain-specific pre-processing or application-specific post-processing was applied, with a threshold of 0.5 used to derive segmentation maps from the outputs of the Sigmoid-activated final layer.

The training methodology framed the semantic segmentation task as a pixel-wise binary classification problem, employing binary cross-entropy as the loss function. The loss for a single image is calculated based on the predicted and ground truth segmentation masks, and the overall loss for a batch of images is averaged. The Adam optimizer, known for its adaptability in learning rates, was utilized for training, with a total of 150 epochs chosen based on observed performance stability.

For evaluation, the authors highlight the limitations of traditional metrics like precision and recall in scenarios where the points of interest occupy a small portion of the image. Instead, they advocate for the Jaccard Index as a more reliable metric for assessing segmentation performance, given its effectiveness in addressing the challenges posed by imbalanced datasets.
