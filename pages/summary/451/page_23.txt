The cited works focus on advancements in medical image analysis and segmentation using various deep learning techniques. Setio et al. discuss the validation and comparison of algorithms for detecting pulmonary nodules in CT images as part of the LUNA16 challenge. Yu et al. present volumetric convolutional networks with mixed residual connections for automated prostate segmentation from 3D MRI. Zeiler et al. introduce deconvolutional networks, which enhance image resolution. Drozdzal et al. emphasize the significance of skip connections in biomedical image segmentation. Szegedy et al. rethink inception architecture for improved computer vision tasks, while Ioffe and Szegedy propose batch normalization to accelerate deep network training. Coelho et al. provide a dataset for nuclear segmentation in microscope images along with algorithm comparisons. Serre et al. detail robust object recognition mechanisms inspired by the cortex. Wang et al. explore convolution's role in semantic segmentation. Mao et al. discuss image restoration using convolutional auto-encoders with symmetric skip connections. Lastly, Arganda-Carreras et al. highlight crowdsourcing efforts for creating image segmentation algorithms in connectomics. These studies collectively contribute to enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of image segmentation in medical imaging.
