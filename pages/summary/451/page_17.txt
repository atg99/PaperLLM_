The paper discusses the performance of U-Net and MultiResUNet in segmenting images with irregularities and outliers. In cases where the foreground is inconsistent, U-Net tends to predict multiple small regions, while it struggles with rough backgrounds, often misclassifying them as foreground. In contrast, MultiResUNet shows improved segmentation in these challenging scenarios, although neither model achieves perfect results.

Additionally, the paper highlights the issue of outliers in fluorescence microscopy images, where U-Net misclassifies small bright particles as cell nuclei. MultiResUNet, however, successfully identifies and excludes these outliers, leading to higher accuracy in segmentation, despite U-Net's Jaccard Index being only slightly affected by these errors.

The paper also notes the unique challenge posed by the Electron Microscopy dataset, where the region of interest constitutes the majority of the images. This situation can lead to over-segmentation as models attempt to minimize training losses. Through the experiments, MultiResUNet consistently demonstrates superior performance in accurately segmenting images compared to U-Net, particularly in the presence of irregularities and outliers.
