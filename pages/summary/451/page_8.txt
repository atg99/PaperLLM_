The proposed architecture introduces the MultiResUNet model, which replaces the standard two convolutional layers in U-Net with MultiRes blocks. Each MultiRes block has a parameter W, which determines the number of filters in its convolutional layers. To ensure the number of parameters remains comparable to U-Net, W is calculated using the formula W = βU, where U is the number of filters in the corresponding U-Net layer and β is a scaling coefficient. A value of β = 1.67 is chosen to keep the parameters slightly below those of the U-Net.

The architecture benefits from gradually increasing the number of filters across successive convolutional layers within a MultiRes block, utilizing W/6, W/3, and W/2 filters, which yielded optimal results in experiments. Similar to U-Net, the number of filters doubles after each pooling or deconvolution operation.

Additionally, the architecture replaces standard shortcut connections with Respaths, which incorporate convolution operations on feature maps transitioning from the encoder to the decoder. To minimize the semantic gap between these feature maps, the number of convolutional blocks along the Respaths is reduced progressively (4, 3, 2, and 1 blocks), using 32, 64, 128, and 256 filters respectively.

All convolutional layers, except the output layer, utilize the ReLU activation function and batch normalization, while the output layer employs a Sigmoid activation function. A diagram and architectural details of the MultiResUNet model are provided.

The paper also discusses challenges in curating medical imaging datasets, highlighting issues such as high costs of imaging equipment, complex image acquisition, expert annotation, and privacy concerns. Consequently, there are few public medical imaging datasets, often with limited image counts. To evaluate the proposed architecture's efficacy, the study selects a diverse range of heterogeneous datasets across various image modalities.
