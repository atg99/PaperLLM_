This paper discusses the segmentation performance of two architectures, U-Net and MultiResUNet, in the context of biomedical image analysis. The authors highlight that while the Jaccard Index values for both models are similar, the visual outputs of their segmentation masks differ significantly. MultiResUNet effectively captures fine separating lines and small clusters of background pixels, which U-Net tends to miss. This discrepancy is attributed to the majority class segmentation inflating the Jaccard Index, making it less reliable as a measure of accuracy in this context.

The majority of the image is identified as foreground, with narrow separations indicated by membranes. U-Net's segmentation is criticized for overlooking these fine separations, while MultiResUNet shows resilience against image noise and provides more accurate visual segmentations. The paper concludes with an analysis of U-Net and proposes enhancements through Res paths and MultiRes blocks to improve feature compatibility and enable multi-resolution analysis, resulting in the development of MultiResUNet. The findings suggest that despite modest improvements in Jaccard Index metrics, the visual accuracy of segmentations is significantly enhanced with MultiResUNet.
