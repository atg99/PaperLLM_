The paper discusses the challenges faced by contractors in Turkey who adopt a low-price bidding strategy for construction projects, particularly in the context of green building. A study analyzing 430 questionnaires revealed that many contractors struggle with project delivery due to inadequate bidding practices and lack of experience in contract pricing. The adoption of the lowest-price approach often leads to increased change order costs.

Several bidding strategies are explored, including multi-attribute approaches and the average bid method, which involves selecting a bidder closest to the average of all submitted prices. The paper also highlights the Peruvian method of excluding outlier bids and using various classification and neural network techniques to enhance the selection process. Game theory and case-based reasoning have been applied to assess risks and improve bidding strategies.

The transition from traditional to smarter contracts in green building projects is emphasized, with the design-build (DB) approach noted for its ability to integrate design and sustainability goals effectively. The paper argues that DB offers significant advantages over low-bid methods by fostering collaboration between contractors and designers, ultimately enhancing quality, cost, and time management in green building projects.

The authors stress the importance of considering multiple factors—such as quality, environmental impact, technical capabilities, reputation, and price—when selecting a winning bid. This comprehensive approach aims to optimize the bidding process and ensure sustainable practices in green construction. The study's methodology focuses on developing a forecast model for accurately estimating green building project costs, utilizing data collection, feature selection, and machine learning algorithms.
