This paper examines the economic performance of green buildings through a study that collected data from various sources, primarily the websites of green building councils in the United States and Canada, between 2010 and 2020. The data was processed using MS Excel to facilitate analysis, with a focus on quantifying and comparing costs associated with different building designs. 

The study differentiates between hard and soft costs in construction. Hard costs encompass direct expenses related to physical construction, such as land acquisition and building implementation, while soft costs include indirect expenses like administration and planning. Soft costs typically range from 1 to 5% of total construction costs.

The paper highlights conflicting findings in existing literature regarding the cost implications of incorporating green elements into building projects. Estimates suggest that green buildings may cost 5 to 10% more than conventional structures, although some studies indicate a smaller increase of 1 to 2%. The inconsistency in these findings presents challenges in accurately assessing green building costs.

Furthermore, the paper categorizes green building costs into hard and soft categories and outlines various factors influencing these costs, including technical requirements and external support. This comprehensive analysis aims to clarify the economic implications of green building practices and provide a framework for future cost assessments.
