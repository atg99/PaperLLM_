The paper discusses the process of data pre-processing and machine learning (ML) techniques used for predicting green building costs. Initially, the study emphasizes the importance of selecting the appropriate variables and conducting pre-processing, which includes removing outliers and normalizing the dataset. Outliers were identified using interquartile ranges and visualized through boxplots. The presence of null indicators helped address missing values, with a small amount (3.2% of the dataset) being replaced by average and median values.

Statistical analysis of the dataset is presented, highlighting key features such as mean, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum values for variables related to population, technology, specific requirements, and green building costs. The study utilized three ML algorithms—Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Deep Neural Networks (DNN), and Random Forest (RF)—to estimate green building costs. The dataset was split into 70% for training and 30% for testing, with 5-fold cross-validation employed to validate the models' performance. The selected algorithms are noted for their scalability, accuracy, and ability to manage overfitting, making them suitable for this predictive analysis.
