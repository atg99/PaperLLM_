The paper discusses the application of the Random Forest (RF) algorithm in data mining for classification and regression tasks. The RF technique utilizes a collection of classification trees generated from bootstrap samples of the dataset, where variables are randomly selected at each split. This method, combined with bagging, enhances the stability and accuracy of the model. The study identifies two key indicators—number of grown trees and leaf size—that influence the RF's performance, establishing optimal settings at 400 grown trees and two leaf sizes for reliable results.

Further, the paper details the experimental setup for machine learning (ML) model development using K-fold cross-validation. This approach partitions the dataset into subsets for training and testing, allowing for effective hyperparameter tuning and model evaluation. The results indicate that five-fold cross-validation yielded the best prediction accuracy.

Hyperparameter optimization results are presented, showing the optimal values for various ML models, including XGBoost, RF, and Deep Neural Networks (DNN). The study also incorporates feature importance analysis through Pearson's correlation to evaluate the relationships between selected features and the costs of green building projects. This comprehensive analysis underscores the effectiveness of the RF algorithm and the ML models employed in the study.
