The paper by Alshboul et al. presents a machine learning approach to predict construction costs for green buildings, a sector that is increasingly gaining traction but lacks sufficient cost estimation models. The study highlights the challenges in accurately forecasting costs associated with green building projects, which are more complex than traditional construction due to the integration of innovative technologies aimed at reducing environmental impacts. 

The authors propose several machine learning algorithms, specifically extreme gradient boosting (XGBOOST), deep neural networks (DNN), and random forest (RF), to enhance cost prediction accuracy. The models take into account both soft and hard cost-related attributes. Among the algorithms tested, XGBOOST outperformed the others, achieving an accuracy of 0.96, followed by DNN at 0.91 and RF at 0.87, as evaluated through metrics such as mean absolute error (MAE), mean squared error (MSE), mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), and R-squared (R2).

This research emphasizes the need for robust cost prediction tools in the green construction industry, which can aid project managers in making informed decisions and facilitate the bidding process. It calls for further exploration into machine learning applications within this niche to support the advancement of sustainable construction practices.
