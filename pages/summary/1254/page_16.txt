This paper presents a machine learning framework designed to predict the construction costs of green buildings based on an analysis of data from 283 projects in North America. The models developed aim to assist vendors, designers, and decision-makers in estimating costs by considering the main influential factors.

However, the research acknowledges several limitations. It primarily focuses on green buildings in the United States and is constrained by the quantity of data and the number of attributes analyzed. The study emphasizes economic and sustainable assessments without addressing social and environmental factors, and it does not incorporate life cycle cost analysis.

The authors argue that the existing models often lack a comprehensive understanding of how various attributes interconnect, which can lead to inaccuracies in cost predictions. They highlight the need for more quantitative, objective methods, as many current approaches rely on surveys and qualitative assessments. A more holistic model that integrates a broader range of factors is necessary for reliable forecasting.

Moreover, the scarcity of historical data on green construction costs poses a challenge for researchers aiming to develop robust prediction models. The paper concludes by advocating for further research that combines additional data and attributes, including recently LEED-accredited green buildings, to enhance the reliability and applicability of cost prediction models in the construction industry.
