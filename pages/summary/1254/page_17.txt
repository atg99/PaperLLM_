The paper discusses the pressing need for effective decision-making tools in the construction industry, particularly for predicting green construction costs. Existing models have limitations, often focusing on single locations and lacking comprehensive evaluations of relevant features. The authors advocate for a global model for green cost prediction that addresses these deficiencies.

The developed models stand out due to their unique processing sequences, which enhance forecast accuracy while minimizing the impact of class dimensions. These models are characterized as efficient, requiring low memory and providing high precision, making them suitable as decision support tools across various construction sectors. The study emphasizes the importance of these models in mitigating financial and legal disputes among stakeholders in the green building sector, especially in the context of estimating acceptable costs.

Key research questions regarding the cost implications of green versus traditional buildings are addressed through machine learning-based cost prediction models. The study incorporates four primary attributes and twenty sub-features related to green building costs, utilizing advanced forecasting techniques to enhance decision-making. Among the models evaluated, XGBOOST and Deep Neural Networks (DNN) demonstrated superior performance in cost prediction, while Random Forest (RF) showed acceptable accuracy.

Overall, the findings suggest that machine learning approaches can reliably predict green building costs and facilitate comparisons with conventional building expenses. The research supports the development of tools that predict total operational and life cycle costs, contributing to the LEED certification process. Future studies are encouraged to expand datasets and explore diverse building types and locations for more robust predictions.
