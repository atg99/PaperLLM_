This paper discusses the complexities involved in selecting bid winners for green building construction, emphasizing that traditional methods, particularly the lowest price approach, can be detrimental. The study highlights the need for a more comprehensive evaluation that includes various sustainability indicators such as environmental impact, material and water usage, and energy efficiency. 

The authors argue that relying solely on the lowest bid can lead to significant issues such as reduced quality, project delays, and increased disputes. They advocate for improved public and private sector policies to enhance the bidding process, suggesting that accurate cost prediction methodologies will foster better competition and ultimately lead to higher construction quality and value. 

The paper also presents a machine learning-based model designed to predict costs associated with green building projects. This model aims to address the challenges of balancing bid costs against actual expenditures, facilitating more informed decision-making in bid selection. The literature review underscores the historical context and growing importance of green buildings, citing the extensive adoption of certification systems like LEED. The authors call for innovative and integrated models that consider multiple influencing factors to streamline the bidding process and reduce conflicts among stakeholders in green construction projects.
