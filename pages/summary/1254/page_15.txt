This paper examines the effectiveness of various machine learning models in predicting the costs of green buildings, focusing on the XGBOOST, DNN, and RF models. The analysis reveals that XGBOOST outperforms the other models across several performance metrics, including MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and R2 values. Specifically, XGBOOST achieved an R2 value of 0.96, indicating a strong fit to the data and superior predictive capability, while DNN and RF had lower performance scores.

The study identified four main features affecting green building costs: people, technology, technical requirements, and special requirements. It was found that promoting a "green" culture among stakeholders is essential for reducing construction costs. Additionally, the use of advanced technologies is crucial for optimizing cost objectives, highlighting the importance of accurate prediction models in this domain.

The findings suggest that the established cost prediction models, particularly XGBOOST, provide valuable insights for decision-makers, facilitating more informed choices that balance economic and sustainability considerations. Overall, the study contributes to the literature on green building cost forecasting by emphasizing the significance of specific attributes and their influence on cost functions.
