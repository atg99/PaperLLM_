The paper discusses hyperparameter optimization and feature importance analysis within machine learning models applied to green building cost forecasting. Hyperparameters for models such as XGBoost, Random Forest (RF), and Deep Neural Networks (DNN) were tuned using K-fold cross-validation, with five-fold cross-validation yielding the best prediction accuracy. The optimal hyperparameter values were specified for each model, including the number of trees, learning rates, and other relevant parameters.

Feature importance analysis was performed using Pearson's correlation to assess the relationships between selected features and cost values. This analysis aimed to provide decision-makers with insights for effective planning and policy formulation. The relative importance of features was ranked, highlighting categories such as people, technological, technical, and specific requirements.

Finally, the performance evaluation of the proposed models was conducted using various statistical measures to determine their predictive capabilities and overall usefulness in forecasting green building costs.
