The paper evaluates the performance of various machine learning algorithms, specifically XGBoost, DNN, and RF, in predicting the costs of green building projects using K-fold cross-validation. The analysis was conducted for K values of 3, 5, and 7, with the most detailed comparison provided for K=5. The performance metrics assessed include Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Squared Error (MSE), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), and R-squared (R2).

The results show that XGBoost outperformed the other models across all metrics at K=5, achieving an MAE of 92.0, MSE of 132.5, MAPE of 19.9, and an R2 of 96.0. In comparison, DNN and RF had significantly higher MAE and MAPE values, indicating less precision in their predictions. The adjusted R2 value for XGBoost was also the highest at 95.9, suggesting it provided a better fit to the data.

The findings indicate that XGBoost not only demonstrated superior prediction capability but also produced results closely aligned with the actual costs of green building projects. Overall, XGBoost was identified as the most effective model for this application, showcasing its reliability in forecasting green building costs.
