The paper investigates the effectiveness of various transformations and augmentations on improving the performance of segmentation models, specifically focusing on the use of equivariant regularization through the SEAM approach. The results presented in tables highlight that simply aggregating different affine transformations does not yield significant improvements in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU). The baseline models tested show varying mIoU results, with the SEAM model performing notably better than the baseline, especially when utilizing multi-scale testing.

The improvement in performance is attributed to the enhanced quality of Class Activation Maps (CAMs) generated by SEAM, which exhibit more complete activation coverage and fewer over-activated regions. Two metrics, mFN and mFP, are introduced to quantify under-activation and over-activation, respectively. The SEAM method demonstrates lower values for both metrics compared to the baseline, indicating improved prediction accuracy that aligns more closely with ground truth segmentation.

Furthermore, the paper compares the SEAM model to state-of-the-art methods, reporting that it significantly enhances the accuracy of pseudo pixel-level annotations when trained on the revised CAMs. The final synthesized pseudo labels achieve a notable mIoU of 63.61% on the PASCAL VOC 2012 train set, leading to improved segmentation results when used in conjunction with the DeepLab model. Overall, the SEAM method showcases superior performance using only image-level labels on the PASCAL VOC 2012 test set, marking a significant advancement in the field.
