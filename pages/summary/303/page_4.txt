The paper discusses a method to enhance the consistency of Class Activation Maps (CAMs) in a Siamese network architecture through the integration of a Pixel Correlation Module (PCM) and equivariant regularization. The PCM refines the original CAM by leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture context information and enhance pixel-wise predictions. This is achieved by calculating pixel similarity using cosine distance, allowing for a more accurate aggregation of pixel features.

The PCM modifies classical self-attention by omitting the residual connection and simplifying the embedding functions to reduce the risk of overfitting. The final CAM is generated as a weighted sum of the original CAM based on normalized similarities, ensuring a smoother attention map in relevant regions.

For training, the network utilizes a global average pooling layer to generate prediction vectors for image classification, employing a multi-label soft margin loss function. The classification loss is computed separately for two branches of the Siamese network, which handle original and transformed images, serving as supervision for object localization.

Equivariant regularization loss is defined to maintain the consistency between original and transformed CAMs, promoting better learning of equivariance. However, early experiments indicated challenges with the PCM output converging to local minima, necessitating further refinement in the approach.
