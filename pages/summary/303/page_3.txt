The paper introduces the SEAM method, which integrates equivariant regularization (ER) and a pixel correlation module (PCM) to enhance weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS). The authors argue that traditional WSSS approaches overlook the differences in properties between classification and segmentation tasks. Specifically, segmentation functions should be equivariant to transformations, while classification functions emphasize invariance. This discrepancy complicates the learning process when transitioning from classification to segmentation.

The motivation for SEAM arises from the need to close the supervision gap in WSSS. The authors propose to incorporate self-attention mechanisms, which improve network approximation by capturing contextual dependencies, thus refining activation maps. The self-attention mechanism is defined mathematically, emphasizing the importance of pixel similarity in generating effective feature maps.

To address the lack of equivariant constraints in WSSS, the authors propose a new regularization term that ensures the segmentation output remains consistent under affine transformations. This is formalized as a loss function that measures the difference between the transformed input and the transformation of the network's output. The SEAM method employs a shared-weight siamese network structure, where one branch processes the transformed output and the other processes the original input, thereby enforcing the equivariant regularization effectively.

Overall, the paper presents a novel approach to improving WSSS through the combined use of self-attention and equivariant regularization, aiming to enhance the segmentation accuracy by ensuring consistent predictions across transformations.
