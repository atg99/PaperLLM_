This paper addresses the challenge of weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS) using image-level class labels. Traditional methods typically rely on class activation maps (CAMs) for object localization; however, these CAMs struggle with accuracy, often only highlighting the most discriminative parts of objects while neglecting others. The authors introduce a self-supervised equivariant attention mechanism (SEAM) to enhance the quality of CAMs by leveraging the inherent equivariance present in fully supervised segmentation tasks.

The key innovation of SEAM is the implementation of consistency regularization, which ensures that CAMs generated from transformed images maintain coherence. This is based on the observation that pixel-level labels in fully supervised settings undergo the same spatial transformations as their corresponding images during data augmentation. By applying this principle to CAMs, the authors seek to bridge the gap between weakly and fully supervised learning.

Additionally, the paper presents a pixel correlation module (PCM) that refines pixel predictions by considering the contextual appearance of similar neighboring pixels. This approach further enhances the consistency and accuracy of CAM predictions.

The authors validate their method through extensive experiments on the PASCAL VOC 2012 dataset, demonstrating that SEAM outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques under the same level of supervision. The results indicate that incorporating self-supervised learning mechanisms can significantly improve the effectiveness of weakly supervised semantic segmentation. The code for the proposed method has been made publicly available.
