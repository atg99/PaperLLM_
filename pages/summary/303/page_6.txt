The paper presents an ablation study of the SEAM (Semantic Equivariant Attention Mechanism), demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing weakly supervised semantic segmentation. Key components evaluated include equivariant regularization (ER), pixel correlation module (PCM), online hard example mining (OHEM), and conditional random fields (CRF), with the results showing a significant increase in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) from a baseline of 47.43% to a maximum of 56.83% with SEAM integration.

The evaluation of various weakly supervised localization methods, including CAM, GradCAM, and GradCAM++, indicates that these methods alone do not effectively reduce the supervision gap between fully and weakly supervised tasks. The results suggest that they fail to produce segmentation masks that closely match ground truth, as they are not designed to account for low-level information necessary for segmentation tasks.

The paper also discusses the role of affine transformations in improving the localization mechanism. The study finds that rescaling significantly enhances the mIoU of pseudo labels, while other transformations like rotation and translation provide minimal benefits. This suggests that rescaling is the most effective transformation for the tasks at hand.

Furthermore, the paper emphasizes the advantages of the siamese network structure in expanding the augmentation range of image sizes, ultimately contributing to the performance improvements observed. Overall, the findings advocate for the SEAM as a robust approach to enhancing weakly supervised semantic segmentation accuracy.
