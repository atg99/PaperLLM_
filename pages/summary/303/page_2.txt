The paper addresses the supervision gap between fully and weakly supervised semantic segmentation by proposing a self-supervised equivariant attention mechanism (SEAM). SEAM utilizes consistency regularization on class activation maps (CAMs) derived from transformed images to enhance network learning. To further bolster prediction consistency, the method incorporates a pixel correlation module (PCM), which captures contextual pixel information and refines original CAMs with learned affinity attention maps.

Implemented through a siamese network architecture, SEAM employs equivariant cross regularization (ECR) loss to regularize both original and revised CAMs across different branches. The results demonstrate improved CAM consistency over various image transformations, reducing the occurrence of over-activated and under-activated regions compared to baseline methods. Extensive experiments on the PASCAL VOC 2012 dataset confirm that the proposed algorithm achieves state-of-the-art results using only image-level annotations.

Key contributions of the paper include:

1. Introduction of SEAM, which combines equivariant regularization with PCM to bridge the supervision gap in semantic segmentation.
2. Development of a siamese network with ECR loss that effectively integrates PCM and self-supervision, yielding improved CAMs.
3. Empirical validation showing the superior performance of the proposed method in weakly supervised settings.

The paper also reviews relevant literature on weakly supervised semantic segmentation and self-supervised learning, discussing various methods that leverage weak labels and self-generated supervision to enhance model performance. The approach is positioned as a means to provide additional guidance for networks operating with limited supervision, particularly in segmentation tasks where precise object boundaries are crucial.
