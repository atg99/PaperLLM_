This paper introduces the self-supervised equivariant attention mechanism (SEAM) to enhance weakly supervised semantic segmentation. The SEAM integrates self-supervision into the weakly supervised learning framework through equivariant regularization, ensuring that class activation maps (CAMs) generated from transformed images maintain consistency. Additionally, a pixel correlation module (PCM) is incorporated to refine CAMs by learning inter-pixel similarities, improving the model's ability to generate accurate CAMs that align closely with ground truth masks.

The effectiveness of SEAM is demonstrated through qualitative segmentation results on the PASCAL VOC 2012 dataset, where the method shows superior performance compared to other state-of-the-art weakly supervised segmentation methods. The results indicate that the performance gains are not due to larger network structures or enhanced saliency detectors, but rather the synergistic effect of self-supervision and PCM in producing better CAMs.

Overall, the implementation of SEAM within a siamese network structure with efficient regularization losses leads to state-of-the-art results on the PASCAL VOC 2012 dataset, validating the proposed approach. The research received partial support from various Chinese governmental programs.
