The paper presents a novel equivariant cross regularization (ECR) loss designed to enhance the performance of a siamese network in semantic segmentation tasks. The proposed loss function, defined as LECR, aims to prevent class activation map (CAM) degeneration during pixel context refinement (PCM). This is achieved by incorporating original CAMs from a different branch of the network, ensuring that background pixels are adequately considered, as they typically do not contribute to gradients.

The background score is defined to account for background pixels, allowing the model to normalize activation vectors and improve feature representation. The total loss for the proposed method, termed SEAM, combines classification loss, equivariant regularization loss, and ECR loss to achieve consistent predictions across various transformations.

Experiments were conducted on the PASCAL VOC 2012 dataset, employing a ResNet38 backbone and augmenting the training set with additional annotations. The model was trained using online hard example mining (OHEM) and was evaluated using mean intersection over union (mIoU) as the performance metric.

The ablation studies highlight the effectiveness of the SEAM approach, demonstrating significant improvements over baseline methods, particularly in enhancing pixel-level pseudo labels derived from revised CAMs. The results indicate that the proposed method, particularly when combined with dense conditional random fields (CRF), achieves superior performance, with the model reaching a mIoU of 56.83% on the dataset. Overall, the findings suggest that the integration of ECR and PCM within a siamese network framework leads to substantial advancements in semantic segmentation.
