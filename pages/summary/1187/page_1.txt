The paper "Graph-based Semi-supervised Learning: A Comprehensive Review" by Zixing Song et al. provides a thorough examination of graph-based semi-supervised learning (GSSL) methods, highlighting their effectiveness in leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data. GSSL methods utilize graph structures to infer label information from unlabelled samples, aligning with the manifold assumption that similar samples are closely located in a low-dimensional space.

The authors present a new taxonomy for GSSL, categorizing it into graph regularization and graph embedding methods, and include updated references along with resources such as codes and datasets. They argue that GSSL methods offer several advantages, including universality across various data types, the ability to formulate learning problems as convex optimization, and scalability to handle large datasets efficiently.

The paper distinguishes itself from previous surveys by focusing exclusively on GSSL and providing a systematic understanding of its developments, while also suggesting future research directions in this rapidly evolving field. Overall, it aims to equip researchers and practitioners with a comprehensive understanding of GSSL and its applications.
