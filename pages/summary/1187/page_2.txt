This paper presents a comprehensive survey of graph-based semi-supervised learning (GSSL), addressing significant gaps in existing literature, particularly a prior work by Chong et al. The authors critique the previous taxonomy, which is based on transductive, inductive, and scalability perspectives, arguing that it fails to illustrate the development context and relationships among methods. Instead, they propose a novel taxonomy centered around two main steps in GSSL: graph construction and label inference.

Key contributions include:

1. **Comprehensive Review**: The paper offers an extensive overview of GSSL methods, detailing key equations, algorithmic contexts, comparisons, strengths, and limitations.

2. **New Taxonomy**: It introduces a two-step framework: constructing a similarity graph and performing label inference. Label inference is further categorized into graph regularization and graph embedding methods, with a focus on presenting a generalized framework for regularizers and a unified representation for graph embedding using an encoder-decoder approach.

3. **Abundant Resources**: The authors compile a rich set of resources, including open-source codes, benchmark datasets, and practical application references, making the survey a valuable guide for researchers.

4. **Future Directions**: The paper outlines open problems and suggests directions for future research, emphasizing aspects like dynamicity, scalability, noise resilience, and robustness against attacks.

The structure of the paper is organized into sections that cover background knowledge, graph construction, label inference methods, and a discussion of applications, datasets, and future research challenges. The survey aims to facilitate understanding and experimentation in GSSL, ultimately aiding the development of new methodologies in the field.
