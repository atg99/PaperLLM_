The fundamental idea is to represent the adjacency matrix as the product of two lower-dimensional matrices, capturing the relationships between nodes while maintaining computational efficiency.

The reconstruction process of node embeddings is key to optimizing similarity measures that reflect the original graph structure. The objective is to minimize the reconstruction loss, ensuring that the decoder's outputs closely match the defined similarity values of the original graph. This is formalized in the optimization problem, where the empirical reconstruction loss is minimized across all training data.

Node embedding approaches can be categorized into shallow and deep embeddings. Shallow embeddings often rely on simple lookup functions, while deep embeddings utilize graph neural networks that incorporate node features and local structures for more complex representations. 

Factorization-based methods, such as Locally Linear Embedding and Laplacian Eigenmaps, use matrix factorization techniques to learn low-dimensional representations from similarity matrices. Locally Linear Embedding assumes that the embedding of each node is a linear combination of its neighbors, while Laplacian Eigenmaps aims to maintain proximity among strongly connected nodes in the embedding space through a pairwise objective function.

Overall, the encoder-decoder framework provides a comprehensive perspective on various embedding methods, facilitating comparisons and understanding of their respective components and functionalities.
