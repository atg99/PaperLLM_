The paper outlines three main assumptions for graph construction: the graph is undirected with symmetric edge weights, the absence of an edge is indicated by a weight of zero, and self-loops are not allowed. These assumptions help simplify the construction process, leading to both unsupervised and supervised methods.

In unsupervised methods, K-nearest neighbor (KNN) approaches and b-Matching techniques are highlighted. KNN methods connect nodes based on proximity, often resulting in graphs with irregular degrees. To address issues associated with hubs in KNN graphs, alternatives like mutual KNN and sequential KNN are proposed. On the other hand, b-Matching ensures that each node has a fixed number of neighbors, enhancing regularity in the graph. It involves two main steps: graph sparsification and edge re-weighting, with various methods for determining edge weights, including binary kernel, Gaussian kernel, and locally linear reconstruction.

Supervised methods leverage labeled data to refine graph construction. These methods utilize prior knowledge from labeled samples to improve the representation of node similarities and enhance the graph's effectiveness for learning tasks. The paper emphasizes the potential for improved graph-based learning outcomes through the integration of labeled information in both the construction and refinement phases.
