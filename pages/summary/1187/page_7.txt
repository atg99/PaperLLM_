The paper presents a summary of various graph regularization methods, highlighting their supervised loss functions and graph regularization losses. Key methods discussed include:

1. **Graph Regularization Framework (GRF)**: Utilizes a loss function based on the difference between the predicted and actual labels, combined with a regularization term that penalizes differences between connected nodes.

2. **Label Graph Classification (LGC)**: Differentiates itself from GRF by allowing inferred labels for labeled nodes to deviate slightly from seed values, accommodating noise in seed labels. It also penalizes labels based on node degree, which is beneficial for irregular graphs.

3. **p-Laplacian Regularization**: An extension of LGC that generalizes the regularization term to include a positive integer parameter p, providing a theoretical foundation and successful applications in deep learning.

4. **Directed Regularization**: Addresses the limitation of traditional methods to undirected graphs by incorporating a framework for directed graphs, such as citation networks. It employs a random walk approach to define a loss function that accounts for edge directionality.

5. **Manifold Regularization**: A versatile approach that integrates spectral graph theory with manifold learning to achieve smoothness in high-dimensional data representation. It formulates a loss function that balances prediction accuracy with complexity, using a kernel-based method to maintain geometric properties of the data distribution.

Overall, these methods demonstrate varying strategies to enhance learning from graph-structured data, emphasizing the importance of graph properties and the relationships between nodes.
