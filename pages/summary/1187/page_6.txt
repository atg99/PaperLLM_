The paper discusses advancements in graph-based semi-supervised learning (GSSL) methods, focusing on their efficiency and effectiveness in classification tasks. The introduction of the Graph-Based Instance Labeling Inference (GBILI) method achieves notable accuracy while maintaining quadratic time complexity. Building on GBILI, the Robust Graph that Considers Labeled Instances (RGCLI) method enhances graph robustness through optimization. A newer approach involves low-rank semi-supervised representation (LRR), which leverages labeled data for improved similarity graph generation, facilitating label inference.

The paper outlines a general framework for graph regularization, emphasizing the need for a prediction function that aligns closely with known labels while remaining smooth across the graph. This framework comprises a supervised loss term and a regularization loss term, with the trade-off between them controlled by a hyper-parameter.

Label Propagation (LP) is highlighted as a prominent method for label inference within GSSL, where labeled nodes influence the labeling of unlabeled nodes based on similarity within the graph. The basic LP algorithm involves a series of steps to propagate labels until convergence.

Gaussian Random Fields (GRF) represent an early application of LP in GSSL, where a prediction function is estimated under specific constraints to label unlabeled nodes. This method leverages a quadratic energy function to ensure smoothness and clustering of labels among connected nodes.

Furthermore, Local and Global Consistency (LGC) extends GRF to multiclass scenarios, aiming to minimize an objective function through an iterative process that combines local and global information. The paper concludes with the mathematical formulations that underpin these methodologies, highlighting their theoretical foundations and practical implications in semi-supervised learning contexts.
