This paper focuses on Graph Semi-Supervised Learning (GSSL), particularly on label inference techniques once a similarity graph is constructed. It distinguishes between transductive and inductive settings in GSSL. In transductive settings, the model infers labels only for unlabeled nodes within the training dataset, while in inductive settings, the model can predict labels for unseen nodes.

The paper emphasizes that most graph-based algorithms are designed for label inference, with graph construction being addressed in a separate section. The GSSL loss function is generalized from the supervised learning framework, incorporating supervised loss from labeled data, unsupervised loss from unlabeled data, and a regularization loss. The unsupervised loss is often integrated into the regularization term in GSSL, leading to a simplified loss function.

The paper also reviews the evolution of semi-supervised learning, which has progressed through three phases: early algorithms before 2000, a mature stage from 2000 to 2015, and advancements post-2015 with deep learning and Graph Neural Networks. It highlights the relevance of GSSL within these phases and categorizes GSSL methods into transductive and inductive settings, providing a foundational understanding of the topic.
