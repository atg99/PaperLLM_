This paper provides a comprehensive overview of GNN-based deep graph embedding methods, focusing on their improvement techniques. The authors summarize various models categorized under basic GNNs, generalized aggregation operations, pooling methods, and updates. The basic GNN framework, based on neural message passing, is introduced, with key equations defining the message passing process, aggregation, and update operations.

The paper emphasizes that despite the numerous variants of GNNs, they all adhere to the fundamental neural message passing framework. A significant section is dedicated to the generalized aggregation operation, which addresses the instability issues in basic neighborhood aggregation. The authors highlight neighborhood normalization as a solution, proposing methods like averaging and symmetric normalization to mitigate the influence of node degree on aggregation stability.

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) are identified as a prominent variant that effectively utilizes normalized aggregation and self-loop update operations. The paper concludes by acknowledging the multitude of GCN variants developed to enhance semi-supervised learning performance, setting the stage for further exploration of these advancements in subsequent sections.
