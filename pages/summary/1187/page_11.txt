The paper provides a comprehensive overview of various shallow graph embedding methods, particularly those based on random walks, and contrasts them with emerging deep embedding techniques. Key methods discussed include DeepWalk, Planetoid, node2vec, LINE, and PTE, highlighting their decoders, similarity measures, loss functions, and time complexities. 

DeepWalk, for instance, utilizes stochastic embeddings to preserve semantic meanings, while HARP enhances performance by improving weight initialization and utilizing node aggregation through graph coarsening. The relationship between random-walk-based and factorization-based methods is explored, with Qiu et al. demonstrating that random-walk approaches can be viewed as implicit matrix factorization. Their framework, NetMF, and its extension, NetSMF, are noted for improving scalability in large networks.

However, shallow embedding methods face limitations, including a lack of shared parameters leading to scalability issues, neglecting node features, and being inherently transductive, which limits their application in inductive settings. To address these drawbacks, deep embedding approaches have been developed, employing deep neural networks to capture both structural and attribute information. 

Autoencoder-based methods are highlighted as a significant advancement, where nodes are represented by high-dimensional vectors, and the aim is to reconstruct these nodes closely from their embeddings. Overall, the paper illustrates a clear progression from shallow to deep embedding methods, emphasizing the need for more complex encoders to enhance performance in semi-supervised learning tasks.
