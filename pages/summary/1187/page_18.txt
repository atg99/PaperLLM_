This paper provides a comprehensive review of graph-based semi-supervised learning (GSSL), detailing its evolution from early methods centered around graph regularization and matrix factorization to the current dominance of graph neural networks (GNNs) introduced with Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) in 2017. The authors classify GSSL methods into two main categories: graph regularization and graph embedding, and propose a new taxonomy within these frameworks.

The review highlights four key research topics for future exploration: 

1. **Dynamicity**: Traditional GSSL methods treat graphs as static entities. Recent work by Ma et al. applies generative models to view graphs as random variables, enhancing robustness to missing data and uncovering deeper relationships among attributes and labels.

2. **Scalability**: As graph sizes increase, scalability of GSSL methods becomes crucial. Strategies like anchor graph regularization (AGR) have been developed to tackle computational complexity, with notable recent contributions focusing on localized bidirectional propagation processes.

3. **Noise-resilience**: Many GSSL methods overly rely on potentially unreliable labels. Graph Agreement Models (GAM) and other approaches aim to enhance resilience to noise by leveraging auxiliary models to predict label probabilities.

4. **Attack-robustness**: The robustness of GSSL systems against data poisoning attacks is a growing concern. Various frameworks have been proposed to both generate and defend against such attacks.

The paper concludes with a chronological overview of GSSL methods and identifies datasets and open-source implementations that support these techniques. The authors emphasize the importance of addressing the outlined challenges to further advance the field.
