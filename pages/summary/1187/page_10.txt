The paper summarizes various factorization-based shallow graph embedding methods and random-walk-based node embedding approaches. 

In the factorization-based methods, key techniques include:

1. **LLE**: Utilizes a specific decoder and similarity measure, optimizing a loss function with a time complexity of O(|E|d^2).
2. **Laplacian Eigenmaps**: Employs a decoder and minimizes a loss function with similar complexity.
3. **Graph Factorization**: Directly uses the adjacency matrix for optimization, incorporating regularization to enhance scalability.
4. **GraRep**: Preserves k-order proximity by minimizing a loss function derived from a transition probability matrix, but struggles with scalability due to potential O(|V|^2) non-zero entries.
5. **HOPE**: Similar to GraRep, it maintains higher-order proximity through a different objective function, leveraging SVD for efficient node embedding.
6. **M-NMF**: Integrates both microscopic and mesoscopic structures in its embedding process by focusing on the consensus relationship between nodes and community structure.

Random-walk-based methods, which are effective for large or partially accessible graphs, include:

1. **DeepWalk**: Inspired by the skip-gram model, it maximizes the likelihood of encountering nodes in a random walk context.
2. **Planetoid**: Combines node embedding with class label prediction, functioning in both transductive and inductive settings while incorporating negative samples.
3. **node2vec**: Enhances the idea of DeepWalk by using biased random walks to balance breadth-first and depth-first explorations.
4. **LINE**: Addresses scalability issues by preserving local and global structures, optimizing through KL divergence metrics.
5. **PTE**: A semi-supervised approach that interprets word co-occurrence in a text network for effective low-dimensional embedding.

The paper highlights the strengths and limitations of each method, focusing on scalability, structural preservation, and the ability to handle different graph types.
