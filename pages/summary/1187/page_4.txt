The paper outlines various notations and definitions critical for understanding Graph-based Semi-Supervised Learning (GSSL). Key definitions include the graph's structure, including nodes, edges, and their weights. It distinguishes between directed and undirected graphs, weighted and unweighted graphs, and provides insights into concepts such as node degree, neighborhood, adjacency matrix, and graph Laplacian matrices.

The paper emphasizes the difference between transductive and inductive learning settings. Transductive methods generally yield higher accuracy but may incur substantial training costs, particularly in large-scale incremental learning scenarios. In contrast, inductive methods are more efficient in terms of training, though they may not perform as well in prediction accuracy.

Ultimately, the paper discusses the necessity of constructing an appropriate graph for GSSL applications, particularly when data does not naturally fit into a graph structure. This construction involves identifying nodes and edges and determining weights that reflect the similarity between data samples, establishing the groundwork for effective semi-supervised learning.
