The paper provides a comparative overview of various AutoEncoder-based deep graph embedding methods, highlighting their encoders, decoders, similarity measures, loss functions, and time complexities. Methods such as SDNE, DNGR, S2S-AE, and DRNE utilize multi-layer perceptrons (MLP) or LSTMs for both encoding and decoding, employing different loss functions based on similarity measures. Graph convolutional networks (GCN) are introduced in GAE and VGAE, which incorporate Kullback-Leibler divergence in their loss functions, while ARGA and ARVGA enhance performance through generative adversarial networks.

The paper also discusses GNN-based methods that address limitations of shallow embedding approaches by leveraging node neighborhood functions. GNNs enable node embeddings to be influenced by both graph structure and node features, utilizing a two-step process of aggregation and update operations. The basic GNN framework emphasizes neural message passing, where each node's hidden state is refined through iterative updates based on aggregated information from neighboring nodes. This results in embeddings that encapsulate local structural information, applicable for various semi-supervised learning tasks. 

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive examination of deep graph embedding methodologies, emphasizing the evolution from traditional approaches to more sophisticated GNN frameworks that enhance node representation learning.
