The paper presents a tutorial focused on the use of multi-task learning for pretraining in natural language processing (NLP). It highlights various pretraining approaches and provides recommendations based on experiments and recent studies. The tutorial emphasizes the strengths and limitations of learned representations, addressing trade-offs among different modeling architectures.

In the second part, the tutorial shifts to the adaptation phase of sequential training, discussing various methods including architectural modifications and optimization schedules for fine-tuning pre-trained models. It aims to summarize current trends and best practices for adapting models to target tasks.

The tutorial also covers downstream applications such as classification, natural language generation, and structured prediction, with hands-on examples to guide researchers and practitioners in applying these models. Finally, it addresses open problems and future directions in transfer learning for NLP.

The outline includes an introduction to transfer learning, discussions on pretraining methods, representation analysis, adaptation strategies, application examples, and a look at future challenges. Prerequisites for attendees include basic knowledge of neural network architectures like RNN, CNN, and Transformers.
