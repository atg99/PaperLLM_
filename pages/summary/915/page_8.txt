The paper introduces the Ranked List Loss (RLL) to enhance the learning of discriminative embeddings by utilizing all informative data points. The method separates positive and negative examples and enforces a margin between them, unlike traditional ranking losses. 

Key findings include:

1. **Weighting Negative Examples**: Experiments show that the temperature parameter T used for weighting negative examples has a minimal effect on performance. With T=0, RLL treats all non-trivial negatives equally, achieving a Recall@1 of 78.8%, only slightly lower than when proper weighting is applied.

2. **Single-level vs. Multilevel Embeddings**: RLL demonstrates improved performance with multilevel embeddings compared to single-level embeddings. Single-level embeddings show a performance gap of around 3%, with low-level embeddings being advantageous for fast inference in resource-constrained environments.

3. **Impact of Batch Size**: The batch size has a negligible impact on RLL performance, with only a 0.6% difference in Recall@1 when the number of classes per mini-batch changes from 120 to 195.

In conclusion, RLL effectively leverages informative data points to achieve state-of-the-art results on popular benchmarks, with enhanced performance noted when using multilevel embeddings.
