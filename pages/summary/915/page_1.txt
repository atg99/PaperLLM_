First, they only utilize a fraction of available data points to construct the similarity structure, leading to the omission of potentially useful examples and a less informative structure. To overcome this, the authors propose a ranked list loss (RLL) that constructs a set-based similarity structure by leveraging all instances in the gallery, dividing them into positive and negative sets. The goal is to ensure that the query is closer to the positive set than to the negative set by a specified margin.

Second, previous methods focus on minimizing the distance between positive pairs, which can neglect the distribution of intraclass data. The proposed RLL addresses this by learning a hypersphere for each class, thereby preserving the internal similarity structure. 

Extensive experiments demonstrate that the RLL outperforms existing methods on three widely recognized benchmarks, indicating its effectiveness in enhancing DML performance. Overall, the work highlights the importance of structured loss functions in DML, proposing significant improvements to current approaches.
