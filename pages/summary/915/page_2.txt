This paper addresses the limitations of current state-of-the-art Deep Metric Learning (DML) approaches, particularly in how they utilize informative examples and consider intraclass data distribution. The authors propose a novel ranking-motivated structured loss called Ranked List Loss (RLL) to enhance the learning of discriminative embeddings.

Firstly, the paper points out that previous ranking-motivated loss functions only incorporate a subset of informative examples, potentially wasting non-trivial examples and limiting the extraction of structured information. RLL aims to utilize all non-trivial data points to create a more informative structure. It organizes samples by ranking all positive examples before negative ones based on similarity, enforcing a margin between them. This set-based similarity structure is richer than point-based approaches like triplet loss.

Secondly, the authors highlight that existing structured losses often overlook the intraclass data distribution, leading to a tendency to compress samples of the same class into a single point in feature space. To counter this, RLL introduces the concept of a hypersphere for each class, where the distance between positive pairs is maintained under a specific threshold, preserving the similarity structure within each class.

The paper asserts that DML methods rely heavily on the visibility of non-trivial samples during training. RLL enhances the learning process by maximizing the number of elements with non-zero losses, thus improving the model's optimization during training. 

The contributions of this work include the introduction of RLL, which incorporates all non-trivial data points and maintains intraclass distribution through hyperspheres, leading to new state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks such as CARS196, CUB-200-2011, and SOP. 

In summary, the paper presents RLL as a significant advancement in DML, addressing critical limitations of previous methods and achieving superior performance in various applications.
