The paper discusses a novel approach to image retrieval and clustering using a modified version of GoogLeNet V2 as the backbone network. It introduces a ranked list loss function, replacing the original fully connected layers with three 512-neuron layers to enhance performance. The model is initialized with pretrained weights from ImageNet and optimized with a significantly larger learning rate for the new layers to ensure faster convergence.

Experiments are conducted on three datasets: CUB-200-2011, CARS196, and SOP, focusing on image retrieval and clustering quality. The evaluation metrics employed include Recall@K and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI). The training is performed on a Tesla V100 GPU, utilizing standard stochastic gradient descent with specific learning rates for different datasets.

The results indicate that the proposed method outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques across all datasets. Notably, RLL-H, which utilizes a high-level embedding, achieves superior performance on the small datasets, while the multilevel embedding approach (RLL-(L,M,H)) demonstrates even better results across all datasets, highlighting the advantage of using discriminative features from multiple levels.

The findings suggest that while Proxy NCA performs well, particularly on larger datasets, the proposed method consistently exceeds it in retrieval tasks. The study concludes that the multilevel embedding strategy significantly enhances the discriminative capability of the model, thereby improving image retrieval and clustering performance.
