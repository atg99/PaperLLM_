The paper presents a comparison of various state-of-the-art methods in deep metric learning on datasets CARS196, CUB-200-2011, and SOP, focusing on performance metrics such as Recall@K (%) and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI). All methods utilize GoogLeNet V2 as the backbone architecture. 

In Tables 1 and 2, it is evident that the proposed method, RLL-(L,M,H), which employs multi-level embedding by concatenating low, mid, and high-level features, achieves superior results across all metrics compared to established methods. Specifically, on CARS196, RLL-(L,M,H) achieves a Recall@1 of 82.1%, significantly higher than the previous best of 73.2% by Proxy NCA. Similarly, on SOP, RLL-(L,M,H) achieves a Recall@1 of 79.8%, outperforming others including Proxy NCA.

The paper also discusses an ablation study focusing on the mining of non-trivial examples. The study emphasizes the significance of properly setting the negative constraint parameter (α) and the margin (m) between negative and positive examples. Results indicate that an appropriate choice of α is crucial for learning discriminative embeddings, while the margin m enhances generalization capability. Notably, the performance is relatively stable across different margin values when m is greater than zero.

In summary, the proposed RLL method demonstrates improved performance and scalability in deep metric learning while effectively utilizing example mining strategies to enhance model robustness.
