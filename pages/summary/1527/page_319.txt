Blockchain technology offers a transparent and auditable ledger through time-stamping, leveraging game theory and crypto-economics to promote good behavior and prevent censorship. Its applications in healthcare are primarily in pilot or proof-of-concept stages. 

One key application is supply chain verification, where blockchain's chronological and auditable nature allows tracking of materials and treatments. This can help identify fraudulent activities, anomalies, or errors in the supply chain, such as ensuring the integrity of the cold delivery chain for medications like insulin. This application is particularly useful in developing countries to combat counterfeit drugs and is being explored for genomic data protection to address privacy issues.

Another potential use of blockchain in healthcare is incentivizing wellness through digital tokens. By engaging individuals in health services and promoting healthier lifestyles, significant healthcare cost savings could be achieved. However, the financial benefits typically accrue to health providers or employers rather than individuals. Blockchain could facilitate a more equitable distribution of these savings.
