Connected medicine offers significant potential for addressing modern health challenges, especially in disease management and elderly care. With the elderly population projected to rise from 12% in 2015 to 22% by 2050, healthcare costs for this demographic are a growing concern, as they incur higher expenses compared to younger age groups. Additionally, non-communicable diseases like type 2 diabetes and obesity are becoming widespread, with estimates suggesting that 600 million people could develop type 2 diabetes by 2050. 

The Internet of Medical Things (medical IoT) presents a promising solution to these challenges. It enables real-time monitoring of chronic conditions such as diabetes and cardiovascular diseases, allowing both patients and healthcare providers to track health metrics effectively. For instance, heart monitors can alert healthcare professionals to changes in heart rate, while smart blood glucose monitors provide parents with immediate access to their children's glucose levels, enhancing the management of type 1 diabetes.

Moreover, technology can help reduce health risks and associated costs in elderly care. Digital applications can facilitate fall detection and emergency assistance through accelerometers in devices, ensuring timely notifications to caregivers during emergencies. Overall, the integration of connected medicine into healthcare can lead to improved outcomes and cost savings as the population ages and the prevalence of chronic diseases increases.
