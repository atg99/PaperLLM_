The paper discusses the ethical implications of using predictive analytics in healthcare, particularly in predicting the risk of pancreatic cancer. Predictive analytics utilizes a diverse dataset of patient information to enhance disease risk predictions. A specific AI model was created to assess the likelihood of pancreatic cancer based on health metrics and behaviors, especially for patients identified as having poor metabolic health.

A significant ethical dilemma arose when considering how to communicate the risk of pancreatic cancer to patients, as the disease is often misdiagnosed and has a low incidence rate of 1 in 10,000. The organization sought input from stakeholders, including a Medical Advisory Panel, and determined that informing patients about this risk could lead to unnecessary anxiety and mental health issues.

The recommended approach to communication emphasized encouraging patients to consult their doctors without inducing fear. The success of this communication strategy relied on minimizing user friction, which facilitated better engagement. Behavioral economics principles were applied to ensure that the message conveyed the patient's data status without causing alarm, comparing individual results to broader demographic and medical norms.
