The paper discusses the effectiveness of ketogenic diets (KDs) in treating epilepsy, highlighting their ability to reduce or prevent seizures in children who do not respond to medication. Research indicates that over 50% of children on KDs experience at least a 50% reduction in seizures, with 10-15% becoming seizure-free. The Epilepsy Society endorses KDs for patients over 12 months old, emphasizing benefits such as reduced medication use and improved seizure control. However, a recent Cochrane review by Levy and Cooper noted a lack of randomized controlled trials on the subject, indicating a gap in rigorous evidence. 

The Ketogenic Program for Epilepsy (KPE) is introduced as a structured program designed to help individuals adopt a lower carbohydrate lifestyle through personalized education, health tracking, and support. This program aims to enhance glycemic control, decrease seizure frequency, reduce medication reliance, and boost patient confidence in managing epilepsy.
