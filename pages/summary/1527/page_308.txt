AI has advanced capabilities that can surpass human performance in various medical applications, including analyzing medical scans, identifying diabetes symptoms, and detecting diseases like retinopathy and breast cancer. Organizations like Cardiogram and Striiv utilize wearable technology to achieve high accuracy rates in diagnosing conditions such as type 2 diabetes and atrial fibrillation. Despite promising developments, many AI diagnostic tools lack peer review and rigorous validation, raising concerns about their implementation. It is essential for these tools to undergo randomized controlled trials similar to drug evaluations to ensure safety and effectiveness. AI also aids clinicians in making informed decisions by providing evidence-based treatment options rather than making decisions autonomously. In drug discovery, AI can streamline the process, which is costly and has a low success rate, with new drug development averaging $2.7 billion per successful market entry. Overall, AI has the potential to enhance diagnostics, decision-making, and drug development in healthcare.
