The paper discusses a comprehensive study on the use of a digital platform for managing epilepsy through real-time health data collection, including blood glucose, medication, and seizure tracking. It emphasizes the importance of unstructured data for understanding patient sentiment and psychology. The research spans 24 months, with evaluations at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months, followed by additional follow-ups at 18 and 24 months to assess health outcomes and program adherence. This initiative aims to be the largest and longest study of its kind and may include a randomized controlled trial on ketogenic diets delivered digitally for epilepsy.

The project seeks to identify effective methods for scaling technology delivery to the NHS and international payers, led by an experienced team known for digital health innovation. The challenge lies in creating a scalable and engaging solution, leveraging the existing infrastructure of the Diabetes Digital Media's Low Carb Program, which has shown clinically validated outcomes and high retention rates. Notably, among 300,000 registered patients in the Low Carb Program, only 3,112 have epilepsy, indicating a demand for nutrition-focused interventions in this population. DDM aims to pioneer the first randomized controlled trial for patients following a ketogenic protocol.
