The paper discusses three types of AI systems: Reactive Machines, Limited Memory, and Theory of Mind. 

Reactive Machines are the most basic form of AI, responding to current scenarios without using past experiences to inform future decisions. An example is Deep Blue, the chess-playing supercomputer that defeated Garry Kasparov in 1996 but could not learn from past games.

Limited Memory systems utilize both pre-programmed knowledge and observations over time to make decisions. They can adapt to changes in their environment, exemplified by their use in autonomous vehicles. The growth of the Internet and IoT provides extensive data for these systems.

Theory of Mind AI aims to understand and interpret emotions and behaviors of people and entities in its environment. Although still in a developmental stage, this type of AI holds potential for caregiving applications, assisting individuals with daily tasks.
