The paper discusses a developed model that predicts the likelihood of foot ulceration and other diabetic foot issues, emphasizing its application in prevention and diagnosis. A key feature of the model is the ability to validate predictions through healthcare professionals who can confirm diagnoses or refer patients to specialists. This feedback loop allows the system to learn in near real time, although gathering data from referred patients proved challenging.

Another significant challenge highlighted is the lack of understanding and resistance from stakeholders towards technology-driven healthcare solutions. Many stakeholders were influenced by past experiences and fears regarding job displacement due to automation. To address this, the project implemented training sessions led by third-party experts and internal stakeholders to demonstrate how AI and machine learning could enhance healthcare tasks and reduce resource burdens. The project scope was shaped with input from a diverse stakeholder group to ensure comprehensive engagement and support.
