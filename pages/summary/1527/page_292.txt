The paper discusses the role of wearables and the Internet of Things (IoT) in advancing connected medicine. These technologies facilitate patient-centric healthcare systems by capturing health data through sensors in devices like smartwatches. Their increasing acceptance is driven by their use in clinical trials and studies to monitor health and lifestyle factors. Participants can track vital signs, lifestyle habits, and medication adherence using various apps.

Insurance companies are beginning to leverage this data to incentivize wellness, transitioning from targeting tech-savvy individuals to broader populations, particularly in addressing non-communicable diseases. This shift enables the monitoring and management of health, potentially leading to improved longevity and reduced morbidity.

As sensor technology advances, patient health records will include comprehensive data such as sleep patterns and continuous monitoring of vital signs. Future smartwatches will integrate multiple diagnostic tools, enhancing patient care. However, it is noted that fitness trackers have limited engagement and do not significantly aid in weight loss.
