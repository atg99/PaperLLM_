This paper presents a study on the outcomes of a digitally delivered, low-carbohydrate self-management program for type 2 diabetes, focusing on a one-year longitudinal analysis. Conducted by researchers from the University of Michigan and a general practice in the UK, the study evaluates the effectiveness of the Low Carb Program, which has been utilized by over 300,000 individuals across 190 countries.

Type 2 diabetes is associated with severe health risks and significant economic costs, prompting a reevaluation of its management strategies. Traditionally seen as a progressive condition, emerging evidence suggests that a carbohydrate-reduced diet can lead to improved health outcomes, including better glycemic control, weight loss, and reduced reliance on medication. The research indicates that both very low-carbohydrate diets (20% or fewer calories from carbohydrates) and lower carbohydrate diets (40% or fewer calories) can yield beneficial results.

The study highlights the potential of digital interventions in dietary management for type 2 diabetes, contrasting with the traditional in-person dietary counseling methods. The findings support the argument that a low-carbohydrate approach may not only enhance diabetes management but also offer a pathway to remission for some patients.
