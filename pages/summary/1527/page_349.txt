The project aimed to enhance junior doctors' exposure to simulation and training environments without relying on traditional simulation centers. Its goals included providing inexperienced doctors with noncritical experience, showcasing effective and ineffective clinical practices, encouraging self-reflection among students, and immersing them in a clinical learning environment. The project sought to determine if virtual reality could help students familiarize themselves with healthcare settings, manage patient unpredictability, and build confidence in high-pressure situations. To maximize accessibility, the project utilized mobile devices like Samsung Gear and Google Pixel, avoiding high-end virtual reality hardware, ensuring a broader reach and easier implementation. The initiative was recognized with an ASME Education Innovation Award in 2016 for its innovative approach.
