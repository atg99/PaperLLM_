The paper discusses the integration of virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) in medical training and healthcare. The 293 Children's Hospital in England utilizes VR headsets and 360-degree videos to train student doctors in handling stressful situations, allowing for the review and analysis of critical decision points with peers and staff. The use of VR is expanding to include medical specialties like surgery and anatomy, where operations can be practiced with real-time expert feedback. The Royal London Hospital pioneered VR surgery in 2016, offering a unique 3-D learning experience. This technology holds promise for enhancing medical training, particularly in resource-limited countries.

In addition to training, the paper highlights the potential of virtual appointments, which are expected to become common as the cost of VR and AR devices decreases. These appointments can save time and resources by eliminating the need for physical clinic visits, making healthcare more accessible. The immersive nature of VR and AR also provides engaging learning experiences for both healthcare professionals and patients, positioning these technologies as essential tools in the future of healthcare education and delivery.
