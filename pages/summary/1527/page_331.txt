High blood glucose levels in diabetes can lead to slower wound healing and peripheral diabetic neuropathy, reducing pain sensation in the feet. This condition increases the risk of foot injuries, which can develop into ulcers due to factors like tight shoes and cuts. Narrowed arteries and poor blood flow further hinder healing, making foot ulcers a common complication in poorly controlled diabetes. Approximately 2.5% of diabetics experience foot ulcers at any time, equating to 68,000 individuals in England, with significant NHS costs associated with treatment and amputations.

Delays in receiving specialized care lead to more severe ulcers and lower healing rates. In 2014-2015, 1 in 390 diabetics faced amputations, and 1 in 33 were hospitalized for foot ulcers, costing the NHS £322 million. Programs promoting foot checks via video have shown success, with Ipswich Hospital NHS Trust saving costs and reducing diabetic bed days. NICE guidelines recommend prompt referral to multidisciplinary teams for active foot problems, but only 20% of patients were seen within the recommended timeframe in a recent audit.
