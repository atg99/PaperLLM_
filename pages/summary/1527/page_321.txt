The paper discusses the evolving role of robotics in healthcare, highlighting that while robotic technology has potential benefits, widespread affordability and implementation remain challenges. It emphasizes that robots are unlikely to fully replace human interaction in medical settings. Key areas of robotic application include:

1. **Robot-Assisted Surgery**: This technology offers enhanced vision, precision, and dexterity, but is currently limited due to high costs and training requirements. Future advancements may necessitate healthcare professionals to learn both surgical techniques and the operation of robotic instruments. Liability issues may hinder adoption.

2. **Exoskeletons**: These devices assist patients, particularly those with spinal cord injuries, in performing tasks such as standing and walking. Companies like Ekso Bionics are pioneering this technology, which is expected to become a standard rehabilitation tool, enhancing mobility across various activities and environments.

Overall, while robotic technologies show promise in transforming healthcare, significant barriers must be addressed before they can be fully integrated into medical practice.
