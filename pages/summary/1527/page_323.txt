The paper discusses the potential of drones in healthcare, particularly for emergency medical response. Drones can transport organs and medical supplies over short and long distances, improving the speed of critical care in emergencies such as strokes or heart failures. An example is the ambulance drone prototype from Tu Delft, which carries a defibrillator and medication to patients before first responders arrive. However, current regulations and technical limitations, like battery life and load capacity, hinder their widespread use.

Additionally, the concept of smart places is introduced, highlighting how intelligent homes and hospitals can enhance patient care through ubiquitous connectivity and advanced sensors. These innovations have contributed to an increase in the centenarian population, particularly in the UK. Smart environments aim to improve healthcare efficiency and personalization while reducing costs. They will utilize automated sensors to gather real-time data, enabling features like facial and voice recognition, and employing analytics platforms to facilitate decision-making in dynamic healthcare settings.
