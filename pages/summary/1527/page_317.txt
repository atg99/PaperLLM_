The transition from traditional patient health records to electronic health records (EHR) represents significant progress in healthcare. While digitalization reduces risks associated with centralized data storage, it still leaves medical records under the control of providers. Blockchain technology, known for its role in cryptocurrency, has the potential to transform data access, privacy, and trust in healthcare. Although not yet widely adopted, blockchain offers features such as an immutable and distributed public ledger, peer-to-peer control for enhanced security, and the ability to execute smart contracts for automated transactions.

Current centralized systems, like those used by PayPal and land registries, are vulnerable to hacking and manipulation. Blockchain aims to address these issues by providing a decentralized database that improves data management, privacy, and security. This technology can facilitate interoperability and streamline data sharing among healthcare providers, patients, and insurers. Patients increasingly seek access to their medical records, with EHR features becoming more prevalent in technology like iOS. Additionally, data from various health devices can be integrated into the blockchain, promoting a secure and transparent environment for health information management.
