The paper presents a comparative analysis of various convolutional neural network (CNN) models on three 360° datasets: Spherical MNIST, Pano2Vid, and Pascal VOC, focusing on their accuracy and effectiveness in handling high-resolution images. Table 2 details the model performance metrics, highlighting that KTN achieves competitive results, particularly in high-resolution datasets, where it outperforms many traditional models like S2CNN and SPHERICAL CNN due to memory constraints.

KTN is shown to replicate feature maps effectively, allowing it to match the accuracy of existing models while maintaining a smaller number of learnable parameters. This compactness contributes to KTN's generalizability, enabling better object classification accuracy compared to SPHCONV, despite the latter's superior RMSE performance in some cases. The paper argues that the independent training of parameters in SPHCONV makes it more susceptible to overfitting.

The analysis also discusses the limitations of models that impose rotation invariance, such as S2CNN and SPHERICAL CNN, which result in degraded performance in tasks like object recognition due to the loss of important orientation information. The paper concludes by evaluating the transferability of KTN across different source models, demonstrating its adaptability in various contexts.

Overall, KTN is presented as a robust solution for 360° image processing, showing significant advantages in accuracy and efficiency over existing methods.
