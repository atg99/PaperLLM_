The paper presents the Kernel Transformation Network (KTN) designed for effectively applying convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to 360-degree images in equirectangular projection. The KTN addresses two primary requirements: reducing computational size and cost while accommodating variable-sized kernels.

KTN employs depthwise separable convolutions to minimize computational costs, achieving a reduction of approximately 8 to 9 times compared to standard convolutions. The architecture uses pointwise and depthwise convolutions to capture cross-channel and spatial correlations, respectively. The model size is significantly reduced, with an overhead of roughly 1/k² of the source kernels, essential for maintaining accuracy in spherical convolutions.

To handle variable-sized kernels, KTN utilizes row-dependent depthwise projections, resizing the source kernel with projection matrices. This approach is coupled with a Residual Network-like architecture, where both residual and shortcut branches apply the resizing and depthwise separable convolutions.

The KTN's objective function focuses on minimizing the difference between feature maps generated by source kernels and those produced by the KTN module. The training process involves projecting 360-degree image content onto a tangent plane to ensure accurate training without requiring annotated data. The KTN is trained on arbitrary 360-degree images, demonstrating its adaptability across different tasks, such as object detection, even when the training images differ from the target task's content. Overall, the KTN effectively transforms source kernels for application in 360-degree image domains while maintaining computational efficiency and accuracy.
