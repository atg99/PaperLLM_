The paper presents the Kernel Transformer Network (KTN), an innovative approach for transferring convolutional neural networks (CNNs) from perspective images to 360-degree images. The study demonstrates that KTN can effectively learn a task-independent transformation, allowing it to be applied to various source CNNs with the same architecture, regardless of the specific tasks they were trained on. The results indicate that KTN and KTN-Transfer perform similarly, confirming the model's versatility in adapting across different CNNs.

In comparison to existing models, KTN shows significantly better performance than the baseline model, PROJECTED, which lacks the capability for such transfer due to its fixed kernel design. The experiments also illustrate the model size and processing speed of KTN relative to other methods. KTN's model size is comparable to other efficient models like EQUIRECTANGULAR and CUBEMAP while achieving superior accuracy. Notably, it is much smaller than SPHCONV and maintains better performance.

While KTN incurs a higher computational cost than EQUIRECTANGULAR due to real-time kernel generation, it is considerably faster than SPHCONV, highlighting its efficiency. The conclusion emphasizes KTN's ability to streamline visual recognition for 360-degree images, showcasing its scalability and transferability advantages over existing methods.
