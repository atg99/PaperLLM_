Texture synthesis is a well-established problem in image processing aimed at creating new textures that closely resemble real textures. Markovian GAN (MGAN) offers a texture synthesis approach that focuses on neural patches instead of entire images, resulting in lower computational costs and real-time synthesis capabilities compared to traditional methods. Spatial GAN (S-GAN) is noted for its unsupervised learning approach to synthesizing large-scale textures, while Periodic Spatial GAN (PSGAN) enhances this model to produce diverse, high-resolution textures with cyclic patterns.

In the realm of sketch synthesis, Texture GAN (TGAN) utilizes inputs of texture, sketch, and color images to generate new images, building upon the Scribbler method. Complementary to TGAN, a CGAN-based auto-painter converts sketches into cartoon images using a pix2pix architecture. Sketchy-GAN simplifies this process by producing realistic images from sketches across various classes. Recently, Composition-Aided GAN (CA-GAN) has advanced this field by generating new face images from inputs of face images and masks, further refined by Stacked Composition-Aided GAN (SCA-GAN).

Image-to-image (I2I) translation methods transform the visual representation of images using GANs. The Pixel-to-Pixel (pix2pix) method employs supervised learning to conditionally translate source images to target images. Alternatives like Perceptual Adversarial Networks (PAN) minimize perceptual loss, while Cycle-GAN and Disco-GAN facilitate unpaired training by employing two GANs to translate styles bidirectionally. Wasserstein distance-Dual GAN (WDGAN) introduces a new loss function to enhance performance. Star-GAN stands out for its ability to perform multi-domain translations, particularly in facial expression synthesis. Unsupervised I2I Translation (UNIT) and its extension, Multimodal UNIT (MUNIT), address limitations in generating varied outputs from a single domain by decomposing latent spaces into style and content codes. The Diverse I2I Translation via Disentangled Representations (DRIT) model further innovates by generating multiple outputs through purpose-specific disentangled representations.
