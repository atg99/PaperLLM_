This paper discusses various approaches to improving the training stability and performance of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). It highlights the use of alternative cost functions to mitigate challenges like the vanishing gradient problem and mode collapse. 

The Earth-Mover (EM) distance is introduced as a concept for measuring the disparity between real and generated data distributions. It is reformulated through Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality to become more tractable. The paper emphasizes the WGAN framework, which incorporates a gradient penalty (WGAN-GP) to enforce the 1-Lipschitz condition, resulting in enhanced training stability and faster convergence.

Additionally, the Boundary Equilibrium GAN (BEGAN) is explored, which uses a modified loss function to maintain a balance between diversity and quality in generated samples. BEGAN treats the discriminator as an autoencoder, optimizing the process of both the generator and discriminator.

The Progressive-Growing GAN (PGGAN) is presented as a multi-scale architecture that begins training with low-resolution images and gradually increases the resolution by adding layers, promoting stable learning and reducing training time.

Lastly, BigGAN is mentioned as a leading model known for its capability to generate high-quality, detailed images due to its large-scale architecture and extensive parameterization.

Overall, the paper illustrates how various GAN architectures and modifications contribute to overcoming traditional training challenges, leading to better image generation outcomes.
