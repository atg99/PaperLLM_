The paper discusses key challenges in the training of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), particularly focusing on Nash equilibrium, internal covariate shift, and mode collapse. 

In the context of GANs, training can be viewed as a competitive scenario between two deep neural networks—the generator and the discriminator—seeking a Nash equilibrium. This equilibrium represents a state where neither network can unilaterally improve its cost function. The paper highlights that simultaneous updates to the cost functions of both networks are crucial for achieving this equilibrium. If the generator and discriminator update their costs independently, it leads to training instability. An example illustrates this concept through a two-player game where one player aims to minimize a function while the other seeks to maximize it. The paper notes that attempts to find the Nash equilibrium using gradient descent may result in oscillations and instability, as shown in accompanying figures.

Additionally, the paper addresses the issue of Internal Covariate Shift (ICS), which occurs when the distribution of inputs to a network changes due to parameter updates in earlier layers. This shift necessitates adjustments in the hidden layers, leading to slower training and increased resource consumption.

Another critical issue discussed is mode collapse (MC), where the generator produces limited or identical outputs, resulting in low diversity in generated samples. This problem can manifest as either partial mode collapse, generating images with minimal diversity, or complete mode collapse, where only a single type of image is produced. The paper emphasizes the significance of addressing these challenges to improve GAN training and application in various fields such as computer vision and graphics.
