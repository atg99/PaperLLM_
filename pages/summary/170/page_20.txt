The paper discusses various remedies to mitigate mode collapse (MC) effects during the training of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Techniques such as Wasserstein GAN (WGAN), Mini-Batch Discrimination (MBD), Unrolled GAN (UGAN), and others aim to enhance the generator's capacity by preventing it from optimizing solely for a rigid discriminator. The challenges in training GANs are largely attributed to the vanishing gradient (VG) problem, which arises from the use of the sigmoid function in the discriminator's objective function. This problem can be exacerbated by a powerful discriminator.

Several methods, including WGAN, Least-Squares GAN (LSGAN), and Spectral Normalization GAN (SN-GAN), have been developed to address the vanishing gradient issue, thereby improving training stability and convergence. The paper emphasizes the importance of stabilizing GAN training, citing feature matching (FM) as a technique that introduces a new cost function for the generator. This function focuses on ensuring that the generated data reflects the statistics of real data, rather than merely maximizing the discriminator's output.

Additionally, the Unrolled GAN (UGAN) approach tackles model collapse by updating the generator based on unrolled updates of the discriminator, in contrast to the standard method of updating the discriminator first. This method has been shown to reduce mode dropping and enhance training stability. The paper includes mathematical formulations of these approaches, highlighting the adjustments made to the loss functions and parameters during the training process. Overall, the techniques surveyed are crucial for the successful implementation of generative models.
