The paper discusses several critical challenges associated with training Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), focusing on mode collapse, vanishing gradients, and the lack of proper evaluation metrics.

Mode collapse occurs when the generator produces a limited variety of outputs, only covering a single mode of the target distribution instead of all possible modes. This is illustrated in a visual example where the generator alternates between modes as the discriminator learns to reject its outputs.

The vanishing gradient problem complicates GAN training, as it leads to very small gradients for earlier layers in the generator network. This diminishes the generator's ability to improve its output quality, as the discriminator confidently rejects generated samples, inhibiting effective learning.

Furthermore, the evaluation of GANs remains problematic due to the absence of standardized metrics. While GANs have achieved success in various applications, the lack of consensus on evaluation criteria makes it challenging to compare different methods effectively.

The paper analyzes these training obstacles, emphasizing the importance of mutual communication between the generator and discriminator to achieve Nash equilibrium—an optimal state where both networks perform best. However, achieving this equilibrium is difficult due to independent updates to their cost functions.

The internal covariate shift phenomenon, characterized by continuous changes in the input distribution during training, also slows down convergence to global minima. Techniques such as Batch Normalization and Weight Normalization are proposed to mitigate this issue.

Overall, the paper highlights that mode collapse and other training shortcomings significantly hinder GAN performance, with potential solutions discussed, including various approaches aimed at enhancing communication and stabilizing training processes.
