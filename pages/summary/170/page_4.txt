The paper discusses three prominent types of generative models: Autoencoders (AE), Variational Autoencoders (VAE), and Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN). 

Autoencoders are neural networks that compress high-dimensional data into a lower-dimensional representation, known as the bottleneck, and then reconstruct the original data from this compressed form. The AE consists of two networks: an encoder that compresses the input data and a decoder that reconstructs it. The reconstruction loss is calculated as the Euclidean distance between the input data and the reconstructed output.

Variational Autoencoders extend the concept of AEs by incorporating probabilistic elements. They consist of a probabilistic encoder that maps input data to latent variables and a probabilistic decoder that reconstructs the input from these latent variables. The VAE's cost function includes a reconstruction loss and a Kullback-Leibler divergence term, which measures the difference between the real data distribution and the learned distribution.

Generative Adversarial Networks introduce a competitive framework involving two networks: a generator and a discriminator. The generator creates data samples, while the discriminator evaluates them against real data, driving the generator to produce increasingly realistic outputs through a min-max game approach.

Overall, these models facilitate the generation of new data samples and are widely used in various machine learning applications.
