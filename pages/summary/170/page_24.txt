The paper discusses the effectiveness of different interpolation methods and optimization techniques in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). It highlights that linear interpolation often results in blurring or visual washout, while spherical interpolation maintains a crisper appearance that aligns better with the endpoints. 

In terms of optimization, the paper evaluates various algorithms, including RMSProp, Stochastic Gradient Descent, AdaGrad, and ADAM. It concludes that ADAM is the most effective optimizer for addressing high-dimensional problems, enhancing convergence speed and stability during GAN training.

Normalization techniques are also examined to mitigate the computational expense associated with GAN training. Batch normalization (BN) is emphasized as crucial for preventing mode collapse by normalizing mini-batches of data based on their mean and variance. The paper provides formulas for calculating these statistics and normalizing input samples. 

Virtual batch normalization (VBN) is introduced as an extension of BN, which uses reference batches for normalization but is computationally intensive, primarily applied in the generator. Layer normalization (LN) is mentioned as another normalization technique, although details are not provided in this excerpt. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of appropriate optimizers and normalization strategies in enhancing GAN training efficiency and stability.
