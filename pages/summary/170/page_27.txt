The paper discusses various techniques to enhance the training of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), focusing on normalization methods, spectral normalization, instance noise, conditional generation, and alternative cost functions.

1. **Normalization Techniques**: The paper highlights the use of different normalization techniques such as Batch Normalization (BN), Instance Normalization (IN), and Layer Normalization (LN) for each normalization layer in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Experiments indicate improved performance when each layer employs its specific normalization method.

2. **Spectral Normalization (SN)**: Introduced in SN-GAN, spectral normalization is a weight normalization approach primarily applied to the discriminator. It ensures that the weights are adjusted to maintain a consistent scale, thus promoting Lipschitz continuity and stabilizing GAN training. Additionally, implementing SN in the generator can prevent issues related to parameter magnitudes and unusual gradients. This method reduces computational costs and simplifies hyper-parameter tuning.

3. **Instance Noise**: Adding independent Gaussian noise to both real and generated data enhances GAN training stability. This noise can be progressively increased throughout training, helping to optimize the discriminator and mitigate instability and vanishing gradient problems.

4. **Training with Labels**: Incorporating labels into the latent space improves GAN performance by allowing for conditional generation. Conditional GANs enable the generation of samples based on specific conditions, which is beneficial in various computer vision applications, such as generating images based on age or sketches.

5. **Alternative Cost Functions**: To address issues like mode collapse and gradient vanishing, the Least-Square GAN (LSGAN) introduces a new cost function that replaces traditional loss functions with least-square loss. This approach not only differentiates between real and fake samples but also encourages the generator to produce samples closer to real data.

Overall, these techniques collectively enhance the stability and effectiveness of GAN training, leading to better performance in generating high-quality data.
