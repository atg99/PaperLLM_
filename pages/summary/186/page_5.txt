This paper discusses AdvAug, a novel approach for augmenting Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) by injecting adversarial perturbations into the intermediate feature embeddings of both the generator (G) and discriminator (D). Unlike standard adversarial training that operates in the pixel space, AdvAug enhances the training process by modifying features, which has shown effectiveness in both natural language processing and computer vision. The pipeline for AdvAug is illustrated in Figure 3, where the adversarial perturbations are generated using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) and applied to the generator's intermediate feature space.

The training algorithm for the GAN with data and feature-level augmentations is summarized in Algorithm 2. It incorporates augmentations through established methods like DiffAug and Adaptive Data Augmentation (ADA) during the training of both the discriminator and generator. This approach aims to prevent direct manipulation of the real data distribution, thus potentially alleviating issues related to distribution leakage.

The experimental section evaluates AdvAug on datasets such as Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, using common metrics like Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS) to assess the performance of GANs. The authors report superior results with their implementation, particularly in lower data regimes, highlighting the effectiveness of their method through extensive ablation studies. The paper concludes by noting that AdvAug successfully generates augmented distributions, improving GAN training without compromising the integrity of the real data.
