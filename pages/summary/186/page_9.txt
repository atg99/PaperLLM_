The paper investigates the performance of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) using a data-efficient approach, specifically training with only 10% of the CIFAR-10 dataset. The study examines the impact of pruning ratios on the quality of identified GAN tickets through the Iterative Magnitude Pruning (IMP) method. Results indicate that lower pruning ratios lead to higher-quality GAN tickets, as measured by the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), although they require more effort to achieve similar sparsity levels compared to higher ratios.

Additionally, the introduction of Adversarial Augmentation (AdvAug) is explored, applied either to the generator (G), the discriminator (D), or both. The findings demonstrate that employing AdvAug significantly improves performance across all sparsity levels, with the greatest benefits observed in sparser GAN tickets. 

An ablation study further analyzes the strength and location of AdvAug application. Results show that a moderate step size and a limited number of Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) steps enhance training effectiveness, while overly aggressive settings can diminish performance. The optimal configuration for applying AdvAug involves targeting the last layer of the discriminator and the first layer of the generator, as this approach effectively addresses overfitting in data-limited scenarios. Overall, the study underscores the importance of careful tuning of pruning strategies and augmentation techniques in achieving high-quality GAN training under constrained data conditions.
