The paper presents a novel approach to training generative adversarial networks (GANs) with limited real image data, addressing the common issues of performance deterioration and model collapse. The authors draw inspiration from the concept of lottery tickets, which are independently trainable and highly sparse subnetworks found within larger networks. They propose a framework where the lottery ticket is first identified from the original GAN using a small set of real images, and then this sparse subnetwork is trained using the same data. 

This approach is shown to complement existing data augmentation techniques, enhancing GAN training efficiency. The authors also introduce a new feature-level augmentation method that can be used alongside traditional data augmentations. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework across various GAN architectures (including SNGAN, BigGAN, and StyleGAN-V2) and datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet, and several few-shot generation datasets). The findings suggest that higher sparsity in the identified subnetworks leads to better data efficiency, as indicated by improved FID scores in experiments. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for data-efficient GAN training methods, particularly in scenarios where large datasets are impractical to obtain, and provides a promising solution through the lottery ticket perspective.
