This paper explores the concept of Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) within the context of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), specifically in data-scarce scenarios. While previous research has demonstrated LTH's applicability across various domains, including visual recognition and natural language processing, this study is pioneering in applying it to GANs. The authors reveal that highly compact subnetworks can maintain similar performance levels while also significantly reducing the amount of training data required, a novel perspective that has not been previously examined.

The paper addresses the challenges posed by adversarial training, which often leads to a decline in benign generalization despite enhancing adversarial robustness. Various defense mechanisms have been proposed, with adversarial training being one of the most effective yet costly in terms of general performance. Recent studies have attempted to improve benign generalization through adversarial training by integrating perturbed inputs and features into the training process. However, the two-player optimization dynamic of GANs complicates the incorporation of adversarial information.

The methodology section revisits the GAN framework, detailing the roles of the generator and discriminator in modeling target distributions through a two-player game. The standard optimization processes for both the generator and discriminator are outlined, emphasizing the challenges of training with limited data.

An algorithm for data-efficient iterative magnitude pruning is proposed, which includes steps for training the models, pruning weights, updating binary masks, and rewinding weights to their initial states. The results indicate that the performance of the SNGAN declines significantly when trained with a limited dataset, as evidenced by the increase in Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and decrease in Inception Score (IS), alongside a collapse in training efficacy. 

Overall, this work contributes to the understanding of LTH in GANs and highlights the potential for improved data efficiency and performance in adversarial training contexts.
