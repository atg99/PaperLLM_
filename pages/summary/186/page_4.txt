The paper addresses the significant challenges faced by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) when trained with limited data. It highlights that under such conditions, the discriminator often overfits the small training samples, leading to a mismatch between the distributions of real and generated samples. This is evidenced by the discriminator's accuracy saturating at nearly 100% when only 10% of the CIFAR-10 dataset is used, indicating that it has memorized the training data rather than generalizing from it.

To mitigate these issues, the authors propose a two-stage data-efficient GAN training workflow. The first stage involves identifying "winning tickets" within the GAN architecture using a method called Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH). The second stage focuses on training these identified tickets with data and feature-level augmentations to improve generalization and prevent overconfidence in the discriminator.

The paper introduces the concept of subnetworks and winning tickets, defining winning tickets as subnetworks that can achieve comparable or better performance than unpruned GANs when trained independently. The authors extend LTH to scenarios with limited data, employing unstructured magnitude pruning to derive sparse masks for the generator and discriminator.

The training process involves initially training the full GAN model, followed by iterative pruning of weights based on their magnitude. The sparsity of the masks enables effective training even with reduced data, maintaining performance stability across different training data volumes.

Additionally, the authors suggest applying advanced data-level and feature-level augmentations to enhance the training of the identified GAN tickets. They discuss the integration of state-of-the-art augmentation techniques, such as DiffAug and Adaptive Data Augmentation (ADA), along with a new adversarial feature-level augmentation method called AdvAug.

In conclusion, the paper presents a robust approach to improving GAN performance under limited data conditions through the identification of efficient subnetworks and the use of augmentations, ultimately aiming to enhance the quality of generated images while reducing the risks of overfitting.
