This paper presents a novel approach to enhance the training efficiency of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in scenarios with limited data by breaking down the training process into two sequential sub-problems. The first step involves identifying independent trainable subnetworks, referred to as "lottery tickets" in GANs, while the second focuses on training these subnetworks. The authors argue that this method is more data-efficient and can further benefit from aggressive augmentations at both the input and feature levels.

Central to this work is the lottery ticket hypothesis, which posits that highly sparse subnetworks, known as "winning tickets," can be located and trained to achieve performance comparable to or better than that of the original dense models. The authors extend previous findings by demonstrating that high-quality GAN tickets can be identified even in data-scarce situations. These GAN tickets serve as a sparse structural prior that allows for effective training with less data, while still maintaining the training capabilities suggested by the lottery ticket hypothesis.

The research introduces a new feature-level augmentation technique that complements existing data-level augmentations. This feature-level augmentation injects adversarial perturbations into the GANs' intermediate features, providing regularization for both the generator and discriminator. The combination of these augmentations leads to improved training stability and establishes new state-of-the-art results for data-efficient GAN training.

Extensive experiments validate the proposed method across various GAN architectures and datasets. For instance, BigGAN tickets with certain sparsity levels achieved competitive performance metrics on Tiny-ImageNet and ImageNet using only a fraction of the training data. The findings suggest that the proposed approach can generate high-quality images even with minimal real sample input, outperforming traditional dense counterparts and other transfer learning methods.

Overall, the paper contributes to the field of GANs by proposing a framework that not only enhances data efficiency but also opens avenues for further exploration in training other deep learning models.
