The paper discusses the effectiveness of training Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) using sparse models and adversarial data augmentation (AdvAug) across various datasets, including Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet, and CIFAR-10. It outlines the experimental setup, detailing specific learning rates, batch sizes, training iterations, and D-steps for different GAN architectures like BigGAN and SNGAN.

Key findings from the experiments show that sparse BigGAN models outperform full models, particularly when limited training data is available. For instance, with only 10% of training data, a BigGAN model with 67.24% sparsity achieved significant improvements in Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS) metrics on Tiny-ImageNet. Additionally, AdvAug consistently enhanced performance across all training scenarios, especially in sparse models with limited data.

The results indicate that while full data regimes do not necessarily favor higher sparsity, limited data scenarios benefit from increased sparsity, as it helps prevent overfitting. Similar trends were observed in SNGAN experiments on CIFAR-10, reinforcing the advantages of using sparse models and AdvAug across varying data availability.

Overall, the paper highlights the potential of leveraging sparse training tickets and adversarial augmentation strategies to improve GAN performance, particularly in data-constrained environments.
