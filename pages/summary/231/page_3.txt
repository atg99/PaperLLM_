The paper discusses various deep learning architectures, primarily focusing on ResNet and DenseNet, and their respective approaches to utilizing dense connections. ResNet incorporates identity shortcuts, while DenseNet employs concatenation of all preceding feature-maps. CondenseNet takes this further by densely connecting layers with different resolution feature-maps. The Dual Path Network and Mixed Link Network integrate both ResNet and DenseNet techniques. The authors propose a novel approach that does not rely on either identity shortcuts or dense concatenation, contrasting with FractalNet, which also avoids these techniques but has shown less favorable performance. 

The paper further elaborates on the differences between ResNet and DenseNet, highlighting that while ResNet connects via summation and DenseNet through concatenation, both ultimately access all preceding feature-maps. The authors demonstrate that dense concatenation can be equivalently represented as dense summation after convolution, suggesting a unified perspective on dense connections. This equivalence allows for a deeper understanding of how these networks can be structured and optimized.
