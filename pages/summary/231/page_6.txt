The paper evaluates the robustness of the proposed DSNet architecture on the CIFAR-100 dataset by conducting experiments that assess its performance at varying depths and widths. The results, detailed in Tables 3 and 4, indicate that DSNet consistently outperforms ResNet across different configurations. Specifically, when the network width is set to 0.25×, DSNet shows lower classification errors compared to ResNet at various depths. Conversely, for width exploration with a fixed depth of 50 layers, DSNet again maintains superior performance.

Notably, the paper explores DSNet's performance against ResNeXt, demonstrating that DSNet can improve upon ResNeXt's results, despite ResNeXt having a higher number of parameters. The findings suggest that increasing width is more effective than depth for enhancing model performance, leading the authors to consistently use a depth of 50 layers in their width experiments.

In addition, the paper contrasts the performance of DSNet with various existing architectures, including DenseNet and FractalNet, showcasing that DSNet surpasses these models in classification accuracy. The results emphasize the effectiveness of dense weight normalized shortcuts as a competitive alternative to traditional dense connections.

The paper also discusses experiments conducted on the ImageNet dataset, adhering to standard training protocols. The implementation details, such as data augmentation and optimization strategies, are specified, and results for top-1 and top-5 classification errors are provided. DS2Net50, in particular, yields significant performance improvements on this benchmark.

Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of the DSNet architecture in terms of robustness and efficiency across different testing scenarios, positioning it favorably against established models in the field.
