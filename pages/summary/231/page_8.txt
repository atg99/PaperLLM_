The paper presents a unified perspective on dense summation, highlighting the differences between ResNet and DenseNet architectures. The authors propose a dense weighted normalized shortcut as an alternative connection method that outperforms existing techniques, specifically the identity shortcut in ResNet and dense concatenation in DenseNet. This new method enhances representational capacity in ResNet while minimizing GPU resource requirements, making it more efficient.

The paper includes performance evaluations on ImageNet, showing that the proposed DSNet models, specifically DSNet50 and DS2Net50, achieve superior results compared to ResNet152 and DenseNet264, despite having fewer parameters. The results indicate that DSNet not only reduces GPU memory usage and training time but also maintains competitive performance, comparable to DenseNet and Res2Net.

Furthermore, Grad-CAM visualizations demonstrate that DSNet has a better focus on objects within images compared to ResNet, suggesting improved interpretability. The findings underscore the effectiveness of the proposed dense shortcut connection in enhancing model performance while optimizing computational efficiency.
