The paper discusses the equivalence between DenseNet and ResNet architectures, focusing on the dense summation of outputs from previous layers. It compares the formulations of both networks, highlighting that ResNet uses the same convolution weight for all preceding outputs, while DenseNet employs different weights, leading to practical differences in training time and GPU memory usage. DenseNet's concatenation feature allows for greater flexibility in utilizing previous feature maps but at a cost of increased resource demands.

To address the limitations of both architectures, the authors propose a new connection method termed "dense shortcut," which maintains the flexibility of DenseNet while using shared convolution weights similar to ResNet. This is represented in their modified equations, where a dense weighted normalized shortcut is introduced. The authors argue that the aggregated output from the dense shortcut contains more meaningful features than the individual outputs, supporting their new approach through experimental results.

The proposed network, named DSNet, retains the ResNet backbone but replaces the identity shortcut with the dense shortcut. The authors acknowledge that redesigning the backbone could enhance DSNet's performance, although this is not explored in the current work. Overall, the paper aims to clarify the differences between DenseNet and ResNet while introducing a novel shortcut technique to improve network efficiency.
