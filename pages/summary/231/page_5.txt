The paper presents a novel architecture called DSNet, which incorporates a dense (weighted normalized) shortcut connection for improved performance in deep learning models. The authors compare the performance of DSNet with traditional models, specifically ResNet50, and highlight the limitations of naive dense identity shortcuts, which do not enhance performance.

The motivation behind the proposed normalization approach is to standardize the scale of preceding features, preventing any single feature from dominating the output. This is achieved without applying affine transformations, and the weighted summation allows for learnable parameters to dynamically adjust the significance of each feature map. The insertion of weighted normalized shortcuts within convolutional blocks further enhances performance, leading to the introduction of DS2Net.

The ablation studies conducted using the ResNet50 backbone on CIFAR datasets reveal critical insights. Firstly, normalization techniques significantly improve performance, with group normalization (GN) outperforming batch normalization (BN). Secondly, the inclusion of channel-wise weights is essential for performance gains, indicating that different channels require distinct weights. The experiments demonstrate that DS2Net consistently outperforms both DSNet and ResNet architectures across various configurations.

Overall, the results indicate that the proposed DSNet and its enhancements provide substantial improvements in classification accuracy on CIFAR-100 and CIFAR-10 datasets, validating the effectiveness of the dense shortcut design.
