The paper introduces a novel dense normalized shortcut that significantly outperforms ResNet while maintaining minimal parameter overhead and achieving comparable performance to DenseNet with reduced computational requirements. The proposed network structure utilizes the same convolutional block design as ResNet, replacing identity shortcuts with the dense normalized shortcut. Unlike previous models, it does not depend on identity shortcuts or dense concatenation, aligning it with the structural concepts of FractalNet, although with superior performance.

The authors present a unified perspective on dense summation, enhancing the understanding of the differences between ResNet and DenseNet. They propose dense weighted normalized shortcuts to address the limitations of existing dense connection techniques. Extensive evaluations show that the proposed DSNet surpasses ResNet and matches or slightly exceeds DenseNet's performance while being computationally efficient.

The paper also discusses related works in deep CNN network design, categorizing advancements into micro-module and macro-architecture designs. Micro-modules, such as normalization and attention techniques, have been widely adopted to boost performance and convergence in deep learning models. The paper emphasizes the role of normalization techniques, especially in dense shortcut paths, highlighting their lightweight properties.

In the macro-architecture section, the paper reviews prominent network architectures, noting the growing preference for ResNet and DenseNet due to their effective designs. It addresses the challenges faced by deep networks, particularly the diminishing performance of ResNet as depth increases, and discusses modifications like preactivation-ResNet that improve performance in extremely deep networks. Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding and development of efficient deep learning architectures.
