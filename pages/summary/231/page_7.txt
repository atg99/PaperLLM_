The paper presents a detailed analysis of various deep learning architectures on the ImageNet validation dataset, focusing on their top-1 and top-5 error rates. Notably, DS2Net50 demonstrates superior performance compared to several established models, including ResNet50 and DenseNet, despite having fewer parameters and computational overhead. It achieves a top-1 error of 21.61%, outperforming ResNet50 significantly. The authors emphasize that DS2Net's main advantage lies in its efficiency and speed during GPU implementation, rather than merely the parameter count.

Further experiments on the COCO detection dataset reveal that DSNet models also outperform ResNet50, achieving higher mean Average Precision (mAP) scores. The paper employs Grad-CAM for visual analysis, showing that DSNet focuses more effectively on relevant image regions compared to ResNet50.

The implementation of the dense normalized shortcut is discussed, highlighting the need for a balance between reducing computational burden and maintaining performance. Overall, the results indicate that DS2Net architectures not only enhance accuracy but also improve convergence speed, making them a valuable advancement in deep learning model design.
