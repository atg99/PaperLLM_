The paper introduces HAWQ, a novel quantization method for neural network training that leverages second-order (Hessian) information to optimize both quantization precision and the order of block-wise fine-tuning. The results demonstrate that HAWQ outperforms the reverse method, achieving an accuracy of 74.26% compared to 66.72%, while also achieving a higher compression ratio of 12.0x versus 7.2x. HAWQ converges significantly faster, taking approximately 30 epochs compared to 50 for the reverse approach.

An ablation study highlights the effectiveness of block-wise fine-tuning, with HAWQ converging in just 25 epochs, while the reverse tuning method exceeds 50 epochs. The method shows promising results across various models, including ResNet20 on Cifar-10 and Inception-V3 on ImageNet, with HAWQ achieving a notable 8x higher compression ratio for activations while maintaining comparable testing performance. Additionally, for ResNet50, HAWQ yields an accuracy of 75.48% with a model size of 7.96MB, outperforming the HAQ method.

The paper acknowledges limitations, such as the computational overhead from second-order information, which is manageable through efficient matrix-free methods. Future work is suggested to explore HAWQ's applicability in more complex tasks beyond image classification and to investigate potential integrations with other quantization and AutoML methods. The research received support from various organizations, including Intel and NVIDIA.
