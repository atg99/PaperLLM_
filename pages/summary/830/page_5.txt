This paper discusses the quantization of neural networks, specifically focusing on optimizing the quantization process for different blocks within the network. The authors present a method to approximate the Hessian as a block diagonal matrix, where each block is scaled by its dominant eigenvalue. This approach allows for sorting the layers based on the Hessian spectrum, which informs the quantization precision required for each block.

The key insight is that layers with a high eigenvalue cannot be quantized to ultra-low precision without significant performance degradation. To manage this, the authors introduce a metric that considers both the eigenvalue and the memory footprint of each block, allowing for a more balanced quantization strategy. The metric is defined as Si=λi/ni, where λi is the top eigenvalue of the Hessian for the ith block, and ni is the number of parameters in that block.

The paper also highlights the necessity of quantization-aware re-training to mitigate performance drops due to low precision. The authors propose a multi-stage fine-tuning approach, emphasizing the importance of the order in which blocks are fine-tuned. They introduce another metric, Ωi=λi/||Q(Wi)−Wi||₂, which prioritizes blocks with high curvature and a large number of parameters to ensure effective fine-tuning.

The results section outlines the quantization outcomes for various networks, including ResNet20 on Cifar-10 and Inception-V3, ResNet50, and SqueezeNext on ImageNet, with further details provided in the appendix regarding training procedures and hyper-parameters.
