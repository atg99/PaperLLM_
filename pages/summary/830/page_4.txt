The paper presents a method for computing the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix associated with the loss function in neural networks, which provides insights into the sensitivity of the model to input perturbations. The proposed algorithm, known as Power Iteration for Hessian Eigenvalue Computation, allows for the estimation of eigenvalues without explicitly forming the Hessian matrix, which is often infeasible in high-dimensional settings.

The algorithm begins by computing the gradient of the loss with respect to the parameters of a given block using backpropagation. It then proceeds with a series of iterations where a random vector is normalized, and the Hessian vector product is computed through further backpropagation. This iterative process aims to converge to the top eigenvalue of the Hessian matrix, which indicates the sensitivity of the loss function to perturbations in the corresponding block parameters.

Following the eigenvalue computation, the paper introduces a method for Hessian Aware Quantization. This involves using the computed eigenvalues to determine the quantization precision for each block, with a focus on reducing the number of bits required in regions of the loss landscape with lower curvature, which are less sensitive to quantization errors. The findings suggest that layers with larger eigenvalues are more susceptible to perturbations, and this sensitivity varies significantly across different blocks of the neural network.

Visualizations demonstrate that blocks with higher Hessian eigenvalues exhibit larger fluctuations in the loss when perturbed, indicating sharper curvature in the loss landscape. The paper includes both 1D and 3D visualizations of the loss landscape, highlighting the differing sensitivity of various blocks in networks such as ResNet20 and Inception-V3.

Overall, the proposed methods provide a framework for understanding the second-order properties of neural networks, facilitating better quantization strategies and potentially improving model robustness.
