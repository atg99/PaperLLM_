This paper investigates the performance of various reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically focusing on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO), as well as their variants that incorporate or exclude specific code-level optimizations. The study employs a comprehensive experimental setup, where at least 80 agents are trained for each algorithm to evaluate their performance on benchmark tasks, with results presented alongside 95% confidence intervals.

The results, summarized in Table 2, indicate that the impact of code-level optimizations on agent performance is significantly greater than the choice between PPO and TRPO. For instance, in the Hopper-v2 task, both PPO and TRPO show marked improvements of 17% and 21%, respectively, when code-level optimizations are applied. This leads to the introduction of two metrics: Average Algorithmic Improvement (AAI), which measures the effect of switching between algorithms, and Average Code-Level Improvement (ACLI), which quantifies the benefit of adding optimizations while keeping the algorithm constant.

Furthermore, the paper explores the necessity of the clipping mechanism in PPO by introducing a variant called PPO-NOCLIP, which employs optimizations without the clipping mechanism. The findings suggest that high performance can still be achieved without clipping, as PPO-NOCLIP consistently outperforms PPO-M, which lacks optimizations.

Overall, the study emphasizes that code-level optimizations play a crucial role in enhancing performance across various algorithms, overshadowing the differences between the algorithms themselves. This insight prompts a reevaluation of the factors contributing to the success of reinforcement learning algorithms.
