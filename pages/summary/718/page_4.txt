The paper presents an ablation study on various optimizations in reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly focusing on the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) methods. The study evaluates four key optimizations: value clipping, reward normalization, network initialization, and learning rate annealing, using two environments, Humanoid-v2 and Walker2d-v2.

Results indicate that these optimizations significantly influence the rewards landscape and are critical for achieving optimal performance in PPO. The authors trained agents across different configurations, utilizing grid search to identify the best learning rates, and found that certain optimizations were necessary for maximizing rewards. The experimental results highlight the importance of these optimizations beyond mere performance improvements, suggesting they fundamentally alter agent behavior and the effectiveness of the underlying algorithms.

The paper also discusses the implications of code-level optimizations on agent behavior, emphasizing that they can impact the principles driving core algorithmic strategies, particularly in maintaining predictiveness in policy updates. The findings provide insights into how algorithmic choices can shape the learning dynamics of reinforcement learning agents.
