This paper investigates the performance of various deep policy gradient algorithms, specifically focusing on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and its variant PPO-M, in relation to Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) within the context of the MuJoCo Humanoid-v2 task. The authors analyze key metrics such as mean reward, maximum ratio, and Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence across training iterations for agents trained using these algorithms.

The findings reveal that while all three algorithms—TRPO, PPO, and PPO-M—are capable of maintaining a KL-based trust region under optimal conditions, they struggle to uphold a ratio-based trust region, a crucial aspect of PPO's design. Notably, PPO-M shows a distinct trend in KL divergence, increasing with iterations, whereas PPO peaks early in training before declining. This suggests that auxiliary optimizations, rather than the core algorithm itself, play a significant role in the behavior of PPO-trained agents.

The paper further argues that the widely acknowledged innovation of PPO's ratio clipping mechanism may not be the sole reason for its superior performance compared to TRPO, as theoretical limitations of this mechanism are identified. The authors emphasize the importance of disentangling the impacts of code-level optimizations from the core algorithmic innovations to better understand the roots of algorithmic progress in deep policy gradient methods.

Overall, the study emphasizes the complexity of deep reinforcement learning algorithms and highlights the need for careful consideration of both their core methodologies and auxiliary components to fully grasp their performance characteristics.
