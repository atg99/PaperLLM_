The paper discusses trust region optimization methods in reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on algorithms such as TRPO (Trust Region Policy Optimization), PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization), and a modified version of PPO (PPO-M). It presents empirical results from experiments conducted on the MuJoCo simulation tasks, specifically Walker2d-v2 and Hopper-v2. 

Key findings include the analysis of mean rewards, maximum ratios, and KL divergence metrics across iterations for each algorithm. The results illustrate the performance of each algorithm in optimizing agent behavior during training. The training curves indicate that PPO and PPO-M exhibit similar trends to TRPO, suggesting effective learning capabilities across the different methods employed. 

The paper emphasizes the importance of measuring these metrics to understand the convergence and stability of reinforcement learning algorithms, as demonstrated in the training performance depicted in the figures. Overall, the investigation highlights the comparative efficacy of trust region optimization strategies in achieving high performance in complex environments.
