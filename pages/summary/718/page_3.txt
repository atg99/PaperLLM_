The paper discusses various optimizations implemented in Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that significantly enhance its performance compared to the standard policy gradient methods. Key optimizations include:

1. **Value Function Clipping**: Instead of using a regression approach to fit the value network, the implementation employs a PPO-like objective that clips value estimates around previous estimates, improving stability.

2. **Reward Scaling**: Rewards are scaled based on the standard deviation of a rolling sum of discounted rewards, rather than being fed directly into the objective.

3. **Orthogonal Initialization and Layer Scaling**: The policy and value networks utilize an orthogonal initialization scheme with varying scaling across layers, enhancing convergence.

4. **Adam Learning Rate Annealing**: The learning rate for the Adam optimizer is sometimes gradually reduced based on the task requirements.

5. **Reward Clipping**: Rewards are clipped to a predefined range (commonly [-5, 5] or [-10, 10]) to prevent extreme values from affecting training.

6. **Observation Normalization and Clipping**: Raw states are normalized and clipped similarly to rewards, ensuring that input data is stable.

7. **Hyperbolic Tangent Activations**: Hyperbolic tangent activations are used in the policy and value networks, which can enhance performance.

8. **Global Gradient Clipping**: The implementation clips gradients to maintain the global `L2 norm` below a threshold, preventing instability during training.

The authors conducted an ablation study to analyze the impact of these optimizations, revealing that they play a crucial role in the effectiveness of PPO. They introduced a simpler variant, PPO-MINIMAL (PPO-M), which excludes these optimizations and retains only the core algorithm features. This variant, alongside PPO and TRPO, was evaluated to demonstrate the sensitivity of performance to implementation choices. The findings underscore the importance of these optimizations for achieving the claimed performance levels of PPO and highlight the brittleness of deep policy gradient methods due to implementation variability.
