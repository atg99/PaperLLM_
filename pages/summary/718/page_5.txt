The paper discusses advancements in policy optimization methods for reinforcement learning, focusing on Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). TRPO constrains the KL divergence between successive policies to maintain a trust region during optimization, which is computationally intensive due to the need for second-order approximations and Hessian-vector products. In contrast, PPO simplifies the process by clipping the objective function, allowing for faster and more sample-efficient training without an explicit projection step.

The authors analyze how trust regions are enforced in both TRPO and PPO through empirical measurements of the mean KL divergence during training. They confirm that TRPO effectively enforces its trust region, while PPO's clipping mechanism approximates this enforcement. A mathematical examination of PPO reveals that the trust region's size is influenced more by the optimization method used for the clipped objective rather than the objective itself. The findings suggest that the steepness of the surrogate landscape significantly impacts the distance moved from the intended trust region, potentially leading to less controlled updates during training. Overall, the study highlights the trade-offs between computational efficiency and the enforcement of trust regions in policy gradient methods.
