This paper explores the significant impact of code-level optimizations on the training dynamics of reinforcement learning (RL) agents, specifically focusing on proximal policy optimization (PPO) and trust region policy optimization (TRPO). The authors argue that these optimizations are not merely auxiliary improvements; rather, they fundamentally alter the operation of algorithms and significantly influence final agent rewards. The findings suggest that the benefits derived from PPO's code optimizations outweigh the differences between the TRPO and PPO algorithms themselves.

The paper emphasizes that these optimizations affect the key principle of trust region enforcement in both TRPO and PPO, challenging the traditional view that the central clipping method of PPO is the primary driver of performance gains. This complexity complicates the comparison of different algorithms, as it becomes clear that a rigorous understanding of code-level optimizations is essential for deriving reliable insights from benchmark tasks.

Furthermore, the authors advocate for a modular approach in developing RL methods, highlighting the necessity of understanding the impact of each component on agent behavior and performance. They reference previous works that have noted issues of brittleness and reproducibility in deep RL algorithms and demonstrate that different code bases can lead to vastly different performance outcomes for the same algorithm.

In summary, the paper calls for a deeper investigation into the implementation details of RL algorithms, particularly the optimizations that may not be well-documented, to improve the reliability and performance of reinforcement learning methods. The code used for the study is made available for further exploration.
