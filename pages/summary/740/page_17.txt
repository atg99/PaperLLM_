This paper explores the application of the Actor-Critic method to high-dimensional static Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations, particularly focusing on the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problem in five, ten, and twenty dimensions. The authors compare two discretization schemes: a naive scheme and an adaptive step size scheme. Results demonstrate that the adaptive scheme provides a more uniform accuracy across the entire domain, especially near the boundaries, as illustrated in the error metrics presented in Table 1.

The study emphasizes the performance of the adaptive step size scheme combined with the Value Regression Least-Squares Temporal Difference (VR-LSTD) loss function for the critic. Numerical experiments reveal significant reductions in error after specific learning rate adjustments, with final errors for value functions and controls decreasing across dimensions.

Furthermore, the authors investigate the sensitivity of their algorithm to hyperparameters, specifically the parameters T and the basic step size τ, in the context of the 5D LQR example. The findings indicate that the algorithm is robust to variations in these hyperparameters.

Additionally, the paper addresses the stochastic Van der Pol oscillator, highlighting its relevance due to chaotic behavior. The authors propose a generalized stochastic version of this oscillator and aim to solve the associated partial differential equation, contributing to the understanding of dynamical systems in high-dimensional settings.
