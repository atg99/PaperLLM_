The paper discusses the application of the actor-critic method in solving stochastic optimal control problems, specifically in the context of controlled stochastic differential equations (SDEs). The generator of the controlled SDE, denoted as LuV(x), is presented alongside the boundary condition V(x) = g(x) on the boundary. It highlights that the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation aligns with the original partial differential equation (PDE), allowing the resolution of the PDE through optimal control problem solutions to derive the optimal value function.

The actor-critic framework is introduced as a dual approach to solving the optimal control problem, where the "actor" refers to the policy or control mechanism and the "critic" represents the value function that evaluates the control's effectiveness. The methodology comprises two main components: policy evaluation (critic) and policy improvement (actor). The paper emphasizes the focus on widely recognized algorithms, specifically temporal difference (TD) learning for value function evaluation and policy gradient methods for control enhancement.

In the context of discrete Markov decision processes (MDPs), the paper outlines the process beginning with an initial state sampled from a distribution. At each time step, an action is selected based on a deterministic policy, resulting in a transition to a new state governed by a probability transition function. The incurred costs are defined as a deterministic function of the current state, the chosen action, and the subsequent state. The objective is to determine the optimal policy that minimizes the expected total discounted cost, with the value function introduced to evaluate the expected cost from a given state under the specified policy.
