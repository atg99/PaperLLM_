This paper discusses the challenges in solving high-dimensional partial differential equations (PDEs), particularly focusing on Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) type equations related to optimal control problems. Traditional methods often struggle with high dimensionality and are problem-dependent, leading to limited applicability. The authors propose using artificial neural networks as a flexible and efficient tool for function approximation, highlighting the rapid advancements in this field.

The deep backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE) method is identified as a closely related approach, reformulating parabolic PDEs as control problems and leveraging deep neural networks for solutions. Although some existing methods have successfully addressed high-dimensional PDEs, they primarily focus on finite time horizons and explicit optimal controls. The authors note a gap in addressing static elliptic HJB equations with implicit controls, which is the focus of their work.

To tackle these challenges, the authors draw inspiration from reinforcement learning (RL), which has seen significant success in recent years. They reformulate fully nonlinear elliptic PDEs into stochastic control problems and apply the actor-critic framework, a popular RL approach. This method involves iteratively evaluating and improving policies (controls) until convergence, with the critic representing the value function of a given policy and the actor representing the policy function.

The proposed approach aims to contribute to the solution of high-dimensional HJB equations by leveraging techniques from RL, thus addressing the limitations of existing methods while exploring new avenues for solving implicit control problems.
