The paper discusses the actor-critic method applied to high-dimensional static Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations. The running cost function is defined, incorporating quadratic terms and interactions between state variables. An explicit formula for the true value function is derived, which leads to corresponding optimal control strategies. The controlled stochastic differential equation (SDE) reformulates the problem, with the objective function expressed in terms of expected values.

Numerical experiments are conducted with specific parameter settings, revealing that the algorithm effectively learns the value function shapes. The final errors in value functions and controls are reported for dimensions 4, 10, and 20, indicating reasonable performance. 

Additionally, the study addresses the diffusive Eikonal equation, which relates to shortest-path problems. A small diffusion term is introduced to regularize the equation and mitigate issues with kinked solutions that complicate neural network approximations in high dimensions. The paper presents the mathematical formulation of the diffusive Eikonal equation and boundary conditions, emphasizing the importance of regularization in high-dimensional settings.
