The paper discusses an adaptive step size strategy for numerical simulations involving stochastic processes. The key points of the approach are as follows:

1. **Step Size Determination**: The step size is adaptive based on the position of the trajectory. When the trajectory is in the interior of a specified domain, a constant step size is used. However, when the trajectory approaches the boundary, the step size is decreased to minimize the probability of exiting the domain in the next time step.

2. **Mathematical Formulation**: For the interior, the step size is set as a constant, while for the boundary region, it is defined based on the distance to the boundary, with a minimum threshold to balance accuracy and computational efficiency.

3. **Discretization of Integrals**: The paper outlines how to discretize integrals with the adaptive step size, highlighting that different trajectories may have varying time steps due to this adaptive approach.

4. **Policy Gradient Calculation**: Instead of directly approximating the functional derivative, the authors utilize autodiﬀerentiation through a computational graph, which simplifies the process despite the complexity introduced by the adaptive step size.

5. **Numerical Experiments**: The authors conducted experiments comparing the use of bounded samples of Brownian increments versus normal samples. They found no significant difference in results, leading them to favor the simplicity of normal samples.

Overall, the adaptive step size method is designed to efficiently handle the complexities of stochastic trajectory simulations while maintaining accuracy and computational feasibility.
