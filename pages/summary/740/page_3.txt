This paper presents an actor-critic method aimed at solving high-dimensional static Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations, with a focus on improving policy evaluation and optimization through a variance-reduced version of the Least Squares Temporal Difference (LSTD) method. The authors adapt policy gradient techniques for continuous-time stochastic control problems.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 provides the theoretical foundation for optimal control problems and formulates the actor-critic framework for continuous-time stochastic control. In this section, a fully nonlinear elliptic partial differential equation (PDE) is reformulated as an optimal control problem. This involves defining a stochastic differential equation (SDE) with a cost functional that includes both running and terminal costs, leading to the definition of an optimal value function.

In Section 3, the authors introduce a numerical algorithm tailored to solve the optimal control problem. Numerical examples illustrating the effectiveness of the proposed method are discussed in Section 4. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper with perspectives on future research directions.

The theoretical background emphasizes the connection between the reformulated elliptic equation and optimal control theory, culminating in the derivation of a time-independent HJB equation that the optimal value function must satisfy. This theoretical framework underpins the actor-critic approach proposed in the study.
