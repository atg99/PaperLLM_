The paper discusses the Actor-Critic method for high-dimensional static Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations, focusing on the approximation of the value function V through finite-dimensional functionals. The authors employ a stochastic gradient method to minimize the loss function in Least-Squares Temporal Difference (LSTD) learning, specifically comparing two approaches: variance reduced LSTD (VR-LSTD) and standard LSTD.

The stochastic gradient estimates are derived from two equations that evaluate the right-hand side terms based on a single trajectory realization. The variance of the stochastic gradient for the VR-LSTD method vanishes for the exact value function, which is a significant advantage as it reduces computational variance compared to LSTD. This property is likened to the variance reduction seen in quantum Monte Carlo methods.

The paper further explores the policy gradient approach for continuous optimal control problems, where the goal is to improve the policy using the dynamical programming principle. A loss function is introduced for the actor component that incorporates stopping times and utilizes a neural network to parametrize the control function. The parameters are optimized through stochastic gradient approximation, taking into account contributions from the control, the stochastic differential equation (SDE) trajectory, the stopping time, and the estimated value function.

Overall, the findings suggest that VR-LSTD outperforms LSTD in numerical experiments, highlighting the effectiveness of variance reduction techniques in high-dimensional control problems.
