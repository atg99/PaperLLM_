This paper discusses the Actor-Critic method as applied to high-dimensional static Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations. It presents two loss functions, L1 and L2, that are used to optimize the value function V and the function G. The paper notes that L1 does not require penalty terms to maintain consistency between V and G, as shown through the manipulation of equations that incorporate the generator of the stochastic differential equation (SDE). The authors highlight that both terms in L1 enforce that V acts as the value function and ensures consistency with G.

For implementing the neural network representation of V, the authors face challenges in applying Dirichlet boundary conditions directly. To address this, they introduce a penalty term in the loss functions to help enforce these boundary conditions.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of discretizing SDEs and stochastic integrals for numerical simulations, particularly concerning the boundary conditions which are crucial for solving the PDE problem. It discusses various strategies for handling the stopping time and exit points of SDEs, including a method where the simulation halts at the last numerical step before exiting the domain, referencing prior work in this area.
