This paper discusses the numerical approximation of value functions in the context of control problems, specifically focusing on the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problem. The authors present an error analysis for their approach using two key metrics: the error in the value function and the error in the control function. They define the density of the true value function and its neural network approximation, employing Monte Carlo sampling for estimation.

The results indicate that their method, particularly the VR-LSTD (Value Regression Least-Squares Temporal Difference), outperforms the standard LSTD in terms of accuracy. The adaptive step size scheme further enhances performance, especially in approximating the control function, as it allows for more sampling near the boundary of the problem domain.

The paper specifically addresses the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation associated with the LQR problem, presenting the exact solution and optimal control. The numerical experiments performed in five dimensions demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach, with the adaptive scheme yielding significantly improved results over the naive method.

The authors conclude that their developed code for these numerical examples is accessible on GitHub, promoting transparency and reproducibility in their research.
