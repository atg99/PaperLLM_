The paper discusses the application of the actor-critic method to high-dimensional static Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations. It begins by noting that while adaptive step size adjustments near boundaries have shown promise, two alternative approaches using constant step sizes did not yield satisfactory results. One method, inspired by previous work, attempted to refine the exit point estimation on the boundary but proved insufficiently accurate. A second method aimed to enhance accuracy through a coefficient adjustment, yet it led to instability in training.

The authors then present numerical examples to demonstrate the effectiveness of their proposed method across various problems, including linear quadratic regulator (LQR) issues, stochastic Van der Pol oscillators, and nonlinear elliptic PDEs. The algorithm operates in high-dimensional spaces, reaching up to 20 dimensions, without presuming knowledge of the true solution. Implemented in Python with TensorFlow 2.0, the algorithm employs specific configurations for training, including piecewise constant learning rates and varying batch sizes tailored to the dimensionality of the problems.

Empirical determination of parameters is emphasized, and a validation set is utilized to assess the accuracy of the value function and control. The paper concludes with the computation of relative L2 errors, indicating that the validation set size is sufficient to provide reliable error estimates with minimal variance.
