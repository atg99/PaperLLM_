The paper discusses the properties of two versions of Temporal Difference (TD) learning in a continuous setting, denoted as TDu1 and TDu2. It begins by establishing that the term involving the expectation of a martingale is valid under the given assumptions regarding boundedness of certain functions. This leads to the formulation of an equation analogous to a discrete setting, highlighting the role of the discount factor.

The authors define TDu1 and TDu2 in terms of stochastic integrals, emphasizing that both depend on the trajectory of the state variable Xt and should be considered random variables. Key results are presented, showing that if V is the exact value function for the control u, then TDu1 equals zero almost surely, while the expected value of TDu2 is also zero. The variance of TDu2, however, is positive under certain conditions, indicating that it is sensitive to approximations of the value function.

The paper introduces two loss functionals for the critic, L1 and L2, inspired by Least-Squares Temporal Difference (LSTD) methods. Both loss functionals measure the discrepancy between the expected returns and the approximated value function, incorporating the trajectory of the process and considering stopping times at domain boundaries. The distinction between TDu1 and TDu2 is framed as a martingale term, reinforcing the relationship between the two loss functionals in the context of approximate value functions.
