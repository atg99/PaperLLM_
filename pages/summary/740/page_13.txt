The paper discusses the application of the actor-critic method to solve high-dimensional static Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations, particularly focusing on the challenges posed by certain derivative terms when using a discretized approach. The authors note that while some terms related to the value function and control can be addressed through automatic differentiation, others become problematic due to the discrete approximation of stopping times, which complicates the calculation of gradients with respect to the control variable.

To tackle these issues, the paper introduces a neural network-based actor-critic algorithm specifically designed for fully nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs). The algorithm, outlined in pseudocode (Algorithm 3.1), involves both critic and actor steps, where the critic estimates the gradient of a chosen loss function and the actor updates the control parameters based on trajectory samples.

Moreover, the paper highlights the limitations of a naive uniform step size in approximating control, especially near the boundary of the domain. To enhance accuracy in these regions, the authors propose an adaptive step size scheme that reduces the time step when the trajectory approaches the boundary, drawing on techniques from existing literature on boundary value problems. This approach aims to improve the trajectory's accuracy, thereby yielding better approximations of both the value function and the control.

Overall, the paper presents a structured methodology for addressing the complexities associated with high-dimensional HJB equations through a refined actor-critic framework, emphasizing the importance of adaptive strategies in numerical implementation.
