The paper discusses the application of the Actor-Critic method to high-dimensional static Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations, emphasizing the use of dynamic programming principles. It begins by defining the value function for a given policy, which is critical for minimizing total cost. The optimal policy is identified through the minimization of the value function across all states.

The paper notes the distinction in terminology between reinforcement learning (RL) and control literature, where the former typically maximizes reward while the latter minimizes cost. This choice aligns the discussion with continuous optimal control problems. It also acknowledges the probabilistic nature of costs and policies, moving away from deterministic assumptions to better fit the continuous setting.

The method of Temporal Difference (TD) learning is introduced as a way to evaluate policies by updating the value function based on a combination of observed costs and future expected costs. The update process is detailed, highlighting the use of n-step TD learning, which allows for adjustments to the value function based on observed trajectories.

Overall, the paper outlines a structured approach to utilizing Actor-Critic methods in the context of high-dimensional static HJB equations, integrating elements of reinforcement learning and control theory to enhance policy evaluation and cost minimization.
