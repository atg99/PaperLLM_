The paper discusses various methods for improving the accuracy of numerical discretization in stochastic differential equations (SDEs), particularly in relation to boundary treatment. The authors highlight several schemes that have been proposed, including approximating exit positions and reducing step sizes near boundaries. A naive approach using the Euler-Maruyama scheme is presented, where the SDE is discretized over a partition of the interval with a constant step size.

The authors define a stopping time based on the maximum index of the discretized process that remains within the domain, referred to as the naive scheme. They note that this approach allows for a straightforward boundary treatment, although the trajectory may still be inside the domain at the stopping time. The paper further details the approximation of stochastic integrals using Monte Carlo methods, where independent sample trajectories are generated for each iteration.

To optimize the parameters of the neural networks involved in the discretization process, the Adam optimizer is utilized. Additionally, the paper emphasizes the need to differentiate the discretized functional with respect to the control in order to apply the policy gradient method effectively. Overall, the authors aim to develop a robust framework for solving value functions in the context of SDEs, distinguishing their approach from previous methods that focus on specific points in the domain.
