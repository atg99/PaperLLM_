This paper presents a novel numerical method for solving high-dimensional Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) type elliptic partial differential equations (PDEs) by leveraging an actor-critic framework inspired by reinforcement learning. The authors, Mo Zhou, Jiequn Han, and Jianfeng Lu, propose a neural network-based parametrization of the value and control functions to reformulate the HJB PDEs as optimal control problems.

In the actor-critic framework, a policy gradient approach is utilized to enhance control, while a variance-reduced least-squares temporal difference method is developed to improve the value function using stochastic calculus. To discretize the stochastic control problem effectively, an adaptive step size scheme is implemented to enhance accuracy near the domain boundaries.

The effectiveness of this method is validated through numerical examples involving up to 20 spatial dimensions, including various applications such as linear quadratic regulators, stochastic Van der Pol oscillators, diffusive Eikonal equations, and fully nonlinear elliptic PDEs derived from a regulator problem.

The introduction discusses the significance of HJB equations in optimal control problems and their theoretical background, including the dynamic programming principle and the relationship between viscosity solutions and backward stochastic differential equations. The paper addresses the limitations of traditional numerical methods in high-dimensional contexts, particularly the curse of dimensionality, and highlights the potential of dimension reduction techniques to alleviate these challenges.

Overall, this work contributes to the field by introducing a promising approach to tackle high-dimensional HJB equations, which are crucial in various applications.
