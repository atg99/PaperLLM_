This paper explores the effectiveness of Stacked Attention Networks (SANs) with a selective mechanism (SSANs) for various natural language processing tasks, including machine translation, natural language inference, and semantic role labeling. The authors set up their experiments with 10 SAN layers, a hidden size of 200, 8 attention heads, and 100-dimensional GloVe embeddings, which are updated during training. 

The experiments were conducted on multiple datasets: English-Japanese, English-Romanian, and a larger English-German corpus, with data pre-processed using byte pair encoding. The SSANs demonstrated significant improvements over traditional SANs, with accuracy increases of 0.8% and 0.5% in natural language inference and semantic role labeling tasks, respectively. In machine translation tasks, SSANs outperformed SANs across all language pairs, achieving relative improvements of 3.0% for English-Romanian, 1.9% for English-Japanese, and 3.3% for English-German.

The authors attribute these improvements to the SSANs' enhanced capabilities in word order encoding and structure modeling. They also discuss the implementation of the selection mechanism, noting that the Gumbel-Softmax approach outperformed the REINFORCE algorithm in preliminary experiments. 

The paper concludes with plans to evaluate SSANs' ability to capture local and global word orders in subsequent sections, highlighting the importance of this capability in natural language tasks.
