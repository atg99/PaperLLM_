The paper discusses advancements in Self-Attention Networks (SANs), particularly focusing on the introduction of Selective Self-Attention Networks (SSANs) to address limitations in word order encoding and syntactic structure modeling. The authors provide experimental results that highlight the effectiveness of SSANs in identifying improper word orders and producing enhanced syntactic representations through selective attention mechanisms. 

Methodologically, SANs transform input layers into queries, keys, and values to compute attention weights efficiently using dot-product attention. However, SANs face challenges in modeling word order due to their reliance on attention mechanisms without recurrence or convolution. Previous attempts to incorporate position information have not fully resolved these issues, indicating a need for improved approaches.

To enhance structure modeling, the paper notes that integrating syntactic structures into SANs can lead to better sentence representations. This includes using attention heads to focus on syntactic relationships and performing self-attention across words within phrases.

The proposed SSANs utilize a selector mechanism that allows for the selection of a subset of elements from the input sequence before applying standard self-attention. This selector is implemented using Gumbel-Softmax and operates based on a binary action sequence indicating whether to select or discard each input element. This approach aims to optimize the attention process by focusing on more relevant content words, thereby improving the overall performance of SANs in natural language understanding and generation tasks.
