The paper discusses the comparative performance of SANs (Self-Attention Networks) and SSANs (Structured Self-Attention Networks) in modeling linguistic structures and their attention distributions on different linguistic roles. Table 6 indicates that SSANs outperform SANs by an average of 4.6% across various metrics, suggesting that SSANs are more effective in parsing and generating constituency trees. The analysis highlights that SSANs assign greater attention to content words, which are crucial for semantic understanding in NLP tasks, as shown in Table 7. 

Content words, such as nouns and verbs, significantly contribute to sentence meaning and are essential in tasks like constituency tree depth and machine translation. SSANs demonstrate improved attention to these words, enhancing their performance in translating and structuring sentences. However, the paper also notes that content-free words, which hold less semantic weight, tend to receive disproportionate attention due to their frequency, particularly in structure probing tasks. The findings emphasize the importance of focusing on content words for effective linguistic modeling, with SSANs showing a clear advantage in this regard.
