The paper presents an evaluation of two models, SANs and SSANs, on their ability to capture syntactic structures through two probing tasks: Tree Depth and Top Constituent. The results indicate that SSANs outperform SANs significantly in both tasks, with an overall performance increase of 22.4% in the Tree Depth task. This improvement is particularly notable as sentence complexity increases, demonstrating SSANs' robustness in handling deeper and more complex sentence structures.

In the Tree Depth task, sentences are categorized based on the depth of their parsing trees, ranging from 5 to 11 levels. The F1 scores reveal that SANs struggle as sentence depth increases, while SSANs maintain higher accuracy across all depths. Similarly, in the Top Constituent task, which classifies sentences according to their top constituents, SSANs show a marked improvement in F1 scores, especially for complex sentence types, reaffirming their superior performance.

The paper further explores the structural capabilities of self-attention by constructing constituency trees from attention distributions. This evaluation utilizes a method where the attention scores within spans of text are used to generate binary constituency trees, which are then assessed for accuracy using standard metrics like bracketing precision and F1 score.

Overall, the findings underscore SSANs' enhanced ability to model complex syntactic structures compared to SANs, highlighting their potential in natural language processing tasks that require deep syntactic understanding.
