The paper investigates the enhancements brought by a selective mechanism in self-attention networks (SANs) for natural language processing (NLP) tasks. It introduces selective self-attention networks (SSANs), which utilize a Gumbel-Softmax implementation to improve performance across various tasks, including natural language inference, semantic role labeling, and machine translation. The authors find that SSANs consistently outperform standard SANs, attributing this improvement to the selective mechanism's ability to focus on content words that are crucial for sentence meaning.

The paper addresses two significant limitations of standard SANs: their challenges in encoding word order and modeling structure. By concentrating on relevant words, SSANs mitigate these weaknesses, leading to better performance. The study emphasizes the need for a comprehensive understanding of selective mechanisms, as previous research lacked thorough validation across diverse NLP tasks. The results underscore the effectiveness of SSANs and suggest avenues for further refinement in self-attention models. The code and data related to this research are made available for public access.
