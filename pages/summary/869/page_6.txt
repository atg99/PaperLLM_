The paper examines the performance of Selective Self-Attention Networks (SSANs) in a global word reordering detection task, building on the work by Yang et al. (2019a). The task involves detecting both the original and the new positions of a randomly relocated word within a sentence. The model architecture includes a six-layer SSAN with specified layer and filter sizes, trained on an open-source dataset.

Results indicate that all SSAN variants outperform traditional Self-Attention Networks (SANs) in prediction accuracy. Specifically, applying the selective mechanism to the first layer yields the highest performance. However, the accuracy gap between SSAN variants is narrower for global reordering detection compared to local reordering tasks, suggesting that global reordering detection may require deeper syntactic and semantic understanding, typically found in higher network layers.

The attention behavior analysis reveals that SSANs effectively focus on distant words, which is crucial for identifying global reorderings, as indicated by attention distributions that favor words more than five positions away from the query. In contrast, SANs tend to concentrate on nearby words. Visualization of attention weights shows that SSANs allocate substantial attention to the relocated word, demonstrating their enhanced capability in detecting global word reordering.

The paper also proposes further evaluations of SSANs in capturing structural information in sentences, including an analysis of syntactic structure knowledge and attention behaviors through constituency trees.
