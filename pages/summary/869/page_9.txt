This paper investigates the selective mechanism for Self-Attention Networks (SANs) by employing a Gumbel-Softmax approach. The authors demonstrate through experiments that this mechanism effectively addresses two significant limitations of SANs: encoding word order and modeling structures, both critical for natural language understanding and generation. The findings suggest that the selective mechanism can enhance performance across various tasks, including machine translation, by improving the modeling of syntactic structures. Future research will explore the applicability of these results to other SAN architectures and broader attention models.
