The paper investigates the effectiveness of Selective Self-Attention Networks (SSANs) in detecting local word reordering through a bigram order shift detection task, building on the work of Conneau et al. (2018). The task involves identifying sentences with swapped adjacent words, requiring models to differentiate between normal and abnormal word orders. The study uses a six-layer SAN model combined with a three-layer MLP classifier, achieving an accuracy of 52.23%, which exceeds previous benchmarks.

Table 2 presents the performance of SSANs across different layers, showing that all SSAN variants outperform standard SANs. Notably, applying SSANs to the first layer yields the highest improvement, with a 19.8% increase in accuracy over SANs. This significant performance gap indicates that local word order detection relies heavily on the lexical information captured in the lower layers.

The attention analysis reveals that both SANs and SSANs focus on words within a close range of the target word. However, SSANs exhibit a stronger emphasis on adjacent words compared to SANs, with attention weights of 14.6% versus 12.4%, respectively. This behavior supports the hypothesis that the selective mechanism enhances the model's ability to capture bigram patterns necessary for the task. Visualizations further illustrate that SSANs prioritize adjacent words, aiding in distinguishing abnormal word orders. Overall, the findings highlight the advantages of SSANs in understanding local word structures.
