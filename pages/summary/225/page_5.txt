The paper discusses the architecture of downsample blocks in Spiking ResNet and SEW ResNet, focusing on the mechanisms that facilitate downsampling and the handling of gradients in deep networks. In particular, it highlights the differences between standard ResNet and SEW ResNet, emphasizing the introduction of spiking neurons (SN) in the shortcut connections of SEW ResNet as a means to enhance performance and mitigate issues related to vanishing or exploding gradients.

The authors explain that when inputs and outputs of a block differ in dimensions, convolutional layers with a stride greater than one are used for downsampling instead of identity connections. They present the SEW block as an extension of the ReLU before addition block, noting that it avoids the infinite output problem typically encountered in artificial neural networks (ANNs). This is achieved because outputs in spiking neural networks (SNNs) remain bounded due to the binary nature of spikes.

The paper formulates the gradient flow through the SEW blocks, demonstrating that the gradients can be calculated layer by layer, which helps to prevent vanishing gradients. The experiments conducted focus on ImageNet classification, with results reported based on validation set accuracy due to the unavailability of the test server.

Overall, the SEW ResNet architecture offers a robust framework for deep learning in spiking neural networks by addressing key challenges associated with gradient propagation and maintaining output stability.
