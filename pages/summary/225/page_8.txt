The paper discusses the firing rates and gradient behaviors of various spiking neural network architectures, specifically SEW IAND, SEW AND, and SEW ADD within a ResNet framework. It highlights that while SEW IAND maintains an adequate firing rate across layers, SEW AND suffers from a silence problem in its deeper layers due to the difficulty of sustaining a sufficient spiking neuron (SN) output. This silence can be attributed to the nature of the AND operation, which leads to infrequent firing events, especially in deeper layers.

The paper further elaborates on the gradient flow within these networks, indicating that gradients tend to decay from deeper to shallower layers, particularly in configurations without zero initialization. The authors utilize a surrogate gradient function and explain how this influences the gradient amplitude across the network. They observe that the SEW ResNet-152 experiences vanishing gradients under certain conditions, especially in configurations without zero initialization, while the SEW ADD and IAND networks exhibit a more stable gradient flow.

Additionally, the paper introduces a compact network design, 7B-Net, tailored for the DVS Gesture classification task. This network is optimized for efficiency, comprising convolutional layers, pooling layers, and a specific spiking neuron model, the Parametric Leaky-Integrate-and-Fire neuron. The authors emphasize the importance of adjusting network architectures to suit specific datasets, as demonstrated by the transition from the larger ResNet to the more compact 7B-Net for gesture classification tasks.
