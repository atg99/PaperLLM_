The paper presents an analysis of Spiking ResNet and introduces the SEW residual block to address issues related to identity mapping and gradient problems in previous models. The SEW block enhances residual learning, allowing for deeper networks without degradation. Experimental results on datasets such as ImageNet, DVS Gesture, and CIFAR10-DVS demonstrate that the SEW ResNet outperforms earlier models, achieving higher accuracy with fewer time-steps. Comparisons reveal that the proposed method achieves 70.2% accuracy with 8 time-steps, surpassing the previous Spiking ResNet's 67.8% accuracy with 10 time-steps. While the Wide-7B-Net shows slightly lower performance than the current state-of-the-art method, it does so with significantly fewer parameters and simulation time-steps. The results suggest that the SEW block effectively mitigates the vanishing/exploding gradient issues and facilitates the development of deeper spiking neural networks.
