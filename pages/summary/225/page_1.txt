The paper discusses the optimization challenges faced by Deep Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) due to their discrete binary activations and complex dynamics. It draws inspiration from the success of Residual Networks (ResNets) in deep learning to propose a new architecture called Spike-Element-Wise (SEW) ResNet. Unlike previous Spiking ResNets that simply replaced activation layers with spiking neurons and encountered degradation issues, SEW ResNet effectively implements identity mapping and mitigates the vanishing/exploding gradient problems.

The authors present evidence that SEW ResNet outperforms state-of-the-art directly trained SNNs in terms of accuracy and time efficiency on datasets such as ImageNet, DVS Gesture, and CIFAR10-DVS. Furthermore, SEW ResNet demonstrates scalability, achieving better performance with additional layers, making it feasible to train deep SNNs with over 100 layers for the first time. The findings suggest that incorporating residual learning can significantly enhance the performance of SNNs, bridging the gap with traditional ANNs in complex tasks. The code for SEW ResNet is made publicly available, supporting further research and development in this area.
