The paper investigates the performance of Spiking ResNet and SEW ResNet architectures, specifically focusing on variations of the SEW block and their impact on training loss and test accuracy. Key findings include that while Spiking ResNet exhibits lower training loss than SEW ADD ResNet, it suffers from higher overfitting, resulting in lower test accuracy (90.97% vs. 97.92%). Different element-wise functions were evaluated, revealing that SEW ADD ResNet achieved the highest test accuracy among the models tested.

Ablation experiments conducted on the DVS Gesture dataset showed that the training loss hierarchy was SEW IAND < Spiking ResNet < SEW ADD < Plain Net < SEW AND. However, lower training loss did not correlate with higher test accuracy due to overfitting concerns. 

The comparison with state-of-the-art methods indicated that SEW ResNet outperformed others in terms of accuracy, parameter count, and simulation time-steps. For CIFAR10-DVS classification, a more complex network structure named Wide-7B-Net was employed, adapting the SEW blocks to handle the increased complexity of the dataset.

Overall, the results highlight the effectiveness of SEW ResNet architectures in achieving high accuracy while managing training dynamics, particularly in spiking neural networks.
