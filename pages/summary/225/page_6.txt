This paper presents a comparative analysis of Spiking ResNet and SEW ResNet architectures in the context of image classification on the ImageNet dataset. The authors evaluate multiple configurations of both network types, including ResNet-18, ResNet-34, ResNet-50, ResNet-101, and ResNet-152, focusing on metrics such as training loss, training accuracy, and test accuracy.

Key findings indicate that Spiking ResNet exhibits a degradation problem as network depth increases, leading to higher training loss and lower test accuracy, particularly in deeper models. In contrast, SEW ResNet demonstrates improved performance with increased depth, showing a consistent decrease in training loss and an increase in both training and test accuracy. This suggests that the SEW block effectively addresses the degradation problem associated with deeper networks.

The paper also includes a detailed comparison of test accuracies between SEW ResNet and Spiking ResNet. While Spiking ResNet-34 performs worse than its shallower counterpart, SEW ResNet-34 outperforms SEW ResNet-18. The results culminate in a demonstration that SEW ResNet architectures achieve superior performance over previous Spiking ResNets, especially for networks exceeding 100 layers.

Overall, this study highlights the advantages of SEW ResNet in achieving higher accuracy and stability during training, making it a significant advancement in the development of spiking neural networks for image classification tasks.
