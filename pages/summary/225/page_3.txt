This paper discusses the architecture of Spiking ResNet, a variant of the traditional ResNet that incorporates spiking neurons. The authors detail the structure of basic blocks in both ResNet and Spiking ResNet, highlighting the differences in activation mechanisms. The equations presented describe the discrete-time dynamics of neuron behavior, including how input current influences membrane potential and spike generation.

Key components of the equations include the Heaviside step function, which determines spike output, and the neuronal dynamics functions for different spiking models, such as Integrate-and-Fire and Leaky Integrate-and-Fire. The paper emphasizes the use of the surrogate gradient method for error backpropagation, allowing for effective training of spiking neural networks.

A significant drawback identified in Spiking ResNet is its inability to achieve identity mapping across all neuron models, which is critical for maintaining performance in deeper networks. This limitation can lead to the degradation problem, where deeper models perform worse than shallower ones. The authors suggest that while traditional ResNet utilizes shortcut connections to facilitate residual learning, Spiking ResNet struggles to implement these effectively, impacting its training efficacy.

Overall, the paper critically analyzes Spiking ResNet's architecture, addressing its potential shortcomings and the challenges of applying traditional neural network concepts to spiking models.
