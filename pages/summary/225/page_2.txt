In this paper, the authors investigate the limitations of Spiking ResNet, a spiking neural network (SNN) variant of ResNet that replaces ReLU activation layers with spiking neurons. While Spiking ResNet achieves high accuracy on various datasets when converted from artificial neural networks (ANNs), it struggles with the degradation problem during direct training. The authors demonstrate that Spiking ResNet is not universally applicable across all neuron models for achieving identity mapping and is susceptible to vanishing and exploding gradient issues.

To address these challenges, the authors propose the Spike-Element-Wise (SEW) ResNet architecture, which facilitates residual learning in SNNs effectively. They provide proof that SEW ResNet can successfully implement identity mapping while simultaneously overcoming gradient problems. Experimental evaluations on the static ImageNet dataset, as well as neuromorphic datasets like DVS Gesture and CIFAR10-DVS, reveal that deeper Spiking ResNet architectures experience increased training loss, indicating degradation. In contrast, SEW ResNet demonstrates improved performance with increased depth and outperforms existing directly trained SNNs in both accuracy and efficiency.

The paper also discusses the existing methods for training SNNs, particularly focusing on the ANN to SNN conversion and backpropagation techniques using surrogate gradients. It highlights previous efforts to incorporate residual structures in SNNs and the adaptations made to facilitate the conversion from ANNs to SNNs. The authors conclude that this work is pioneering in exploring directly-trained deep SNNs exceeding 100 layers, providing insights into the potential of SEW ResNet in enhancing the capabilities of spiking neural networks.
