In this section, the paper discusses the formulation and implementation of residual blocks in Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), specifically comparing traditional residual blocks with those in Spiking ResNet. Equation (6) defines a typical residual block in artificial neural networks (ANNs) using a ReLU activation function, which facilitates identity mapping. This is particularly effective when the function Fl(Xl) is non-negative, resulting in Yl equating to Xl.

In contrast, Spiking ResNet's residual block, as defined in Equation (7), introduces constraints that complicate identity mapping. The output Ol[t] of the spiking neuron must equal the input Sl[t] under specific conditions related to neuronal firing dynamics. The challenges arise from the inherent dynamics of spiking neurons, especially models like the Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron, where achieving the condition SN(Sl[t]) = Sl[t] becomes difficult due to the variability in the membrane time constant.

The paper highlights issues of vanishing and exploding gradients in Spiking ResNet, particularly when using sequential blocks. The gradient calculation reveals that the identity mapping condition can lead to gradient issues, especially when the spiking outputs are binary, complicating the learning process.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a new architecture called the Spike-Element-Wise (SEW) residual block, which utilizes an element-wise function g to facilitate residual learning in SNNs. The SEW block can maintain identity mapping through various functions, such as ADD and IAND, which exploit the binary nature of spikes. This innovation aims to overcome the limitations of previous Spiking ResNet designs while enabling effective residual learning.
