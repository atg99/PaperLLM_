The paper presents a comparison of various Spiking ResNet architectures on the ImageNet dataset, focusing on their accuracy and firing rates. The results indicate that SEW ResNet models generally achieve competitive accuracy, with SEW ResNet-34 attaining an accuracy of 67.04%, slightly lower than the Spiking ResNet-34 (large) with td-BN at 67.05%. However, the latter uses significantly more time-steps and parameters, suggesting that SEW ResNet models are more efficient.

The analysis of firing rates across different SEW ResNet architectures shows that firing rates are generally low, implying that most SEW blocks function close to identity mappings. For instance, the firing rates in SEW ResNet-18/34/50 remain below 0.25, indicating that these neurons fire less than one spike on average during simulations. In SEW ResNet-101/152, firing rates do not exceed 0.5, suggesting similar low activity.

Additionally, the paper discusses the gradients of SEW ResNet-152, emphasizing its capability to mitigate vanishing and exploding gradient issues. The analysis reveals that while initial firing rates can be influenced by network initialization, SEW networks maintain stable firing rates across identity mapping areas, which is crucial for effective training.

Overall, the findings highlight the viability of SEW ResNet architectures in spiking neural networks, balancing accuracy and computational efficiency while addressing gradient stability.
