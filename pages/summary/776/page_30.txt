The paper addresses the vulnerabilities of deep learning models to adversarial attacks and proposes a solution to automate the assessment of these vulnerabilities during the training phase. Malicious adversaries can exploit public datasets by introducing incorrectly labeled data, which can facilitate future attacks on models trained with such data. Additionally, adversarial attacks can manipulate input data through small perturbations, leading models to generate incorrect predictions. 

To combat these issues, the authors developed customized Callbacks that integrate vulnerability assessments into the training process, reducing the need for user intervention. Initial trials demonstrated that these Callbacks could effectively evaluate a model's vulnerability to attacks like the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM). In their experiments with a LeNet architecture on the MNIST-Digits dataset, they observed a significant drop in accuracy—from approximately 98% without adversarial attacks to below 10% when subjected to a specific level of perturbation.

The authors acknowledge contributions from colleagues and reviewers, emphasizing the collaborative nature of this research. They express gratitude to the individuals involved in the implementation and evaluation of their software system, as well as recognizing the support from funding sources. Overall, the paper highlights the importance of proactively assessing model vulnerabilities to enhance the security and privacy of deep learning applications.
