The paper compares conventional software development with machine learning development, highlighting key differences that introduce management challenges. The primary goal in traditional software development is to meet functional requirements, while in machine learning, it is to optimize metrics like accuracy. Quality in traditional development relies on the developers' expertise and the written code, whereas in machine learning, it is heavily influenced by the quality of data and hyperparameters.

The tools used in traditional development are specific to industries, while machine learning development involves a variety of libraries and algorithms. Maintenance in traditional software is less frequent and version-based, whereas machine learning requires ongoing monitoring and retraining with new data. Management practices differ as well, with traditional development using mature tools like Git, while machine learning often relies on manual methods due to a lack of robust management tools.

Deployment in traditional settings is handled by familiar software developers, while machine learning deployment necessitates collaboration between ML and software engineers. Sharing models in traditional development utilizes established platforms like GitHub, whereas machine learning sharing often occurs through less efficient means. Reproducibility is straightforward in traditional software but poses significant challenges in machine learning due to limited tools for tracking hyperparameters and model requirements.

The paper emphasizes the need for novel software tools to support the machine learning lifecycle management, given the ad-hoc and trial-and-error nature of model development. The modeling lifecycle in deep learning is complex, involving various phases that require careful consideration and understanding to improve efficiency and effectiveness in machine learning projects.
