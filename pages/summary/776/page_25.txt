The paper presents findings from a survey of 13 participants regarding the utility of an automatically generated inference function in ModelKB. Out of the participants, 10 agreed on its usefulness, including 5 who strongly agreed. However, 2 participants encountered issues with the function, specifically while training a time series model using voice data. The inference function failed due to the need for extensive external dependencies that were not automatically handled by the system. 

The system not only deploys models but also provides a web interface for testing, which participants found beneficial. A notable positive outcome was observed among researchers collaborating with medical professionals. The automatically generated inference function allowed physicians, who lacked technical expertise, to evaluate model performance without the need for manual reporting, significantly improving efficiency.

The paper also discusses model reproducibility, which is crucial for validating previous experiments. Eight participants agreed that ModelKB aids in reproducibility, with 5 strongly agreeing. While exact replication of results is challenging due to the inherent randomness in deep learning, the use of extracted metadata has shown to yield similar results with identical datasets. In experiments without a seed generator, the maximum accuracy difference was 3.7%, which is considered acceptable given the circumstances.

Lastly, ModelKB facilitates model sharing, allowing developers to upload and share trained models easily. Participants reported that this feature is very useful, with 8 participants strongly agreeing and 5 agreeing that ModelKB enhances model sharing capabilities.
