The paper evaluates the execution time overhead associated with training three neural network architectures: LeNet, ResNet50, and ResNet150, both with and without the use of ModelKB. The models were trained for varying epochs using the MNIST dataset for LeNet and the CIFAR-10 dataset for the ResNet models on a specified hardware setup. The results indicated an average increase of 2 to 6 seconds in execution time when using ModelKB, which is deemed negligible compared to the overall training time that spans several hours. Participants in the study expressed satisfaction with ModelKB, highlighting its efficiency in managing hyperparameters and metadata without requiring significant code modifications.

Furthermore, 9 out of 13 participants strongly agreed that ModelKB significantly reduces time and effort in lifecycle management tasks, while 11 participants noted that it allows them to concentrate more on modeling rather than management. The survey revealed that over 70% of participants did not previously utilize management tools due to various barriers, and among those who did, 75% found ModelKB easier to use compared to other tools.

The paper acknowledges potential threats to validity, including the limited number of participants and the use of a predefined cloud serving system for model deployment, which may not be suitable for large-scale production. Nonetheless, the diverse backgrounds of the participants and the variety of models tested provide a comprehensive representation of deep learning developers. 

The discussion also references existing lifecycle management approaches, particularly highlighting the contrast between ModelKB and graphical workflow management systems commonly used in industry.
