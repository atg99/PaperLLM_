The paper discusses the lifecycle of deep learning model development, highlighting key stages and associated challenges. The process begins with data preparation, where datasets are collected and prepared for model development. Subsequently, model search focuses on identifying reusable models through transfer learning, or creating new models if none exist. The training and evaluation phase is noted as the most resource-intensive, requiring numerous iterations to achieve satisfactory results. Once a model is trained, it must be deployed in a production environment or made accessible via APIs. Continuous maintenance is essential for deployed models to ensure performance and address defects, often involving retraining with new data.

The paper emphasizes the iterative nature of deep learning projects, which introduces complexities in managing the modeling lifecycle. Key challenges identified include the need for effective monitoring of experiments, as the process relies heavily on trial-and-error methods involving various hyperparameters and configurations. The lack of a robust management system can lead to loss of valuable metadata, resulting in wasted time and resources. Tracking experiments, analyzing results, and managing the associated artifacts are critical for optimizing future experiments and identifying high-performing models. The large volume of metadata complicates manual tracking, and developing efficient automated systems is both costly and time-consuming.
