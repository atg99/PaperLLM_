The paper discusses the critical challenges of monitoring experiments in deep learning, focusing on three primary areas: reproducibility, deployment, and sharing of models.

Reproducibility is emphasized as a significant challenge due to factors such as random weight initialization, dataset shuffling, and variations in frameworks. To reproduce a model effectively, detailed metadata from the training phase is essential, including information on data transformations, architecture, and hyperparameters. This is particularly relevant in scenarios where models need to be recreated from offline training or older versions that lack available implementations.

Deployment presents its own complexities as models transition from training to production environments. The paper notes the absence of standardized methods for this transfer, as models can be deployed in various formats such as REST services or mobile applications. Furthermore, it highlights the necessity for consistent preprocessing of data during inference and the importance of ongoing monitoring and retraining of deployed models.

Sharing is identified as a barrier to the evolution and reusability of models due to the lack of a centralized repository. Current practices often involve sharing models on personal or framework-specific websites, which can be inefficient. Although some repositories have been established, they typically require manual curation, hindering widespread accessibility.

To address these issues, the paper introduces ModelKB, a proposed system comprising a local client and a cloud-based server. ModelKB aims to streamline the management of trained models by enhancing storage, organization, and sharing capabilities. It facilitates the extraction of metadata and supports various frameworks, thereby providing a structured approach to model management throughout the deep learning lifecycle.
