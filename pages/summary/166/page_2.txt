The paper discusses advancements in generative modeling, particularly focusing on generative adversarial networks (GANs) and their role in approximating complex density functions. Traditional methods for generative modeling often rely on explicit density estimation, which can be computationally challenging, especially for high-resolution image generation. This has driven the exploration of GANs, which represent a novel approach by avoiding the need for a tractable density function and instead learning a sampling process directly.

GANs consist of two neural networks, a generator and a discriminator, that engage in a game-theoretic framework. The generator implicitly defines the model distribution without needing to evaluate the density function. This allows GANs to generate high-quality samples in a single step, contrasting with earlier methods like generative stochastic networks that utilized incremental sampling processes based on Markov chains.

The paper highlights that GANs, along with variational autoencoders and fully-visible belief networks, are among the most popular generative modeling techniques today. The interest in GANs is primarily due to their effectiveness in producing realistic images and their versatility in various tasks beyond simple generation. The research underscores the potential for GANs to tackle scenarios where the density function is computationally intractable, thus paving the way for further exploration and improvement in generative modeling techniques.
