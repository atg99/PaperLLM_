In this paper, the authors introduce LCU-Net, a convolutional neural network designed for electron microscopy image segmentation. LCU-Net is built upon the architectures of U-Net and Inception, employing concatenate operations to enhance performance. Additionally, the authors utilize Dense Conditional Random Fields (CRF) in the post-processing phase to integrate global context, resulting in improved segmentation outcomes, referred to as LCU-Net+. The performance metrics for LCU-Net+ show significant advancements over the original U-Net, with improvements in Dice, Jaccard, Precision, Recall, and Accuracy, while also reducing memory usage from 355 MB to 103 MB. 

Future work will focus on expanding the dataset and optimizing LCU-Net's computational efficiency. The authors plan to explore strategies such as the addition-based approach from AdderNet to lower computational complexity and the implementation of fuzzy binarization for enhanced training of separate deep learning models. They also consider applying Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for data augmentation.
