In the paper by J. Zhang et al., the authors explore the replacement of traditional convolution filters with sequences of 1×3 and 3×1 filters to maintain performance while reducing memory costs in neural network architectures. Specifically, they demonstrate that 3×3, 5×5, and 7×7 convolution filters can be effectively substituted with one, two, and three sequences of the proposed filters, respectively. This substitution is facilitated through concatenation operations, allowing the new BLOCK-II structure to achieve similar receptive fields as the larger filters.

The proposed BLOCK-II is integrated into LCU-Net, which is designed to enhance image segmentation tasks. The authors also implement batch normalization after each convolutional layer to mitigate internal covariate shift. A comparison with the original BLOCK-I is provided through the U-Net-BI architecture.

Additionally, the paper addresses the limitations of CNNs in capturing local dependencies by incorporating a post-processing step using Dense Conditional Random Fields (CRF). This method enhances segmentation results by establishing pairwise potentials across pixels in the image. The energy function of the Dense CRF combines unary and pairwise potentials, with the unary potential defined in terms of the label assignment probabilities from the neural network.

Overall, the study presents a novel approach to convolutional architectures and post-processing techniques aimed at improving segmentation performance while optimizing computational resources.
