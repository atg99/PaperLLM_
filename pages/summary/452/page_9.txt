The paper by J. Zhang, C. Li, S. Kosov et al. published in Pattern Recognition discusses the effectiveness of the proposed LCU-Net method for electron microscopy (EM) image segmentation, comparing its performance with other classical and state-of-the-art methods.

Key findings include the evaluation of memory and time costs for three models: U-Net, U-Net-BI, and LCU-Net. Table 4 presents that U-Net has a memory cost of 355 MB, training time of approximately 36 minutes, and an average test time of 0.05 seconds per image. In contrast, LCU-Net significantly reduces memory usage to 103 MB while maintaining similar training times. Although LCU-Net's average test time is slightly longer, the overall efficiency is notable, saving 70.99% in memory compared to U-Net.

Segmentation performance metrics are illustrated in Fig. 13, indicating that U-Net performs well, but LCU-Net and U-Net-BI outperform it, with LCU-Net achieving improvements in Dice, Jaccard, Recall, Accuracy, and VOE metrics. Although U-Net-BI has slightly better segmentation performance, LCU-Net's lower memory cost makes it a more efficient choice.

The incorporation of Dense CRF as post-processing enhances segmentation results for all methods. The evaluation of segmentation performance, detailed in Table 5 and Fig. 14, shows that improved methods effectively cover main EM parts and demonstrate superior detail acquisition compared to U-Net, with fewer under- and over-segmentation issues.

Finally, the paper compares LCU-Net with various segmentation methods, including Otsu, Canny, and SegNet. Results suggest that LCU-Net consistently outperforms these methods, with overall better segmentation performance evidenced by the average evaluation indices presented in Fig. 15. The findings indicate that LCU-Net is a promising approach for EM image segmentation, combining low memory costs, efficient processing times, and improved accuracy.
