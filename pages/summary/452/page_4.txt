In this paper, Zhang et al. discuss advancements in convolutional neural networks, particularly focusing on the Inception architecture and its adaptations in Inception-V2 and Inception-V3. The original Inception model, introduced in GoogleNet, employed multiple filter sizes to capture multi-scale objects, but it faced challenges such as increased parameters, overfitting, and vanishing gradients. Inception-V2 mitigated these issues by replacing the 5x5 filter with two 3x3 filters, which maintain the same receptive field while reducing parameters. Inception-V3 further optimized this by using a combination of 1xN and N×1 filters instead of a single N×N filter, effectively achieving similar receptive fields with fewer parameters.

The authors introduce LCU-Net to better handle multi-scale objects in electron microscopy (EM) images, which the traditional U-Net struggles with due to its limited receptive field. LCU-Net incorporates BLOCK-I, which utilizes parallel 1x1, 3x3, 5x5, and 7x7 convolution filters to enhance adaptability, although this increases memory costs. The proposed architecture draws on strategies from Inception-V2 and Inception-V3, suggesting that a sequence of three 3x3 filters can effectively replace a 7x7 filter, and a combination of 1x3 and 3x1 filters can substitute a 3x3 filter. This approach aims to optimize performance while addressing the challenges posed by EM images.
