In this paper, the authors introduce a novel Low-cost U-Net (LCU-Net) designed for the segmentation of environmental microorganisms (EMs) in images. The LCU-Net is an enhancement of the traditional U-Net architecture, incorporating elements from Inception networks and concatenation operations to address the limitations of the conventional U-Net. Specifically, it overcomes the issues of single receptive field settings and high memory costs associated with U-Net, making it more efficient for practical applications in EM image segmentation.

The study emphasizes the importance of identifying EMs, which play a crucial role in managing environmental pollution through their natural decomposition capabilities. Traditional methods for EM identification, including chemical, physical, molecular biological, and morphological approaches, each have their own disadvantages such as secondary pollution, high costs, or the need for skilled operators. Given these challenges, the authors argue for the adoption of CNNs, particularly U-Net, for their superior performance in segmentation tasks.

The experimental results presented in the paper demonstrate the effectiveness and potential of the LCU-Net in assisting microbiologists with the detection and identification of EMs, highlighting its ability to operate efficiently with smaller training datasets. Overall, the proposed LCU-Net represents a significant advancement in the field of environmental microbiology image analysis.
