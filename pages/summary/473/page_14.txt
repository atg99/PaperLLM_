The paper discusses the Mayfly Optimization Algorithm (MOA), a metaheuristic inspired by the mating behavior of mayflies, used for solving optimization problems. The algorithm initializes a population of male and female mayflies with random positions and velocities, evaluating the fitness of female mayflies using an objective function to determine the global best solution. It iteratively updates the positions and velocities of the mayflies based on a set of equations, ranks them by fitness, and selects pairs for mating through tournament selection.

Offspring are generated and assigned genders, with the least fit females replaced by the best offspring. The process continues until a stopping criterion is met, such as reaching a maximum number of iterations or achieving a set accuracy threshold. The paper notes that the enhanced MOA outperformed baseline algorithms in simulations, particularly for certain non-symmetric benchmark functions, although the improvements were not significantly better than the original algorithm for all cases. Additionally, the algorithm incorporates visualizations of feature importance and performance metrics like accuracy and F1-score to aid in understanding classification outcomes.
