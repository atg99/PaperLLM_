The cited works discuss various approaches to using machine learning and deep learning techniques for the classification and diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease through neuroimaging data. Korolev et al. explore the use of residual and plain convolutional neural networks for 3D brain MRI classification, highlighting advancements in image processing for medical applications. Ding et al. present a deep learning model that predicts Alzheimer's diagnoses using 18F-FDG PET scans, demonstrating the potential of PET imaging in diagnostic procedures. Beheshti et al. focus on structural MRI-based detection of Alzheimer's, utilizing feature ranking and classification methods to enhance accuracy. The Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) provides a valuable dataset for researchers, as noted by Naz et al., who employ transfer learning techniques for neurological disorder detection using this dataset. Zervoudakis and Tsafarakis introduce a Mayfly optimization algorithm, contributing to the computational methods in the field.
