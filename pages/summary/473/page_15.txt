The paper presents the Mayfly Optimization Algorithm for Alzheimer's Disease (AD) detection and classification using brain image datasets. The algorithm aims to identify the best feature subset for classification through a population-based approach inspired by the mating behavior of mayflies.

The algorithm begins with the initialization of a population of female and male mayflies, each representing potential solutions with random positions and velocities. The classification performance of each female mayfly is evaluated using a classifier, such as Support Vector Machine (SVM), and cross-validation is employed to determine the global best solution (gbest).

The main loop of the algorithm iteratively updates the feature subsets based on the male mayflies' positions and recalculates classification performance. Key steps include ranking the mayflies by performance, mating selected female mayflies to create offspring, and evaluating these offspring. The worst-performing solutions are replaced with the best offspring to maintain the population size.

The algorithm also emphasizes updating personal best solutions (pbest) and the global best solution (gbest) as better solutions are discovered. The process continues until stopping criteria are met, ultimately returning the subset of features associated with the highest classification performance.

Equations are integral to the algorithm, guiding the updates of velocities and positions for both male and female mayflies, as well as the selection of features based on male positions.

In summary, this algorithm leverages biological inspiration and systematic evaluation to enhance the detection and classification of AD through effective feature selection from brain imaging data.
