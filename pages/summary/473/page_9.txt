The paper discusses the reconstruction of an invisible variable v' from a sample of hidden activations h and the subsequent collection of a new set of hidden activations h'. The Gibbs algorithm's negative phase plays a crucial role in this process, particularly through the interaction between v' and h', as described in Equation (4). The paper proposes amendments to the weight matrix law, introducing an update mechanism for the weights and biases (Equations 5 to 7). A logistic activation function is utilized to normalize outputs between 0 and 1, which is essential for processing within the network.

The architecture of the Deep Belief Network (DBN) is highlighted, emphasizing the symmetric nature of the connections between visible and hidden nodes through a weight matrix and biases. The learning process is complicated when both visible and hidden units are modeled as Gaussian distributions, necessitating the use of standard deviations to manage noise levels effectively. The energy function, as indicated in Equation (2), incorporates these aspects to ensure that activities remain within acceptable limits. The training data is leveraged to estimate the probabilities of the hidden units, which is visually represented in the analysis.
