The paper discusses the data pre-processing steps taken to analyze multimodal time-series data for prognostic evaluations of Alzheimer's Disease (AD). The study utilizes a dataset comprising MRI scans of participants aged 18 to 96, including 100 diagnosed AD patients and 100 healthy controls. The MRI images, characterized by a resolution of 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm, underwent several preprocessing techniques—skull stripping, intensity normalization, and spatial normalization—using SPM12 and FSL software for segmentation into grey matter, white matter, and cerebrospinal fluid.

Data labeling was performed for binary classification based on the Clinical Dementia Ratio (CDR), where a CDR of 0 indicates no signs of dementia and a CDR of 1 indicates severe AD. The study focuses on 28 patients diagnosed with CDR 1 and an equal number of controls, using two images per patient. The methodology included a leave-one-out cross-validation approach, enhancing the reliability of the findings. The detailed description of the MRI data aims to assist other researchers in reproducing or comparing studies. The results emphasize the relationship between cognitive tests, medication, and mental health in the context of AD.
