The paper presents a novel approach for diagnosing Alzheimer's disease (AD) using a Deep Belief Network with Multi-Objective Ant Lion Optimizer (DBN-MAO). The study employs a two-phase data handling method, where half of the dataset is used for training and the other half for testing. The DBN-MAO algorithm enhances classification accuracy, which is confirmed through independent validation, showcasing its superiority over existing methods. 

The research utilizes the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) dataset, comprising 416 participants aged between 18 to 96 years, including individuals diagnosed with varying degrees of AD. The methodology involves collecting T1-weighted MRI scans, followed by data pre-processing steps such as skull stripping, intensity normalization, and spatial normalization using specialized software.

The paper emphasizes the importance of data fusion and feature ranking techniques, noting that the most significant feature for distinguishing between healthy controls and AD patients is vector size, which minimizes classification error. The proposed framework achieved an impressive diagnostic accuracy of 92.48% when tested on the ADNI dataset. Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of integrating advanced machine learning algorithms with multimodal data for improved AD diagnosis.
