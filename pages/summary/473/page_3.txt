This paper presents advancements in the classification of Alzheimer's disease (AD) using a Deep Belief Network (DBN) combined with a mayfly optimization algorithm (MOA). The research focuses on improving the accuracy of patient status classification and the ability to process voice features. The DBN employs multiple processing layers linked by weighted connections, enabling it to model complex data structures effectively.

Key contributions of the study include:
1. Evaluation of the current model's ability to predict AD progression utilizing the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) database.
2. Development of a framework for classifying dementia subjects into AD and non-AD categories using longitudinal brain MRI features alongside DBN and MOA.
3. Optimization of DBN-MOA models by incorporating a diverse range of low-cost time-series features, including patient comorbidities, cognitive scores, medication histories, and demographic data.

The paper's structure consists of a literature review in Section 2, an exploration of DBN fundamentals and the proposed DBN-MOA approach in Section 3, a comparison of results with similar studies in Section 4, and a discussion on implications and future research directions in Section 5.

The literature survey highlights various approaches to improving classification performance, including new optimization strategies and deep learning architectures. Comparisons of different models reveal significant variations in accuracy, with SVM models performing particularly well when applied to deep features extracted from medical images. The study underscores the potential of AI algorithms trained on extensive datasets for enhancing medical image analysis, while acknowledging challenges when faced with novel imaging conditions.
