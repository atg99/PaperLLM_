The paper discusses the evolving role of instrumental tests and neuroimaging techniques in the clinical diagnosis of dementia, particularly Alzheimer’s disease (AD). It highlights the significance of advanced neuroimaging methods, such as MRI, in identifying molecular and structural biomarkers that can detect early stages of dementia, including mild cognitive impairment (MCI). The diagnostic accuracy of these techniques has been shown to improve, with MRI potentially predicting the progression from MCI to AD through the detection of characteristic brain morphology abnormalities.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of multimodal biological markers, integrating various data types such as EEG, neuropsychological assessments, and genetic information to enhance diagnostic capabilities. Machine learning (ML) approaches are explored, with studies demonstrating the effectiveness of different models, including gradient boosting and recurrent neural networks, in predicting AD onset and progression. Notably, the use of deep learning models has shown promise in diagnosing AD at earlier stages.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the necessity of effective data handling, such as discretizing MRI data and managing outliers, to improve ML classification accuracy. The integration of Internet of Things (IoT) applications in the medical field is mentioned, underscoring its potential for real-time patient monitoring and data processing, which could streamline diagnostic processes and reduce human error. Overall, the research points towards a future where advanced computational techniques and neuroimaging play a critical role in the early detection and management of Alzheimer’s disease.
