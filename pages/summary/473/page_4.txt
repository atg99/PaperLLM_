This paper discusses various methodologies for detecting Alzheimer's Disease (AD) using machine learning techniques, particularly focusing on the efficacy of different models and data sources. The authors conducted trials on the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) dataset, achieving average precision rates for stable Mild Cognitive Impairment (sMCI), Alzheimer's Disease (AD), and progressive Mild Cognitive Impairment (pMCI) of 64.47%, 94.29%, and 84.66%, respectively.

The paper references multiple studies that highlight the benefits of integrating various data sources, such as MRI scans, to enhance prediction accuracy. For example, Jenugopalan et al. combined data from different modalities, training CNNs on each type and utilizing integrated classification methods like random forests and SVMs, though noted that a limited dataset size is a drawback. 

A survey table outlines key studies, summarizing their methods, performance metrics, advantages, and publication years. Notably, AI-Atroshi et al. achieved an accuracy of 90.28% using a Gaussian Mixture Model and Deep Belief Network, while Ramzan et al. reported high accuracies with ResNet-18 and transfer learning approaches. Other significant contributions include the use of Siamese CNNs to improve dataset contrast and DenseNets for their rapid gradient propagation capabilities.

The paper emphasizes the importance of employing multimodal time series data and appropriate machine learning models to enhance predictions of AD progression, suggesting that such approaches could lead to more accurate and clinically relevant outcomes.
