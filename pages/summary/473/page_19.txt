DBN-MOA method significantly enhances accuracy in Alzheimer's disease (AD) detection compared to traditional approaches. The accuracy achieved by the DBN-MOA model is 97.456%, outperforming various other models such as SVM, RF, KNN, LR, and DT, which recorded lower accuracy rates. Notably, the DBN-MOA method demonstrated an accuracy of 91.435% on a dataset of 100, while other models ranged from approximately 74.982% to 84.78%. Furthermore, with fewer than 600 data points, the DBN-MOA model reached an impressive accuracy of 97.456%, whereas the highest accuracy among the other models was 89.465%.

The study utilized the ADNI dataset, which includes data on 1029 individuals, to explore the integration of machine learning with neuroscience for improved AD classification. By leveraging a comprehensive set of features—including patients' comorbidities, cognitive scores, medication histories, and demographics—the DBN-MOA model exhibited superior performance. The findings underscore the importance of early feature fusion, particularly in combining diagnostic and therapeutic elements, and validate the potential of the DBN-MOA method to provide more accurate distinctions between healthy and ill patients, as evidenced by its high recall and precision scores. Overall, the research supports the effectiveness of the DBN-MOA approach in advancing AD detection methodologies.
