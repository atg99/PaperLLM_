The study evaluates the efficacy of an existing model in predicting the progression of Alzheimer's Disease (AD) over a 2.5-year period using data from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) database. The analysis utilized a confusion matrix to assess the model's performance, categorizing patients into true positives, true negatives, false negatives, and false positives, highlighting the critical nature of false negatives in medical diagnostics. Various performance metrics were derived from the confusion matrix, including accuracy and the Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), which measures the average discrepancy between observed and predicted values.

Results indicated that the proposed DBN-MOA method outperformed other machine learning models, such as SVM, RF, KNN, LR, and DT, in terms of precision and recall. For a dataset of 100, the precision values were 65.821%, 69.832%, 72.465%, 77.682%, and 83.312% for the respective models, while DBN-MOA achieved a precision of 94.621%. Similarly, recall for DBN-MOA was 90.162%, compared to 70.632% to 85.652% for the other models. These results demonstrate the superior performance of the DBN-MOA model across varying dataset sizes, particularly evident under 600 data points, where it recorded a recall of 95.789%. Overall, the findings underscore the potential of the DBN-MOA method in enhancing diagnostic accuracy for Alzheimer's Disease.
