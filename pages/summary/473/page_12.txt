The Mayfly Optimization Algorithm is a metaheuristic inspired by the mating behavior of mayflies, designed for optimization tasks. The algorithm starts by initializing a population of female and male mayflies with random positions and velocities. The fitness of each female is evaluated using an objective function, and the global best solution is determined as the female with the highest fitness.

The algorithm proceeds iteratively until stopping criteria are met. In each iteration, the velocities and positions of the mayflies are updated based on specific equations. The fitness of the female mayflies is re-evaluated, and they are ranked based on fitness. Mating occurs among selected pairs of females using a tournament selection method, generating offspring whose positions and velocities are also computed.

Offspring are randomly assigned genders, and the worst-performing females are replaced by the best offspring to maintain population size. The personal best solutions for each female mayfly are updated, as well as the global best if a superior solution is found. The process continues until the maximum number of iterations is reached, ultimately returning the best solution identified, which corresponds to the position of the female with the highest fitness.
