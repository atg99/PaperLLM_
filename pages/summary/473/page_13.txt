The Mayfly Optimization Algorithm (MOA) has demonstrated efficacy in addressing optimization challenges across various fields, including image processing and classification tasks. The enhancements made to the MOA algorithm significantly improved its performance over baseline algorithms in simulation experiments, particularly for non-symmetric benchmark functions, although the improvements were modest.

In adapting the MOA for Alzheimer's disease (AD) detection and classification, specific features such as cortical thickness and hippocampal volume were identified as critical for the objective function. The algorithm was meticulously tailored to optimize the selection of features relevant to distinguishing between healthy individuals and those with AD. This involved generating an initial population of solutions, evaluating their fitness based on the defined objective function, and continuously refining the solutions through iterative updates and comparisons with established methods like random forests and support vector machines (SVM).

Overall, the proposed MOA for AD detection and classification aims to effectively identify the optimal feature set from brain MRI images, thereby enhancing the accuracy of patient classification. The detailed methodology outlined in the paper elucidates the strengths and limitations of the modified algorithm in comparison to other approaches within the field.
