The paper by Alqahtani et al. focuses on improving the detection and classification of Alzheimer's disease (AD) through the integration of Deep Belief Networks (DBN) and Internet of Things (IoT) technology. The authors highlight the challenges of traditional diagnostic methods, which have only an 85% accuracy rate and often require postmortem validation. They propose a novel framework that utilizes the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) dataset, combining longitudinal brain MRI features and machine learning techniques to enhance diagnostic precision.

The study employs a DBN trained with the Mayfly Optimization Algorithm (MOA) to analyze real-time MRI data captured by an IoT-enabled imaging device. The research demonstrates that incorporating demographic data and patient comorbidities significantly improves model performance. Among various models tested, the random forest model exhibited the highest precision, while the DBN-MOA achieved impressive results with an accuracy of 97.456%, f-Score of 93.187%, recall of 95.789%, and precision of 94.621%.

The findings underscore the potential of using multimodal time-series data for AD forecasting, marking a significant advancement in the field. This research not only validates the effectiveness of the proposed DBN-MOA algorithm but also emphasizes its applicability in real-world healthcare settings for early AD detection.
