The paper presents results on data augmentation metrics, focusing on the performance of machine learning models across different epochs. Key metrics include JS Real-Fake, JS between Classes, FID, accuracy, and F1 score. The optimal configuration is identified at 3000 epochs, which yields the lowest FID and the highest validated rules, accuracy, and F1 score. The findings suggest that FID is a reliable indicator of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) quality, potentially reducing the need for extensive testing of various data augmentation strategies.

The study highlights the significance of the MAP feature, which adds value to the knowledge extraction process beyond the baseline condition based solely on FEV1. The generated rules, derived from the model, are statistically validated and can be presented to clinicians for further evaluation. This method emphasizes the role of AI in automating the rule generation process, streamlining analysis without human intervention. The rules produced include conditions related to FEV1 and MAP, indicating classifications of high and low risk based on specific thresholds. Future work will focus on clinician validation of these findings.
