The paper discusses a data augmentation approach in the context of medical monitoring, particularly focusing on physiological signals collected from a patient over a three-month period. It emphasizes the use of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) to assess model evolution under different data conditions, specifically comparing baseline data to augmented data. 

Data was collected daily from various sensors, measuring parameters such as oxygen levels, body temperature, heart rate, weight, Body Mass Index, forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1), Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF), and blood pressure metrics. The classification problem is centered on PEF values, where a threshold of 400 liters per minute is established.

The baseline data consists of 43 samples collected from a single 45-year-old male patient. The study highlights the challenges of limited data availability in clinical practice and utilizes Fisher’s Exact Test for statistical validation of the inferred rules. These rules, derived from the data, facilitate the stratification of physiological signals, showcasing the multivariate nature of the dataset.

The paper proposes a novel approach to data augmentation, emphasizing subject-specific data handling to maintain the integrity and correlation of variables within individual subjects. This contrasts with traditional augmentation methods that often rely on univariate traces. The implications of this approach align with the principles of precision medicine, where treatment is tailored to the unique characteristics of each patient based on comprehensive data analysis.
