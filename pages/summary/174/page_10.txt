The paper discusses the evaluation of synthetic datasets generated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) using the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score, Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence, and explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) metrics. The FID score is a measure that compares real images with generated ones, assuming a Gaussian distribution for both. Lower FID values indicate better quality and diversity of the generated data, with sensitivity to mode collapse, which occurs when the GAN produces limited variations within classes.

The authors focus on a bioinformatics context, specifically modeling disease severity, which presents unique challenges compared to typical image analytics applications of GANs. They emphasize the need for coordination between the synthetic dataset and the reliability of XAI, aiming to validate the significance of rules derived from synthetic data against real data.

In their methodology, they define baseline models (rules from real data) and candidate augmented models (rules from synthetic data), which are statistically validated using tests such as the Fischer test, accuracy, and F1 score. The success of the candidate model hinges on its performance when tested against real data, leading to the creation of an augmented model.

The goal is to explore potential correlations between JS, FID, and XAI metrics, particularly in predicting the quality of XAI models based on JS or FID scores without direct testing. Preliminary results from experiments involving data augmentation across different epochs are presented, with a total of one thousand synthetic samples generated. The findings are summarized in a table and figure, indicating the outcomes of the analysis.
