The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of a Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) aimed at generating synthetic datasets. The training process is designed to achieve a game equilibrium where the generator's outputs become indistinguishable from real data. The pseudo-code provided outlines the structure of the GAN, detailing the configurations for the discriminator and generator hidden layers, as well as the training loop that iterates through a maximum number of epochs to optimize performance.

In terms of evaluation metrics, the paper highlights the challenges in assessing GAN performance due to the absence of a conventional objective function. To address this, it employs Jensen–Shannon divergence and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) to measure the quality and diversity of the generated synthetic datasets. These metrics, while primarily used in image data augmentation, provide a foundation for evaluating the GAN's effectiveness in generating synthetic data. The Jensen–Shannon divergence formula is specifically noted, emphasizing its role in comparing probability distributions.

The paper suggests that while GAN applications in image processing are well-established, their potential in the biomedical sector remains an area ripe for exploration.
