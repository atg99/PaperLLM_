The paper discusses the design and implementation of a smart healthcare monitoring system using Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) and artificial intelligence. The system utilizes a central node, specifically a Raspberry Pi 4 Model B, to collect data from various physical sensors distributed across different environments such as homes and offices. These sensors measure parameters including temperature, humidity, atmospheric pressure, air speed, and concentrations of pollutants like CO, CO2, formaldehyde, and fine dust.

The communication between the sensors and the central node employs the Message Queuing Telemetry Transport (MQTT) protocol, allowing data retrieval every five minutes and aggregation into packets sent to a web server every fifteen minutes. Although the data from these sensors is not analyzed in this initial implementation, future work will focus on processing this data to observe and analyze the relationship between patient health metrics and environmental conditions.

The paper further explores the application of artificial intelligence to extract knowledge regarding the quality of medical treatment based on sensor data. A machine learning framework is proposed, where a prediction function models the relationship between sensor measurements and disease progression. This involves a supervised learning approach aimed at identifying medical key performance indicators relevant to the disease being monitored.

The authors emphasize the importance of eXplainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) in making machine learning processes understandable to healthcare professionals. The system's performance evaluation involves comparing rules derived from the model under different conditions, enabling insights into treatment effectiveness. The classification problem is framed as finding optimal boundary functions to distinguish between effective and ineffective treatments, with the rules structured in a logical format to facilitate interpretability.
