This paper reviews various applications of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in medical image segmentation, specifically focusing on brain tumors, among other medical conditions. It highlights the challenges of automatic segmentation in MRI imaging due to the need for high-level feature extraction.

1. **Small Kernel CNN**: This approach uses patch-wise training with small 3x3 filter sizes for glioma segmentation, allowing for deep architectures while maintaining receptive fields. Two models were created for high and low gliomas, achieving notable performance in the BRATS-2015 challenge through data augmentation techniques like rotation.

2. **Fully Blown CNN**: This method enhances the segmentation of sub-cortical brain structures by applying a Markov random field on CNN outputs to ensure volumetric homogeneity, outperforming several existing methods.

3. **Multipath CNN**: A dual pathway model for automatic segmentation of MS lesions was introduced, featuring convolutional and deconvolutional pathways. It utilized convolutional RBMs for pretraining on a GPU-accelerated system, demonstrating improved true positive and false positive rates, though it had a lower Dice Similarity Coefficient compared to other models.

4. **Cascaded CNN**: This model addresses imbalanced label distributions through two-phase training, allowing for simultaneous learning of global contextual features and local detailed features.

The paper also includes a summary table of CNN applications in medical image classification, detailing different architectures, preprocessing methods, datasets, and performance metrics, indicating their effectiveness across various medical imaging tasks. The findings underscore the advancements and potential of CNNs in enhancing medical image analysis and segmentation.
