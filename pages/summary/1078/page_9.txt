The paper discusses advancements in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for classifying HEp-2 cells and breast tumors. Key findings include:

1. **HEp-2 Cell Classification**:
   - **CUDA ConvNet CNN**: Preprocessing techniques such as histogram equalization and zero-mean normalization led to a 10% increase in classification accuracy, achieving an average accuracy of 80.3%, surpassing the previous best of 75.6%. The Caffe library was utilized for feature extraction.
   - **Six-layer CNN**: A framework involving image preprocessing, network training, and classification achieved a mean accuracy of 96.7% on the ICPR-2012 dataset, showcasing the effectiveness of augmentation and preprocessing.

2. **Breast Tumor Classification**:
   - **Stopping Monitoring CNN**: Proposed to reduce computation time while maintaining accuracy, this method achieved an AUC of 82.2% for tumor classification, outperforming traditional methods like HOG.
   - **Ensemble CNN**: By fine-tuning a model similar to AlexNet on a limited dataset, the method achieved 96.7% accuracy. It effectively extracted features for use in SVM classifiers, outperforming bag-of-words and HOG techniques.
   - **Semi-supervised CNN**: This approach addressed the challenge of sparse labeled data by employing a graph-based technique, achieving an AUC of 88% and accuracy of 82%, compared to 85% and 80% for SVM and ANN, respectively.

The paper highlights the significant improvements in classification accuracy and efficiency achievable through advanced CNN architectures and preprocessing techniques for both HEp-2 cells and breast tumors.
