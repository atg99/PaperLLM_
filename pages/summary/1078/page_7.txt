The table provides a comparative overview of various existing Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) frameworks, highlighting their respective strengths and weaknesses. 

- **Caffe**: Developed at the Berkeley Vision and Learning Centre, it is known for its cross-platform deployment and fast performance but struggles with usability outside of ConvNets and new architectures.
- **Keras**: Created by Francois Chollet at Google, it is user-friendly and has a large community but lacks support for multi-node training and cannot function efficiently as a standalone framework.
- **TensorFlow**: Developed by Google Brain, it features a modular architecture and supports multiple platforms. However, it is slower than competitors and lacks many pre-trained models.
- **Torch**: Originating from New York University, it has a modular setup and helpful error messages but is challenging to integrate due to its Lua implementation.
- **PyTorch**: Developed by Facebook's AI group, it is fast and easy to debug, making it suitable for research but relies on external libraries for visualization and lacks distributed training capabilities.
- **Theano**: Known for its expressive syntax and support for high-level wrappers, it is limited by its need for secondary libraries and less helpful error messages.
- **Neon**: Created by Nervana Systems, it excels in speed but suffers from dependency issues and a lack of user-friendly architecture.
- **Deeplearning4j**: A Java-based framework that matches Caffe's performance but is less popular due to Java's unpopularity in the machine learning community.
- **Cognitive Toolkit (CNTK)**: Developed by Microsoft, it offers fast and accurate performance with support for distributed training but lacks visualization tools and can be prone to crashes.

This summary captures the essential features and drawbacks of each framework, providing insights for selecting appropriate tools for deep learning projects.
