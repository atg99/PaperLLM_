This paper discusses the evaluation of latent representations learned from different modalities within a dataset. It introduces a measure, γS(M,N), that assesses the quality of these representations, demonstrating through Theorem 1 that incorporating additional modalities can enhance learning by reducing population risk. The paper also addresses the Radamacher complexity of function classes, establishing that it is typically bounded by the intrinsic properties of these classes divided by the sample size.

In section 4.2, the authors provide an upper bound for the closeness of the learned latent representation to the true representation, formalized in Theorem 2. This theorem indicates that the quality of latent representation can be controlled during the training process, with empirical risk minimizers being influenced by the choice of modalities.

The paper emphasizes that optimizing over larger function classes leads to lower empirical risk, supporting the principle that learning with additional modalities can improve latent representation quality. This principle is particularly relevant when sample sizes are large, as it suggests that the intrinsic complexity of function classes diminishes in impact, thus making the case for utilizing more modalities to optimize empirical risk.

The discussion also highlights the role of intrinsic properties of hypothesis function classes, noting that over-parameterization in deep learning may not adversely affect complexity when additional modalities are employed. Overall, the paper provides theoretical underpinnings for the advantages of multimodal learning in improving representation quality and performance in machine learning tasks.
