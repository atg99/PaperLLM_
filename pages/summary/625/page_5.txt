The paper discusses several assumptions related to loss functions and the implications for representation learning. Assumption 1 establishes a regularity condition that is essential for theoretical analysis. Assumption 2, known as the realizability condition, ensures that the function class being optimized includes the true latent representation and task mapping. Assumption 3 states that for any function g′ in the class G′ and a subset M of modalities, the composition g′◦p′M remains in G′, indicating that the inclusion relationship among modality subsets is preserved in the latent function class.

The paper further explores the quality of latent representations in relation to multi-modal learning. It introduces a definition for latent representation quality, which quantifies the effectiveness of a learned representation g by measuring the loss incurred due to the distance between g and the optimal representation g∗. This is captured by the infimum of the population risk achievable with the fixed representation g.

Moreover, the Rademacher complexity measure is introduced to assess model complexity and is used to evaluate the population risk performance across different modalities. The paper presents Theorem 1, which establishes a bound on the difference in empirical risks when training on distinct subsets of modalities. This theorem demonstrates that the performance gap between the two representations can be quantified and is influenced by the quality of the learned latent representations as well as the complexity of the function classes involved.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of latent space representation in multi-modal learning and provides formal definitions and results that connect representation quality to empirical risk minimization.
