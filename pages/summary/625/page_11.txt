The references listed provide a comprehensive overview of various studies and advancements in machine learning, particularly focusing on multimodal learning, audio-visual datasets, and classification techniques. 

1. Amini et al. discuss multilingual text categorization using multiple partially observed views.
2. Anderson et al. explore vision-and-language navigation by interpreting navigation instructions in real environments.
3. Baltrušaitis et al. present a survey on multimodal machine learning and its taxonomy.
4. Bartlett et al. introduce spectrally-normalized margin bounds for neural networks.
5. Bartlett and Mendelson address Rademacher and Gaussian complexities in risk bounds.
6. Busso et al. describe the IEMOCAP database for interactive emotional dyadic motion capture.
7. Cavallanti et al. propose linear algorithms for online multitask classification.
8. Chen et al. present VGGSound, a large-scale audio-visual dataset.
9. Du et al. improve multi-modal learning using uni-modal teachers.
10. Du et al. also investigate few-shot learning through representation learning.
11. Ephrat et al. develop an audio-visual model for speech separation in noisy environments.
12. Fayek and Kumar focus on large-scale audiovisual learning with weakly labeled data.
13. Federici et al. address learning robust representations via multi-view information bottleneck.
14. Feichtenhofer et al. fuse convolutional two-stream networks for video action recognition.
15. Gat et al. work on removing bias in multi-modal classifiers through regularization.
16. Gemmeke et al. introduce AudioSet, an ontology and dataset for audio events.
17. Goyal et al. emphasize the importance of image understanding in visual question answering.
18. Gupta et al. explore cross-modal distillation for supervision transfer.

These references collectively highlight the ongoing research and methodologies aimed at enhancing the interplay between different modalities in machine learning applications.
