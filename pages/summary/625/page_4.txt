The paper introduces a framework for multi-modal learning, defining an extended representation space X′ derived from the original multi-modal input X. The mapping pM is established to relate elements from X to X′, allowing for the exclusion of certain modalities by designating them as ⊥. A function class GM is defined, which maps the input space to a latent space Z based on the selected modalities.

The learning objective follows the Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) principle, aiming to jointly minimize empirical risk through a function h from a hypothesis space H and a function gM from GM. The empirical risk is represented as a loss function over a dataset S, and the corresponding population risk is defined for performance evaluation.

The paper highlights a specific example in video classification using a late-fusion model, where various modalities such as RGB frames and audio are processed through distinct deep networks before being combined for classification. The authors argue for the composite multi-modal framework's necessity, citing its prevalence in recent literature, while noting the absence of a formal theoretical foundation in existing works.

A critical aspect of multi-modal learning discussed is the inherent heterogeneity among modalities, allowing for varying relationships and dimensions across different data sources. This contrasts with previous analyses that assumed each modality was sufficient for the task at hand, which may not be valid in all cases, particularly in applications like object detection.

The paper proceeds to outline key theoretical results supporting the assertion that multi-modal approaches outperform single-modality methods, underpinned by specific assumptions regarding the properties of the loss function and the existence of true latent representations within defined function classes.
