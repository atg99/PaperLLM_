The paper titled "What Makes Multi-modal Learning Better than Single (Provably)" by Yu Huang et al. investigates the advantages of multi-modal learning over uni-modal learning, particularly in the context of deep learning. While empirical evidence suggests that models combining different modalities tend to outperform those using a single modality, the authors aim to provide a theoretical foundation for this observation.

The study focuses on a prominent multi-modal fusion framework that encodes features from various modalities into a shared latent space, which is then mapped to the task space. The authors prove that utilizing multiple modalities reduces the population risk compared to using only a subset of these modalities. The core argument presented is that multi-modal learning offers a more precise estimate of latent space representations, leading to improved performance.

This research is significant as it addresses a gap in theoretical understanding within the field of deep multi-modal learning, which has been largely empirical thus far. Previous studies have imposed strict assumptions regarding probability distributions across modalities, which may not reflect real-world scenarios. The authors emphasize that their findings provide a formal guarantee for the effectiveness of multi-modal learning, supported by experimental results.

In summary, this paper offers a theoretical justification for the enhanced performance of multi-modal learning, highlighting its potential in various applications while addressing the limitations of prior works.
