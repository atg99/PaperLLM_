The referenced works span various aspects of multi-modality learning, meta-learning, and transfer learning. 

Xinwei Sun et al. propose an information-theoretic framework for semi-supervised multi-modality learning, emphasizing the integration of diverse data types to enhance model performance. Tripuraneni and colleagues explore the principles of meta-learning, providing a theoretical foundation for linear representations and highlighting the significance of task diversity in transfer learning.

Tsai et al. discuss self-supervised learning from a multi-view perspective, aiming to leverage multiple data representations for improved learning efficiency. Wang, Tran, and Feiszli investigate the challenges in training multi-modal classification networks, identifying key factors that complicate the process.

Wang et al. introduce a novel approach for deep multimodal fusion through channel exchanging, which enhances the synergy between different modalities. Xu et al. provide a comprehensive survey on multi-view learning, synthesizing existing methodologies and their applications.

Other notable contributions include Xu et al.'s work on cross-modal deep representations for pedestrian detection, Yang et al.'s auxiliary information regularized machine for multi-modality feature learning, and the development of CPM-Nets for cross partial multi-view learning by Zhang et al. 

Zhao et al. present an innovative approach that combines auditory and visual data, while Zhou et al. focus on a multi-modal ensemble SVM classifier for early dementia diagnosis using neuroimaging data, demonstrating the practical applications of multi-modality in medical contexts. 

Overall, these studies contribute to the understanding and advancement of multi-modal learning techniques across various domains.
