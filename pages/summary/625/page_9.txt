The study investigates the impact of sample size on the quality of latent representations in a multi-modal context. Results presented in Table 2 indicate that as the sample size increases, the latent representation quality, measured by test accuracy, improves. This aligns with the theoretical upper bound for latent quality, suggesting a relationship proportional to the square root of the sample size. Notably, when using a combination of Text, Video, and Audio modalities, performance lags behind uni-modal systems at smaller sample sizes. This is explained by the inherent complexity introduced by multiple modalities, which can hinder latent representation quality when training data is scarce.

The research also explores the effects of modality correlation on latent representation quality through synthetic data generation. Three correlation scenarios are examined: (i) modalities with no shared information, (ii) modalities sharing all information with no unique content, and (iii) a balanced scenario with both shared and unique information. The synthetic dataset is created with four modalities, where the degree of overlap is controlled by a parameter w. The training utilizes a multi-layer perceptron neural network, with input dimensions adjusted based on the number of modalities.

In summary, the findings underscore the importance of sample size and modality correlation in achieving effective latent representations in multi-modal learning environments.
