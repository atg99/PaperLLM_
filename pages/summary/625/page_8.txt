This paper discusses the effectiveness of multi-modal late-fusion training in improving classification accuracy in emotion recognition tasks using the IEMOCAP dataset. The authors utilize the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.01 and a batch size of 2048, measuring performance through top-1 accuracy. The results demonstrate that combining multiple modalities—text, video, and audio—consistently outperforms single-modal approaches.

In Table 1, the classification accuracy for various combinations of modalities is presented, revealing that the inclusion of additional modalities enhances performance. The authors validate their theoretical findings, particularly Theorem 1, by analyzing accuracy differences between different modality subsets, as shown in Table 3. The empirical evaluation of latent representation quality further supports the notion that increased modality combinations lead to improved accuracy.

The paper also discusses the relationship between sample size and latent representation quality, illustrating how performance improves with larger sample sizes across different modalities. The results in Table 3 confirm the theoretical analysis of Theorem 2, indicating that the larger set of modalities allows for better latent space exploration compared to its subsets.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of multi-modal approaches in enhancing classification accuracy and the role of latent representation quality in this process.
