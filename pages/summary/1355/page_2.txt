The paper discusses the implementation of large language models (LLMs) in the context of autonomous driving (AD) by focusing on prompt conditioning and design. It outlines a structured approach for responding to user commands for self-driving vehicles by breaking down the response into eight specific steps, each addressing critical aspects of the command. 

The first step emphasizes the need for external perception systems, which include sensors that detect the surrounding environment. The second step assesses whether in-cabin monitoring is necessary, involving various internal vehicle systems. Subsequent steps evaluate additional factors related to safety, legality, and operational requirements of the vehicle.

The paper provides examples to illustrate this process, such as commands to call a friend or make an illegal U-turn. Each example demonstrates how the steps lead to a structured output indicating the necessity of certain systems and the potential risks associated with the command.

The authors highlight the importance of effective prompting strategies, noting that traditional methods may not suffice for complex tasks. They reference chain-of-thought prompting, which enhances reasoning capabilities in LLMs by providing a sequence of thoughtful demonstrations. This approach is particularly relevant in human-centric AD, where understanding user intent through natural language is vital.

Recent datasets that incorporate text with autonomous driving tasks are also mentioned, suggesting a growing field that aims to integrate human intent into the planning and execution of autonomous systems. The paper concludes that leveraging LLMs can significantly improve the interaction between humans and autonomous vehicles, paving the way for more intuitive and responsive driving systems.
