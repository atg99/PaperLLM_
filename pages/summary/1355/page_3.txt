The paper presents a performance comparison of various large language models (LLMs) in handling user commands within autonomous driving (AD) systems. The models evaluated include GPT-3.5, GPT-4, CodeLlama-34b-Instruct, and Llama-2-70b-Chat, with results measured against random guessing and rule-based methods. The accuracy of the models varies significantly, with GPT-4 achieving the highest overall accuracy of 38.03%, followed by GPT-3.5 at 36.21%. 

The study emphasizes the importance of LLMs in enhancing the understanding of user intent and driving nuances, particularly in complex driving scenarios. It highlights how LLMs can improve decision-making and personalize driving experiences through verbal feedback. The methodology involves inputting user commands and having the models provide binary responses ('Yes' or 'No') to eight specific questions related to perception, monitoring, localization, control, network access, entertainment, privacy, and traffic laws.

Data for the evaluation came from the UCU Dataset, which included 1,099 in-cabin user commands. The paper outlines the experimental setup, metrics for evaluation, and the significance of few-shot learning in optimizing model accuracy. Overall, the findings suggest that integrating LLMs into AD systems can significantly enhance their capability to interpret user commands and improve operational safety and efficacy.
