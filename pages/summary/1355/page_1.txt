The paper discusses the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into autonomous driving systems to enhance the understanding of user commands, particularly in complex scenarios. The authors emphasize the importance of accurately interpreting user intent for successful human-centric adoption of autonomous driving technologies. They propose leveraging LLMs' reasoning capabilities to infer system requirements from natural language commands given by users in the vehicle.

The research includes experiments with various LLM models and prompt designs, focusing on their ability to perform few-shot multivariate binary classification of commands. The findings suggest that while LLMs generally demonstrate a strong understanding of prompts, their effectiveness is significantly influenced by the quality of the models and the design of the prompts used.

The introduction highlights the advancements in autonomous driving, pointing out the need for a human-centered approach that incorporates AI's reasoning abilities to ensure effective communication between humans and autonomous systems. The authors note that current challenges include facilitating natural language interactions, which can be addressed through the application of pre-trained models and few-shot learning techniques.

The paper also references related research on the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, noting their potential for sophisticated tasks when properly utilized. The authors aim to condition LLMs for the specific task of classifying user commands in autonomous driving contexts, thereby improving the interaction between users and these systems.
