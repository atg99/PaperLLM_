The paper discusses various optimization strategies in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), summarizing key aspects that address early challenges in the field. The four main strategies highlighted are:

1. **Modular Search Space**: This approach simplifies the search from a global perspective to a modular one, treating the neural architecture as a combination of multiple types of modules. By employing a cell or block-based search space, the complexity of the search task is reduced, allowing for the stacking of small cell structures to form a complete neural architecture. This modularity enhances flexibility and adaptability across different datasets.

2. **Continuous Search Strategy**: Unlike discrete search strategies, this method allows for the continuous relaxation of structural parameters, enabling gradient optimization similar to network parameters, which can streamline the search process.

3. **Neural Architecture Recycling**: This strategy builds upon existing high-performance architectures, utilizing NAS methods for network transformations to enhance performance, rather than starting from scratch.

4. **Incomplete Training**: This approach focuses on expediting the performance ranking of candidate architectures by leveraging shared structures or performance predictions, thereby reducing the resource demands associated with fully training all candidates.

The design of the search space is critical, as it influences both the freedom of the search and the potential performance limits of the NAS algorithms. The modular search space, particularly through cell-based architectures, draws inspiration from successful artificially designed architectures, which often consist of repeated small structures known as cells. An example provided is NASNet, which utilizes normal and reduction cells to construct effective neural architectures. This method not only enhances performance but also improves generalizability across tasks.
