The paper provides a thorough examination of Neural Architecture Search (NAS), highlighting both challenges and solutions within the field. It discusses the use of unit operations in neural architectures, particularly in densely connected models like Dpp-net, emphasizing the importance of low-level feature extraction and downsampling through repeated operations.

The authors note that while stacking identical cells simplifies training and searching, it can lead to poor generalization. This insight underlines the significance of cell-based NAS, with references to various related works. The paper also introduces FPNAS, which enhances performance by diversifying the blocks during stacking and treats the search as a bi-level optimization problem, thereby reducing search costs significantly.

FBNet is mentioned as a method that explores a layer-wise search space, fixing the macro-architecture while allowing for varied block structures. This modular approach to NAS is identified as a way to make the search process more efficient compared to global search strategies, although the latter still holds value due to its flexibility.

The paper addresses the high computational demands associated with NAS, citing examples like NASNet and AmoebaNet-A, which utilized extensive GPU resources. It critiques mainstream search methods for treating NAS as a black-box optimization problem in a discrete context, which contributes to inefficiencies.

To mitigate these challenges, the paper discusses the Differentiable Architecture Search (DAS) approach, which transforms the discrete architecture space into a continuously differentiable format, allowing for gradient-based optimization. Additionally, MaskConnect critiques the fixed connection methods in existing cell-based architectures, suggesting the need for more adaptable connection strategies. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of the current landscape of NAS and proposes avenues for future research and improvement.
