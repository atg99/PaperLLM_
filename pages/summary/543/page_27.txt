This paper presents a comprehensive survey of Neural Architecture Search (NAS), highlighting its challenges and potential solutions. It emphasizes the role of NAS in enhancing image classification accuracy while reducing search costs. The authors discuss future applications of NAS in complex areas such as multi-object architecture search, network transformation, model compression, target detection, and segmentation.

RobNet is mentioned as an example of a NAS method that generates diverse neural architectures by analyzing successful and unsuccessful designs. The paper suggests that leveraging robust structural features from high-performing architectures could refine the search space, allowing for a more autonomous adjustment of network structures without imposed constraints.

Additionally, the paper addresses the challenge of setting non-architecture hyperparameters, which significantly impact network performance. The integration of these hyperparameters into the NAS framework is proposed as a promising direction, with recent experiments indicating potential for discovering higher accuracy architecture combinations.

The authors conclude that while NAS is a promising development in neural network design, it remains in its early stages. Further theoretical and experimental work is needed to understand which designs yield improved performance. The survey also outlines the characteristics of early NAS efforts and provides a systematic analysis of existing work, raising important questions for future research.

Overall, this survey serves as a valuable resource for understanding the current landscape of NAS and guiding future investigations in this field.
