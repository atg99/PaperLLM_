The paper discusses the Efficient Neural Architecture Search (ENAS) mechanism, which introduces a parameter sharing approach for neural architecture search (NAS). In NAS, candidate architectures are represented as directed acyclic subgraphs within a larger supercomputing graph, where nodes correspond to local computations and edges indicate information flow. ENAS allows for the sharing of parameters among different candidate architectures, enhancing efficiency by avoiding the need to train each architecture from scratch. This method utilizes Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks as controllers to identify optimal subgraphs, thus improving performance rankings based on empirical results. 

ENAS's parameter sharing concept reflects principles from transfer learning, where weights from one model can benefit others. This innovative approach has led to significant advancements in search efficiency compared to previous methods. Following ENAS, the Continuous Architecture Search (CAS) further builds on this framework, addressing challenges related to multi-task learning and the "forgetting" problem in continuous learning settings. The findings underscore the potential of ENAS and its extensions in optimizing neural architecture search processes.
