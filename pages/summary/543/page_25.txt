The paper provides a comprehensive survey on Neural Architecture Search (NAS), focusing on the challenges and solutions in the field. It includes a performance comparison of various state-of-the-art NAS methods against mainstream artificial networks on the ImageNet dataset, highlighting key metrics such as accuracy, parameter count, and resource requirements. For instance, methods like AmoebaNet-A and PNAS demonstrate high accuracy with significant parameter sizes, while other approaches like DARTS and ProxylessNAS showcase efficiency in resource usage.

The paper notes that while NAS techniques can yield competitive results, the performance improvements are generally smaller than those achieved through manually designed networks. It emphasizes the potential of NAS to alleviate the burdensome process of manual design and the hope that it may lead to innovative architectures that diverge from traditional designs.

Future directions in NAS are discussed, suggesting the need for further exploration into efficient search strategies and the direct application of NAS on large datasets without relying on smaller agent tasks. The overall goal is to enhance the capabilities of NAS in creating advanced neural architectures that can surpass existing designs.
