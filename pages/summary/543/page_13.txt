This paper presents a comprehensive survey of Neural Architecture Search (NAS), focusing on the challenges and solutions related to gradient-based methods such as GDAS and DARTS. GDAS utilizes a differentiable architecture sampler to optimize a single subgraph within a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) during each training iteration, which enhances search efficiency by minimizing the optimization burden. In contrast, PC-DARTS improves upon DARTS by sampling channels rather than subgraphs, using edge normalization to mitigate issues arising from inconsistent information, thus achieving greater memory efficiency and stability.

The paper also highlights problems associated with local optimization in gradient-based methods. Experimental results indicate that a suitable learning rate can lead to better convergence. Variants such as I-DARTS address local decision-making biases by relaxing softmax constraints, while PC-DARTS's channel sampling helps reduce the likelihood of local optima. Despite advancements, local optimization remains a significant challenge, necessitating further research in this area. Overall, the survey emphasizes the ongoing need for improvements in NAS methodologies, particularly those derived from DARTS.
