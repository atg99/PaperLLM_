The paper discusses advancements and challenges in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), particularly focusing on parameter sharing techniques and their implications for multi-task learning and generative adversarial networks (GANs). It highlights the benefits of Continuous Architecture Search (CAS) in leveraging prior task experience for improved model training on new tasks, which is crucial for multi-task NAS.

AutoGAN is noted for integrating NAS into GANs, utilizing the Inception score as a reinforcement learning reward to enhance search efficiency via parameter sharing and dynamic resetting. Furthermore, the introduction of multi-level architecture search (MLAS) through progressive GAN training positions AutoGAN competitively against leading manual GANs.

The paper elaborates on the One-Shot NAS methods, particularly SMASH, which employs a HyperNet to generate weights for various neural architectures. This approach allows for quicker identification of optimal architectures without the need for extensive training of each candidate. Understanding One-Shot Models critically analyzes the effectiveness of parameter sharing in SMASH and ENAS, suggesting that satisfactory results can be achieved without complex mechanisms like HyperNet or reinforcement learning controllers.

Graph HyperNetwork (GHN) is presented as a more advanced alternative to SMASH, utilizing computation graphs to enable faster and more accurate architecture performance predictions. However, the paper also notes the inherent difficulties in identifying the best architecture from sampled candidates due to the coupling of shared parameters and architectural parameters, which can skew results toward simpler networks. SETN addresses this issue with uniformly stochastic training to mitigate biases in the search process.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of parameter sharing in NAS while acknowledging the challenges that arise from it, particularly in achieving a balance between complexity and performance in architecture searches.
