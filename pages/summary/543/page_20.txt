The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) strategies, focusing on the SETN approach, which treats candidate architectures fairly by ensuring they are fully trained for accurate verification performance. SETN incorporates a template architecture estimator that evaluates the probability of lower verification loss for candidate architectures, improving upon previous methods like Understanding One-Shot Models and SMASH. 

The research highlights issues with weight sharing strategies in NAS, such as those used in ENAS, which can lead to inaccurate performance evaluations and hinder the identification of optimal architectures. Studies on Fairnas and others confirm that parameter sharing methods often result in inadequate training of candidate architectures, causing inaccurate rankings. To address these challenges, the DNA method modularizes the search space, ensuring thorough training of candidate architectures and employing block-wise search for improved evaluation accuracy.

Additionally, GDAS-NSAS introduces an improved weight sharing mechanism, addressing the multi-model forgetting issue during super network training. The paper also touches on differentiable neural architecture search methods, such as DARTS, which share parameters among sub-networks and optimize a super network directly.

The consideration of early performance curves is proposed as a potential strategy for terminating unpromising models early in the training process, which could optimize resource allocation for exploring new architectures. This approach allows for quicker assessments of a model's effectiveness without waiting for full convergence, contributing to more efficient NAS practices.
