The paper discusses advancements in neural architecture search (NAS) methods, particularly focusing on DARTS and its variants, I-DARTS and P-DARTS. DARTS, or Differentiable Architecture Search, has limitations in its ability to compare edges between nodes, as it only allows a single directed edge between each pair, which restricts its search space and can lead to suboptimal architecture choices. In contrast, I-DARTS enhances this by employing a softmax function that considers all input edges of a node, allowing it to identify the importance of connections and potentially utilize multiple edges or none, thus improving decision-making in architecture selection.

P-DARTS addresses the gap between the search and evaluation phases of DARTS by progressively increasing network depth during the search and gradually reducing the candidate operation set based on the weights of mixed operations. This approach mitigates the computational burden associated with deeper networks and introduces regularization to stabilize the search process, particularly in deep architectures where algorithms tend to favor skip-connect operations.

Overall, while DARTS improves search efficiency compared to reinforcement learning (RL) and evolutionary algorithms (EA), it still faces issues such as high memory usage and slow search speeds due to simultaneous optimization of all parameters in the directed acyclic graph (DAG). The paper highlights the need for further enhancements in the efficiency and stability of NAS methods.
