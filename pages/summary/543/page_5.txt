The paper provides a comprehensive survey of Neural Architecture Search (NAS), focusing on the challenges and solutions within the field. It outlines key components of NAS, including search space, optimization methods, and performance estimation strategies, which are essential for effective architecture design.

One significant challenge addressed is the limitation of early NAS approaches, which often involve searching from scratch and fully training candidate architectures. This means that existing architectural knowledge is not leveraged, and training can be inefficient. The paper critiques the necessity of training every candidate architecture to convergence, suggesting that relative performance rankings may suffice.

The survey also highlights the structure of search spaces, particularly through examples of chain structures in early NAS works. These structures define how operations within the candidate set interact to generate outputs from inputs. The paper distinguishes between two types of chain structures: a simple one with a single input node and a more complex version that incorporates skip connections, allowing multiple inputs for feature maps.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of considering existing architectural knowledge and optimizing search strategies to improve the efficiency of NAS.
