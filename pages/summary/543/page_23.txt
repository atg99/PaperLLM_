The paper provides a comprehensive survey of Neural Architecture Search (NAS), highlighting the various frameworks and optimization strategies used in the field. It compares three general frameworks: Reinforcement Learning (RL), Sequential Model-Based Optimization (SMBO), and Evolutionary Algorithms (EA) in the context of NAS. These frameworks are illustrated through diagrams that outline their processes, including actions taken by the controller, candidate selection, evaluation, and the updating of surrogate models.

The performance of state-of-the-art NAS methods is analyzed using the CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets, with results summarized in tables. The findings indicate a preference for modular search strategies among popular NAS approaches, which simplify the search space compared to global searches. However, the paper notes that a modular search space is not inherently superior. It also emphasizes the common practice of incomplete training to expedite the evaluation of candidate architectures, thus reducing search time.

Among the various optimization strategies, gradient-based continuous search methods are highlighted for their ability to significantly lower search costs, supported by extensive research. Additionally, while random search strategies have demonstrated competitive results, the paper identifies a lack of thorough exploration in this area. Architecture recycling strategies also show promise but are less studied. Overall, the survey underscores the diversity of approaches in NAS and the ongoing challenges that researchers face.
