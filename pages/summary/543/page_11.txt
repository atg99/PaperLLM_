This paper provides a comprehensive survey of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods, focusing on the challenges and solutions associated with them. A significant portion discusses DARTS (Differentiable Architecture Search), which employs continuous relaxation to optimize neural architecture. In DARTS, a cell structure is learned where operations are mixed probabilistically, allowing for efficient architecture search. However, the method faces challenges such as performance bias between derived child networks and converged parent networks, necessitating retraining of parameters.

To address these issues, SNAS (Stochastic Neural Architecture Search) introduces a delayed reward mechanism from reinforcement learning to improve convergence speed and optimize architecture and operation parameters jointly. Despite these improvements, both SNAS and DARTS still encounter the problem of needing to select a single path during the verification phase, leading to discrepancies between search and verification performance.

DATA (Decoupled Architecture Search) proposes the Ensemble Gumbel-Softmax estimator to decouple path dependencies and facilitate seamless gradient transfer during architecture search and verification. Additionally, I-DARTS highlights the limitations of softmax-based relaxation in DARTS, suggesting that it may restrict the model to local optima due to its one-directional edge connections between nodes.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution and refinement of NAS techniques, presenting various strategies to enhance efficiency and reduce performance gaps between different phases of the search process.
