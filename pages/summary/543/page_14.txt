This section provides an overview of optimized Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods that utilize gradient strategies within a continuous search framework, highlighting the significance of the DARTS architecture in this domain. Traditional NAS approaches often initiate searches from scratch, which can yield innovative neural architectures but also increases time complexity due to a lack of leveraging existing high-performance designs. 

To address this, the concept of "network transformation" is introduced, where existing architectures serve as a foundation for modification through NAS techniques. Notably, the Net2Net framework facilitates function-preserving transformations, allowing for efficient parameter reuse and expedited training of larger networks. The Efficient Architecture Search (EAS) extends this idea by employing an encoder network as a meta-controller to adjust architectures at the layer level, utilizing reinforcement learning and a bidirectional recurrent network (Bi-LSTM) for comprehensive analysis.

In contrast, N2N learning focuses on compressing the teacher network through a two-stage process of layer removal and shrinkage, achieving significant compression ratios while maintaining performance. Additionally, Path-level EAS innovates by enabling transformations at the path level, inspired by the advantages seen in multi-branch architectures, which utilize allocation and merge strategies to enhance the network's capabilities. Overall, these approaches illustrate a trend towards optimizing existing architectures, reducing computational costs, and improving performance in neural architecture design.
