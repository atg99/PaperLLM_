The paper discusses the evolution of neural architecture search (NAS) methods, highlighting the progression from simple neural architectures to complex designs. It emphasizes various operations such as convolution, Batch Normalization, and ReLU, and notes the significance of connection methods, specifically the summation operation, which has been favored over merge operations based on experimental results from NASNet. 

Early NAS approaches, like NAS-RL, utilized reinforcement learning to generate architectures represented as variable-length strings. MetaQNN treated layer selection as a Markov decision process, employing Q-learning for optimization. GeNet introduced a fixed-length binary code representation for architectures, using evolutionary learning for population selection and improvement.

The paper illustrates Large-scale Evolution as a notable example of NAS that begins with a single-layer model and employs evolutionary methods for architecture enhancement. It concludes by acknowledging the common challenges faced by early NAS works and indicates that solutions to these challenges will be discussed in subsequent sections.
