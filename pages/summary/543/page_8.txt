The paper discusses the structure of search spaces in neural architecture search (NAS), particularly focusing on the design of normal and reduction cells as seen in earlier works like NASNet. The normal cell maintains the size of the feature map with a stride of 1, while the reduction cell halves the feature map size with a stride of 2. This framework has influenced many subsequent studies, which often adopt similar cell-based architectures. 

In experiments conducted by ENAS, the effectiveness of this search space structure was demonstrated, leading to its widespread adoption. Various methods have emerged that replace the reduction cell with alternative operations like pooling or convolution for down-sampling tasks. For instance, Block-QNN employs pooling, and Hierarchical-EAS uses a convolutional stride to reduce spatial resolution. Dpp-net utilizes average pooling and incorporates dense connections inspired by DenseNet for multi-objective optimization.

The paper also highlights the adaptability of cell structures for complex video tasks, as seen in AutoDispNet, which searches for normal, reduction, and upsampling cells in a U-Net-like architecture. Additionally, a study on the commonalities among cells from various search spaces reveals that wider and shallower cells tend to converge more easily, further emphasizing the importance of cell architecture in NAS.
