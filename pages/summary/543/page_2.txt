The paper discusses the advancements in neural architecture search (NAS), emphasizing the transition from manual feature design to automated feature extraction enabled by deep learning. While deep learning simplifies feature extraction, the design of neural architectures still requires significant prior knowledge, posing challenges for beginners and potentially limiting innovation. NAS has emerged to automate this design process, optimizing architectures for performance with reduced human input. Early methods like NAS-RL and MetaQNN demonstrated the feasibility of automated design but consumed extensive computational resources, prompting research focused on efficiency improvements.

The paper highlights the application of NAS across various fields, including object detection and speech recognition, while also noting the ongoing challenges in comparing and reproducing different NAS methods due to variability in search space and hyperparameters. The motivation section outlines how previous NAS methods have evolved; for instance, ENAS introduced a weight sharing strategy to enhance search efficiency, though it was later found to lead to inaccuracies in ranking candidate architectures. Subsequent approaches like DNA and GDAS-NSAS aimed to address these issues by modularizing search spaces and mitigating problems associated with weight sharing.

Overall, the paper underscores the rapid development in NAS research and the continuous efforts to refine methodologies for better performance and efficiency.
