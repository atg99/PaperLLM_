The paper provides a comprehensive overview of Neural Architecture Search (NAS), focusing on its challenges and potential solutions. A key framework discussed is FNA, which begins with a seed network that is expanded into a super network. This super network is then adapted to produce a target architecture through parameter remapping and fine-tuning.

The paper highlights the inefficiency of traditional NAS methods that fully train each candidate architecture, leading to excessive time consumption due to the vast number of candidates. Early NAS implementations, such as NAS-RL and MetaQNN, introduced strategies to speed up the ranking process by using parallel updates and performance comparisons after initial training epochs. However, these methods still face limitations in terms of computational requirements and the effectiveness of acceleration.

One critical point raised is the potential of parameter sharing to enhance training efficiency. This approach suggests that training each candidate architecture from scratch may overlook the relationships between different architectures, indicating the need for further research to optimize the training process and improve the ranking of candidate architectures.
