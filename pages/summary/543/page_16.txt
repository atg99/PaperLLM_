The paper discusses advancements in neural architecture search (NAS) focusing on path-level network transformation and its application in complex tasks like semantic segmentation and object detection. Traditional approaches often repurpose networks designed for image classification, which can hinder performance. The authors highlight Fast Neural Network Adaptation (FNA), a method that allows for efficient adaptation of a seed network into a super network with minimal computational cost. This process involves expanding the seed network, utilizing NAS for architecture adaptation, and fine-tuning for specific tasks, making it viable for large-scale applications.

Additionally, the Evolved Transformer is introduced as a NAS approach aimed at improving feedforward architectures for sequence-to-sequence tasks. It employs a large search space and evolutionary architecture search, starting with a Transformer as a seed. This method includes the Progressive Dynamic Hurdles approach to allocate resources effectively among candidate networks, resulting in performance enhancements across various language tasks.

The paper emphasizes the significance of NAS based on architecture recycling, which leverages existing high-performance networks to streamline the search process, minimizing unnecessary explorations in vast search spaces. This approach contrasts with traditional optimization strategies and highlights the need for further exploration in this area.
