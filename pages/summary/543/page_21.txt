The paper discusses the challenges and solutions in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), particularly focusing on the use of early termination strategies in training neural networks. The learning curve, illustrated in Figure 14, highlights how early performance can predict the final validation accuracy of neural architectures. The authors argue that by employing performance prediction and early ranking assumptions, training can be halted for architectures that demonstrate low potential early on, thus conserving computational resources and expediting the search process.

Early termination is not a novel concept, with prior research exploring probabilistic methods to simulate learning curves and terminate training for underperforming models. However, these methods often require extensive early training to make accurate predictions. The paper references various approaches that have utilized partial early performance data to inform learning curve predictions, including the work that integrates these predictions with NAS tasks.

Notably, the paper describes how the combination of learning curve prediction with NAS has been applied to automatically identify substandard architectures, allowing early training termination. For instance, a frequentist regression model is trained using features derived from the neural architecture and hyperparameters to predict final performance based on early training data. The performance predictor is also leveraged in PNAS to evaluate candidate architectures without the need for full training.

Additionally, the NAO method employs a performance predictor to optimize neural architecture representation through gradient ascent, thereby enhancing the search efficiency for effective architectures. Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of early performance assessment in optimizing the NAS process, improving both efficiency and resource allocation in neural network training.
