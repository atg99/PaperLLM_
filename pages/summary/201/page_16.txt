This paper discusses various approaches utilizing Deep Q-Learning (DQL) for network access and adaptive rate control in wireless networks, highlighting several models and their corresponding learning algorithms, agent states, actions, and rewards. 

For network access, different frameworks like Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and Markov Decision Process (MDP) are employed. Notable models include those using Deep Q-Networks (DQN) with feedforward neural networks (FNN), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, and convolutional neural networks (CNN) for tasks such as channel selection and traffic management in IoT, LTE, and satellite systems. Each model is designed to optimize specific actions, such as channel selection or power allocation, based on the state of the network and user demands, with rewards measured in terms of throughput, packet transmission ratios, or utility.

In terms of rate control, the paper emphasizes the importance of adaptive bitrate selection based on various factors like buffer state and rebuffering time, particularly in Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP (DASH) systems. Here, DQL is leveraged to enhance video quality while minimizing rebuffering events.

The paper also introduces the concept of wireless proactive caching, which aims to alleviate traffic loads by pre-caching popular content at base stations or edge devices. This approach is particularly beneficial given the time-varying nature of content popularity. The authors propose a DQL-based scheme for improving caching performance, addressing the challenges of high-dimensional state and action spaces.

In summary, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of DQL applications in managing network access and adaptive rate control, as well as proactive caching strategies, emphasizing the potential of learning-based approaches to optimize performance in complex wireless environments.
