In the first section, the paper presents a model for detecting cyber-physical attacks on IoT devices using a cloud-based approach. The model employs Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units to analyze signals generated by IoT devices and extract features such as flatness, skewness, and kurtosis. These features, referred to as fingerprints, are sent back to the IoT devices, which embed them into their signals. This dynamic watermarking technique enables the cloud to authenticate the signals and detect potential attacks. However, the method requires significant computational resources, limiting the number of devices that can be authenticated simultaneously. To address this limitation, the authors propose a Deep Q-Learning (DQL) approach that allows the cloud to select which devices to authenticate based on incomplete information, focusing on devices with more valuable data. Simulation results indicate that this DQL method can enhance the cloud's utility by up to 30% compared to random device selection.

The second section focuses on preserving connectivity in multi-robot systems, particularly in cooperative networks of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). The paper critiques the Artificial Potential Field (APF) algorithm for its limitations in dynamic environments and presents a DQL-based solution that allows each robot to make real-time decisions regarding its state. The model describes a leader-follower scenario where a base station adjusts the follower's velocity to maintain communication with the leader. The problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), and a Deep Q-Network (DQN) is employed to learn an optimal policy for maximizing connectivity. Simulation results demonstrate improved connectivity compared to the APF method. Additionally, the authors explore scenarios with multiple leaders and followers, introducing the concept of algebraic connectivity to assess the overall connectivity of the robot system.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of DQL and LSTM in enhancing security and connectivity in IoT and multi-robot systems, respectively.
