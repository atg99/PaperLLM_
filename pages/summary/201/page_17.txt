The focus is on enhancing cache hit rates and efficiently managing content delivery based on user requests. The base station (BS) acts as an agent that decides whether to store new content in the cache, replacing existing content as necessary. The decision-making process is guided by a feature space that includes the number of requests for each content over different time frames.

The proposed Deep Q-Learning (DQL) scheme utilizes the Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) method and the Wolpertinger architecture to reduce the action space and improve policy optimization. This architecture consists of an actor network to manage actions, a critic network to validate decisions, and a K-Nearest Neighbors (K-NN) component to explore actions, with the simulation results indicating superior performance compared to traditional FIFO caching methods.

The paper also explores the optimization of cache expiration times using Normalized Advantage Functions (NAFs) in dynamic environments. This method simplifies the continuous DQL process by allowing a single neural network to generate both value and advantage functions, enhancing the efficiency of cache management based on query loads.

Furthermore, the integration of caching with transmission control is crucial for effective content delivery in multi-user wireless networks. The authors propose a DQL framework that addresses interference alignment and resource management in MIMO systems. A central scheduler collects channel state information (CSI) and cache status to optimize user transmission and resource allocation while minimizing backhaul demands. The DQL agent employs a deep neural network to approximate the Q-function, enhancing throughput and energy efficiency even under imperfect conditions.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of intelligent caching and transmission strategies in improving the performance of wireless networks, highlighting novel approaches in DQL for optimizing both caching policies and transmission control.
