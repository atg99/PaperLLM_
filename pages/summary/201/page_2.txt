This paper discusses the application of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) in communications and networking, highlighting how it enables network entities to learn optimal policies for various tasks without complete network information. DRL facilitates autonomous decision-making, allowing entities like mobile users to select base stations, manage channel access, and make caching and offloading decisions with minimal information exchange, thereby reducing communication overhead and enhancing security.

The paper emphasizes DRL's ability to accelerate learning in complex environments, particularly in large-scale networks such as IoT systems, where it can dynamically manage user associations, spectrum access, and transmit power. It also notes that many communication problems, including cyber-physical attacks and interference management, can be framed as non-cooperative games, where DRL can effectively find solutions like Nash equilibria without requiring complete information.

The authors point out a gap in existing literature, as most surveys do not focus specifically on DRL applications in communications and networking. This survey aims to fill that gap by providing a comprehensive review of DRL applications across various networking issues, categorized into areas such as network access, data rate control, wireless caching, and network security. The paper includes statistical analyses of DRL-related works across different network types and issues, revealing that cellular networks and wireless caching are the most studied areas.

The structure of the paper is organized into sections that introduce reinforcement learning and DRL techniques, review applications for dynamic network access and data rate control, and discuss wireless caching, data offloading, network security, and other relevant issues. The paper concludes by identifying challenges and future research directions in the field of DRL for communications and networking.
