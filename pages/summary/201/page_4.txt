This approach enables the agent to adapt its policy dynamically as it interacts with the environment, making it suitable for real-time applications.

The SARSA algorithm follows a similar process to Q-learning but differs in how it updates the Q-values. The update rule for SARSA is given by:

Qt+1(s;a) = Qt(s;a) + αt(rt(s;a) + Qt(s0;a0) - Qt(s;a))

Here, the agent takes action a in state s, receives a reward r, and transitions to state s0, where it selects action a0 based on its current policy. The learning rate αt is also subject to Assumption 1, ensuring convergence.

The key advantage of SARSA is that it considers the action taken in the next state, leading to a more conservative approach that can be beneficial in certain environments where exploration and exploitation need to be balanced effectively.

In summary, this paper discusses the concepts of Markov games and reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the Q-learning and SARSA algorithms. The Q-learning algorithm is a powerful method for finding optimal policies in Markov decision processes, while SARSA provides an online learning alternative that adapts the policy in real-time. Both algorithms are grounded in the principles of maximizing long-term rewards through iterative updates based on observed rewards and state transitions.
