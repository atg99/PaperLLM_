This section discusses various applications of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) in wireless and networked systems, emphasizing its adaptability in handling dynamic system modeling and multi-agent interactions. The authors explore the use of transfer learning to enhance data efficiency in training DRQN models, demonstrating a reduction of up to 15% in sensed cells while maintaining inference quality, validated through simulations on real-world datasets.

The paper also highlights a DQL-based control framework for mobile crowdsensing, where unmanned vehicles coordinate data collection while managing energy constraints. This framework incorporates CNNs for training efficiency and employs a prioritized experience replay (PER) method for effective learning. Results indicate superior data collection rates compared to traditional methods.

Additionally, the authors address the challenge of faked sensing attacks in crowdsensing applications by modeling interactions as a Stackelberg game, where a server incentivizes user efforts through a dynamic payment policy. Utilizing DQL with deep CNNs, the proposed scheme significantly enhances sensing quality and server utility.

In the context of social networking within smart cities, the authors propose a Decentralized DRL (DDRL) framework that leverages mutual knowledge among users to estimate behavior patterns and market equilibrium. They introduce a semi-supervised DRL framework utilizing variational auto-encoders to improve classification accuracy in scenarios involving both labeled and unlabeled data.

Finally, the authors underscore the versatility of DRL across various applications, particularly in addressing complex network control problems in the 5G ecosystem, where both discrete and continuous control variables are prevalent. The study concludes that DRL serves as a potent tool for optimizing resource allocation and traffic engineering in modern wireless networks.
