This paper summarizes various approaches using Deep Q-Learning (DQL) for network security and connectivity preservation. The table outlines different models, learning algorithms, agent states, actions, rewards, and the types of networks involved.

For network security, several studies employ DQN with CNN and LSTM architectures to manage signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) and mobility costs in cognitive radio networks, underwater acoustic networks, and IoT devices. The actions typically involve channel selection and decisions to stay or leave, with rewards based on SINR, energy consumption, and relay costs.

In terms of connectivity preservation, multiple studies utilize DQL methods, including A3C and LSTM, for mobile users and robots. These models focus on relative positions, velocities, and received signal quality to optimize decisions concerning serving base station selection and velocity decisions, with rewards structured around performance scores.

The paper also discusses the limitations of conventional DDPG in continuous control scenarios, particularly in traffic engineering, and introduces optimizations like TE-aware exploration and prioritized experience replay (PER) to improve performance. The proposed DQL scheme significantly enhances network utility and reduces end-to-end delay compared to baseline methods.

Additionally, challenges in UAV-based wireless communications are addressed by modeling autonomous navigation as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP). The study presents a novel fast-RDPG method for policy updates, emphasizing the importance of maximizing long-term rewards while navigating complex environments.

Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of DQL techniques in addressing network security and connectivity challenges in various communication scenarios.
