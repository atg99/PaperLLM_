This paper discusses various strategies for computation offloading in mobile networks, particularly focusing on the optimization of mobile users' decisions in ultra-dense networks with multiple base stations (BSs) and mobile edge computing (MEC) servers. The authors propose a pre-classification step to limit the action space for mobile users, which enhances decision-making efficiency. The work builds on previous studies by incorporating dynamic network conditions and formulating mobile users' decision-making as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). 

Key contributions include the development of a Deep Double Q-Network (DDQN)-based algorithm that learns optimal offloading policies without prior knowledge of network dynamics. This algorithm demonstrates significant performance improvements over baseline methods, particularly during peak traffic. Additionally, the paper addresses energy consumption challenges associated with high-density small BSs by proposing a Deep Q-Learning (DQL) strategy to control the activation of these stations, ensuring quality of service while minimizing energy use.

Further applications include cloud-based malware detection, where mobile users offload tasks to a more capable MEC/security server. A dynamic malware detection game model is introduced, utilizing DQL to enhance detection accuracy and efficiency. The concept of hotbooting Q-learning is introduced to expedite the learning process by leveraging previous offloading experiences.

The authors also explore offloading policies for IoT devices with energy harvesting capabilities, employing similar MDP frameworks to evaluate energy consumption and task execution delays. The paper concludes by discussing QoS-aware computation offloading in ad-hoc mobile networks, where users can offload tasks to nearby peers, forming a mobile cloudlet.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of optimizing offloading decisions in mobile networks to improve computational efficiency, reduce delays, and enhance security in the context of resource constraints.
