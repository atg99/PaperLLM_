The paper discusses advanced anti-jamming schemes utilizing Deep Q-Learning (DQL) in the context of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) and autonomous vehicle systems. In the first scenario, a DQL approach based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) is employed to optimize relay policies for UAVs under jamming attacks. The results demonstrate that this method converges to an optimal policy in 200 time slots, significantly faster than traditional Q-learning methods, while also reducing the Bit Error Rate (BER) by 46.6% compared to other relay schemes.

In a more complex scenario, the paper explores the use of DQL against a smarter attacker who can analyze past communication channels and adapt its jamming strategies. The UAV must determine optimal transmit power levels to maximize its secrecy capacity in the face of this threat. The proposed DQL solution outperforms a baseline method by enhancing the UAV's utility by 13% and increasing its safe rate by 7%. However, this approach currently addresses only single-UAV systems, indicating the need for future research into multi-UAV scenarios.

Additionally, the paper addresses cyber-physical attacks in Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS). Here, attackers can inject faulty data into the information transmitted from sensors to autonomous vehicles (AVs), potentially leading to unsafe spacing between vehicles. The authors propose a DQL framework to allow AVs to adaptively learn optimal responses to these attacks, modeled as a zero-sum game. By utilizing Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units to manage continuous state spaces, the DQL method provides faster convergence and lower steady-state deviation compared to traditional algorithms. Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of DQL and LSTM in enhancing the resilience of UAVs and AVs against sophisticated cyber threats.
