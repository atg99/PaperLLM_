The paper discusses the application of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and deep Q-learning (DQL) in network slicing, service function chaining (SFC), resource allocation, and power control in various networking contexts. 

In the context of network slicing, the authors highlight how multi-layer slices can be managed independently to minimize conflicts. They present a DQL scheme designed for QoS and QoE-aware SFC in NFV-enabled 5G systems, where QoS metrics such as bandwidth and delay are related to user experience (QoE) through principles like the Weber-Fechner Law. The DQL agent operates by selecting VNF instances based on the network environment and system state, optimizing rewards that consider QoE gains and penalties associated with QoS constraints and operational expenses.

The paper also explores resource allocation in computer clusters, introducing DeepRM, a DRL-based solution that utilizes policy gradient methods to manage resources effectively. DeepRM aims to minimize job slowdown and completion time, outperforming traditional heuristics through simulations. Additionally, the authors apply an actor-critic method for scheduling in distributed data stream processing systems, targeting average processing time reduction.

In terms of power control, the paper addresses mobile crowdsensing in cognitive radio networks, where a secondary user (SU) utilizes distributed sensing devices to optimize its power control strategy. The DQL approach allows the SU to learn and adjust transmit power effectively based on received signal strength measurements. The authors also explore mobile crowdsensing paradigms, proposing a DQL-based mechanism for optimizing data collection in designated sensing areas, incorporating temporal correlations through DRQN with LSTM layers.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of DQL and DRL frameworks to enhance network resource management, scheduling, and power control in diverse scenarios, achieving improved performance and adaptability in resource-constrained environments.
