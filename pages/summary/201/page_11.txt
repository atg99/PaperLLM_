This paper discusses the application of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and deep Q-learning (DQL) techniques in modern decentralized networks, particularly focusing on dynamic spectrum access, user association, and adaptive rate control. 

The authors introduce the Neural Fictitious Self-Play (NFSP) framework, where multiple agents learn through self-play, utilizing two distinct memory types to enhance their learning. NFSP agents maintain experience from game transitions and their best response behavior, training a neural network to predict action values and another to mimic past behavior. This approach enables agents to converge toward approximate Nash equilibria in competitive scenarios, such as small poker games.

The paper further explores the challenges faced by entities in decentralized networks, such as sensors and mobile users, who must make independent decisions under dynamic conditions. DQL is highlighted as a powerful tool for enabling optimal decision-making in these environments.

Key applications of DQL in network scenarios include:
1. Dynamic Spectrum Access: DQL aids users in selecting channels to maximize throughput despite incomplete system observations.
2. Joint User Association and Spectrum Access: DQL provides distributed solutions to the combinatorial challenges of assigning users to base stations while accessing the spectrum.
3. Adaptive Rate Control: DQL is proposed as an effective method for bitrate control in unpredictable environments, improving user Quality of Experience (QoE) without the complexity of traditional dynamic programming methods.

The paper reviews pioneering works utilizing DQL for channel access, including a scheme for sensors that dynamically select channels based on interference levels and an adaptive DQL method that retrains the model when performance declines. The discussion extends to multi-sensor scenarios, emphasizing the need for collaborative strategies in packet forwarding and channel selection.

Overall, the paper underscores the potential of DQL techniques in enhancing decision-making processes in evolving network environments, paving the way for more efficient and responsive communication systems.
