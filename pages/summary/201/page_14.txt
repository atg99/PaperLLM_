The paper discusses a dynamic adaptive streaming system based on the HTTP standard, focusing on the use of Deep Q-Learning (DQL) for optimizing content request distribution and resource allocation in user association (UA) scenarios. The Liquid State Machine (LSM) approach is utilized to predict user content requests, enabling effective resource management at the cloud level. Each UA acts as an agent leveraging LSM-based learning to determine optimal user associations, subsequently guiding content caching and spectrum allocation through linear programming. The proposed DQL demonstrates convergence within approximately 400 iterations, outperforming traditional Q-learning by enhancing user queue stability by up to 50% and improving convergence time by 33%.

The paper also addresses Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP (DASH), a prevalent standard for video streaming that optimizes video quality through adaptive bitrate selection. The client, modeled as an agent in a Markov Decision Process (MDP), aims to maximize Quality of Experience (QoE) by selecting video segments based on various factors, including visual quality and buffer state. The DQL algorithm, enhanced by LSTM networks with peephole connections, shows faster convergence and better QoE compared to traditional methods, reducing rebuffering incidents significantly.

Further, the Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C) method is explored as an alternative for DQL, incorporating actor and critic networks to improve bitrate selection and reward prediction. This method allows for parallel training of multiple clients, facilitating real-time updates and minimal QoE degradation due to Round-Trip Time (RTT). Overall, the DQL approaches presented in the paper significantly advance the efficiency and quality of adaptive video streaming in dynamic network environments.
