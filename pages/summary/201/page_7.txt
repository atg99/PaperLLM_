This paper discusses advancements in Deep Q-Learning (DQL) algorithms, focusing on techniques such as Experience Replay, Double Deep Q-Learning (DDQL), Prioritized Experience Replay (PER), and Dueling Deep Q-Learning.

The DQL algorithm utilizes a replay memory to store transitions and employs a Q-network and a target Q-network. The weights of the Q-network are updated to minimize the loss between the expected and observed rewards. To address the over-estimation bias in traditional Q-learning, DDQL employs two Q-value functions, allowing for simultaneous action selection and evaluation. This method uses a target network to fairly assess the value of the greedy policy, enhancing performance in various applications, including dynamic spectrum access and resource allocation.

PER improves learning efficiency by prioritizing significant experiences in the replay memory, allowing more critical transitions to be sampled more frequently. This technique has shown to outperform standard DQL in multiple Atari games, provided that important experiences can be identified.

Dueling Deep Q-Learning introduces a two-stream architecture that separately estimates state and action values, reducing the need to evaluate both simultaneously. This approach simplifies the learning process in scenarios where the importance of actions varies depending on the context, such as in racing games. The combined output from the two streams generates the Q-value, which is optimized using a derived loss function similar to that in traditional DQL.

Overall, these innovations enhance the efficiency and performance of reinforcement learning agents, enabling them to better navigate complex environments.
