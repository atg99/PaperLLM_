The paper emphasizes the application of Deep Q-Learning (DQL) techniques in countering jamming and cyber-physical attacks within Cognitive Radio Networks (CRNs) and Internet of Things (IoT) frameworks. The initial model focuses on a CRN with Secondary Users (SUs) and Primary Users (PUs), where the SU must choose between transmitting on various channels or relocating to avoid jamming. The objective for the SU is to maximize its utility, defined by the Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR) and mobility costs. The DQL method shows improved convergence and utility outcomes compared to traditional Q-learning, particularly in scenarios with multiple jammers.

Subsequent models expand on this by incorporating Recursive Convolutional Neural Networks (RCNNs) to handle noisy SINR data, leading to better state representation and decision-making. In IoT contexts, DQL is applied to optimize power control in the presence of jammers, balancing transmission power against energy costs to enhance utility. The introduction of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) as relays further diversifies strategies against jamming, allowing mobile users to maintain connectivity through adaptive relay power adjustments.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of DQL in enhancing network resilience against jamming attacks, with various strategies tailored to specific network architectures and conditions.
