This paper explores various strategies for optimizing channel selection and packet forwarding in Internet of Things (IoT) systems, focusing on maximizing utility while addressing constraints such as energy consumption and latency. It begins with a discussion of a sensor's approach to channel selection formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), solved using Deep Q-Learning (DQL). The utility function of the sensor is shown to be bounded, ensuring convergence of the algorithm, which significantly outperforms random action selection, although increased packet arrival rates can negatively impact overall system utility due to higher power consumption.

The paper also examines channel access in energy harvesting-enabled IoT systems, where a base station (BS) allocates channels to sensors with uncertain energy availability. A DQN model predicts battery states and formulates a channel access policy, achieving better total rates than traditional methods while maintaining low battery prediction error.

In the context of Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) communications, the paper addresses the dual challenge of maximizing capacity while adhering to latency constraints. A DQN model enables V2V transmitters to optimize channel and power selection dynamically, enhancing the likelihood of meeting latency requirements compared to random allocations.

Lastly, the paper tackles the interference issues arising from the use of unlicensed channels in IoT systems. A DQN-based approach is proposed for Small Base Stations (SBSs) to manage channel access and interference in an LTE network, achieving improved throughput and maintaining performance without degrading existing WLAN services.

Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of DQL and DQN frameworks in optimizing performance across various IoT applications while considering critical constraints such as energy efficiency and latency.
