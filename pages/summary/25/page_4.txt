The paper presents the XNet architecture for image segmentation, emphasizing its design to retain fine-grained details through feature concatenation of layers with the same dimensions. The architecture consists of an encoder-decoder structure, where the encoder extracts features while the decoder upsamples to produce a segmented mask matching the input image size. 

Key components include convolutional layers with ReLU activation, batch normalization, and softmax activation for pixel-wise classification. The architecture avoids excessive downsampling, which can compromise boundary detail, by employing two encoder-decoder modules in succession. This allows for effective feature extraction while maintaining image resolution. The use of stored encoder feature maps during upsampling enhances detail retention, reducing the risk of losing important information.

To improve generalization and mitigate overfitting, L2 norm regularization is applied to each convolutional layer. The model is trained on augmented data derived from an initial dataset of 150 images, with 108 images allocated for training and augmentation. The remaining images are reserved for validation and testing. The paper suggests that this approach effectively leverages a smaller dataset while maintaining segmentation accuracy. Further details on the architecture and training parameters are available in the supplementary material.
