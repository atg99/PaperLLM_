The paper discusses the training and testing of a neural network for image segmentation using one-hot encoded ground truth masks. The optimization is performed using categorical cross-entropy loss and the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0001. To accommodate non-specialist institutions with limited GPU resources, the authors opted for small mini-batch and kernel sizes, training with a batch size of 5 and a 3x3 kernel size. Early stopping was employed to optimize training time, monitoring validation loss with a patience of 20, and training took 7 hours on a GTX 1060 6GB GPU.

For testing, images were selected without augmentation, focusing on challenging cases characterized by factors like bone structure complexity and noise. The network's performance was evaluated using categorical accuracy and the F1 score, the latter being preferred due to class imbalance in the dataset. The network achieved classification accuracies of 96% for open beam, 94% for soft tissue, and 88% for bone, with an overall weighted average accuracy of 92%. The corresponding F1 scores were 0.97, 0.87, and 0.90, leading to a weighted average F1 score of 0.92.
