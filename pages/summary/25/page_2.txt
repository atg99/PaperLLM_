This paper explores advancements in X-Ray image segmentation, particularly through the use of a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture that surpasses existing leading networks in performance. The structure of the paper is organized into several sections: an overview of related literature in Section 2, a discussion on dataset collection and augmentation methods in Section 3, details of the CNN design and training in Section 4, results and post-processing in Section 5, a comparative analysis with classical and machine learning methods in Section 6, and future applications in Section 7.

In the related work section, the paper highlights the long-standing interest in X-Ray image segmentation, noting that many traditional techniques, such as pixel clustering, have been utilized. While some methods achieve high accuracy, they often require extensive hyperparameter tuning or struggle with boundary smoothness. The paper references various studies that have employed different approaches, including entropy-based methods and edge detection algorithms, each with their strengths and limitations.

The paper also discusses the rise of machine learning applications in healthcare, particularly in analyzing X-Ray images for anomaly detection. It highlights the importance of tailored datasets and the development of neural networks for specific applications, while noting that many existing networks are not suitable for comprehensive pixel-level segmentation.

Overall, the research contributes to the growing field of multi-class image segmentation, particularly in medical contexts, and benchmarks its CNN against established architectures like SegNet-Basic, emphasizing the significance of network design and augmentation techniques in enhancing performance.
