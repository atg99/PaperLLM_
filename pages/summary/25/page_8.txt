The paper discusses the performance of a neural network architecture called XNet, which was trained on an artificially augmented dataset of 7000 images derived from 150 original images. The network achieved an overall accuracy of 92%, an F1 score of 0.92, and an AUC of 0.98. When compared to the SegNet-Basic architecture, XNet showed superior results, particularly in the soft tissue category, where it achieved an 82% true positive rate compared to SegNet's 75%. Additionally, XNet outperformed SegNet in detecting the open beam region, with an overall weighted F1 score of 0.89 for SegNet, lower than XNet's scores across all categories.

The paper also highlights XNet's advantages over classical image processing techniques, demonstrating its ability to produce smooth boundaries around bone regions and effectively differentiate between bone and soft tissue. This was achieved despite the training on high-resolution TIF images while the comparison was made on lower quality JPEG images, indicating the network's robustness and generalizability across different body part classes without specific tuning.

Future work aims to reduce the false positive rate during post-processing and explore methods such as conditional random fields to enhance segmentation mask outputs. Additionally, the possibility of training an adversarial network alongside XNet is considered to detect inconsistencies in segmentation, although this may increase training time and the risk of local minima.
