Our findings demonstrate that the network, XNet, is well calibrated, as indicated by the close alignment between average confidence and accuracy across output categories. Specifically, the soft tissue category exhibits the best calibration, while bone shows the least. 

To address the challenge of false positives, particularly in the soft tissue category, we implement a probability thresholding approach. By requiring a higher confidence level for pixel classification, we successfully reduce false positives, although this comes at the cost of true positives. For instance, setting a threshold of 0.90 decreases false positives to 3% while maintaining a true positive rate of 71%. The selection of the threshold is tailored to specific use cases, with more stringent thresholds applied to categories where the network performs less effectively.

Overall, our method achieves a high classification performance, as evidenced by an area under the curve of 1.00 for the open beam region and 0.97 for both bone and soft tissue classes, underscoring the network's capability in medical image segmentation.
