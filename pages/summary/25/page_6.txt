The paper presents results from a study on the performance of a convolutional neural network (CNN) called XNet for medical image segmentation. It includes a normalized confusion matrix and ROC curves for three categories: Open Beam, Soft Tissue, and Bone. The confusion matrix indicates that the majority of classification errors occur when soft tissue is misclassified as bone. The ROC curves demonstrate the model's performance, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 1.00 for Open Beam, and 0.97 for both Bone and Soft Tissue, indicating high sensitivity and specificity.

Additionally, the paper addresses the issue of calibration in CNNs, noting that many modern networks tend to be overconfident in their predictions. This overconfidence can negatively impact downstream processes, such as reducing false positives. The output from XNet generates a 3-dimensional probability map, where each pixel is assigned a probability of belonging to a specific category. The authors define network confidence in a category based on the probabilities assigned to the pixels within that category. This aspect of calibration is crucial for improving the reliability of medical image segmentation tasks.
