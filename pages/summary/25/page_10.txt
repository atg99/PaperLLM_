The referenced works primarily focus on advancements in deep learning and image analysis techniques for medical imaging, particularly in the context of chest radiography. 

Candemir et al. introduce an atlas-based rib-bone detection method in chest X-rays, highlighting the importance of accurate anatomical feature identification. Badrinarayanan et al. present SegNet, a deep convolutional encoder-decoder architecture designed for robust semantic pixel-wise labeling, which has applications in medical image segmentation.

Mansoor et al. discuss current approaches and challenges in the segmentation and analysis of abnormal lungs in CT scans, indicating the need for improved methodologies in this area. Sobel's and Canny's works on image gradient operators and edge detection, respectively, lay foundational techniques for image processing that are relevant to medical imaging.

Rajpurkar et al. showcase CheXNet, a deep learning model capable of detecting pneumonia in chest X-rays at a level comparable to radiologists. Similarly, Lakhani and Sundaram demonstrate the use of convolutional neural networks for the automated classification of pulmonary tuberculosis in chest radiography.

Several studies, including those by Yan et al. and Islam et al., explore weakly supervised learning and abnormality detection in chest X-rays, respectively, emphasizing the potential of deep learning in enhancing diagnostic accuracy. 

The survey by Qin et al. discusses the broader implications of artificial intelligence in computer-aided detection for chest radiography, while other contributions, such as those by Badrinarayanan et al. and Ronneberger et al., focus on advancements in segmentation techniques through various neural network architectures.

Overall, these studies collectively advance the field of medical imaging by leveraging deep learning approaches for improved diagnosis and analysis of thoracic diseases.
