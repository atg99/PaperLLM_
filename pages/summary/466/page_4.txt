The paper discusses the Duo-SegNet, a segmentation model that operates effectively within defined parameter ranges, specifically setting parameters as s=1.0, u=0.3, and c=0.2 for their experiments. The authors start by explaining the supervised loss, which utilizes labeled data and can be represented as cross-entropy or dice loss, or a combination of both, as commonly practiced in segmentation tasks.

The supervised loss is mathematically defined in two forms: Lce for cross-entropy and Ldice for dice loss. The unsupervised loss is introduced as a mechanism to enforce consensus during dual-view training, where two segmentation networks are expected to produce similar segmentation masks for unlabeled data. This unsupervised loss is a symmetric form of the cross-entropy loss.

The paper distinguishes its approach from previous works by employing a critic that fosters diversity among segmentation models, rather than using adversarial examples. For labeled data, two networks are trained using both supervised loss and adversarial loss. The adversarial loss for labeled data is defined using a normalized critic, while for unlabeled data, only the adversarial loss is applicable since ground truth annotations are absent.

Overall, the proposed method integrates both supervised and unsupervised learning strategies to enhance segmentation performance while ensuring model diversity and consensus.
