In this paper, Peiris et al. address the challenge of leveraging abundant unlabeled data for image segmentation, a task traditionally dominated by classification approaches. They introduce a dual-view UNet model, referred to as Duo-SegNet, which aims to enhance segmentation accuracy by utilizing multiple distinct views of the data. The authors apply the consensus principle, which suggests that minimizing disagreement between two hypotheses can reduce the overall error, to the context of segmentation.

The proposed model incorporates a critic network to serve two main objectives: ensuring that the outputs of the UNets closely resemble ground-truth segmentation masks and identifying confident areas in the prediction masks to promote agreement across views. This is crucial since segmentation involves dense pixel-level predictions, where naive approaches might lead to overfitting on background regions.

The learning process is formulated as a min-max problem, with the critic acting as a subjective referee to regulate training. The authors demonstrate that their method effectively utilizes a small fraction of labeled data while achieving strong qualitative and quantitative results.

In summary, the key contributions of this work are: 
1. The introduction of a dual-view learning framework for semi-supervised medical image segmentation.
2. The employment of a critic to enhance training regularization and facilitate learning from unlabeled data.
