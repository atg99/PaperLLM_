The paper presents the Duo-SegNet model, optimized using RMSProp with a learning rate of 5e-05, to enhance medical image segmentation in a semi-supervised learning context. The study utilized three medical image datasets: 670 Fluorescence Microscopy images, 20 MRI volumes, and 41 CT volumes, with 2D slices resized to 256x256 pixels. The datasets were split into 80% training and 20% testing, with experiments conducted using 5%, 20%, and 50% of labeled training data.

Duo-SegNet was compared against several methods, including Mean Teacher, Pseudo Labelling, VAT, Deep Co-training, and fully supervised U-Net, using metrics such as Dice Sørensen coefficient (DSC) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE). The results indicated that Duo-SegNet outperformed competing methods, particularly with limited labeled data, achieving notable success in the Spleen dataset at 5% labeled data. Performance improvements were also observed in the Heart and Spleen datasets as labeled data increased.

The model effectively produced accurate prediction masks and confidence maps, which are valuable for training on unlabeled data. Visual analysis of confidence maps demonstrated the model's ability to indicate regions with high prediction reliability. The paper also included ablation studies highlighting the benefits of incorporating a critic in dual-view learning and underscoring the significance of the dual-view network structure in improving performance.
