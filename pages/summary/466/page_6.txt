The paper discusses various semi-supervised image segmentation methods, comparing the proposed Duo-SegNet approach with others such as Pseudo Labelling, mean teacher model, Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT), and deep co-training. Pseudo Labelling uses predicted labels from unlabeled data as ground truth, which can lead to generalization issues if the pseudo labels are incorrect. Duo-SegNet addresses this by incorporating a critic that enhances tolerance to these errors, thus stabilizing generalization performance.

The mean teacher model, which involves a student and a teacher network, benefits from a slow update mechanism that averages the student's parameters to reduce the impact of noisy gradients. Unlike this model, Duo-SegNet trains both networks simultaneously, allowing them to learn from each other.

VAT serves as a regularization technique, generating adversarial perturbations to improve model robustness against noisy inputs. In contrast, Duo-SegNet uses a critic to evaluate whether predictions align with labeled data distributions, promoting consistent predictive distributions across labeled and unlabeled data. The paper also references deep co-training, where two models learn from distinct views, highlighting the recent work by Peng et al. that incorporates adversarial examples to achieve diversity.

However, the authors argue that adversarial examples can exacerbate wrong predictions, which poses a challenge for diversity in unlabeled data. Inspired by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), the proposed method utilizes a min-max formulation in dual-view learning to segment medical images effectively, leveraging high-confidence predictions for unlabeled data.

The implementation of the proposed model is conducted in PyTorch without pre-trained weights, employing a stochastic gradient descent optimizer for training the segmentation network and critic.
