The paper presents a comparative analysis of a new segmentation method, Duo-SegNet, against several state-of-the-art models using three datasets: nuclei, heart, and spleen. The results demonstrate that Duo-SegNet outperforms the other methods in terms of Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across varying percentages of labeled training data.

In the nuclei dataset, Duo-SegNet achieved a DSC of 87.14 and an MAE of 3.57 with only 5% labeled data, significantly better than methods like Pseudo Labeling and Mean Teacher. For the heart dataset, it attained a DSC of 86.79 and an MAE of 0.10, outperforming all other techniques at 5% labeled data. Similarly, in the spleen dataset, Duo-SegNet recorded a DSC of 88.02 and an MAE of 0.10, again showing superior performance.

The paper also discusses the impact of using unlabeled data, indicating that performance varies with different values of a parameter, ϵu, which was optimized between 0.3 and 0.4 for the experiments conducted on the spleen dataset with 5% annotated data. Overall, the findings suggest that Duo-SegNet is an effective approach for segmentation tasks, particularly when labeled data is limited.
