However, the paper indicates that these unsupervised scores may not provide reliable guidance for model selection or hyperparameter tuning. The analysis reveals that the disentanglement scores are significantly influenced by randomness and hyperparameter choices rather than the objective function itself. 

The authors assert that model selection should not depend on disentanglement metrics that require substantial labeled data, as this could introduce bias. They argue that simply identifying a good model through a specific hyperparameter setting and random seed does not aid practitioners in finding optimal configurations in practice, especially in scenarios where labels are unavailable or where the underlying factors of variation are difficult to interpret.

The study emphasizes the need for better strategies in hyperparameter selection, noting the variability in results across different datasets and models. They propose that future work should focus on identifying robust methods for selecting hyperparameters that can be applied across various contexts in unsupervised learning. The findings highlight a significant challenge in the field of disentangled representation learning, underscoring the complexity of achieving reliable model selection without access to labeled data.
