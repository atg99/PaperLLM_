The paper investigates the challenges of unsupervised learning of disentangled representations, specifically questioning the common assumption that such learning is feasible without inductive biases. The authors present a theorem demonstrating the impossibility of achieving unsupervised disentanglement using arbitrary generative models unless specific biases are imposed on both the models and datasets. The theorem shows that for any distribution with a density that can be factored, it is possible to create an infinite number of bijective functions that maintain the same marginal distribution of observations while leading to complete entanglement of latent variables. This highlights that a change in one latent dimension can affect all others, rendering disentanglement unachievable with only observational data.

The authors argue that while this theoretical result indicates a fundamental limitation, practical applications may still succeed by leveraging structured real-world generative models with appropriate inductive biases. They emphasize the need to explicitly incorporate and investigate these biases in future research.

The paper also outlines an experimental design to explore various methods that augment the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) loss with regularizers aimed at promoting disentanglement. These methods include Beta-VAE, Annealed VAE, FactorVAE, and others that utilize different strategies to penalize entanglement. Additionally, performance metrics for evaluating disentanglement, such as the BetaVAE metric and Mutual Information Gap, are discussed.

In summary, the paper challenges the notion of unsupervised disentanglement as a straightforward task and underscores the importance of inductive biases in achieving meaningful representation learning.
