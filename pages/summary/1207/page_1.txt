In this paper, the authors critically assess the current state of unsupervised learning for disentangled representations, highlighting that many assumptions in the field may be misguided. They argue that achieving disentangled representations without any form of inductive bias—whether from the model architecture or the data—is fundamentally impossible. Through extensive experimentation involving over 12,000 models across various methods and metrics on seven datasets, the authors find that while existing methods can enforce certain properties suggested by their respective loss functions, identifying well-disentangled models remains elusive without supervision. 

Moreover, the research indicates that increasing the level of disentanglement does not necessarily reduce the sample complexity required for downstream tasks. Based on these findings, the authors advocate for future research to focus on clarifying the importance of inductive biases and implicit supervision in disentanglement learning. They also recommend that studies be conducted in reproducible experimental setups that encompass diverse datasets, emphasizing the need for concrete evaluations of the benefits of disentangled representations.
