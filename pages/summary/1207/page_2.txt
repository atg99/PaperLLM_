In this paper, we critically examine and challenge widely accepted assumptions in the field of unsupervised learning of disentangled representations, primarily focusing on Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). We establish that the unsupervised learning of disentangled representations is fundamentally impossible without specific inductive biases related to both the learning methods and the datasets used. 

Our contributions include a comprehensive experimental study where we implement six current unsupervised disentanglement methods and evaluate them against six disentanglement measures, training over 12,000 models across seven datasets. We introduce a new library, disentanglement_lib, which facilitates the training and evaluation of disentangled representations, and we release over 10,000 trained models to serve as baselines for future research.

Our findings challenge prevailing beliefs in this domain. We found that while the methods effectively prevent correlation among the sampled dimensions of the aggregated posterior, the dimensions of the learned representation (mean values) remain correlated. Furthermore, we observed that the choice of random seeds and hyperparameters significantly affected model performance, overshadowing the impact of model selection. We also could not validate the assumption that disentanglement benefits downstream tasks, such as reducing sample complexity.

Based on our results, we propose three key areas for further investigation: the explicit role of inductive biases and supervision in unsupervised model selection, the practical advantages of enforcing specific disentanglement notions in learned representations, and the importance of reproducible experimental setups across various datasets.

Additionally, we reference related work in independent component analysis and highlight the challenges faced in identifying underlying sources in nonlinear settings, which have prompted the exploration of alternative machine learning approaches. Overall, our study emphasizes the necessity for a reevaluation of assumptions in unsupervised disentangled representation learning.
