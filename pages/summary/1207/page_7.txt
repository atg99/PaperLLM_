This paper challenges common assumptions regarding the unsupervised learning of disentangled representations. It investigates the effectiveness of various hyperparameter selection strategies and their impact on model performance. The authors explore the correlation between different disentanglement metrics and model performance across multiple datasets, particularly focusing on the dSprites dataset.

The analysis includes a comparison of hyperparameter selection techniques based on transferability from labeled datasets. Results indicate that while there is some correlation between hyperparameters from different datasets, this approach does not reliably distinguish between good and bad models on target datasets. Notably, the performance of hyperparameter selection based on transfer is significantly higher when using the same metric and dataset, achieving an 80.7% success rate compared to 54.9% when transferring across both metrics and datasets.

Additionally, the paper examines the utility of disentangled representations for downstream tasks, positing that such representations should enhance sample efficiency in learning. However, findings reveal that higher disentanglement scores do not consistently lead to improved sample efficiency, challenging the assumption that disentangled representations inherently improve the sample complexity of learning tasks.

Overall, the study concludes that unsupervised model selection remains a challenging problem, and the anticipated benefits of disentangled representations in terms of downstream task performance and sample efficiency are not as pronounced as previously suggested.
