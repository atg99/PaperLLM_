The paper discusses the challenges associated with the common assumptions in the unsupervised learning of disentangled representations. It highlights the importance of disentangled representations in understanding complex data, allowing for more interpretable and flexible models. The authors critique existing methodologies and propose alternative approaches that challenge traditional views on how these representations should be learned. They reference various foundational works in the field, including methods for independent component analysis, variational inference, and adversarial training, illustrating how these techniques have evolved and their implications for disentangled representation learning. The authors emphasize the need for rigorous frameworks to assess the performance of unsupervised learning methods and suggest directions for future research, aiming to enhance the reliability and effectiveness of learning disentangled representations from sequential data. Overall, the paper advocates for a reevaluation of assumptions that may limit the advancement of unsupervised learning techniques.
