The paper addresses the challenges in unsupervised learning of disentangled representations, focusing on the evaluation of various metrics and methods used to measure disentanglement. It discusses several metrics, including Modularity, DCI Disentanglement, and the SAP score, which assess the independence of learned representations concerning factors of variation.

The study utilizes four deterministic datasets—dSprites, Cars3D, SmallNORB, and Shapes3D—and introduces three stochastic datasets—Color-dSprites, Noisy-dSprites, and Scream-dSprites—where the observations are influenced by random factors. The authors emphasize the importance of separating the impact of regularization from other inductive biases, ensuring that all methods are evaluated under identical conditions with a consistent convolutional architecture.

Key experimental findings reveal that while many methods aim to produce uncorrelated aggregated posteriors, they often rely on the mean of the Gaussian encoder rather than sampled representations. This distinction is crucial as the paper demonstrates that the total correlation of the sampled representation tends to decrease with increasing regularization strength, whereas the total correlation of the mean representation typically increases. This suggests that enforcing uncorrelated representations may not be effectively achieved through current methodologies.

Overall, the paper provides insights into the limitations of existing methods in achieving true disentanglement and highlights the necessity for further exploration in this domain. The findings are supported by comprehensive experimental setups and results detailed in the appendices.
