The paper investigates common assumptions in unsupervised learning for disentangled representations, focusing on the effectiveness of various methods and metrics. It presents experimental findings that suggest while models can enforce an aggregated posterior with uncorrelated dimensions, this does not guarantee that the mean representation dimensions are uncorrelated. The study highlights correlations among different disentanglement metrics, revealing a strong relationship among most metrics except for Modularity, with variances in correlation observed across different datasets.

Furthermore, the analysis emphasizes the significant impact of model choice, hyperparameter selection, and randomness (i.e., different random seeds) on disentanglement performance. The results indicate that the choice of hyperparameters and random seeds is more critical than the objective function itself, as evidenced by overlapping Factor-VAE scores across various models. The study concludes that while certain models may achieve better scores in specific contexts, no model consistently outperforms others across all metrics and datasets. These insights challenge existing assumptions and suggest a need for careful consideration of multiple factors when evaluating disentangled representations in unsupervised learning.
