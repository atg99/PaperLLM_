In this paper, we introduce a method for filter pruning in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) that focuses on reducing redundancy by identifying and removing filters with high correlation. This approach is motivated by the observation that uncorrelated filters can mitigate overfitting and enhance model compactness. We propose using the Pearson correlation coefficient to assess the redundancy of filter pairs, assigning the lowest importance to those with the highest correlation. 

Our method involves an iterative process where we first optimize the correlation between filter pairs before pruning. This optimization allows us to retain essential information even after discarding one filter from a highly correlated pair. For instance, if two filters initially have a 60% correlation, optimizing can increase this to 99%, allowing safe pruning with minimal information loss. 

The paper also reviews related work on deep model compression, categorizing it into three main types: connection pruning, filter pruning, and quantization. Connection pruning focuses on parameter importance but often requires special hardware. Filter pruning, which is the primary focus of our work, estimates filter importance and discards less important filters, necessitating retraining to recover from accuracy loss. Lastly, quantization techniques compress models but also typically need specialized hardware.

Our approach stands out as it does not require any special hardware or software, making it a versatile solution for model compression in deep learning.
