The paper presents results of model pruning on VGG-16 and ResNet architectures using the CIFAR-10 dataset, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed CFP (Correlation-based Filter Pruning) method. In the VGG-16 experiments, the baseline model achieved an error rate of 6.51% with a specific number of FLOPs. The CFP-1 and CFP-2 models showed slightly higher error rates of 6.77% and 7.02% respectively while significantly reducing FLOPs, with CFP-2 achieving 81.93% pruning.

For the ResNet-56 architecture, the baseline error was 6.43%. The pruning cycles effectively reduced FLOPs while maintaining competitive accuracy. The CFP method outperformed previously established methods, achieving a 76.56% reduction in FLOPs with only a 7.37% error rate, compared to other methods that had higher error rates with similar pruning percentages.

The study also extends to ResNet-50 on the ImageNet dataset, where the proposed method demonstrated comparable FLOPs pruning to existing approaches but with substantially improved accuracy. 

An ablation study further investigates the correlation-based criterion's role in maintaining information during pruning and the benefits of fine-tuning post-pruning. Overall, the results underscore the efficacy of the CFP method in achieving high FLOPs reduction while preserving model accuracy across different architectures and datasets.
