The references provided encompass a wide range of research on neural network compression techniques, particularly focusing on methods such as pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation. Key contributions include:

1. Structural compression through filter pruning (Abbasi-Asl and Yu, 2017) and redundant feature pruning for efficient convolutional networks (Ayinde and Zurada, 2018).
2. Learning to optimize the number of neurons in deep networks (Alvarez and Salzmann, 2016).
3. Techniques for reducing overfitting by decorrelating representations (Cogswell et al., 2016) and exploiting linear structures within networks for efficient evaluation (Denton et al., 2014).
4. Advanced pruning methods such as auto-balanced filter pruning (Ding et al., 2018) and geometric median-based pruning (He et al., 2019).
5. Deep compression strategies that combine pruning, quantization, and Huffman coding (Han et al., 2016).
6. Bayesian approaches for model compression and efficient inference (Louizos et al., 2017; Neklyudov et al., 2017).
7. Innovations like the Thinet method for filter-level pruning (Luo et al., 2017) and contextual point-wise convolution for object recognition (Mazumder et al., 2019).

These references collectively illustrate the ongoing efforts to enhance the efficiency of deep learning models while maintaining performance, which is critical for deploying models on resource-constrained devices.
