The paper introduces a novel approach for compressing deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by leveraging filter correlations. The method iteratively identifies pairs of highly correlated filters and discards one from each pair. Importantly, rather than simply removing a filter, the model is re-optimized to enhance the correlation between the remaining filters, minimizing information loss. After this initial filter removal, the model undergoes fine-tuning to recover from any small loss incurred during compression.

The authors evaluate their approach through extensive experiments and ablation studies, demonstrating that their method achieves state-of-the-art compression rates in terms of floating point operations per second (FLOPs) across various benchmark architectures, including LeNet-5, VGG-16, and ResNet-50, while maintaining strong predictive performance in tasks such as object detection.

The paper also discusses the limitations of existing model compression techniques, categorizing them into three main types: sparsity-based methods, quantization-based pruning, and filter pruning. While sparsity and quantization can enhance compression, they often require special hardware or software. Filter pruning, which the proposed method falls under, is more practical and offers significant compression without needing additional support. However, traditional filter pruning methods often struggle with accurately assessing filter importance and fail to address redundancy in the model.

Overall, the proposed filter correlation-based approach aims to enhance the efficiency of deep learning models by effectively reducing their complexity while preserving performance.
