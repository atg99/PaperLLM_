The paper discusses a novel approach to filter pruning in neural networks, focusing on the correlation between filters to enhance model compression without sacrificing performance. Traditional methods that increase the ℓ2 penalty often lead to underfitting, limiting their effectiveness in pruning filters. In contrast, the proposed method aims to learn a minimal basis of uncorrelated filters.

The authors define "Ready-to-prune" (Ri) as filter pairs selected based on their correlation and "Pruned" (Pi) as the filters chosen for removal post-optimization. The correlation coefficient is calculated using the expected values and standard deviations of filter outputs, allowing the identification of highly correlated filters. The most correlated pairs are considered less important, and through the process detailed in the paper, a selected set of filter pairs (St) is formed for optimization.

The optimization involves a new regularizer that encourages high correlation between filter pairs while maintaining overall model performance. The objective function incorporates this regularizer to maximize the correlation magnitude, facilitating the safe removal of one filter from each correlated pair. This method aims to enhance model efficiency by reducing redundancy among filters while preserving their functional integrity.
