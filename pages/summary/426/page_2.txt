The paper presents Decoupled Faster R-CNN (DeFRCN), an enhanced architecture for few-shot object detection that addresses the limitations of the conventional Faster R-CNN model. The authors identify significant conflicts in joint optimization between the class-agnostic Region Proposal Network (RPN) and the class-relevant RCNN, particularly due to their shared backbone. This conflict is exacerbated in few-shot scenarios where limited training samples are available. 

To mitigate these issues, DeFRCN employs two key strategies: multi-stage decoupling and multi-task decoupling. The architecture integrates Gradient Decoupled Layers (GDL) to modulate the interaction between the backbone, RPN, and RCNN, allowing for tailored decoupling through gradient manipulation. Additionally, a Prototypical Calibration Block (PCB) enhances classification performance by calibrating scores in the classification branch while maintaining separation from the regression task.

The main contributions of the paper are threefold: it introduces DeFRCN as an effective solution for few-shot detection, proposes GDL and PCB to improve model performance in data-scarce conditions, and demonstrates that DeFRCN outperforms state-of-the-art methods across various benchmarks. Overall, the proposed architecture aims to enhance both classification and localization tasks in object detection by addressing inherent task conflicts and optimizing feature utilization.
