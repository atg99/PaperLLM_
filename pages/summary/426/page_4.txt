The paper discusses the problem setting for few-shot object detection, organizing the learning process into a two-stage fine-tuning paradigm. This involves utilizing a large base set with abundant annotations (Dbase) for initial training and adapting quickly on a novel support set (Dnovel) with limited samples. The goal is to optimize a robust detector capable of classifying and localizing unlabelled objects from a query set (Dquery) containing classes from both the base and novel sets.

The authors revisit the Faster R-CNN architecture, which consists of a shared convolutional backbone, a Region Proposal Network (RPN), and an RCNN head. The backbone extracts features, while RPN generates class-agnostic proposals and the RCNN head performs classification and localization. The learning process follows a multi-task learning paradigm, aiming to jointly optimize these components.

However, the paper identifies problems in this multi-task learning approach. The RPN and RCNN have inconsistent optimization goals, which may lead to suboptimal solutions for individual tasks. Additionally, the shared backbone can create conflicts that diminish the few-shot detection capabilities of the framework, particularly during the transition from base to novel tasks, leading to confusion between foreground and background classes.

To address these issues, the authors propose Decoupled Faster R-CNN (DeFRCN). This approach decouples the functional modules of the model and the tasks of classification and localization, employing Gradient Decoupled Layers to manage the degree of decoupling and an offline Prototypical Calibration Block to enhance classification performance during inference. This method aims to leverage the full potential of Faster R-CNN in few-shot scenarios.
