The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the DeFRCN model for few-shot object detection (FSOD) across different datasets and settings. The results are compared against several state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods, highlighting the performance improvements achieved by DeFRCN. 

In the experiments conducted on the VOC dataset, DeFRCN consistently outperformed other methods in terms of average precision (AP50) across varying shot numbers, particularly excelling in the G-FSOD setting. Notably, the model demonstrated a significant performance drop when the number of shots increased from 5 to 10 in some instances, suggesting that the quality of samples is crucial in data-scarce scenarios. 

On the COCO dataset, DeFRCN achieved superior mean average precision (mAP) compared to existing methods, with marked improvements in both FSOD and G-FSOD settings. The results indicated robust performance, particularly in the 10-shot and 30-shot scenarios, underscoring the model's strong generalization capabilities. 

Furthermore, cross-domain experiments revealed that DeFRCN achieved a mAP of 55.9% on the VOC novel set, outperforming previous methods by a substantial margin, thus demonstrating its enhanced generalization ability in cross-domain contexts. 

Overall, the findings suggest that DeFRCN not only maintains a competitive number of learnable parameters but also effectively mitigates overfitting, making it a promising approach for few-shot object detection tasks.
