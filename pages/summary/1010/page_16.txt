The paper discusses the potential of neural network (NN) models in integrating data from various laboratories, suggesting they may outperform traditional machine learning (ML) models in extracting relevant features for clinical applications. However, it acknowledges limitations in its methodology, such as the potential oversight of related articles due to specific keyword choices and the exclusive use of PubMed for a nearly decade-long literature review. The authors also note that they focused primarily on commonly used models without delving into less common alternatives.

The authors advocate for the concept of Clinlabomics, which leverages machine learning on laboratory data for personalized medicine, highlighting its advantages such as cost-effectiveness, reduction of unnecessary treatments, and lower toxicity risks. They call for more attention from clinicians and researchers to this emerging field, emphasizing the need for standardized clinical test data across medical institutions to enhance diagnostic accuracy and treatment efficacy, ultimately contributing to the advancement of precision medicine.

The paper concludes with acknowledgments of contributions from various authors and mentions funding sources, asserting no competing interests.
