Clinlabomics plays a significant role in clinical laboratory management by enhancing laboratory efficiency and quality, particularly in resource-limited settings. It addresses issues such as laboratory quality control, reference range formulation, and automated interpretation of test results. A critical preanalytic error in laboratory medicine is the wrong blood in tube (WBIT) error, which can lead to mislabeling of specimens. Continuous monitoring of specimen acceptability can help identify and rectify such errors, ultimately improving patient care and reducing unnecessary procedures.

Research by Rosenbaum demonstrated the effectiveness of machine learning algorithms in detecting WBIT errors, achieving a notable area under the curve of 0.97 with a support vector machine algorithm. Similarly, Farrell's study utilized various machine learning models to identify mislabeled samples, with an artificial neural network achieving 92.1% accuracy, surpassing human performance at 77.8%.

Additionally, serum quality in the pre-analytical phase is crucial, as shown in Fang's study, where machine learning models effectively identified clot specimens, achieving high ROC curve values. Wilkes explored the use of machine learning classifiers on urine steroid profile data, yielding a mean ROC curve area of 0.955 for binary classification and a balanced accuracy of 0.873 for multiclass interpretation.

Furthermore, Salama's work with deep neural networks aimed to enhance the detection of minimal residual disease in chronic lymphocytic leukemia, showing strong correlation with expert analysis. These studies collectively highlight the transformative potential of machine learning in improving laboratory management and patient outcomes.
