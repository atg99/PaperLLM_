The paper by Wen et al. presents a summary of various applications of Clinlabomics, highlighting its role in clinical prediction and diagnosis. The research spans multiple years and sample sizes, employing different analytical models to achieve specific objectives. 

In clinical prediction, significant studies include the use of Deep Neural Networks (DNN) for biological aging prediction with 149,000 samples (2019) and smoking status prediction with 62,419 samples (2016). Additionally, Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) was utilized in 2021 to predict diabetes risk among 285,965 diabetes patients and over 1.2 million healthy samples. Prognosis for paraquat poisoning was predicted using Support Vector Machine (SVM) with 79 patients in 2017. Other notable predictions include ovarian cancer (2020, Decision Tree Model) and COVID-19 mortality (2021, XGBoost).

In the realm of clinical diagnosis, applications included an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) for diagnosing iron deficiency anemia in 203 patients (2012) and the Mahalanobis–Taguchi System (MTS) for asthma diagnosis in a cohort of 355 asthma patients. Logistic Regression (LR) was employed for chronic kidney disease severity diagnosis (551 patients, 2019) and colorectal cancer diagnosis (2021, 1168 patients). Random Forest (RF) was used for diagnosing COVID-19 infection (279 subjects in 2020) and lung cancer (183 patients in 2019).

Overall, the table highlights the diverse applications of machine learning techniques in improving clinical outcomes through effective prediction and diagnosis across various conditions.
