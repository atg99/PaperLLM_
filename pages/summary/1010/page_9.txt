The paper discusses the application of deep neural networks (DNNs) and machine learning techniques in clinical laboratory settings, particularly for predicting human chronological age and various diseases. A study by Putin utilized a modular ensemble of 21 DNNs trained on over 60,000 blood test samples, achieving an accuracy of 81.5% in age prediction. Key predictors identified included albumin, glucose, alkaline phosphatase, urea, and red blood cells.

Further, the paper highlights the importance of early diabetes prediction, citing research by Yang that analyzed over 1.5 million records from healthy individuals and diabetes patients. This study integrated laboratory values, demographics, and vital signs, employing statistical methods to rank features for a computational model.

The document also provides a summary of various applications of machine learning in clinical laboratory management, showcasing different models and their objectives, such as identifying wrong blood tube errors, mislabeled samples, and improving workflow efficiency in chronic lymphocytic leukemia detection. Additionally, it mentions the establishment of reference intervals for thyroid-related hormones and screening urine samples using advanced algorithms.
