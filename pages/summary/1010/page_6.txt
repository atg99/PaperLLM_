Clinlabomics in clinical settings highlights the importance of integrating artificial intelligence (AI) with routine clinical laboratory tests to enhance disease diagnosis and treatment outcomes. Various studies have established the prognostic significance of specific clinical scores and ratios, such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and modified Glasgow prognostic score (mGPS), across multiple cancers including gastric and colorectal cancer. These scores, which are based on readily available test items, reinforce the notion that inexpensive clinical evaluations can significantly impact patient management.

AI, particularly machine learning (ML), has revolutionized medical practice by enabling the analysis of extensive clinical datasets. This technology aids in identifying disease subtypes, prognostic indicators, and generating new hypotheses, thereby transforming clinical decision-making. The progression of clinical laboratory automation and data standardization has enabled the formation of comprehensive clinical databases that enhance diagnostic capabilities.

Clinlabomics utilizes routine blood and body fluid tests to extract and analyze key clinical indices linked to relevant diseases. By applying advanced ML techniques, it aims to establish predictive models that improve diagnostic accuracy, risk stratification, and prognostic assessments. This approach not only supplements existing clinical predictors but also uncovers additional insights from laboratory data that are crucial for patient care.
