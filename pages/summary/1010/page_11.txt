The study by Wen et al. explores the use of routine blood tests and demographic factors to develop predictive models for chronic kidney disease (CKD) severity and other health conditions. It highlights nine models that utilize statistical, machine learning, and neural network approaches to predict urinary protein progression in CKD patients, achieving an average AUC above 0.87 and precision over 0.8, with logistic regression yielding the highest AUC of 0.873.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the application of Clinlabomics in detecting COVID-19 and various cancers. Brinati's models based on hematochemical values demonstrated accuracy rates between 82% and 86%, while Domínguez-Olmedo's model effectively predicted COVID-19 mortality using laboratory values. Simon's work on brain tumor diagnosis achieved a sensitivity of 96% and specificity of 74% through a machine learning model, indicating the potential of routine blood tests to enhance diagnostic accuracy compared to traditional imaging methods. Similarly, Wu's random forest model identified lung cancer with high sensitivity and specificity, while Li's models for colorectal cancer showed logistic regression as the most effective with an AUC of 0.865.

Overall, the findings underscore the growing significance of Clinlabomics in precision medicine, offering more convenient, accurate, and cost-effective diagnostic methods, while revealing that routine blood tests contain valuable information often overlooked by clinicians.
