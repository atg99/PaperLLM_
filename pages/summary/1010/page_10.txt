The paper discusses the application of Clinlabomics in the prediction and diagnosis of clinical diseases, highlighting its effectiveness in risk assessment and diagnostic accuracy. A diabetes risk assessment model utilizing a selection strategy combined with XGBoost achieved a high accuracy (AUC = 0.8763), demonstrating that Clinlabomics can assist high-risk individuals in making timely lifestyle changes to reduce diabetes risk. Additionally, Chen's research on predicting paraquat poisoning risk through support vector machine methods revealed significant differences in blood routine indexes, particularly in white blood cells and neutrophils, suggesting that blood tests can preliminarily predict toxicity outcomes.

The paper also emphasizes the role of Clinlabomics in clinical diagnosis. Muhsen's summary of machine learning applications in hematology highlighted the development of an artificial neural network (ANN) and an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS) for diagnosing iron deficiency anemia (IDA), achieving up to 97% accuracy. Zhan's study on asthma used routine blood test data to construct a Mahalanobis space, classifying 94.15% of asthma patients and 97.20% of healthy individuals accurately, showcasing Clinlabomics' potential to simplify diagnostic processes. Overall, the findings illustrate that Clinlabomics can enhance predictive models and diagnostic accuracy using standard blood tests.
