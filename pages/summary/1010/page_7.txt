The paper by Wen et al. discusses Clinlabomics, a proposed concept aimed at enhancing disease diagnosis, treatment, and prediction through the integration of clinical laboratory data and artificial intelligence (AI). It highlights the current challenges in the field, particularly the lack of standardized terminology such as transcriptomics and metabolomics, which hampers proper classification of research projects.

The authors note the growing trend of using clinical laboratory testing data with AI for various medical applications, including disease diagnosis and prognosis. A specific study is summarized, which focuses on predicting biological aging and assessing the impact of smoking. Researchers utilized a dataset of 149,000 anonymized records to train deep neural networks that could predict chronological age based on data from non-smokers. By incorporating smoking status as a feature, they developed a model that demonstrated smoking accelerates aging and that smoking status can be inferred from blood biochemical and cell count markers.

Although the blood aging clock model was found to be less accurate than DNA methylation-based predictors, it offers a more cost-effective and practical approach. The paper concludes with a workflow diagram illustrating the Clinlabomics process, which encompasses sample collection, feature extraction from clinical test data, and analysis for diagnostic and prognostic applications, ultimately aiming to advance precision and personalized medicine.
