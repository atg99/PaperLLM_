The papers listed focus on various aspects of reinforcement learning, particularly in hierarchical structures and multi-agent systems. 

Stulp and Schaal propose a method for hierarchical reinforcement learning that incorporates movement primitives, emphasizing the organization of actions for humanoid robots. Konidaris et al. discuss autonomous skill acquisition in mobile manipulators, highlighting the importance of learning skills to improve robotic performance. They further delve into learning symbolic representations for high-level planning, which is crucial for abstract reasoning in robotics.

Wu et al. introduce spatial action maps as a means to enhance mobile manipulation, while He et al. address efficient planning under uncertainty for micro-aerial vehicles. Hsiao et al. present task-driven tactile exploration, which focuses on using touch feedback in robotic tasks.

Lee et al. propose MAGIC, a system for learning macro-actions in online POMDP planning, while Theocharous and Kaelbling explore approximate planning in POMDPs with macro-actions. Amato and colleagues investigate planning with macro-actions in decentralized POMDPs, emphasizing multi-robot coordination under uncertainty.

Subsequent work by Amato and others expands on decentralized control of multiple robots, addressing challenges in uncertain environments. Hoang et al. present adversarial policy switching for decentralized systems, while Omidshaﬁei et al. explore graph-based methods for solving multi-robot decentralized POMDPs and the use of belief space macro-actions.

Finally, research by Schroeder de Witt et al. and Han et al. contributes to multi-agent reinforcement learning, focusing on common knowledge among agents and dynamic termination in hierarchical frameworks. Collectively, these contributions advance the understanding and application of reinforcement learning in robotics and multi-agent systems.
