This paper presents a novel approach to asynchronous multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) utilizing macro-actions under partial observability. It introduces several methods, including decentralized actor-critic (Mac-IAC) and centralized actor-critic (Mac-CAC) frameworks, as well as two centralized training with decentralized execution methods (Naive Mac-IACC and Mac-IAICC). These methods effectively incorporate controllers with varying execution times, enabling agents to learn high-quality policies for asynchronous collaboration in complex, long-horizon tasks.

The empirical results demonstrate that Mac-IAICC outperforms other approaches, allowing agents to execute macro-actions efficiently while minimizing delays and optimizing tool delivery in a warehouse scenario. The hardware experiments reveal collaborative behaviors among robots, showcasing their ability to find and deliver tools effectively without unnecessary waiting, thus enhancing operational efficiency.

Overall, this work lays the groundwork for future developments in macro-action-based MARL algorithms and highlights the potential for integrating various policy gradient methods and learning macro-actions within multi-agent systems. The research is supported by multiple funding sources, and acknowledgments are given to contributors of the hardware experiments.
