The paper discusses the MacDec-POMDP framework, a model designed for cooperative multi-agent settings that face partial observability and varying action durations, enabling asynchronous decision-making among agents. While previous research in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has focused on hierarchical approaches, they often overlook the asynchronicity inherent in real-world scenarios. The authors emphasize that existing studies on asynchronicity lack a comprehensive formulation for multi-agent policy gradients that accommodate asynchronous learning and execution.

The paper introduces macro-actions as predefined actions for each agent, analogous to how primitive actions are treated in traditional reinforcement learning. The authors propose a suite of macro-action-based multi-agent actor-critic methods, beginning with a macro-action-based independent actor-critic (Mac-IAC) method, which allows for online learning despite the challenges posed by environmental non-stationarity.

Additionally, they present a centralized actor-critic (Mac-CAC) method that operates under full communication conditions and a centralized training for decentralized execution (CTDE) variant. This approach leverages centralized information during training to enhance decentralized policy learning. A Naive Independent Actor with Centralized Critic (Naive IACC) method is introduced, which utilizes a joint macro-action-value function for policy gradient estimation. The paper then proposes the Independent Actor with Individual Centralized Critic (Mac-IAICC) method, which aims to refine this process by allowing individual critics to learn from centralized data.

The proposed methods are evaluated across different macro-action-based tasks, including benchmark domains like Box Pushing and Overcooked, as well as a larger warehouse service domain. Results indicate that the new methods outperform traditional primitive-action-based approaches, particularly highlighting the effectiveness of Mac-IAICC in developing decentralized policies. Furthermore, the decentralized policies derived from Mac-IAICC were successfully implemented in real robots for warehouse tool delivery tasks. This work represents a significant advancement in formalizing macro-action-based multi-agent actor-critic frameworks within contemporary multi-agent training paradigms.
