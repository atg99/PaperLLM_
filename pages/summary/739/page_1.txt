The paper presents a novel approach to multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) by introducing asynchronous actor-critic methods that address the limitations of existing policy gradient methods. Traditional MARL frameworks face challenges when synchronizing decisions among agents, which can hinder performance in complex, real-world scenarios. The authors propose asynchronous learning that allows agents to operate independently, optimizing their policies without waiting for others, thus accommodating temporally extended actions that vary in execution time based on context.

The study outlines three training paradigms: decentralized learning, centralized learning, and centralized training for decentralized execution, enabling agents to effectively learn in asynchronous environments. Empirical evidence from simulations and hardware tests across various domains demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed methods, highlighting their ability to tackle large-scale multi-agent problems and produce high-quality solutions. The work underscores the potential of asynchronous actor-critic methods in enhancing the scalability and complexity management of multi-agent systems.
