The paper discusses advancements in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) with a focus on decentralized partially observable Markov decision processes (Dec-POMDPs) and the integration of macro-actions. It emphasizes the use of a centralized critic to aid in optimizing the decentralized policies for better overall performance, despite introducing additional variance in the actions of other agents. 

The authors present the Independent Actor with Centralized Critic (IACC) model, which employs a variance reduction technique to enhance policy gradient updates. The policy gradient is defined in a way that incorporates centralized information, such as joint observations and action histories.

The study also highlights the limitations of existing MARL methods when dealing with asynchronous macro-action-based agents, where agents may execute macro-actions at different time intervals. To address this, the paper introduces the Macro-Action Concurrent Experience Replay Trajectories (Mac-CERTs) buffer, which collects data on each agent's macro-observations, actions, and rewards. The transition experiences are structured to facilitate training through a mini-batch sampling process that focuses on completed macro-actions.

Additionally, the paper presents the Macro-Action Joint Experience Replay Trajectories (Mac-JERTs) buffer for centralized learning, which gathers joint transition experiences across agents. The paper proposes a method for optimizing joint macro-action-value functions using temporal difference learning based on these joint experiences.

The authors further propose on-policy macro-action-based actor-critic methods tailored for decentralized, centralized, and centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE). They outline a Macro-Action-Based Independent Actor-Critic (Mac-IAC) approach that allows agents to independently optimize their macro-action policies while considering local and joint histories. The proposed methods aim to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of policy updates in asynchronous settings.
