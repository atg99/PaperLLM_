The paper investigates the performance of macro-action-based asynchronous actor-critic methods in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) environments, specifically focusing on their scalability and effectiveness compared to value-based approaches. The study reveals that both Mac-IAICC and Naive Mac-IACC excel in smaller environments, such as Warehouse A. However, as the complexity increases in larger environments (Warehouses B, C, and D), Naive Mac-IACC struggles due to mismatches in termination perspectives between individual agents and the centralized system. This results in a significant drop in its performance, even falling below that of Mac-IAC in Warehouse D. In contrast, Mac-IAICC maintains superior performance with lower variance, demonstrating its scalability and the advantages of having individual critics for each agent to manage varying levels of asynchronicity in decision-making.

Additionally, the paper compares actor-critic methods with value-based approaches, finding that while value-based methods (like Mac-Dec-Q and Mac-Cen-Q) offer better sample efficiency in certain scenarios, they struggle in more complex environments due to the large joint action spaces. Actor-critic methods, on the other hand, effectively scale to larger domains and yield high-quality solutions, particularly highlighted in the Box Pushing task and Warehouse A. The findings underscore the potential of macro-action-based actor-critic methods in complex MARL settings, emphasizing their robustness and adaptability.
