This collection of references highlights significant advancements in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) and its applications. Notably, the works of Nachum et al. (2019) and Wang et al. (2020b) focus on manipulating agents through locomotion and decentralized rendezvous strategies, respectively. The concept of learning roles to decompose tasks is explored by Wang et al. (2021b), while Xu et al. (2021) introduce a hierarchical cooperative framework with dual coordination mechanisms.

Yang et al. (2020b) investigate skill discovery in hierarchical cooperative MARL, and Chakravorty et al. (2019) discuss the option-critic framework within cooperative systems. Menda et al. (2019) address event-driven decision processes, and Xiao et al. (2019) propose macro-action strategies for deep MARL.

Wu et al. (2021a) present spatial intention maps for mobile manipulation, and Kraemer and Banerjee (2016) examine rehearsal methods for decentralized planning. The foundational work of Oliehoek et al. (2008) on decentralized partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) is also crucial in understanding optimal and approximate Q-value functions.

Wu et al. (2021b) delve into coordination through inverse planning, while Sutton et al. (1999) and Sutton et al. (2000) provide essential frameworks and methods for temporal abstraction and policy gradient approaches in reinforcement learning. Hausknecht and Stone (2015) contribute to the understanding of partially observable MDPs, and Konda and Tsitsiklis (2000) discuss actor-critic algorithms, which are vital for effective learning in MARL contexts. Lastly, Sutton (1988) and Weaver and Tao (2001) address predictive learning and optimal reward baselines, respectively, further enriching the theoretical landscape of reinforcement learning.
