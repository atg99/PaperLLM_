The paper discusses the development of macro-action-based policies within the framework of multi-agent reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on the policy gradient theorem and its extensions to multi-agent settings. The authors derive a macro-action-based policy gradient theorem by integrating the Bellman equation for macro-action policies into the foundational policy gradient theorem for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). This extension is then applied to MacDec-POMDPs, allowing each agent to optimize its policy based on its macro-actions.

Key components include the training of a critic using on-policy temporal-difference learning, where agents utilize their own trajectories. The paper introduces a local history value function to serve as a baseline for variance reduction in policy updates. The policy gradient for each agent is derived and expressed in terms of the cumulative rewards associated with their macro-actions.

The paper also presents three algorithms: 

1. **Macro-Action-Based Centralized Actor-Critic (Mac-CAC)**, where agents are treated as a single unit to learn a centralized actor and critic, optimizing joint actions through a combined policy gradient.

2. **Macro-Action-Based Independent Actor with Centralized Critic (Mac-IACC)**, designed to address the limitations of fully centralized and decentralized learning by utilizing a centralized critic to improve decentralized policy learning.

3. **Naive Mac-IACC**, which adapts the existing IACC framework to incorporate macro-actions, offering a simplistic approach to utilizing a joint macro-action-value function while maintaining a decentralized policy gradient.

The paper emphasizes the challenges posed by joint macro-action termination and the necessity for a centralized critic in the decentralized context, ultimately aiming to develop more effective learning strategies for agents operating in complex environments.
