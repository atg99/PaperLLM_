This paper investigates the effectiveness of using macro-actions in decentralized learning for robotic tasks compared to primitive actions. The analysis focuses on two domains: Box Pushing and Overcooked. In the Box Pushing domain, using primitive actions leads to intractable problems, causing robots to perform poorly. In contrast, the Mac-CAC approach utilizing macro-actions significantly improves performance, allowing robots to coordinate effectively when pushing a large box. However, the decentralized critic struggles to attribute penalties accurately, which leads to suboptimal behavior.

In the Overcooked domain, the paper highlights the importance of asynchronous independent subtasks for effective teamwork among robots. The Mac-IAC method excels in this scenario, demonstrating that local information suffices for achieving high-quality outcomes. While Mac-CAC learns slower due to the complexity of joint information, it still performs better in long-horizon tasks than primitive-action-based methods.

The results indicate that having individual centralized critics enhances performance. In Box Pushing, Mac-IAICC consistently outperforms Naive Mac-IACC, especially as the task complexity increases. In the Overcooked scenarios, Mac-IAICC shows better sample efficiency and stability compared to other methods. Conversely, Mac-IAC struggles due to its limitations in partial observability, and while Mac-CAC benefits from global information initially, it eventually gets trapped in local optima.

Overall, the findings suggest that macro-actions and centralized critics lead to more efficient learning and better performance in multi-agent robotic systems, particularly in complex environments.
