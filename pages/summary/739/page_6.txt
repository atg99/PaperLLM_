The paper discusses the Independent Actor with Individual Centralized Critic (Mac-IAICC) approach for multi-agent reinforcement learning, specifically addressing issues related to asynchronous macro-action terminations. It identifies two main problems with the naive Mac-IACC: the cumulative reward may not accurately reflect the value of an agent's macro-action due to reliance on joint terminations, and this can lead to high variance in policy gradient estimations. 

To improve upon this, the authors propose a method where each agent learns a separate centralized critic through Temporal Difference (TD) learning. This critic updates based on the reward accumulated specifically from the agent's own macro-action, which enhances the accuracy of the value prediction for the macro-action. The resulting policy gradient estimation, incorporating the updated reward and value function, reduces variance and improves learning efficiency.

The paper then presents simulation experiments across various multi-agent environments, including Box Pushing and Overcooked, where macro-actions are defined based on domain knowledge. These environments allow agents to learn complex policies by combining macro-actions with primitive actions. The experimental setup and results are elaborated in the subsequent sections of the paper.
