The PTDAE+DNN model excels in detecting attacks categorized as DDoS, DoS, Bot, and Brute Force, achieving detection rates close to 100%. In contrast, the model struggles with infiltration attacks, identifying only 23.69% of these attempts, largely due to the nature of such attacks being stealthy and often misclassified as benign packets. Additionally, web attack categories, including Brute Force Web, brute force-XSS, and SQL injection, also present challenges, with detection rates falling below 70%. This low performance is attributed to insufficient training data for these specific attack classes.

The study concludes that while the PTDAE+DNN model shows promise in classifying and detecting various malicious attacks effectively, there remains a need for enhanced training data and strategies to improve detection rates, particularly for infiltration and web-based attacks. The comparison of feature extraction methods highlights the advantages of the PTDAE approach in achieving superior classification performance across datasets.
