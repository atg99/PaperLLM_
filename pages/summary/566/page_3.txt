The paper presents a framework for improving intrusion detection systems (IDS) using a hybrid model that combines Deep Autoencoders (DAE) for feature extraction and Deep Neural Networks (DNN) for attack classification. The authors emphasize the importance of hyperparameter optimization (HPO) in enhancing the performance of machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) algorithms, citing methods such as Bayesian optimization and random search to fine-tune hyperparameters effectively.

The proposed architecture consists of three main stages: data preparation and preprocessing, development of the deep structured learning model with HPO, and evaluation of classifiers to determine the best performing model. The hyperparameter tuning process focuses on key parameters including the number of hidden layers, learning rate, batch size, and activation functions, utilizing grid search and random search techniques to identify optimal values.

The DAE model, characterized by multiple hidden layers, performs encoding and decoding to create a low-dimensional representation of the input data. This representation is then used in the DNN for binary and multi-class attack classification. The paper details the encoding and decoding phases, including the mathematical formulations for the forward propagation and the cost function, which is minimized through backpropagation.

The effectiveness of the proposed IDS framework is evaluated using the NSL-KDD and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 datasets, demonstrating improved detection rates for both binary and multi-class attack classifications due to the optimized feature extraction and classification processes. The results underline the significance of combining DAE for feature extraction with DNN for classification in enhancing IDS performance.
