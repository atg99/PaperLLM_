The paper discusses the impact of hyperparameters on the performance of deep learning models in intrusion detection, specifically analyzing two datasets: NSL-KDD and CSE-CIC-IDS2018. It presents a comprehensive overview of various models generated through combinations of hyperparameters, detailing the number of models created based on different configurations of hidden layers in Denoising Autoencoders (DAE) and Deep Neural Networks (DNN).

Key findings indicate that the learning rate is the most influential hyperparameter affecting detection rates, followed by the number of hidden layers. The study employs different activation functions, with ReLU identified as yielding the best performance. The Adam optimization algorithm is utilized, focusing on tuning the learning rate while keeping other parameters at default values.

The results show that for the NSL-KDD dataset, the learning rate significantly impacts model performance, while the batch size does not have a substantial effect on detection sensitivity. Conversely, in the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset, a smaller learning rate enhances detection rates, with findings indicating that higher learning rates lead to performance degradation.

Overall, the research emphasizes the importance of hyperparameter tuning in improving the accuracy of intrusion detection systems, highlighting differences in the impact of parameters across diverse datasets. The paper concludes that careful selection and tuning of hyperparameters are crucial for optimizing model performance in cybersecurity applications.
