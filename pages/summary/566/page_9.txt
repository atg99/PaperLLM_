The paper discusses the impact of various hyperparameters on the performance of deep learning models for intrusion detection using two datasets: NSL-KDD and CSE-CIC-IDS2018. It highlights that while certain hyperparameters like learning rate and batch size play significant roles in model performance, their effects can vary between datasets.

The study finds that a high learning rate (0.1) generally results in lower detection performance across all models, while a lower learning rate is preferable for more complex models with multiple hidden layers due to the intricate error surfaces they present. The batch size is shown to significantly affect detection rates in the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset, with a batch size of 256 yielding more stable results, whereas in the NSL-KDD dataset, it primarily influences convergence speed without affecting performance.

Kernel initializers and activation functions also impact model performance. It is observed that the 'he_uniform' and 'lecun_uniform' initializers provide better average values for the NSL-KDD dataset, while 'he_uniform' and 'he_normal' yield more stable results for CSE-CIC-IDS2018. The ELU activation function is noted for its stability and robustness against noise, outperforming others in both datasets. Leaky ReLU and PReLU functions are also highlighted for their advantages in mitigating the "dying ReLU" problem.

The hyperparameter optimization (HPO) process involved testing numerous models across both datasets, revealing overfitting issues in the NSL-KDD dataset due to excessive learning for attack classification. In contrast, the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset demonstrated good performance without overfitting, especially when using deep learning models combined with denoising autoencoders (DAE) for feature extraction.

Finally, the paper presents the best-performing models along with precision, recall, and F1-score metrics, emphasizing the importance of weighted averages for evaluating performance in imbalanced class situations. The findings underscore the critical role of hyperparameter selection in optimizing model performance for intrusion detection tasks.
