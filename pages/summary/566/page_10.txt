The paper evaluates the performance of deep structured models using various configurations on the NSL-KDD and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 datasets. Key findings include the optimal configurations of deep neural networks (DNNs) with different numbers of hidden layers and hyperparameters. For the NSL-KDD dataset, the best DNN structure was identified as having four layers with a configuration of 122-100-20-100-122, utilizing ReLU and sigmoid activation functions. The learning rate of 0.01 was optimal for models with one to three hidden layers, while a smaller batch size (32) paired with a lower learning rate (0.0001) also yielded high performance.

In contrast, the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset showed slight improvements in classification performance with additional hidden layers, with an optimal learning rate of 0.001. The Leaky ReLU activation and lecun_uniform kernel initializer were most effective for models with two and four hidden layers. Results indicated that larger batch sizes (256) improved detection rates across models.

For multi-class classification, performance metrics such as true positive rate, false positive rate, and accuracy were thoroughly analyzed, with notable results showing a weight-average precision of 99.81%, recall of 99.81%, accuracy of 99.89%, and an F1-score of 99.81% for the best models trained on KDDTrain+ data. Overall, the study highlights the impact of architectural choices and hyperparameter tuning on the effectiveness of intrusion detection systems.
