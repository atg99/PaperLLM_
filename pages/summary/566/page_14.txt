The paper discusses the development of a deep learning-based intrusion detection system (DLIDS) with a focus on optimizing feature extraction and hyperparameter selection. It emphasizes the complexity involved in choosing the right deep learning architecture, including the number of hidden layers, neurons, learning rate, kernel initialization, activation functions, and optimization techniques. The authors advocate for the use of automatic hyperparameter optimization (HPO) techniques to identify the best combinations of parameters, noting that optimizing many hyperparameters simultaneously can extend processing time. They highlight the effectiveness of random search methods in accelerating model selection and improving attack detection rates.

Using the NSL-KDD and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 datasets, the study finds that the learning rate is a critical parameter affecting performance, alongside the choice of deep learning structures such as the number of layers and neurons. The authors compare various feature extraction methods utilizing different autoencoder models integrated with deep neural networks (DNNs), evaluating performance based on precision, recall, F1-Score, training, and prediction times. The proposed DAE+DNN architecture demonstrates superior performance relative to other recent studies.

Future work aims to explore other combinations of deep learning algorithms, feature extraction methods, and datasets, while also addressing performance enhancement techniques for imbalanced attack datasets.
