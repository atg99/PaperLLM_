The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of a deep learning-based intrusion detection system (IDS) using various datasets, specifically focusing on the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and NSL-KDD datasets. The study highlights the performance metrics of different deep neural network (DNN) architectures, including precision, recall, accuracy, and F1-score, under varying configurations of hidden layers and hyperparameters.

For the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset, the best-performing model achieved high metrics with a DNN structure featuring four hidden layers, yielding a precision of 95.38%, recall of 95.79%, accuracy of 95.79%, and an F1-score of 95.11%. The paper emphasizes the challenges in detecting certain attack classes, particularly the U2R and R2L attacks, which exhibited low true positive rates (TPR) due to their stealthy nature and limited training data representation. For instance, U2R attacks had a TPR of only 63.46% in training and significantly lower rates in testing datasets, indicating difficulties in detection influenced by the lack of sufficient data during the learning phase.

In contrast, the model demonstrated robust detection capabilities for DoS and probe attacks, with TPRs nearing 100% during training. However, the introduction of new subclasses in the testing data slightly reduced detection rates for these attacks. The findings suggest that while the proposed anomaly-based IDS is effective for many attack types, stealthy attacks require more advanced detection strategies that combine network anomaly detection with host-based monitoring.

Overall, the research underscores the importance of comprehensive training datasets and the need for adaptive models that can recognize diverse attack patterns, particularly for stealthy and evolving threats. The results indicate that the depth of the DNN does not significantly enhance performance, suggesting a need for further investigation into model architectures and training methodologies to improve detection rates across all attack classes.
