This paper addresses the limitations of existing methods in extracting long-range semantic information from skeleton sequences, which is crucial for understanding human actions. While previous approaches primarily utilized recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to model temporal and spatio-temporal dynamics, they often struggled with capturing long-range dependencies due to their local operation nature.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose a novel model called Self-Attention Network (SAN) and its variants (SAN-V1, SAN-V2, SAN-V3). This model leverages self-attention mechanisms to effectively correlate features across different positions in a sequence, allowing for better understanding of long-term dependencies. The framework is inspired by the Temporal Segment Network (TSN), which extracts short-term information from video sequences. The SAN variants compute weighted sums of features, enabling the model to capture deeper semantic understanding through consensus operations.

The key contributions of the paper include:
1. Introduction of SAN variants specifically designed for capturing deep semantic correlations from human skeleton action sequences.
2. Integration of TSN with SAN variants, resulting in improved performance on large-scale datasets.
3. Visualization of self-attention probabilities to elucidate the correlations between frames.
4. Achievement of state-of-the-art results on the NTU RGB+D and Kinetics-skeleton datasets.

The authors emphasize that while previous methodologies, including RNNs and CNNs, provided significant advancements, they fell short in extracting long-term correlations. The proposed SAN model effectively addresses this gap, enhancing the semantic extraction capabilities from skeleton sequences.
