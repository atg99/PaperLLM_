This paper discusses the development of a novel self-attention network (SAN) architecture aimed at enhancing skeleton-based action recognition. The SAN utilizes a multi-layer structure where hidden states in upper layers are constructed from those in lower layers through a self-attention mechanism. This mechanism aggregates information based on the similarity of hidden states, resulting in representations that effectively capture deep contextual information from input sequences. The SAN, particularly with multi-head attention, has shown superior performance in various tasks, including machine translation and natural language inference, often outperforming traditional recurrent neural networks.

The paper highlights the gap in the application of self-attentive networks for skeleton-based action recognition, despite their success in other areas. To address this, the authors propose a model that leverages both position and motion data of joints, encoding these sequences through simple non-linear projections and convolutional neural networks. The raw position of joints is transformed into velocity data by calculating the differences between consecutive frames. The proposed framework aims to efficiently capture contextual information using three different SAN-based architectures.

The self-attention mechanism itself computes correlations between arbitrary positions in the input sequence through an attention function involving queries, keys, and values. The output is generated as a weighted sum of values, with weights determined by a scaled dot-product of queries and keys. This approach allows the network to extract diverse correlation information through multiple attention heads, ultimately enhancing representation learning.

In summary, this paper presents a self-attention-based model for skeleton-based action recognition, detailing the encoding of raw joint position and motion data, and introducing a framework that effectively utilizes deep contextual information to improve action recognition performance.
