The paper discusses a framework for processing video data using Self-Attention Networks (SANs) to analyze skeleton joint movements over time. It outlines several variants of SAN, including SAN-V1, SAN-V2, and SAN-V3, each designed to capture different aspects of the input data.

The input consists of a sequence of skeleton joint data formatted as F×J′×C, which is processed through convolutional layers to produce an output tensor of size F×8×64. The SAN layers compute attention probabilities and use learned parameters to output concatenated sequences, ultimately leading to video-level features via a global average layer.

SAN-V1 serves as a baseline, combining position and motion data into a single input sequence for classification. It employs CNN and non-linear encoders to extract contextual information from the data. The classification layer follows a ReLU activation and dropout, predicting class probabilities based on cross-entropy loss.

SAN-V2 builds on this by focusing on individual subjects within a scene, combining their positional and motion data, and leveraging late fusion strategies to extract the strongest action signals. Element-wise max operations are found to be most effective in this context.

SAN-V3 introduces a dual approach for handling different modalities of data (position and velocity), using separate classifiers for each and aggregating their outputs. This variant is also scalable to varying numbers of subjects.

Additionally, the paper presents the Temporal Segment Self-Attention Network (TS-SAN), which enhances the model's ability to capture long-range information by dividing videos into segments. Each segment is processed with shared weights across clips, improving the learning of temporal dynamics.

Overall, the proposed SAN framework is designed to effectively analyze complex movement patterns in video data, accommodating varying numbers of subjects and different data modalities.
