The references provided encompass a range of studies focused on action recognition in computer vision, particularly utilizing deep learning techniques. Key contributions include the modeling of temporal dynamics and spatial configurations through two-stream recurrent neural networks, as presented by H. Wang and L. Wang. L. Wang and colleagues introduced a latent hierarchical model for classifying complex activities based on their temporal structures. The development of temporal segment networks by L. Wang et al. emphasizes best practices in deep action recognition, while P. Wang and collaborators explored action recognition through joint trajectory maps with convolutional neural networks. The survey by D. Weinland et al. critically reviews vision-based methods for action representation and recognition. Furthermore, advancements in skeleton-based action recognition are highlighted by Yan et al. with spatial temporal graph convolutional networks and by Zhang et al. with view adaptive recurrent neural networks. Lastly, Zhu et al. addressed co-occurrence feature learning for skeleton-based actions using regularized deep LSTM networks. Collectively, these works contribute to the understanding and enhancement of action recognition systems in various contexts.
