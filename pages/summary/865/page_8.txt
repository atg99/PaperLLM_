This paper presents a conjecture that the self-attention output signals, derived from weighted average computations, are better aggregated using an element-wise average function. This approach is posited to enhance video-level self-attention computation, ultimately improving performance.

Section 5.4.4 investigates the effects of varying the number of layers and multi-heads within the SAN block of the TS-SAN-V2 model. It is found that while the number of heads has a marginal impact on performance, both excessive and insufficient parameters lead to underperformance. This suggests that an optimal configuration of layers and heads is necessary for achieving the best results on specific datasets.

In Section 5.5, the paper visualizes the self-attention layer's response to highlight frame correlations in action videos. Using a visualization tool, the authors show how different frames relate to each other across multiple heads. The visualization of self-attention responses indicates strong correlations, such as between frame 31 and frame 0, demonstrating the model's ability to capture both short and long-range correlations. The patterns observed for the same action class reveal similarities across different videos, even with variations in subjects and views, while differing action classes show distinct response patterns.

The conclusion emphasizes the development of three SAN variations that effectively extract high-level context from both short and long-range self-attentions, outperforming existing methods. The integration of SAN with temporal segment networks is noted to yield significant performance improvements. Extensive experiments conducted on NTU RGB+D and Kinetics-skeleton datasets validate the effectiveness of the proposed models for skeleton-based action recognition. Future work will focus on applying the model to video-based recognition tasks involving key point annotations and exploring new methods for feature representation extraction.
