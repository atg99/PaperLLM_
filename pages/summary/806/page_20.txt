This section discusses various hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) algorithms: Grid Search, Random Search, and Bayesian Optimization. The effectiveness of HPO is influenced by the selection of hyper-parameter ranges, which should be grounded in prior experience.

Grid Search, or Parameter Sweep, systematically explores all possible combinations of specified hyper-parameter values. While it allows for parallel execution of trials due to their independence, it suffers from the curse of dimensionality, leading to an exponential increase in trials as the number of parameters grows.

In contrast, Random Search samples trials randomly from the search space. Its key advantages include the ability to adjust the resolution of the search dynamically, an increased likelihood of finding the optimal hyper-parameter with more trials, and improved performance in low-dimensional search spaces, as it does not treat all hyper-parameters equally.

Bayesian Optimization (BO) employs a model-based, sequential approach, utilizing a surrogate function to estimate the objective function's value and guide the search process. This method refines its estimates based on prior trials, which allows it to require fewer trials to reach an optimum compared to Random Search. However, BO's sequential nature may lead to some inefficiencies, even though it can accommodate parallel trials.

A strategy for enhancing resource efficiency in these algorithms is Early Stopping, which terminates unpromising trials. Google's Vizier implements this through a Median Stopping Rule, optimizing the search process further.
