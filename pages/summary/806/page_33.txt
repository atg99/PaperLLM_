The paper discusses the balance between model quality and footprint in deep learning, emphasizing the trade-offs practitioners face when optimizing models. It introduces a framework for achieving pareto-optimal models, where one dimension, typically quality, is maximized while keeping another dimension, usually footprint, constant. Quality metrics include accuracy and precision, while footprint metrics encompass model size and latency. 

The authors highlight that deeper models tend to provide better accuracy but at the expense of increased size and latency. Conversely, shallower models may be more deployable but often yield lower accuracy. Strategies for managing these trade-offs include model shrinking through compression techniques like quantization and pruning, and model growth by adjusting capacity and hyper-parameters.

The paper proposes two main strategies: Shrink-and-Improve, aimed at reducing the model footprint without significantly compromising quality, which is particularly relevant for on-device and server-side applications. This approach advocates for minimally lossy shrinking methods, leveraging learned compression techniques and architecture search to achieve efficient deep learning models.
