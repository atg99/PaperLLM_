In the paper, Gaurav Menghani discusses quantization-aware training and its implications for model inference and learning techniques. Figure 6(a) illustrates the process of integrating fake-quantization nodes to simulate quantization effects, enabling the collection of tensor statistics for the development of a fully fixed-point inference graph, as shown in Figure 6(b). This graph operates with inputs and weights in uint8 format and performs operations in uint32 format, while biases remain in uint32.

The paper emphasizes learning techniques that enhance model performance metrics such as accuracy, F1 score, precision, and recall, while potentially reducing model size by decreasing the number of parameters or layers. These techniques focus solely on the training phase, leaving inference unaffected.

One key learning technique is distillation, which leverages ensembles to improve generalization by learning multiple independent hypotheses. The paper references various ensembling methods, including bagging, boosting, and averaging. It highlights Bucila et al.'s work, which demonstrated that a smaller neural network could achieve performance comparable to larger ensembles by learning from both labeled and weakly labeled synthetic data, thus showcasing the potential for knowledge transfer to smaller models.

Additionally, Hinton et al. introduced a method where smaller networks (students) learn from larger models (teachers) by extracting "dark knowledge" through soft-labels generated from the teacher model on existing labeled data. This approach allows the student model to capture the relationships between classes, enhancing its learning capability beyond traditional hard labels.
