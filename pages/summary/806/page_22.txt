The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), particularly highlighting the role of a controller that encodes the search space, generates candidate models, and utilizes feedback for improvement. It references the work by Zoph et al., which demonstrated that Reinforcement Learning can be employed to create end-to-end neural network architectures. Their approach involves a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) as the controller, generating hyper-parameters layer by layer, including aspects like the number of filters and skip connections. The training process for the controller is computationally intensive, requiring substantial GPU resources.

In subsequent research, Zoph et al. refined their methodology by focusing on searching for individual cells—Normal and Reduction cells—rather than complete architectures, which significantly enhanced search efficiency. This cell-based approach led to a sevenfold reduction in search time while achieving state-of-the-art results on CIFAR-10.

The paper also mentions alternative techniques like evolutionary strategies and differentiable architecture search that aim to lower the computational costs associated with architecture search. While early research prioritized accuracy without considering resource constraints, there is now a focus on balancing quality and efficiency. MNasNet exemplifies this multi-objective approach by optimizing for both accuracy and latency.
