The paper discusses the concept of model distillation in deep learning, where a smaller student network is trained to mimic a larger teacher model. The student minimizes a combined loss function that includes both the traditional cross-entropy loss from hard labels and a distillation loss from softened probabilities generated by the teacher model. The softening process uses a temperature parameter to adjust the output distribution, making it easier for the student to learn from the teacher's predictions.

Specifically, the softened probabilities are calculated using the softmax function, with the temperature value influencing the sharpness of the output distribution. The loss function incorporates weights for both loss types, allowing control over their relative importance during training. The teacher model is typically pre-trained and remains frozen, while only the student model is updated.

The effectiveness of this approach is evidenced by prior work, such as Hinton et al., who demonstrated that a single distilled model could achieve accuracy comparable to a large ensemble of models in speech recognition. Urban et al. showed that distillation enhances the performance of smaller networks on datasets like CIFAR-10. Moreover, Sanh et al. successfully compressed a BERT model while retaining high performance, achieving significant reductions in model size and computational speed.

The paper also highlights adaptations of distillation techniques, such as transferring intermediate outputs or attention maps between models, which further improves the learning process. MobileBERT exemplifies this by employing a progressive knowledge transfer strategy, resulting in a notably smaller and faster model with minimal quality loss.
