The paper discusses two primary pretext tasks in vision: detecting the relative order of image patches and predicting the degree of rotation of images. These tasks help in training models to learn general representations effectively. The study shows that models pre-trained using these self-supervised methods significantly improve the performance of final object detection tasks compared to those initialized randomly.

Additionally, the paper highlights the approach of Contrastive Learning, exemplified by frameworks like SimCLR, which trains models to differentiate between similar and dissimilar inputs by maximizing or minimizing the cosine similarity of their representations. The results indicate that models trained using only a small fraction of labeled data can achieve competitive accuracy on benchmarks like ImageNet.

Overall, the discussion emphasizes the promise of Self-Supervised Learning as a foundational technique in machine learning, suggesting a shift in focus towards leveraging unlabeled data for representation learning, with supervised learning serving as an enhancement to this process.
