This paper discusses the efficiency of deep learning models, particularly through the process of pre-training on unlabeled data followed by fine-tuning on labeled data. It highlights that fine-tuning pre-trained models, especially those trained with self-supervised learning, is data-efficient, requiring significantly fewer labeled examples compared to training from scratch. The ULMFiT model is cited as a pioneer in this approach, demonstrating that pre-training on a large corpus, such as WikiText-103, allows the model to learn general language properties. This enables it to achieve effective performance on specific tasks, like binary classification on the IMDb dataset, with only 100 labeled examples—about ten times fewer than needed otherwise. 

The paper also mentions that introducing an additional pre-training step using unlabeled data from the target dataset can further reduce the requirement for labeled examples by approximately twenty times. Additionally, the paper references models like BERT, which employ a similar pre-training strategy involving masked language modeling and next sentence prediction, allowing for effective fine-tuning on various downstream tasks. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of leveraging unlabeled data to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of deep learning models.
