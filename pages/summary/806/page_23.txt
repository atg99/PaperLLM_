The paper discusses strategies for enhancing the efficiency of deep learning models by making them smaller, faster, and more effective. It highlights the importance of incorporating latency directly into the model evaluation process, as seen in approaches like the one presented in reference [147]. This method maximizes a compound function that combines accuracy (ACC) and latency (LAT), with a specific target latency (T) and a recommended weight (w). FBNet is another example that utilizes a similar reward function, employing a pre-computed lookup table to approximate latency, resulting in networks that are significantly smaller and faster than MobileNet.

The discussion emphasizes the vital role of automation in improving model efficiency through Hyper-Parameter Optimization (HPO). Techniques such as Bayesian Optimization and early stopping are suggested to manage the costs associated with HPO, which is increasingly accessible via various software packages. Additionally, advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) enable the creation of optimized architectures while adhering to quality and size constraints, making NAS a cost-effective alternative to manual architecture experimentation.

The paper also points out that designing efficient architectures from the ground up can address efficiency challenges. It provides examples of efficient layers, particularly in the vision domain, where convolutional layers have shown improvements over fully connected layers by overcoming inherent limitations. This approach underscores the potential of tailored designs to enhance model performance and efficiency.
