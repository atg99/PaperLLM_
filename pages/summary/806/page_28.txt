The paper discusses advancements in model efficiency and infrastructure for machine learning, particularly focusing on alternative architectures to traditional LSTMs and BERT-like models. Notably, the Proformer architecture is highlighted for its innovative Local Projected Attention (LPA) Layer, which enables it to achieve approximately 97.2% of BERT-base's performance while using only 13% of its memory and featuring significantly fewer parameters (14.4 million compared to BERT-base's 110 million).

In terms of infrastructure, the paper emphasizes the importance of a robust software and hardware foundation for effective training and inference of machine learning models. It provides a survey of critical components that contribute to model efficiency, illustrated through a visualization of the interaction between hardware and software during the model-training and inference phases.

The TensorFlow ecosystem is presented as a key player in this context, particularly TensorFlow Lite (TFLite), which is designed for low-resource environments. TFLite comprises an interpreter for executing specialized models and a collection of operation kernels optimized for ARM-based processors and DSPs. It also includes a converter tool that transforms TensorFlow models into a flatbuffer file suitable for inference, facilitating efficient deployment on devices with limited resources.
