The paper discusses various strategies for optimizing neural network training and inference by leveraging pointwise operations, buffer checkpointing, device-specific optimizations, and hardware-optimized libraries. 

Pointwise operations can be fused by the TorchScript compiler, enhancing computational efficiency. Buffer checkpointing allows certain layer outputs to be retained in memory while the rest are computed during the backward pass, which is beneficial for layers with large outputs, enabling a larger batch size and improved utilization of training resources.

Device-specific optimizations, such as using the cuDNN library and Mixed Precision Training on NVIDIA GPUs, further enhance performance. Distributed Data Parallel Training is recommended for large datasets across multiple GPUs, where each GPU processes its own data subset and averages gradients during training.

The paper also highlights the importance of hardware-optimized libraries like QNNPACK and XNNPACK, which are tailored for ARM and x86 architectures, facilitating efficient operations in mobile and embedded devices. Additionally, libraries such as Accelerate for iOS and NNAPI for Android abstract hardware-level decisions for higher-level machine learning frameworks.

The role of GPUs in deep learning is emphasized, detailing their evolution from graphics processing to general-purpose computing with the advent of libraries like CUDA and cuBLAS. The introduction of Tensor Cores in NVIDIA GPUs, designed for deep learning applications, allows for training and inference across various precision formats, demonstrating that reduced precision often maintains model quality with significant performance benefits.
