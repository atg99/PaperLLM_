The paper "Efficient Deep Learning: A Survey on Making Deep Learning Models Smaller, Faster, and Better" by Gaurav Menghani from Google Research addresses the growing challenges associated with the increasing size and complexity of deep learning models. While deep learning has significantly advanced fields such as computer vision and natural language processing, the models have become larger, requiring more resources and resulting in higher latency, which poses difficulties for real-world deployment. 

The paper emphasizes the importance of model efficiency alongside performance metrics, presenting a comprehensive survey of five core areas related to model efficiency, including modeling techniques, infrastructure, and hardware. It highlights seminal works in these areas and provides an experimental guide with code to help practitioners optimize model training and deployment.

The introduction traces the evolution of deep learning, noting how models like AlexNet, VGGNet, and Transformers have led to increased complexity and resource demands. For instance, GPT-3 features 175 billion parameters and incurs substantial training costs. Despite their effectiveness, many models lack the efficiency needed for practical application.

The author identifies challenges such as sustainable server-side scaling, where the long-term costs of deploying large models can be prohibitive. The hope is that this survey equips readers with a foundational understanding of efficiency techniques, enabling immediate improvements and fostering further research in the field.
