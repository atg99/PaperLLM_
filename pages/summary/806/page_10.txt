The paper discusses advancements in quantization techniques for neural networks, particularly focusing on the benefits of using fixed-point representations during inference. Gaurav Menghani highlights a study by Vanhoucke et al. that achieved a threefold inference speedup on an x86 CPU using fixed-point models compared to floating-point models, without compromising accuracy. This improvement is attributed to fixed-point SIMD instructions in Intel’s SSE4 instruction set, enabling parallelized operations like Multiply-Accumulate.

The paper further examines Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) as a method to mitigate quality loss associated with post-training quantization, especially in complex networks. It identifies issues such as outlier weights skewing quantized values and varying weight distributions within layers as potential causes of this loss. Wang et al. propose heuristics for better precision allocation during post-training quantization, while Jacob et al. introduce a QAT regime where the training simulates quantization effects, allowing weights and activations to adapt to the noise introduced during inference.

Jacob et al. detail a method for fake-quantizing tensors, which collects statistics on weights and activations to optimize their quantization. They employ the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) to make the non-differentiable quantization process manageable during training, allowing the network to learn to tolerate the noise from clamping and rounding. This approach enhances the robustness of the network to quantization effects, ultimately leading to improved performance in deployment scenarios.
