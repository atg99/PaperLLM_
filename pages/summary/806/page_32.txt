The paper discusses the implementation and applications of Systolic Arrays in Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) and other specialized hardware for deep learning. 

Section (a) illustrates a Systolic Array Cell performing a Multiply-Accumulate (MAC) operation, where inputs A and B are received, and output C is stored in memory. The array facilitates matrix multiplication, as shown in section (b) with a 4x4 matrix example, where the i-th row receives the i-th column of matrix A and the i-th column receives the i-th column of matrix B, with delays incorporated for synchronization.

TPUs can be interconnected in a mesh topology to form 'pods', achieving significant computational power, with each TPU chip (v3) delivering 420 teraflops and a TPU pod exceeding 100 petaflops. TPUs are utilized within Google for various applications, including training models for Google Search and DeepMind's AlphaGo, and they have set benchmarks in MLPerf.

Additionally, the paper introduces the EdgeTPU, a custom ASIC designed for low-power inference tasks on edge devices, capable of 4 TOPS using only 2 watts. It is optimized for linear algebra operations and operates with int8 quantized Tensorflow Lite models. The EdgeTPU is available in various form factors, including the Coral platform and the Pixel Neural Core in smartphones.

Lastly, the Jetson family by Nvidia is presented as accelerators for deep learning in embedded and IoT devices. This family includes the low-powered Nano module and more powerful variants like Xavier and TX, which are based on Nvidia's GPU architectures. The differences among Jetson models primarily relate to the types and numbers of GPU cores.

Overall, the paper highlights the advancements in specialized hardware for deep learning, emphasizing their efficiency and applications across various platforms.
