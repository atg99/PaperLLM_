The paper presents a detailed comparison of hyperparameter optimization methods for deep neural networks, specifically focusing on three datasets and two architectures: RNN and CNN. It evaluates the performance of the proposed method against state-of-the-art techniques, including grid search, random search, and Bayesian optimization (BO), across metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. 

Key findings indicate that the proposed method consistently achieves competitive results, with notable performance in terms of accuracy and F1 scores while also demonstrating efficiency in running time compared to other methods. For instance, in the EEG dataset using RNN, the proposed method achieved an accuracy of 0.925, outperforming random search and BO.

The methodology for hyperparameter optimization involves several systematic steps: 

1. **Building the F-L table**: This step defines four hyperparameters (learning rate, l2 norm coefficient, number of hidden layers, and number of nodes) with three levels based on previous research and empirical evidence.
2. **Choosing the OATM table**: An Orthogonal Array table is selected to ensure balanced and diverse combinations of the hyperparameters during experimentation.
3. **Running experiments**: Each of the nine combinations from the OATM table is tested, with five repetitions per experiment to ensure accuracy and reliability.
4. **Conducting range analysis**: The results from the experiments are analyzed to determine the impact of each hyperparameter on the overall classification accuracy.

The study emphasizes the importance of systematic hyperparameter tuning in enhancing the performance of deep learning models, providing a structured approach that can be applied in real-world scenarios.
