This paper discusses two types of activity recognition systems: IMU-based and RFID-based. The IMU-based dataset consists of 1,200,000 samples collected from 9 participants, focusing on 8 activities of daily living (ADLs). Each activity is monitored using three inertial measurement units (IMUs) that gather data across 14 dimensions, including accelerometers, gyroscopes, magnetometers, and a thermometer. The RFID-based dataset contains 3,100 samples from 6 subjects, covering 21 activities, including 18 ADLs and 3 abnormal falls, with each sample characterized by 12 dimensions linked to RFID tags, using Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) for environmental measurement.

The paper also outlines the deep learning structures employed for activity recognition, specifically Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). RNNs are highlighted for their ability to manage temporal dependencies through internal memory, making them suitable for time-series tasks. The specific RNN architecture includes two Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) layers, which are fundamental for recurrent networks. Key hyper-parameters affecting performance include learning rate, regularization coefficient, number of hidden layers, and number of nodes in each hidden layer, with loss calculated using a cross-entropy function and optimized via the AdamOptimizer algorithm.

CNNs are mentioned as another effective deep learning approach, known for their capability to capture spatial relationships in input data. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of tuning hyper-parameters to enhance the performance of the proposed deep learning structures in activity recognition tasks.
