In this paper, the authors, Xiang Zhang et al., present a comparison of their proposed method, the Orthogonal Array Tuning Method (OATM), with existing hyper-parameter tuning techniques including grid search, random search, and Bayesian optimization. The study highlights the efficiency of OATM, which requires only 9 runs to find optimal hyper-parameters, compared to grid search's exhaustive 81 runs. Random search and Bayesian optimization are also evaluated under the same condition of 9 runs for a fair comparison.

The authors detail how grid search systematically explores all combinations, while random search selects combinations randomly and is favored for its efficiency. Bayesian optimization employs a Gaussian process to enhance performance based on loss function minimization. The hyper-parameters for all methods are derived from empirical values, with specific ranges given for Bayesian optimization.

Results indicate that OATM surpasses random search and Bayesian optimization across all datasets and architectures, showing competitive performance against grid search. Notably, in the EEG dataset example using RNN, OATM achieves an 88% reduction in tuning time, requiring only 821.9 seconds compared to grid search's 6853.6 seconds, while still closely approaching the global optimal factors identified by grid search.

The paper proceeds to outline a case study focusing on EEG classification using RNN and CNN architectures, presenting a clear five-step procedure for implementing OATM.
