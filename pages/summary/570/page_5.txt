The paper discusses the Orthogonal Array Tuning Method (OATM) for optimizing hyperparameters in deep neural networks. The process involves several steps: 

1. **Setting Up the Orthogonal Array Tuning Table (LM(hk))**: This table is prepared with k factors, h levels, and M total rows.
2. **Running Experiments**: Experiments are conducted based on the hyperparameters defined in the tuning table.
3. **Range Analysis**: This critical step analyzes the results to identify optimal levels and the significance of each factor influencing the outcomes. The method allows for the combination of optimized hyperparameters beyond the existing table.
4. **Final Experimentation**: An experiment is conducted using the identified optimal hyperparameters. The OATM demonstrates efficiency by requiring only 9 combinations for tuning, compared to 27 combinations needed for traditional grid search methods, leading to a 67% reduction in the tuning workload.

The evaluation of OATM is conducted on two popular deep learning architectures—Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)—across three real-world applications: human intention recognition from EEG signals, activity recognition using wearable sensors, and activity recognition through pervasive sensors.

For the data setting, the EEG-based intention recognition task uses a subset of the eegmmidb dataset from PhysioNet, containing 28,000 samples with 64 channels each. The datasets are split into training (80%) and testing (20%) sets to assess the performance of the proposed method.
