The paper discusses the process of conducting a systematic literature review using various databases, including Scopus, IEEE Xplore, PubMed, and Web of Science. Each database search utilized specific advanced queries to gather relevant documents. 

In the Scopus search, 99 documents were initially returned, which were then filtered for duplicates and screened for relevance to the study's focus on GPT applications in research, data augmentation, data generation, and solving research problems. An example of an irrelevant document is provided, highlighting the need for careful screening.

The IEEE Xplore search yielded 119 documents, with a similar process of removing duplicates and screening for relevance. The first record was included, while a second was excluded due to its lack of focus on GPT in research.

PubMed returned 47 documents, and the Web of Science search resulted in 306 documents. In both cases, duplicates were removed, and records were screened for relevance, with specific examples of irrelevant studies noted, such as those focused on nanotechnology.

The systematic approach ensures that the literature review is thorough and relevant to the study's objectives.
