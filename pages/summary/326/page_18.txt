The paper presents a systematic literature review focused on the application of Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT) in research, particularly in data generation and augmentation. It identifies a gap in existing reviews which did not adequately explore how GPT can assist researchers in generating and analyzing research-related data. The review meticulously examines 412 scholarly works, ultimately distilling 77 significant contributions and developing a classification framework for the use of GPT in research data.

The findings emphasize the practical implications of integrating GPT technologies into research practices, guiding researchers in generating and processing data, analyzing complex datasets, and improving research design and problem-solving. Additionally, the paper includes a systematic comparison of 54 existing literary works, assessing various research domains, methodologies, and their respective advantages and disadvantages, thereby providing a comprehensive roadmap for researchers. 

The PRISMA flow diagram illustrates the identification process of studies, highlighting that after deduplication, 275 studies were filtered down to 106 for full-text analysis, with 29 ultimately excluded due to irrelevance to the theme of using GPT for research. This thorough approach aims to enhance the understanding of GPT's multifaceted applications in academic research.
