The paper discusses the PRISMA method, a structured framework for conducting and reporting systematic literature reviews and meta-analyses in academic research. PRISMA emphasizes reducing bias and enhancing reproducibility through a systematic approach that includes identifying, screening, assessing eligibility, and including relevant studies, as well as extracting and synthesizing data.

The authors developed a comprehensive set of search keywords, which were applied across various databases such as IEEE Xplore, Scopus, ACM Library, Web of Science, and PubMed, employing advanced queries tailored to each platform. They also utilized Litmaps, a visualization tool that identified related research, leading to the discovery of nine additional resources. 

The screening process involved two stages: an initial assessment of titles and abstracts followed by a detailed review of full-text articles. Records that did not align with the research focus were excluded, resulting in the inclusion of 77 articles relevant to their research questions. The paper highlights the importance of structured criteria and provides a schematic representation of the systematic review process, underlining the rigor involved in the literature review methodology.
