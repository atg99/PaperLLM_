The paper discusses the challenges and ethical considerations surrounding the use of Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT) and Large Language Models (LLMs) in research writing. Key concerns include accuracy, potential biases, and the need for ethical guidelines to ensure responsible use of these technologies. The academic discourse emphasizes maintaining scientific rigor and transparency, especially regarding biases in LLM outputs. Various studies indicate that while LLMs present innovative tools for research writing, their application must be carefully managed to uphold ethical standards and mitigate biases.

The review shifts its focus from authoring challenges to the positive applications of GPT and LLMs in data augmentation. It highlights how these technologies can enhance research data, generate features, and synthesize information. With advanced language understanding, GPT simplifies feature extraction from plain text, which traditionally required complex natural language processing techniques. Additionally, GPT can contribute semantically similar content to improve data diversity and robustness, and it can generate synthetic data to support machine learning processes during times of data scarcity or confidentiality.

Despite existing reviews on GPT's adoption in research, a significant gap remains, as none thoroughly explore its comprehensive capabilities in enhancing research methodologies.
