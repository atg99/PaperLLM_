This literature review addresses a gap in existing research on the utility of Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT) for researchers, particularly in generating and augmenting research data. Through a thorough analysis of 412 scholarly works, the study highlights how GPT can enhance data generation, analysis, and research design. It distills insights from 77 selected contributions and proposes a classification framework for GPT's application in research data.

The findings serve as a guide for researchers to effectively integrate GPT technologies into their work, thereby improving data handling and problem-solving capabilities. A systematic comparison of 54 existing studies across various research domains provides a practical roadmap for scientists aiming to adopt GPT in their academic efforts, promoting innovation and efficiency.

However, the deployment of GPT is accompanied by limitations such as ethical concerns, biases, hallucinations, and sycophantic behavior, which can compromise the reliability of its outputs. The review stresses the need for careful evaluation of these issues when utilizing GPT in research contexts.

Future research directions include refining GPT through advanced training to reduce biases and hallucinations and developing algorithms to counteract sycophantic tendencies, thereby enhancing the objectivity of outputs for academic use.
