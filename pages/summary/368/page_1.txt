The paper introduces RobBERT, a Dutch language model based on the RoBERTa architecture, which is an optimized version of BERT. The authors, Pieter Delobelle, Thomas Winters, and Bettina Berendt, highlight the significant performance gains that pre-trained language models have achieved in natural language processing (NLP). They argue that while multilingual models like Multilingual BERT perform well, models trained on a single language, such as Dutch, can yield better results.

The study emphasizes the advantages of using RoBERTa for training a Dutch language model, as it allows for unsupervised pre-training on large corpora followed by fine-tuning with smaller annotated datasets. The authors evaluated RobBERT's performance on various NLP tasks, focusing on the size of the fine-tuning dataset, the impact of language-specific tokenizers, and the model's fairness. Results indicate that RobBERT outperforms existing models, particularly with smaller datasets, making it a robust tool for a wide range of Dutch NLP applications.

The authors conclude by making the pre-trained and fine-tuned models publicly available to facilitate further research and application in Dutch NLP tasks.
