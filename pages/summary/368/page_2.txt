The study introduces new tasks to evaluate the model’s zero-shot capabilities and examines its performance on smaller datasets. Additionally, it assesses the significance of a language-specific tokenizer and conducts a comprehensive fairness evaluation using advanced techniques and a newly translated dataset.

The paper discusses the evolution of transformer models, highlighting their initial success in machine translation and subsequent application in various natural language processing tasks. BERT, which employs masked language modeling and next sentence prediction, is identified as a pivotal model that set new benchmarks. It is noted for its ability to generate contextualized word embeddings, addressing limitations of traditional word embeddings by incorporating context into word representations.

The authors critique BERT’s training methodology, referencing RoBERTa's findings that question the efficacy of the next sentence prediction task. They also mention advancements in multilingual models, such as mBERT, while noting their inferior performance compared to monolingual models like CamemBERT and BERT-NL, particularly in Dutch language tasks.

To improve upon existing models, RobBERT was pre-trained with a focus on Dutch language data, with two versions developed: one altering only the corpus and another adjusting both the corpus and tokenizer. This approach aims to provide a more robust language model tailored for the Dutch language, enabling better performance across a variety of tasks.
