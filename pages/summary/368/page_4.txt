The paper presents the results of RobBERT, a model fine-tuned on various classification tasks, comparing its performance to state-of-the-art models. In the Sentiment Analysis task using the DBRD dataset, RobBERT v2 achieved an accuracy of 94.379% and an F1 score of 94.378%, outperforming previous models. For the Die/Dat disambiguation task on the Europarl corpus, RobBERT v2 also excelled, achieving 97.816% accuracy and 97.514% F1 score, surpassing other models including mBERT and BERTje.

The authors note that while ULMFiT utilized unlabeled data in its training process, the other BERT models' approaches regarding unlabeled reviews remain unclear. They acknowledge that further improvements could be possible by pre-training on unlabeled in-domain data. The paper highlights the challenge of handling long reviews, as many exceed the model's maximum token capacity. The training regimen involved 2000 iterations with a batch size of 128, leading to a validation accuracy of 0.994 for the best-performing RobBERT v2 model.

Additionally, the authors explore the zero-shot capabilities of RobBERT on the die/dat disambiguation task, where the usage of Dutch pronouns "die" and "dat" depends on contextual gender references. They employed a masked language model approach for this task, allowing for a comparison of zero-shot performance without prior fine-tuning. Overall, RobBERT demonstrates superior performance on both tasks, indicating its effectiveness in classification and disambiguation challenges.
