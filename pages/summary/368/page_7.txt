The paper investigates gender stereotyping in language models, specifically focusing on how these models, like RobBERT, associate professions with gendered pronouns. Through a template-based association test, the authors reveal a consistent bias where male pronouns are ranked more likely than female pronouns across various professions. This bias persists even in control templates where no direct correlation exists between the gender of the pronoun and the profession, indicating a systemic issue in the training data that favors male representations.

The authors utilized templates to evaluate the language model's behavior and found that when professions and pronouns referred to the same entity, the model still favored male pronouns, except for the term "actress." The study highlights a concerning trend where the training corpus's imbalance, favoring male representations, affects the model's predictions.

Additionally, the paper addresses the implications of this bias on predictive performance in downstream tasks, such as sentiment analysis of book reviews. By augmenting a dataset with gender as a sensitive attribute, the authors explore the model's performance discrepancies based on gender. Overall, the findings emphasize the need for fairness in language models to prevent the reinforcement of harmful stereotypes and ensure equitable outcomes in applications that rely on these models.
