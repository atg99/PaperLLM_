The paper discusses the application and specific characteristics of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the context of video generation, highlighting the distinct challenges posed by the temporal dimension of video data compared to image GANs. It identifies four main strategies for video GAN generators: combining Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) with 2D convolutional networks, using 3D convolutional networks, employing a coarse-to-fine strategy through progressive growing GANs, and utilizing a two-stream architecture that specializes each stream for different video aspects. 

The paper emphasizes the complexity of unconditional video generation, where models must capture data distribution without input signals, presenting a significant challenge. It notes that despite these challenges, some unconditional models serve as foundational frameworks for conditional GANs, which utilize additional signals such as audio, text, and images. The first video GAN, referred to as Video-GAN (VGAN), is described as employing a two-stream generator consisting of both 3D and 2D convolutional networks to effectively generate video frames. 

The review aims to address the gap in systematic analysis of video GANs, providing insights into their architectures, methodologies, and the evolution from unconditional to conditional models. Tables summarizing various models, their goals, datasets, and evaluation metrics are included to facilitate understanding of the advancements in this domain.
