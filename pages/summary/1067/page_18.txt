The paper discusses the datasets used in video generation applications powered by deep learning models, emphasizing the necessity of diverse data samples for high-quality outputs. It highlights that many datasets, such as UCF-101 and Kinetics, were initially developed for different purposes like action recognition. UCF-101, a widely cited dataset, includes 101 action categories sourced from YouTube, serving as a benchmark for various applications due to its balanced complexity. Kinetics, another significant dataset, also comprises human actions from YouTube but is more complex, featuring 600 categories. The paper notes that some studies utilize subsets of Kinetics for benchmarking.

In speech synchronization, GRID and Lip Reading in the Wild (LRW) are prominent datasets, with LRW being more complex due to its diverse grammar and real-world context. Other datasets for image-to-video mapping mentioned include Human3.6M, Moving MNIST, and Weizmann human action, each with unique characteristics and applications.

The evaluation metrics for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are categorized into qualitative and quantitative measures. Human evaluation is a common qualitative approach, where observers assess generated videos based on criteria such as naturalness, realism, diversity, and synchronization. However, this method has limitations since visual assessments may not accurately reflect the model's capabilities. Quantitative metrics like Inception Score (IS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and Mean Squared Error (MSE) are also prevalent. IS focuses on the quality and diversity of generated images without referencing original frames, while FID improves upon this by comparing generated videos to original ones after extracting spatio-temporal feature maps.
