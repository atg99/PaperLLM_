The paper provides a comprehensive review of Video Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), focusing on architectures such as vid2vid and few-shot-vid2vid. Both architectures utilize a generator network conditioned on previous frames and semantic videos, comprising three modules: W for optical flow extraction, M for occlusion map prediction, and H for generating intermediate frames. The key distinction lies in the dynamic weights of module H in few-shot-vid2vid, allowing it to generate videos of previously unseen objects by using multiple images at test time.

The review also contrasts the use of a single semantic label map in Pan et al.'s approach, which simplifies the synthesizing process by generating an initial frame in an image-to-image phase followed by video generation using conditional Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). Additionally, the paper addresses video inpainting, where missing regions are filled with coherent content, highlighting a framework using a 3D gated convolution network and a Temporal PatchGAN discriminator to balance local and global features. However, the limitation of this model is its inability to capture content from distant frames, which is addressed by the Spatial-Temporal Transformer Network (STTN) that incorporates an attention mechanism.

Furthermore, the review discusses image-to-video synthesis frameworks aimed at predicting future frames based on given frames. Early architectures struggled with blurriness due to not disentangling training video representations. Notable advancements include the use of adversarial training by Mathieu et al. and the combination of VAEs with GANs by Lee et al. to improve realism and diversity in generated images. Multi-Discriminator GANs (MD-GAN) and two-stage frameworks based on pose estimation further enhance frame generation quality, as shown in various studies that emphasize the benefits of information decomposition in video synthesis.
