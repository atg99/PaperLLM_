The paper provides a comprehensive review of Video Generative Adversarial Networks (VideoGANs), focusing on frameworks for text-to-video synthesis. It discusses various models that utilize conditional information to generate videos based on textual input, highlighting their primary tasks and methodologies.

In Table 3, the reviewed frameworks are summarized, detailing the type of conditional information used and the main tasks they perform. For instance, TGANs-C generates videos using three discriminators for image, video, and frame-caption matching, while the model by Li et al. combines an initial frame with a text description to create videos. TFGAN employs a multi-scale text-conditioning scheme, and StoryGAN focuses on generating animated stories from paragraphs.

The paper also explores approaches that synchronize audio with video, noting that some models focus solely on lip movements while others synchronize audio with the entire face. Notable contributions include a progressive framework by Jalalifar et al. that uses LSTM to extract mouth region landmarks for generating synchronized talking faces. Vougioukas et al. present a model that directly converts audio waveforms to synchronized videos without intermediate landmark extraction.

The concept of disentangled representations is addressed, particularly through the Disentangled Audio-Visual System (DAVS), which separates person-related and speech-related information to improve video coherence. Mittal et al. further enhance this by decoupling audio into content, emotion, and noise, employing a Variational AutoEncoder to preprocess audio before generating video frames.

The main objectives of the reviewed text-to-video GANs are to ensure semantic consistency between the text condition and generated video while producing realistic and coherent videos.
