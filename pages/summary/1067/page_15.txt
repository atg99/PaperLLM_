The paper provides a comprehensive review of Video Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) with a specific focus on video-to-video synthesis frameworks. It categorizes various frameworks based on their conditional inputs and primary tasks, as summarized in Table 6. 

Key frameworks discussed include:

- Zhou et al. utilizes body parts heat maps to generate videos of a dancing person different from the input while maintaining motion.
- Chan et al. employs a joint map for similar purposes.
- Vid2Game introduces control signals to manipulate the pose of a person in generated videos.
- Monkey-net uses keypoints for generating videos of different individuals maintaining the motion of the input video.
- Recycle-GAN focuses on generating videos with the style of the input while altering the content.
- Liu et al. implements a 3D character model for similar tasks.
- Deep Video Portraits and ReenactGAN both aim to create videos of talking individuals with different faces while preserving motion, using monocular face and face boundary techniques respectively.
- TransMoMo generates videos based on skeleton data.

The review highlights the dominant use of image-to-video GAN frameworks for video prediction but also notes other applications such as video colorization and virtual fitting. For instance, Zhang et al. address exemplar-based colorization with a focus on maintaining temporal consistency, while Dong et al. propose an architecture for virtual clothing fitting using multiple encoders and decoders.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the versatility of GANs in video synthesis, particularly in retargeting motion and generating realistic animations across various applications, as illustrated in the provided tables.
