The paper discusses various frameworks for speech-to-video generation using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). It highlights several key models, including Vougioukas et al., DAVS, Mittal et al., Chen et al., and Jalalifari et al., each focusing on generating talking person videos from audio inputs, initial images, or lip landmarks.

The paper emphasizes the computational challenges faced by these models, particularly in the generator's latter stages where spatial resolution increases. TGANv2, an unsupervised model, is noted for requiring larger batch sizes for better generalization and employs a subsampling technique to manage computational load.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts MoCoGAN and TGAN, which synthesize frames based on motion vectors, with G3GAN, which separates motion and content vectors to enhance generation quality through self-attention mechanisms. DVD-GAN is also introduced as an advanced model capable of producing high-quality images while being trained on comprehensive datasets like Kinetics, unlike previous models that used limited subsets.

The section on conditional video generation outlines various GANs that utilize different types of conditional signals such as audio, text, and images to control the output. Specifically, in speech-to-video synthesis, initial frameworks focused on synchronizing lip movements with audio, with Chen et al.'s model serving as a notable example that combines encoded audio features with initial images to generate coherent video outputs assessed by a three-stream discriminator.
