The paper reviews various frameworks for Image-to-Video Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) focusing on their conditional inputs and main tasks. Each framework utilizes different types of input information to predict future frames. For instance, Mathieu et al. utilize input frames to predict future frames through a multiscale network, while Lee et al. combine GANs and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) for frame prediction. MCnet and TwoStreamVAN also address motion and content separately to enhance prediction accuracy.

Walker et al. extract human skeletons from input frames for future frame prediction, paralleling the techniques employed by Liang et al. and DRNET, which also separate dynamics from content. Hu et al. introduce a unique approach by using motion strokes to represent object movement instead of extracting motion from the input frames. This model encodes both the initial image and motion strokes, followed by generating sequential frames with two discriminators assessing image and sequence levels.

Overall, the frameworks exhibit a trend of decomposing motion from content to enhance the predictive capabilities of future frames, leveraging various architectures and techniques, including dual GANs and specific encoding strategies.
