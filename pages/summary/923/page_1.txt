The paper presents a novel supervised learning framework for oversegmenting 3D point clouds into superpoints, aiming to improve the quality of semantic segmentation. The authors propose a method that learns deep embeddings of local geometry and radiometry of 3D points, enhancing the distinction at object borders. This approach utilizes a lightweight neural network to compute embeddings based on the local neighborhoods of points.

Oversegmentation is reformulated as a graph partition problem using the learned embeddings, allowing the method to set new benchmarks in point cloud oversegmentation on both dense indoor (S3DIS) and sparse outdoor (vKITTI) datasets. Notably, the proposed solution achieves comparable performance with over five times fewer superpoints than existing methods on S3DIS.

The framework builds on previous work in superpixel-based methods and deep learning, particularly in the context of 3D point clouds. The authors introduce a graph-structured contrastive loss that encourages homogeneous embeddings within objects while maximizing contrast at their borders. The Local Point Embedder (LPE), inspired by PointNet, is employed to generate these embeddings efficiently.

Key contributions of the paper include the establishment of the first supervised framework for 3D point cloud oversegmentation, the introduction of the graph-structured contrastive loss combined with a cross-partition weighting strategy, and the development of the LPE architecture. The proposed method also enhances superpoint-based semantic segmentation, yielding new state-of-the-art results in this area.
