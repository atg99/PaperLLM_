The paper presents a comparative analysis of various algorithms for semantic segmentation in point clouds using datasets S3DIS and vKITTI. It focuses on the performance of the proposed Supervised SuperPoint (SSP) method, which utilizes learned point embeddings and graph-based techniques to generate superpoints, compared to other state-of-the-art methods like Geom-Graph, VCCS, and Lin's approach.

The results, illustrated in Figure 7, reveal that SSP significantly outperforms competing algorithms across all metrics on the S3DIS dataset, achieving comparable performance with fewer superpoints—under 350 compared to over 1,800 for VCCS. The paper highlights that SSP excels in boundary quality, although performance on vKITTI is less impressive, possibly due to the challenges of sparse data and the absence of color information.

The authors also note that while Geom-Graph achieves good accuracy, its reliance on handcrafted features limits its boundary detection capabilities. SSP-Cluster, another variant of the proposed method, shows better performance than traditional clustering approaches but still faces challenges related to initialization sensitivity.

Computational efficiency is emphasized, with the learned embeddings being processed rapidly on a GPU, though the graph partitioning remains the main bottleneck, processing around 100,000 points per second. The paper concludes by demonstrating the effective application of the point cloud oversegmentation framework in semantic segmentation tasks, as detailed in the subsequent tables.
