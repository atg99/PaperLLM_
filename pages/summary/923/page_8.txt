This paper introduces a supervised framework for 3D point cloud oversegmentation, enhancing the superpoint-based semantic segmentation technique known as SPG. The authors replace the unsupervised superpoint computation with their proposed method, SSP, leading to improved performance across standard classification metrics including overall accuracy (OA), mean per-class accuracy (mAcc), and mean per-class intersection-over-union (mIoU). The results indicate a notable performance increase, particularly in the retrieval of small objects, although the overall accuracy may not surpass the latest state-of-the-art algorithms.

An ablation study is conducted to evaluate various design choices and their impacts on performance metrics. The study compares the proposed method with alternative weighting strategies, revealing that the chosen cross-partition weighting significantly enhances results, while simpler weighting methods yield inferior outcomes. Additional experiments with different configurations, such as stacked layers and residual structures, demonstrate that a shallow architecture with a small receptive field is sufficient for effective border detection.

The paper concludes by emphasizing the significant improvements achieved through their embedding network and graph-structured loss function compared to existing methods. The authors claim their approach sets a new state-of-the-art in semantic segmentation for point clouds. Future work will aim to enhance the computational efficiency of their method and address challenges associated with the generalized minimum minimal partition problem. The source code and trained networks will be made publicly available.
