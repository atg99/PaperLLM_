The section discusses graph-based point cloud oversegmentation, focusing on the Generalized Minimal Partition (GMP) problem and the graph-structured contrastive loss. 

3.2.1 Generalized Minimal Partition Problem:
After computing embeddings, superpoints are defined using an adjacency graph G derived from the point cloud. A smaller neighborhood captures the adjacency structure. Superpoints are determined as the connected components in G, based on a piecewise-constant approximation of embeddings. The optimization problem aims to minimize the difference between embeddings and their approximations while encouraging separations along high contrast areas. The edge weights are defined to enhance this separation. The GMP is a non-continuous, non-convex problem, making global optimization challenging. However, the ℓ0-cut pursuit algorithm provides fast approximate solutions. Although the approach may yield suboptimal solutions due to constraints on the optimization variable, numerical experiments indicate satisfactory partition results.

3.2.2 Graph-Structured Contrastive Loss:
The semantic purity of superpoints is crucial, but directly optimizing this due to the non-convex nature of GMP is difficult. Instead, a graph-structured contrastive loss is proposed, focusing on accurately detecting object boundaries. Intra-edges connect points within the same object, while inter-edges connect points from different objects. The loss encourages similarity among embeddings linked by intra-edges and dissimilarity among those linked by inter-edges. This method contrasts with traditional triplet loss by involving all vertices in a graph, thus eliminating the need for example selection. The loss functions, φ and ψ, are designed to promote homogeneity within objects and penalize similarity at object boundaries, respectively. The functions are tailored to respect the constraints of embeddings on the unit sphere, ensuring effective segmentation without emphasizing semantic information.
