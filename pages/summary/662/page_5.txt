The paper presents I-GCN, an accelerator for Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) that enhances runtime locality through a method called islandization. This approach focuses on processing real-world graphs characterized by component structures, where the majority of data is fetched just once. Even when certain data cannot fit into on-chip memory, I-GCN effectively minimizes off-chip data movement.

Islandization offers significant advantages by consolidating redundant computations among shared neighbors within each island. The internal structure of these islands, formed by nodes with high internal connectivity, allows for the identification and elimination of unnecessary repeated operations. This optimization is illustrated through the processing of dense sub-graphs, where redundant aggregations can be skipped.

The architecture of I-GCN comprises two main components: the Island Locator and the Island Consumer. The Island Locator identifies hubs in the graph and utilizes the TP-BFS (Threshold-based and Parallel Breadth-First Search) algorithm to locate islands. It operates through three concurrent asynchronous tasks focused on hub detection, task generation, and island exploration.

The workflow of the Island Locator is outlined in a simplified algorithm, which includes mechanisms for adjusting hub detection thresholds dynamically. The algorithm ensures parallel execution of tasks while maintaining synchronization, ultimately outputting a list of islands along with their associated hub information.

In conclusion, I-GCN leverages the properties of real-world graph structures to enhance GCN processing efficiency by reducing data movement and eliminating redundant computations through innovative architectural design and algorithmic strategies.
