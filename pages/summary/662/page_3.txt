In this paper, the authors introduce I-GCN, an accelerator designed to enhance the performance of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) by improving runtime locality through a technique called "Islandization." GCNs consist of multiple GraphCONV layers, where each layer performs an aggregation and combination of node features. The authors highlight that the execution order of these phases can significantly impact performance, advocating for a combination-first approach that minimizes arithmetic computation.

The paper further explores the aggregation phase, the performance bottleneck of GCNs, and compares two main aggregation strategies: PULL and PUSH. PULL-based methods aggregate node features sequentially, with variations such as PULL-Inner-Product and PULL-Row-Wise, each having specific strengths and weaknesses concerning data reuse and memory access patterns. In contrast, PUSH-based methods utilize a broadcasting mechanism to simultaneously update node features, offering an alternative approach to feature aggregation.

The authors argue that while PULL-Row-Wise methods have been favored in prior research due to efficiency in accessing entire rows, they still suffer from poor data locality due to the scattered nature of non-zero entries in the adjacency matrix. By clustering non-zeros effectively, I-GCN aims to improve data locality and overall performance for GCNs, providing a novel solution to the challenges faced in processing graph-based data structures.
