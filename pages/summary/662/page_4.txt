The paper discusses various methods for optimizing data locality in Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), particularly focusing on the differences between PULL and PUSH strategies. The PULL method has lower on-chip storage requirements but can lead to inefficient off-chip access patterns, while the PUSH method allows for better data reuse at the expense of random access to on-chip data. The authors introduce the islandization technique, which enhances data locality by clustering graph nodes into islands based on their connectivity, allowing for more efficient processing.

The I-GCN architecture is designed to leverage islandization to improve computation efficiency. The workflow consists of two main modules: the Island Locator, which identifies and clusters nodes into islands, and the Island Consumer, which processes these islands as dense sub-graphs. This architecture enables parallel processing of islands as they are formed, minimizing the need for synchronization and maximizing resource utilization.

The proposed approach effectively addresses the challenges of data locality and redundancy in GCN inference, resulting in improved performance and reduced memory overhead. The paper concludes that the islandization method significantly enhances the processing of large graphs by optimizing data reuse and locality.
