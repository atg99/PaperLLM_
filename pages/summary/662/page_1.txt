The paper presents I-GCN, a hardware accelerator designed to enhance the performance of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) during inference. GCNs have gained significant traction for their ability to extract meaningful insights from graph data, yet their implementation faces challenges due to poor data locality and redundant computations stemming from the sparse and irregular nature of real-world graphs.

I-GCN addresses these challenges through a novel online graph restructuring algorithm called islandization, which identifies clusters of nodes with strong internal connections and weak external connections. This approach allows for two main advantages: improved on-chip data reuse and reduced off-chip memory accesses by processing these clusters (or islands) instead of individual nodes, and minimized redundant computations by reusing aggregated results for shared neighbors within an island.

Importantly, the islandization process operates entirely in hardware at runtime, eliminating the need for any preprocessing of graph data or alterations to the GCN model itself. Experimental results demonstrate that I-GCN significantly reduces off-chip memory accesses and prunes 38% of aggregation operations, achieving performance speedups of 5549 times over CPUs, 403 times over GPUs, and 5.7 times over previous GCN accelerators on average.

The work emphasizes the growing need for efficient hardware solutions in the realm of GCNs, particularly as their applications in various fields continue to expand, necessitating adherence to strict performance constraints.
