The paper presents I-GCN, a hardware accelerator designed to address the inefficiencies in Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) caused by irregular data access patterns in adjacency matrices. Existing methods, such as HyGCN and AWB-GCN, use either PULL or PUSH approaches to aggregate node features, but both face challenges related to off-chip bandwidth limitations, data locality issues, and workload imbalances. Given the irregular distribution of non-zero elements in adjacency matrices, these methods struggle with frequent off-chip accesses, leading to performance bottlenecks.

To mitigate these issues, the authors introduce an innovative online graph restructuring algorithm called islandization, implemented in the I-GCN architecture. The Island Locator module identifies hub nodes and their neighbors, forming clusters termed "islands" that exhibit strong internal connections but limited external links. This clustering improves data locality by consolidating accesses to feature and result matrices and reduces redundant computations during aggregation by allowing shared neighbors to be processed collectively.

The advantages of islandization are twofold: it enhances on-chip data reuse by minimizing off-chip accesses and it decreases redundant calculations by leveraging the shared neighbor information within clusters. The paper claims that this approach addresses the fundamental data locality problem in GCN acceleration, providing a substantial performance boost.

Key contributions of the paper include the development of the islandization algorithm, the design of the I-GCN hardware architecture that implements this algorithm, and experimental results demonstrating significant reductions in off-chip accesses and aggregation operations, achieving speedups compared to various CPU and GPU implementations of GCNs as well as previous accelerators.
