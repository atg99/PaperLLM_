The paper presents I-GCN, a Graph Convolutional Network accelerator that enhances runtime locality through a method called islandization. This approach aims to improve efficiency in processing graph data by clustering non-zero elements of adjacency matrices into L-shaped patterns, thereby optimizing data access and computation.

The architecture of I-GCN includes processing elements (PEs) designed for PULL-based combinations, which access feature data based on island node IDs and utilize on-chip weight matrices for efficient computation. The architecture minimizes data movement and maximizes resource utilization by processing nodes sequentially and storing results for future reuse.

The evaluation of I-GCN is conducted using a Stratix 10 SX FPGA across various models, including GCN, GraphSage, and GIN, with datasets such as Cora, Citeseer, PubMed, Nell, and Reddit. The results demonstrate significant improvements in latency, energy efficiency, and off-chip data movement compared to existing graph reordering algorithms and prior GCN accelerators.

The paper also illustrates the effectiveness of the islandization algorithm in clustering non-zero entries in adjacency matrices, particularly highlighting the challenges posed by sparse datasets like NELL. Additionally, the island-based redundancy removal technique shows a substantial reduction in unnecessary aggregation operations, achieving an average pruning rate of 38%.

Overall, I-GCN showcases a promising advancement in GCN acceleration through its innovative architectural design and efficient processing techniques.
