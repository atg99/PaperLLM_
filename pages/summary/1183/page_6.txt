This paper discusses advancements in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) tailored for semi-supervised learning (SSL). It highlights various architectures and methodologies that integrate GANs with classification tasks. Key components of GANs, namely the Discriminator (D), Generator (G), and Encoder (E), are illustrated, showcasing how they are utilized in different models.

One notable approach is CatGAN, which enhances the discriminator's role by maximizing mutual information between input examples and their predicted class distributions. To address the limitations of CatGAN, Semi-supervised GAN (SGAN) is introduced, allowing simultaneous learning of a generator and a classifier. SGAN's classifier outputs are designed to accommodate both real and generated examples.

The Improved GAN variant proposes several training techniques including feature matching and mini-batch discrimination. The generator is trained to minimize the discrepancy between real and generated features, rather than solely maximizing the classification likelihood.

GoodBadGAN further refines this concept by suggesting that a suboptimal generator can still enhance SSL performance. By incorporating a penalty term for generating "bad" samples, GoodBadGAN improves the discriminator's generalization capabilities. Overall, these approaches illustrate the evolving landscape of GANs in semi-supervised contexts, emphasizing the balance between generator and discriminator performance to optimize learning outcomes.
