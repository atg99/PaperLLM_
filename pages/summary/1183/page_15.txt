6.2 Self-training methods
Self-training methods utilize high-confidence predictions made by a model to generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled data. The model is first trained on the labeled dataset, and then it predicts labels for the unlabeled data. These predictions are added to the training set, allowing the model to iteratively refine its learning. This approach has gained traction due to its effectiveness in leveraging the large amounts of unlabeled data often available in real-world scenarios.

It emphasizes the importance of graph-based models in deep semi-supervised learning (DSSL) for label inference through similarity graphs, integrating topological and feature knowledge. Additionally, it contrasts pseudo-labeling methods with consistency regularization methods, detailing disagreement-based and self-training approaches for improving model performance through high-confidence pseudo-labels.
