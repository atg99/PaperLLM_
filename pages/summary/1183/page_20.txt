The paper explores various aspects of deep semi-supervised learning (DSSL), emphasizing how geometry interacts with the training process. It examines the effects of data augmentation and labeled dataset size on both pre-training and self-training, highlighting their limitations and interactions. The incorporation of domain knowledge is discussed as a means to improve performance when labeled and unlabeled data distributions differ, which can degrade learning outcomes.

The paper also addresses learning with noisy labels, proposing methods to enhance the standard cross-entropy loss function when dealing with inaccurate labels. It reviews strategies to handle class imbalance in semi-supervised learning, noting that traditional methods often assume a uniform class distribution, which may not reflect real-world scenarios.

Additionally, robust semi-supervised learning approaches are presented, focusing on consistency training and advanced data augmentation techniques to improve model performance. The concept of safe semi-supervised learning is introduced, where the aim is to avoid performance degradation when incorporating unlabeled data.

In conclusion, the paper categorizes existing DSSL methods into generative models, consistency regularization models, graph-based models, pseudo-labeling models, and hybrid models. It identifies challenges within DSSL and proposes future research directions to address these issues.
