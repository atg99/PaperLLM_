This technique involves switching to an averaging procedure after a certain number of epochs, allowing the model to converge to a flatter region of the loss landscape, which is believed to lead to better generalization. Consequently, the final model weights are computed as the average of the weights collected during training, which can mitigate overfitting.

In summary, the paper discusses various architectures for consistency regularization in semi-supervised learning. Key models include the  Model, which minimizes prediction differences through unsupervised consistency loss, and Temporal Ensembling, which uses Exponential Moving Average for predictions to reduce computational load. The Mean Teacher model leverages EMA for model weights to enhance accuracy, while Virtual Adversarial Training introduces adversarial perturbations to enforce consistency. The Dual Student model improves upon Mean Teacher by employing two independent student models to learn distinct representations. Finally, Stochastic Weight Averaging aims to improve generalization by averaging model weights over training epochs. Each method presents unique strategies to enhance consistency and performance in semi-supervised settings.
