This paper presents a comprehensive survey of deep semi-supervised learning (SSL), an evolving field with significant applications across various domains. The authors introduce a taxonomy that categorizes existing methods into five main groups: deep generative methods, consistency regularization methods, graph-based methods, pseudo-labeling methods, and hybrid methods. They review 52 representative methods, comparing them based on loss types, contributions, and architectural differences.

The paper emphasizes the challenge of acquiring labeled data, particularly in fields like medical imaging, where obtaining expert-annotated labels is resource-intensive. In contrast, unlabeled data is often more accessible, making SSL a valuable approach to enhance learning performance by leveraging both labeled and unlabeled datasets.

The authors define three key SSL tasks—semi-supervised classification, clustering, and regression—each aimed at improving model performance beyond what can be achieved with labeled data alone. While the survey primarily focuses on image classification, the concepts discussed can be adapted to other domains such as object detection and semantic segmentation.

Additionally, the paper addresses the limitations of existing methods and proposes heuristic solutions for overcoming these challenges, marking the importance of continued research in the field of deep semi-supervised learning.
