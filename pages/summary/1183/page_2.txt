The paper provides a comprehensive overview of deep semi-supervised learning (DSSL), emphasizing its significance in leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data through deep neural networks. The authors classify DSSL methods into five main categories: generative methods, consistency regularization methods, graph-based methods, pseudo-labeling methods, and hybrid methods, as depicted in a taxonomy figure. 

The review highlights previous surveys on semi-supervised learning (SSL) that either lack a unified taxonomy or fail to encompass recent advancements, such as adversarial training. The authors aim to fill these gaps by summarizing fundamental theories, comparing various DSSL methods, and discussing open problems and future trends in the field.

The paper is structured into sections that introduce SSL background knowledge, outline primary DSSL techniques, and address challenges along with potential solutions. The optimization problem central to SSL is formally presented, demonstrating the interplay between supervised loss, unsupervised loss, and regularization. The authors emphasize that the distinctions between unsupervised loss and regularization are often blurred, as both contribute to the model's performance in a semi-supervised context.
