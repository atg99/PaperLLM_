Co-training, for instance, leverages two distinct views of the same data to train two separate classifiers. Each classifier predicts labels for the unlabeled data, iteratively refining the training process until either the unlabeled data is exhausted or a predetermined condition is met. The core assumption is that the classifiers will yield consistent predictions across different views. The loss functions employed ensure that this consistency is maintained, while the introduction of a View Difference Constraint encourages diversity in predictions, particularly in the presence of adversarial examples.

Tri-Net expands on this concept by utilizing three classifiers trained on three different datasets created through bootstrap sampling. By incorporating output smearing, which adds noise to the labeled samples, Tri-Net generates diverse training sets. The classifiers predict pseudo-labels for unlabeled data, and when two predictions agree, the label is deemed reliable. The integration of multiple classifiers enhances the model's robustness, as it encourages diversity while ensuring that the models are fine-tuned to maintain consistency in their predictions.

In summary, disagreement-based semi-supervised learning methods capitalize on the potential of multiple learners to better harness unlabeled data, promoting both consistency in predictions and diversity in model training.
