The paper discusses various methodologies within the realm of semi-supervised learning (SSL) and their applications. It begins by explaining generative models, which assume a joint distribution of data and labels, and highlights their reliance on identifiable distributions like polynomials or Gaussian mixtures. The paper notes that while generative models can be optimized using iterative algorithms such as the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm, they can suffer from performance degradation if the assumed distribution does not align well with the actual data.

Transductive Support Vector Machines (TSVMs) are presented as a prime example of models following the low-density separation principle. TSVMs aim to maximize the gap between decision boundaries and data points while incorporating unlabeled data into the optimization process through various algorithms.

Graph-based methods are introduced, emphasizing the use of data geometry to propagate label information from labeled to unlabeled nodes through label propagation techniques. This method is adaptable to deep learning contexts.

The paper also explores related learning paradigms that leverage additional data sources to enhance learning performance, including transfer learning, which applies knowledge from source domains to target domains; weakly-supervised learning, which deals with incomplete, inexact, or inaccurate labels; positive and unlabeled learning, focusing on training with only positive and unlabeled samples; meta-learning, which enables rapid adaptation to new tasks; and self-supervised learning, which utilizes input data for supervision without manual labeling.

Lastly, the paper highlights the diverse applications of SSL across various domains such as computer vision and natural language processing, mentioning datasets and methodologies relevant to tasks like image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. It emphasizes the adaptability of SSL methods to different applications and refers to a summary of widely used datasets in the field.
