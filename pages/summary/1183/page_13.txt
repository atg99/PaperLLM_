The paper discusses consistency constraints in semi-supervised learning and graph-based semi-supervised learning (GSSL). It identifies three levels where consistency can be applied: the input dataset, neural networks, and the training process. Perturbations like additive noise and adversarial training are introduced at the input level, while network architecture can be modified by dropping layers or connections. The training process can utilize Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) or Exponential Moving Average (EMA) parameters to enhance consistency.

In the realm of GSSL, the paper emphasizes the importance of graph structures, where nodes represent training samples and edges indicate their similarities. The goal of graph embedding methods is to encode nodes into vectors that reflect their roles and neighborhood structure. The paper outlines a unified loss function for these methods and categorizes them into shallow and deep embedding techniques. Shallow methods include DeepWalk and node2vec, whereas deep methods leverage complex neural networks.

The paper further explores AutoEncoder-based methods, which utilize a unary decoder to correlate nodes with their neighborhood vectors. Notable techniques discussed include Structural Deep Network Embedding (SDNE), which maintains network proximities through deep autoencoders, and DNGR, which combines random surfing with stacked denoising autoencoders to enhance robustness against noise. Additionally, the Graph AutoEncoder (GAE) and Variational Graph AutoEncoder (VGAE) are introduced, which incorporate both contextual and attribute information through graph convolutional layers, optimizing the reconstruction of adjacency matrices while considering data distributions.
