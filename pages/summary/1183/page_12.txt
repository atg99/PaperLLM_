The paper summarizes various consistency regularization methods used in semi-supervised learning (SSL). Each method employs unique techniques, transformations, and consistency constraints to enhance model performance.

1. **Ladder Network**: Introduces Gaussian noise at every neural layer and maintains consistency between outputs of the original and perturbed inputs.

2. **Temporal Ensembling**: Utilizes different stochastic augmentations and exponential moving average (EMA) of predictions for consistent outputs.

3. **Mean Teacher**: Applies stochastic augmentations with EMA on model weights to ensure output consistency.

4. **VAT (Virtual Adversarial Training)**: Implements adversarial perturbations on inputs to improve generalization, ensuring output consistency between perturbed and original inputs.

5. **Dual Student**: Focuses on stable samples with a stabilization constraint, comparing outputs from perturbed inputs.

6. **SWA (Stochastic Weight Averaging)**: Averages multiple network weights during training to promote consistency in predictions, addressing issues with SGD convergence.

7. **VAdD (Virtual Adversarial Dropout)**: Combines adversarial training with dropout techniques to enhance model robustness through consistency across different dropout masks.

8. **WCP (Worst-case Perturbation)**: Proposes a regularization mechanism that minimizes vulnerability by enhancing resilient weights against perturbations, using both additive noise and structural changes.

9. **UDA (Unsupervised Data Augmentation)**: Focuses on high-quality data augmentations for images and text to maintain output consistency.

The overarching principle of these methods is that the model's output should remain consistent despite various transformations or perturbations applied to the input data. This approach aims to improve the model's robustness and generalization capabilities in SSL contexts.
