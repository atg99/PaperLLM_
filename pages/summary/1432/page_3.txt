This paper discusses the identification and mitigation of unimodal biases in Visual Question Answering (VQA) models. It highlights the tendency of VQA models to rely on superficial correlations from one input modality, such as questions, which can lead to biased predictions. Previous methods have addressed biases related to gender, human reporting, and distribution shifts between lab-curated and real-world data. The authors emphasize the importance of assessing unimodal biases by training models using only one modality, particularly through the question-only model, which reveals the biases learned from the question data.

To combat these biases, the paper presents the RUBi learning strategy, which modifies loss values during training to diminish the influence of biased examples while enhancing the significance of complementary examples. The authors also propose the use of balanced datasets to reduce biases, mentioning the limitations of rejection sampling due to annotation costs and the introduction of datasets like VQA v2 to counteract language priors.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts their end-to-end approach with existing models, such as the Grounded VQA model, which employs a more complex architecture. The authors assert that their model-agnostic strategy is more versatile and effective in reducing unwanted biases during training. They also highlight a related work that uses an adversarial question-only branch but argue that their approach more comprehensively targets the full VQA model parameters, leading to more effective bias mitigation.
