The paper discusses the shortcomings of traditional Visual Question Answering (VQA) models, particularly their tendency to learn unimodal biases from training data. These biases occur when models rely heavily on one modality, such as questions, to make predictions, often neglecting the complementary modality, such as images. This issue is highlighted through examples where models predict answers based solely on question context, leading to poor generalization on diverse datasets.

To address this, the authors propose a novel learning strategy called RUBi, which modifies the standard cross-entropy loss function used in VQA models. This approach aims to mitigate the learning of biases by incorporating a question-only branch, allowing the model to better capture and measure biases present in the training data. The authors argue that by integrating this strategy, VQA models can achieve improved performance and robustness when faced with real-world data distributions.

The paper emphasizes the importance of optimizing the model parameters to ensure that both modalities—questions and images—are utilized effectively in making predictions. The proposed RUBi strategy is visually compared to classical learning methods, demonstrating its potential to enhance VQA model capabilities.
