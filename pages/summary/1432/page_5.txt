The paper discusses two learning strategies in Visual Question Answering (VQA): the classical learning strategy and the RUBi (Reducing Unwanted Biases) learning strategy. The RUBi strategy aims to mitigate biases in VQA models by employing a question-only branch that captures question biases, allowing the main model to focus on examples that require both question and image modalities for correct answers. This branch is formalized as a function that processes questions through an encoder, a neural network, and a classifier to generate predictions. 

During training, the question-only branch acts as a proxy to prevent the main model from learning biases inherent in the questions. After training, this branch is removed, and the base model's predictions are utilized. To further prevent bias, the predictions are merged with a dynamic mask that modifies the loss function based on the predictions. This mask is created by applying a sigmoid function to the output of the question-only neural network. 

The approach is demonstrated through two scenarios: one where the importance of biased examples is reduced, specifically those that can be correctly classified without the image modality. The RUBi strategy aims to enhance the accuracy of VQA models by addressing biases that can otherwise distort learning outcomes.
