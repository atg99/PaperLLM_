The paper presents RUBi, a novel training strategy aimed at reducing unimodal biases in Visual Question Answering (VQA) models. Traditional VQA models often exploit statistical correlations between questions and answers, neglecting the image modality. This reliance on shortcuts can lead to high accuracy on certain benchmarks without truly understanding the visual content. The authors highlight that when evaluated on datasets with different statistical patterns, these models experience a significant drop in performance.

To address this issue, RUBi introduces a question-only branch during training, which helps to adjust the loss dynamically. This mechanism reduces the influence of biased examples that can be answered without reference to the image, thereby encouraging the model to utilize both question and image modalities effectively. After training, the question-only branch is removed, allowing the model to function independently.

Extensive experiments on the VQA-CP v2 dataset demonstrate that RUBi significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, showing its effectiveness in enhancing model robustness against question modality biases. The framework is also shown to be competitive on the standard VQA v2 dataset, indicating its broader applicability across various VQA architectures. The paper underscores the necessity of developing strategies to mitigate biases inherent in real-world datasets, which can adversely affect machine learning models.
