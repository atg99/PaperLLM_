The paper "RUBi: Reducing Unimodal Biases for Visual Question Answering" addresses the issue of Visual Question Answering (VQA) models that rely on unimodal biases, allowing them to provide correct answers without fully utilizing image information. This reliance leads to significant performance drops when models are tested with data outside their training distribution, making them inadequate for practical applications.

To tackle this problem, the authors introduce RUBi, a novel learning strategy aimed at minimizing biases in VQA models. RUBi emphasizes the importance of using both visual and textual inputs by downweighting biased examples—those that can be answered correctly without reference to the image. The method involves a question-only model that identifies and captures language biases, thereby preventing the primary VQA model from learning these undesirable correlations. This approach dynamically adjusts the loss function to compensate for biases, encouraging the model to engage both modalities effectively.

The effectiveness of RUBi is validated through experiments on the VQA-CP v2 dataset, which is designed to evaluate the robustness of VQA models against varying question biases at test time compared to training time. The results demonstrate that RUBi surpasses existing state-of-the-art performance, showcasing its potential for improving the reliability of VQA systems in real-world scenarios. The authors have made their code publicly available for further research.

In summary, RUBi presents a significant advancement in VQA by reducing reliance on unimodal biases, thereby promoting a more integrated use of visual and textual information, which is critical for the practical deployment of VQA systems.
