The paper discusses the design motivations and advantages of SegFormer compared to SETR in semantic segmentation tasks. It emphasizes that relying solely on non-local attention from Stage-4 is insufficient for optimal results, as evidenced in the experimental results presented in Table 1d. Key distinctions between SegFormer and SETR include:

1. SegFormer is pre-trained only on ImageNet-1K, whereas SETR uses the larger ImageNet-22K.
2. SegFormer’s encoder features a hierarchical architecture that captures both high-resolution and low-resolution features, contrasting with SETR’s single low-resolution feature map.
3. Positional Embedding is eliminated in SegFormer, avoiding accuracy issues when the resolution during inference differs from training, unlike SETR which uses fixed Positional Embedding.
4. The MLP decoder in SegFormer is more compact and computationally efficient than SETR's heavy decoder that relies on multiple convolutions.

In the experimental section, the authors utilized four public datasets: Cityscapes, ADE20K, and COCO-Stuff, detailing their implementation approach, including data augmentation techniques and training configurations. They trained their models using the AdamW optimizer, reporting performance via mean Intersection over Union (mIoU).

Ablation studies revealed that increasing the encoder size consistently improves performance across all datasets, with the lightweight SegFormer-B0 model demonstrating efficiency suitable for real-time applications. In contrast, the largest model, SegFormer-B5, achieved state-of-the-art performance across the datasets, highlighting the effectiveness of their Transformer encoder design.
