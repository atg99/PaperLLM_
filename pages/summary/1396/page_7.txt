The paper presents ablation studies and comparisons of different model configurations in semantic segmentation tasks using various datasets, notably ADE20K and Cityscapes. 

In Table 1(a), the performance metrics of several model sizes (MiT-B0 to MiT-B5) are detailed, including accuracy, parameters, and FLOPs. As model size increases, both accuracy and computational cost (FLOPs) generally improve, with MiT-B4 and MiT-B5 achieving the highest mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) scores across datasets. 

Table 1(b) examines the impact of the MLP decoder channel dimension (C) on performance. It shows that while increasing C improves performance, it also raises computational costs and that performance plateaus beyond a dimension of 768. Consequently, C=256 is selected for real-time models, while C=768 is used for larger models.

Table 1(c) compares the Mix-FeedForward Network (Mix-FFN) against traditional positional encoding (PE) in the encoder. Results indicate that Mix-FFN yields better accuracy and is less sensitive to test resolution compared to models using PE.

Table 1(d) contrasts the performance of CNN and Transformer encoders with an MLP decoder, highlighting that the MiT models outperform traditional CNNs like ResNet in terms of mIoU while maintaining lower FLOPs.

Table 2 provides a comparison of the proposed SegFormer models against state-of-the-art methods, emphasizing SegFormer’s advantages in parameters, FLOPs, speed, and accuracy across datasets. Notably, SegFormer-B4 and B5 achieve high accuracy while maintaining competitive computational efficiency.

Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed SegFormer architecture effectively balances accuracy and computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time applications in semantic segmentation.
