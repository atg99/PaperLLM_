This paper discusses the evolution of semantic segmentation methods, highlighting advancements in backbone architectures from early VGG models to the more powerful and deeper networks. It emphasizes the importance of contextual information in segmentation, notably through techniques like dilated convolutions. The introduction of Transformers, particularly the vision Transformer (ViT) and its application in semantic segmentation via models like SETR, is examined. Despite their success, ViT faces limitations related to single-scale outputs and high computational costs.

To address these challenges, the authors present SegFormer, a novel Transformer framework specifically designed for semantic segmentation. SegFormer incorporates significant innovations in both its encoder and decoder. The encoder is designed to be positional-encoding-free and hierarchical, allowing it to adapt to various image resolutions without performance loss while generating both high-resolution and low-resolution features. The decoder is a lightweight All-MLP design that efficiently combines local and global attentions from different layers of the Transformer.

The performance of SegFormer is demonstrated across three datasets: ADE20K, Cityscapes, and COCO-Stuff. The results show that SegFormer-B0 achieves a mean intersection over union (mIoU) of 71.9% on Cityscapes at 48 frames per second, significantly outperforming the ICNet model. The larger SegFormer-B5 model reaches an mIoU of 84.0%, surpassing SETR while being faster. On ADE20K, SegFormer sets a new state-of-the-art with 51.8% mIoU, all while maintaining a smaller model size and demonstrating robustness against common corruptions. The authors conclude that SegFormer is a highly efficient and effective framework suitable for safety-critical applications, with plans to release the code publicly.
