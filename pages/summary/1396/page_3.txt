The paper presents the SegFormer framework, which is designed for semantic segmentation and consists of two primary components: a hierarchical Transformer encoder and a lightweight All-MLP decoder. The encoder extracts both coarse and fine features, while the decoder fuses these multi-level features to predict the semantic segmentation mask. The framework addresses the complexities and computational demands of previous methods, particularly those that enhance FCN architectures and Transformer-based approaches.

The paper highlights the evolution of semantic segmentation from image classification, noting significant advancements made since the introduction of Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN). Researchers have explored various techniques to improve segmentation, including enlarging the receptive field, refining contextual information, and integrating attention modules. While Transformer architectures have shown promise in semantic segmentation, they often remain computationally intensive.

The SegFormer framework innovates by using smaller 4x4 patches instead of the larger 16x16 patches commonly used in Vision Transformers (ViT), enabling better performance in semantic segmentation tasks. The hierarchical structure allows for the extraction of multi-level features at different resolutions, enhancing the model's ability to process images effectively.

Overall, SegFormer aims to provide a more efficient solution for semantic segmentation while maintaining high performance, making it suitable for real-time applications.
