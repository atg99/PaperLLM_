The paper discusses advancements in semantic segmentation, specifically focusing on the role of positional encodings (PE) and decoder design. It critiques previous works such as CPVT and CvT for their reliance on adding PEs to feature maps, arguing that this is unnecessary for semantic segmentation. Instead, the authors propose removing PEs entirely to address training/testing resolution mismatches.

The lightweight All-MLP decoder used in SegFormer is highlighted for its simplicity, consisting solely of MLP layers, which avoids the complexity of traditional methods. This decoder leverages a hierarchical Transformer encoder that has a larger effective receptive field (ERF) compared to conventional CNN encoders. The decoder operates in four key steps: unifying channel dimensions, up-sampling and concatenating features, fusing these features, and predicting the segmentation mask.

Effective receptive field analysis reveals that while DeepLabv3+ has a relatively small ERF, SegFormer’s architecture supports both local and non-local attention, enabling better context capture. This design allows SegFormer to generate powerful representations with fewer parameters by effectively combining local and non-local attention. The paper emphasizes that this decoder design is particularly effective due to the inherent properties of Transformers, setting it apart from CNN-based approaches that are limited by their receptive fields.
