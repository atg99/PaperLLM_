The paper presents a novel approach for semantic segmentation using a hierarchical Transformer encoder and an All-MLP decoder. The key components of the proposed architecture include the Mix Transformer encoders (MiT-B0 to MiT-B5), which utilize a hierarchical structure and efficient self-attention mechanisms to generate multi-level features from input images.

The hierarchical feature representation allows the encoder to create both high-resolution coarse features and low-resolution fine-grained features, enhancing segmentation performance. The authors implement a sequence reduction process to address the computational complexity of self-attention, reducing it from O(N^2) to O(N^2/R) by reshaping and linearly transforming the input sequence.

In addition, the paper introduces an overlapping patch merging technique, which retains local continuity better than traditional non-overlapping methods. This involves defining patch size, stride, and padding to produce hierarchical feature maps while maintaining spatial coherence.

Furthermore, the authors propose a positional-encoding-free design, utilizing a Mix-FFN that integrates convolutional layers to provide positional information without the need for interpolation of fixed positional encodings. This design choice addresses accuracy drops due to resolution mismatches commonly encountered in semantic segmentation tasks.

Overall, the proposed methods demonstrate significant improvements in segmentation performance, distinguishing it from existing approaches like SETR.
