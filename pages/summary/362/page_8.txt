In this paper, the authors introduce XLNet-CNN, a novel model that integrates the global context understanding of XLNet with the local feature extraction capabilities of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for multi-label text classification. The model was evaluated across three datasets: Ohsumed, CAVES, and HateXplain. Results indicate that XLNet-CNN outperformed both the base XLNet and various domain-specific pretrained BERT models, including HateBERT, BioBERT, and CT-BERT, particularly excelling in the F1-score metric. This success was primarily attributed to XLNet-CNN's significantly higher recall, enabling it to capture a larger number of relevant labels, thus enhancing its overall performance.

The authors note that while XLNet-CNN achieved an AUC-ROC score of 88.31, closely trailing HateBERT at 88.68, it consistently surpassed the other models in F1-score due to its effective label identification. The paper discusses the challenges faced in model training due to limited computational resources, which necessitated techniques like gradient accumulation and mixed precision. These constraints limited the batch size to 8, potentially affecting the models' performance. The authors suggest that removing these limitations in future work could yield further improvements, alongside architectural enhancements and hyper-parameter tuning.

Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of combining XLNet and CNN for handling diverse datasets in multi-label classification tasks, underscoring the potential for future advancements in this area.
