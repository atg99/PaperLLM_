This paper discusses advancements in natural language processing (NLP) through the use of transformer-based models, particularly focusing on BERT and XLNet. BERT processes entire sequences of words simultaneously, enabling it to capture contextual relationships through its pre-training tasks, Masked Language Model (MLM) and Next Sentence Prediction (NSP). Domain-specific adaptations of BERT, such as BioBERT and HateBERT, enhance performance in specialized fields by fine-tuning on relevant corpora.

XLNet, introduced in 2019, addresses limitations of BERT by predicting words based on all permutations of input sequences, which improves its understanding of language nuances. However, XLNet sometimes struggles with local context, which is crucial for tasks like multi-label text classification (MLTC). The authors propose a new model, XLNet-CNN, which integrates a 1D convolutional neural network (CNN) with XLNet to better capture local dependencies and enhance text classification capabilities.

The performance of XLNet-CNN was evaluated on three multi-label datasets from distinct domains: Ohsumed (medical abstracts), CAVES (anti-COVID vaccine tweets), and HateXplain (cyberbullying comments). Results indicated that XLNet-CNN significantly outperformed both XLNet and domain-specific models in terms of F1-score, primarily due to its superior recall. Though precision was slightly lower, the improved recall led to better overall performance metrics. The combination of XLNet's global context and CNN's local feature extraction proved effective for diverse datasets.

The paper proceeds with a detailed examination of the datasets used, the architecture of the XLNet-CNN model, and the methodology for training and evaluating performance. It highlights the importance of the F1-score as a key metric for MLTC and discusses limitations encountered during the experiments along with their potential impact on the results.
