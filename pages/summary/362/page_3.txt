The paper discusses three key datasets utilized for text classification: the Ohsumed dataset, the CAVES dataset, and the HateXplain dataset. 

The Ohsumed dataset comprises 13,931 documents, divided into a training set of 6,287 documents and a test set of 7,644 documents. 

The CAVES dataset focuses on anti-COVID vaccine tweets, containing approximately 10,000 entries that are manually labeled with 11 categories of anti-vaccine concerns. These concerns encompass issues like vaccine ingredients, side effects, pharmaceutical motivations, and doubts about vaccine-producing countries. Notably, the dataset is multi-labeled, reflecting that a single tweet may express multiple concerns, and includes human-annotated explanations linking specific text segments to the respective concerns.

The HateXplain dataset is designed for analyzing cyberbullying on social media, featuring annotated comments categorized by attributes such as race, religion, gender, and sexual orientation. Similar to the CAVES dataset, individual comments can possess multiple labels, allowing for a nuanced understanding of cyberbullying incidents.

Overall, the paper highlights the significance of these datasets in advancing text classification methodologies, particularly in contexts of public health and social media behavior.
