The paper presents a model training and evaluation methodology for a multi-label classification task using a proposed architecture called XLNet-CNN. The training was conducted with a set of hyperparameters optimized for resource constraints, utilizing a Kaggle P100 GPU with 16GB memory. Key hyperparameters included a sequence length of 512 tokens, a train batch size of 8, and a learning rate of 1e-5. To optimize the training process, strategies such as gradient accumulation, mixed precision training, and the Adam optimizer were employed. The model’s predictions were processed through a sigmoid function with a threshold of 0.5, and binary cross-entropy was used as the loss function.

Model evaluation was conducted at the end of each epoch on the validation set, utilizing metrics such as subset accuracy, F1-score, precision, recall, AUC-ROC, and AUPR. The best model was selected based on the highest F1-score. After training and validating, the model was tested on a separate test set using the same evaluation metrics.

The comparative performance section highlighted that XLNet-CNN outperformed XLNet and other domain-specific pre-trained BERT models across three datasets. Notably, BERT displayed poor performance on the Ohsumed dataset with a consistent F1-score of 0.0, indicating it failed to make correct predictions. In contrast, XLNet-CNN achieved the highest F1-scores across all datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in multi-label text classification tasks. The results underscore XLNet-CNN's superiority in this context, particularly evident in the Ohsumed test dataset where it achieved an F1-score of 61.41, significantly outperforming other models.
