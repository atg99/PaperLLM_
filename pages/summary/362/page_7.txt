The paper presents an evaluation of the XLNet-CNN model against various benchmarks, highlighting its performance on multiple datasets, including CAVES, Ohsumed, and HateXplain. Key findings from the analysis include:

1. **F1-Score**: The XLNet-CNN model consistently achieved the highest F1-scores across all datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in multi-label classification. Notably, it scored 62.47 on CAVES, 61.41 on Ohsumed, and 74.91 on HateXplain, outperforming domain-specific models like CT-BERT and HateBERT.

2. **AUPR (Area Under Precision-Recall Curve)**: While XLNet-CNN performed competitively, it did not always surpass domain-specific models. It achieved AUPR scores of 67.97 on CAVES, 64.62 on Ohsumed, and 80.42 on HateXplain, performing closely to HateBERT.

3. **Precision and Recall**: The model showed high recall rates, indicating its ability to identify relevant labels effectively. In CAVES, it had a recall of 66.75, higher than competitors, while its precision was lower, leading to the highest F1-score due to the balance between the two metrics.

4. **AUC-ROC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve)**: XLNet-CNN achieved AUC-ROC scores of 89.79 on CAVES and 89.21 on Ohsumed, indicating strong performance in distinguishing relevant from irrelevant labels.

5. **Subset Accuracy**: Although XLNet-CNN had a lower subset accuracy (41.17 on CAVES) compared to CT-BERT (52.60), this metric is deemed strict for multi-label classification, as it requires all predicted labels to be correct.

The superior performance of XLNet-CNN is attributed to its integration of XLNet’s global context understanding with CNN’s capability to capture local patterns, enhancing its effectiveness in identifying interrelated labels in imbalanced datasets.
