The paper presents the XLNet-CNN model for multi-label classification tasks, specifically targeting hate speech detection using a processed version of the hateXplain dataset. The dataset, originally in JSON format, has been converted into a CSV format that categorizes comments as normal, offensive, or hate speech, along with attributes related to race, religion, gender, and sexual orientation. Preprocessing involved filtering for offensive and hate speech comments and converting categorical attributes into binary values.

The XLNet-CNN model integrates the XLNet Transformer with a 1D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to leverage the strengths of both architectures. XLNet is effective in capturing long-range dependencies and global context through its self-attention mechanism, while CNNs are adept at recognizing local patterns such as n-grams or short phrases. This combination aims to enhance performance in text classification tasks that require understanding both long and short-range dependencies.

The model architecture involves several key components. Input data is prepared by tokenizing text sequences and transforming them into a suitable format for XLNet. The XLNet layer produces contextualized embeddings for each token, represented mathematically as hidden states. To mitigate overfitting, a dropout layer is applied to the XLNet output before passing it to the CNN layer. The CNN processes the embeddings to learn local patterns, utilizing 128 filters with a kernel size of 3 and applying a ReLU activation function for non-linearity. The architecture is designed to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of hate speech classification.
