The paper discusses various hardware acceleration techniques for deep neural networks, focusing on Field Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs), Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs), and emerging device technologies.

In the FPGA section, it highlights the advantages of fine-grain and coarse-grain reconfiguration capabilities. Flexible hierarchical memory systems enhance on-chip memory efficiency and reduce energy consumption. Notable experiments demonstrate significant speedups in convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures, with specific designs achieving up to 17.42 times faster performance than CPUs. Innovative designs like the Memsqueezer optimize memory usage, achieving an 80% reduction in energy consumption. The Caffeine engine improves memory access efficiency, yielding peak performances of 365 GOPS and 636 GOPS on different FPGA models. Other designs, such as a 3D array architecture and fused layer accelerators, further enhance performance across CNN layers.

The ASIC section discusses methods to optimize deep neural network implementations by expanding memory and improving data locality. The Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) is noted for its efficiency in low-precision computations, achieving speeds up to 30 times faster than Nvidia GPUs. Other architectures, like TETRIS and a 64-chip system by Luo et al., focus on minimizing data movement to enhance performance and reduce energy consumption. Chain-NN and SIMD processors are mentioned for their effectiveness in real-time image processing tasks.

Finally, the paper touches on new devices, suggesting that advancements in very large scale integration systems could significantly accelerate deep convolutional neural networks by emulating complex biological neuron architectures. Overall, the advancements in FPGA and ASIC designs, along with new device technologies, promise substantial improvements in the efficiency and speed of deep learning applications.
