The paper provides an overview of recent advancements in accelerating convolutional neural networks (CNNs) at various levels, including structure, algorithm, and implementation. It highlights the trade-offs between different hardware options, such as ASIC, FPGA, and GPU, in terms of design flexibility, power efficiency, and throughput. FPGAs are noted for their ease of programming and reconfi-gurability, making them suitable for prototyping and adapting to various neural network models, despite their limited memory access and lower throughput compared to GPUs.

The authors emphasize the importance of optimizing CNNs for inference time, which requires less memory access than training. They discuss how CNNs can be compressed without significant accuracy loss due to redundancy in their architectures. Additionally, the paper mentions the role of Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) methods in improving training convergence speed and computational efficiency.

The conclusion stresses the necessity of accelerating CNNs for practical applications, especially as they become increasingly complex and data-intensive. The authors note the lack of a standardized evaluation system for comparing acceleration performance across different methods and levels, making it challenging to assess advancements in this field. The research was supported by various foundations and councils, and references are provided for further reading on related topics.
