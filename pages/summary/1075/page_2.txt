This paper provides a comprehensive review of recent advancements in convolutional neural network (CNN) acceleration methods, distinguishing itself from existing literature in three key ways. First, while other reviews often focus on various applications of deep neural networks, such as computer vision and natural language processing, this paper specifically emphasizes CNNs and their acceleration methods. Second, it prioritizes recent research in the context of hardware limitations and efficiency, rather than providing a historical overview of neural network development. Third, it integrates discussions of hardware with algorithmic strategies for acceleration, acknowledging their interdependence.

The structure of the paper is organized into several sections. Section 2 presents an overview of modern CNN architectures, highlighting typical layers and areas of improvement. Section 3 introduces a taxonomy for CNN acceleration methods, followed by detailed discussions in Sections 4 (CNN compression), 5 (algorithm optimization), and 6 (hardware-oriented acceleration). Section 7 offers a discussion on these methods from various perspectives, and Section 8 concludes with future challenges in the field.

The paper begins by discussing the foundational LeNet-5 architecture, a 7-layer CNN structure, and mentions modifications that have led to more complex networks like AlexNet, GoogLeNet, VGG-16, and ResNet. The aim is to summarize state-of-the-art CNNs and their respective features, setting the stage for a deeper exploration of acceleration techniques in subsequent sections.
