The paper discusses the recent advances in accelerating convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which have achieved impressive results in various fields such as image classification and multimedia compression. The authors highlight the inherent properties of CNNs, such as local connectivity and weight sharing, which help reduce parameters and improve processing speed. However, as data dimensions and CNN architectures grow more complex, the computational demands increase, posing a challenge to effective implementation.

To address this, the paper proposes a taxonomy of acceleration methods categorized into three levels: structure level, algorithm level, and implementation level. The authors review existing research on CNN acceleration, analyzing methods related to CNN architecture compression, algorithm optimization, and hardware enhancements. They conclude that there remains significant potential for exploration within each category of acceleration methods.

The paper emphasizes the importance of these advancements given the growing industrial impact of CNNs. It also references notable developments in hardware accelerators, such as Google's Tensor Processing Unit and NVIDIA's Deep Learning Accelerator, which enhance performance for deep learning applications.

In summary, the authors aim to provide a comprehensive overview of CNN acceleration techniques to guide researchers in future explorations within this vital area of study.
