The paper discusses the architecture and functionality of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), emphasizing their layered structure and the benefits of weight sharing, which reduces the total number of parameters and enhances translation invariance. It highlights the role of subsampling layers, which follow convolutional layers to decrease feature map resolution and mitigate overfitting through various pooling methods, including max pooling, average pooling, spatial pyramids pooling, and spectral pooling. 

The fully-connected layer converts high-level features into a one-dimensional format, connecting all units from the previous layer to extract complex patterns. The output layer serves as the final stage for predictions, linking neuron units in a fully-connected manner.

The paper also presents a taxonomy for CNN acceleration methods, categorized into design, training, and hardware implementation. It identifies redundancies in weights and representation, suggesting techniques such as layer decomposition, network pruning, and fixed-point representation for optimization. The discussion includes training algorithms like gradient descent and explores efficient convolution methods, as well as hardware platforms such as GPUs, FPGAs, and ASICs for implementation. The authors aim to provide a comprehensive understanding of CNN acceleration from structural, algorithmic, and hardware perspectives, particularly for deep CNNs.
