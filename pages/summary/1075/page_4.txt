The paper discusses various activation functions used in neural networks, highlighting their characteristics and implications for training. It categorizes these functions into saturated and non-saturated types, as illustrated in Table 2 and Figure 2.

Saturated functions include Sigmoid and Tanh, which exhibit saturation at extreme input values, leading to gradients that approach zero. This saturation hampers backpropagation, making it challenging for networks to converge during training. Additionally, weight initialization becomes critical when using these functions, as they may hinder initial learning.

To address the saturation issue, several non-saturated activation functions have been developed, including Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU), Leaky ReLU, Parametric ReLU (PReLU), Randomized Leaky ReLU (RReLU), and Exponential Linear Unit (ELU). These functions maintain non-zero gradients across a wider range of inputs, facilitating improved learning dynamics in neural networks.

Overall, the choice of activation function is crucial for the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), as it influences both convergence speed and learning efficiency.
