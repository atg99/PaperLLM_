This paper discusses methods to reduce computation complexity in neural networks, focusing on redundancy in representations and algorithmic optimizations. A significant observation is that many weights in neural networks are minimal, often requiring high precision for representation, typically using 32-bit floating-point formats. This precision increases computation workload and memory usage. To mitigate this, the paper suggests using fixed-point representations, which can deliver satisfactory performance with reduced resource requirements. Techniques like 16-bit fixed-point and 10-bit dynamic fixed-point representations have been explored, along with quantization methods that reduce weights to 8-bit without sacrificing accuracy.

The paper highlights advancements in binarization, where weights and inputs are represented as binary values. Several methods are introduced, including BinaryConnect, XNOR-Nets, and Bitwise Neural Networks, which enhance computational efficiency and speed up convolution operations. Ternary CNNs are also mentioned as a middle ground between binary and full precision networks, balancing compression and accuracy.

Stochastic computing (SC) is presented as a technique that simplifies computations into bit-wise operations, leveraging random bit streams to represent continuous values. This approach shows promise for efficient deep CNN implementations, particularly in resource-constrained environments like ASIC and embedded devices. The paper references successful implementations of SC in CNNs, highlighting improvements in accuracy and resource consumption.

At the algorithmic level, the paper emphasizes the importance of optimizing gradient descent methods during training, particularly for convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It notes that while convolution operations reduce the number of weights, they also increase computational intensity. The paper concludes by discussing the potential for further optimizations in both backward training methods and forward inference processes.
