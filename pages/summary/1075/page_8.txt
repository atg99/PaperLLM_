The paper discusses various methods for reducing the complexity and size of neural network models, focusing on techniques such as pruning, block-circulant projections, and knowledge distillation.

Pruning techniques are highlighted, where Li et al. achieve significant reductions in inference costs for VGG-16 and ResNet-110 by removing whole filters and their corresponding feature maps. Their approach utilizes the sum of filter absolute values to assess filter importance, maintaining filter-based connectivity. Molchanov et al. further contribute by employing an iterative pruning method in transfer learning, based on Taylor expansion of cost functions.

The paper introduces block-circulant matrices as a structured approach for dimension reduction. These matrices can efficiently represent unstructured matrices with vectors, leading to reduced storage requirements. Cheng et al. and Yang et al. demonstrate improvements in efficiency with minimal accuracy loss by applying circulant structures in fully connected layers. Ding et al. extend this concept to both fully connected and convolutional layers, proving that fewer weights do not compromise the network's performance.

Knowledge distillation is presented as a method for transferring knowledge from complex neural networks to simpler, compact models. This process involves training the compact model on data labeled by the complex network and using synthetic data generated by it. The importance of synthetic data is emphasized, as it helps the compact model generalize well and reduces overfitting. Bucilu et al. explore different methods for generating pseudo data, while Hinton et al. and Romero et al. illustrate successful applications of the teacher-student framework in compressing neural networks.

Overall, the paper underscores the effectiveness of these techniques in achieving model compression and enhancing computational efficiency without significantly sacrificing performance.
