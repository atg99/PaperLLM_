This paper presents a comprehensive taxonomy of methods for accelerating Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) across three levels: implementation, structure, and algorithm. 

At the **implementation level**, strategies focus on hardware characteristics, such as extending memory, exploring parallelism, and reducing data movement using devices like GPUs, FPGAs, and ASICs. 

The **structure level** emphasizes the reduction of redundancy, specifically in weights and representations. It highlights that many neural network weights exhibit significant redundancy, as shown in studies by Denil et al. and Sainath et al. Techniques such as low-rank approximation and layer decomposition are proposed to mitigate this redundancy. For instance, the paper discusses how weight matrices can be decomposed into products of smaller matrices, which can significantly reduce the number of parameters and improve computational efficiency without substantial accuracy loss. Various tensor decomposition methods are also explored, achieving considerable speedups in inference while maintaining acceptable error rates.

Finally, at the **algorithm level**, the paper suggests improving training and inference speed through various optimization techniques, including gradient descent algorithms, efficient convolution methods, pruning, knowledge distillation, and adaptive learning rates. These methods aim to enhance the overall performance of CNNs while managing computational resources effectively.

In summary, the paper outlines a structured approach to CNN acceleration, advocating for hardware optimization, structural efficiency through redundancy reduction, and algorithmic improvements to achieve faster and more efficient neural network performance.
