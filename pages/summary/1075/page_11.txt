The paper discusses advancements in the implementation of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) through various techniques and hardware accelerators, focusing on optimizing computation efficiency. It highlights the role of Winograd's minimal filtering algorithms, which can reduce convolution computation demands at the cost of memory bandwidth. The integration of Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) techniques is also explored, with studies demonstrating its application in accelerating CNN performance, particularly in large perceptive areas. However, the paper notes the challenge of resource-intensive transformations between time and frequency domains, suggesting that training weights directly in the frequency domain could be a solution. 

The implementation section emphasizes the resurgence of neural networks due to high-performance hardware, transitioning from CPUs to GPUs, FPGAs, and ASICs. GPU clusters are particularly effective for training large neural networks with distributed Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) algorithms. The paper details strategies employed by organizations, such as Baidu, to enhance parallelism and communication among GPU clusters, thereby improving training efficiency. Additionally, it mentions innovations like the nearest content addressable memory block (NNCAM), which significantly speeds up CNN operations on general-purpose GPUs. The overall focus is on how these advancements in algorithms and hardware can optimize the training and performance of deep learning models.
