This section of the paper discusses gradient descent optimization techniques and efficient convolution methods for convolutional neural networks (CNNs). 

Gradient descent is a widely used algorithm for optimizing neural networks, focusing on finding global minima of error functions. The basic update rule involves adjusting parameters in the opposite direction of the gradient of the error function. Distributed gradient descent methods, such as those used by Google for training CNNs, help alleviate computational workloads by utilizing parallelism. This can be achieved through either averaging gradients across replicas or distributing computations across different cores.

The back-propagation algorithm, a variant of gradient descent, is commonly employed in neural networks. Researchers have developed hybrid approaches, integrating back-propagation with algorithms like cuckoo search and ant colony algorithms to enhance speed and stability. For deep CNNs, challenges like vanishing and exploding gradients have led to innovations involving adaptive learning rates, momentum factors, and partial gradients. Techniques such as using exponentially decaying averages for learning rates and Nesterov's method for partial gradients have proven effective in improving convergence rates.

Despite these advancements, gradient descent with back-propagation does not guarantee finding the global minimum due to the non-convex nature of error functions in neural networks. Non-gradient-based methods, like genetic algorithms, offer alternatives but are often limited to simpler datasets and structures.

The paper also summarizes three efficient convolution methods: im2col-based algorithms, Winograd-based methods, and FFT-based methods. The im2col method transforms the input matrix into lower-dimensional vectors, enhancing computational efficiency. The Winograd method leverages minimal filtering algorithms to reduce the number of multiplications required in convolution operations, thereby improving efficiency. Overall, these methods aim to address the computational cost associated with convolution in CNNs.
