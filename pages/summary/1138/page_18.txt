The paper discusses the use of virtual actuators and sensors in fault detection and isolation (FDI) schemes, particularly through the design of Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) fuzzy observer-based controllers. It highlights the theoretical challenges in Lyapunov-based studies of Piecewise Multiaffine (PMA) systems compared to T-S fuzzy systems, pointing out that research in PMA control systems is still in its early stages.

Several key open issues are identified for future research:
1. Developing tractable stability conditions for high-order PMA systems using triangular membership functions (MFs).
2. Creating effective frameworks for designing Look-Up Table (LUT) controllers based on PMA stability results, addressing numerical difficulties and conservativeness in high-order systems.
3. Extending theoretical results of classical PMA systems to more complex system classes, such as time-delay and switching systems.
4. Investigating whether the stability of PMA systems implies stability in the corresponding nonlinear systems, requiring an analysis of approximation errors.
5. Determining the "best" PMA model for a given nonlinear system to enhance modeling accuracy and reduce numerical complexity.

The paper concludes that PMA model-based approaches hold promise for advancing fuzzy control systems, potentially overcoming limitations associated with T-S fuzzy models and offering systematic LUT-based control solutions for real-time applications. The authors emphasize that practical and theoretical advancements are crucial for evolving the discipline of fuzzy control.
