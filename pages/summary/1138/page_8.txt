The paper discusses the stability analysis of Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) fuzzy systems using fuzzy Lyapunov functions (FLFs) and compares them to quadratic Lyapunov functions (QLFs). It identifies that FLFs may not always yield results that are less conservative than QLFs due to the handling of unknown time-derivatives of membership functions (MFs). The authors propose a method to manage these unknown derivatives by leveraging properties of MFs, particularly the convex sum property. 

The paper presents Theorem 2, which establishes conditions for asymptotic stability under specific linear matrix inequalities (LMIs) when upper bounds on the time-derivatives of MFs are known. Unlike the previous theorem, this theorem is limited to local stability analysis, with an emphasis on maximizing the domain of attraction. The use of slack variables in the LMIs allows for a more relaxed stability analysis, indicating that FLFs can provide less conservative results compared to QLFs.

Additionally, the paper highlights that for discrete-time T-S fuzzy systems, the challenges associated with time-derivatives of MFs are less pronounced. It also introduces a line integral form of Lyapunov functions that can be utilized when the premise variables are directly related to state variables, potentially simplifying the analysis further. 

Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of managing time-derivatives of MFs in stability analysis and demonstrate the advantages of FLFs in certain scenarios, while also noting their limitations in local stability contexts.
