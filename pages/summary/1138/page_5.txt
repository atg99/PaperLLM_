This paper provides an overview of fuzzy control research, focusing on the historical development and current status of fuzzy control systems, particularly those based on Mamdani's work. Unlike comprehensive literature reviews, it highlights key breakthroughs in fuzzy control while maintaining simplicity and avoiding complex mathematics. The discussion centers on fuzzy model-based stability analysis, specifically using Lyapunov stability theory for continuous-time systems.

Mamdani fuzzy control (MFC) is introduced as a method that utilizes linguistic variables and fuzzy IF-THEN rules, allowing the integration of expert knowledge into control processes. MFC has proven effective in scenarios where mathematical models are unavailable or when human operators contribute qualitative insights. Its task-oriented nature enables the achievement of multiple objectives through fuzzy reasoning, leading to successful applications in various industrial processes.

However, the paper notes that the design of MFC is predominantly model-free and heuristic, lacking a systematic framework for rigorous stability analysis. The challenge stems from the novelty of fuzzy mathematics. To address this, fuzzy model-based control approaches developed by Sugeno's group provide a structured method for stability analysis and control design of nonlinear dynamical systems.

The paper sets the stage for future research by discussing the perspectives and challenges facing the field of fuzzy control, emphasizing the need for more comprehensive theoretical frameworks and analytical tools.
