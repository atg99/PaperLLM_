The paper discusses the stability analysis of piecewise affine (PMA) systems using a Lyapunov function approach. The Lyapunov function combines quadratic functions near equilibrium points with piecewise functions, leading to potentially less conservative stability results compared to traditional quadratic Lyapunov functions. This is achieved by defining the Lyapunov function in a manner that incorporates free parameters represented in a symmetric matrix, allowing for a linear matrix inequality (LMI)-based framework.

Theorem 5 establishes conditions under which a PMA system is asymptotically stable if specific LMI constraints are satisfied involving symmetric matrices with nonnegative entries. The conditions ensure that the Lyapunov function decreases along the system's trajectories, confirming its validity as a Lyapunov function.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of leveraging the parametric expressions of the system's state and input, which can enhance the theoretical understanding and reduce conservatism in stability analysis. This contrasts with Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) fuzzy systems, where membership functions are often treated as uncertainties.

Furthermore, it introduces a Lyapunov-based control design for PMA systems, where a linear feedback gain can stabilize the system around an equilibrium point, leading to a more structured approach for control design in these systems.
