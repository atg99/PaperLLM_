This paper discusses various types of fuzzy systems, focusing on Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) fuzzy systems, Mamdani fuzzy systems, and piecewise multiaffine (PMA) systems. T-S fuzzy systems utilize functional consequents, where the output is represented as a linear function of the inputs. In contrast, Mamdani systems employ linguistic variables in both the antecedents and consequents, making them more interpretable. PMA systems, characterized by singleton outputs, are a simplified version of both T-S and Mamdani systems, allowing for a piecewise linear representation of input-output relationships.

The paper highlights that while all three types of fuzzy systems can approximate nonlinear functions, T-S fuzzy modeling notably reduces the number of fuzzy rules needed for high-dimensional systems. The initial criticism of fuzzy control, primarily due to the lack of stability analysis, led to the introduction of T-S systems, which enable the application of conventional stability analysis techniques. However, T-S systems face limitations in providing non-conservative stability analyses due to the uncertainties introduced by nonlinear membership functions.

To address these challenges, PMA systems were introduced as fully parametric models, allowing for necessary and sufficient stability conditions akin to linear systems. The paper posits that theoretical frameworks developed for T-S and PMA systems may be more general than those for traditional linear systems, suggesting their potential for broader application in control theory.
