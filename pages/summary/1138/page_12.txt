The paper discusses PMA (Parametric Model Approximation) systems and their application to nonlinear dynamics. It begins by considering an autonomous nonlinear system represented by the equation _x = f(x). The authors emphasize the challenges associated with Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) fuzzy model-based approaches, particularly the lack of a systematic method to select the optimal parameterization that ensures stability and meets performance criteria. This non-uniqueness in T-S fuzzy representation remains an unresolved issue.

In contrast, PMA modeling directly addresses the form of the system without the same conservatism seen in T-S approaches, though it introduces approximation errors that complicate stability analysis. The authors define the state space of the system and partition it into regions, introducing multi-index sets corresponding to the vertices and regions of the partition.

Fuzzy rules are established for these hyper-rectangle regions, linking the state variables to the system's behavior. The triangular membership functions (MFs) are defined, which help in determining the system's dynamics through a weighted average of fuzzy rules. The framework allows the derivation of expressions for the state variables based on the triangular MFs, illustrating how the PMA modeling approach can be leveraged to analyze nonlinear systems effectively.
