The paper discusses the application of a PDC (Proportional-Derivative Control) law for T-S (Takagi-Sugeno) fuzzy systems and explores the associated stability analysis using a quadratic Lyapunov function. The closed-loop dynamics of the system are derived, leading to a condition for stability expressed in terms of a matrix inequality. Specifically, it highlights the challenge of coupling between the Lyapunov matrix and feedback gains, which introduces bilinear matrix inequalities (BMIs). The paper proposes a congruence transformation to simplify the stability condition into a linear matrix inequality (LMI) that ensures the asymptotic stability of the T-S fuzzy system.

The discussion includes remarks on the implications of using the same membership functions (MFs) for both PDC control and T-S fuzzy systems, resulting in complex convex sums that increase computational demands. It contrasts the difficulties of control design with stability analysis, noting that while fuzzy Lyapunov functions (FLFs) facilitate synthesis, they complicate the validation of designed controllers due to their dependence on system dynamics.

Moreover, the paper outlines various control strategies in the T-S fuzzy context, including non-PDC control and fuzzy observer-based control, emphasizing the challenges that arise when premise variables are not measurable. The section concludes with a transition to discuss piecewise multiaffine (PMA) systems, indicating a broader application of the concepts presented in the context of nonlinear systems.
