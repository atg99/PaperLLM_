In summary, the paper discusses the advancements and future challenges in fuzzy control systems, particularly focusing on Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) fuzzy systems and singleton-type fuzzy systems. It highlights the importance of reducing conservativeness in stability analysis and control design, suggesting that interval type-2 T-S fuzzy models could enhance methodologies by accommodating uncertain membership grades. The paper emphasizes the need for further research on stability analysis and control design, particularly in developing T-S fuzzy controllers that do not share membership functions with their models. Additionally, it addresses the significance of fault detection and control reconfiguration in maintaining robust system operations, proposing integrated design approaches for effective fault management. Overall, the paper presents a forward-looking perspective on the evolution and potential of fuzzy control systems, underscoring the need for innovation in methodologies to tackle existing challenges.
