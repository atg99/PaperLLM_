The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of a new algorithm called Implicit Diffusion Q-learning (IDQL), which integrates diffusion models into Q-learning for continuous action spaces. It highlights the challenges faced when applying denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) to continuous data, particularly issues with outlier generation and distribution accuracy. The authors demonstrate that a simple multi-layer perceptron (MLP) architecture struggles to capture data distributions, leading to many out-of-distribution samples. To address this, they propose using a residual network with layer normalization (LN_Resnet), which shows improved sample quality and fewer outliers.

The paper also details experimental evaluations comparing IDQL to existing methods on D4RL benchmarks, focusing on both unrestricted and restricted hyperparameter tuning scenarios. The results indicate that IDQL is competitive with leading offline reinforcement learning methods in locomotion tasks and outperforms them in antmaze tasks. The performance degradation in the one hyperparameter tuning scenario is minimal for IDQL, showcasing its robustness. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of architecture choices, batch size, and capacity in effectively modeling continuous action spaces using diffusion techniques.
