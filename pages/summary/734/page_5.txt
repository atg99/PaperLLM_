The paper presents a comparison of different loss functions for generalized Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) using a simple bandit model characterized by a continuous action space divided into clusters of varying rewards. The study investigates the performance of various objectives, including expectiles, quantiles, and exponential functions, revealing how each influences the implicit actor's behavior and the resulting policy.

Expectiles focus on the conditional mean of a distribution, where a parameter influences the deviation from the behavior policy. As the parameter increases, the expectile actor captures a broader range of outcomes. In contrast, quantiles measure the top percentage of the behavior policy's performance, balancing median behavior with Q-learning. An increase in the quantile parameter also leads to greater extrapolation from the behavior policy, with the implicit policy clustering around the quantile.

The exponential loss function, characterized by a linex function, applies a KL-divergence constraint and aims to optimize the critic while matching the behavior policy. Although this method appears to yield the most optimal policy, it is noted to be unstable in practice.

Overall, the findings suggest that the choice of loss function significantly affects the implicit policy derived from IQL, with the expectile, quantile, and exponential functions each displaying unique characteristics and behaviors. The paper emphasizes the need for careful selection of hyperparameters that control the extent of deviation from the behavior policy to achieve stable and effective learning outcomes.
