The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the IDQL algorithm in comparison to existing state-of-the-art offline reinforcement learning methods across various datasets, including locomotion-v2 and antmaze. The results, summarized in Tables 1 and 2, demonstrate that IDQL consistently outperforms other methods, particularly excelling in the challenging antmaze domain where it surpasses IQL by a significant margin of 70 points. The training time for IDQL remains efficient, comparable to IQL, and is notably faster than other diffusion methods, indicating its computational advantages.

Additionally, the paper explores online fine-tuning strategies for policies post-offline training, showing that IDQL yields substantial improvements in performance, particularly in the antmaze-large environments. It competes effectively with current leading fine-tuning methods such as RLPD and Cal-QL, showcasing stronger pre-training results.

The authors also conduct ablation studies on different objectives used in IQL, revealing that while expectiles and quantiles yield good results, the exponential loss is less stable and requires more tuning. Overall, the findings suggest that IDQL not only matches but often exceeds the performance of prior methods with limited hyperparameter tuning, highlighting its potential as a robust approach in offline reinforcement learning.
