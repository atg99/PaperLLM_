The paper presents an evaluation of various offline reinforcement learning (RL) methods, focusing on the performance of the proposed method, IDQL, compared to state-of-the-art algorithms. The results are summarized in two key tables. Table 4 shows the performance across different datasets, where IDQL generally performs on par or better than other methods, particularly excelling in the antmaze tasks. Notably, it outperforms IQL and remains competitive with DQL and other leading algorithms. The training times for each method are also provided, highlighting IDQL's efficiency.

Table 5 compares IDQL with IQL and Diffuser in the Maze2D environment. While IDQL outperforms IQL, it falls short of Diffuser, suggesting that model-based planning may offer advantages in this context.

The paper also discusses the importance of hyperparameter tuning, specifically the number of diffusion steps (T) and the beta schedule, which significantly impact performance. It was found that T needs to be larger for simpler datasets compared to the D4RL datasets, with optimal values identified for each. The choice of beta schedule also influences performance, with the variance-preserving schedule yielding the best results for D4RL.

Overall, the findings indicate that IDQL is a strong contender in the landscape of offline RL methods, demonstrating robust performance across various tasks while providing insights into the factors affecting its effectiveness.
