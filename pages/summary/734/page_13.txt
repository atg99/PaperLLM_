The paper discusses the framework of Reinforcement Learning (RL) within the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), characterized by a tuple consisting of state space, action space, initial state distribution, transition dynamics, reward function, and discount factor. The primary objective in RL is to derive a policy that maximizes the expected discounted sum of rewards, represented mathematically through the optimization of a policy that influences the trajectory distribution.

A key method in off-policy RL is Q-learning, which aims to fit a Q-function that estimates the expected returns from a given state and action, subsequently guiding the policy towards optimality by selecting actions that maximize the Q-function in future states.

The paper further presents theoretical proofs, including the proof of Theorem 4.1, which involves demonstrating the convexity of the objective function and establishing the conditions under which the implicit policy is defined. This leads to the conclusion that a particular value function is a solution to the optimization problem.

Additionally, the paper explores the derivation of the optimal solution for an exponential objective, simplifying the objective function by excluding irrelevant terms and expanding the expectation to facilitate the minimization process.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive examination of RL formulations, theoretical underpinnings, and proofs related to policy optimization and value function convergence.
