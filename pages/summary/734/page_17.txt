The paper discusses the impact of different scheduling and batch size on the performance of models in D4RL tasks. Figure 5 illustrates that a variance-preserving schedule with low temperature (T) tends to yield the best results, emphasizing the importance of the signal-to-noise ratio during the noising process. It highlights that the first noise step is particularly crucial for effective modeling of the action space.

In Section F.4, the significance of batch size is analyzed through ablation studies on the DDPM loss across D4RL benchmarks, as shown in Figure 6. The findings indicate that while batch size has minimal effect on locomotion tasks, a larger batch size enhances performance in antmaze tasks. However, batch size is deemed less critical than the model's architecture or capacity.

Section F.5 presents an ablation study on a weighted DDPM approach using Advantage Weighted Regression (AWR). Two sampling methods are compared: one with a single sample and another with multiple samples (N=64) to select the maximum according to the critic. Results in Figure 7 reveal that the single-sample method generally performs worse than the IDQL baseline, and the multi-sample approach only matches its performance. Consequently, AWR is found to be unnecessary for strong performance, introducing an extra training parameter without clear benefits.
