The paper introduces Implicit Diffusion Q-learning (IDQL), an advancement over existing methods like IQL (Implicit Q-Learning). IDQL addresses the limitations of unimodal Gaussian policies used in IQL, which fail to accurately capture the implicit actor's complex, potentially multimodal nature. This is evidenced through a comparison of the actor's performance in a 2D bandit scenario, where the diffusion extraction method demonstrates superior accuracy in approximating the implicit policy compared to AWR-based methods.

The authors argue that the implicit actor derived from critic learning can be effectively expressed as a reweighting of the behavior policy, facilitating policy extraction through a diffusion model. This method allows for the accurate representation of multimodal distributions, enhancing the policy extraction process. Key contributions of IDQL include the integration of a diffusion-based policy extraction algorithm and the generalization of IQL to an actor-critic framework that maintains a decoupled critic and actor training approach.

IDQL has shown improved performance on D4RL offline RL benchmarks, outperforming both IQL and other diffusion-based methods while requiring minimal hyperparameter tuning across various domains. The paper also situates IDQL within the broader context of offline RL, discussing related methods that seek to mitigate overestimation of out-of-distribution actions and enhance policy robustness. Overall, IDQL represents a significant step forward in the development of effective offline reinforcement learning strategies.
