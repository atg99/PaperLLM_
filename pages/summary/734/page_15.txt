The paper discusses the evaluation of various off-policy reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, specifically focusing on one hyperparameter settings for methods like IQL, CQL, and DiffusionQL. Each method was re-implemented and tested by varying a single main hyperparameter while keeping others constant. The goal was to identify the best-performing hyperparameter for different tasks, such as locomotion and antmaze, with the findings indicating that IQL methods perform well with minimal tuning compared to others, which often require extensive adjustments for optimal performance across domains.

The fine-tuning process involved pretraining the critic and diffusion behavior cloning actor for specified steps, followed by online fine-tuning with gradient updates. It was noted that fine-tuning did not yield improvements for adroit tasks, likely due to the need for significant exploration, which behavior regularization could hinder.

The paper also includes detailed hyperparameter settings for the critic and actor networks, highlighting their architecture and learning rates. Additionally, it compares the proposed method against other recent off-policy RL algorithms, establishing a framework for fair comparisons among different approaches in the field. The authors emphasize the importance of a standardized protocol for evaluating and presenting results to ensure comparability and reliability of findings in off-policy RL research.
