The paper presents a novel approach called Implicit Diffusion Q-learning (IDQL), which enhances the framework of implicit Q-learning (IQL) by reinterpreting it as an actor-critic method. IQL addresses the challenge of out-of-distribution actions in offline reinforcement learning (RL) by training a Q-function using a modified Bellman backup that relies solely on actions from a static dataset. However, the original IQL does not clarify which policy aligns with the values represented by the Q-function, complicating the understanding of its performance limitations.

The authors propose a generalization of the critic's objective that connects it to a behavior-regularized implicit actor. This connection allows the induced actor to balance the maximization of rewards and divergence from the behavior policy, with the choice of loss function influencing this balance. Notably, the proposed actor can exhibit complex, multimodal characteristics, contrasting with previous methods that relied on a conditional Gaussian fit through advantage weighted regression (AWR).

IDQL utilizes samples from a diffusion-parameterized behavior policy and weights derived from the critic to effectively sample the intended policy. This approach retains the implementation simplicity of IQL while significantly improving its performance and demonstrating robustness to hyperparameter choices. The authors claim that IDQL outperforms existing offline RL methods, addressing key challenges in the field. The code for the proposed method is made available for public access.
