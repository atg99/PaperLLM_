The paper discusses various tensor decomposition methods applied to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to improve computational efficiency while examining their impact on classification accuracy. Jaderberg et al. introduced a tensor decomposition scheme that increased processing speed by 4.5 times but resulted in a 1% decrease in text recognition accuracy. The low-rank decomposition technique analyzed layers individually, fixing parameters of completed layers before fine-tuning subsequent layers based on reconstruction error.

Lebedev et al. proposed a canonical polyadic (CP) decomposition of the kernel tensor, utilizing nonlinear least squares for computation. Tai et al. introduced a new algorithm for training low-rank constrained CNNs from scratch, employing batch normalization (BN) to enhance internal activation. Both CP and BN schemes allow for training CNNs from the beginning, although they share similarities, including challenges in determining the optimal low-rank decomposition.

The paper includes a comparison of various models on the ILSVPRC-2012 dataset, highlighting their top-5 accuracy, speed-up, and compression rates. For instance, the BN low-rank version of AlexNet achieved a top-5 accuracy of 80.56% with a speed-up of 1.09 and a compression rate of 4.94, while the CP low-rank version had a lower accuracy of 79.66% but a higher speed-up of 1.82 and a compression rate of 5.00. Similar results were observed for VGG16 and GoogleNet, demonstrating trade-offs between accuracy and computational efficiency across the different methods.
