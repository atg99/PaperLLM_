The paper discusses various methods for channel pruning in deep neural networks (DNNs), highlighting their efficiency and impact on performance. Kuang et al. introduced a task-dependent loss function to assess channel importance, leading Li et al. to develop a layer-wise refined pruning method that accelerates inference. Sawant et al. proposed an optimal-score-based filter pruning (OSFP) technique that prunes redundant filters to enhance image segmentation and learning speed. Other methods include Chu et al.'s three-stage model-compression process, which incorporates dynamic sparse training and group channel pruning, and Chang et al.'s automatic channel pruning utilizing hierarchical clustering and particle-swarm optimization.

Liu et al. presented a network slimming approach that identifies and prunes unimportant channels during training using L1 regularization. Yang et al. proposed an energy-aware pruning algorithm that minimizes output feature map errors, enhancing effectiveness. Fan et al. introduced hierarchical channel pruning to reduce computational resources while maintaining model accuracy. Chen et al.'s only-train-once (OTO) framework simplifies the pruning process significantly by requiring only one training phase. Additionally, Chung et al. focused on pruning the first layer of pre-trained CNNs to facilitate subsequent compression, while Chen et al. addressed neuron manipulation to optimize model performance after pruning. The paper concludes that advancements in structured pruning methods have streamlined the pruning and fine-tuning processes.
