The paper discusses various methods of knowledge distillation in deep neural networks (DNNs), focusing on feature maps from teacher and student networks. It highlights the transformation functions that adjust these feature maps to the same shape and introduces a similarity function, LF, for matching them. 

The authors categorize knowledge into response-based, feature-based, and relation-based methods. The relation-based approach, primarily inspired by Yim et al., utilizes the flow of the solution process (FSP) represented by the Gram matrix, which captures the relationships between different layers' feature maps. This approach relies on correlations as prior knowledge, with additional methods like singular value decomposition (SVD) and graph-based frameworks enhancing knowledge extraction from multiple teachers.

The paper also delves into lightweight model design strategies aimed at reducing parameters and computational complexity. It reviews various techniques, such as SqueezeNet's use of 1x1 convolution kernels to replace larger kernels, thereby decreasing parameter count. It also discusses MobileNet's depth-wise and point-wise convolution, which optimizes operations, and ShuffleNet's strategy of shuffling input groups to improve model learning capabilities. These advancements in model design contribute to more efficient DNNs without compromising performance.
