Tan et al. introduced a hybrid scaling method that optimizes multiple dimensions of a network simultaneously, improving accuracy without tedious manual tuning. Han et al. developed the Ghost module, which efficiently extracts features with fewer parameters, leading to the creation of GhostNet, known for its effective compression and accuracy retention. Ma et al. proposed WeightNet, a dynamic generative network that adapts convolutional weights based on sample features, balancing accuracy and speed. Li et al.'s MicroNet utilized micro-factorized convolution and dynamic shift-max to enhance connectivity and non-linearity despite reduced depth. Radosavovic et al. presented RegNet, merging advantages of manual design and neural architecture search (NAS). 

Self-supervised representation learning has gained traction, yet smaller model sizes typically result in decreased performance. To address this, Gao et al. introduced distillation contrast learning (DisCo), which aligns the knowledge transfer between lightweight student models and teacher models.

The discussion highlights the rapid advancements in DNNs, driven by increasing computational power, and emphasizes the importance of lightweight architectures for deployment on edge devices. Key challenges include effective model pruning to avoid unstructured risks, the complexity and accuracy loss associated with parameter quantization, and the implementation challenges of low-rank decomposition. Overall, the survey summarizes recent achievements and outlines the ongoing challenges and future directions in DNN research.
