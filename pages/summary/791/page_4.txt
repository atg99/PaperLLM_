2.2. Unstructured Pruning
Unstructured pruning, in contrast, focuses on individual weights rather than entire channels. This method allows for fine-grained removal of less significant weights, which can lead to a more compact model. Techniques such as weight thresholding are commonly employed, where weights below a certain threshold are pruned. However, unstructured pruning may result in irregular sparsity patterns that can complicate the implementation on hardware, as it relies on specialized libraries for sparse matrix operations.

3. Parameter Quantization
Parameter quantization is another critical aspect of model compression, aimed at reducing the precision of weights and activations to decrease memory usage and improve computational efficiency. This section discusses three primary techniques: post-training quantization, quantization-aware training, and their implications for model performance.

4. Low-Rank Decomposition
Low-rank decomposition techniques are employed to approximate weight matrices with lower-rank representations, effectively reducing the number of parameters while preserving essential information. This section outlines various methods for achieving low-rank decomposition.

5. Knowledge Distillation
Knowledge distillation involves transferring knowledge from a larger, more complex model (teacher) to a smaller, more efficient model (student). This section explores different approaches to knowledge distillation, including response-based, feature-based, and relation-based methods.

6. Lightweight Model Design
Strategies for designing lightweight models are discussed, emphasizing the balance between model efficiency and performance. Recent advancements in this area are highlighted, showcasing innovative architectures that achieve high accuracy with reduced resource consumption.
