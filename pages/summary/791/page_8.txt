The paper discusses two primary techniques for optimizing neural network models: Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) and Post-Training Quantization (PTQ), along with the concept of Low-Rank Decomposition.

QAT aims to address the challenges posed by quantization, which can lead to deviations in model parameters and convergence issues. It quantifies parameters during both forward and backward propagation, emphasizing the importance of performing weight updates in floating-point precision to minimize errors from low-precision gradients.

PTQ offers a more straightforward alternative to QAT, allowing for quantization with minimal or no fine-tuning. While it is cost-effective and can work with limited data, PTQ often results in lower accuracy, particularly with low-precision quantization. Researchers have developed various methods to improve PTQ's effectiveness, such as bias correction, balancing weight ranges, and adaptive rounding techniques like AdaRound and AdaQuant. Despite its speed and efficiency, PTQ generally does not achieve the accuracy levels of QAT.

Low-Rank Decomposition involves approximating the weight matrix in neural networks with a low-rank matrix, particularly beneficial for fully connected layers. While this approach can compress models significantly, it doesn’t notably enhance computational speed since most operations occur in convolution layers. The paper also highlights the use of low-rank filters and tensor products to facilitate faster convolutions and discusses various clustering schemes aimed at improving efficiency in deep neural networks.

In summary, while QAT and PTQ both serve to optimize neural network performance, they each have distinct advantages and limitations regarding accuracy, efficiency, and implementation complexity. Low-Rank Decomposition provides an additional method for model compression, emphasizing the need for balance between speed and model accuracy in neural network design.
