The paper discusses various lightweight model architectures and their methods, focusing on optimizing deep neural networks (DNNs) for efficiency. 

Key models include:

- **SqueezeNet**: Utilizes 1x1 convolutions to replace larger 3x3 convolutions, reduces input channels, and incorporates downsampling.
- **SqueezeNext**: Features compressed channels, low-rank separable convolutions, and reduced input channels in fully connected layers.
- **MobileNetV1 and V2**: Employ depthwise separable convolutions and linear bottleneck structures, with hyperparameters for optimization.
- **ShufﬂeNet series**: Implements group convolutions and channel shuffling to enhance performance while maintaining efficiency.

Innovations in lightweight models include:

- Gao et al.'s improvement of self-supervised learning effectiveness.
- Tan et al.'s MnasNet, which integrates time consumption into neural architecture search through multi-objective optimization, balancing accuracy and efficiency.
- Huang et al.'s learnable group convolution, which enhances pruning accuracy.
- Mehta et al.'s ESPNet for semantic segmentation, featuring an efficient ESP module.
- Gao et al.'s channel-wise and depth-wise separable convolution, optimizing parameter usage.

The interleaved group convolution (IGC) series further refines group convolution, minimizing parameters while enhancing information flow. The FBNet series, based on neural architecture search, explores resource constraints and accuracy prediction to improve network structure.

The paper concludes with a focus on optimizing DNN performance through increased network width, depth, and input image resolution.
