The paper discusses various methods for exploiting low rankings in fully connected (FC) layers of deep neural networks (DNNs) to achieve model compression. It highlights techniques such as low-rank methods and matrix factorization, which help reduce the number of dynamic parameters in deep models. Notably, Denil et al. and Sainath et al. demonstrated the effectiveness of low-rank decompositions in DNNs for tasks like acoustic modeling and compact multi-task models. However, the implementation of these methods is challenging due to the complexity of decomposition operations and the limitation of layer-by-layer approaches that prevent global parameter compression.

The paper also addresses the representation of neural network filters as tensors, emphasizing the significance of the convolution kernel dimensions in model compression. While low-rank decomposition methods enhance compression and speed, recent trends towards 1x1 convolutions complicate their application. Additionally, the high computational cost of matrix decomposition and the need for extensive retraining present further hurdles.

To mitigate these challenges, Jaderberg et al. proposed a two-step method that combines tensor decomposition with discriminative fine-tuning to accelerate convolution layers in large networks. 

Furthermore, the concept of knowledge distillation is introduced, which involves a teacher-student architecture where a complex pre-trained network (teacher) imparts knowledge to a simpler, smaller network (student). This method allows for the effective training of smaller models without significant loss of accuracy, making it particularly relevant for deploying deep models on resource-constrained mobile devices.
