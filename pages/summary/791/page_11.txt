The paper discusses a novel knowledge-distilling strategy aimed at enhancing model performance by enabling a student model to imitate a larger teacher model. The core concept revolves around transferring knowledge from the teacher to the student, with emphasis on the types of knowledge, distillation strategies, and the architectures used in the teacher-student relationship. Key elements such as activations, neurons, and features from the teacher model's middle layers serve as guidance during the student's learning process.

Three methods of knowledge distillation are highlighted: response-based, feature-based, and instance-relation-based. 

1. **Response-Based Knowledge**: This method focuses on mimicking the final output of the teacher model. It utilizes soft targets, which are the probabilities of different classes generated by the Softmax function. The temperature parameter T adjusts the importance of these soft targets.

2. **Feature-Based Knowledge**: This approach leverages the feature maps from both the final and middle layers of the teacher model to supervise the training of the student model. It expands on response-based knowledge by introducing a feature-based knowledge-distillation loss to facilitate learning.

The paper provides a detailed exploration of these methods, emphasizing their effectiveness in model compression and performance enhancement.
