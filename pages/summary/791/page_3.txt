The paper presents a summary of various approaches for model compression, highlighting their core ideas, operational objects, architecture, advantages, and disadvantages. 

1. **Model Pruning**: This method evaluates parameter importance to remove unimportant parameters, utilizing structured and unstructured pruning. Structured pruning aids hardware acceleration but may reduce accuracy, while unstructured pruning can lead to irregular architectures that are challenging to accelerate.

2. **Parameter Quantization**: This approach converts floating-point calculations to low-bitrate integer calculations, employing post-training quantization and quantization-aware training. It effectively reduces storage space, speeds up operations, and lowers energy consumption but requires extensive training and fine-tuning, making it inflexible.

3. **Low-Rank Decomposition**: It decomposes original tensors into low-rank tensors, with methods like binary and multivariate decomposition. This technique provides good compression and acceleration for larger convolution kernels and smaller networks but struggles with simplified convolution kernels and may hinder global parameter compression.

4. **Knowledge Distillation**: Involves using a complex teacher network to guide a simpler student network. This method compresses large models into smaller ones suitable for resource-limited devices but necessitates multiple training sessions, leading to increased training time.

5. **Lightweight Model Design**: Focuses on creating compact and efficient network architectures for mobile deployment. It results in simpler networks with shorter training times and good performance but may have poor generalization and compatibility with other compression methods.

Overall, each approach offers distinct advantages for model compression while also presenting specific challenges that need to be addressed.
