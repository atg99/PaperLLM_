The paper discusses the principles of autonomous path planning using an artificial potential field approach. It defines key components such as the repulsive field constant (η), the distance from the robot's current position (q) to the nearest obstacle (D(q)), and a threshold (Q*) that determines when a repulsive force is activated. 

The overall potential force acting on a mobile robot is represented by a combination of attractive and repulsive forces, expressed mathematically as U(q) = U_att(q) + U_rep(q). The force vector at any point is determined by the gradient of the potential function, indicated as ∇U(q).

The attractive force (F_att) is derived from the negative gradient of the attractive potential, while the repulsive force (F_rep) is calculated based on the distance to obstacles, applying the defined conditions depending on whether D(q) is less than or greater than Q*. The total force acting on the robot is the sum of these two forces, expressed as F(q) = F_att(q) + F_rep(q). 

This framework allows for effective navigation in environments with obstacles, demonstrating the utility of artificial potential fields in robotics.
