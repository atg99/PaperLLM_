The paper presents a comparison of four algorithms—DQN, Dueling DQN, M-DQN, and DM-DQN—designed for robotic path planning in both static and dynamic environments. The focus is on the performance of these algorithms in terms of the number of moves, success rates, and convergence times when navigating from position 1 to multiple target positions and back, as illustrated in the provided figures and tables.

Key findings indicate that the DM-DQN algorithm outperforms the others by achieving a lower average number of moves and higher success rates. Specifically, DM-DQN shows an 18.3% improvement over DQN, 3.3% over Dueling DQN, and 17.2% over M-DQN in success rates. In terms of convergence speed, DM-DQN also excels, taking only 112 minutes to reach a reward of 8000, compared to 294 minutes for DQN, 148 minutes for Dueling DQN, and 127 minutes for M-DQN.

Furthermore, the paper highlights the effectiveness of a novel reward function proposed for path planning. This new function leads to smoother paths for the robot and minimizes the risk of collisions by keeping the planned routes farther from obstacles, as shown in the comparative analysis of the reward functions.

Overall, the results emphasize the superiority of the DM-DQN algorithm in robotic navigation tasks, both in static and dynamic environments, thereby supporting its potential application in real-world scenarios.
