The paper discusses a novel approach to autonomous path planning for mobile robots utilizing the DM-DQN (Deep Mind Deep Q-Network) algorithm. It addresses the identifiability problem in reinforcement learning by centralizing the advantage function, which helps in determining the Q-value updates based on various parameters and actions in a given state.

The proposed algorithm begins by modeling the path planning problem as a Markov decision process. The robot gathers environmental data through sensors to assess the locations of obstacles and targets, creating a reward function based on the artificial potential field method. This method simulates the robot's movement as influenced by virtual forces, where gravitational forces attract the robot to the target and repulsive forces prevent collisions with obstacles.

The reward function is derived from the potential functions, with the gravitational potential increasing as the robot approaches the target and the repulsive potential rising as it nears obstacles. The paper outlines how the robot selects actions based on a buffer of previously experienced actions and their associated rewards, processes these through a neural network, and iteratively updates its values to minimize the loss function.

The learning continues until the robot reaches a convergence state, at which point it evaluates the success of its path planning and resets the environment if necessary. The overall goal is to enable the mobile robot to navigate effectively and safely towards its target while avoiding obstacles.
