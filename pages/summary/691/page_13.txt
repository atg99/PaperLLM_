The paper presents a novel approach to path planning for mobile robots in both dynamic and static environments by establishing a continuous simulation framework. It enhances the existing M-DQN (Multi-Dimensional Deep Q-Network) by improving its network structure, which accelerates convergence through the separation of value and advantage functions. This decoupling allows for more efficient action selection and evaluation.

A key innovation is the development of a reward function based on an artificial potential field, designed to maintain an optimal distance between the robot, the target point, and any obstacles. The results indicate that the proposed DM-DQN (Dynamic M-DQN) outperforms traditional methods like Dueling DQN and DQN in terms of convergence speed and decision-making efficiency, resulting in smoother paths that effectively avoid obstacles.

The study also acknowledges the limitations of reinforcement learning in long-distance path planning and suggests future research could explore integrating traditional algorithms with reinforcement learning to improve performance in such scenarios.
