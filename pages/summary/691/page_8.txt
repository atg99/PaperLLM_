The paper presents an experimental study on various Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithms, specifically the standard DQN, Dueling DQN, M-DQN, and DM-DQN, focusing on their obstacle avoidance strategies. The experiments were conducted on a Windows 10.1 platform using TensorFlow 1.13.1 and CUDA 10.0, with an Intel i7-8550U processor. The simulation utilized the Gazebo platform, which provides a virtual environment for robotic simulations, allowing for the modeling of robots and the implementation of path planning tasks without prior knowledge of the environment.

The study involved training the algorithms over 320 rounds, analyzing the effectiveness of each algorithm's obstacle avoidance strategy, and examining the correlation between the success rate of obstacle avoidance and the number of training rounds. Key training parameters included a learning rate of 0.01, a discount factor of 0.9, pre-training steps of 800, a mini-batch size of 128, and a replay memory size of 20,000.

The research leveraged the Robot Operating System (ROS) to manage hardware and software resources effectively, allowing for modular communication through a distributed framework. The ROS nodes communicated with the Gazebo simulator, facilitating the acquisition of sensory information and enabling the DQN algorithms to learn and adapt their strategies based on feedback from the environment. Overall, the study aims to enhance the understanding of DQN algorithms in robotic path planning and obstacle avoidance.
