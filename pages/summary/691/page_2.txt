The paper discusses advancements in deep reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the proposed DM-DQN algorithm, which enhances the existing M-DQN framework. Mnih et al. introduced DQN, blending deep neural networks with Q-learning, and subsequent works have built on this foundation to improve learning efficiency and convergence rates. Key contributions of the paper include:

1. **Simulation Environment**: A virtual simulation environment was created using the Gazebo platform, which closely mirrors real-world conditions compared to traditional raster maps, thereby improving the applicability of learned strategies to real robotic problems.

2. **Network Structure Improvement**: The M-DQN's network is restructured into a value function and an advantage function. This decoupling allows for independent evaluations of actions, enhancing convergence speed and generalization by reducing the dependency of state evaluations on action values.

3. **Obstacle Avoidance**: The DM-DQN incorporates an artificial potential field to design a reward function that balances obstacle avoidance with target approach, enabling more effective path planning around obstacles.

The paper is organized into several sections: the "Theoretical Background" discusses the mobile robot model, the "Proposed Algorithm" details the DM-DQN, "Materials and Methods" describes the simulation environment and experimental comparisons, and "Experiments and Results" presents the findings. Overall, the DM-DQN demonstrates improved performance in mobile robot path planning in both static and dynamic environments compared to existing algorithms.
