The paper discusses the use of the Gazebo simulation environment, which is built on the ROS platform, to conduct experiments on deep reinforcement learning (DRL) algorithms for robot path planning. Gazebo offers a range of features, including the ability to edit environments easily, simulate various sensors, and provide realistic 3D visualizations.

In the experiments, the authors validate the performance of four DRL algorithms—DQN, Dueling DQN, M-DQN, and DM-DQN—within a static environment. They generated seven target points and used a mobile robot to navigate around static obstacles. The algorithms were trained over 320 iterations, and their convergence rates were analyzed through cumulative and average reward metrics.

Results indicate that all algorithms initially struggled due to limited exploration experience, reflected in low rewards during the first 100 rounds. However, Dueling DQN, M-DQN, and DM-DQN showed faster convergence compared to DQN after this initial phase. The Dueling DQN benefits from updating multiple Q-values simultaneously, while M-DQN incorporates maximum entropy for increased exploration. DM-DQN further enhances learning by decoupling action selection and evaluation, leading to more effective use of early exploration experiences.

Ultimately, DM-DQN achieved the highest reward value, converging at 7000, compared to 4000 for Dueling DQN and M-DQN, and 2000 for DQN. This suggests that DM-DQN is more effective in reaching target points within the given environment.
