The paper presents a comparison of various algorithms for path planning in both static and dynamic environments. It evaluates four algorithms: DQN, Dueling DQN, M-DQN, and DM-DQN, focusing on their performance metrics such as average moving steps, success rate, and convergence time.

In static environments, the results indicate that DM-DQN outperforms the other algorithms, achieving the lowest average moving steps (20.81), the highest success rate (67.6), and the quickest convergence time (112). This demonstrates DM-DQN's efficiency and effectiveness in navigating obstacles.

When applied to dynamic environments, where obstacles move randomly, the performance of all algorithms decreases. However, DM-DQN maintains its advantage, starting to converge earlier (around round 120) compared to the others, which only begin to show improvement around round 150. This suggests that DM-DQN possesses superior generalization capabilities. The results highlight the challenges posed by dynamic obstacles but confirm DM-DQN's robustness in adapting to changing conditions.

Overall, the findings emphasize the potential of DM-DQN for effective path planning in both static and dynamic scenarios, showcasing its ability to handle increased complexity in the environment.
