The paper presents a comparative analysis of four reinforcement learning algorithms for robotic path planning in both dynamic and static environments. The algorithms evaluated include DQN, Dueling DQN, M-DQN, and DM-DQN. Key findings indicate that DM-DQN achieved a 27.3% improvement in success rate over DQN, a 12.6% increase over Dueling DQN, and a 9.3% improvement compared to M-DQN. Additionally, DM-DQN demonstrated the fastest convergence time, requiring only 131 minutes to reach an 8000 reward, outperforming the other algorithms.

The study also examines the effectiveness of different reward functions in path planning. It contrasts a basic reward function that solely considers the distance to the target with a more sophisticated function proposed in the paper that accounts for both the target distance and proximity to dynamic obstacles. The results indicate that the proposed reward function significantly enhances the robot's ability to navigate around dynamic obstacles, thereby improving path planning efficiency.
