The paper presents a novel approach to robot path planning using a Dueling Munchausen deep Q network (DM-DQN) aimed at achieving collision-free navigation in complex environments. The authors build upon the Munchausen deep Q-learning network (M-DQN), which enhances exploration through a scaled log-policy reward mechanism. However, M-DQN faces challenges with slow convergence.

To improve convergence speed and generalization performance, the authors propose DM-DQN, which decouples action selection from action evaluation by separating the network structure into value and advantage functions. Additionally, to prevent the robot from navigating too close to obstacles, they introduce an artificial potential field to define a reward function that encourages safer trajectories.

Simulation results demonstrate that DM-DQN outperforms DQN, Dueling DQN, and M-DQN in both static and dynamic environments, effectively learning more efficiently and converging faster while successfully planning collision-free paths away from obstacles. The integration of deep reinforcement learning techniques allows the DM-DQN to adapt to the complexities of real-world environments, addressing limitations of traditional path planning algorithms.
