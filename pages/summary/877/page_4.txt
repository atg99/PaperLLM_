This paper introduces the Adaptive Attention Normalization (AdaAttN) module, designed to enhance feature distribution transfer in style transfer tasks. The AdaAttN module operates through three main steps: first, it generates an attention map that incorporates both low-level and high-level features; second, it computes weighted mean and standard variance maps of the style features; and third, it adaptively normalizes the content features for point-wise feature distribution alignment.

The attention map is generated by using a combination of content and style features, employing a structure that includes learnable convolution layers for queries, keys, and values. The attention mechanism measures the similarity between content and style features across different layers, enabling a more comprehensive analysis than previous methods that focused solely on deeper layers.

In the second step, the attention score matrix is applied to the style features, allowing for the calculation of attention-weighted mean and standard variance statistics for each target style feature point. This process provides a distribution-based perspective on the style features, enhancing the transfer capabilities of the model.

The final step involves adaptive normalization, where the computed statistics are used to transform the content feature map, resulting in a feature map that is aligned with the style features on a per-point basis. This approach allows AdaAttN to generalize better than existing methods like AdaIN and SANet by focusing on localized statistics rather than global ones.

The paper concludes with a discussion of the overall loss function, which combines global style loss and local feature loss, controlled by hyper-parameters that balance their contributions. This comprehensive framework demonstrates the effectiveness of AdaAttN in style transfer applications.
