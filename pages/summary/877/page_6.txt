The paper discusses an extension of a style transfer method for video applications, highlighting its advantages over other attention-based methods. The proposed model generates more natural stylized results with reduced local distortions, making it particularly suitable for video style transfer. 

To adapt the model for video, two modifications are introduced. First, the authors replace the Softmax function in the computation of attention scores with cosine similarity. This change results in a flatter attention score distribution, which enhances stability and prevents overemphasis on local style patterns. Secondly, a novel cross-image similarity loss term is designed to regularize the relevant content between two images, ensuring that the stylized outputs maintain similar local similarity patterns. This approach promotes consistency across frames in video style transfer.

The experiments section details the implementation, using the MS-COCO dataset for content images and the WikiArt dataset for style images. The paper also includes a comparison of running speeds for various methods at different resolutions, illustrating the efficiency of the proposed approach.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of the modified model for producing stable and coherent video style transfer results, supported by experimental findings.
