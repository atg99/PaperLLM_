The paper discusses a novel approach to arbitrary image style transfer, focusing on two key loss functions: global style loss (Lgs) and local feature loss (Llf). The global style loss penalizes the differences in mean and standard deviation between the generated image and the style image in the VGG feature space, ensuring a cohesive global stylization effect. This is represented mathematically as Lgs, which aggregates the differences across multiple layers of the VGG encoder. 

In contrast, the local feature loss constrains the feature map of the stylized image to align with the transformations produced by the AdaAttN function. This loss, denoted as Llf, ensures that local features are preserved during the stylization process. The AdaAttN function is implemented in a parameter-free manner, avoiding the use of learnable convolution kernels, which enhances the deterministic nature of the supervision signal. The introduction of local feature loss improves the quality of stylized outputs in localized areas, surpassing the performance of traditional content loss methods. The paper positions these advancements within the context of existing state-of-the-art techniques in style transfer.
