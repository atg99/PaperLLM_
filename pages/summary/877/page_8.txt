The paper presents the AdaAttN module, designed for arbitrary style transfer, demonstrating its effectiveness through quantitative and qualitative evaluations. The results in Table 2 show that AdaAttN outperforms state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods like SANet, Linear, and MCCNet in terms of optical flow error, indicating better temporal consistency. The ablation studies reveal the significance of the proposed local feature loss (Llf) over traditional content loss (Lc) in preserving style patterns while maintaining content integrity. Removing the global style loss (Lgs) also leads to reduced color saturation, highlighting its necessity. 

The paper further explores the importance of low-level features in AdaAttN, finding that their exclusion results in noticeable quality degradation. For video stylization, the method is confirmed to be more stable than existing attention-based techniques, with modifications like cosine distance based attention enhancing temporal consistency.

Additionally, the study investigates multi-style transfer, employing both style interpolation through mean and variance maps and concatenation of multiple style images. Results indicate that AdaAttN can flexibly manage various styles, yielding plausible outcomes.

In conclusion, the AdaAttN module is shown to effectively facilitate high-quality style transfer for both images and videos, suggesting its potential application in other image manipulation tasks, which the authors plan to explore in future research.
