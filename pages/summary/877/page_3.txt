The paper discusses advancements in style transfer techniques, focusing on the integration of global statistics and local patches through a multi-scale framework. Avatar-Net is mentioned for its innovative approach combining style swap and the Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN) function. Recent developments in attention mechanisms have significantly enhanced arbitrary style transfer by effectively modeling nuanced correspondences between local features of style and content images. Notably, the Style-Attentional Network (SANet) was introduced to better match content and style features, with subsequent works exploring various stroke types and attention mechanisms.

However, many existing methods primarily rely on deep CNN features, neglecting shallow features, which can lead to structural distortions in the content. The objective of the current work is to find a balance between transferring style patterns and preserving content structure.

In the context of video style transfer, the paper highlights the challenges posed by flickering effects due to temporal inconsistencies when applying image style transfer techniques to video sequences. Various approaches, including those utilizing optical flow constraints, have been proposed to enhance stability. Nevertheless, these methods often depend on accurate optical flow extraction. The authors introduce a novel image-wise similarity loss based on attention mechanisms to address flickering artifacts, achieving comparable or superior stability without relying on optical flow.

The proposed framework employs a pre-trained VGG-19 network for multi-scale feature extraction, integrating three AdaAttN modules at different layers to exploit both shallow and deep features effectively. The architecture allows for concatenation of features across layers to enhance the representation of local patterns. The embedded features are synthesized into a stylized image using a decoder.

The paper emphasizes that the adaptive attention normalization (AdaAttN) module is crucial for arbitrary style transfer, improving upon previous frameworks by considering local style patterns and facilitating better alignment of content and style features.
