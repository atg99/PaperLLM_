The paper addresses the challenges of local stylization in arbitrary style transfer models, where global mean and variance calculations often neglect local details, leading to degraded performance. The existing methods that use attention mechanisms improve local style transfer but do not completely resolve issues of local distortions. The authors identify key limitations of current attention-based solutions, such as the over-reliance on high-level features while disregarding low-level textures and the simplistic fusion of re-weighted features without considering feature distribution.

To tackle these issues, the authors propose a novel module called Adaptive Attention Normalization (AdaAttN), which adaptively normalizes features on a per-point basis, enhancing locality awareness. This approach incorporates both shallow and deep features from content and style images for calculating attention scores. The module aligns local feature statistics of content with attention-weighted statistics from style features, thereby achieving a better balance between transferring style patterns and preserving content structure.

The paper introduces a new optimization objective, termed local feature loss, which regularizes local features in the generated images, improving the quality of arbitrary style transfer. The authors conduct extensive experiments to validate the effectiveness of their method against state-of-the-art techniques. Additionally, they extend their model for video style transfer, achieving stable and visually appealing results.

In summary, the key contributions of the paper are:
- The introduction of the AdaAttN module for improved arbitrary style transfer by considering both shallow and deep features.
- The proposal of local feature loss to enhance model training and output quality.
- Comprehensive experiments demonstrating the method's effectiveness.
- An extension for video style transfer that maintains quality and stability.
