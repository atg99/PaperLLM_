The paper discusses the capabilities of a hypernetwork approach for semantic face and portrait editing, showcasing its ability to manipulate attributes such as age, smile, and pose through various vectors. While this method excels in maintaining high-quality semantic structure and allows for artistic controls, it faces a significant limitation due to high VRAM consumption, requiring 22GB for a batch size of 1 during training. This constraint restricts the model's capacity to implement weight deltas effectively, primarily affecting the target generator on a per-channel mean shifting basis. The authors suggest that increasing the batch size, as seen in Swapping Autoencoders, could enhance style transfer quality. They propose exploring smaller alternatives to StyleGAN2 and utilizing GPU optimization techniques like gradient checkpointing to reduce VRAM usage and increase batch size. Additionally, they recommend investigating which layers can be excluded from training by the hypernetwork. While the study focuses on human portraits, the authors highlight the potential for extending this approach to other domains, advocating for further research in neural artistic stylization using more comprehensive semantic segmentation methods or larger models like StyleGAN XL as hardware capabilities improve.
