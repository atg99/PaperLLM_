The paper discusses HyperNST, a method that employs hyper-networks for neural style transfer, focusing on optimizing stylization while preserving structural integrity, particularly in facial features. The authors identify Layer 13 as optimal for balancing stylization with the accurate reconstruction of face structures. 

A significant modification is made to the patch co-occurrence discriminator, with the resolution of generated images set at 256x256 pixels, prompting a reduction in patch sizes to 32x32 pixels. The paper introduces a region mask-driven patch discriminator that utilizes facial semantic segmentation masks to enhance style transfer accuracy by ensuring style signals are aligned with corresponding semantic regions. Instead of random patch extraction, the approach involves selecting patches based on semantic classes, such as hair, face, and background, thus improving the mean intersection over union (IoU) for style learning.

Implementation challenges arise from the small size of individual semantic regions, leading to the consolidation of these regions into three larger classes to effectively utilize them in patch extraction. The evaluation section notes that experiments required about 24 hours to converge, conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 with a batch size of one, consuming significant VRAM due to the hypernetwork's complexity. The dataset for evaluation was compiled from FFHQ and AAHQ, consisting of 100 random content images and 100 random style images.
