In this paper, the authors present their approach to Neural Style Transfer (NST) using a hypernetwork model, referred to as HyperNST, which operates within the StyleGAN2 framework. They evaluate their model against seven baseline methods using 10,000 stylized images generated from the FFHQ and AAHQ datasets. The evaluation metrics include LPIPS, which measures content similarity, and SIFID, which assesses style similarity. The results indicate that HyperNST outperforms other methods in preserving semantic structure, as evidenced by the lowest LPIPS score. Although SIFID values are comparable to Swapping Autoencoders, HyperNST uniquely allows for embedding portraits while retaining semantic editing functionalities. 

The authors note that the inference time of HyperNST is competitive with other methods, making it practical for real-world applications. However, they acknowledge a limitation in the hypernetwork approach regarding the fine-tuning of the StyleGAN2 model, as current hardware constraints prevent optimal tuning of individual weights. Despite these challenges, the authors emphasize the potential of hypernetwork-based NST and anticipate that future advancements in models and hardware will mitigate these limitations, allowing for direct comparison with fully-trained methods in the future.
