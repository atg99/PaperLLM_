The paper by D. Ruta et al. presents a method for generating stylized images using StyleGAN2 by training a hypernetwork to find weight deltas that reconstruct a given content image. The process begins with GAN inversion using e4e to create an initial approximation of the content image. An encoder generates a tensor from this image, which is then used alongside style information to predict weight deltas that modify the pre-trained StyleGAN2.

To enhance the stylization process, the authors introduce conditioning on a target style image by incorporating a style representation embedding. They utilize ALADIN codes, which are 1x256 vectors representing artistic styles, to create a second tensor that is concatenated with the initial tensor. This combined tensor is crucial for the hypernetwork's conditioning.

Recognizing the importance of semantic structure in facial portraits, the authors condition their stylization on semantic regions using segmentation masks obtained through FaRL. They average ALADIN codes extracted from image patches within these regions to ensure that the style aligns with specific semantic classes. This average style code is then tiled and rearranged to correspond with the segmentation maps, forming a final tensor for the hypernetwork.

The training process involves a three-iteration refinement methodology and employs both a patch co-occurrence discriminator and an image-level discriminator to introduce style loss. The hypernetwork is conditioned on the encoded content images and the semantically arranged ALADIN style codes, which enhances the quality of the generated stylized images while preserving content fidelity.
