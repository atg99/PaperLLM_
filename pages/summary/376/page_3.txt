The paper discusses the creation and analysis of a dataset derived from various subreddits focusing on mental illness and general topics. It includes approximately 3000 posts from mental illness subreddits and 300 posts from each of eight general topic subreddits, ensuring a balanced representation of class labels. The dataset underwent preprocessing to eliminate sensitive information, preparing it for public release to facilitate further research.

To evaluate data quality, the authors analyzed the lowest voted posts in the mental illness subreddits to confirm that they conveyed relevant emotional content. They also ensured that general topic subreddits did not overlap significantly with mental illness posts, thereby reducing the chances of false negatives in label assignments. A count of mental illness terms in the respective posts indicated a higher prevalence in mental illness subreddits. Furthermore, the cosine similarity analysis demonstrated that posts from mental illness subreddits were more closely related to each other than to posts from general topic subreddits.

The authors attempted data augmentation using Easy Data Augmentation but found no significant improvement in evaluation metrics, attributing this to the method's effectiveness with smaller datasets.

In the model section, the authors introduce a RoBERTa-based classifier for multi-class mental illness classification, comparing its performance against LSTM and BERT classifiers. The LSTM model, serving as the baseline, utilized a two-layer architecture with specific tokenization and vocabulary strategies. The BERT classifier was fine-tuned from a pre-trained model, reflecting its status as a leading approach in NLP for text classification. Both models were implemented using Pytorch and Hugging-Face Transformers, highlighting the advancements in the field.
