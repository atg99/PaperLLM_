Additionally, the RoBERTa model shows strong performance across various mental illness categories such as ADHD, anxiety, bipolar disorder, depression, and PTSD, achieving F1 scores ranging from 0.84 to 0.91.

In comparison, the BERT model also demonstrates commendable performance, though it slightly lags behind RoBERTa. The results indicate that both models significantly outperform the LSTM baseline, confirming the advantages of using pre-trained Transformer architectures for this classification task.

Moreover, the analysis reveals that while combining posts and titles provides some performance gains, the posts alone carry substantial information, with the RoBERTa model achieving an F1 score of 0.86 on posts alone. This implies that the model can effectively classify mental health issues based on the content of the posts without needing additional structured data.

In summary, the findings highlight the robustness of the RoBERTa model in multi-class mental illness classification, showcasing its potential for application in real-world social media analysis without reliance on structured inputs. The results underscore the importance of leveraging advanced models to enhance classification accuracy in complex tasks.
