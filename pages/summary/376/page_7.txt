In the synonym replacement test, where 10% of tokens were altered, the drop in classification accuracy was minimal, suggesting that the model can maintain performance despite changes that preserve semantic meaning. However, as the percentage of modified tokens increased to 100%, a more significant decline in performance was observed, particularly for titles, highlighting the model's sensitivity to extensive alterations in input.

The masking test revealed the model's dependency on the presence of root words associated with mental illness. Removing these words resulted in a notable decrease in accuracy, while replacing them with a generic token introduced noise, further degrading performance. This demonstrated that while the model can handle some level of perturbation, its reliance on specific keywords is critical for accurate classification.

Overall, these behavioral tests underscore the importance of both contextual understanding and keyword recognition in the RoBERTa model's performance, suggesting areas for improvement and the potential benefits of expert annotation in refining the dataset for future iterations.
