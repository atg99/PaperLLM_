The paper presents an analysis of model performance in categorizing modified social media posts and titles related to mental health. It outlines various test scenarios, including synonym replacement and label removal, and provides precision, recall, F1 score, and accuracy metrics for different modification percentages. The results indicate a decrease in model performance across all tests compared to previous evaluations, suggesting a dependency on root words in input text. Notably, the model demonstrated some resilience, with an F1 score of 0.82 at 10% modification for posts.

The authors emphasize the relevance of their research in the context of the ongoing pandemic, highlighting the role of social media as a primary communication tool. Future work aims to involve domain experts for data annotation, develop a multi-label classifier for users with multiple mental health issues, and enhance model performance in behavioral tests. The research encompasses both long and short texts, making it adaptable across various online platforms. The authors believe their work contributes to the intersection of natural language processing and mental health support, aiming to assist individuals in need of medical attention.
