The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of various natural language processing tasks, categorized into different probing tasks and target tasks. It investigates the relationships between performance on probing tasks and the corresponding target tasks, revealing significant correlations. 

Probing tasks include CB, COPA, WSC, RTE, MultiRC, WiC, BoolQ, CSenseQA, CosmosQA, ReCoRD, and several others related to entity recognition, coreference resolution, and syntactic parsing. The analysis employs Spearman correlation coefficients to quantify the relationships across different training conditions and random restarts.

The findings suggest that performance on probing tasks can serve as a predictive measure for performance on target tasks, indicating the effectiveness of the probing approach in understanding model behavior. The correlations are visualized in a figure, which highlights the strength and direction of these relationships, contributing to the broader discourse on model interpretability and task performance in NLP.

Overall, the study underscores the importance of probing tasks as diagnostic tools for assessing model capabilities and guiding future research directions in the field.
