The paper investigates the transfer performance of intermediate tasks on target tasks using various probing tasks to understand the skills learned during training. It highlights that semantic probing tasks, such as coreference and semantic proto-role labeling, show significant correlation with target tasks focused on semantics and reasoning. Notably, commonsense-oriented tasks like ReCoRD, CommonsenseQA, and CosmosQA exhibit strong inter-correlations, suggesting that they benefit similarly from probing tasks. The study builds on existing research that emphasizes positive transfer in multi-task training and leverages a broader set of intermediate-target task pairs. It also contrasts with previous works by incorporating a single-intermediate task approach and probing tasks for deeper insights into learned skills, offering a comprehensive view of the relationships between tasks and the effectiveness of transfer learning in language models. The findings indicate that certain tasks are more conducive to positive transfer, underscoring the importance of selecting appropriate intermediate tasks to enhance performance in downstream applications.
