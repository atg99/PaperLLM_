The paper presents an overview of various intermediate and target tasks used in natural language processing experiments. It categorizes tasks based on their metrics, sources, and genres. The intermediate tasks include CommonsenseQA, SciTail, Cosmos QA, and others, each focusing on different aspects of question answering, natural language inference, tagging, and sentiment classification. Key metrics include accuracy, F1 score, and Exact Match, derived from diverse sources such as ConceptNet, science exams, and blogs.

Target tasks largely stem from the SuperGLUE benchmark, featuring tasks like QQP for paraphrase detection, MNLI for natural language inference, and others that assess reasoning capabilities in various contexts. For example, SocialIQA evaluates social and emotional reasoning, while HellaSwag tests commonsense reasoning through story continuation. The paper also highlights specific methodologies, such as adversarial filtering in HellaSwag and the unique focus of QA-SRL on verb-centric relationships in semantic role labeling. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of diverse datasets and metrics in advancing natural language understanding capabilities.
