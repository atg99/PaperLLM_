This paper discusses various tasks and performance metrics in the context of natural language processing, focusing on correlations between probing tasks and target tasks. It lists a comprehensive array of tasks such as CB, COPA, WSC, RTE, MultiRC, WiC, BoolQ, and several others, categorized under different probing aspects like EP (Entity Parsing), SE (Structural Evaluation), and AJ (Adversarial Judgment). 

The paper presents detailed performance metrics for these tasks, showcasing their interrelations through Pearson correlation coefficients. These coefficients illustrate how well the performance on probing tasks aligns with that on target tasks across various training conditions. The findings indicate that understanding and evaluating model performance through probing tasks can provide significant insights into the underlying capabilities of language models. 

Overall, the research emphasizes the importance of probing tasks in enhancing our comprehension of language model behaviors and the potential implications for future model development and evaluation strategies.
