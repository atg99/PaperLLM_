This paper investigates the impact of intermediate-task training on the performance of target and probing tasks in natural language processing. The results are summarized in Figure 2, which displays the performance differences (deltas) between models trained with intermediate tasks and baseline models that were fine-tuned without such training. A positive delta indicates successful transfer of learning.

The authors identify effective intermediate tasks as those that enhance target task performance, particularly those requiring complex reasoning and inference, such as MNLI and commonsense-oriented tasks like CommonsenseQA, HellaSWAG, and CosmosQA. In contrast, tasks like SocialIQA, CCG, and QQP resulted in negative transfer effects across all target tasks.

The study also explores the influence of dataset size on intermediate tasks but finds no consistent relationship with downstream task performance. Smaller target tasks, such as RTE, BoolQ, MultiRC, WiC, and WSC, appear to benefit the most from intermediate-task training. Notably, the CommitmentBank task does not show positive transfer since the baseline model already achieves perfect accuracy.

Additionally, probing task performance is influenced by intermediate-task training, although the authors note that deltas for experiments involving the same intermediate and target tasks are not zero due to the distinct training phases involved.

Overall, the findings highlight the significance of selecting appropriate intermediate tasks to enhance the performance of NLP models on complex reasoning tasks.
