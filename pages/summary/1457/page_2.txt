The paper investigates the effectiveness of intermediate tasks in enhancing the skills of models trained for natural language understanding. It conducts experiments involving 11 intermediate tasks and 10 target tasks, resulting in 110 intermediate-target task pairs, and utilizes 25 probing tasks to analyze the skills learned from each intermediate task.

Key findings include: 

1. Natural language inference and commonsense reasoning tasks generally improve model performance on target tasks.
2. Some intermediate tasks, such as SocialIQA and QQP, do not contribute to skill development as effectively as others like MNLI and CosmosQA, which enhance overall skill acquisition.
3. A model's performance on masked language modeling (MLM) is crucial for downstream tasks, while low-level skills, such as knowledge of sentence attributes, show less correlation with target task success. The analysis suggests that catastrophic forgetting during pretraining may influence results.

The methodology includes a two-step experimental pipeline: first, fine-tuning a pretrained model like RoBERTa on intermediate tasks, and then further fine-tuning on target or probing tasks. This approach allows the isolation of skills gained from individual intermediate tasks.

The intermediate tasks cover various domains, including question answering, commonsense reasoning, and natural language inference. Examples of these tasks include QAMR, CommonsenseQA, SciTail, and Cosmos QA, each designed to test different aspects of language understanding.
