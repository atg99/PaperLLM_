This paper conducts a comprehensive study on the effectiveness of intermediate-task training with pretrained models, specifically focusing on RoBERTa. The experiments involve 110 pairs of intermediate and target tasks alongside an analysis using 25 probing tasks that examine various semantic and syntactic aspects. The findings indicate that tasks requiring complex reasoning, such as Cosmos QA and HellaSwag, yield the best results when used as intermediate tasks.

The analysis reveals that intermediate tasks that enhance RoBERTa's performance across multiple downstream tasks foster positive transfer. However, it remains challenging to pinpoint the specific skills that facilitate this transfer. While there is some evidence suggesting that intermediate-task training may improve syntactic handling, a correlation between target-task performance and probing-task performance for these skills is minimal. In contrast, probes for higher-level semantic capabilities show a stronger correlation with target-task performance, though the results are too varied to draw definitive conclusions.

The study also raises concerns regarding a significant correlation between target-task performance and probing tasks closely related to RoBERTa's masked language modeling pretraining objective. This suggests that the results of the intermediate-task training could be influenced by the forgetting of knowledge acquired during pretraining. Consequently, the paper advocates for further exploration of efficient transfer learning mechanisms.

Future research should focus on expanding the range and depth of available probing tasks to enhance understanding in this domain. The project received support from various organizations, highlighting the collaborative nature of advancements in deep learning and natural language processing.
