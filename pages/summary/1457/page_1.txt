The paper investigates the effectiveness of intermediate-task transfer learning using pretrained models like RoBERTa in the context of natural language understanding. While pretrained models have significantly advanced performance in various tasks, the authors explore the conditions under which further training on an intermediate task before fine-tuning on a target task is beneficial. 

The study involves a large-scale analysis of 110 combinations of intermediate and target tasks, along with evaluations using 25 probing tasks designed to assess the specific skills that contribute to successful transfer learning. Key findings indicate that intermediate tasks that require high-level inference and reasoning are most effective. Additionally, there is a strong correlation between target task performance and advanced skills such as coreference resolution. However, the authors note a lack of detailed correlations between probing tasks and target performance, suggesting a need for comprehensive probing benchmarks. 

The research also highlights the potential issue of knowledge forgetting from pretraining, which may limit the analysis of transfer learning. The paper aims to clarify which types of tasks serve as effective intermediaries and the linguistic skills acquired during this process, thus addressing the broader questions of when and why intermediate-task training is advantageous.
