This paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) for object detection, particularly focusing on anchor-free fully convolutional models. The authors highlight the limitations of previous methods in speeding up reward evaluation for reinforcement learning-based NAS, noting that techniques like fast decoder adaptation, while effective in semantic segmentation, do not yield similar benefits for object detection due to the need for complex post-processing.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose utilizing the FCOS framework, which eliminates the anchor-box matching step found in traditional methods like RetinaNet, thereby enhancing search efficiency and reducing training memory requirements. Their approach involves a systematic search for optimal decoder structures, specifically focusing on the feature pyramid network (FPN) and prediction heads.

The search problem is framed within a one-stage framework, employing training tuples that consist of input images and FCOS output targets. The architecture includes a backbone for feature extraction, an FPN for mapping features to a pyramid representation, and prediction heads for generating final predictions. The authors aim to refine the processes of feature selection and merging to accommodate objects of varying scales, employing NAS to explore different configurations.

The search space is divided into two distinct parts for the FPN structure and the prediction head, utilizing atomic operations for flexibility. The paper outlines the candidate operations available for constructing the decoder, which includes various types of convolutions and aggregation methods to enhance the model's efficiency. Through this approach, the authors seek to optimize the architecture for improved performance in object detection tasks.
