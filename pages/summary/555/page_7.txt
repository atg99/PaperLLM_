The paper discusses the effectiveness of weight sharing in multi-scale detection models and the performance of a novel neural architecture search (NAS) method for object detection. It presents a trend graph showing that as the search progresses, the proportion of fully shared head structures increases, highlighting the necessity of this approach. 

The authors compare their method with existing NAS techniques, demonstrating that their architecture searches twice as many designs per GPU-day compared to DetNAS. They also note that their model surpasses NAS-FPN by 1.3 average precision (AP) points while utilizing significantly fewer floating-point operations (FLOPs) and reduced computational cost.

Figures illustrate the relationship between FLOPs, model parameters, and AP for various architectures, showing that adjusting channel numbers in the feature pyramid network (FPN) can balance accuracy and computational efficiency. Additionally, a strong correlation is observed between search rewards and APs on the COCO dataset, indicating that the reward structure effectively distinguishes between high and low-performing architectures.

Lastly, an ablation study is mentioned that examines the design of the reinforcement learning reward used in the search process, suggesting that established metrics are typically employed as rewards for guiding architecture optimization.
