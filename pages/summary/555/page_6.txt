The paper presents a comparison of various object detection models using different backbone architectures on the MS COCO dataset, focusing particularly on the performance of a newly introduced model termed NAS-FCOS. The results are summarized in a table that details the number of Floating Point Operations (FLOPs), parameters (Params), and Average Precision (AP) scores for various configurations of the models.

Key findings include:

1. **Performance Metrics**: The NAS-FCOS models, particularly those with the MobileNetV backbone, show a significant improvement in AP while maintaining lower FLOPs and parameters compared to traditional models like FPN-RetinaNet and FPN-FCOS. For instance, the NAS-FCOS configuration with a 128-channel decoder achieves an AP of 32.0 with only 239.3 FLOPs and 5 million parameters.

2. **Decoder Structure**: The paper introduces a new decoder structure discovered through a search process, which utilizes deformable convolutions and concatenation to optimize performance. This structure allows for a trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency, as it reduces FLOPs and parameters while enhancing performance.

3. **Comparison with FCOS**: The NAS-FCOS models consistently outperform the traditional FCOS models across various backbones. For example, the NAS-FCOS with a 128-channel decoder surpasses the original FCOS with the same backbone by achieving 0.8 AP points with only one-third of the FLOPs.

4. **Design Choices**: The authors emphasize the importance of the selected operations in the decoder architecture and highlight that the discovered structure with fewer operations (4 instead of the maximum of 6) achieves better efficiency.

In conclusion, the proposed NAS-FCOS model demonstrates superior performance and efficiency, making it suitable for both powerful and resource-constrained environments. The paper effectively showcases how architectural innovations can lead to advancements in object detection tasks.
