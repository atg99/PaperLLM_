This paper presents the use of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) to enhance the design of object detection networks. The authors compare various NAS methods, highlighting the performance of their proposed model, NAS-FCOS, against others like NAS-FPN and DetNAS. The results indicate that NAS-FCOS achieves competitive average precision (AP) scores with significantly lower search costs in GPU days, showcasing its efficiency and effectiveness.

The paper discusses the impact of different search spaces for the network architecture, specifically focusing on the decoder components. Experiments reveal that searching for specific components (f) yields slightly better results than searching others (h) alone, and a combined search (f+h) further improves performance. The inclusion of deformable convolutions in the search space is also examined, with NAS-FCOS outperforming a variant using only deformable convolutions.

The findings suggest that optimized proxy tasks and search strategies can lead to the efficient discovery of high-performing detectors, as demonstrated through experiments on the COCO dataset. The authors conclude that NAS-FCOS is a flexible model adaptable to various backbone architectures, contributing to advancements in object detection methodologies.
