The paper presents a novel architecture search framework for the NAS-FCOS decoder, which integrates a feature pyramid network (FPN) and multiple prediction heads with partially shared weights. This approach differentiates itself from existing FPN-based one-stage detectors by allowing for shared weights only in the last few layers of prediction heads, with the number of layers determined by a search algorithm. 

The methodology involves training a sampled architecture on a meta-training set of 4,000 images and evaluating it on a meta-validation set of 1,715 images. Input images are resized to a short size of 384 and cropped to 384x384. The Adam optimizer is utilized with a learning rate of 8e-4 and a batch size of 200, while Polyak averaging is applied with a decay rate of 0.9. The backbone features are fixed and cached during the search phase, initialized with pre-trained weights from FCOS and fine-tuned on the VOC dataset.

The search strategy is progressive, beginning with the FPN part while retaining the original head. The FPN structure consists of operations with 64 output channels, and a fixed FPN structure is established before searching for the head, which has an output channel width adjusted to 128. The controller model for searching FPN architectures converged after evaluating 2.8K architectures, leading to the selection of the top 20 for full training. The head search follows, taking approximately 600 rounds for convergence, resulting in the selection of the top 10 heads based on proxy task performance.

In the full training phase, the selected models are trained on the MS COCO dataset, adhering to training configurations similar to FCOS to ensure fairness. Input images are resized to a short size of 800 with a maximum long side of 1333, and training is conducted over 90K iterations using 4 V100 GPUs and a batch size of 16, with a learning rate schedule in place. The paper concludes with a focus on the effectiveness of the reinforcement learning model in improving performance during the architecture search process.
