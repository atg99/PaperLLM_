The paper discusses the current state of neural network pruning, emphasizing the need for rigorous evaluation and standardized methodologies in research. It advocates for the use of statistical measures such as means and sample standard deviations rather than relying on singular measurements. Additionally, it stresses that all methods compared should utilize the same libraries, data loading, and code to ensure fairness in evaluation.

To facilitate better research practices, the authors introduce ShrinkBench, an open-source library designed for pruning neural networks. ShrinkBench offers a standardized framework for training, pruning, fine-tuning, and metric computation using predefined models and datasets based on PyTorch. The library allows for customizable pruning methods and is capable of handling multiple models and datasets while tracking various metrics.

Various baseline pruning methods implemented in ShrinkBench include Global and Layerwise Magnitude Pruning, Global and Layerwise Gradient Magnitude Pruning, and Random Pruning. These methods serve as benchmarks for new pruning techniques. The paper highlights the importance of avoiding common pitfalls in experimental practices, which can lead to misleading conclusions about the efficacy of different pruning methods.

The authors note that metrics such as parameter reduction and FLOPs are often reported interchangeably, but their correlation varies by method, suggesting that relying on a single metric can be insufficient. Their findings indicate that the performance of pruning methods can differ significantly based on the model, dataset, and extent of pruning applied, underscoring the need for comprehensive evaluations across diverse conditions.
