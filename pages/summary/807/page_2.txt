Neural network pruning involves modifying a model's architecture to create a more efficient version by removing less important parameters or structures. This process begins with training a model to convergence, followed by scoring its parameters to determine which to prune. The pruning process can lead to a reduction in model accuracy, necessitating a fine-tuning phase to recover performance. This cycle of pruning and fine-tuning can be repeated multiple times to iteratively reduce the network size.

Pruning methods can be categorized based on their approach to sparsity structure, scoring criteria, scheduling of pruning actions, and fine-tuning strategies. Unstructured pruning targets individual parameters, while structured pruning removes entire groups such as neurons or filters to better align with hardware efficiencies. Scoring can be based on absolute values, importance coefficients, or contributions to activations, and can be applied either locally or globally within the network. The scheduling of pruning can vary from simultaneous removal to gradual reduction over iterations. Fine-tuning methods also differ, with options ranging from continuing training with existing weights to completely reinitializing the network.

Evaluating the effectiveness of pruning depends on the specific goals, such as reducing storage, computational cost, or energy consumption, with different metrics required for each objective. Overall, the state of neural network pruning reflects a diverse set of strategies aimed at enhancing model efficiency while balancing performance.
