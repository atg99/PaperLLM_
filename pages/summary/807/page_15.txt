The paper discusses the state of neural network pruning and outlines the methodology for selecting and analyzing 81 relevant papers in the domain. The authors conducted a literature search using Google Scholar to find influential pruning methods and their citations. They also reviewed recent conference proceedings from NeurIPS, ICML, CVPR, ECCV, and ICLR, incorporating papers that cited or compared to existing works. The corpus includes both published and unpublished papers of reasonable quality, capturing a broad spectrum of pruning methodologies.

To standardize the reported metrics across different studies, the authors converted various results into common formats, such as changing Top-1 error rates to Top-1 accuracies and fractions of parameters pruned to compression ratios. However, they noted the challenges in converting size metrics to speedup metrics due to dependency on the specific layers within the neural networks. The analysis primarily focuses on model size reduction and theoretical speedup, acknowledging that it does not cover all metrics, such as power consumption or memory usage, which may limit the comprehensiveness of the evaluation.

The paper also presents a checklist for evaluating pruning methods, emphasizing the need for contextualization within the existing literature, detailed descriptions of algorithms and architectures, and comprehensive reporting of experimental conditions and results. The checklist aims to ensure that pruning techniques are reproducible and that results are presented with sufficient rigor, including varying compression ratios, accuracy measures, and statistical information. The authors invite corrections to their analysis and refer readers to the arXiv version for the latest updates.
