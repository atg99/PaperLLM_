The paper investigates the state of neural network pruning, focusing on the performance of various pruning methods under different conditions. It presents findings that highlight the superiority of global methods over layerwise methods and magnitude-based methods over gradient-based methods, particularly in terms of accuracy for different compression ratios on CIFAR-VGG and ResNet-56 models. 

Notably, the results indicate that the choice of initial model significantly influences the performance outcomes of pruning techniques. The study demonstrates that using different pretrained weights can lead to varying effectiveness of pruning methods, complicating direct comparisons between them. This suggests that simply evaluating accuracy changes is insufficient to account for initial model discrepancies.

The authors emphasize the need for standardized experimental practices in neural network pruning research, as inconsistencies and missing baselines in the literature hinder the assessment of techniques. To address these issues, they propose best practices and introduce an open-source library named ShrinkBench to facilitate more reliable and comparable results in future studies.

In conclusion, the paper calls for a more rigorous and uniform approach to evaluating pruning methods to advance the understanding of their efficacy in neural network optimization.
