The paper discusses the state of neural network pruning, focusing on the performance of ResNet-110 and ResNet-18 architectures when subjected to varying levels of compression on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets. Key findings include the relationship between compression ratio and accuracy, demonstrating that different pruning strategies—global weight, layer weight, global gradient, layer gradient, and random—impact the accuracy differently across various compression levels.

For ResNet-110 on CIFAR-10, accuracy is shown to decrease as the compression ratio increases, with specific strategies yielding different performance outcomes. The theoretical speedup also correlates with accuracy, indicating that while higher compression can lead to faster inference, it may compromise model performance.

Similarly, for ResNet-18 on ImageNet, the accuracy trends follow a comparable pattern to that of ResNet-110, illustrating the trade-offs between model size reduction and retention of accuracy. The analysis emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate pruning techniques to balance efficiency and performance in neural networks. Overall, the findings highlight the ongoing challenges and considerations in the field of neural network pruning, suggesting avenues for future research.
