The paper discusses the state of neural network pruning, highlighting significant issues related to model ambiguity and metric reporting in the literature. It identifies confusion surrounding the use of VGG networks, where many studies claim to use a standard architecture but often apply custom variations without clear documentation. This lack of clarity extends to other architectures as well, where authors fail to specify the exact models used, leading to difficulties in replication and comparison.

The paper also critiques the inconsistent use of metrics like "Pruned%" and "compression ratio," which can vary significantly in meaning across different studies. This inconsistency complicates the assessment of pruning methods and their effectiveness. The reported speedup values are similarly ambiguous, as they can refer to different calculations based on wall time or FLOPs without standardized definitions.

To improve the clarity and comparability of pruning research, the authors recommend several best practices: clearly identify the architectures, datasets, and metrics used; include a minimum of three modern dataset and architecture combinations; report both compression ratios and theoretical speedups; provide accuracy metrics for both Top-1 and Top-5; include control metrics for original models; plot tradeoff curves with multiple operating points; and ensure comprehensive documentation of experimental setups.

These recommendations aim to facilitate direct comparisons between different pruning methods, ultimately enhancing the understanding and advancement of neural network pruning techniques.
