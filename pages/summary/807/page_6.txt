The paper discusses the current state of neural network pruning, highlighting the challenges and inconsistencies in reported results related to compression ratios and accuracy metrics across various architectures and datasets. It presents a graphical representation of the fragmentation of results from numerous studies, illustrating how different papers yield varying accuracies for the same model configurations. The authors emphasize the importance of using consistent code and reporting sufficient measurements to mitigate random variations, which remains rare in the literature. They note that while some papers, such as those by Gale et al. and Liu et al., provide valuable insights into existing pruning methods, there is a general lack of novel approaches that comprehensively address the confounding variables affecting the evaluation of pruning techniques.
