The paper discusses the current state of neural network pruning, highlighting several challenges in the literature that hinder effective comparisons of different pruning methods. A significant issue is the fragmentation of metrics and operating points across studies, which complicates the assessment of the efficiency versus accuracy tradeoff. Although there is an observable tradeoff, the results from various methods are often incomparable due to differences in reporting standards and experimental setups.

The research indicates that many papers utilize a limited number of dataset and architecture combinations, leading to a lack of comprehensive evaluation. For example, the most common (dataset, architecture) pairs are listed, showing that a few models dominate the literature. Additionally, papers frequently report results at only a few points on their tradeoff curves, further complicating direct comparisons.

Confounding variables, such as the initial model's accuracy and efficiency, data augmentation techniques, training randomness, pruning schedules, and the deep learning libraries used, also pose challenges for meaningful comparisons. Overall, the paper underscores the need for more standardized reporting and methodology in neural network pruning research to facilitate clearer assessments and comparisons across studies.
