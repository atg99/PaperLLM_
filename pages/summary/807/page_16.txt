The paper discusses the state of neural network pruning, specifically focusing on various comparative baselines to evaluate pruning techniques. Key comparisons include:

1. **Random Pruning Baselines**: 
   - Global random pruning.
   - Random pruning with layer-wise proportions matching the proposed technique.

2. **Magnitude Pruning Baselines**: 
   - Global uniform layer-wise magnitude pruning.
   - Magnitude pruning maintaining the same layer-wise proportions as the proposed method.

3. **Other State-of-the-Art Techniques**: 
   - The paper emphasizes the importance of detailing how comparisons were made, including the data source (whether from the original paper, reimplementation, or code reuse) and any discrepancies that may exist between the experimental settings.

In the experimental setup, ShrinkBench is utilized to ensure reproducibility by fixing random seeds for dependencies such as PyTorch and NumPy. The pruning methods used do not include the classifier layer before the softmax, though this is an available option. Both Global and Layerwise Gradient Magnitude Pruning techniques rely on a single minibatch to compute gradients, with experiments conducted across three independent runs to account for variance due to randomness.

The finetuning setup varies between datasets. For CIFAR10, the setup includes a batch size of 64, 30 epochs, Adam optimizer, and a fixed learning rate of 3e-4. For ImageNet, a larger batch size of 256, 20 epochs, SGD with Nesterov Momentum, and a fixed learning rate of 1e-3 are employed.

Additional results from ShrinkBench are reported for CIFAR10 using models such as CIFAR-VGG and ResNets, with standard deviations plotted as error bars across three different runs. For ImageNet, results are provided for ResNet-18.
