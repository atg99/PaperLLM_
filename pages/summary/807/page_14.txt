The state of neural network pruning focuses on optimizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to enhance energy efficiency and computational performance. Key studies in this area include Yang et al. (2017), who introduced energy-aware pruning techniques to design energy-efficient CNNs, and Yao et al. (2018), who proposed balanced sparsity for efficient deep neural network (DNN) inference on GPUs. 

Yu et al. (2018) advanced the field with their Neuron Importance Score Propagation (NISP) method, which effectively prunes networks by evaluating the importance of neurons. Additionally, significant contributions were made by Zagoruyko (2015) in achieving high performance on the CIFAR-10 dataset, and Zhang et al. (2015), who focused on accelerating very deep CNNs for classification and detection tasks.

Overall, these works emphasize the importance of pruning techniques in reducing model size and improving inference speed without significantly sacrificing accuracy, thus making neural networks more practical for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
