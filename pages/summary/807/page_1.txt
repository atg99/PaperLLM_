The paper "What is the State of Neural Network Pruning" by Davis Blalock and colleagues provides a comprehensive meta-analysis of neural network pruning, a technique aimed at reducing network size by removing parameters while maintaining accuracy. The authors review 81 research papers, highlighting the lack of standardized benchmarks and metrics in the field, which complicates comparisons of pruning techniques and hinders progress assessment over the past three decades. 

Key findings indicate that while pruning based on parameter magnitude effectively compresses networks without accuracy loss, many methods lack comparative analysis with others, leading to inconsistent methodologies across studies. The authors note that a significant portion of papers do not compare multiple pruning methods, and there is a diversity in datasets, metrics, and hyperparameters used.

To address these issues, the paper introduces ShrinkBench, an open-source framework designed for standardized evaluations of pruning methods. This framework aims to facilitate adherence to best practices and improve comparability in pruning research. The contributions of the paper include a detailed analysis of pruning literature, identification of common pitfalls, recommendations for best practices, and the introduction of ShrinkBench to enhance the integrity of future pruning evaluations.
