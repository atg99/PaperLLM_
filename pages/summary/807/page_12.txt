The paper discusses the state of neural network pruning, a technique aimed at reducing the size and complexity of neural networks while maintaining their performance. The authors review various methods and approaches to pruning, highlighting their effectiveness and efficiency. 

Key contributions in the field include techniques such as optimal brain damage, which removes unnecessary weights, and connection sensitivity-based methods that prune connections based on their importance. The paper also examines data-driven approaches for selecting sparse structures in deep networks and the impact of pruning on different neural architectures, such as convolutional networks.

The authors note advancements like Gpipe for efficient training of large networks and the significance of filter pruning for optimizing convolutional networks. They emphasize the relevance of pruning in real-world applications, particularly in mobile environments where computational resources are limited. 

Overall, the review underscores the ongoing evolution of pruning techniques, their theoretical foundations, and practical implementations, suggesting that while pruning has made significant strides, further research is essential to refine these methods for broader applications in deep learning.
