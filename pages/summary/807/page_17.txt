The paper discusses the state of neural network pruning, focusing on its impact on accuracy and theoretical speedup across various models, specifically CIFAR-VGG and ResNet architectures on the CIFAR-10 dataset. It presents data on compression ratios ranging from 1 to 32, illustrating how different pruning strategies—global weight, layer weight, global gradient, layer gradient, and random—affect model accuracy.

Figures indicate that as the compression ratio increases, accuracy tends to vary, with some pruning methods maintaining higher accuracy than others. Theoretical speedup is also analyzed in relation to accuracy, showing how efficient pruning can lead to significant performance improvements without compromising model effectiveness. The results highlight the balance between achieving a high compression ratio and maintaining acceptable accuracy levels across different neural network architectures.
