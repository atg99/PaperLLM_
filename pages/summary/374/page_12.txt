The paper discusses the evaluation of various probing tasks and fine-tuning methods for assessing knowledge in language models, particularly focusing on RoBERTa. It references several probing packages, including LKT, CAT, and OLM PICS, highlighting that LKT is unique in requiring additional training. The authors emphasize reproducibility by adhering to established hyperparameters and setups from prior research.

The environmental impact of the experiments is quantified, with probing estimated to generate a carbon footprint of 165.54 kg CO2e and consume 345.26 kWh of energy. Fine-tuning, conducted using public packages such as jiant and Huggingface, is noted to emit 54.98 kg CO2e and consume 121.64 kWh. All experiments were executed on specific hardware configurations, including an Intel Core i9-9820X CPU and a GTX 2080 Ti GPU.

Further sections provide detailed results from various probing tasks, illustrating learning patterns and correlations among different probes. The results indicate that while successful probes often correlate, a low correlation does not necessarily indicate failure. Additionally, the paper explores the effects of varying pretraining corpus domains and sizes, concluding that larger and more diverse corpora generally lead to better performance in the RoBERTa model, although some specific transitions, like from downsampled to original English Wikipedia, do not yield significant improvements.
