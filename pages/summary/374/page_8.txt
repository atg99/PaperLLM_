The paper analyzes the finetuning performance of a reproduced RoBERTa model across various research benchmarks, revealing that most tasks achieve high performance with less than half of the pretraining steps. However, tasks requiring complex knowledge, such as ReCoRD and WSC, benefit from extended pretraining. The authors utilize a diverse set of probes to examine the learning dynamics, finding that factual and commonsense knowledge improves as pretraining progresses, contrasting with Chiang et al.'s findings that linguistic and factual knowledge do not generally improve. The authors attribute this difference to their systematic choice of probes, which includes more categories and tasks.

Additionally, the paper discusses the impact of the pretraining corpus on knowledge dynamics, highlighting that while linguistic knowledge is relatively easy to learn, other types of knowledge require larger datasets and more training iterations. The authors' findings align with previous work showing that a larger corpus size improves knowledge acquisition, particularly in commonsense reasoning. They also address differing views on probing, contrasting their ease-of-extraction perspective with an information-theoretic approach. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of pretraining dynamics and the corpus selection in enhancing model performance across various knowledge types.
