The paper "Probing Across Time: What Does RoBERTa Know and When?" investigates the timeline of knowledge acquisition in the RoBERTa language model during its pretraining phase. The authors build upon previous research by systematically analyzing when various types of knowledge—linguistic, factual, commonsense, and reasoning—are acquired throughout pretraining iterations.

Key findings indicate that linguistic knowledge is acquired quickly and consistently, while factual and commonsense knowledge develop more slowly and are sensitive to the domain of the training data. Reasoning abilities, on the other hand, are generally not stably learned. The study employs a probing framework applied at multiple checkpoints during RoBERTa's pretraining, utilizing a diverse suite of probes to assess different knowledge types.

Additionally, the authors examine how variations in training data affect the speed and effectiveness of knowledge acquisition. Their results suggest that a diverse dataset is more crucial than sheer volume for effective learning. The paper also compares the probing results with benchmark task performance, revealing that tasks requiring simpler knowledge achieve high performance with fewer pretraining steps, while those demanding complex reasoning benefit from longer training durations.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of understanding the nuanced learning processes of language models to inform future pretraining strategies.
