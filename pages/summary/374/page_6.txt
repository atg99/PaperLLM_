Specifically, the model's ability to learn linguistic knowledge rapidly translates to improved performance on benchmarks that require understanding of language structure. However, the slower acquisition of factual and commonsense knowledge suggests that tasks requiring these types of knowledge may benefit from additional training strategies or data sources.

We also observed that fine-tuning on larger and more diverse datasets leads to better performance across the benchmarks. This reinforces the idea that not only the size of the corpus but also its diversity plays a critical role in enhancing the model's capabilities. The experiments indicate that while RoBERTa can achieve competitive results, further improvements can be made by focusing on the integration of various knowledge types during training. 

In conclusion, our findings emphasize the importance of both the domain and corpus size in shaping the learning trajectory of RoBERTa, with implications for future model training and application in downstream tasks. This suggests that careful selection and preparation of training data are crucial for optimizing the performance of pretrained models in practical applications.
