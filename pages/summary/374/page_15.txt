The paper presents results from the BLIMP (Benchmarking Linguistic Minimal Pairs) evaluation, focusing on various linguistic phenomena and their accuracy rates. It details the performance of different syntactic structures, including Subject-Verb Agreement, Quantifier, Ellipsis, Control/Raising, Binding, Argument Structure, Anaphor Agreement, NPI Licensing, Determiner-Noun Agreement, Irregular Forms, Filler-Gap, and Island Effects. Each category is represented with accuracy scores ranging from 0 to 1, illustrating the effectiveness of the model in understanding these linguistic constructs.

Additionally, the paper compares the performance of the original RoBERTaBASE model with checkpoints at varying pretraining steps, emphasizing a learning progress curve that indicates improvements in accuracy as more training steps are applied. The data suggests a consistent increase in accuracy across various linguistic tasks, demonstrating the model's capability to learn and adapt to complex linguistic rules. Overall, the results underscore the importance of fine-tuning and extensive pretraining in achieving high accuracy in linguistic tasks.
