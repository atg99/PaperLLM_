The paper discusses the pretraining and probing of the RoBERTa model in the context of aligning books and movies for generating story-like visual explanations. The authors selected the base-size RoBERTa model (125M parameters) for their experiments due to computational constraints. They implemented a version of RoBERTa using the TPU framework from the fairseq library, acknowledging that their implementation differs slightly from the original model, which may account for a minor performance gap. Despite this, they assert that these differences are unlikely to significantly alter learning patterns.

For pretraining, the authors conducted less than three passes over a large dataset, suggesting that dynamic masking would not substantially impact results, as probing performance stabilizes quickly. They detail the pretraining hyperparameters and note that using 8 TPU-v3 cores resulted in approximately 1 million steps over 15 days. To highlight the environmental impact of their large-scale pretraining, they estimate that each pretraining session consumed about 684.02 kWh of energy, contributing a carbon footprint of 173.19 kg CO2e, leading to a total of 4104.12 kWh and 1039.14 kg CO2e for all experiments.

In the probing and finetuning section, the authors utilized publicly available probing packages, specifically LKT for linguistic knowledge, BLIMP for linguistic probing, and LAMA for factual and common knowledge assessments, running these on the reported checkpoints.
