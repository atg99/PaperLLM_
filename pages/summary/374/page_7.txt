The experiments reveal that tasks such as CoLA and SST-2 achieve high performance with significantly fewer pretraining steps compared to tasks like ReCoRD and WSC, which require more extensive pretraining. This suggests a hierarchy of knowledge types, where linguistic knowledge is acquired more quickly than commonsense or factual knowledge.

The paper also discusses the implications of these findings for the understanding of pretrained language models. The selected probing packages and tasks reflect a range of downstream applications, indicating the diverse capabilities that models like RoBERTa can develop. The results illustrate that while certain tasks benefit from extensive pretraining, others may not necessarily improve with longer training periods, as evidenced by the performance drop in MNLI towards the end of pretraining.

Overall, the study enhances our understanding of how pretrained language models acquire different types of knowledge and the complexities involved in fine-tuning for various tasks. The results emphasize the importance of considering the nature of the tasks when evaluating model performance and the role of pretraining in shaping the learning dynamics of language models.
