The paper discusses the structured nature of knowledge representations in neural language models, particularly focusing on the masked language model RoBERTa. It argues that while traditional views of these models suggest a deterministic outcome, the actual utility of the representations hinges on their extractability. The authors conducted a pilot study using multi-layer perceptron (MLP) classifiers to probe the structural representations, finding that MLPs often outperformed linear classifiers even on randomly initialized models. However, they acknowledge that MLP classifiers do not adequately capture the learning process of the models or explain the gradual improvements observed in downstream tasks.

The authors also address ethical considerations related to their research, specifically the carbon costs of experimentation and the potential variability introduced by hyperparameter changes. They refer to previous work that supports their findings, indicating consistent linguistic patterns across models trained with different hyperparameters.

In conclusion, the paper highlights the temporal acquisition of knowledge by RoBERTa during pretraining, noting that it learns linguistic knowledge more rapidly than factual or commonsense knowledge, while facing challenges in developing reasoning skills. The authors propose that their probing framework can serve as a valuable tool for future research in understanding the evolution of language models.
