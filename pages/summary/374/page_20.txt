The paper presents results from experiments on OLM PICS (Reasoning) and LKT (Linguistics) across different domains and corpus sizes. The performance metrics, including accuracy and F1 scores, are plotted against the number of pretraining steps for various tasks such as taxonomy conjunction, antonym negation, object comparison, and syntactic tasks. 

For OLM PICS, the average performance shows a positive correlation with the number of pretraining steps, with specific tasks achieving varying levels of accuracy. For instance, the accuracy for objects comparison peaked at 0.65, while always/never conjunction had a lower accuracy of 0.4. 

In the LKT section, results indicate that larger corpus sizes, like NEWS+WIKI+STORIES (36GB), enhance performance on tasks such as POS tagging and syntactic chunking, with accuracy reaching up to 0.9 for POS tagging. The plots utilize exponential moving averages to smooth the results, providing a clearer view of trends in performance as pretraining steps increase. 

Overall, the findings underline the importance of both the quantity of pretraining data and the number of training steps in improving model performance across various linguistic tasks.
