The paper evaluates the performance of a reproduced RoBERTa model across various tasks related to linguistic, factual, commonsense, and reasoning knowledge. The findings indicate that linguistic information is acquired quickly, with 97% of performance improvement occurring within the first 20% of training updates. This rapid learning is consistent across various linguistic tasks, such as POS tagging and syntactic chunking.

In contrast, the learning of factual and commonsense knowledge, measured by LAMA and CAT, is notably slower and exhibits more instability. Many tasks require more than half of the pretraining steps to reach 97% of their best performance, with significant variability in results. For instance, while the model steadily learns from SQuAD and ConceptNet, it shows fluctuations in others like argument reasoning and Winograd, and struggles with certain factual knowledge tasks.

Reasoning abilities, assessed through OLM PICS, are particularly underdeveloped, with many reasoning tasks not being learned during the pretraining phase. The paper also notes discrepancies in final performance between the reproduced and original RoBERTa models, which may stem from differences in replication methods.

Overall, the study highlights the distinct learning patterns for different types of knowledge, emphasizing the challenges in acquiring factual and reasoning skills compared to linguistic knowledge.
