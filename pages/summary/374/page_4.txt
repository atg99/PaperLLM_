This paper investigates the ease of extracting targeted knowledge from complex probing models, specifically using RoBERTa as a case study. The authors assess the effectiveness of knowledge extraction by comparing RoBERTa's performance with various baselines, including random guessing, random vector classifiers, GloVe vectors, and the original RoBERTa checkpoints. 

The pretraining setup for RoBERTa involved replicating its pretraining procedure while saving checkpoints at regular intervals. The authors utilized a large dataset comprising multiple corpora, totaling 205 GB of unprocessed text. This setup allowed for a detailed analysis of the knowledge acquired by the model over time.

The study focuses on the learning patterns exhibited by RoBERTa, particularly in relation to linguistic knowledge. The authors employ probing tasks to evaluate how effectively the model learns various types of knowledge, measuring the number of updates required to achieve a certain percentage of maximum performance. The results indicate that RoBERTa demonstrates rapid and stable learning of linguistic knowledge, with consistent performance across both class-based and behavioral probes.

Overall, the findings contribute to understanding the dynamics of knowledge acquisition in pre-trained language models, highlighting RoBERTa's capabilities and the implications of training data variations on performance.
