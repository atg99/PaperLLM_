The paper presents results from evaluating the performance of language models on various tasks related to factual and commonsense reasoning. Figures 15 and 16 illustrate the average performance across different domains and corpus sizes, respectively. 

In Figure 15, the performance metrics include accuracy on tasks such as Google RE, SQuAD, T-REx, and ConceptNet, with a noticeable increase in accuracy as the number of pretraining steps increases. The data indicates that larger corpora, such as NEWS+WIKI+STORIES, tend to yield better performance across tasks.

Figure 16 focuses on the commonsense reasoning capabilities of the models, showing how different corpus sizes impact accuracy on tasks like conjunction acceptability, Winograd, and HellaSWAG. The results are smoothed using an exponential moving average to provide clearer trends.

Overall, the findings highlight the significance of pretraining duration and corpus size on the performance of models in understanding and generating factual and commonsense knowledge.
