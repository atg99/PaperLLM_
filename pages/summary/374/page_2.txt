This paper discusses the concept of "probing across time" in the context of pretrained language models, particularly focusing on the RoBERTa model. The aim is to analyze how the knowledge encoded in these models evolves during the pretraining process. Unlike previous studies that evaluated models only after pretraining, this approach examines various checkpoints throughout the training trajectory to determine not only what knowledge is acquired but also when it is acquired.

The authors emphasize the importance of using a diverse set of probes to assess different types of knowledge encoded in the model. They categorize probes into two families: structural probes, which utilize a lightweight classifier to predict linguistic labels, and behavioral probes, which rely on the model's inherent capabilities without additional training. The paper incorporates various established probing tasks from publicly available packages to systematically evaluate linguistic, factual, commonsense, and reasoning knowledge.

Key components of the probe suite include LKT for linguistic knowledge, BLIMP for grammatical acceptability, LAMA for factual knowledge, CAT for commonsense reasoning, and OLM PICS for reasoning tasks. The study provides an overview of these tasks and their application, highlighting the methodology for assessing model performance through accuracy and F1 scores.

Overall, the research aims to provide insights into the temporal dynamics of knowledge acquisition in pretrained language models, contributing to a deeper understanding of their capabilities. The authors have made their code and pretraining checkpoints available for future research to facilitate further exploration in this area.
