The paper discusses various algorithms for clustering data streams, highlighting four main categories: refinement for concept drift (CluStream and DenStream), density-based algorithms using fixed or dynamic grid-cells (notably D-Stream), statistical model-based algorithms employing Expectation Maximization, and subspace clustering algorithms for high-dimensional data streams. A central challenge in stream clustering is selecting the appropriate parameter settings due to the dynamic nature of data streams, which complicates the application of systematic automated algorithm configurations. Future research should focus on benchmarking and configuring leading stream clustering algorithms to assess their strengths and weaknesses, particularly concerning computational complexity and clustering quality. The paper also emphasizes the need to compare real-life applications of these algorithms against traditional methods. Acknowledgments are given for support received, and references to relevant literature are provided for further exploration of the topic.
