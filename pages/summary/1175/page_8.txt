The paper discusses various clustering algorithms designed for dynamic data streams, focusing on adaptations and extensions of established methods like DBSCAN and BIRCH. It highlights the DenStream algorithm, which introduces temporal aspects to clustering by maintaining potential core micro-clusters and outlier micro-clusters, utilizing a time-faded clustering feature. The potential core micro-clusters are stable and decay over time, while outlier micro-clusters are unstable and tracked by their creation time.

DenStream initially employs DBSCAN to establish potential core micro-clusters, allowing new observations to be assigned based on proximity and weight thresholds. Regular evaluations of micro-cluster weights determine their status, promoting or demoting clusters as necessary.

Several extensions of DenStream are explored, including C-DenStream, which incorporates domain knowledge through instance-level constraints, and rDenStream, which temporarily stores outlier micro-clusters for later refinement. HDenStream adapts DenStream for categorical data, while E-Stream utilizes a histogram approach for data point binning and cluster management.

Further developments include HUE-Stream, which accommodates uncertain data streams, ClusTree, which integrates time-faded CFs into a tree structure for efficient processing, and LiarTree, which enhances noise handling within ClusTree. FlockStream introduces a nature-inspired flocking behavior to identify groupings in data, and LeaDen-Stream focuses on leader density-based clustering in evolving data contexts.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of managing temporal dynamics and uncertainty in clustering algorithms to improve their effectiveness in processing real-time data streams.
