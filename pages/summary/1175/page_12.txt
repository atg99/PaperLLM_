The paper discusses various algorithms for clustering data streams using grid-based methods. It begins with transitional grids that merge with neighboring dense cells, evaluating cell weights and types at regular intervals. Tu and Chen (2009) introduced a measure of attraction that relies on the positional information of data within grid cells, allowing for merging of neighboring cells only if they share many points at their borders.

DD-Stream (Jia et al. 2008) addresses points on grid boundaries by calculating distances to adjacent cell centers, assigning points to the closest cell, and using density to resolve ties. ExCC (Bhatnagar and Kaur 2007; Bhatnagar et al. 2014) allows user-defined grid configurations to handle categorical data, identifying clusters based on neighboring dense cells and employing similarity thresholds for categorical variables.

DCUStream (Yang et al. 2012) manages uncertain data streams by mapping observations to a grid and defining cell density based on existence probabilities, clustering dense cells together while treating neighboring sparse cells as cluster boundaries. DENGRIS-Stream (Amini et al. 2012) uses a sliding window model to maintain cell densities, decaying them relative to the total number of observations, and regularly removing cells that drop below a density threshold or exit the sliding window.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolution and optimization of density-based stream clustering algorithms, emphasizing their adaptability and efficiency in handling various types of data streams.
