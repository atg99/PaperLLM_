The paper discusses various time window models used in stream clustering, focusing on the damped time window, sliding time window, landmark time window, and pyramidal time model. The damped time window assigns weights to micro-clusters based on observations, with weights fading over time according to a decay factor. This process can be computationally intensive, leading to alternatives like updating weights at fixed intervals or upon cluster updates. The sliding time window employs a First-In-First-Out principle, adapting quickly to concept drift with a smaller window size or maintaining accuracy in stable streams with a larger size. The landmark time window captures data in chunks based on significant events, while the pyramidal time model summarizes recent data more accurately and aggregates older data progressively.

The paper also reviews related work in the field of stream clustering, highlighting significant surveys that categorize algorithms into distance-based and grid-based approaches. Amini et al. provide a structured overview of these methods, while other works, such as Silva et al. and Ghesmoune et al., expand on algorithm taxonomies and discuss various clustering techniques. This survey aims to cover a broader range of algorithms, totaling 51, and identifies four common research streams, enhancing the existing literature in stream clustering.
