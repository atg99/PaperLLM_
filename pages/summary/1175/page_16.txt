The paper discusses various algorithms for projected stream clustering, which are designed to handle high-dimensional data streams where prior feature selection is not feasible. The process involves iteratively updating clusters based on associated dimensions, with new data points being assigned to clusters if they do not exceed a specified radius threshold. Key algorithms mentioned include SiblingTree, which extends CellTree to accommodate subspace clusters, and HDDStream, which uses a density-based approach similar to DBSCAN to manage micro-clusters and outlier-clusters. PreDeConStream, developed concurrently with HDDStream, also employs the PreDeCon algorithm for initialization and adjusts clustering at regular intervals.

Projected stream clustering algorithms are noted for their complexity due to subspace selection but excel in identifying clusters in high-dimensional spaces while mitigating the curse of dimensionality. HPStream is highlighted as a significant algorithm in this category. 

The application of stream clustering is increasingly relevant in the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), where devices generate large volumes of data. Clustering can facilitate predictive maintenance by identifying devices likely to fail, enhance market segmentation by grouping customers with similar behaviors, and analyze conversational topics or detect fraudulent activities. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance and versatility of stream clustering in various domains.
