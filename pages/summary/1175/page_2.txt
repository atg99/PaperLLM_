The paper provides a comprehensive overview of stream clustering algorithms, focusing on their applicability to numerical data while excluding textual data due to differing methodologies. The authors identify the constraints inherent in stream clustering, including data availability, order, speed, volume, and limitations in memory and computational power. The main contribution of the survey is the extensive collection and categorization of 51 algorithms, organized into four groups: distance threshold-based algorithms, density-grid algorithms, statistical model-based algorithms, and those addressing high-dimensional data streams.

The paper discusses the challenges of parameter selection in stream clustering algorithms, proposing automatic algorithm configuration as a potential solution. It also includes a website that compiles resources related to stream clustering, such as datasets and algorithm implementations.

The methodological background section elaborates on data stream characteristics and clustering optimization objectives, emphasizing the goal of maximizing intra-cluster homogeneity and inter-cluster heterogeneity. The authors highlight various strategies for achieving these objectives under streaming constraints, such as similarity thresholds, grid partitioning, model fitting, and dimensionality reduction.

Overall, the paper serves as a detailed resource for understanding stream clustering algorithms, their development, and practical applications, concluding with a summary of key findings.
