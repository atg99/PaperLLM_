The paper discusses various clustering algorithms designed for data streams, focusing on the use of Clustering Features (CFs) to efficiently manage and process large datasets. It highlights the BIRCH algorithm and its extensions, such as Improved BIRCH and A-BIRCH, which adapt distance thresholds for better clustering performance. 

ScaleKM is introduced as an incremental algorithm that utilizes CFs to optimize clustering by summarizing data points and identifying micro-clusters through k-means and hierarchical clustering. Single pass k-means simplifies this approach by retaining only k CFs.

CluStream extends CFs to allow clustering over different time horizons, using a pyramidal scheme to maintain snapshots of CFs, enabling the algorithm to manage both current and historical data effectively. HCluStream further adapts CluStream for categorical data, introducing a specific distance measure for categorical attributes.

SWClustering builds on CluStream's concepts, employing an Exponential Histogram of Cluster Features (EHCF) to manage data with varying granularity, ensuring that recent observations are stored individually while older data is summarized. SDStream combines EHCF with DenStream to represent core and outlier micro-clusters, enforcing limits on the number of micro-clusters through merging and deletion strategies.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution of clustering algorithms in handling dynamic and large-scale data streams, highlighting their efficiency and adaptability in processing complex datasets.
