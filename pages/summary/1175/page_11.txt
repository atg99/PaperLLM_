The paper discusses various approaches to stream clustering, highlighting the challenges and limitations of widely used algorithms. Traditional distance-based methods, while popular, often require extensive parameter tuning and are limited to identifying spherical clusters. The authors suggest that newer algorithms like DBSTREAM offer simpler designs and improved functionality.

A significant alternative to distance-based clustering is grid-based approaches, which organize data into grid cells to estimate density. The paper presents several grid-based algorithms, with D-Stream being noted as the most influential. D-Stream categorizes grid cells into dense, sparse, and transitional types, and employs a fixed grid structure to manage data points and clusters effectively.

One notable algorithm, DUCstream, initializes clustering by partitioning the data space into fixed-size grid cells and incrementally updates clusters as new data is processed. The paper also provides an overview of various density-based stream clustering algorithms, detailing their characteristics, such as time window models and grid partitioning strategies, emphasizing the evolution of these methods over time.
