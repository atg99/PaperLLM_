The paper discusses various clustering algorithms for data streams, focusing on grid-based and hybrid approaches. 

Fractal Clustering, introduced by Barbara and Chen, employs a grid-based method using fractal dimensions to assess the size of point sets. It initializes clusters by recursively grouping close points and evaluates the impact of new observations on existing clusters based on fractal dimension changes. If the change is excessive, the point is classified as noise.

Recursive Partitioning techniques, such as Stats-Grid and its extension Cell-Tree by Park and Lee, partition data into grid-cells of fixed size, maintaining density and statistical measures. Stats-Grid recursively divides cells until they become unit cells to form macro-clusters, while Cell-Tree uses a predefined number of evenly sized subcells, employing a siblings list for efficient management.

MR-Stream, developed by Wan et al., utilizes a binary tree structure to recursively assign new observations to child-cells, pruning less relevant nodes to streamline the clustering process. PKSStream builds on MR-Stream but maintains intermediate nodes only when necessary, optimizing memory usage.

Hybrid approaches like HDCStream integrate grid-based and distance-based clustering methods, allowing dense grid cells to evolve into micro-clusters, thereby enhancing the clustering process for data streams.

Overall, the paper outlines advancements in clustering methodologies, highlighting their mechanisms and efficiencies in handling dynamic data environments.
