The paper discusses strategies for maintaining clusters over time in data streams without storing individual observations. Key methods include using synopsis data structures for calculating cluster location and radius, maintaining centroids, and employing competitive learning strategies to update cluster centers. An overview of 28 distance-based stream clustering algorithms is provided, highlighting the influence of foundational algorithms like BIRCH, CluStream, and DenStream.

BIRCH, introduced by Zhang et al., is one of the earliest algorithms for stream clustering. It summarizes cluster information using Clustering Features (CFs), which consist of three components: the number of data points (n), a linear sum vector (LS), and a sum of squares scalar (SS). These components enable the calculation of the centroid and radius of clusters. BIRCH incrementally builds a balanced tree structure to efficiently maintain CFs, allowing for easy updates and merging of CFs. The paper also includes figures illustrating the relationships between algorithms and the structure of the CF tree.
