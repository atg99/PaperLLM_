The paper discusses the evolution and challenges of stream clustering algorithms, highlighting a total of 51 algorithms developed from 1987 to 2018, with 26 implementations and three frameworks. A key focus is on the configuration of these algorithms, proposing automatic configuration as a solution to enhance usability. Prior empirical comparisons indicated that DBSTREAM yields the highest cluster quality, although it is sensitive to insertion order and complex in parameter tuning. D-Stream is noted as a competitive alternative, albeit requiring more micro-clusters due to its grid-based nature.

The paper further categorizes distance-based stream clustering methods, which generally evaluate the distance of new observations to existing micro-clusters to decide on insertion or cluster initiation. A table summarizes various distance-based algorithms, detailing their time window models and offline clustering methods, showcasing the diversity and innovations in stream clustering approaches over the years. The authors emphasize the need for improved algorithm configuration and understanding of distance-based methodologies to enhance practical applications in data stream clustering.
