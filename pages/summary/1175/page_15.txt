The paper discusses various algorithms for clustering data streams, focusing on their methodologies and characteristics. It begins by outlining the decision-making processes in clustering, which include inserting new data points into existing clusters, creating new clusters, merging clusters, and splitting existing ones. The effectiveness of these decisions is evaluated using Category Utility (CU), which balances intra-class similarity and inter-class distance.

ICFR (Incremental Clustering using F-value by Regression analysis) utilizes concepts from linear regression to cluster data streams, assigning points based on cosine similarity and merging clusters if their combined F-value exceeds the sum of individual F-values. WStream employs multivariate kernel density estimates to manage data windows, adjusting their size based on the density of points. SVStream, based on Support Vector Clustering, processes data in chunks, maintaining spheres that encapsulate clusters and updating them as new data arrives.

The paper notes that model-based stream clustering algorithms are less common and often more complex than distance-based and grid-based approaches. It highlights the lack of available implementations for these algorithms, which limits their practical use.

In addressing high-dimensional data streams, the paper introduces projected clustering algorithms that mitigate the curse of dimensionality. HPStream is presented as an extension of CluStream for high-dimensional data, utilizing a time-faded CF and a bit vector for dimension association. The overview of projected clustering algorithms includes HPStream, SiblingTree, HDDStream, and PreDeConStream, each with unique approaches to handling high-dimensional data. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for effective algorithms to manage the challenges posed by data streams, particularly in high-dimensional settings, while also noting the limitations of current model-based approaches.
