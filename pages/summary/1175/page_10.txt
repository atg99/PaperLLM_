The paper discusses methods for identifying new and drifting clusters in data streams, primarily using k-means clustering. Initially, k-means generates clusters, and the distance from each cluster center to its furthest point defines the cluster's boundary. Points outside these boundaries are classified as unknown and stored in a buffer for further analysis. Regular scans of this buffer using k-means help detect emerging structures, determining if they are valid based on their variance relative to existing clusters. Distinguishing between new and drifting clusters relies on the assumption that drifts occur near existing clusters, while new clusters are formed farther away.

The paper also explores competitive learning strategies for adapting cluster centroids over time. Inspired by Self-Organizing Maps, algorithms like SOStream and DBSTREAM utilize density-based approaches to manage clusters in data streams. SOStream merges new observations into the nearest cluster if they fall within its radius, while DBSTREAM focuses on shared density between micro-clusters to classify them as part of the same macro-cluster, updating their centers as necessary.

evoStream introduces an evolutionary algorithm to enhance clustering by iteratively improving macro-clusters, allowing for real-time updates without significant computational overhead. G-Stream employs a neural gas model, where clusters are represented as nodes in a graph, adapting connections based on the proximity of data points.

In summary, distance-based algorithms dominate stream clustering due to their efficiency and ability to create accurate summaries without storing all observations. They typically summarize cluster information, such as location and radius, while some maintain medoids or centroids. The incorporation of competitive learning strategies enhances adaptability, making these algorithms computationally efficient for real-time applications.
