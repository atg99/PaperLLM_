This paper discusses the integration of edge computing and cognitive computing to form a new paradigm called Edge Cognitive Computing (ECC). It highlights the advantages of edge computing, such as reduced traffic load on backhaul networks and improved control over data management and processing through localized computing capabilities. This is particularly beneficial for real-time applications, like online gaming, where low latency is crucial.

However, the paper also addresses the limitations of edge computing, including the restricted operational capabilities of edge devices and the lack of intelligence in current edge services, which primarily rely on AI for data storage and processing without incorporating advanced features like load balancing and network optimization.

Cognitive computing, which draws from cognitive scientific theory, aims to achieve machine-like intelligence through interactive recognition processes involving machines, cyberspace, and humans. It enhances machines' capabilities to analyze existing data, reinterpret information, and generate new insights, thereby providing more intelligent cognitive services. Despite its potential, cognitive computing has largely relied on cloud-based machine learning models, leading to latency issues in service delivery.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose the ECC architecture, which combines the strengths of edge and cognitive computing. This architecture enables dynamic and elastic storage and computing services at the network edge, facilitating personalized, human-centered cognitive intelligence. The paper outlines the design issues related to merging cognitive and edge computing technologies, aiming to enhance the efficiency and responsiveness of network services.

In summary, the paper presents ECC as a promising solution to address the limitations of both edge and cognitive computing, advocating for a more integrated approach to improve data processing and service delivery in edge networks.
