The paper presents a dynamic service migration mechanism using a Q-learning algorithm to optimize agent decision-making in edge computing environments. The Q-value matrix captures the expected benefits of state-action pairs, allowing the agent to move from an initial state to an optimal final state by updating its knowledge based on immediate rewards received during interactions with the environment. The learning process involves adjusting Q-values with a learning rate and a discount factor to prioritize recent rewards.

In the experimental evaluation, an edge computing testbed was created to assess the performance of the proposed architecture, which includes emotion detection and video streaming functionalities. The setup utilized four edge nodes and two servers, with a focus on comparing the proposed scheme against two alternatives: a no-migration scheme and a nearest migration scheme. Various task loads were tested, and the results demonstrated the convergence performance of the proposed deep reinforcement learning approach, indicating significant improvements in total utility when compared to the other schemes. The findings highlight the effectiveness of the dynamic service migration mechanism in enhancing user mobility and overall system performance.
