The paper proposes an ECC-based dynamic cognitive service-migration mechanism that addresses the elastic allocation of cognitive computing services in relation to user mobility. This mobility-aware dynamic service adjustment scheme aims to enhance service delivery based on varying user demands. An ECC-based test platform is developed to evaluate the mechanism through several experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness in providing dynamic services.

The paper is structured as follows: Section II reviews related work, Section III details the proposed edge cognitive computing architecture, Section IV discusses design issues, Section V presents the dynamic cognitive service migration mechanism, and Section VI showcases the ECC test platform. Finally, Section VII concludes the paper and highlights some open issues.

In the related work section, the need for on-demand services and the latency challenges associated with edge computing are discussed. Research emphasizes the importance of cooperation between cloud and edge devices to reduce energy consumption while maintaining Quality of Service (QoS). However, frequent migrations between edge devices and cloud servers can lead to network congestion. Previous studies have primarily focused on communication issues, such as network efficiency and transmission delays, without addressing the personalization of AI applications or the provision of elastic storage and computing services.

The application of cognitive computing in smart cities is explored, highlighting the role of intelligent algorithms like machine learning and data analytics in addressing big data challenges. Despite significant advancements in algorithm design, the paper argues that for large-scale deployment of cognitive computing, a comprehensive approach that includes service migration mechanisms is essential.
