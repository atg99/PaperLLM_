The paper discusses the development and performance evaluation of an edge computing architecture designed to enhance the efficiency of service delivery, particularly in the context of emotion detection applications. Key concepts include the establishment of connectivity among edge nodes, the allocation of resources based on user movement, and the application of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms to optimize service resolution.

The experimental setup consists of hardware and software interfaces, with specific parameters established for bandwidth, CPU cycles, content size, learning rate, and discount factor. The findings indicate that as the number of episodes in the learning process increases, total utility stabilizes at around 400 for high-resolution services, while low-resolution services yield lower rewards initially.

The paper also highlights the relationship between response time and the number of concurrent requests, revealing that the proposed RL-based scheme demonstrates superior performance with reduced latency compared to other methods. The results suggest that low-resolution services should be migrated first when users switch access points, followed by high-resolution services once a stable connection is established. Overall, the study emphasizes the advantages of a well-structured edge computing framework in delivering timely and efficient services.
