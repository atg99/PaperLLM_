This paper addresses the challenge of speech emotion recognition (SER), which is essential for enhancing human-machine interaction. The authors highlight a limitation of traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in SER, specifically their inability to capture spatial relationships in spectrogram features. To tackle this issue, the paper proposes a novel architecture based on capsule networks (CapsNets), which effectively accounts for the spatial relationships of speech features.

The proposed CapsNet model incorporates a recurrent connection to enhance temporal sensitivity, enabling it to process utterance-level features more effectively. The authors compare their model against existing CNN-long short-term memory (CNN-LSTM) systems using the IEMOCAP benchmark corpus, focusing on four emotions: neutral, angry, happy, and sad. Results indicate that the CapsNet model outperforms the baseline, achieving a weighted accuracy of 72.73% compared to 68.8% and an unweighted accuracy of 59.71% versus 59.4%. This demonstrates the potential of CapsNets in improving SER performance by better capturing spatial and temporal features essential for emotion classification.

The paper emphasizes the importance of extracting both detailed local information and global characteristics for accurate emotion recognition, contributing to advancements in intelligent human-machine interaction.
