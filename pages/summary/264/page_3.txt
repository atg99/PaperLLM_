The paper presents a novel architecture for emotion recognition in speech, termed CNN GRU-SeqCap, which integrates Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) with Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) and Capsule Networks (CapsNets). The proposed system architecture includes multiple layers, such as max pooling and convolutional layers, designed to extract relevant features from spectrogram inputs. 

Key components of the architecture are:

1. **Feature Extraction**: The system uses two CNN layers with different kernel sizes to capture spatial relationships in spectrograms, followed by additional convolutional and pooling layers to refine the feature representation.

2. **Recurrent Capsules (RecCaps)**: To enhance temporal information capture, recurrent connections are introduced in the routing algorithm of capsules. This allows the model to utilize information from previous time windows to improve emotion prediction.

3. **Bidirectional GRU**: The GRU layer processes the output from the CNN layers in both forward and backward directions, allowing for a comprehensive understanding of sequential data.

4. **Capsule Networks**: The architecture further incorporates SeqCaps, where outputs are reshaped into emo-vectors that contain class vectors and their lengths. This structure is designed to improve the model's ability to recognize emotional states.

5. **Combined Architecture**: The CNN GRU-SeqCap model combines outputs from both the capsule and GRU branches, facilitating a more robust emotion recognition capability. The losses from both branches are summed to optimize the overall system performance.

The experimental evaluation is conducted on the IEMOCAP dataset, focusing on four emotions: Neutral, Angry, Happy, and Sad. The study employs five-fold cross-validation, utilizing improvised speech data to mitigate biases from scripted content. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed architecture in capturing both spatial and temporal cues for emotion recognition in speech.
