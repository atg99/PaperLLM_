The paper introduces a novel filter pruning method for deep neural networks, termed Scientifically Controlled Pruning (SCOP). This method leverages knockoff data, which are synthetic features mirroring the distribution of real features but are independent of true labels, to identify and eliminate redundant filters in pre-trained networks. The fundamental hypothesis is that filters that exhibit a greater importance for knockoff features—indicated by larger scaling factors—are deemed unnecessary and can be pruned without compromising network performance.

The paper critiques existing filter pruning approaches, highlighting their reliance on various potentially misleading metrics for filter importance, such as norms or geometric medians. These methods can be sensitive to data variations, leading to unstable pruning outcomes. In contrast, the proposed method aims to mitigate disturbances from irrelevant factors by employing knockoff data as a control mechanism.

The authors provide theoretical justification for the use of knockoff features, demonstrating that they serve as valid counterparts to real data features during training. The methodology involves generating knockoff data, integrating it with the original dataset, and applying learnable scaling factors to guide the pruning process.

Experimental validation shows that the SCOP method not only effectively identifies redundant filters but also achieves superior performance and compression ratios compared to existing state-of-the-art pruning techniques. Overall, the approach presents a systematic way to enhance the efficiency of neural networks while maintaining high performance.
