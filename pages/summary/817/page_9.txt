The paper introduces a novel network pruning method called SCOP, which enhances the reliability of neural network pruning by utilizing knockoff features as a control group. These knockoff features, generated to have a similar distribution to real features but lacking ground-truth label information, help mitigate the influence of irrelevant factors during the pruning process. The method evaluates the importance of each filter based on its performance with both real and knockoff features, removing filters that focus more on knockoffs, thereby creating more compact neural networks.

The experimental results indicate that SCOP outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, achieving a reduction of 57.8% in parameters and 60.2% in FLOPs for ResNet-101 with only a 0.01% loss in top-1 accuracy on ImageNet. The broader impact of this work lies in its potential to facilitate the deployment of deep neural networks on resource-constrained devices, thereby making artificial intelligence more accessible in everyday applications. Future research will aim to extend the concept of scientific control to other deep learning challenges, including neural architecture search.
