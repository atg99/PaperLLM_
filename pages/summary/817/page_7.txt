The paper presents a comparison of pruned ResNet models on the ImageNet dataset, highlighting the performance of their proposed pruning methods, SCOP-A and SCOP-B, against several state-of-the-art (SOTA) filter pruning techniques. The results, summarized in Table 2, show the top-1 and top-5 error rates for both original and pruned networks, alongside the reduction percentages in parameters and FLOPs.

Key findings indicate that SCOP methods achieve lower top-1 error rates compared to traditional methods like SFP, FPGM, and Taylor, while also managing to prune a greater percentage of FLOPs. For instance, SCOP-B on ResNet-50 results in a top-1 error of 24.74% with a 54.6% reduction in FLOPs, outperforming Taylor's method, which has a top-1 error of 25.50% and a 44.9% reduction in FLOPs. This demonstrates that the SCOP method effectively identifies and removes redundant filters more accurately.

The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of SCOP in maintaining performance while achieving significant model compression. The experimental results suggest that this method could be beneficial for deploying deep learning models in resource-constrained environments, as it balances the trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency.
