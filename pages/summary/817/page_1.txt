The paper presents SCOP, a novel neural network pruning algorithm that incorporates a scientific control to enhance the reliability of pruning results. Traditional pruning methods often rely on various hypotheses to assess the importance of filters, which can lead to unreliable outcomes. To address this, the authors propose a rigorous research design that integrates a control group, utilizing a knockoff feature that mimics the feature map produced by the network filter but remains conditionally independent of the example label.

The theoretical framework suggests that this knockoff condition can be maintained through information propagation across network layers. By introducing the corresponding knockoff feature as an auxiliary input for subsequent layers, the method can identify redundant filters through an adversarial process involving different features.

Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of SCOP, achieving a significant reduction of 57.8% in parameters and 60.2% in FLOPs for ResNet-101, while maintaining only a 0.01% loss in top-1 accuracy on the ImageNet dataset. The proposed approach outperforms existing state-of-the-art pruning techniques, highlighting its practical applicability for deploying CNNs on resource-constrained edge devices.

The code for SCOP is publicly available, promoting further research and application in the field of neural network compression.
