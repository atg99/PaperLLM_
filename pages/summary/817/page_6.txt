The paper presents a new filter pruning method called SCOP, which is evaluated against various state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods on the CIFAR-10 dataset. The results are summarized in Table 1, which compares the test errors, parameter reductions, and FLOPs reductions of pruned networks. The models used include ResNet-20, ResNet-32, ResNet-56, and MobileNetV2, with the SCOP method consistently achieving lower test errors compared to other pruning methods like SFP, FPGM, and HRank while also reducing a greater percentage of parameters and FLOPs.

For instance, SCOP on ResNet-56 results in a test error of 6.30% with a 56.0% reduction in FLOPs, demonstrating its effectiveness in maintaining performance while optimizing network size. The paper details the experimental setup, including the training configurations for both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets, and emphasizes the uniform pruning rate applied across layers for fair comparisons.

Overall, the findings suggest that SCOP is a promising approach for network pruning, offering a balance between efficiency and accuracy.
