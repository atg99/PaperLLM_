The paper discusses methods for generating knockoff features in the context of neural network pruning. Knockoff features are constructed using a generator G, where ~Al = G(Al), and several approaches for generating these features are highlighted, including methods by Candes et al. and Jordon et al. While these methods are effective, they face challenges when applied to each layer of a deep neural network.

To address this, the authors propose a more efficient approach by analyzing the information flow within the neural network. They theoretically establish that the knockoff condition can be approximately preserved across the network, allowing for the generation of knockoff features solely from the input data. The neural network components are categorized into nonlinear activation layers (e.g., ReLU, Sigmoid) and linear transformation layers (e.g., fully connected and convolutional layers).

For activation layers, the authors present Lemma 1, which states that if ~Al is the knockoff of Al, then the output features after applying an activation function remain knockoffs. This is because element-wise operations do not alter the dependencies between elements, thus preserving the exchangeability and independence properties.

In linear transformation layers, represented by the transformation t(vl) = vlW, the authors introduce Lemma 2. This lemma asserts that if ~vl is the second-order knockoff of vl, the resulting output after the transformation also remains a second-order knockoff. The proof involves checking the equality of moments (expectation and covariance) of the distributions of the original and knockoff features.

Overall, the findings suggest that the knockoff condition can be approximately maintained through both activation and linear transformation layers, enabling efficient generation of knockoff features for neural network pruning. The authors also indicate that they will explore the impact of biases in their empirical studies.
