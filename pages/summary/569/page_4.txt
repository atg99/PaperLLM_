Clathrin is a crucial adaptor protein involved in the vesicle-coating complex, facilitating membrane cleavage to release invaginated vesicles from the plasma membrane. It has a triskelion structure composed of three clathrin heavy chains and three light chains, playing a significant role in vesicular transport and membrane trafficking. Clathrin-coated vesicles are responsible for selectively transporting cargo at the cell membrane, trans-Golgi network, and endosomal compartments, with the coat disassembling post-sprouting to allow for clathrin reuse during transport to various sub-locations. Extracellular molecules, including proteins, membrane receptors, and ion channels, utilize clathrin for uptake, highlighting its role in the cellular intake of DNA-chitosan nanoparticles and involvement in cholesterol-rich pathways like caveolae-mediated transport. The absence of functional clathrins has been linked to various human diseases, including cancer and neurodegenerative disorders, prompting extensive research into clathrin proteins. Recent studies have employed various biological techniques to identify clathrin proteins, including the Tom1-Tollip complex and agarose gel electrophoresis, while new clathrins such as assembly protein AP180 and γ2-adaptin have also been discovered.
