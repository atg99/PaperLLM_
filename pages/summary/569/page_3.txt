In this study, the authors developed a model utilizing a 2D convolutional neural network (CNN) to identify clathrin proteins, focusing on enhancing model performance through parameter adjustments and hyper-parameter optimization based on 10-fold cross-validation. The model demonstrated strong predictive capabilities on an independent dataset, achieving a sensitivity of 92.2%, specificity of 91.2%, accuracy of 91.8%, and a Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) of 0.83. These results indicate a significant improvement over traditional neural networks in identifying clathrin proteins. The authors highlight the potential of deep learning in biomedical research and provide their source codes and dataset for public access at the specified GitHub link. Key concepts include clathrin-coated pits, convolutional neural networks, and molecular function.
