The paper discusses an efficient framework for identifying clathrin proteins using a 2D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and Position-Specific Scoring Matrix (PSSM) profiles. The framework involves four key procedures: data collection, feature extraction, CNN generation, and model evaluation. 

In the data collection phase, the authors retrieved clathrin protein sequences from the NCBI database, specifically from the UniProt resource, using the keyword "clathrin." They aimed to create a binary classification model distinguishing clathrin proteins from general proteins, necessitating the collection of a negative dataset with similar functions and structures. Vesicular transport proteins were chosen as the negative dataset to enhance model precision.

The authors highlight the importance of removing redundant sequences to improve model performance. They opted for a stringent cut-off of 100% similarity to ensure that the dataset was sufficiently diverse for deep learning applications. This approach allowed for the effective generation of hidden information within the protein sequences, utilizing BLAST for clustering and sequence management. 

Overall, the study emphasizes the methodological advancements in protein function prediction through the application of deep learning techniques, specifically in the context of clathrin proteins.
