The study outlines a systematic approach to predicting clathrin proteins, distinguishing between clathrin ("Positive") and non-clathrin proteins ("Negative"). The methodology involves fitting training data to the model, followed by performance assessment on a validation dataset, and iterating through various hyperparameters to optimize model performance. A 10-fold cross-validation technique was employed to train the model, ensuring robust evaluation. The final performance was measured using an independent dataset to assess the model's predictive ability. Key evaluation metrics included sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, and Matthews's correlation coefficient (MCC), calculated based on true positives, false positives, true negatives, and false negatives. This structured approach highlights the importance of rigorous testing and validation in predictive modeling.
