This study investigates the effectiveness of various machine learning classifiers in identifying clathrin proteins, specifically comparing the performance of a 2D CNN against traditional classifiers such as kNN, Random Forest, SVM, MLP, and 1D CNN. All classifiers were evaluated using optimal parameters to ensure a fair comparison, with results indicating that the 2D CNN significantly outperformed the other methods, particularly with independent datasets.

The research provides a computational model for classifying the molecular functions of clathrin proteins, offering biologists access to source codes and data for reproducibility. This work addresses a gap in the literature, as prior studies primarily focused on biological experiments for clathrin identification. It represents the first computational analysis of this dataset, contributing valuable insights into the molecular functions of clathrins and their potential implications in drug target design for human diseases.

The study also emphasizes the development of an advanced deep learning architecture that optimizes performance through hyper-parameter tuning, enhancing the analysis of protein sequences. Overall, the findings underscore the utility of machine learning techniques in biological research, particularly in understanding clathrin's role in vesicular transport systems.
