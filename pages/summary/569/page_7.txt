The architecture of the 2D convolutional neural network utilized in this study is designed to process the extracted PSSM profiles, allowing for the identification of clathrin and non-clathrin proteins. The network employs multiple layers, including convolutional layers, pooling layers, and fully connected layers, to learn patterns within the data.

The dataset was divided into cross-validation and independent sets to ensure the robustness of the model. The cross-validation set comprised 1288 clathrins and 1133 non-clathrins, while the independent set included 258 clathrins and 227 non-clathrins. The feature extraction process involved converting protein secondary structures into PSSM profiles, which were then used as input for the neural network.

Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of using PSSM matrices and 2D CNNs for the classification of protein sequences, particularly in distinguishing clathrin proteins from non-clathrins, thereby contributing to advancements in bioinformatics and protein analysis.
