The study investigates the interaction between mannose-binding lectin (MBL) and the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. It demonstrates that MBL can specifically bind to the spike protein due to its glycosylation, particularly at oligomannose-type glycans. The research employed competition assays with d-mannose and N-acetyl-glucosamine, confirming that these ligands inhibit MBL binding to the spike protein, indicating a Ca2+-dependent interaction.

Analysis of the spike protein's glycosylation revealed potential binding sites for MBL, specifically at N603, N801, and N1074, which are located across the S1 and S2 regions of the spike protein. This suggests that the glycosylation state is crucial for MBL's interaction with the spike protein, potentially impacting the inhibition mechanism.

Furthermore, the study explored whether MBL could recognize spike proteins from variants of concern (VoC), suggesting that mutations might influence this interaction. The data collectively highlight the importance of glycosylation in the spike protein's recognition by MBL and its potential implications for immune responses against SARS-CoV-2.
