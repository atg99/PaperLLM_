The study investigates the interaction between various proteins and SARS-CoV-2, focusing on the role of mannose-binding lectin (MBL) in complement activation and its potential antiviral effects. The authors conducted binding experiments with recombinant ficolin-2, surfactant protein D, and collectin-11, finding no interactions with viral proteins. The research underlines the significance of the lectin pathway in SARS-CoV-2 recognition, confirming that mutations in variants like Omicron do not impact glycosylation sites recognized by MBL. MBL demonstrated antiviral activity, with effective concentrations aligning with levels found in healthy individuals, suggesting its importance in immune response.

The study also explored the genetic variations in the MBL2 gene and their association with COVID-19 severity. While previous studies indicated a correlation, this research found only a borderline association with one haplotype, but significant predisposition linked to biallelic functional variants. The findings were supported by a meta-analysis of over 113,000 individuals, indicating that certain genetic variants may influence susceptibility to severe COVID-19. The association analysis results presented detailed data on single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) related to MBL levels, emphasizing the complex interplay between genetics and disease severity.
