This study investigates the role of Mannose-Binding Lectin (MBL) in inhibiting SARS-CoV-2 infection and chemokine production in cultured cells. The research focuses on two variants of the virus, D614G and B.1.1.7 (α), using Calu-3 cells. MBL was tested at various concentrations (0.01 to 10 µg/mL) either preincubated with the virus or with both the virus and cells. The infectivity of SARS-CoV-2 was assessed through plaque-forming assays after 48 and 72 hours.

Results indicated significant inhibition of viral infectivity with MBL, with statistical significance noted (P values ranging from 0.0011 to less than 0.001 across different conditions). Additionally, the study examined the production of SARS-CoV-2 at the apical surface of human bronchial epithelial cells (HBEC) in the presence of PTX3, and the influence of MBL on chemokine production in infected HBEC. Statistical analyses employed included two-way and one-way ANOVA, with Bonferroni's multiple comparison test for further validation of results. 

Overall, the findings suggest that MBL may play a crucial role in modulating viral infectivity and the inflammatory response during SARS-CoV-2 infection.
