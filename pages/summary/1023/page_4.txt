The paper investigates the interaction between mannose-binding lectin (MBL) and the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2, particularly focusing on glycosylation sites and the potential for MBL to activate the complement lectin pathway and inhibit viral entry. It identifies 22 N-linked glycosylation sites on the spike protein and notes that mutations in variants of concern (VoC), including Omicron, do not alter these sites, allowing MBL to bind with similar affinity across variants. Binding assays confirmed that MBL effectively interacted with spike proteins from various VoC.

The study further explores the role of MBL in activating the complement lectin pathway through a series of serum incubations, demonstrating that MBL-mediated complement activation leads to deposition of the membrane attack complex (C5b-9). Importantly, MBL was shown to inhibit SARS-CoV-2 entry into susceptible cell lines, reducing viral entry significantly and showing a concentration-dependent effect.

Additionally, MBL's antiviral activity was confirmed in lung epithelial models, where it decreased viral production significantly after infection. The study also highlights that MBL retains its effectiveness against multiple variants of SARS-CoV-2, indicating its potential as a therapeutic agent in mitigating COVID-19 infections. Overall, the findings suggest that MBL plays a crucial role in the immune response against SARS-CoV-2 through both complement activation and direct viral inhibition.
