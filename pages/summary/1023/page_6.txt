This study investigates the role of Mannose-Binding Lectin (MBL) in inhibiting SARS-CoV-2 infection and its association with severe COVID-19. MBL has been shown to inhibit SARS-CoV-2 in human lung epithelial cells and reduce inflammatory responses, colocalizing with the virus's spike protein. The MBL2 gene, which encodes MBL, has polymorphic variants linked to protein levels and susceptibility to infections.

In a cohort of 332 COVID-19 patients and 1,668 healthy individuals, the study focused on six SNPs associated with MBL levels. Although a significant difference was noted in the rs5030737-A allele frequency between infected and healthy groups, this finding did not survive multiple testing corrections. Analysis of haplotypes revealed that the CCGGCC haplotype was less frequent in severe COVID-19 cases, suggesting a protective effect.

Further investigations into a larger genomic region identified three SNPs with suggestive associations with disease severity. The findings indicate a complex relationship between MBL2 genetic variants and COVID-19 outcomes, highlighting the potential for MBL as both a therapeutic target and a biomarker for severe disease.
