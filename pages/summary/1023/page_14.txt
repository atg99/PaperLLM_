This paper details the methodologies employed in studying the interactions between SARS-CoV-2 and various human cell lines, including 293T and Calu-3 cells, as well as primary human bronchial epithelial cells (HBECs). The 293T cells were genetically modified to express the ACE2 receptor, facilitating entry assays with SARS-CoV-2 spike-pseudotyped lentivirus particles. The Calu-3 cell line was cultured in complete medium and used for infection assays to evaluate the effects of mannose-binding lectin (MBL) on viral infection.

Primary HBECs were isolated from lung transplant patients, with ethical approval and informed consent obtained. These cells were differentiated under air-liquid interface conditions to study viral interactions. Various SARS-CoV-2 isolates, including different lineages (Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Delta), were utilized, with their isolation and use approved by relevant institutional review boards.

Infection experiments involved incubating cells with MBL and SARS-CoV-2, followed by viral titer determination through plaque-forming assays. Chemokines IL-8 and CXCL5 were quantified from the culture medium using ELISA. Advanced imaging techniques, including confocal and STED super-resolution microscopy, were employed to analyze cellular responses and protein interactions.

The study also incorporated genetic analysis, including SNP imputation and principal component analysis, to investigate genetic factors associated with MBL and its plasma concentrations in patients. Blood samples were collected for MBL concentration measurement using ELISA, ensuring that results were derived from distinct samples with appropriate controls.

Overall, the research provides insights into the cellular and molecular responses to SARS-CoV-2, with a focus on the role of MBL and the genetic underpinnings that may influence susceptibility to infection.
