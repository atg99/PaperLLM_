The referenced articles explore the interactions between SARS-CoV-2 and various host factors, particularly focusing on the role of lectins and the complement system in viral infection and immune response. Zhou et al. highlight how a specific glycosylation site on the SARS-CoV spike protein can impact its interaction with mannose-binding lectin. Lu et al. demonstrate that SARS-CoV-2 enhances inflammatory responses in myeloid cells via C-type lectin receptors. Lempp et al. discuss how lectins can facilitate SARS-CoV-2 infection and affect neutralizing antibody responses.

Chiodo et al. identify ACE2-independent binding of the virus to host lectins, suggesting alternative entry mechanisms. Ali et al. show that the lectin pathway mediates complement activation by SARS-CoV-2 proteins, while Hsieh et al. reveal that human surfactant protein D binds to the spike protein, inhibiting viral entry. Several studies investigate genetic polymorphisms in mannose-binding lectin and their association with susceptibility to severe COVID-19 outcomes.

The implications of complement activation in COVID-19 are further explored by Risitano et al. and Carvelli et al., who suggest that targeting complement pathways may be beneficial in managing COVID-19-related inflammation. The dual role of type I interferons in the context of SARS-CoV-2 is examined by King and Sprent, indicating their complex involvement in inflammatory responses. The body of work collectively underscores the importance of lectins and the complement system in understanding SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis and potential therapeutic strategies.
