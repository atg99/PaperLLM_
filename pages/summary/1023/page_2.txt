The study investigates the role of human humoral pattern recognition molecules (PRMs) in recognizing SARS-CoV-2, focusing on pentraxin 3 (PTX3) and mannose-binding lectin (MBL). PTX3 was found to specifically bind to the nucleocapsid protein of SARS-CoV-2, while MBL interacted with the spike protein of the virus. Notably, MBL demonstrated antiviral activity and activated the complement lectin pathway. Genetic variations in the MBL2 locus were linked to disease severity, highlighting the importance of these PRMs in COVID-19 resistance and pathogenesis.

The research utilized solid-phase binding assays to assess interactions between recombinant human PRMs and SARS-CoV-2 proteins. While PTX3 exhibited a dose-dependent binding to the nucleocapsid protein, no binding was observed for other pentraxins like CRP or SAP. MBL specifically bound to the trimeric form of the spike protein but not to its individual subunits, indicating the necessity of a native structure for recognition. Surface plasmon resonance analysis confirmed the strong interaction between MBL and the spike protein.

Further experiments with pseudotyped lentivirus demonstrated MBL's ability to bind viral particles, reinforcing its role in the immune response against SARS-CoV-2. The findings suggest that selected fluid-phase PRMs are crucial in the immune resistance to COVID-19 and have potential translational implications for therapeutic strategies.
