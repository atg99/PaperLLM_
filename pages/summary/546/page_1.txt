The paper by Joseph Mellor et al. addresses the challenges associated with the time-consuming and resource-intensive process of designing deep neural networks. The authors propose a novel approach to Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that eliminates the need for training candidate networks, significantly speeding up the search process. Traditional NAS methods require extensive computational resources, often training numerous architectures to evaluate their performance, which can be prohibitively slow.

The authors explore the relationship between the overlap of activations in untrained networks and their eventual trained performance. They introduce a measure derived from the Hamming distance between binary codes representing the activation patterns of rectified linear units in the network. This measure, referred to as KH, serves as an indicator of the network's potential effectiveness.

The proposed algorithm allows for rapid search and evaluation of neural architectures without any training, achieving results in seconds on a single GPU. The authors validate their method on several benchmark datasets, including NAS-Bench-101, NAS-Bench-201, and NATS-Bench, demonstrating its efficacy. Furthermore, the approach can be integrated with more complex search methodologies, such as regularized evolutionary search, enhancing its applicability.

The findings suggest that it is feasible to conduct effective NAS without the traditional training phase, offering a promising avenue for future research and practical applications in architecture design. The authors provide access to their code for reproducibility, facilitating further exploration of their approach.
