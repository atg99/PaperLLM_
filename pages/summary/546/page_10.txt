The paper explores the concept of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) without the need for training, presenting a novel approach that aims to streamline the architecture design process. Traditional NAS methods often require extensive computational resources and time for training various architecture candidates, which can be prohibitive for many applications. By eliminating the training phase, the authors propose a more efficient strategy to evaluate potential neural architectures.

Key concepts discussed include the use of zero-cost proxies, which allow for the assessment of architecture performance without actual training, and the exploration of alternative benchmarks and datasets to facilitate the NAS process. The paper references various significant works in the field, such as ProxylessNAS and DARTS, highlighting advancements in one-shot and differentiable architecture search techniques.

The authors emphasize the importance of performance prediction models, such as Tapas, which can estimate the accuracy of architectures prior to training, thereby reducing the overall search space. The implications of this research suggest that a train-less approach to NAS could democratize access to advanced neural network design, making it more feasible for practitioners with limited computational resources.

In summary, the paper contributes to the ongoing discourse on optimizing neural architecture search methodologies, advocating for a shift towards techniques that minimize training costs while maintaining effective architecture evaluation.
