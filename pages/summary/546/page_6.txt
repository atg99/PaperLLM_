In the paper "Neural Architecture Search without Training," the authors investigate the effectiveness of a novel scoring method for neural architectures, comparing it to existing methods such as grad norm and synflow. They present empirical results indicating a positive correlation between their score and validation accuracy across various CIFAR-10 search spaces, particularly strong in NAS-Bench-201 and NDS-DARTS.

The authors conduct an ablation study to assess the robustness of their scoring method against variations in mini-batch selection, input data, weight initialization, and mini-batch size. The findings reveal that the ranking of scores remains consistent across different CIFAR-100 mini-batches, suggesting that the score reflects inherent properties of the architecture rather than being overly dependent on specific data. Additionally, while some noise is observed across different initializations, high-performing architectures consistently stand out.

The study also examines how mini-batch size affects the score, normalizing the results to allow for fair comparison across sizes. The authors conclude that their scoring method effectively distinguishes between architectures, maintaining its reliability throughout different experimental conditions. Overall, the results demonstrate the potential of the proposed score for neural architecture search without the need for extensive training.
