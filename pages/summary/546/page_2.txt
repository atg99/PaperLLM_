In this paper, the authors present a novel method for Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that eliminates the need for training, significantly speeding up the architecture search process. By utilizing a metric called KH, which measures similarity between untrained architectures, the authors demonstrate that it is possible to predict the final performance of a network without training it. This method allows for rapid searches, achieving a CIFAR-10 classification accuracy of 92.81% in just 30 seconds within the NAS-Bench-201 search space, which is substantially faster than traditional NAS methods.

The authors highlight the potential of their approach as a proof-of-concept for NAS without training, showcasing its efficiency and integration capabilities with existing NAS techniques, such as regularized evolutionary search. This work addresses the challenges of manual architecture design, which is often time-consuming and resource-intensive, and opens up new avenues for faster and more flexible network design. Overall, this paper contributes to the ongoing efforts to streamline the NAS process while maintaining accuracy and adaptability for various applications.
