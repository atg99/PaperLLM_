The paper presents a novel approach to Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that eliminates the need for extensive training of networks, termed NASWOT. The motivation is to evaluate uninitialized networks using a scoring mechanism derived from expected performance, allowing for rapid architecture selection. 

In the study, ten NAS-Bench-201 networks with over 90% validation accuracy on CIFAR-10 were analyzed. The authors trained these networks for 100 epochs using stochastic gradient descent and monitored the evolution of their scores. Results indicated that scores increased sharply after initial training and stabilized thereafter, maintaining a consistent relative ranking among the networks.

NASWOT operates by randomly generating candidate architectures from the search space and scoring them without training, utilizing the derived scoring equation. This method was tested against traditional approaches such as REA, which employs evolutionary algorithms and requires extensive training. The results demonstrated that NASWOT achieved competitive accuracy in significantly less time, with a mean accuracy of 91.77% in under a minute, compared to 93.87% for REA using 12000 seconds.

The authors provide a proof of concept for NASWOT and make their code available for reproducibility. This research highlights the potential for training-free NAS methods to streamline the architecture search process while maintaining effective performance metrics.
