The paper presents a novel approach to Neural Architecture Search (NAS) called NASWOT, which operates without training. The performance of NASWOT is evaluated using the NAS-Bench-201 and NATS-Bench SSS datasets, with results summarized in Table 2. In NAS-Bench-201, NASWOT is compared against various weight-sharing and non-weight-sharing methods, with accuracies reported for different sample sizes (N=10, N=100, N=1000). Notably, NASWOT achieves competitive accuracies while significantly reducing search time compared to traditional weight-sharing methods, which typically require extensive training.

Specifically, NASWOT demonstrates performance that approaches the best results of non-weight-sharing methods but with a fraction of the search time. For example, with N=1000, NASWOT achieves accuracies of around 89.69% on CIFAR-10, while weight-sharing methods like GDAS and DARTS show much lower performance despite longer search times. The results indicate that while non-weight-sharing methods outperform NASWOT in accuracy, they come with a much higher computational cost.

The paper emphasizes the potential of NASWOT to efficiently discover effective neural architectures and highlights the importance of reducing search time in NAS methodologies. Overall, the findings suggest that training-free approaches like NASWOT could be a viable alternative to traditional NAS methods, especially in scenarios where computational resources are limited.
