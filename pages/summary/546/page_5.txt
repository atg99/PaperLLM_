The paper presents a study on Neural Architecture Search (NAS) without the need for training the architectures. It includes various plots (a)-(j) that illustrate the correlation between a scoring system for randomly sampled untrained architectures and their validation accuracy once trained. The data is sourced from multiple NAS benchmarks, including NAS-Bench-201, NAS-Bench-101, and NDS models applied to datasets like CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet.

Key findings indicate that there is a noticeable correlation between the proposed score for untrained networks and their eventual validation accuracy when trained. This correlation is quantified through the τ (Kendall's tau) values provided for each plot, indicating varying degrees of association. For instance, the τ values range from 0.223 to 0.611 across different datasets and architectures, suggesting that the scoring mechanism can effectively predict performance without prior training.

The inclusion of a plot from NDS-DARTS on a more challenging subset of ImageNet (ImageNette2) further supports the applicability of the scoring method beyond simpler datasets. Overall, the study emphasizes the potential of scoring untrained architectures to streamline the NAS process by reducing the reliance on extensive training.
