The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) by proposing a method to score network architectures at initialization, aiming to predict their final accuracy without the need for extensive training. The study utilizes several benchmarks, including NAS-Bench-201, NATS-Bench, and the Network Design Spaces (NDS), comprising a variety of network architectures trained on datasets like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet.

The novel scoring method focuses on the binary activation patterns of Rectified Linear Units (ReLU) within the neural networks. Each input generates a binary code based on the active and inactive states of the ReLU units. The core idea is that inputs with similar binary codes are harder for the network to distinguish, indicating potential challenges in learning. The paper introduces the use of Hamming distance to measure the dissimilarity between these binary codes, leading to the creation of a kernel matrix that reflects the network's ability to separate inputs.

The authors demonstrate that high-performing networks tend to have kernel matrices with fewer off-diagonal elements indicating high similarity, suggesting a more effective separation of inputs. The score for each network is derived from the logarithm of the determinant of the kernel matrix, where a higher score correlates with better expected accuracy post-training.

Empirical results show strong correlations between the proposed scores for untrained networks and their actual validation accuracies upon training, across various NAS benchmarks. This scoring method potentially streamlines the NAS process by reducing reliance on costly training iterations, thereby improving exploration efficiency in existing NAS algorithms.
