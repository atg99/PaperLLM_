The paper presents a novel approach to Neural Architecture Search (NAS) called NASWOT, which allows for the evaluation of neural architectures without the need for extensive training. The authors argue that the architectures contain significant information about their potential performance even at initialization. They demonstrate that increasing sample size enhances the performance of NASWOT, which shows results comparable to existing methods when tested on NATS-Bench SSS.

A key advantage of NASWOT is its rapid execution time, enabling efficient exploration of architectures across different hardware and datasets. The paper introduces an enhancement to the Regularised Evolutionary Algorithm (REA), termed Assisted-REA (AREA), which utilizes a larger initial population sampled based on the NASWOT score to improve performance on NAS-Bench datasets.

The authors conclude that NASWOT successfully navigates search spaces quickly by leveraging insights from initialized networks, potentially reducing the cost and time associated with architecture search. While the study focuses on convolutional architectures for image classification, it sets the groundwork for future research to further eliminate training from NAS processes, making architecture search more accessible.
