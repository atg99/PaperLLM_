This paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) with a focus on methods that do not require training. It outlines how binary activation codes of ReLU units delineate linear regions in the input space, highlighting the significance of activation patterns in defining these regions. The paper reviews existing NAS techniques, such as the use of weight sharing in Efficient Neural Architecture Search (ENAS), which reduces computational costs significantly but may hinder the discovery of optimal architectures due to the vast search space.

The authors note that while traditional NAS methods face challenges, including an overwhelming number of possible architectures, alternative approaches estimate accuracy prior to training without relying on predictive models. They reference recent works that integrate training-free heuristics into existing search strategies, which aligns with their own contributions.

To evaluate NAS algorithms effectively, the paper emphasizes the importance of benchmarks, such as NAS-Bench-101, NAS-Bench-201, and NATS-Bench, which provide manageable search spaces and metadata for assessing network performance. By utilizing these benchmarks, the authors aim to demonstrate the efficacy of their proposed method in identifying high-performing architectures.
