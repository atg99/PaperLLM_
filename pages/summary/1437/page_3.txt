The paper presents an integration of user-defined neural network models into the PyTorch-Kaldi toolkit, showcasing the implementation of various state-of-the-art architectures such as MLPs, CNNs, RNNs, LSTMs, GRUs, and advanced models like Light GRU and SincNet for raw waveform speech recognition. The toolkit allows tuning of hyperparameters via a random search algorithm.

In the decoding and scoring process, the neural network's acoustic posterior probabilities are normalized and combined with language probabilities from an n-gram model for word sequence retrieval using a beam-search algorithm. The Word-Error-Rate (WER) is computed with the NIST SCTK scoring toolkit.

The experimental setup involves the TIMIT corpus for phoneme recognition, the DIRHA-English dataset for distant-talking conditions, and the CHiME 4 dataset for noisy environments. The LibriSpeech dataset is also used, focusing on various acoustic features such as MFCCs, FBANKS, and fMLLRs. The experiments utilize specific DNN settings including weight initialization, recurrent dropout, and batch normalization, with optimization performed using the RMSprop algorithm.

Baseline performance results from the experiments indicate that fMLLR features yield the best performance, outperforming MFCCs and FBANKs. Recurrent models, particularly LSTM, GRU, and Li-GRU, demonstrate superior performance compared to MLPs, with the Li-GRU model achieving the lowest average phone error rate of 14.2%, highlighting its efficiency in computation. Overall, the findings illustrate the effectiveness of the implemented models and techniques in enhancing automatic speech recognition performance.
