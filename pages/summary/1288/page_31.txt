This paper discusses advancements in video abnormal detection, a critical aspect of video surveillance that has garnered significant attention within computer vision. It highlights the use of Autoencoders (AEs) for unsupervised feature extraction, emphasizing their high performance in processing video data and addressing issues like blurriness.

The paper categorizes abnormal detection methods into unsupervised and supervised approaches, noting the challenges faced in unsupervised learning due to the absence of labeled data to model normal distributions. AEs are presented as powerful tools for this task, capable of modeling high-dimensional data effectively. The authors reference various studies that utilize AEs, such as one that visualizes optical flow images and employs deep AEs to extract features, and another that generates cubic patches for detecting local and global anomalies.

A critical limitation mentioned is that AEs may sometimes reconstruct anomalies well, leading to missed detections. To counter this, an improved version, the memory-augmented Autoencoder (MemAE), is introduced, which incorporates a memory storage module to enhance reconstruction error for abnormal inputs.

Additionally, the paper discusses the importance of sparse representation in anomaly detection, suggesting two novel cubic patch-based detectors that utilize both reconstruction error and sparse representation. The authors also explore the training of multiple AEs for learning latent features related to object appearance and motion.

In contrast to existing methods, this work proposes framing abnormal event detection as a one-versus-rest binary classification problem, enhancing the ability to differentiate between normal and abnormal events effectively. This approach aims to provide a more robust framework for detecting anomalies in video surveillance.
