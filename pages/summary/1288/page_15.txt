This paper discusses various auto-encoder architectures, focusing on the differences and similarities between them. It highlights the Denoising Auto-Encoder (DAE) as a specific form of Convolutional Auto-Encoder (CAE1) that incorporates minimal Gaussian corruption and Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, with a hyper-parameter that balances reconstruction accuracy and robustness. The paper also introduces the Stacked What-Where Auto-Encoder (SWWAE), which integrates concepts from cognitive neuroscience to differentiate between “what” (object identification) and “where” (spatial location). The SWWAE employs a loss function that combines classification loss, reconstruction loss, and intermediate reconstruction terms, allowing it to factor data into semantically relevant variations while using reconstruction as a regularizer.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts the SWWAE with the Deep Convolutional Auto-Encoder (CAE2), which utilizes a convolutional neural network for encoding and decoding. The CAE2, developed with logistic sigmoid units, has not been fully explored in terms of learning properties and connections to other models. The Convolutional Sparse Auto-Encoder (CSAE) is introduced as an advancement, incorporating a sparsifying module to predict sparse feature maps and establishing connections to Convolutional Sparse Coding (CSC). The CAE2 is noted for its scalability to high-dimensional inputs and its ability to maintain spatial locality, distinguishing it from fully connected networks like Sparse Auto-Encoders (SAE) and DAEs, which may introduce computational complexity and global feature constraints.
