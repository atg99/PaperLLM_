This paper provides an extensive overview of autoencoders (AEs) and their variants, structured into several sections. Section 2 introduces the basic AE, detailing its concept and structure, while also categorizing various models and their recent developments. The basic AE is defined as an auto-associative neural network derived from the multi-layer perceptron, aimed at reproducing its input. It consists of an input layer, a hidden layer, and an output layer, functioning through a deterministic mapping to transform input vectors into hidden representations.

In Section 3, the paper analyzes AEs from three perspectives, while Section 4 explores their relationships with shallow models and other deep learning frameworks. Section 5 discusses the practical applications of AEs and their variants across diverse fields such as pattern recognition, computer vision, data generation, and recommender systems. Section 6 reviews the available toolkits for implementing AEs. Finally, the paper concludes with a discussion on future trends and challenges in the design and training of AEs, emphasizing the potential for further research and development in this area.
