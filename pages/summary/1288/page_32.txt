The paper discusses various approaches for video anomaly detection, focusing on methods that utilize binary support vector machines (SVMs) and autoencoders (AEs). One method employs binary SVM models to classify test samples, using the highest classification score as an abnormality score. Another approach utilizes Gaussian Mixture Fully Convolutional Variational Auto-encoders (GMFC-VAEs), which combines appearance and motion anomalies by training exclusively on normal samples. If a test sample does not match any Gaussian component, it is classified as an anomaly. The GMFC-VAE leverages a fully convolutional network structure, demonstrating effectiveness on challenging datasets.

Additionally, the paper highlights the use of multiple AEs that adaptively learn to reconstruct normal patterns, with reconstruction errors indicating anomalies during testing. A table summarizes various AE models applied in the video domain, detailing their specific tasks and characteristics. The analysis suggests that multiple AEs can enhance detection performance and that understanding existing methods can lead to improvements in current autoencoder frameworks.
