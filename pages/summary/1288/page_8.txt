The paper discusses the structure and variations of autoencoders (AEs), particularly focusing on regularized AEs. AEs can be categorized into narrow (under-complete) and wide (over-complete) based on the relationship between the sizes of the hidden and input layers. Regularized AEs utilize different types of regularizers, such as sparsity, contractive, and denoising regularization, to enhance their performance. The encoder function maps inputs to internal representations, while the decoder reconstructs the input from these representations. Regularizers aim to simplify the representation by discarding some information from the input.

The paper also presents a table summarizing various regularized AEs, highlighting their characteristics and advantages. Notable methods include the Sparse Autoencoder, which imposes a sparsity constraint; Denoising Autoencoder, which captures structure in noisy inputs; and Variational Autoencoder, known for its elegant theory. Other methods like the Wasserstein Autoencoder and Convolutional Autoencoder extend the capabilities of traditional AEs to better handle different data distributions and structures.
