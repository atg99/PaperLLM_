The paper discusses the concept of ideal encoding in the context of autoencoders (AEs) and their relationship with other machine learning models, particularly shallow architectures. It highlights that the total number of bits used in encoding is derived from the sum of two steps, adjusted by a penalty for using a sub-optimal encoding method.

The connection between AEs and shallow models is explored, particularly focusing on principal components analysis (PCA). The authors note that traditional AEs share similarities with PCA, as both are unsupervised learning algorithms. When AEs utilize linear neurons and mean squared error (MSE), they can learn the same subspace as PCA, although their weight matrices may not represent the exact same basis.

Moreover, the paper emphasizes the advantages of nonlinear AEs over PCA, particularly their ability to learn nonlinear manifolds, which PCA, being a linear method, cannot achieve. Both methods can be framed within an energy-based framework, minimizing energy loss, but AEs manage to do so without needing to adjust for unobserved patterns.

The discussion also introduces the Generalized Auto-encoder (GAE), which enhances traditional AEs by incorporating data relations to better exploit manifold structures. A variant known as GAE-PCA is mentioned as a formulation of PCA that considers zero mean.

Finally, the paper notes recent trends in combining AEs with PCA for various applications, including feature learning methods that integrate stacked AEs with convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for tasks such as American Sign Language recognition. The exploration indicates a growing interest in utilizing the strengths of both AEs and PCA in machine learning applications.
