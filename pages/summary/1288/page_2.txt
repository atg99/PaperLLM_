The paper discusses auto-encoders (AEs), which are fundamental components in building hierarchical deep models for unsupervised learning and non-linear feature extraction without labeled data. Historically, AEs, initially referred to as "auto-associators," have evolved since the 1980s, with significant advancements including the introduction of denoising auto-encoders (DAEs) and sparse auto-encoders (SAEs). Recent developments include the k sparse auto-encoder (kSA), which enhances feature aggregation accuracy through novel algorithms.

The importance of this survey lies in its comprehensive review of deep learning-based AEs, addressing a gap in existing literature. While some previous works have attempted to outline this field, few have provided an in-depth synthesis or tackled ongoing challenges. This survey aims to summarize current research and suggest future directions, drawing from nearly 300 studies collected via Google Scholar and the Web of Science, as well as key academic conferences.

The contributions of this survey are threefold: it reviews AE models and highlights influential prototypes; it summarizes the state of the art in AE applications, particularly in computer vision; and it discusses future research avenues to advance the understanding and application of AEs in deep learning. This survey is intended for researchers in computer vision and neural computing, providing insights into state-of-the-art developments and challenges in the field.
