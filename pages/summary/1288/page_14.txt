This paper discusses various advancements in auto-encoder models, particularly focusing on the Variational Auto-encoder (VAE) and its extensions. The Triplet based Variational Auto-encoder (TVAE) is introduced, which utilizes a novel loss function that integrates a triplet loss with the standard evidence lower bound (ELBO) to improve semantic encoding of structural information in latent space. The loss function is expressed as LTVAE = Lrec + LKL + Ltriplet, where Lrec represents reconstruction loss, LKL denotes KL Divergence loss, and Ltriplet is the triplet loss.

Additionally, the Conditional Variational Auto-encoder (CVAE) is highlighted as an advanced model that generates outputs conditioned on input observations, capable of producing diverse outputs such as human faces based on skin color.

The paper also introduces the Wasserstein Auto-encoder (WAE), which features a loss function comprising reconstruction cost and a regularizer. Unlike the VAE, the WAE's regularizer aligns the aggregated posterior with the prior distribution rather than requiring point-wise alignment. The WAE focuses on minimizing optimal transport between the latent variable model distribution and the data distribution, resulting in higher-quality sample generation. Studies have shown the WAE’s effectiveness in disentangled representation learning and the advantages of random encoders over deterministic ones.

Lastly, the Contractive Auto-encoder (CAE1) is presented, which incorporates a penalty term based on the Frobenius norm of the Jacobian matrix of the encoder’s activations to enhance the model. The CAE1's loss function is defined as JCAE(q) = Σx∈D L(x, g(f(x))) + λkJf(x)k²_F, where the reconstruction error can be specified as mean squared error or cross-entropy loss. The paper contrasts CAE1 with Denoising Auto-encoders (DAE), emphasizing its unique characteristics.
