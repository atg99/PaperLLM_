The paper discusses the significance of sparse representations in the context of encoder-decoder architectures, particularly in sparse autoencoders (SAE). Sparse representations, or feature vectors, offer advantages such as robustness to noise and improved classification in higher-dimensional spaces. This concept is inspired by observations of sparse neural activity in the brain, leading to increased interest in sparse coding.

The paper introduces sparsity as a form of regularization within autoencoders, achieved by penalizing either the biases or the activations of hidden units. Two forms of sparsity are identified: parameterization sparsity, which focuses on regularizing the model parameters, and representational sparsity, which penalizes the activations of hidden units.

The regularized objective function is defined, incorporating a norm penalty that balances the original loss with the regularization term. The paper contrasts L1 and L2 regularization methods, with L1 being preferred for inducing sparser solutions. Representational sparsity is similarly enforced through penalties on representation elements, and the paper notes the utility of Kullback-Leibler divergence for constrained representations. Overall, the findings emphasize the benefits of sparsity in enhancing the performance and interpretability of autoencoder models.
