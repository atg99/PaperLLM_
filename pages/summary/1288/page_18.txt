The analysis highlights that the design of Autoencoders (AEs) can be understood through the lens of information theory, particularly using concepts such as Mutual Information and Kullback–Leibler divergence. The goal of AEs, including Denoising Autoencoders (DAEs) and Conditional AEs (CAEs), is to encode input data efficiently while preserving as much relevant information as possible, even when the inputs are corrupted.

The DAE specifically aims to learn a mapping from lower probability points to higher probability points on a manifold, making it robust to small input perturbations. This property is exploited in applications like brain MRI analysis, where the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) can generate images based on manifold coordinates. The Frobenius norm of the Jacobian and its relationship to entropy serve as measures of efficiency in the representation learned by CAEs.

Overall, this work emphasizes the relevance of information-theoretic principles in understanding and designing deep learning architectures, particularly in the context of autoencoding models.
