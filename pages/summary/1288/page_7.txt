The paper discusses various optimization techniques for training autoencoders (AEs), focusing on the effectiveness of mini-batch L-BFGS and conjugate gradient (CG) methods compared to standard stochastic gradient descent (SGD). It highlights that L-BFGS and CG converge faster, especially for sparse AEs, due to their preference for larger mini-batch sizes, which facilitates better estimation of hidden activations. The text also addresses the use of recirculation for training AEs, noting its biological plausibility despite its limited application in machine learning.

Furthermore, the paper explores the application of genetic algorithms (GAs) in training neural networks, particularly as alternatives or supplements to backpropagation (BP). A GA-assisted method for deep AEs is proposed, which reportedly enhances performance by introducing randomness in weight adjustments, akin to dropout techniques.

The taxonomy of basic AEs is outlined, emphasizing their three-layer structure: input, hidden, and output layers. AEs aim to learn an identity function, but their primary purpose is to derive useful hidden representations. Regularized AEs impose constraints, such as limiting hidden layer units to force compressed representations. Under-complete AEs, with fewer hidden units than input dimensions, can effectively capture salient features.

The relationship between AEs and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is discussed, particularly how linear AEs can replicate PCA results under certain conditions. However, over-complete AEs may struggle to learn meaningful representations without proper regularization. Alternative constraints, including sparsity, robustness to noise, and small derivatives, are suggested to enhance the performance of AEs in various contexts.
