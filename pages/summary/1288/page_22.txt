The paper discusses the use of convolutional autoencoders (CAEs) and their relationship with matrix factorization techniques, specifically non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) and truncated singular value decomposition (tSVD). It highlights the architecture of a convolutional neural network (CNN) and the process of extracting image patches for training an autoencoder (AE). A key challenge noted is the ability of the receptive field to capture relevant features, with smaller patches potentially missing significant details.

The relationship between NMF and AE is explored, emphasizing that both methods are effective for unsupervised learning. NMF aims to decompose a non-negative matrix into two non-negative matrices, enabling dimensionality reduction. A one-hidden layer AE can replicate NMF, producing lower-dimensional representations. The proposed PAE-NMF architecture leverages variational autoencoder (VAE) principles to enhance NMF, benefiting from non-negative latent spaces while also addressing data uncertainty.

In addition, the paper examines tSVD, another matrix factorization approach that generates low-rank approximations by truncating less significant singular values from the singular value decomposition (SVD) of a matrix. This relationship underscores the versatility of AEs in conjunction with matrix factorization methods for effective data representation and dimensionality reduction.
