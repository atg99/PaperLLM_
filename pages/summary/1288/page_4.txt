The paper discusses the architecture and optimization of autoencoders (AEs), focusing on the mapping process from input to hidden representation and back to reconstruction. The latent representation y, derived from input x, is reconstructed into vector z using a decoder function with parameters q, which includes weight matrices and biases. The reconstruction aims to minimize a loss function, typically chosen based on the input's characteristics. 

Key loss functions mentioned include mean squared error (MSE) for unbounded domains, and cross-entropy (CE) for inputs constrained between 0 and 1 or interpreted as Bernoulli vectors. The CE is highlighted for its non-negativity and tendency to approach zero as output accuracy improves. Conversely, MSE is suitable when linear neurons are used, avoiding learning slowdowns.

Additionally, the paper introduces an exponential (EXP) cost function, which offers flexibility and robustness against outliers. The authors compare the performances of MSE, CE, and EXP in pre-training and fine-tuning deep networks, noting their impacts on classification performance. The Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle is also referenced, showcasing its application in deriving an energy-based objective function for training AEs.
