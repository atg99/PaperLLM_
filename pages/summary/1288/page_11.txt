The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in autoencoders (AEs), highlighting two types: population sparsity and lifetime sparsity. Population sparsity refers to scenarios where only a few neurons are active at any given time, while lifetime sparsity involves high kurtosis in the lifetime response distribution of each neuron. The k-sparse autoencoder, which retains only the k highest activities in its hidden layers, is presented as a method to enforce population sparsity. However, this method has limitations, such as its dependency on specific target sparsity and the complexity of tuning parameters.

In contrast, the Fully Connected Winner-Take-All (FC-WTA) autoencoder addresses these issues by allowing for any target sparsity rate without the need for extensive hyper-parameter tuning. This model enforces lifetime sparsity through mini-batch statistics, ensuring that all hidden units are updated with every mini-batch, thus avoiding the problem of inactive dictionary components.

The paper also emphasizes that sparse representations are inherently compressible due to the large number of zeros in the output. However, this sparsity implies that AEs cannot reconstruct every possible input accurately, as the configurations of sparse vectors are fewer than those of dense vectors, leading to lower entropy in the representation.

Additionally, the paper introduces denoising autoencoders (DAEs), which differ from traditional AEs by focusing on reconstructing clean inputs from corrupted versions. This approach involves a stochastic mapping process to generate corrupted inputs, which the DAE then learns to repair, thus enhancing feature extraction capabilities. Overall, the discussion highlights the evolving strategies in training AEs to achieve effective representations through various forms of sparsity and denoising techniques.
