The paper discusses the application of Autoencoders (AEs) in image processing, specifically focusing on saliency detection and image restoration. It highlights the effectiveness of stacked AEs (SAEs) in generating saliency maps through their robust data reconstruction capabilities. A notable framework mentioned is the center-surround (C-S) inference network, which integrates global and local sampling methods to enhance saliency detection by modeling human visual perception. The paper also touches on co-saliency detection, where two stacked denoising autoencoders (SDAEs) are employed to extract common salient objects across multiple images, emphasizing their ability to learn abstract features and facilitate knowledge transfer.

In the context of image restoration, the paper outlines various techniques aimed at recovering original images from noisy or corrupted versions. It describes the use of Denoising Autoencoders (DAEs) for tasks like image denoising and blind inpainting, noting a new training scheme that improves performance over traditional methods. The findings suggest that AEs and their variants offer significant advantages in both saliency detection and image restoration due to their capacity for deep feature learning and effective data reconstruction. Key conclusions drawn include the versatility of AEs, the potential for integrating multiple AEs into cohesive models, and the importance of considering reconstruction residuals in model design.
