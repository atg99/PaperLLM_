This paper discusses the architecture and functionality of Denoising Autoencoders (DAEs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). DAEs map input data to a hidden representation and then reconstruct the input from this representation, aiming to minimize the reconstruction error. The authors highlight the importance of noise injection in the input data, which enhances the robustness of neural networks and serves as a form of data augmentation. Various types of noise, including salt-and-pepper and Gaussian noise, can be applied to input data and hidden units, leading to techniques like dropout, where random units are temporarily removed from the network.

The paper also explores theoretical perspectives on DAEs, including manifold learning and information theory, and discusses their relation to energy-based models (EBMs). The authors note that using linear reconstruction and squared error in DAEs is akin to learning an energy-based model closely related to Gaussian RBMs. Additionally, denoising score matching is presented as a regularized parameter estimation technique for training.

The section concludes with the introduction of VAEs, which represent a more modern approach in the field, although further details on VAEs are expected in subsequent sections. Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of DAEs and VAEs in learning robust representations from corrupted data.
