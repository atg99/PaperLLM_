The paper discusses various models that utilize autoencoders (AEs) for enhancing recommendation systems (RSs). It highlights the importance of expanding the hidden layer's dimensions to improve the model's capability in simulating input features. Notably, collaborative denoising autoencoders (DAEs) are introduced as a method for ranking predictions, differing from standard DAEs by incorporating user-specific inputs corrupted by Gaussian noise.

Further, the paper explores the integration of AEs with traditional recommendation methods like matrix factorization (MF), singular value decomposition (SVD), and probability matrix factorization (PMF). For instance, the Stacked Discriminative Denoising Autoencoder (SDAE) is combined with MF to enhance performance by learning from corrupted training data. Additionally, marginalized DAEs are discussed for their lower computational cost while maintaining effective user-item relationship modeling.

The paper also covers the integration of AEs with other deep learning methods, showcasing models like the Collaborative Knowledge Base Embedding (CKE) that leverage textual, structural, and visual knowledge for improved latent representation learning. This hybrid approach demonstrates the flexibility of AEs in creating robust recommendation systems by combining various neural architectures to effectively handle sparse data challenges in collaborative filtering.
