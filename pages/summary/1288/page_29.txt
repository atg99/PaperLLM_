The paper discusses the integration of wavelet transforms with a Multimodal Image Multitask Deep Autoencoder (MIMT-DAE) for improved image retrieval performance, specifically in shape and texture recognition. This method outperforms traditional single-task deep autoencoders while requiring significantly fewer training parameters.

In the realm of video processing, various autoencoder (AE) models are applied to tasks such as video classification, object tracking, and anomaly detection. The paper highlights the importance of automatic video annotation and classification to enhance video retrieval efficiency. It reviews studies that leverage multimodal feature learning—combining image, audio, and text modalities—to improve classification accuracy. These studies demonstrate that integrating multiple features yields better results compared to using single modalities alone.

The paper also addresses the challenge of video forgery detection, proposing methods that utilize AEs in conjunction with recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and unsupervised approaches to enhance salient object detection in videos. Additionally, it discusses the emerging interest in disentangled representations for video sequence modeling, which can separate static object identities from dynamic information such as position and pose. This approach is facilitated by a hierarchical variational autoencoder (VAE) that captures both consistent and variable aspects of video data, thereby advancing the understanding of complex video features.
