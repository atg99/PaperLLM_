In this paper, we introduce robust model pipelines for natural language processing tasks specifically tailored for biomedical text, utilizing scispaCy models that incorporate a custom tokenizer and various sentence segmentation techniques. We compare scispaCy to existing tools in the field, such as Apache cTakes, MetaMap, and the GENIA Tagger, highlighting that while these tools serve specific functions, scispaCy enhances performance with a full dependency parser and superior named entity recognition without sacrificing speed. We also reference the GENIA corpus and various studies on domain adaptation that inform our development of scispaCy.

The paper concludes by emphasizing the models' efficiency, scalability, and near state-of-the-art performance, encouraging their use for biomedical information extraction. Future enhancements will focus on developing a comprehensive entity linker and incorporating additional components like negation detection, broadening the applicability of scispaCy in the biomedical NLP landscape.
