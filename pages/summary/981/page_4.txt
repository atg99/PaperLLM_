The paper discusses the performance and robustness of the scispaCy models for dependency parsing and named entity recognition (NER) in biomedical text processing. It compares the Biaffine parser by Dozat and Manning (2016) with scispaCy models, noting that while the Biaffine parser has a slight edge in accuracy (2-3%), scispaCy models are significantly faster (approximately nine times) due to optimizations in the spaCy library.

A key aspect of the scispaCy models is their versatility across various biomedical text types, including clinical notes and academic papers. The authors conducted experiments incorporating training data from the OntoNotes 5.0 corpus to enhance robustness across different domains. The results indicate that adding web data during training improves performance on the OntoNotes corpus without negatively impacting biomedical text performance.

For NER, the scispaCy models utilize a transition-based system based on the chunking model and are primarily trained on the MedMentions dataset. This dataset enables recognition of diverse entity types but does not classify them. To address specific user needs, the authors released additional models fine-tuned on various datasets, such as BC5CDR and CRAFT, targeting specific biomedical entities.

The paper details experiments to evaluate scispaCy's NER performance across multiple datasets, highlighting the wide variety of entity types and sizes represented. A comprehensive comparison of scispaCy NER models against other models is presented, illustrating their effectiveness in handling diverse biomedical tasks.
