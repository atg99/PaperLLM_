The paper discusses a joint part of speech (POS) tagging and dependency parsing model implemented in spaCy, which utilizes an arc-eager transition-based parsing approach trained with a dynamic oracle, akin to the methodology proposed by Goldberg and Nivre in 2012. The model employs convolutional neural network (CNN) representations for token features that are shared across various pipeline models, as established by Kiperwasser and Goldberg, and Zhang and Weiss in 2016.

For training, the authors utilized datasets derived from the GENIA 1.0 Dependencies by converting the treebank from McClosky and Charniak's 2008 work into Universal Dependencies v1.0 using the Stanford Dependency Converter. This dataset, enriched with POS tags, serves to train the POS tagger alongside the dependency parser. The authors released the converted Universal Dependencies as a separate resource, aligning them with original text spans from un-tokenized abstracts and incorporating PubMed metadata that was previously omitted. This format is intended to support practical evaluations of core natural language processing tasks in the biomedical domain.

Additionally, the paper highlights the retrieval of original metadata from PubMed associated with each abstract, which includes named entities, Medical Subject Headings (MeSH terms), and various ontologies related to chemicals and drugs, along with author and publication details.

The paper presents experimental results comparing their models against state-of-the-art systems for POS tagging and dependency parsing on the GENIA test set, showing competitive performance. The authors also used the OntoNotes 5.0 corpus to enhance the robustness of their models, focusing on POS and dependency parsing annotations. Overall, the models released in scispaCy demonstrate strong capabilities in handling biomedical data, as evidenced by the results in the provided tables.
