The paper discusses the performance of scispaCy models in the context of Named Entity Recognition (NER) across various specialized datasets. The authors note that strong baselines do not utilize multi-task learning across multiple datasets or semi-supervised learning with large pretrained language models. Despite this, scispaCy models demonstrate competitive recall on five out of nine datasets tested. 

The evaluation of the recall for the pipeline mention detector, trained on the MedMentions dataset, reveals a modest drop in average recall when applied to the nine specialized NER datasets. This is considered significant given the diversity of the datasets.

The paper presents recall metrics for specific datasets, highlighting performance differences between two models, encore scism and encore scimd, with average recalls of 62.81 and 64.14 respectively. Performance on the MedMentions corpus shows precision, recall, and F1 scores for both models, indicating their effectiveness.

Additionally, the paper outlines initial steps toward building an Entity Linking model that utilizes a subset of the Unified Medical Language System (UMLS). The candidate generation process employs an approximate nearest neighbors search for linking mentions to UMLS concepts. The methodology includes using TF-IDF scores of character 3-grams for encoding concepts and aliases, leading to efficient storage of candidate generation data.

The authors also address challenges posed by abbreviated mentions, which often result in failed entity matches. To improve this, they implement an unsupervised abbreviation detection algorithm, which enhances recall by substituting abbreviations with their full definitions prior to the nearest neighbor search. This adjustment demonstrates a notable improvement in the recall of correctly identified concepts.
