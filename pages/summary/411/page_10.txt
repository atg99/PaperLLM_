The paper discusses the effectiveness of various YOLO models for detecting individuals in video footage, particularly in the context of capacity restrictions during the COVID-19 pandemic. It highlights the challenges faced when using Intersection over Union (IoU) and confidence thresholds in low-resolution images, which can lead to under-recognition of individuals. The study compares the performance of YOLO v3, v4, v5s, and their Tiny versions based on the mean Average Precision (mAP) at thresholds of 0.50 and 0.75.

Results indicate that YOLO v3 and v5s outperform other models, achieving high detection accuracy rates of 96.89% and 96.12%, respectively. The Tiny models, while faster, exhibit significantly lower recognition rates due to reduced complexity. The study emphasizes the correlation between the number of detected persons and detection accuracy, noting that higher resolution images yield better results.

The authors propose an algorithm that utilizes bounding boxes generated by YOLO models to estimate the area of specified regions in square meters, facilitating the approximation of indoor capacity. They conclude that YOLO v5s is versatile for both low- and high-performance systems, making it ideal for real-time applications. Future research aims to explore social distancing measurements and compare different object detection architectures to enhance performance further.
