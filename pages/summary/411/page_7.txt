This study focuses on a method for counting people in specified regions of video frames, utilizing pre-trained YOLO models for object detection. Initially, a defined area, which can take the shape of triangles, squares, or polygons, is established within the video. The counting process involves discarding non-person objects from the MS COCO dataset and incrementing a counter for each detected person's bounding box center point that falls within the specified region. The algorithm calculates the area of this region in pixels and estimates the maximum capacity based on the assumption of one person per square meter.

The consistency of results was maintained by keeping parameters constant across experiments, including video resolution and duration. The study employed YOLO v5, which was converted to ONNX format for compatibility with OpenCV, and various resolutions (800 × 600 and 1024 × 768 pixels) were tested. The analysis included performance metrics such as Intersection over Union (IoU) and confidence thresholds, both set at 0.4.

To enhance processing speed, the study utilized an NVIDIA GPU alongside an Intel Xeon CPU, addressing the slow frame rates typical of deep learning models when run on CPUs. The results indicated the number of persons detected and the maximum capacity for the specified regions, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method in real-time applications. Detailed characteristics of the video and specified regions were provided to support the findings.
