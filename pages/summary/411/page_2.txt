This paper discusses the application of deep learning methods, particularly YOLO (You Only Look Once) models, for estimating the number of people in designated indoor spaces using a Region of Interest (ROI) based approach. The study emphasizes two primary methods: the ROI-based method, which estimates the number of individuals within a specified area, and the Line of Interest (LOI) method, which counts people crossing a defined line.

The authors highlight the significance of object detection in video surveillance systems, particularly for counting individuals in indoor environments. YOLO models, known for their ability to perform real-time object detection with high accuracy, are central to this research. The study reviews various versions of YOLO, including YOLO v3, YOLO v4, and YOLO v5, noting their effectiveness in different scenarios, such as low-light conditions and varying crowd densities.

The paper presents an algorithm designed to estimate the area of a specified region and determine the maximum occupancy based on YOLO models. Performance analyses reveal that YOLO v3, YOLO v5s, and YOLO v4 achieved detection accuracies of 96.89%, 96.12%, and 94.57%, respectively.

In the methods section, the authors explain that YOLO operates as a single-stage Convolutional Neural Network, dividing images into grids to predict bounding boxes and class scores. YOLO v3, in particular, features a complex architecture with 53 convolutional layers and residual blocks, enhancing its feature extraction capabilities.

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of using advanced deep learning techniques for effective people counting and area estimation in indoor settings, with a focus on the practical applications of YOLO models in real-time scenarios.
