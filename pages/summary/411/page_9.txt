The paper investigates the performance of various YOLO models in detecting persons within specified regions in video frames, focusing on accuracy and processing speed. The study uses two image resolutions to assess the consistency of the YOLO v5 algorithm's results. 

From the analysis, the area in one region was estimated at 18.8 m², allowing for a maximum capacity of 19 persons, while only 8 were detected. In another region, the capacity was 28 persons. The height and width of detected individuals varied with image resolution, affecting capacity estimates.

Table 4 compares the performance metrics of different YOLO models. YOLO v3 achieved the highest accuracy of 88.39% but had a lower frame rate of 9.91 FPS, processing a 14.3-second video in 36.09 seconds. In contrast, YOLO v5s demonstrated a better frame rate of 20.06 FPS, processing the same video in 17.84 seconds, with an accuracy of 78.14%. Despite its smaller pre-trained weight, YOLO v5s performed competitively against other models.

The detection performance was further evaluated across video frames, revealing that the accuracy rates for detecting persons were 96.89% for YOLO v3 and 96.12% for YOLO v5s. YOLO v3 outperformed other models, achieving the highest mean Average Precision (mAP) of 98.8%. The YOLO v3-Tiny model exhibited the lowest performance in accuracy and mAP.

Overall, the findings indicate that while YOLO v3 has superior accuracy, YOLO v5s offers improved processing speed, making it a viable alternative for real-time applications. The results highlight the importance of tuning IoU and confidence thresholds in detection accuracy across different models.
