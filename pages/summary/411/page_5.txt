This paper discusses a method for estimating the area occupied by individuals in an image using bounding box data from YOLO models. The approach involves using a reference object of known size, specifically an average person, to estimate the area in square meters. The average area occupied by a person is taken as 0.66 square meters. By calculating the bounding box dimensions provided by the YOLO model in pixels and applying specific equations, the paper derives a conversion coefficient from pixels to square meters.

The algorithm estimates the area of a specified region by checking whether detected individuals fall within predetermined borders. The height and width of these individuals are used to approximate their area to 1 square meter. The calculation process includes deriving the average bounding box area and adjusting it to establish the region's area in square meters. The final output indicates the human capacity of the specified area.

The advantages of this approach lie in its reliance on direct measurements of detected individuals to compute the area, facilitating accurate estimations of space utilization. The methodology is supported by anthropometric data, ensuring that calculations are grounded in real-world measurements.
