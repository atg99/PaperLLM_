This study presents a YOLO-based method for real-time crowd detection in video footage, particularly relevant in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic where maintaining physical distance is crucial. The method involves measuring the size of a specified indoor area, counting the number of people present, and determining the maximum occupancy based on the area size. Key steps include defining the region boundaries, identifying and counting individuals, and estimating the area size to enforce capacity rules.

The authors utilized the You Only Look Once (YOLO) object detection model and pre-trained weights from the Microsoft COCO dataset to facilitate person detection. Performance metrics such as mean average precision (mAP), frames per second (fps), and accuracy rates were evaluated for different YOLO models. The YOLO v3 model demonstrated superior accuracy and mAP performance, while the YOLO v5s model achieved the highest fps among non-Tiny variants.

The study emphasizes the potential of computer vision technologies in managing crowd control in various indoor environments, thereby enhancing public health safety during pandemics.
