The paper discusses an algorithm for person detection and tracking in videos, emphasizing its adaptability to varying camera resolutions. The algorithm maintains consistent area calculations in pixels for detected individuals regardless of resolution changes. However, it notes a limitation: the accuracy of height and width determinations for individuals can be compromised in low-resolution videos, leading to discrepancies in area calculations.

The experimental study utilized Python and the OPENCV library, employing the Deepsort algorithm for tracking. This algorithm integrates a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for object classification, enabling the identification of distinctive features of detected objects. Each object generates an appearance feature vector, allowing for effective tracking by associating current and previous frame data without needing to process the entire video simultaneously. 

Object detection is achieved through bounding boxes, where the location, type, and class of detected objects are identified. The YOLO (You Only Look Once) model is employed for this purpose, which categorizes detected objects and utilizes non-maximum suppression to ensure a single bounding box is assigned per individual. The total count of people in the video is determined based on the number of bounding boxes produced.
