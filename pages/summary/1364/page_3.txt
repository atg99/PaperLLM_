This paper presents a survey on shared control (SC) and shared autonomy (SA) approaches in physical human-robot interaction (pHRI) systems. The authors categorize recent research in these areas, discuss open challenges, and outline future research directions, emphasizing that the survey is not a comprehensive literature review but rather an overview of various methodologies.

The structure of the paper is organized into several sections. Section II introduces the concept of autonomy in pHRI, describing the dynamics of robotic systems where the control input is modulated by an arbitration function that balances human inputs and autonomous control. This arbitration is influenced by the robot's understanding of human actions and environmental conditions.

Section III focuses on SC approaches, differentiating between proximate and remote interaction types. SC is defined as a control architecture where human commands and robot autonomy interact, with three main classes: direct control (no autonomy), supervisory control (higher-level commands with local autonomy), and shared control (a combination of both). The paper illustrates the application of SC in scenarios such as robotic surgery, where autonomy can stabilize the patient’s movements, allowing for more precise human operation.

Overall, the paper highlights the different approaches to integrating autonomy in pHRI systems while identifying key areas for further exploration and improvement.
