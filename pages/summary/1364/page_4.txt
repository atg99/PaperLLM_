The paper by Selva et al. discusses the evolution and application of Shared Control (SC) paradigms in physical Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI). SC is designed to integrate human cognitive abilities with the precision of robots, aiming to reduce user workload in complex robotic systems across various domains such as telerobotics, autonomous driving, and surgical robotics.

In the context of proximate interaction, SC methods facilitate collaborative and cooperative robotic systems. These systems enable tasks like the cooperative manipulation of objects and semi-autonomous driving, where both human and robot share control. For example, in wheelchair control, drivers can command preferred velocities transformed into smooth, collision-free motions. However, while SC allows human override, it lacks the advanced intelligence that incorporates human intentions, which could enhance system responsiveness.

In remote interaction scenarios, SC implements task- or trajectory-level interactions. This can involve autonomous actions that assist in fulfilling sub-tasks, like a robot maintaining grip direction during waste sorting. Hierarchical control architectures enable humanoid robots to perform multiple tasks while accepting human commands. Real-time trajectory adjustments by humans are also facilitated, with autonomous systems ensuring safety through obstacle avoidance techniques.

The paper highlights the advantages of SC, particularly in reducing the workload for human operators managing complex systems, including dual-arm robotic systems for large object manipulation and groups of mobile robots in search and rescue missions. Overall, SC enhances the effectiveness of human-robot collaboration by balancing control responsibilities between the user and the robot.
