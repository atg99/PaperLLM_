The paper titled "Autonomy in Physical Human-Robot Interaction: a Brief Survey" provides an overview of the current state of autonomy in the context of human-robot interactions, particularly focusing on physical engagements. The authors, Mario Selvaggio, Marco Cognetti, Stefanos Nikolaidis, Serena Ivaldi, and Bruno Siciliano, explore various approaches and methodologies that facilitate autonomous behaviors in robots when interacting with humans. 

Key concepts discussed include the definition of autonomy, the challenges posed by physical interactions, and the frameworks that support safe and effective collaboration between humans and robots. The survey highlights existing research efforts, identifies gaps in current methodologies, and suggests directions for future work in enhancing robot autonomy in dynamic environments. The authors emphasize the importance of developing robots that can adapt to human actions and intentions while ensuring safety and efficiency in their interactions. 

This survey serves as a resource for researchers and practitioners in the field, aiming to advance the understanding and implementation of autonomous systems in collaborative robotics.
