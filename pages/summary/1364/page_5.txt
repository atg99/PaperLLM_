The paper discusses the evolution from shared control (SC) to shared autonomy (SA) in human-robot interactions, emphasizing the need for dynamic adaptation of autonomy levels based on human input and environmental conditions. SC typically involves a static or manually adjusted level of autonomy, while SA enables a more fluid interaction that resembles human-human collaboration, where the robot adapts its actions based on real-time information from the human operator and the task context.

Key approaches to human-based adaptation within the SA framework include inferring human intentions, monitoring muscle activity, and assessing human skills. For intentions, systems utilize probabilistic models to predict human actions and adjust robot assistance accordingly. Muscle activity is monitored to minimize operator fatigue, with robots adjusting their support based on detected exertion levels. Finally, skill assessment allows robots to tailor their assistance based on the operator's proficiency, enhancing the collaborative experience.

This paper reviews various methodologies and applications of SA, highlighting its potential in fields such as robotic surgery, assistive robotics, and manufacturing, ultimately aiming to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of human-robot collaboration.
