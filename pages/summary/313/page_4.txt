The paper discusses a method for knowledge extraction using a model called BERTese, which is fine-tuned to output the exact input queries for knowledge extraction tasks. The experiments utilize the LAMA dataset, particularly the T-REx subset, to evaluate the model's performance. The model is trained on a separate dataset, T-REx-train, ensuring no overlap with the evaluation set.

The implementation leverages BERT base with default settings from the Huggingface platform, utilizing AdamW for optimization. The training is conducted on a powerful NVIDIA V100 GPU over five epochs with a batch size of 64. The study compares BERTese against three baselines: a non-fine-tuned BERT model, LPAQA which uses additional paraphrase queries, and FT-BERT which is fine-tuned for knowledge extraction.

Results indicate that BERTese outperforms all baselines, with notable improvements in performance metrics such as precision at one (P@1). The model demonstrates effectiveness in producing semantically plausible rewrites, which enhance information extraction from BERT. An ablation study reveals that auxiliary losses significantly contribute to performance, with the combination of "Single [MASK] Loss" and "Valid Token Loss" yielding the best results. Additionally, a part-of-speech analysis shows that over 70% of changes made by the rewriter involve nouns and verbs, indicating a focus on semantically significant content.
