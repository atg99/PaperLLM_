The paper "BERTese: Learning to Speak to BERT" by Adi Haviv, Jonathan Berant, and Amir Globerson discusses a novel method for optimizing knowledge extraction from large pre-trained language models (LMs) by automatically rewriting queries into a form they refer to as "BERTese". Traditional methods for extracting knowledge from LMs involve manually crafted queries, which can lead to uncertainty about whether a failure to retrieve information is due to the model's lack of knowledge or its inability to understand the query. 

The authors introduce a BERT-based rewriter that transforms knowledge-seeking queries into more effective formats for LMs, improving the model's understanding and the accuracy of the responses. They incorporate auxiliary loss functions to ensure that the rewritten queries align with actual language tokens, enhancing the relevance of the queries. The empirical results demonstrate that their approach surpasses existing baseline methods and eliminates the need for complex multi-step pipelines. Additionally, the study provides insights into the types of language that facilitate better knowledge extraction from language models.
