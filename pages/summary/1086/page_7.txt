The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various methods for inductive dynamic link detection on dynamic graphs, specifically focusing on the performance of the proposed VGRNN and SI-VGRNN models. Table 2 summarizes the AUC and AP scores across multiple datasets, including Enron, COLAB, Facebook, Social Evo, HEP-TH, and Cora. The results indicate that VGRNN and SI-VGRNN consistently outperform other methods, such as VGAE, DynAE, and DynRNN, highlighting the effectiveness of stochastic latent variables in capturing complex temporal changes within dynamic graphs.

The study emphasizes that the improvements observed with SI-VGRNN over GRNN and DynAERNN validate the significance of latent random variables in dynamic scenarios. Furthermore, the comparison between VGRNN and VGAE illustrates that the superior performance of the proposed methods is attributed not only to the inclusion of stochastic elements but also to their adeptness in modeling temporal dependencies.

Results from the Cora dataset, which includes node attributes, underscore the advantage of methods utilizing these attributes, as they yield better performance compared to those that do not. The paper also discusses the nuanced differences between SI-VGRNN and VGRNN, noting that while the former allows for more complex latent structures, its benefits may vary depending on the dataset's characteristics.

Additionally, the paper addresses dynamic link prediction tasks, where VGRNN and SI-VGRNN demonstrate significant predictive capabilities, unlike GRNN, which is limited due to its autoencoder training. Overall, the findings advocate for the effectiveness of the proposed models in dynamic link detection and prediction, with particular emphasis on the role of flexible variational inference in enhancing model performance.
