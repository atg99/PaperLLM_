The paper presents an analysis of dynamic link prediction on real-world dynamic graphs, highlighting the performance of various methods measured by Area Under the Curve (AUC) and Average Precision (AP) scores. The results are summarized in Tables 3 and 4, which showcase the performance of different models across datasets like Enron, COLAB, Facebook, and Social Evo.

Key findings include that the proposed models, particularly VGRNN and SI-VGRNN, demonstrate superior generalization capabilities due to the incorporation of random latent variables. The models show a significant improvement in new link prediction, suggesting they effectively capture temporal trends compared to other methods. The comparison between VGRNN and SI-VGRNN indicates minimal difference in prediction results across datasets, attributed to the Gaussian prior still being utilized in the latter.

The analysis of dataset characteristics reveals that the prediction performance of (SI-)VGRNN is notably better for sparse graphs like Facebook, where traditional methods struggle. The evolution of graph statistics, such as density and clustering coefficients, is also examined, revealing that denser graphs with more clusters present less challenge for link prediction than sparse graphs with few clusters. The findings suggest that the stochastic latent variables in the proposed models help mitigate overfitting, thereby enhancing predictive accuracy in complex temporal scenarios. Future work may explore more flexible priors to further improve model performance.
