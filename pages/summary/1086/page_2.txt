In this paper, the authors present a novel approach to enhancing the capabilities of Graph Convolutional Recurrent Networks (GCRN) for modeling dynamic graphs. They argue that traditional GCRNs lack the expressive power needed to fully capture the complex dependencies between the evolving topology of a graph and the time-varying attributes of its nodes. This limitation arises from the standard RNNs' reliance on unimodal or mixture unimodal distributions for output probabilities.

To address this, the authors propose the Variational Graph Recurrent Neural Network (VGRNN), which incorporates high-level latent random variables to improve the modeling of uncertainties in node representations and to better handle sparse dynamic graphs. To further enhance VGRNN's expressive power and interpretability, they integrate semi-implicit variational inference (SIVI), resulting in the Semi-implicit Variational Graph Recurrent Neural Network (SI-VGRNN). This new framework allows for the inference of more flexible and complex posterior distributions.

The effectiveness of VGRNN and SI-VGRNN is demonstrated through experiments on dynamic link prediction tasks across various real-world dynamic graph datasets, where they outperform both baseline and state-of-the-art methods.

The background section elaborates on the foundational concepts of GCRN, which combines graph convolutional networks with recurrent neural networks to effectively capture spatial and temporal patterns in data. The authors highlight the limitations of GCRN in handling high variability in sequences, which can lead to overfitting. They introduce the Graph RNN (GRNN) framework as an extension that accommodates dynamic graphs by allowing different adjacency matrices at different time snapshots. By integrating stochastic latent variables from variational graph autoencoders, GRNN can better capture time dependencies and predict future links.

Overall, the paper contributes to the field of dynamic graph modeling by introducing VGRNN and SI-VGRNN, which leverage advanced variational inference techniques to improve predictive performance and interpretability in dynamic settings.
