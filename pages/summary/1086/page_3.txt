The paper discusses a new model called Variational Graph Recurrent Neural Network (VGRNN), which integrates concepts from variational inference and graph neural networks to address the challenges of dynamic graphs. The authors build upon the existing framework of Stochastic Inference Variational Inference (SIVI), which employs a hierarchical structure to enhance the variational family of posterior distributions. This enables the model to capture more complex relationships and expressiveness without being constrained to an analytic form.

VGRNN is designed to operate on dynamic graphs that evolve over time, represented as a sequence of graphs with potentially changing node and edge sets. The model accommodates variations in both the number of nodes and edges, allowing for the addition or removal of nodes and edges across time steps. It takes variable-length adjacency matrices and node attribute sequences as input, which can differ across snapshots.

The architecture of VGRNN combines the strengths of Variational Graph Autoencoders (VGAE) and Graph Recurrent Neural Networks (GRNN). Each snapshot of the graph is modeled using a VGAE, which is conditioned on the hidden state from the previous time step, enabling the model to account for temporal dependencies. Unlike standard VGAEs, the VGRNN allows for the prior distribution of latent variables to be modeled as complex functions of previous time step information, enhancing the flexibility and expressiveness of the latent representations.

The paper details the construction of the prior distribution for the latent variables, emphasizing the use of parameters that adapt according to the hidden states over time. This approach allows VGRNN to effectively model complex interactions within the evolving graph structure and node attributes, ultimately providing a more interpretable and flexible framework for dynamic graph embeddings.
