The paper presents a novel approach for representation learning in dynamic graphs through the development of a hierarchical variational model called Variational Graph Recurrent Neural Networks (VGRNN). Unlike traditional methods that focus on static graphs, this model incorporates latent random variables to effectively capture both the changes in graph topology and node attributes over time. The authors argue that the inclusion of high-level latent variables allows for a better representation of the inherent variability and uncertainty in dynamic graphs.

To enhance the performance of the VGRNN, the authors introduce a semi-implicit variational inference method (SI-VGRNN), which facilitates the use of flexible non-Gaussian latent representations. This is particularly beneficial for dynamic graph analytics tasks. The experimental results demonstrate that both SI-VGRNN and VGRNN significantly outperform existing baseline and state-of-the-art methods in dynamic link prediction across multiple real-world datasets.

The introduction emphasizes the limitations of existing node embedding techniques that typically treat dynamic graphs as static snapshots, neglecting the temporal dependencies inherent in evolving graphs. In contrast, the proposed method maps each node to a random vector, thereby addressing the uncertainty associated with node representations and enhancing the model's ability to process dynamic graph data.
