The referenced papers collectively address various advancements in machine learning, particularly in the realms of variational inference, automatic differentiation, and network representation learning. 

Dmitry Molchanov and colleagues introduce "Doubly semi-implicit variational inference," which enhances traditional variational methods by incorporating a doubly semi-implicit framework to improve inference accuracy. Adam Paszke et al. present "Automatic differentiation in PyTorch," detailing the capabilities of the PyTorch framework for seamless automatic differentiation, crucial for deep learning applications.

Bryan Perozzi and his team propose "DeepWalk," a method for online learning of social representations, leveraging random walks for effective node embedding in networks. Leonardo Ribeiro and co-authors introduce "struc2vec," which focuses on learning node representations based on structural identity, enabling better performance in graph-based tasks.

Youngjoo Seo and colleagues explore "Structured sequence modeling with graph convolutional recurrent networks," combining graph convolutional networks with recurrent architectures for enhanced modeling of sequential data. Samira Shabanian and others discuss "Variational bi-LSTMs," which apply variational inference to bi-directional LSTM networks, improving sequence modeling.

Jian Tang et al. present "LINE," a method for large-scale information network embedding, emphasizing efficiency and scalability in network representation. Rakshit Trivedi and his team introduce "DyRep," a framework for learning representations over dynamic graphs, capturing temporal changes in network structure.

Mingzhang Yin and Mingyuan Zhou's "Semi-implicit variational inference" offers a new perspective on variational methods, while Lekui Zhou and colleagues investigate "Dynamic network embedding by modeling triadic closure process," contributing to the understanding of network dynamics through embedding techniques.

Overall, these works highlight significant progress in machine learning methodologies, particularly in inference techniques and graph-based learning approaches.
