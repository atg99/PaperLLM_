The paper discusses methods for dynamic graph representation learning, specifically focusing on the Variational Graph Recurrent Neural Network (VGRNN) and its variant, the Stochastic Inference Variational Graph RNN (SI-VGRNN). The authors present a detailed analysis of the datasets used for evaluation, which include Enron, COLAB, Facebook, HEP-TH, Cora, and Social Evolution, highlighting their characteristics such as the number of snapshots, nodes, edges, average density, and node attributes.

The learning framework is based on the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) approach, which allows for the optimization of variational parameters despite the complexity of direct ELBO optimization. The paper introduces a lower bound for the ELBO to facilitate this process.

The experiments compare the proposed methods against four competing node embedding techniques, including two RNN-based methods (DynRNN and DynAERNN), a deep autoencoder (DynAE), and a Variational Graph Autoencoder (VGAE). The goal is to assess the improvements gained by integrating a new prior construction into the RNN backbone compared to traditional deterministic hidden states.

Evaluation tasks focus on three dynamic link prediction problems: dynamic link detection, dynamic link prediction, and dynamic new link prediction. The experimental setup involves assessing the ability of different methods to classify edges correctly, utilizing metrics such as average precision (AP) and area under the ROC curve (AUC). The experiments are conducted using the last three snapshots of the dynamic graphs, with specific validation and testing protocols established for each task.

Overall, the paper aims to demonstrate the effectiveness of VGRNN and SI-VGRNN in capturing temporal dependencies in dynamic graphs, contributing to advancements in dynamic link prediction and representation learning.
