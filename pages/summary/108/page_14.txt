The paper presents an analysis of the performance of Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models in predicting future values of various variables, focusing on different activation functions and architectures. It compares the Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for models using ReLU and sigmoid functions with varying numbers of units in the hidden layer. Key findings indicate that the GRU with the sigmoid activation function and 50 units in the hidden layer yields the lowest Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), achieving an RMSE of 1.06 with 40 units previously tested.

Table 5 summarizes the best results obtained for both MAPE and MAE across different configurations. The GRU with 50 units using the sigmoid function outperformed the ReLU function in terms of error metrics across multiple variables, indicating superior predictive performance.

Furthermore, the paper explores the effectiveness of many-to-many versus many-to-one architectures for the GRU model. The results suggest that the many-to-many architecture, which predicts multiple outputs simultaneously, slightly outperforms the many-to-one approach, where separate models are used for each variable. This indicates that training a single model for all variables can be more efficient without sacrificing accuracy.
