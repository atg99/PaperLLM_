The paper discusses the performance of Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models in predicting many-to-many and many-to-one variables. It explores the impact of different activation functions in the hidden and output layers, specifically examining sigmoid, hyperbolic tangent (tanh), and ReLU functions. The results indicate that using ReLU in the hidden layer and tanh in the output layer yields the best average root mean square error (RMSE) performance, with ReLU-sigmoid and ReLU-ReLU combinations also performing well.

Table 6 presents RMSE values for various activation function combinations, highlighting that different variables can benefit from specific functions. Generally, a configuration of ReLU for the first layer followed by ReLU, sigmoid, or tanh for the subsequent layer is recommended.

The paper notes that raw output predictions exhibited sharp variations, which are not ideal for predictive systems. To address this, the authors applied a median filter for smoothing the predictions, resulting in decreased mean square error (MSE) values, as shown in Table 7. This indicates that smoothing enhances the reliability of predictions.
