The paper discusses the architecture and evaluation of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) in comparison to Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. It highlights the structure of GRUs, which includes an update port that determines the retention of old memory, and a reset port that merges new inputs with existing memory. Unlike LSTMs, GRUs utilize integration to expose memory content with an adaptive time constant.

The sensitivity of deep learning networks to hyperparameters is emphasized, with key parameters such as the number of hidden units, dropout values, and learning rates identified as critical to performance. The paper references studies that underline the importance of hyperparameter tuning in optimizing the learning process and achieving effective outcomes.

For model evaluation, the paper compares LSTM and GRU models using performance metrics including root mean square error (RMSE), mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), and mean absolute error (MAE). These metrics assess the accuracy of model predictions against actual data values.

The data for the experiments arises from industrial presses monitored by six sensors over a period from February 2018 to December 2020, totaling 1004 days of data. Key variables monitored include electric current intensity and hydraulic unit oil, providing a foundation for the analysis conducted in the study.
