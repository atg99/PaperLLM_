The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model for time series data analysis, comparing it with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models. The dataset was split into training and testing subsets, with 70% of samples used for training and 30% for testing. Different resampling rates were examined; aggressive resampling reduced dataset size, enhancing speed while minimizing outliers' impact, but lower rates were preferred for precision.

The study tested various sliding window sizes, ultimately finding that resampling to one sample per hour provided a balanced approach between computational efficiency and model performance. Experiments were conducted to optimize hyperparameters for both LSTM and GRU models, avoiding dense search methods due to their time-intensive nature.

Results indicated that the GRU model, with 40 hidden units and a window of 12 samples, converged rapidly, achieving minimal loss in under 10 epochs. This rapid learning was consistent with earlier findings for LSTM models, prompting the authors to limit future experiments to 15 epochs for further evaluation.
