The paper compares the performance of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models for predicting the condition of pulp paper presses, emphasizing the importance of predictive maintenance in optimizing costs and enhancing equipment availability. Traditional statistical models like ARIMA and SARIMA are inadequate for capturing the stochastic nature of data, making neural networks, particularly LSTM and GRU, more suitable for both univariate and multivariate predictions.

The study indicates that GRU models generally outperform LSTM models when various hyperparameters are considered, specifically in the context of pulp paper presses. The findings suggest that employing GRU can maximize equipment availability, thus supporting the goal of predictive maintenance to efficiently schedule maintenance interventions and avoid unnecessary costs associated with equipment failures.

The introduction highlights the evolution of maintenance strategies towards predictive maintenance, which focuses on anticipating equipment failures using sensory data and advanced forecasting algorithms to determine optimal maintenance timing. This proactive approach aims to prevent unexpected failures while minimizing maintenance costs, ultimately enhancing operational efficiency.
