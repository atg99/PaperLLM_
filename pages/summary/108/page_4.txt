The paper discusses the structure and functionality of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), which are types of recurrent neural networks used in various applications, including electricity generation prediction systems. 

In the LSTM unit, several vectors are defined: the activation vector (houtput), the hidden state vector (hhidden), the cell input activation vector (˜ct), and the cell state vector (ct). The parameters involved, such as weight matrices (W, U) and bias vectors (b), are essential for training the network.

The GRU, introduced by Cho et al., simplifies the LSTM structure by combining the incoming and forgetting ports into a single update port, which enhances computational efficiency. This design retains the advantages of LSTM, particularly its robustness against the vanishing gradient problem. The GRU utilizes an update gate (zt) to determine the retention of previous state information and a reset gate (rt) to control the incorporation of past data into the current state. The mathematical functions governing these mechanisms are outlined, emphasizing the roles of weight matrices and bias in the operations.

Overall, the paper highlights the efficiency and functionality of GRUs compared to LSTMs, particularly in contexts requiring effective state management.
