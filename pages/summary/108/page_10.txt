The paper presents an analysis of the performance of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models in forecasting tasks, focusing on the impact of different window sizes and activation functions. The experiments utilized a dataset comprising 1004 daily samples, with both models configured with 40 hidden layer units. 

Results indicated that the GRU consistently outperformed the LSTM across various window sizes and activation functions, demonstrating minimal performance variation when window sizes were adjusted from two to 12 days. Notably, the ReLU activation function yielded better results than the sigmoid function, with the performance gap between GRU and LSTM widening under the latter.

Error metrics such as Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) were analyzed, revealing that the LSTM with ReLU activation exhibited lower errors compared to its performance with the sigmoid function. Overall, the GRU model showed greater stability and lower prediction errors than the LSTM across all tested conditions. The paper includes tables detailing the optimal window sizes for both models, reinforcing the GRU's superior performance.
