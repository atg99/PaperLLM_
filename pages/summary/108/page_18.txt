The paper investigates the use of Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) networks in predicting future behavior of an industrial paper press, comparing its performance to Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models. The findings indicate that GRU models, particularly the ReLU-tanh configuration, outperform LSTM models in terms of prediction accuracy and processing efficiency when predicting six sensor variables simultaneously. The study highlights that training a single model for all variables is more effective than optimizing separate models for each variable. GRU networks demonstrate lower prediction errors and greater resilience to issues like exploding or vanishing gradients, enabling them to learn effectively across a broader range of configurations.

The research emphasizes the importance of predictive maintenance in minimizing equipment downtime and enhancing production efficiency. While previous studies have primarily focused on univariate data, this paper contributes to the literature by examining multivariate time series. The results suggest that GRU is a superior choice for predictive modeling in industrial applications, with future work planned to explore its performance across varying time gaps.
