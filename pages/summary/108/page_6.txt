The paper discusses the importance of data quality in modeling and planning, specifically in the context of sensor data from hydraulic units. It identifies six key variables: hydraulic unit level, VAT pressure, motor velocity, temperature at the hydraulic unit, and torque. A plot of the raw data reveals typical operational zones and spikes indicating discrepant data. A Q–Q plot further illustrates the non-homogeneity of the data, highlighting extreme values and a non-linear distribution.

To address the issues posed by these discrepant values, the authors emphasize the necessity of data cleaning before analysis, as poor data quality can hinder machine learning models. They propose using the quantile method for data treatment, which effectively removes extreme values often caused by sensor errors or abnormal situations. After applying this method, the resulting data distributions appear more normal, as shown in subsequent figures. This approach ensures that the integrity of the data is maintained while facilitating more accurate analysis.
