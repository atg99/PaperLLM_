This section of the paper presents the results of experiments conducted using Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models to predict various parameters, focusing on the prediction errors measured by Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for different activation functions and resampling rates. 

The data in Table 4 summarizes the best prediction errors for both activation functions (ReLU and Sigmoid) across various parameters, including intensity, hydraulic torque, pressure, velocity, and temperature. Generally, the MAPE values indicate that the models with the Sigmoid activation function produced lower prediction errors compared to those with the ReLU function. Similarly, the MAE results show that the Sigmoid function outperformed ReLU in most categories, suggesting better accuracy in forecasting.

Additionally, the paper discusses experiments that varied the number of units in the hidden layers of the GRU model. The findings indicate that the GRU model can effectively learn from larger datasets and different configurations. Notably, the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) model was unable to yield results with a 1-hour resampling rate, highlighting the GRU's superior adaptability. The optimal time windows for the experiments were identified as 10 days for the sigmoid function and 12 days for the ReLU function.

Overall, the results demonstrate the effectiveness of GRU models in time-series forecasting, particularly under varying activation functions and layer configurations.
