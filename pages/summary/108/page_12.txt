The experiments conducted aimed to optimize dataset size through undersampling techniques, resulting in a significant reduction of data points. Various sampling intervals were utilized: one sample every 12 hours, 6 hours, 3 hours, and 1 hour. The optimal window sizes identified were 4 days for LSTM and 12 days for GRU with the ReLU activation function, while 6 days for LSTM and 10 days for GRU with the sigmoid function.

Results indicated that while LSTM occasionally outperformed GRU, particularly with the sigmoid function at 6 and 3-hour intervals, the differences were not statistically significant. GRU consistently demonstrated robustness, maintaining an RMSE error of approximately 1 across all conditions, whereas LSTM exhibited instability, particularly at shorter sampling periods.

Further analysis, as shown in Figures 13 and 14, revealed that errors were generally lower with the sigmoid function. The LSTM model struggled with learning at 6-hour intervals, where error gradients escalated significantly, while GRU continued to learn effectively with acceptable error rates.
