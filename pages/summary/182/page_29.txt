The paper discusses the statistical properties of natural images, highlighting two key aspects: scale invariance and non-Gaussianity. Scale invariance refers to the consistency of image statistics when images are scaled, while non-Gaussianity indicates that the distribution of linear filter responses is sharply peaked at zero, with heavy tails and high kurtosis. Recent findings suggest that the contrast statistics of natural images follow a Weibull distribution.

Zeng et al. proposed evaluating generative models based on low-level statistics of generated images compared to natural scenes. They examined four statistics: mean power spectrum, number of connected components, distribution of random filter responses, and contrast distribution. Their findings revealed that while generated images from models like DCGAN, WGAN, and VAE resemble natural images in some statistics, they still exhibit significant differences, particularly in scale invariant mean power spectrum magnitude, suggesting additional structures due to deconvolution processes.

The use of low-level image statistics is recommended for regularizing GANs, allowing for better alignment of the generator's output with real sample statistics through a feature matching loss function. Karras et al. further explored multi-scale statistical similarities using the Wasserstein distance to compare local image patches from generated and real images, enabling detailed analysis of both large-scale structures and pixel-level attributes.

Lucic et al. introduced precision, recall, and F1 score as metrics to assess overfitting in GANs. Precision reflects the quality of generated samples, while recall assesses the coverage of the learned distribution relative to the reference distribution. They argue that traditional metrics like Inception Score (IS) do not adequately account for completeness, whereas the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) effectively captures both precision and recall. To estimate these scores, they suggest using toy datasets with known data manifolds.
