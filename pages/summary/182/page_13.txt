The paper discusses a method for determining whether two probability distributions, P and Q, are equal by evaluating a generator on a held-out test set. The process involves several steps:

1. **Dataset Construction**: A combined dataset D is created from two samples, SP and SQ, where each sample consists of n instances.

2. **Data Splitting**: The dataset D is shuffled and divided into two disjoint subsets: a training set (Dtrain) and a testing set (Dtest).

3. **Binary Classifier Training**: A binary classifier is trained on the training set to distinguish between real and generated images.

4. **Accuracy Calculation**: The classification accuracy on the testing set is computed using a specific formula, which serves as the C2ST statistic. If the distributions are equal, the accuracy should be near chance-level. A significant performance above chance indicates that P and Q are not equal.

5. **Hypothesis Testing**: A p-value is computed using the null distribution of the C2ST to accept or reject the null hypothesis that P equals Q.

The paper highlights the use of the 1-Nearest Neighbor (1-NN) classifier as a variation of this method, noting that it requires minimal training and tuning. The accuracy of the 1-NN classifier can range from 0% to 100%, depending on the similarity between the real and generated samples. A performance of 50% indicates random labeling, while extremes approach 0% or 100% based on the overlap between the sample sets. The work references previous literature on two-sample tests, emphasizing its relevance in the context of generative models.
