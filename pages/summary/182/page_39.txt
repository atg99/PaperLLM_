The discussion section of the paper highlights alternative evaluation measures for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) beyond the commonly used metrics. Vedantam et al. developed measures for visually grounded imagination, including correctness, coverage, and compositionality, to assess the quality of generated images. Zhu et al. focused on measuring diversity by calculating average pair-wise distances in a deep feature space, comparing generated samples to real images. Im et al. explored divergence and distance measures used in GAN training, demonstrating consistency across four metrics: Jensen-Shannon Divergence, Constrained Pearson Chi-squared, Maximum Mean Discrepancy, and Wasserstein Distance.

The section also emphasizes the importance of sample and computational efficiencies in evaluating GANs. Huang et al. argue that effective evaluation measures should require a reasonable number of samples and manageable computational costs, especially for monitoring training. They propose criteria for assessing measures based on sample efficiency—how well a measure can distinguish between generated and real samples—and computational efficiency, which pertains to the speed of computation relative to the number of evaluated samples.
