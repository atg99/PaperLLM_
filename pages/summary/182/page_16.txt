The paper discusses the evaluation of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and their impact on classifier performance due to covariate shift. Figure 5 illustrates the spectrum of a vanilla GAN's learned distribution in a 200D latent space compared to a true 75D spherical unimodal Gaussian distribution. It highlights how covariate shift can distort the learned decision boundary of a logistic regression classifier, with implications for classifier performance on hold-out sets of true data.

To quantify the diversity of generated samples and detect mode collapse, the authors introduce the Number of Statistically-Different Bins (NDB) metric, following the methodology of Richardson and Weiss. They note that when samples are drawn from the same distribution, the distribution of samples across bins should be similar, barring random noise. The NDB metric uses an indicator function to assess whether the proportions of samples in specific bins from two distributions (training and testing) differ significantly. The z-score is calculated to determine statistical differences, with results being aggregated across all bins.

To address challenges in high-dimensional spaces where uniform binning may lead to empty bins, the authors advocate for the use of Voronoi cells. This method ensures that each bin contains samples by clustering training samples into clusters using K-means, allowing for effective assignment of generated samples to the nearest cluster. Figure 6 provides a visual representation of this binning approach in a two-dimensional space.
