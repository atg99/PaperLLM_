The paper discusses the evaluation of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) with a focus on the desired properties that effective evaluation measures should possess. It highlights a schematic layout for a typical sample-based GAN evaluation approach, indicating real samples (Sr) and generated samples (Sg). The measures used for evaluation are categorized, with most returning a single value, while some (GAM and NRDS) allow for relative comparisons to determine which model produces better images in the absence of a perfect measure.

The author outlines several key properties that an efficient GAN evaluation measure should fulfill, which can serve as criteria for assessing the effectiveness of various measures. These properties include:

1. Discriminability: The ability to distinguish generated samples from real ones.
2. Diversity: Sensitivity to issues like overfitting, mode collapse, and mode drop, ensuring models produce a wide range of outputs.
3. Disentangled latent spaces and continuity: Allowing for controllable sampling.
4. Well-defined bounds: Establishing lower, upper, and chance bounds for evaluation.
5. Sensitivity to distortions and transformations: The measure should remain consistent despite minor changes in the images, such as shifts or rotations.
6. Agreement with human perceptual judgments: The measures should align with how humans rank and perceive model outputs.
7. Low sample and computational complexity: Ensuring efficiency in evaluation.

The paper aims to discuss and assess various GAN evaluation measures based on these desiderata, culminating in a summary of findings in a later section.
