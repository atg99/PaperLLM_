The paper discusses the evaluation of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and highlights the lack of comprehensive quantitative and qualitative assessment methods. It categorizes generative models into explicit approaches, which include models like variational auto-encoders and PixelCNN, and implicit approaches, mainly represented by GANs. Explicit models rely on access to the likelihood function, while GANs utilize a generator and discriminator in an adversarial framework to approximate a data distribution.

The paper addresses the challenges in evaluating GANs, noting that existing evaluation measures vary in their focus—some are quantitative while others are qualitative, such as user studies. Each approach has its strengths and weaknesses; for instance, a qualitative measure might favor models that overfit to specific data sections, whereas quantitative measures may not align with human perception of generated images.

The author aims to critically review the available GAN evaluation measures, categorizing them into quantitative and qualitative types, while also discussing their advantages and disadvantages. Additionally, the paper proposes a set of desired properties for effective GAN measures and evaluates how well existing measures align with these properties. The ultimate goal is to provide recommendations for developing more efficient measures for fair evaluation and comparison of GANs.
