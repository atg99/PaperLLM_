The paper discusses various methodologies for evaluating the diversity of images generated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). It highlights the work of Shmelkov et al., who introduced a comparative framework using class-conditional GANs. They focused on two metrics: GAN-train, which measures the performance of a classifier trained on GAN-generated images evaluated on real images, and GAN-test, which assesses a classifier trained on real images evaluated on generated images. Their analysis shows that low diversity in generated samples leads to redundant outputs, diminishing the effectiveness of additional data generation, while high diversity enhances the GAN-train score.

Furthermore, the concept of boundary distortion, explored by Santurkar et al., is presented as a form of covariate shift in GANs where the generator tends to concentrate on a few modes of the true distribution. This is illustrated through two examples: one involving a unimodal Gaussian distribution and another focusing on binary classification. Both examples reveal that boundary distortion can significantly impair the generalization performance on true data. To measure this distortion, Santurkar et al. proposed a method involving the training of separate GAN instances for each class, generating a balanced dataset, and then training a binary classifier on this dataset and comparing it to a classifier trained on true data.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of evaluating generative models not only based on their performance in surrogate tasks but also concerning the diversity and quality of the generated samples.
