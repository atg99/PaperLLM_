The paper discusses the Generative Adversarial Metric (GAM) and its application in evaluating Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). During training, two generators (G1 and G2) compete against two discriminators (D1 and D2). At test time, the effectiveness of the models M1 and M2 is assessed by whether G1 can successfully deceive D2 more than G2 can deceive D1, and vice versa.

The evaluations serve two primary purposes: monitoring the progress of a single model throughout its training (within trajectory tournament) and comparing the performance of different fully trained models (multiple trajectory tournament). In these tournaments, the players consist of either generators, which aim to convince discriminators that fake data is real, or discriminators, which strive to differentiate real data from fake.

The tournament win rate is calculated based on how well the discriminator judges samples from the generator compared to real data. A win for the generator occurs when the discriminator fails to correctly identify generated samples. A win rate of 0.5 indicates that the discriminator's performance is no better than random chance. The overall win rate for a generator is derived by averaging its performance against all discriminators involved in the tournament.

Olsson et al. conducted a tournament using 20 checkpoints of discriminators and generators from a DCGAN trained on the SVHN dataset. The results included both raw tournament outcomes and summarized metrics such as tournament win rate, skill rating, SVHN classifier score, and Fréchet distance, providing a comprehensive comparison of model performance.
