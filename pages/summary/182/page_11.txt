The paper discusses two evaluation measures for comparing probability distributions: the kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) and the Wasserstein distance. 

Kernel MMD quantifies the difference between two distributions Pr and Pg using a characteristic kernel function, such as the Gaussian kernel. The MMD is estimated using finite samples from the distributions, leading to an estimator that accounts for sampling variance. Despite this variance, kernel MMD effectively distinguishes between generated and real images, particularly when applied in the feature space of a pre-trained Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). Additionally, kernel MMD has been integrated into Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), such as in the Generative Moment Matching Network (GMMN), replacing traditional discriminators with a two-sample test based on MMD.

The Wasserstein critic approximates the Wasserstein distance between real and generated data distributions. It employs a Lipschitz continuous function, typically represented by a neural network, which is trained to assign high values to real samples and low values to generated ones. The practical implementation calculates the distance by averaging the outputs of the critic over batches of test and generated samples. For discrete distributions, the Wasserstein distance is also known as Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD), representing the minimum effort required to transform one distribution into another. A variant, the sliced Wasserstein distance (SWD), offers an approximation of this measure. 

Overall, both kernel MMD and the Wasserstein distance provide valuable metrics for evaluating generative models, with distinct advantages in their application and computation.
