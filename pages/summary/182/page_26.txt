The paper discusses two key image quality assessment metrics: the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) and the Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR). 

SSIM evaluates the similarity between two images by considering luminance, contrast, and structure components. It calculates a sample correlation coefficient between corresponding pixels in a defined neighborhood. The SSIM score is given by combining these components, with constants added for numerical stability. A variant, Multi-Scale SSIM (MS-SSIM), extends this by operating at multiple scales, applying the contrast and structure components across different downsampled versions of the images, while the luminance component is applied only at the coarsest scale. MS-SSIM ranges from 0 to 1, indicating low to high similarity, respectively. Furthermore, Snellet al. introduced a loss function for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) based on the sum of MS-SSIM scores across all pixels, facilitating gradient descent due to its simple analytical derivative.

PSNR, on the other hand, measures the quality of a generated image against a reference image, typically used in scenarios like conditional GANs. It is calculated based on the mean squared error (MSE) between the images and is expressed in decibels (dB). A higher PSNR indicates better image quality, making it a valuable metric when paired data is available for training.

Overall, both SSIM and PSNR serve as critical tools for evaluating image quality in various applications, particularly in the context of GANs.
