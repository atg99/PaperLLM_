The paper discusses mode collapse in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) using examples from the CelebA and LSUN datasets. It highlights how mode collapse leads to a covariate shift, impacting the distribution of generated samples compared to the true data distribution. The left panel of Figure 18 illustrates this discrepancy, while the right panel tracks the evolution of class distributions during training.

Additionally, the paper explores methods for evaluating generative models by investigating their internal representations and latent space dynamics. It emphasizes the concept of disentangled representations, where semantic visual concepts align with axes in the latent space. Various tests, including those proposed by prior studies, assess whether these representations are semantically meaningful and capture true factors of variation. Radford et al. introduced methods for analyzing the learned manifold, suggesting that significant transitions during latent space navigation could indicate memorization or hierarchical collapse. They also demonstrated that semantic changes in generated images during latent space manipulation indicate the model's ability to learn relevant representations.
