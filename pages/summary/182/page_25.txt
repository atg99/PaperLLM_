The figure illustrates the construction and analysis of simplicial complexes based on proximity parameters in a dataset X. In part A, a fixed proximity parameter epsilon is chosen, and balls of radius epsilon centered at each point in the dataset are examined. If the pairwise intersections of these balls for a subset of size k+1 are nonempty, a k-dimensional simplex is added to the simplicial complex Rx.

Part B shows the impact of varying epsilon values on the simplicial complex. For epsilon equal to epsilon1, the balls do not intersect, resulting in 10 isolated components. When epsilon increases to epsilon2, components merge, forming a loop, while the filled triangle from the triple intersection remains topologically trivial. At epsilon3, all components merge into one, maintaining a single hole. An interval between epsilon2 and epsilon3 reveals a transient smaller hole. The persistence barcode in part C summarizes the number of connected components and holes in relation to specific epsilon values, with H0 and H1 representing connected components and holes, respectively.

Part D presents the Mean Relative Living Times (MRLT) for various synthetic 2D datasets, accurately identifying the number of 1D holes. The MRLTs indicate that the second dataset closely aligns with the ground truth. Additionally, it compares MRLTs of the MNIST dataset with those generated by WGAN and WGAN-GP, showing that while both models perform similarly, WGAN-GP exhibits slightly superior results across most classes.
