The paper discusses the comparison between classifier two-sample tests and Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) in evaluating unsupervised representation learning. MMD is highlighted for its U-statistic estimator with Gaussian asymptotic distribution, making it advantageous when U-statistic convergence is prioritized over the potential power of classifiers, especially deep networks. Conversely, classifier-based tests may outperform MMD if the classifier surpasses the chosen kernel.

In terms of classification performance, the paper outlines a common approach of using unsupervised learning algorithms as feature extractors for labeled datasets. For instance, Radford et al. utilized features from a DC-GAN trained on ImageNet to classify CIFAR-10 images, achieving an accuracy of 82.8%, comparable to direct CIFAR-10 training baselines. Similar methodologies were applied to evaluate conditional GANs, where Zhang et al. measured the realism of generated images using a pre-trained VGG classifier, linking classifier performance to the semantic accuracy of colorizations.

The concept of the GAN Quality Index (GQI) is introduced by Ye et al. to assess GANs, which involves training a generator on a labeled dataset and comparing the accuracies of a classifier on real versus generated images. A higher GQI indicates better alignment of the GAN's output with real data distributions.

Lastly, the paper touches on data augmentation, noting that certain studies evaluate GANs' utility in generating diverse training samples. Lesort et al. suggested mixing real and generated data to train classifiers, subsequently testing them on labeled datasets to measure performance. This approach emphasizes the importance of diversity in the generated images for enhancing model training.
