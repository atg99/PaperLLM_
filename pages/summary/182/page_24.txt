The paper discusses various performance metrics for evaluating generative models, particularly focusing on Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP), which outperforms other models across most classes. A notable metric mentioned is the geometry score, which assesses topological properties without relying on auxiliary networks or visual data. However, it is limited in evaluating visual quality alone, prompting the authors to suggest using it alongside other measures like Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) when analyzing natural images.

The paper also addresses reconstruction error as a key evaluation metric for generative models, particularly in the context of Variational Autoencoders and GANs. The reconstruction error is defined mathematically and involves minimizing the L2 norm between generated samples and test samples. The method proposed by Xiang and Li for estimating this error involves optimizing a latent code through gradient descent to reduce computation time during training.

Additionally, the authors highlight the use of image quality measures such as Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) and Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) for evaluating GANs. SSIM, in particular, is emphasized for its perceptual similarity capabilities, focusing on luminance, contrast, and structure to compare images effectively. The paper outlines the mathematical foundations of these measures to underscore their relevance in assessing generative model performance.
