The paper discusses the NDB evaluation method for assessing the performance of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) using a toy example in R2. It illustrates the training data and the resulting Voronoi cells, alongside samples generated by a GAN. The NDB measure differs from traditional metrics like IS and FID, as it operates directly on image pixels, making it domain agnostic and sensitive to various image artifacts. Unlike MS-SSIM and the Birthday Paradox Test, NDB assesses the relationship between actual data and generated distributions rather than merely evaluating sample diversity. However, a noted concern is the potential lack of meaningfulness when using L2 distance in pixel space for similarity measurement.

Additionally, the paper presents an image retrieval performance evaluation proposed by Wang et al., which focuses on identifying poorly modeled images by the GAN. This method uses a discriminatively trained CNN to represent both test and generated images, retrieving nearest neighbors from the test dataset. Two measures are introduced to evaluate retrieval quality: one involves the Wilcoxon signed-rank test to compare distance distributions between generated and test images, while the other examines the distribution of distances from training images to the test dataset. This approach aims to statistically determine the effectiveness of different GAN methods based on image retrieval performance.
