The paper critiques the Inception Score (IS) used for evaluating Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and highlights several limitations. 

Firstly, IS tends to favor a "memory GAN" that retains all training samples, which can lead to overfitting and misrepresentation of generated data modes. Secondly, it does not identify mode collapse, where the model gets stuck generating only a few types of outputs. Thirdly, the IS relies on the Inception model trained on ImageNet, which may prioritize generating recognizable objects over realistic images. Fourthly, IS only evaluates the generator's distribution while ignoring the real data distribution, making it susceptible to manipulation from unrelated natural images. Fifthly, IS is an asymmetric measure, and lastly, it is influenced by image resolution.

Zhou et al. provide insights into the behavior of the entropy components within the IS, noting discrepancies in class distributions when using different Inception models. They argue that while low IS values can indicate mode collapse, high scores can also be achieved by models that simply memorize examples, undermining the reliability of IS as a collapse indicator.

To address these issues, the paper introduces the Modified Inception Score (m-IS), which incorporates a cross-entropy score to better assess diversity within generated samples from the same category. This modification aims to improve the evaluation of GANs by promoting diversity in outputs while still considering the quality of generated images.
