The paper discusses two methods for evaluating the performance of models in the context of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) using the SVHN dataset. 

The first method is the Skill Rating system, which employs the Glicko2 system to summarize tournament outcomes based on the information provided by each match. In this system, a player's skill rating is modeled as a Gaussian distribution with a mean and standard deviation, reflecting the current understanding of their true skill. The authors, Olsson et al., organized a tournament using various GAN snapshots that differed in loss functions and architectures, incorporating checkpoints from discriminators and generators. This approach allows for adaptability to useful features and the inclusion of human judges as discriminators, although it results in relative scores that can complicate reproducibility.

The second method is the Normalized Relative Discriminative Score (NRDS), proposed by Zhang et al. This metric aims to measure how effectively a model generates samples compared to real ones by assessing the performance of a binary classifier (discriminator). The premise is that distinguishing high-quality generated samples from real samples requires more training epochs than distinguishing poor samples. The NRDS involves three steps: generating curves of discriminator output over epochs for each model, calculating the area under these curves to determine the decrement rate, and comparing these rates to evaluate model performance. Models that produce samples closer to real data show slower decrement rates, indicating better performance.

Overall, both methods provide insights into training progress and model effectiveness in generating realistic samples, with distinct advantages and challenges.
