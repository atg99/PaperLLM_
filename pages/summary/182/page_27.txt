The paper discusses various metrics used to evaluate the sharpness and diversity of generated images in the context of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Sharpness Difference (SD) is introduced as a measure of sharpness loss during image generation, calculated using the gradient of the images. Odena et al. utilized Multi-Scale Structural Similarity Index (MS-SSIM) to assess the diversity of generated images, noting that higher MS-SSIM scores indicate greater similarity between image pairs. They found variability in MS-SSIM scores across ImageNet classes, revealing that a significant number of classes exhibited lower mean MS-SSIM scores compared to maximum training data scores, suggesting potential generator collapse.

Additionally, Juefei-Xu et al. applied SSIM and PSNR metrics for evaluating GAN performance in image completion tasks, emphasizing the clarity of direct comparisons between ground-truth and generated images. Other quality measures like Universal Quality Index (UQI) and Visual Information Fidelity (VIF) are also mentioned. The paper highlights that while MS-SSIM effectively identifies large-scale mode collapses, it struggles with smaller variations in color and texture, and does not assess image quality in relation to the training set. Finally, it notes that natural scenes represent only a small subset of all possible images, possessing unique characteristics that need consideration in quality assessments.
