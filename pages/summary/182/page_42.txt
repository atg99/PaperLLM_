This paper reviews various measures used to evaluate Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), highlighting their strengths and limitations. Different evaluation metrics are suitable for different scenarios, such as likelihood for compression methods and psychophysical assessments for image reconstruction. The paper distinguishes between measures for generic GANs and conditional GANs, noting that some scores are based on similar concepts like class separability and distribution distance.

Key evaluation aspects include fidelity, diversity, and controllable sampling. While some qualitative measures focus solely on visual fidelity, they often neglect diversity, which is crucial for comprehensive evaluation. Conversely, certain quantitative measures prioritize diversity at the expense of fidelity. The paper emphasizes the need for a balanced metric that captures both aspects effectively.

The review also discusses the computational efficiency of various measures, indicating that some are more practical for smaller sample sizes, while others struggle with larger datasets. The ongoing challenge of identifying appropriate evaluation measures is underscored, as it is vital for model comparison and the advancement of generative models. Overall, the paper calls for further research in this area to enhance the understanding and development of GANs.
