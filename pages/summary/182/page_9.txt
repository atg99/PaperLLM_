The paper discusses metrics for evaluating the quality of generated images, specifically focusing on the Inception Score (IS) and the Adversarial Metric (AM) score. Figure 3 illustrates training curves for IS and its components, highlighting that while the entropy term H(y) decreases contrary to expectations, the conditional entropy H(y|x) decreases as anticipated during training. The AM score is defined as the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the training label distribution and the predicted label distribution, plus the expected conditional entropy. A lower AM score indicates better performance.

The paper emphasizes that the Inception score, when using a model trained on ImageNet, does not adequately reflect the distribution of CIFAR-10 data. Instead, utilizing a pre-trained CIFAR-10 classifier improves the accuracy of the AM score in capturing the dataset's statistics. 

Additionally, the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) is introduced as a method for assessing the quality of generated samples. By embedding samples into a feature space from a specific layer of a CNN, the FID computes the distance between the means and covariances of the generated and real data distributions, providing a quantitative measure of sample quality.
