The paper discusses the Wasserstein-1 distance as a measure of statistical similarity between real and generated images, utilizing local image patches from Laplacian pyramid representations. This measure, referred to as the Statistical Wasserstein Distance (SWD), is effective in addressing overfitting and mode collapse in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). If the generator memorizes training data, the critic can easily distinguish between real samples and generated ones. Similarly, in the case of mode collapse, the critic can differentiate between the two distributions without saturation when they overlap. However, the Wasserstein distance has limitations, including high sample and time complexity, making it less favorable compared to other evaluation metrics.

The paper also introduces the Birthday Paradox Test, proposed by Arora and Zhang, to estimate the support size of a discrete distribution in the context of GANs. The test involves sampling from the generated distribution, identifying the most similar pairs, and visually inspecting them for duplicates. This method estimates the support size based on the presence of near-duplicates, with findings indicating that a sample of about 400 images from the CelebA dataset has a high probability of containing duplicates, thus highlighting potential mode collapse.

Additionally, Classifier Two-sample Tests (C2ST) are mentioned as a means to determine whether two samples originate from the same distribution. Overall, these methodologies provide insights into evaluating the performance and robustness of GANs in generating diverse and high-quality images.
