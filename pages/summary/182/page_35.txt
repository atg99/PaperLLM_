The paper discusses the challenge of detecting mode collapse in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), particularly when trained on large-scale image datasets. Mode collapse occurs when the GAN generates samples concentrated around a single mode, which can be difficult to evaluate without known distributions. Srivastava et al. proposed a method to quantify mode collapse by sampling points from the generator and assessing their proximity to mode centers. A sample is deemed high quality if it is within a specific distance from a mode center, and the number of modes captured corresponds to the number of mixture components nearest to high-quality samples.

Santurkar et al. introduced a method to investigate mode distribution by training GANs on balanced datasets and testing the generated data for balance. This involves training a classifier on the dataset and using it to label synthetic samples produced by the GAN. Their findings indicate that GANs frequently exhibit mode collapse.

Additionally, the reverse KL divergence has been utilized to measure mode collapse quality by comparing the distributions of generated and real samples based on their proximity to modes. However, the measures discussed are limited to datasets with known modes, making it challenging to quantify mode collapse and mode drop in real-world scenarios. The difficulty of identifying nearest neighbors in high-dimensional spaces adds to the complexity, highlighting the need for ongoing research in this area.
