The paper discusses the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) as a measure for evaluating the quality of generated images. FID assesses the distance between the distributions of synthetic and real data by comparing their means and covariances. A lower FID indicates a closer match between the two distributions, making it a strong metric in terms of discriminability, robustness, and computational efficiency. While FID effectively captures the first two moments of distributions, it assumes Gaussian distribution characteristics, which may not always hold true. The measure correlates well with human judgment and outperforms Inception Score (IS) in robustness to noise, as evidenced by a negative correlation between FID and the visual quality of generated samples. Unlike IS, FID can detect intra-class mode dropping and is adversely affected by image artifacts. Additionally, Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) is introduced as another method for assessing dissimilarity between two probability distributions, where a lower MMD indicates closer alignment with the true data distribution. MMD functions as a two-sample test, evaluating the proximity of models to actual data distributions.
