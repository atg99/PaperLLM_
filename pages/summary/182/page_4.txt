This section discusses quantitative measures for evaluating Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), focusing on model-agnostic methods and those requiring density estimation. 

One key measure is Average Log-likelihood, which utilizes Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to estimate the probability density function from samples. This involves using a probability kernel, often an isotropic Gaussian, to define the density at a point. While log-likelihood, or Kullback-Leibler divergence, has been standard for evaluating generative models, its effectiveness in high-dimensional spaces has been questioned. 

The Parzen window method, which estimates density using a finite set of generated samples as centroids of a Gaussian mixture, can struggle with high-dimensional data, necessitating a large sample size for accuracy. Alternative methods like annealed importance sampling (AIS) have been proposed, showing significantly improved accuracy over KDE. However, AIS relies on a Gaussian observation model, which may not be suitable for all high-dimensional spaces. 

Despite its intuitive appeal, log-likelihood has drawbacks, particularly in high dimensions, where Parzen window estimates can fail to approximate true log-likelihood effectively, even with a moderate number of samples.
