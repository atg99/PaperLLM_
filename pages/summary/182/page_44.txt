The paper presents a measurement of wall-clock time required to compute various distance measures as a function of sample size. It indicates that while most measures are feasible for sample sizes up to 2000, the Wasserstein distance struggles to scale effectively with larger sample sizes. This observation highlights the computational limitations of the Wasserstein distance in practical applications. The references provided include significant works related to domain-adversarial training, adversarial domain adaptation, generative adversarial networks, and image processing techniques, underscoring the relevance of these methodologies in the context of the discussed measures.
