The paper discusses various metrics for evaluating Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). One key concept introduced is the relative increase in mean nearest neighbor distance, which measures how closely generated images resemble those in the training set. This metric is calculated using the formula provided, where a value of ^d1=0.1 indicates that the average distance to the nearest neighbor of a generated image is 10% greater than that of real images.

Another significant metric is the Generative Adversarial Metric (GAM), proposed by Imet et al. It involves comparing two GANs by swapping their discriminators or generators and assessing their performance based on likelihood ratios. The hypothesis states that one GAN is superior if it can fool the opponent's discriminator more effectively. The paper also mentions a variation called the generative multi-adversarial metric (GMAM), which accommodates multiple discriminators.

However, GAM has limitations, including the need for similar performance levels between discriminators on calibration datasets and the computational expense of conducting pairwise comparisons across multiple models.

Additionally, the paper describes the Tournament Win Rate and Skill Rating method introduced by Olsson et al., which is inspired by the aforementioned metrics and skill rating systems in competitive games. This method involves organizing tournaments between generators and discriminators to summarize performance outcomes through win rates.
