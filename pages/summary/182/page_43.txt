The paper discusses the robustness of different evaluation measures for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in light of small image transformations like rotations and translations. It emphasizes that an effective evaluation measure should remain stable despite these transformations, as they do not affect the image's semantics. The authors critique the lack of empirical evidence supporting claims of superiority among various GAN models, particularly when compared to the original GAN. They suggest that insights from fields such as natural scene statistics and cognitive vision could enhance the understanding of image generation. To advance research, the authors propose three directions: creating a repository of evaluation measures, performing comparative studies of these measures, and standardizing benchmarking conditions across models.
