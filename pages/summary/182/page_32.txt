Figure 14 presents three key observations related to image analysis and modeling. 

Part A illustrates how minor shifts in an image, ranging from 1 to 4 pixels, can significantly alter the Euclidean distance, thereby influencing the selection of the nearest neighbor from a training set. The left column displays the query images, while the right column shows their corresponding nearest neighbors. The gray lines represent the Euclidean distances between the query image and 100 randomly selected images from the training set.

Part B focuses on the limitations of Parzen window estimates for Gaussian distributions when applied to 6 by 6 pixel image patches from the CIFAR-10 dataset. Despite utilizing a vast number of samples, the Parzen window estimate remains significantly distant from the actual log-likelihood.

Part C examines the application of Parzen window estimates in evaluating different models trained on the MNIST dataset. It highlights that samples derived from the true distribution perform worse than those from a simpler model trained using k-means clustering.

Overall, the figure underscores the challenges in image representation and modeling accuracy, particularly when relying on Euclidean distance and density estimation techniques.
