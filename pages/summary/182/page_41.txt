the evaluation of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) lacks a consensus on the best measure. A review of various evaluation measures reveals several shortcomings: only two measures explicitly address overfitting, most do not consider disentangled representations, and few have both lower and upper bounds. There is unclear agreement between measures and human perceptual judgments, and while some measures are computationally efficient, their sensitivity to image distortions has not been thoroughly explored.

The analysis indicates that different scores assess distinct aspects of the image generation process, making it unlikely for a single score to encompass all necessary evaluations. The Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score is noted as more plausible, while average likelihood and Parzen window estimations are criticized for favoring trivial models and failing to rank models effectively in high dimensions. The reliance of widely accepted scores like Inception Score and FID on pre-trained deep networks introduces limitations, as these networks may overlook image artifacts and may not generalize well across different domains.

Methods aimed at assessing sample diversity, such as MS-SSIM and the Birthday Paradox Test, can identify severe mode collapse but do not effectively measure adherence to the true data distribution. Quality measures may inadvertently favor overfitting, highlighting the challenge of simultaneously evaluating diversity and visual fidelity. Theis et al. suggest that GAN evaluation should be application-specific rather than task-independent, advocating for tailored measures based on the intended use of the generated images.
