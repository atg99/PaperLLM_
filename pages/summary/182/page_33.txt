The paper discusses the effectiveness of different generative adversarial networks (GANs) in producing realistic images, with a focus on human evaluation metrics. In a study comparing real CIFAR10 images and samples from various GAN models, the class conditional LAPGAN demonstrated a 40% success rate in fooling human evaluators, significantly outperforming the standard GAN model, which had only a 10% success rate. However, both models fell short of the >90% realism associated with actual images.

To assess the quality of generated samples, a rapid scene categorization task was employed, where subjects were asked to distinguish between real and generated images under varying viewing times (50ms to 2000ms). This "Turing-like" test indicated that the class conditional LAPGAN was superior in deceiving subjects compared to the original GAN. Despite its intuitive nature, the paper highlights several concerns regarding the practical implementation of such tests, including control over experimental conditions and potential biases that could favor models that overfit training data.

Additionally, the paper mentions rating and preference judgment experiments, where subjects evaluate the fidelity of generated images. These evaluations are critical in determining the perceived quality of generative models and their ability to produce diverse and realistic outputs. Overall, the findings underscore the challenges and considerations in evaluating generative models through human judgment.
