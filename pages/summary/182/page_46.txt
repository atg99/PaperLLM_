This collection of references focuses on various aspects of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and their evaluation metrics. Key studies include empirical analyses on the effectiveness of GANs in learning distributions and generating images. Shmelkov et al. investigate the quality of GAN outputs, while Huang et al. conduct an empirical study on evaluation metrics for GANs. Arora and Zhang explore whether GANs genuinely learn data distributions. Chen et al. propose metrics for assessing deep generative models, and Wu et al. analyze decoder-based generative models quantitatively.

Other notable works include Alemi et al.'s information-theoretic analysis of latent-variable models and Im et al.'s development of recurrent adversarial networks for image generation. Additionally, Zhang et al. present decoupled learning for conditional GANs, and Tolstikhin et al. introduce Adagan, a boosting method for generative models. Gurumurthy et al. discuss GANs designed for diverse and limited datasets, while Che et al. focus on mode regularization in GANs. The references also cover convergence properties of GANs trained with a two-time-scale update rule, as reported by Heusel et al., and introduce kernel two-sample tests for evaluating distributions, as detailed by Gretton et al. Overall, these studies contribute to the understanding and improvement of GAN methodologies and their evaluation.
