The paper discusses methods for qualitatively assessing the quality of images generated by models, particularly focusing on the nearest neighbor approach. Figure 13 illustrates real images from the CIFAR-10 dataset alongside the nearest generated images by various models including DCGAN, ALI, Unrolled GAN, and VEEGAN. The author highlights two main concerns with the traditional nearest neighbor evaluation method: 

1. Nearest neighbors are often identified using Euclidean distance, which can be overly sensitive to slight perceptual changes, leading to misleading comparisons. This issue is supported by findings in psychophysics literature.
  
2. Models that retain modified training images, such as memory GANs, can easily pass nearest-neighbor evaluations, raising questions about their validity.

To address these issues, the author suggests using perceptual metrics for identifying nearest neighbors and recommends displaying multiple nearest neighbors to provide a more robust evaluation.
