The paper presents a comparative analysis of various quantitative evaluation scores for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The evaluation metrics are assessed based on several desiderata including discriminability, detecting overfitting, disentangled latent spaces, well-defined bounds, perceptual judgments, sensitivity to distortions, computational efficiency, and sample efficiency.

The metrics are rated on a scale of low to high across these criteria. Key findings include:

1. Average Log-likelihood, Coverage Metric, and Boundary Distortion score low on most measures.
2. Metrics like Inception Score, Modified Inception Score, and Fréchet Inception Distance score high in discriminability and perceptual judgments but moderate in detecting overfitting.
3. The Wasserstein Critic shows high discriminability but low sensitivity to distortions.
4. Classifier Two Sample Test and Classification Performance demonstrate high discriminability but low sample efficiency.
5. Many measures lack systematic evaluation of disentangled latent spaces, indicating a gap in current research.

Overall, the table highlights the strengths and weaknesses of each metric, suggesting that while some metrics perform well in certain areas, there is a need for further research to improve their comprehensiveness and effectiveness in evaluating GANs.
