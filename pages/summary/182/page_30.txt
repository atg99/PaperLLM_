The paper discusses the evaluation of generative models, particularly focusing on the precision and recall metrics used to assess their performance in generating samples from low-dimensional manifolds, such as those formed by convex shapes. It explains that precision measures how closely generated samples align with the manifold, while recall assesses the generator's ability to reproduce samples from the manifold. High precision indicates that generated samples are generally close to the manifold, whereas high recall signifies that the generator can closely reproduce a majority of the manifold's samples.

The authors acknowledge the limitations of these metrics, particularly when applied to real images where the data manifold is unknown, confining their utility to synthetic datasets. To address this, they reference recent work by Sajjadi et al., which proposes an alternative definition of precision and recall suitable for real data.

In addition to quantitative measures, the paper highlights qualitative assessments through human evaluation of generated images. While this method is intuitive and useful for model tuning, it has significant drawbacks. Human evaluations can be costly, biased, and difficult to reproduce, potentially leading to high variance in results. Furthermore, such evaluations may inadvertently favor models that overfit the training data, ultimately failing to indicate a model's true density estimation capability, particularly regarding mode dropping, which can enhance visual sample quality.
