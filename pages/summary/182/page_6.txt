The paper discusses the relationship between likelihood and sample quality in generative models, highlighting that they are generally uncorrelated. The authors provide examples illustrating this point: a Gaussian mixture model may produce high-quality samples despite having poor log-likelihood, while a mixture model with a good component weighted low may yield high log-likelihood but poor samples. This discrepancy complicates the evaluation of generative adversarial networks (GANs) regarding their memorization of training data or their ability to capture data distribution modes.

To address evaluation challenges, Tolstikhin et al. proposed a coverage metric that quantifies the probability mass of real data covered by the model distribution, using kernel density estimation for approximation. This metric is claimed to be more interpretable than likelihood.

Additionally, the Inception Score (IS), introduced by Salimans et al., is widely used for GAN evaluation. It leverages a pre-trained Inception network to assess the quality and diversity of generated samples by measuring the KL divergence between conditional and marginal label distributions. While IS correlates well with sample quality and diversity, it also has several limitations that need to be considered.
