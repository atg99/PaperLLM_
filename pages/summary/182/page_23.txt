This section discusses two methods for evaluating the quality of generated data: Adversarial Divergence and Geometry Score. 

Adversarial Divergence calculates the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the generated distribution Pg(y|x) and the real distribution Pr(y|x). A lower divergence indicates that the two distributions are similar, with a minimum value of zero signifying perfect equality. A challenge with this approach is the need for extensive human labeling of samples. To address this, Yang et al. proposed training a distinct generator for each category using a labeled dataset, then generating samples across all categories. This method shares overlap with classification performance metrics.

The Geometry Score, introduced by Khrulkov and Oseledets, evaluates the geometric properties of the underlying data manifold of real versus generated data. This involves constructing a simplicial complex based on proximity information, such as pairwise distances between samples. By varying a threshold, simplices are added and topological properties, specifically homologies, are computed, which reflect the number of dimensional holes in the space. A barcode is created to represent the persistence of these holes, with the Relative Living Times (RLT) calculated for each number of holes. The average of these RLTs across random subsets yields the Mean Relative Living Times (MRLT), which can be used to quantify topological differences between datasets through L2 distance.

Illustrative examples demonstrate the method's effectiveness, showing how MRLTs can reveal the closeness of generated data to ground truth patterns. Additionally, comparisons between two Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), WGAN and WGAN-GP, are presented using the MNIST dataset, highlighting the distributions produced by both models.
