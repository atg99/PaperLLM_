The paper presents a comparative analysis of retrieval performance for various methods on two datasets: In-Shop Clothes Retrieval and PKU VehicleID. The results demonstrate that the FastAP method, utilizing ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 architectures, significantly outperforms existing models across multiple metrics, particularly in R@1 retrieval rates.

In the In-Shop Clothes Retrieval dataset, FastAP achieves a R@1 score of 90.9 with ResNet-50 and shows a notable 15% relative improvement over DREML, which employs a complex ensemble of models. The table highlights that even with a single ResNet-18 model and 512-dimensional embeddings, FastAP consistently surpasses strong ensemble methods like A-BIER and ABE-8.

For the PKU VehicleID dataset, FastAP maintains its superiority, achieving R@1 scores of 91.9 (large set) and consistently outperforming ensemble methods such as BIER and DREML. Interestingly, it is observed that ResNet-18 outperforms ResNet-50 in R@1, likely due to its ability to handle larger batch sizes, which enhances retrieval effectiveness despite its simpler architecture.

The paper also discusses the impact of batch size on training, indicating that the size influences the performance of in-batch retrieval problems during stochastic gradient descent (SGD) training. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of FastAP in both datasets, emphasizing its advantages over more complex ensemble strategies.
