In this section, the authors present a method for optimizing the FastAP algorithm using stochastic gradient descent (SGD) within the context of a retrieval problem. The FastAP algorithm allows for efficient approximations of average precision (AP) by utilizing histogram binning and cumulative sums of bin counts. The authors define key variables such as Hj for the cumulative sum of the histogram and h+ j for the number of neighbors in the j-th bin. The computational complexity for histogram binning and FastAP approximation is established at O(NL), where L is the number of bins.

To implement SGD, the authors emphasize the need for differentiable histogram construction. They propose using a linear interpolation technique to convert integer-valued histogram counts into continuous values, thus enabling gradient backpropagation. This approach allows for the computation of partial derivatives necessary for updating model parameters.

The authors also discuss the challenges of large-batch training in the context of FastAP, highlighting that the objective function is non-decomposable, meaning the performance of each example in a minibatch depends on the others. They suggest a heuristic that involves breaking large batches into smaller chunks to compute gradients efficiently, facilitating training even on single GPUs.

Additionally, the authors address minibatch sampling strategies, noting that traditional methods based on class labels may not suffice due to the listwise formulation of FastAP. They argue that sampling should be conducted at the class level to ensure that each example has neighboring instances in the minibatch, thus avoiding poorly defined retrieval problems. The authors advocate for a strategy that samples classes in a way that generates sufficiently challenging retrieval problems, enhancing the training process.
