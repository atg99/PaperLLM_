The paper discusses a novel hard sampling strategy for minibatch sampling in deep metric learning, particularly in the context of image retrieval tasks. This strategy focuses on sampling classes from a limited number of categories, utilizing available side information about class similarities, such as WordNet similarity for ImageNet labels. By grouping similar classes within categories, the hard sampling method creates more challenging in-batch retrieval problems, leading to improved performance over traditional random class sampling. 

The effectiveness of the proposed method, named FastAP, is evaluated across three standard image retrieval datasets: Stanford Online Products, In-Shop Clothes Retrieval, and PKU VehicleID. Each dataset presents unique challenges, including limited images per class and differing class definitions, which complicate few-shot retrieval tasks. The experimental setup involves fine-tuning ResNet models, replacing the softmax layer with an embedding layer, and employing a binary relevance approach to measure retrieval effectiveness through Recall at k (R@k).

The experiments utilize pretrained ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 models, with a focus on optimizing the embedding layer's learning rate. Data augmentation techniques such as random cropping and flipping are employed during training, while images are resized to a standard dimension for consistent input. The results confirm that the hard sampling strategy consistently outperforms random sampling, underscoring its significance in enhancing retrieval performance in deep metric learning tasks.
