The cited works span various advancements in deep learning, particularly in the areas of face recognition, metric learning, and optimization techniques. Schroff et al. introduced FaceNet, a unified approach for face recognition and clustering through embedding techniques. Schultz and Joachims focused on learning distance metrics based on relative comparisons, contributing to the understanding of metric learning. Smith et al. proposed increasing batch size instead of decaying learning rates for improved training efficiency.

In the context of deep metric learning, Song et al. presented methods such as facility location and lifted structured feature embedding, enhancing performance in clustering tasks. Other notable contributions include direct loss minimization techniques by Song et al., and Taylor et al.'s work on optimizing non-smooth rank metrics through SoftRank.

The exploration of few-shot learning through information retrieval by Triantafillou et al. and the development of deep embeddings with histogram loss by Ustinova and Lempitsky highlight the ongoing efforts to refine learning paradigms. Wu et al. emphasized the significance of sampling in deep embedding learning, while Xing et al. tackled distance metric learning with clustering applications.

The advancements in ensemble methods for metric learning by Xuan et al. and the hard-aware cascaded embedding by Yuan et al. further illustrate the diverse approaches to improving model performance. Finally, Yue et al. and Zhao et al. focused on optimizing average precision in retrieval tasks and adversarial methods for triplet generation, respectively, showcasing the breadth of research in this domain.
