The paper introduces FastAP, a method for approximating Average Precision (AP) in information retrieval, addressing the limitations of traditional rank-based metrics due to the discrete sorting operation. This sorting presents challenges for gradient-based optimization, as it is non-differentiable at certain points. FastAP offers an alternative interpretation of AP by reformulating precision and recall as functions of distance rather than ranked items, thus enabling differentiable relaxations.

The relationship between AP and the area under the precision-recall curve (AUPR) is highlighted, suggesting that as the size of the neighbor set increases, AP approaches AUPR. By defining a continuous precision-recall curve and using probabilistic quantities, the paper derives expressions for precision and recall in terms of distance distributions. This leads to a new formulation for AP that can be approximated using finite sums.

To implement FastAP, the authors propose quantizing the distance interval into discrete bins, allowing the approximation to be computed as a summation over these bins. This approach not only circumvents the issues associated with sorting but also provides an efficient way to evaluate AP using histogram representations of distance distributions. Overall, FastAP represents a significant advancement in the ability to optimize retrieval systems with respect to rank-based metrics.
