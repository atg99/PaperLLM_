The paper presents a comparison of various deep metric learning methods on the Stanford Online Products dataset, focusing particularly on the FastAP method. Table 1 summarizes the retrieval performance metrics (R@k for k=1, 10, 100, 1000) for several techniques, highlighting FastAP's state-of-the-art results with both ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 architectures. FastAP achieves competitive performance even with fewer embedding dimensions compared to leading methods, notably outperforming Margin, a leading triplet-based approach, with fewer dimensions.

FastAP's advantage lies in its ability to train a single embedding network efficiently, contrasting with ensemble methods like BIER and ABE-8, which require complex training setups and larger GPU memory. The paper also critiques the Histogram method, which, despite introducing a quadruplet-based loss, is less effective in correlating with retrieval performance compared to FastAP.

Furthermore, the paper mentions the inclusion of the In-Shop Clothes Retrieval dataset in their comparisons, introducing additional methods such as FashionNet and DREML. Overall, the results emphasize FastAP's efficiency and effectiveness in deep metric learning, showcasing its superiority over more complex and memory-intensive methods.
