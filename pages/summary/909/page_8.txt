The paper presents an ablation study examining the performance of the FastAP method for deep metric learning, focusing on the impact of training batch size and distance quantization on retrieval accuracy (R@1) across various datasets. 

In the first part of the study, it is shown that increasing the batch size leads to improved R@1 scores on all datasets. This is attributed to the reduction of stochastic gradient variance, which enhances generalization by creating more challenging in-batch retrieval scenarios. 

The second part investigates the effect of the number of histogram bins used in distance quantization, a hyper-parameter of FastAP. The results reveal that while more bins can lead to a closer approximation of Average Precision (AP), optimal performance is achieved with around 10 bins. A finer quantization can result in zero gradients, halting learning, while coarser quantization maintains non-zero gradients that aid in separating positive and negative samples. Thus, a balance is needed to avoid poor approximation or excessive simplification.

The paper concludes by emphasizing FastAP's efficiency in optimizing AP in a stochastic framework, supported by a new category-based minibatch sampling strategy and large-batch training heuristic. FastAP outperforms existing methods in few-shot image retrieval across standard datasets, highlighting its effective performance-complexity balance.
