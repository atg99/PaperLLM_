The paper evaluates the performance of different fuzzy logic systems, specifically focusing on the Zadeh-based General Type-2 Fuzzy Logic System (Z-GT2-FLS), the Modified Jang's GT2-FLS (MJ-GT2-FLS), the Interval Type-2 FLS (IT2-FLS-H), and its more complex variant (IT2-FLS-HS) across three datasets: White Wine, Parkinson’s, and AIDS. Performance is assessed using metrics such as Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Prediction Interval Coverage Probability (PICP), and Prediction Interval Normalized Averaged Width (PINAW).

Key findings indicate that the Z-GT2-FLS consistently outperforms the others in terms of RMSE across all datasets, demonstrating statistically significant differences in performance. For the White Wine dataset, Z-GT2-FLS achieves the lowest RMSE and exhibits lower variability. In the Parkinson’s dataset, it also achieves the highest PICP and the lowest mean PINAW, suggesting it generates high-quality prediction intervals (HQ-PIs). For the AIDS dataset, while all systems show similar performance, Z-GT2-FLS maintains superior RMSE and tighter PIs compared to IT2-FLS-HS, which has better coverage but wider intervals.

The paper concludes that the Z-GT2-FLS is a robust performer, providing high predictive accuracy and reliable HQ-PIs even in high-dimensional contexts, despite having fewer linear parameters compared to IT2-FLSs and MJ-GT2-FLS. Future work may explore further enhancements in design flexibility and performance optimization of fuzzy systems.
