The paper discusses the functionality and advantages of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) in the context of time series analysis and predictive modeling. It highlights how GRUs simplify the architecture of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks by merging the input and forget gates into a single update gate, which effectively manages the retention of new and old information. This design reduces the number of parameters, leading to shorter training times while maintaining performance comparable to LSTMs.

The study emphasizes the integration of Autoencoders (AEs) for feature extraction and the use of GRUs alongside Deep Fully Connected Neural Networks (DNNs) for predicting Remaining Useful Life (RUL). The authors advocate for GRUs due to their efficiency and effectiveness in handling time series data.

In the section on RNN applications in time series forecasting, various case studies are presented. For instance, Chen et al. focus on predicting mechanical states using sensor data, while Zheng et al. apply LSTM models to forecast power system load, addressing the non-linear and non-stationary characteristics of the data. ElSaid et al. predict aircraft engine vibrations with a selection of key features normalized for accuracy. Cenggoro and Siahaan develop a DLSTM model for traffic flow prediction, and Bao et al. conduct stock price predictions through a multi-stage process involving noise reduction, feature extraction, and LSTM application.

Overall, the paper underlines the growing preference for GRUs in deep learning applications for time series analysis, leveraging their advantages in computational efficiency and model simplicity.
