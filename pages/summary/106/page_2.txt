The paper discusses the evolution of equipment maintenance methods, categorizing them into repair maintenance, preventive maintenance, and predictive maintenance (PdM). While repair maintenance occurs post-failure, preventive maintenance is proactive, involving scheduled inspections and replacements. PdM distinguishes itself by utilizing historical health data to anticipate equipment failures, thereby reducing costs and preventing unexpected breakdowns.

A crucial concept in PdM is Remaining Useful Life (RUL), which measures the time until equipment begins to malfunction. RUL prediction can be approached via model-based or data-driven methods. Model-based methods rely on historical trends, while data-driven methods leverage sensor data to establish correlations between inputs and outputs. Data-driven approaches generally offer greater stability and accuracy when ample data is available.

The paper frames RUL prediction as a time series forecasting challenge, discussing various forecasting methodologies, including statistical, machine learning, and deep learning models. Statistical models like ARIMA and machine learning techniques such as Support Vector Machines and Random Forests are highlighted, though they often require substantial domain knowledge for effective application. In contrast, deep learning excels in feature extraction and can handle multi-sensor data without extensive pre-processing.

The authors note that deep learning, through its use of artificial neural networks, surpasses traditional methods and even many machine learning techniques in analyzing time series data. They reference studies showing that neural network-based algorithms are more adept at learning from time series datasets, emphasizing the importance of effective feature extraction for enhancing prediction accuracy. Principal Component Analysis remains a common method for dimension reduction, although its limitations in capturing essential information in complex datasets are acknowledged.
