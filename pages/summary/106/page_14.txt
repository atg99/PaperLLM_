The paper presents a comparative analysis of various deep learning models, including DNN, RNN, LSTM, GRU, and AE-GRU, for predicting Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of engines. The study highlights the challenges faced by these models, particularly the RNN, which suffers from gradient vanishing issues when time steps are set to 10. In contrast, DNN lacks time series characteristics, resulting in suboptimal prediction performance.

The results from 5-fold cross-validation show that different models yield varying Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) values depending on the number of time steps. For time steps of 5, the average RMSE results are as follows: DNN (18.86), RNN (18.32), LSTM (18.16), GRU (17.96), and AE-GRU (17.94). When time steps are increased to 10, the average RMSE values improve significantly for LSTM, GRU, and AE-GRU, with AE-GRU achieving the best performance (10.79).

The analysis of the RUL prediction indicates that while DNN and RNN are not well-suited for either training or testing, LSTM, GRU, and AE-GRU perform well during training but struggle with testing due to insufficient data. The AE-GRU model shows promise in predicting RUL accurately, particularly when the RUL remains stable, and it can forecast declines in RUL effectively, unlike the fluctuations observed with DNN and RNN.
