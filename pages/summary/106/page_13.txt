The paper presents an analysis of different network architectures and their performance in predicting Remaining Useful Life (RUL) using an Autoencoder-Gated Recurrent Unit (AE-GRU) model. Two sets of experiments were conducted, varying the batch size and evaluating the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) results.

The results indicated that increasing the batch size improves training speed and RMSE results, leading to a decision to set the batch size to 128. The network architecture selected for further evaluation was G(64,64)N(8,8). 

Model evaluation involved a 5-fold cross-validation on a dataset of 218 engines, where the Autoencoder (AE) reduced the dimensionality of the input data from 24 to 15 dimensions. The study demonstrated that reducing the input to 15 dimensions yielded the best performance, as lower dimensions (10 and 5) resulted in significant information loss and poorer restoration effectiveness.

After conducting the 5-fold cross-validation and analyzing RMSE results, it was concluded that the AE-GRU model could effectively extract features and accurately predict RUL, particularly when utilizing historical data points with a time step of 5. This comprehensive evaluation confirmed the advantages of the AE-GRU architecture in the context of RUL prediction.
