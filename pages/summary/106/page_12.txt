The section discusses the optimization process for a GRU model aimed at minimizing the loss function and improving prediction accuracy. The experiment uses specific parameters including 100 epochs, ReLU activation, a batch size of 128, one hidden layer with 50 neurons, and 24 input features. A 5-fold cross-validation method is utilized to evaluate seven optimization algorithms: SGD, RMSprop, Adagrad, Adadelta, Adam, Adamax, and Nadam, with Nadam achieving the best convergence speed on the PHM08 dataset. 

The study highlights Adam's advantages in rapid convergence and its effectiveness in handling noisy and sparse gradient issues. Nadam, a variant of Adam, further enhances convergence rates by modifying the momentum component. 

Additionally, the experiment examines the impact of different batch sizes (32, 64, 128) on model performance, employing an early stopping function to prevent overfitting. The Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) outcomes for varying configurations of neurons and training times demonstrate the effectiveness of the model under different settings. Results indicate that configurations with a batch size of 32 yield the most consistent RMSE values, emphasizing the importance of optimizer choice and network architecture in model training.
