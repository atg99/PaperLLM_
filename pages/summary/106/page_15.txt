This study introduces the AE-GRU model aimed at predicting the Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of equipment. The approach begins with the pre-processing of sensor data collected from various sources, where standardization is applied to align the scales of the data while preserving the time series characteristics. RUL is defined to enable precise predictions of the operational lifespan of the equipment.

Feature extraction is performed through deep learning techniques to identify critical characteristics of the sensor data, which are vital for enhancing the model's predictive accuracy. The methodology incorporates data dimension conversion, considering the historical nature of the sensor data. A maximum life value transformation is also executed, setting engines with RUL exceeding 130 cycles to this threshold, thereby prioritizing predictions when the RUL is low.

The model utilizes a combination of the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) and back-propagation to optimize parameter learning, leveraging GPU acceleration to manage the extensive number of parameters efficiently. The use of the ReLU activation function and the Nadam optimizer further expedites the convergence of the learning process.

The main contribution of this research lies in utilizing the GRU, an optimized version of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), which effectively captures sensor data characteristics while maintaining a lower parameter count compared to LSTM. Overall, the AE-GRU model demonstrates improved performance in RUL prediction, highlighting its relevance in predictive maintenance applications.
