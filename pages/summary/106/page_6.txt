This paper discusses the development of an Autoencoder Gated Recurrent Unit (AE-GRU) model aimed at predicting Remaining Useful Life (RUL) to enhance equipment longevity, minimize the risks associated with unexpected machinery shutdowns, and improve operational reliability. The AE-GRU model consists of three main stages: data pre-processing, feature extraction, and RUL prediction.

In the data pre-processing phase, the engine life is defined to accurately predict RUL, followed by standardization of the data to mitigate errors stemming from differing data scales. An autoencoder is then employed to extract relevant features from the original dataset. The model utilizes Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), which are adept at handling time series data and account for historical changes.

The data transformation process converts 2D data into a 3D format, where the new dimension represents time. Additionally, adjustments are made to the RUL value range to optimize model training and prediction outcomes, as the original data trends linearly downward. Ultimately, RUL expectancy is forecasted using the GRU model in conjunction with a fully connected neural network.

The paper also references several studies that utilize various models, such as LSTM and GRU, for different predictive tasks, including Sea Surface Temperature forecasts and traffic volume predictions, emphasizing the relevance of historical data and feature extraction techniques in enhancing predictive accuracy.
