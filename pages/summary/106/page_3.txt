To address this, variants like Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) networks have been developed, which incorporate mechanisms to better manage the flow of information through the network.

The paper discusses the application of Autoencoders (AEs) and GRUs in predicting Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of equipment. Autoencoders serve as a non-linear dimension reduction technique that compresses high-dimensional data into a lower-dimensional representation while retaining essential features. This capability is particularly beneficial for time series data, as it allows for effective feature extraction without manual selection, reducing the time and effort involved in feature engineering.

The authors highlight the advantages of deep learning approaches over traditional machine learning methods, particularly in RUL prediction tasks. They cite various studies demonstrating the effectiveness of LSTMs and GRUs in predicting RUL based on sensor data from equipment, such as lithium batteries and jet engines. The paper proposes a novel Autoencoder Gated Recurrent Unit (AE-GRU) model that combines the feature extraction capabilities of AEs with the temporal analysis strengths of GRUs, enhanced further by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for final prediction.

In summary, the AE-GRU model aims to improve RUL prediction accuracy by leveraging both feature extraction and temporal correlation analysis, thereby offering a comprehensive approach to analyzing time series data in predictive maintenance contexts. The literature review emphasizes the importance of recurrent neural networks in time series analysis, particularly their ability to utilize historical data for forecasting future events.
