The paper discusses the transformation and prediction of Remaining Useful Life (RUL) for engines. It begins by defining the maximum and minimum life cycles of the engine as 356 and 127, respectively, with an average life of 209 cycles. A transformation process is introduced where RUL values greater than a threshold T (set at 130) are capped at T, while values below remain unchanged. This transformation changes the RUL from a linear to a nonlinear decline, enhancing model training and estimation accuracy.

The study then outlines a four-step RUL prediction model that integrates effective feature extraction and rapid computation. The first step involves collecting sensor data at the current and previous time points, allowing for adjustable historical data input. The second step utilizes a two-layer Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) to identify temporal correlations in the data. Since GRU alone cannot predict RUL, a Deep Neural Network (DNN) is introduced in the third step to process the features extracted by GRU and generate RUL predictions. The DNN employs Mean Square Error (MSE) as its objective function, with gradient descent used for optimization.

Finally, the model's output is the predicted RUL, and its performance is evaluated using Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of the proposed transformation and model structure in improving RUL predictions.
