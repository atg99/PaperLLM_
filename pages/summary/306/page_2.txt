The introduction emphasizes the critical role of data in the 21st century, likening it to oil due to its immense value and potential insights for organizations. With an astonishing influx of 2.5 quintillion bytes of data generated daily, exemplified by Walmart's collection of 2.5 petabytes of customer transaction data every hour, the trend of data proliferation is expected to continue. A notable portion of this data consists of text, which includes structured, semi-structured, and unstructured formats. Online consumer reviews are highlighted as a significant source of this text data, utilized in various research areas such as predicting consumer ratings, forecasting stock market performance, and analyzing market structures.

However, the paper also addresses the challenges associated with interpreting text data, particularly the necessity of understanding context to derive meaningful insights. For instance, the phrase "raging bull" can have different implications depending on the context of its use. Additionally, the inherent fuzziness and incoherence of some reviews complicate the process of meaning extraction. The authors call attention to the nascent state of models capable of handling large-scale unstructured data, suggesting a need for further research in this domain.
