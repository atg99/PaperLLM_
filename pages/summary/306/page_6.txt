The paper discusses two primary approaches to sentiment analysis: unsupervised lexicon-based models and supervised machine learning models, specifically logistic regression.

The unsupervised lexicon-based model, exemplified by Sent WordNet, utilizes curated lexicons that contain word-specific sentiment information, including polarity and parts of speech. This model involves associating words in a corpus with their corresponding lexicon data and contextual information to compute sentiment scores for documents. Popular lexicons mentioned include TextBlob, AFINN, VADER, and Sent WordNet.

In contrast, the supervised machine learning approach employs logistic regression for sentiment classification, categorizing sentiments as positive or negative based on review content. This method utilizes a bag of words model, treating each document as a collection of unstructured words, leading to the creation of a term-document matrix. The logistic regression model then calculates the odds of a document being positive and classifies it based on a predetermined cutoff value. This method is grounded in utility maximization theory, incorporating random factors to enhance predictive accuracy.
