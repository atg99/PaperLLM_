In Section 5.1, the paper details the training of the view-GCN architecture, which includes a backbone view feature extractor and a view-GCN, for 3D shape recognition through end-to-end training. The gradients of the loss with respect to the parameters are computed using auto-differentiation in PyTorch. 

The training procedure involves fine-tuning the view feature extraction network using an SGD optimizer with specified parameters, including a momentum of 0.9, a weight decay of 10^-2, and a batch size of 400 for 30 epochs, with a learning rate that is halved every 10 epochs. For the entire architecture, the learning rate is set to 10^-3 for 15 epochs, with varying batch sizes for different versions of view-GCN. A learning rate warm-up strategy is employed, increasing from 0 to 10^-3 in the first epoch, followed by a reduction using a cosine quarter-cycle.

The experiments conducted evaluate the view-GCN for 3D shape classification and retrieval across several datasets: ModelNet40, ShapeNet Core55, and RGBD. ModelNet40 consists of 12,311 3D shapes across 40 categories, while ShapeNet Core55 includes 51,162 models across 55 classes. The RGBD dataset consists of RGB and RGBD images of 300 household objects.

In the classification experiments on ModelNet40, the view-GCN achieves the highest accuracy scores compared to various 3D object classification methods, particularly outperforming traditional view-pooling methods by significant margins. The results indicate that view-GCN surpasses previous methods, including RotationNet, by 3.4% in per class accuracy and 2.6% in per instance accuracy. Additionally, when compared to methods exploiting sequential view relations, view-GCN demonstrates improved performance, emphasizing its effectiveness in capturing multi-view feature relationships.
