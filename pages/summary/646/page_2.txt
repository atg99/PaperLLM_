The paper presents a novel approach for 3D shape recognition using a flexible representation known as a view-graph, which models multiple views of a 3D shape as nodes in a graph. Each node is associated with view features extracted from a 2D image classification network, and edges between nodes are determined by the k-nearest neighbors of camera coordinates. This method allows for various view configurations, including irregular positions, and leverages a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) to aggregate multi-view features effectively.

The proposed view-GCN architecture operates hierarchically across multiple levels of coarsened view-graphs, employing local graph convolution and non-local message passing to capture relationships among both neighboring and distant views. A selective view-sampling strategy is introduced for graph coarsening, ensuring that the most representative views are included. The combination of features learned at different levels yields a comprehensive global shape descriptor.

The effectiveness of the view-GCN is demonstrated through evaluations on 3D datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance in shape classification and retrieval tasks, including high accuracy on ModelNet40 and ShapeNet Core55 datasets. This approach offers a significant advancement over traditional methods, which often rely on sequential view representations or assume homogeneous view configurations.

In the context of related works, the paper discusses existing methods in multi-view 3D shape recognition that utilize various feature aggregation strategies, including pooling and attention mechanisms. It contrasts these with the proposed view-graph representation, which enables more complex relationships between views to be captured through GCNs. The paper concludes with an overview of the motivation and potential of the view-GCN approach for enhancing 3D shape descriptor learning.
