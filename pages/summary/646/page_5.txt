The paper introduces a novel hierarchical network architecture called view-GCN, designed to process multi-view features through a series of local graph convolutions, non-local message passing, and selective view-sampling. Each level of the view-GCN coarsens the graph representation, reducing the number of views while retaining essential shape features. The architecture leverages learnable view-selectors, which are implemented as two-layer MLPs, to identify the most discriminative views from local neighborhoods.

The view-GCN is demonstrated through two configurations: a 12-view circular arrangement and a 20-view dodecahedral setup. Each configuration generates a view-graph with a defined number of nodes, and the network is structured hierarchically, allowing for effective multi-level feature extraction.

The training process incorporates both shape loss and view loss to optimize the network. The shape loss is derived from a classifier applied to the global shape features, while the view loss enforces that each view-selector accurately discriminates shape categories based on local view features. The network comprises a significant number of parameters, totaling 73.4 million for the 20-view version, which includes both the backbone feature extractor and the view-GCN components.

Training is conducted in two phases, beginning with fine-tuning a pre-trained feature extractor on multi-view images before integrating the view-GCN architecture. This approach aims to enhance classification performance by effectively leveraging the spatial relationships among views.
