The paper presents a novel graph convolutional network (GCN) for 3D shape recognition, referred to as view-GCN, which models multiple views of a shape using a hierarchical view-graph structure. The effectiveness of this approach is demonstrated through extensive experiments on the ShapeNet Core55 dataset for shape classification.

In the experimental results summarized in Table 5, view-GCN outperforms the baseline method MVCNN-new, achieving 79.8% per class accuracy and 90.9% per instance accuracy. The analysis highlights the contributions of various components of the view-GCN architecture. For instance, removing local graph convolution and non-local message passing results in lower accuracies, indicating their positive impact on performance. Additionally, the study compares selective view-sampling against simple farthest point sampling (FPS), showing that the former significantly improves accuracy.

The paper also evaluates the effect of the view loss function, which enhances the discriminative ability of view-selectors, and finds that even without it, view-GCN performs better than using FPS. Learning the affinity matrix for view-graphs is shown to be beneficial, as indicated by superior results from different configurations of the matrix.

Furthermore, the hierarchical structure of view-GCN is validated, with improvements noted when increasing the levels of view-graphs. However, increasing the sampling rate leads to slight drops in accuracy, suggesting a balance is necessary. The selection of pooling layers for constructing the global feature is also discussed, with results indicating that non-local max-pooling is less effective than local max-pooling.

Lastly, the paper demonstrates the flexibility of view-GCN in handling irregular view configurations, achieving competitive accuracies even with randomly perturbed views. The authors conclude by affirming the effectiveness of their proposed method and express intentions to explore multi-modality feature fusion in future work.
