This excerpt presents findings on the mean-squared error (MSE) between original and imputed values using four random forest proximity measures across various datasets with different levels of missing data, specifically missing completely at random (MCAR). The MSE scores, which are scaled from 0 to 1 for consistency, are calculated over 1 to 10 imputation iterations and repeated five times for accuracy. The four proximity measures evaluated include original proximities, out-of-bag (OOB) proximities, RF-GAP proximities, and RFProxIH, with the latter being excluded from comparison due to inefficiency.

The results indicate that RF-GAP generally provides superior performance in terms of lower MSE compared to other methods. The analysis includes five different MCAR percentages (5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, and 75%) across multiple datasets sourced from the UCI repository, with a detailed dataset description provided in Appendix B. The initial iteration (Iteration 0) serves as a benchmark for median-filled imputation. Overall, the findings suggest that the choice of proximity measure significantly impacts the quality of data imputation in the presence of missing values.
