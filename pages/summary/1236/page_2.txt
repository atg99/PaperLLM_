The paper introduces a new measure called Random Forest-Geometry- and Accuracy-Preserving proximities (RF-GAP), which aims to enhance the interpretability of random forests by ensuring that proximity-weighted predictions align with the random forest's out-of-bag predictions. This is significant because previous proximity definitions did not accurately reflect the data geometry learned by the random forest, which limited their application in tasks such as data visualization and imputation.

RF-GAP is based on the idea that proximities for out-of-bag observations can be derived from in-bag observations, thus preserving the actual data geometry. The authors demonstrate that RF-GAP maintains consistency with the predictions of random forests in both regression and classification contexts. Empirical results show that RF-GAP outperforms existing proximity definitions in various applications, including data imputation, visualization, and outlier detection.

The paper also reviews the applications of random forest proximities in clustering, outlier detection, data imputation, and variable importance assessment. Previous studies have utilized random forest proximities for tasks such as visualizing data via dimensionality reduction and detecting anomalies in various datasets. The authors emphasize that their new approach, RF-GAP, provides a more accurate representation of the random forest's learning, thereby enhancing its utility in these applications.
