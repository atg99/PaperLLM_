The document presents additional imputation results across various datasets and missing data percentages. It compares the performance of three imputation methods: Original, RF-GAP, and RFProxIH, under different scenarios of missing completely at random (MCAR) data rates (5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, and 75%).

Key findings indicate that RF-GAP generally outperforms the other methods, particularly in scenarios with lower missing data rates. However, it is noted that the imputation becomes noisier for smaller datasets and less reliable when the percentage of missing values increases, especially at the 75% missing data mark. The datasets examined include Auto-Mpg, Arrhythmia, Balance Scale, Banknote, Breast Cancer, and Diabetes, with variations in performance observed across these datasets. The results emphasize the limitations of imputation techniques when faced with high levels of missing data.
