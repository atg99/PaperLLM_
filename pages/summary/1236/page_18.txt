The paper presents findings on the effectiveness of proximity-weighted predictions using various random forest methods, specifically RF-GAP, Original, OOB, PBK, and RFProxIH. The results, illustrated in Figures A.5 and A.6, demonstrate that RF-GAP generally aligns with the predictions of the standard random forest model, with exact matches observed in regression scenarios. It is noted that the accuracy of matches improves with an increasing number of trees.

In terms of prediction agreement, RF-GAP shows a lower proportion of matches compared to the Original, PBK, and RFProxIH methods, especially in training datasets. Conversely, for test datasets, the Original, OOB, and PBK methods exhibit higher agreement rates.

Additionally, the paper discusses data imputation results based on random forest proximities, summarizing average outcomes from 100 trials across 17 datasets sourced from the UCI repository. It highlights that larger datasets were excluded due to computational inefficiencies associated with PBK and RFProxIH proximities, as well as datasets that already contained missing values.
