This section of the paper investigates the impact of sample size on the accuracy of proximity-weighted predictions made by a random forest algorithm. The analysis involves a synthetic dataset with binary class labels and varying sample sizes ranging from 100 to 10,000. The proximity-weighted predictions from the Original, OOB, and RF-GAP methods are compared, with the findings indicating that while the proximity-weighted predictions align with the random forest's predictions as sample size increases, the discrepancies do not diminish to zero. Notably, RF-GAP consistently matches the random forest predictions regardless of sample size.

Furthermore, the paper presents the error rates for both OOB and test datasets, illustrating that these rates decrease with larger sample sizes. This trend could explain the observed increase in the proportion of matching proximity-weighted predictions in the earlier figure. The computational complexity of certain methods limited the comparison to the three mentioned proximity types. Overall, the analysis emphasizes the significance of sample size in enhancing the reliability of proximity-weighted predictions in random forests.
