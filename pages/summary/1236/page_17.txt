The paper presents an analysis of proximity-weighted prediction errors across various minimum node sizes in a random forest context. Figures A.4 illustrate the distribution of errors, showing that as the minimum node size increases, the proximity-weighted training errors also tend to increase. 

In the section on multidimensional scaling (MDS) and outlier detection, the authors apply MDS to different random forest proximities using the Ionosphere dataset. The RF-GAP embeddings reveal that misclassified points often lie on the border between class clusters, a pattern not consistently observed in other proximity measures. Subsequent figures (A.8, A.9, A.10) reinforce these observations, highlighting exaggerated separation in original and RFProxIH proximities, while RF-GAP effectively illustrates the reasons behind misclassifications.

Further figures (A.10, A.11, A.12) provide examples of proximity-based outlier scores, indicating that points distant from their class clusters may be misclassified as outliers. However, the clarity of this outlier identification can diminish when multiple points are close to one another despite being far from their clusters.
