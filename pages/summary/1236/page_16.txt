The paper discusses the performance of random forest models and the comparison of various proximity measures in predicting class labels. It highlights an experiment involving 16 variables, each with a variance of 1, repeated 20 times to assess prediction errors, as shown in Figures A.1 and A.2. The findings reveal that the original proximities align more closely with random forest test predictions compared to out-of-bag (OOB) proximities, although the latter converges to a consistent proportion of unmatched predictions as sample size increases. 

Section A.3 demonstrates that RF-GAP proximities, while initially asymmetric, become more symmetric with an increasing number of trees in the forest. This was analyzed using a synthetic dataset of 500 observations, replicated 10 times, with results depicted in Figure A.3.

In Section A.4, the paper indicates that both random forest and proximity-weighted training errors increase with the minimum node size, as illustrated in Figure A.4. Section A.5 discusses the relationship between the number of trees and prediction accuracy, confirming that larger forests generally yield better predictions, though computational complexity is a consideration. The RF-GAP predictions maintain alignment with random forest predictions even with fewer trees, though classification matches decrease due to the necessity for tie-breaking.

Finally, Section A.6 compares agreement levels between different proximity measures. The proximity-weighted test predictions exhibit less variability, with the RF-GAP showing the greatest differences compared to original, OOB, and PBK proximities, particularly in predicting training examples where RF-GAP diverges from others.
