The paper presents a comparative analysis of multi-dimensional scaling (MDS) embeddings derived from different random forest (RF) proximity definitions using the two-class Sonar dataset. It highlights the effectiveness of RF-GAP proximities in accurately representing the classification problem, where correctly classified points form distinct clusters, while misclassified points are typically near the decision boundary. Other proximity definitions, such as the original, PBK, and RFProxIH, tend to exaggerate class separability, misrepresenting the random forest's performance.

Additionally, the paper discusses the imputation of missing values in the optical digits dataset under different rates of missing completely at random (MCAR). RF-GAP proximities yielded the most accurate imputations over multiple iterations.

The paper also explores the use of random forests for outlier detection, noting that observations with significantly different measurements can adversely affect classification algorithms. It proposes a method for identifying within-class outliers based on proximity measures, where smaller average proximity values signal potential outliers. The effectiveness of RF-GAP in improving outlier detection is demonstrated through visualizations using the Gene Expression Cancer dataset and the MNIST dataset, where outlier scores help identify anomalous points within classes.

Overall, the findings suggest that RF-GAP proximities enhance both the visualization of classification problems and the detection of outliers in various datasets.
