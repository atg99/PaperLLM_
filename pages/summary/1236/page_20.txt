The paper presents extended imputation results across various datasets, detailing the performance of different imputation methods at varying levels of missing data (5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, and 75%). Each dataset is characterized by the number of observations and variables. The average mean-squared errors (MSE) of the imputed values compared to the original values were calculated, along with standard deviations, indicating the effectiveness of the imputation methods.

The RF-GAP method consistently outperformed other methods across most datasets, demonstrating lower MSE values at all missing data rates. For instance, in the Arrhythmia dataset, RF-GAP achieved an MSE of 0.637 at 5% missing data, while the original method recorded 0.665. Similar trends were observed in other datasets, such as Auto-Mpg and Banknote, where RF-GAP maintained lower MSE values.

The analysis also includes performance comparisons for methods labeled as Original and OOB. In some cases, RFProxIH did not provide results, indicating potential limitations or gaps in the method's application. Overall, the findings highlight the superiority of RF-GAP in handling missing data imputation across diverse datasets, suggesting its utility for practitioners in data analysis and machine learning.
