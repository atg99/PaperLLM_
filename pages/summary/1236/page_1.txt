The paper "Geometry- and Accuracy-Preserving Random Forest Proximities" by Jake S. Rhodes, Adele Cutler, and Kevin R. Moon addresses the limitations of traditional pairwise proximity measures derived from random forests. While random forests are recognized for their strong predictive performance and versatility in handling various data types, the authors argue that existing definitions of random forest proximities do not adequately capture the underlying data geometry learned by the model.

To address this issue, the authors propose a new definition termed Random Forest-Geometry- and Accuracy-Preserving proximities (RF-GAP). They demonstrate that using RF-GAP for proximity-weighted predictions aligns perfectly with the out-of-bag predictions of the random forest, thereby preserving the learned data geometry. The empirical results show that RF-GAP significantly outperforms traditional proximity measures in applications such as data imputation, outlier detection, and data visualization, providing a more accurate representation of the relationships among data points.

The paper emphasizes the importance of incorporating a supervised similarity measure in random forest proximities, which can enhance the interpretability of the model and improve various applications that rely on these proximities. Overall, the proposed RF-GAP represents a meaningful advancement in the application of random forests, enabling more accurate and geometrically consistent proximity measures.
