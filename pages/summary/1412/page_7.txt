The paper presents an ablation study on the efficiency and effectiveness of a dynamic head for object detection by varying the number of blocks used. The results indicate that adding more blocks up to eight improves performance while maintaining reasonable computational costs. For instance, a configuration with two blocks already surpasses the baseline performance at a lower cost. The dynamic head demonstrates generalization capabilities across various popular object detectors, enhancing their average precision (AP) by 1.2 to 3.2 points.

The compatibility of the dynamic head with different backbones, such as ResNet-50 and ResNeXt-101, is also evaluated, showing consistent performance improvements over existing methods, including significant gains against state-of-the-art detectors. For example, when integrated with Faster R-CNN and other frameworks, the dynamic head achieves notable AP increases, outperforming the best previous methods.

The paper further compares the proposed method with state-of-the-art detectors, demonstrating that it achieves a new high of 52.3 AP with only a 2x training schedule, which is more efficient than other methods requiring longer training times. Additionally, the dynamic head outperforms recent Transformer-based approaches by over 2.0 AP while using less training time. Finally, with test time augmentation, the dynamic head achieves a state-of-the-art result of 54.0 AP, surpassing concurrent best methods by 1.3 AP, showcasing its overall effectiveness and efficiency in object detection tasks.
