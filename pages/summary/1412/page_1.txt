In this paper, the authors introduce a novel framework called the dynamic head, which aims to unify object detection heads through the use of attention mechanisms. The challenge of combining localization and classification in object detection has led to a proliferation of methods, yet previous approaches have failed to provide a cohesive solution. The proposed dynamic head framework addresses three critical challenges in object detection: scale-awareness, spatial-awareness, and task-awareness.

Scale-awareness is crucial because objects of varying sizes often appear in the same image. Spatial-awareness is necessary to account for the diverse shapes and orientations of objects, while task-awareness is required to manage the different representations of objects, such as bounding boxes and key points. The authors note that existing studies have primarily focused on solving these issues in isolation, leaving a gap for a unified approach.

The dynamic head utilizes multiple self-attention mechanisms applied separately across the dimensions of a feature tensor—level, spatial, and channel dimensions. The scale-aware attention module enhances features based on the relative importance of semantic levels, while the spatial-aware attention module focuses on discriminative representations across spatial locations. The task-aware attention module directs feature channels towards specific tasks, such as classification or regression.

By integrating these attention mechanisms, the dynamic head significantly improves the representation capability of object detection heads without incurring additional computational costs. Experimental results on the COCO benchmark demonstrate its effectiveness, achieving a state-of-the-art performance of 54.0 average precision when tested with a ResNeXt-101-DCN backbone. The authors plan to release the code for their dynamic head framework, further contributing to advancements in object detection methodologies.
