The paper discusses various decoding techniques for solving the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) using self-attention mechanisms in neural networks. It highlights different methods of initiating the decoding process, such as using the first and last cities or a random selection of recent cities. The authors introduce a novel token city to optimize the starting point for decoding, contrasting it with existing methods.

The decoding technique involves representing a tour as an ordered sequence of city indices, casting the TSP as a sequence optimization problem. The authors utilize a probabilistic framework where the goal is to maximize the likelihood of a sequence of city visits. Due to the intractable complexity of exact solutions, approximations like greedy search are employed, which selects the next city based on the highest probability at each step, resulting in linear complexity.

To enhance results beyond greedy search, the paper discusses more sophisticated sampling techniques like beam search and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MTCS), which explore multiple paths and improve decision-making. Beam search, in particular, maintains a fixed number of the best paths, allowing for more effective exploration of potential city sequences.

The authors present numerical experiments comparing their proposed architecture against existing methods on TSP datasets, detailing their experimental setup and performance metrics. Results indicate the efficiency and effectiveness of their approach, with code made available for further research and application.
