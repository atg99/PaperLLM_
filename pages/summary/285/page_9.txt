The provided references focus on various methodologies and advancements in solving the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP), a classic optimization challenge. Richard Bellman's dynamic programming approach laid foundational work in 1962, while Georges A. Croes introduced a method for TSP in 1958. Subsequent studies, such as those by Held and Karp, explored dynamic programming further, and Christofides analyzed heuristics for worst-case scenarios in 1976.

Recent developments have incorporated machine learning techniques. For instance, Bello et al. in 2016 examined neural combinatorial optimization using reinforcement learning, while Dai et al. in 2017 focused on learning combinatorial optimization algorithms specifically for graphs. The works of Deudon et al. in 2018 and Gasse et al. in 2019 highlighted the use of policy gradients and graph convolutional networks for learning heuristics applicable to TSP.

Optimization techniques have also evolved, with Fisher's Lagrangian relaxation method and Helsgaun's effective implementation of the Lin-Kernighan heuristic providing significant improvements in solving TSP. Additionally, tools such as Google’s OR-tools and Gurobi have emerged, facilitating practical applications of these advanced algorithms.

Overall, the literature illustrates a transition from classical methods to sophisticated machine learning approaches, enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of TSP solutions.
