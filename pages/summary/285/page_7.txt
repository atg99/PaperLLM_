The paper presents a comparison of various methods for solving the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP), specifically focusing on the performance of a neural network model based on the Transformer architecture. The results indicate that the proposed model achieves a minimal optimal gap of 0.004% for TSP50 and 0.39% for TSP100, demonstrating its effectiveness in combinatorial optimization.

Table 1 summarizes the performance of different methods, including traditional solvers like Concorde and LKH-3, which still outperform neural network approaches in terms of overall performance and generalization. However, the Transformer-based model offers significantly faster inference times compared to traditional methods.

The authors suggest that further improvements can be made by employing advanced sampling techniques, such as group beam-search and Monte Carlo Tree Search, as well as incorporating heuristics like 2-Opt to enhance the learning process. The paper also notes that scaling the model to larger TSP sizes presents challenges due to GPU memory limitations and the computational complexity of Transformer architectures.

In conclusion, while the Transformer architecture shows promise for TSP optimization, traditional solvers remain superior in performance, highlighting the need for ongoing research to bridge this gap and expand capabilities to larger problem sizes.
