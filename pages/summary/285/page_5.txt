This paper discusses the architecture of a Transformer-based model designed for solving the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP). The model employs traditional positional encoding to order nodes in the tour, with specific sine and cosine functions defined for even and odd indices. 

In the decoding process, the model consists of four main parts. The first part utilizes self-attention over the partial tour, where multi-head attention, residual connections, and layer normalization are applied. The complexity of this step is O(t), where t represents the decoding step. Key equations are provided to illustrate how the query, key, and value matrices are computed at each layer.

The second part of the decoder focuses on querying the next city from the non-visited cities using a query-attention layer. This also employs multi-head attention and carries a complexity of O(n) per recursive step.

The third part performs a final query with single-head attention to produce a distribution over non-visited cities, from which the next node is sampled using Bernoulli sampling during training and a greedy approach during inference. The complexity remains O(n).

The paper contrasts the use of Transformers in natural language processing (NLP) and TSP, noting that while the input sequence order is irrelevant for TSP, positional encodings are still essential for output sequence order in both domains. The TSP encoder benefits from Batch Normalization due to the simultaneous consideration of all cities, while Layer Normalization is preferred in the TSP decoder due to its auto-regressive nature. 

Additionally, the model is trained using Reinforcement Learning, eliminating the need for prior TSP solutions or approximations. It is noted that both the NLP and TSP Transformers exhibit quadratic complexity. The paper concludes by highlighting differences in the decoding architecture compared to closed neural network models previously proposed, specifically in the construction of queries.
