The paper discusses the application of the Transformer architecture, originally designed for natural language processing, to the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP), a well-known NP-hard combinatorial problem. The TSP requires finding the shortest possible route that visits a list of cities exactly once and returns to the starting point. Despite extensive research and the development of various optimization techniques such as cutting planes and simulated annealing, the problem remains computationally challenging.

The authors question whether deep learning can replace traditional human-engineered heuristics with data-driven heuristics. They propose a method that leverages reinforcement learning for training, without the need for pre-existing TSP solutions, and employs beam search for decoding. Their approach demonstrates improved performance over recent learned heuristics, achieving an optimal gap of 0.004% for TSP50 and 0.39% for TSP100.

The paper outlines traditional TSP solving methods, which include exact algorithms that guarantee optimal solutions but become impractical as the number of cities increases, and approximate algorithms that prioritize computational efficiency over optimality. Notable exact algorithms include dynamic programming and integer programming methods, with Concorde being highlighted as the fastest exact TSP solver for large instances. Overall, the research aims to advance the use of neural networks in solving combinatorial problems effectively.
