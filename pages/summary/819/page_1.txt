The paper "Neural Network Pruning with Residual-Connections and Limited-Data" by Jian-Hao Luo and Jianxin Wu addresses key challenges in filter-level pruning of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The authors focus on two main issues: the pruning of residual connections and the effectiveness of pruning with limited data.

Firstly, the paper highlights the difficulty of pruning residual connections, where existing methods often prune only the filters within these connections, leading to suboptimal network structures. The authors propose a novel approach that prunes both channels inside and outside the residual connections based on a KL-divergence criterion. This method results in a more efficient model structure, referred to as the "wallet structure," which enhances representation ability, accuracy, inference speed, and storage efficiency compared to the traditional hourglass structure.

Secondly, the authors investigate the challenges of pruning with limited data. They observe that directly pruning on small datasets often yields poorer results than fine-tuning a smaller model that is either pruned or trained from a larger dataset. To mitigate the impact of label noise from teacher models during knowledge distillation, they introduce a label refinement technique. Their proposed method, CURL (Compression Using Residual-connections and Limited-data), demonstrates superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods on the ImageNet dataset. Notably, CURL achieves comparable or even better results when pruning on small datasets compared to fine-tuning a pretrained small model.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of addressing residual connection pruning and the challenges posed by limited data, offering effective solutions that improve both model efficiency and accuracy.
