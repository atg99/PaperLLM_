The paper presents pruning results on small-scale datasets using various methods applied to models like MobileNetV2 and ResNet50. Four methods are compared: fine-tuning large models trained on ImageNet, fine-tuning small models trained or pruned on ImageNet, direct pruning of large models using slimmable neural networks, and direct pruning using the proposed CURL method. Results indicate that CURL significantly outperforms MobileNetV2-0.5 across all datasets and even exceeds the performance of the original large model on the Oxford Car dataset.

For ResNet50, while CURL provides a strong baseline, the improvement from directly pruning on small datasets is less pronounced. However, CURL still achieves better results than ResNet50-CURL on certain datasets. 

The paper also explores the impact of different fine-tuning strategies through ablation studies, particularly focusing on fine-tuning methodology, pruning criteria, and label refinement. Various fine-tuning strategies are evaluated, revealing that standard approaches yield lower accuracy compared to methods utilizing mixup and knowledge distillation. The most effective combination, incorporating both mixup and knowledge distillation along with label refinement, leads to an accuracy of 78.72% for the pruned model, closely matching the unpruned large model's performance.

Training from scratch results in significantly lower accuracy, reinforcing the advantages of pruning and fine-tuning strategies. Overall, the findings demonstrate the effectiveness of the CURL method in model pruning with limited data, emphasizing the importance of fine-tuning and data augmentation techniques in enhancing model performance.
