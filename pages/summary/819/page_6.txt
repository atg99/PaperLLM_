This paper presents a comparison of various pruning methods applied to the ResNet50 architecture on the ImageNet dataset, as shown in Table 1. The results indicate that the proposed CURL method outperforms previous state-of-the-art techniques in terms of accuracy while requiring fewer Multiply-ACcumulate operations (MACs) and parameters. For instance, CURL achieves a top-1 accuracy of 73.39% with 1.11G MACs and 6.67M parameters, compared to the original ResNet50 which has a top-1 accuracy of 76.15% but requires significantly more resources.

The authors also highlight the advantages of their pruning strategy, which involves removing both channels inside and outside of residual blocks, leading to a more efficient model structure. This structure, referred to as "wallet," is not only more accurate than the "hourglass" structure produced by other methods but also faster, with a shorter inference time on a NVIDIA Tesla M40 GPU.

In the subsequent sections, the paper discusses the application of CURL on smaller datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in achieving comparable or superior performance compared to fine-tuned small models that were trained on larger datasets like ImageNet. The authors establish three baselines for comparison: fine-tuning a large pretrained model, fine-tuning a small pretrained or pruned model, and directly pruning a fine-tuned large model on the target small dataset.

Results from experiments on four small datasets show that while pretraining on ImageNet remains beneficial, CURL can yield competitive results without the need for extensive training on large datasets. The proposed approach thus addresses the challenges of model training in scenarios where large datasets are not available, making it a practical solution for real-world applications.
