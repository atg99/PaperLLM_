The paper discusses the limitations of pruning neural networks directly on small datasets, highlighting that this often results in lower accuracy compared to fine-tuning a model that has been compressed using a larger dataset. The authors note that in many practical scenarios, access to large datasets is restricted, making it necessary to compress and fine-tune models based solely on limited data. They introduce CURL, which stands for Compression Using Residual-connections and Limited-data, to tackle the challenges associated with pruning on small datasets.

CURL proposes a method for pruning channels within residual blocks, emphasizing the simultaneous pruning of all blocks in a stage due to the shortcut connections. The authors utilize a KL-divergence based criterion to assess filter importance, leading to a unique wallet-shaped network structure that is more effective than traditional hourglass-shaped structures. 

To mitigate the impact of limited training data, the paper combines knowledge distillation with data augmentation techniques such as mixup, resulting in an expanded training dataset through image transformations. Additionally, the authors introduce a novel strategy for correcting noise in the teacher model's logits, which further enhances accuracy in pruning scenarios with restricted data.

The contributions of the paper include:
1. A new approach to compressing residual blocks by pruning both channels in the residual branch and output activation maps, resulting in a more advantageous wallet-shaped structure.
2. The effective combination of data augmentation and knowledge distillation to improve model fine-tuning performance with limited data, along with a label refinement strategy to address label noise.

The paper also reviews related work in network pruning, differentiating between non-structured and structured methods, with a focus on filter-level pruning techniques. Overall, the proposed methods aim to enhance the performance of model pruning in real-world applications where large datasets are often unavailable.
