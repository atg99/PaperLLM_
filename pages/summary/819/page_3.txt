The paper introduces CURL, a novel method for network pruning that addresses the challenges of pruning with limited data. Traditional pruning techniques often overlook the impact of pruning on residual connections and typically result in hourglass-shaped models. CURL aims to compress neural networks more effectively by focusing on two key components: pruning residual connections and adapting to small-scale datasets.

The method begins with an evaluation of the importance of each filter in the residual blocks, which are essential components of architectures like ResNet. The authors emphasize the need to prune both the channels within the residual blocks and the output channels, which is a departure from previous approaches that only considered internal pruning. This dual-channel pruning not only speeds up inference but also reduces memory usage and increases the potential for higher accuracy at the same compression ratio.

To implement this pruning strategy, the authors propose a new importance evaluation criterion that assesses multiple filters simultaneously, overcoming the limitations of existing methods that focus on individual layers. This approach is inspired by ThiNet, which minimizes reconstruction error. In contrast, CURL aims to minimize information loss from the final layer, specifically targeting the softmax layer.

The proposed method involves removing output channels from all relevant blocks simultaneously, ensuring that the structural integrity of the network is maintained. The authors utilize batch normalization (BN) parameters to facilitate this process, allowing for the selective zeroing out of filters while evaluating the performance impacts of these removals.

Overall, CURL represents a significant advancement in the field of network pruning, particularly for scenarios with limited data, by providing a comprehensive strategy that integrates residual connection pruning with a focus on maintaining model performance.
