The paper presents a novel filter-level pruning method for MobileNetV2, specifically applied to the CUB200 dataset, aiming to enhance inference speed while maintaining accuracy. The study prunes 50% of filters from the last bottleneck's middle layers without fine-tuning, examining various evaluation criteria for filter importance.

Different pruning strategies are compared: random pruning yields 6.80% accuracy, while weight sum and loss change methods improve this to 8.25% and 21.83%, respectively. The proposed KL-divergence criterion significantly enhances accuracy to 66.33%, demonstrating its effectiveness in assessing information loss during pruning. This suggests that identifying critical filters is crucial, especially when training data is limited.

Additionally, a label refinement technique is introduced to mitigate the effects of label noise by enlarging the dataset through image transformations and updating soft targets during training. Results indicate that this method outperforms standard knowledge distillation approaches, achieving improved accuracy across various datasets.

In conclusion, the proposed CURL method enables effective pruning on small datasets, achieving performance comparable to or exceeding that of fine-tuning larger models, which is particularly valuable in real-world applications where large datasets may not be available.
