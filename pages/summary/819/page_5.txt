In section 3.2.2, the paper discusses label refinement through knowledge distillation, where a large model serves as the teacher and a pruned smaller model acts as the student. The typical approach involves training the student model using both soft targets from the teacher and hard targets from ground truth labels. However, since the teacher model has not been trained on new data, its outputs can be unreliable. To address this, the paper introduces a two-step process for training the student model.

In Step 1, knowledge distillation is combined with mixup, a technique that generates new training examples by blending two random samples. This step aims to improve the performance of the small model using a loss function that balances KL-divergence and cross-entropy losses. This combination helps the pruned model converge to a good local minimum.

Step 2 involves fine-tuning the small model on an expanded dataset with label refinement. The teacher model's soft targets are extracted and stored, and a new loss function, which employs a reversed KL-divergence, is used to enhance label quality during training. The soft targets are updated through stochastic gradient descent to mitigate label noise.

The effectiveness of the proposed method, CURL, is evaluated through experiments on the ImageNet dataset and small-scale datasets, showing that it achieves competitive or superior performance compared to existing models, even without using additional fine-tuning techniques for the ImageNet tests. The experiments utilize PyTorch for implementation.
