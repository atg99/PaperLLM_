This paper discusses a method for pruning neural networks to create smaller models while maintaining performance. Initially, a subset of 256 images from the training dataset is randomly selected to extract prediction probabilities, with the output of the softmax layer denoted as p for the original network and q for the network after filter removal. The similarity between these two probability distributions is assessed using KL-divergence, resulting in a filter importance score calculated as DKL(p||q). A score approaching zero indicates redundancy in the filter, while a higher score suggests greater importance.

The pruning process involves evaluating filter importance across layers, particularly within residual blocks, and involves removing the top k filters based on their scores. The choice of k is determined by computational constraints, with a minimum compression rate set to prevent excessive filter removal in any layer. The advantages of this pruning method include a global assessment of filter impact and adaptive compression, contrasting with previous methods that required fixed ratios per layer.

Following pruning, the smaller model undergoes fine-tuning on a target dataset, which poses challenges due to limited data. To address this, data augmentation techniques, including mixup and image transformations (rotation, cutout, and shuffling), are employed to expand the training dataset. These transformations focus on enhancing local image features, which are critical for object recognition, thereby allowing the model to concentrate on discriminative regions rather than global information. The process is complemented by a label refinement method to improve the quality of logits derived from the teacher model during knowledge distillation.
