In semi-supervised classification, autoencoders can learn useful representations from the unlabeled data and then leverage these representations to improve the classification accuracy on the labeled data. The encoder part of the autoencoder extracts features from the input data, which can then be used as input for a classifier. This approach benefits from the ability of autoencoders to capture the underlying structure of the data, leading to better generalization in classification tasks.

4.3 Applications in Image Denoising and Reconstruction 
Autoencoders are also effective for image denoising and reconstruction. By training on noisy images, autoencoders learn to reconstruct the original clean images, thereby removing noise. This application is particularly useful in scenarios where high-quality images are required but the available data is corrupted. The autoencoder's ability to learn a compact representation of the data allows it to differentiate between noise and actual signal, resulting in improved image quality.

In summary, autoencoders serve as powerful generative models and feature extractors in various applications, including generative modeling, semi-supervised classification, and image denoising. Their ability to learn from both labeled and unlabeled data enhances their versatility and effectiveness in machine learning tasks.
