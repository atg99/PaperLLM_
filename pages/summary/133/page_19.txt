In this chapter, the evolution of autoencoders from naive architectures to advanced models is discussed, highlighting their ability to learn meaningful representations and model generative processes. The chapter identifies a significant limitation of autoencoders: their reconstruction errors do not reflect the realism of the outputs. While variational and disentangled autoencoders have shown success, determining the optimal size and distribution of the hidden state remains an experimental process, reliant on reconstruction error and post-training adjustments. Future research is necessary to establish better parameter settings for these models.

The primary goal of autoencoders is to achieve a compressed and meaningful representation that balances usefulness and reconstruction quality. Identifying architectures that meet these criteria is crucial for advancing their application.
