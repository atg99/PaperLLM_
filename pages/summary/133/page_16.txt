The paper discusses the implementation of Wasserstein Generative Adversarial Networks (WGAN), which utilize the Wasserstein distance to optimize loss functions effectively. The Wasserstein distance, a specific case of Optimal Transport distance, quantifies the distance between two probability distributions, denoted as PX and PG. This distance is defined mathematically using a cost function and has various forms, including the 1-Wasserstein distance, also known as the Earth Moving Distance.

The authors highlight a challenge in traditional autoencoders, where the regularization pushes all samples to appear similar, limiting the use of the entire latent space. In contrast, WGANs leverage the Optimal Transport distance to differentiate between real and generated image distributions. The paper introduces Wasserstein autoencoders (WAE), modifying the loss function to include both reconstruction loss and a penalty for the distance between the latent space distribution and a prior distribution, typically a multivariate standard normal distribution.

The paper also presents a new approach called the Deep Feature Consistent Variational Autoencoder, which employs a different loss function that focuses on the correlation between pixel values in the original and reconstructed images, rather than merely measuring pixel-wise differences. This approach aims to enhance the quality of reconstruction by considering deeper feature representations.
