The paper discusses the application of autoencoders in various contexts, particularly focusing on their use in regularization for supervised models and anomaly detection. In the context of regularization, the loss function is modified to incorporate both the classification loss and a reconstruction loss, weighted by a regularization parameter. This approach enhances the model's performance by imposing a structure on the learned representations.

In anomaly detection, the paper highlights the utility of autoencoders in learning a normal profile from normal data alone. Once trained, the autoencoder is expected to exhibit low reconstruction error for normal samples while showing high reconstruction error for anomalies. This characteristic enables effective identification of outliers in various applications, such as fraud detection and system monitoring. The integration of these methodologies demonstrates the versatility of autoencoders in both supervised and unsupervised learning tasks.
