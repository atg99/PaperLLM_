In this section, the authors discuss the implementation and implications of normal distribution in the context of variational autoencoders. They approximate the posterior distribution \( q_\phi(z|x) \) with a Gaussian distribution defined by the encoder's mean \( g(x) \) and covariance \( h(x) \). To facilitate backpropagation, the reparametrization trick is employed to express \( z \) as a function of \( h(x) \) and a standard normal variable \( \xi \).

The overall loss function for the network combines reconstruction loss and a Kullback-Leibler divergence term, which regularizes the latent distribution \( q_\phi(z|x_i) \) towards a prior \( p_\theta(z) \). The authors introduce disentangled autoencoders, which extend variational autoencoders by incorporating a multiplicative factor \( \beta \) to the KL divergence, allowing for control over the correlation of latent features. A higher \( \beta \) value leads to more disentangled features.

The paper also highlights the versatility of autoencoders in various applications, indicating that different configurations can be adapted for tasks such as classification. This versatility underscores the importance of understanding the underlying mechanisms of autoencoders for effective application in machine learning tasks.
