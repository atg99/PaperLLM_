The study focuses on the structural analysis of the Omicron spike protein and its interactions with hACE2 and S309 antibodies using cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM). The researchers improved the density map quality of the receptor-binding domain (RBD) region by employing heterogeneous refinement followed by RBD-focused 3D classification and homogeneous refinement, achieving a resolution of 2.85 Å for the Omicron spike bound to one hACE2. The methodology included the collection of super-resolution movies and detailed image processing using software like MotionCor2 and cryoSPARC. A total of 7,582,944 particles were analyzed, leading to the reconstruction of various spike conformations.

For the Omicron spike-S309 complex, 2D and 3D classifications identified different conformations, including one-RBD-up and two-RBD-up structures. The final models were refined to resolutions of 2.50 Å and 2.80 Å, respectively. Further local refinement of the RBD-S309 structure yielded a density map at 2.66 Å resolution.

The study also examined the binding affinities of the S304 antibody to both the Omicron and prototypic spikes using surface plasmon resonance (SPR), while pseudotyped viruses were created for neutralization assays. The results indicated the effectiveness of the S304 antibody against both variants.

Model building involved docking known spike protein structures into cryo-EM maps, followed by iterative refinement. The data supporting this study, including atomic structure coordinates and electron microscopy maps, are publicly available. Overall, the research contributes to understanding the structural dynamics of the Omicron variant and its interactions with neutralizing antibodies, which is vital for therapeutic development.
