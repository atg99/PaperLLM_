The study investigates the binding and neutralizing activity of the S304 antibody against the SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variant compared to the prototypic strain. Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis revealed that S304 has a higher binding affinity for the Omicron receptor-binding domain (RBD) (KD=1.01 nM) than for the prototypic RBD (KD=5.61 nM). However, its neutralizing efficacy against Omicron is significantly reduced (IC50 > 100 μg/mL) compared to the prototype (IC50 = 10.94 μg/mL).

Structural analysis of the Omicron RBD-hACE2-S304 complex indicated that while the epitope targeted by S304 is conserved, the hydrogen bond network in the Omicron variant is altered, replaced by salt bridges. This structural change does not affect the binding affinity but contributes to reduced neutralization capacity. The study also notes that the Omicron spike protein exhibits a preference for the one-RBD-up conformation, which may require more energy to transition from down- to up-RBD states, indicating weaker RBD dynamics compared to the prototype.

Additionally, the S304 antibody may bind to the spike during RBD motion, but the tighter packing of the Omicron spike stabilizes the one-RBD-up conformation. The overall findings highlight the complex interplay between binding affinity and neutralizing activity, emphasizing the impact of conformational changes in the RBD on antibody efficacy against different variants.
