The study investigates the structural dynamics of the Omicron variant of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, particularly focusing on the one-RBD-up conformation upon binding to the ACE2 receptor. It was found that the one-RBD-up state does not undergo significant conformational changes after ACE2 binding, maintaining the interface between the down- and up-RBDs. The research specifically examines the impact of three mutations in Omicron (S371L, S373P, and S375F) on the stability of this conformation. When these residues were reversed to their prototypic counterparts in a mutant protein, the structure predominantly adopted a three-RBD-up conformation upon ACE2 binding, suggesting that these mutations facilitate the transition to an open conformation.

The study further refines the cryo-EM structure of this complex, revealing notable conformational changes in the residues 363-380 of the mutant compared to the Omicron RBD, indicating that the observed changes in structure are directly linked to the three specific substitutions. Additionally, the research explores the S309 antibody's neutralizing activity against Omicron, contributing to a deeper understanding of the spike protein's structural adaptations and implications for vaccine and therapeutic development.
