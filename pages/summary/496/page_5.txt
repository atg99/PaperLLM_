The study investigates the binding and neutralizing properties of the S309 antibody against the Omicron variant of SARS-CoV-2. It was found that key residues targeted by S309 remain largely unaffected by mutations in the Omicron spike protein, allowing S309 to retain its binding and neutralizing capabilities. The antibody binds to the receptor-binding domain (RBD) of the spike protein, which is accessible in both its up and down configurations. Structural analysis revealed that the S309-RBD interaction maintains the integrity of the RBD interface post-binding.

In contrast, the S304 antibody, which is a weaker neutralizing antibody (NAb) from a convalescent SARS patient, shows a reduced neutralizing capacity against Omicron. S304 recognizes the inner face of the RBD, specifically in the down configuration, limiting its effectiveness against the variant. This difference in recognition and binding mechanism between S309 and S304 underscores the impact of specific structural features on antibody efficacy against emerging viral mutations.
