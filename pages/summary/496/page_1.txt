The paper investigates the structural properties of the Omicron variant of SARS-CoV-2, focusing on the spike protein's mutations and their implications for receptor binding and antibody evasion. Using cryo-electron microscopy, the authors reveal that the Omicron spike predominantly adopts a one-RBD-up conformation both before and after binding to the human ACE2 receptor. This contrasts with previous variants, which exhibit more dynamic conformational changes upon receptor interaction.

Key findings include the identification of specific mutations—S371L, S373P, and S375F—that enhance the stability of the one-RBD-up conformation. This stabilization limits the exposure of additional up-RBDs during ACE2 binding, thereby restricting the accessibility of neutralizing antibodies, particularly S304, which targets a cryptic epitope in the closed spike conformation.

The study underscores the implications of these mutations for Omicron's ability to evade immune responses, contributing to the variant's rapid global spread. By elucidating the conformational dynamics of the Omicron spike, the research enhances the understanding of its receptor binding and antibody escape mechanisms, which are critical for developing effective strategies against COVID-19.
