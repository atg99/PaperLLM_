The study examines the structural differences between the S304 Fab bound to the prototypic spike and the Omicron RBD-S304 structure. Alignment of these structures with the Omicron spike in its two-RBD-up conformation reveals persistent steric clashes between S304 and the up-RBD and NTD. To eliminate this steric hindrance, an outward shift of the RBDs and NTDs in the Omicron spike is necessary. However, due to the reduced motion of the Omicron RBDs, this shift is constrained. The more stable one-RBD-up state of the Omicron spike diminishes the exposure of the S304 neutralizing antibody epitope, thereby compromising its neutralizing efficacy. The findings highlight the implications of structural stability on antibody recognition in the context of viral variants.
