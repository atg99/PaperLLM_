The paper discusses fuzzy logic operators, specifically focusing on the AND, OR, and NOT operations applied to fuzzy sets A and B. The AND operator is defined as the minimum of the truth degrees of the sets, expressed as µ(A AND B) = min(µ(A), µ(B)). The OR operator corresponds to the maximum value, represented as µ(A OR B) = max(µ(A), µ(B)). The NOT operator indicates negation, calculated as µ(NOT A) = 1 − µ(A). 

The paper also highlights the role of IF-THEN rules in fuzzy inference systems (FIS), which allow for the formulation of conditional statements based on domain knowledge. These rules are activated when the conditions of the IF part are met, leading to the application of the THEN part. An example provided illustrates this with a rule concerning speed and distance in relation to accident severity.

Furthermore, the section introduces the Mamdani-type fuzzy inference system, noted for its widespread use, which consists of four main components. The details of these components are further illustrated in the accompanying figures.
