The paper discusses membership functions used in fuzzy logic, specifically focusing on triangular and trapezoidal membership functions, as well as Gaussian membership functions. 

To calculate the degree of membership of a value, such as x = 20, in a triangular membership function, the paper demonstrates the use of max and min operators. The degree of membership is computed through a series of calculations that yield a result of 1/3 for the example provided.

The trapezoidal membership function is introduced as a more flexible alternative to the triangular function. It is defined by parameters a, b, c, and d, where a and d mark the base's endpoints and b and c indicate the core's endpoints. The concepts of the core and support of the trapezoidal membership function are defined, with Core(A) representing values where the membership degree is 1, and Support(A) encompassing all x-values with a membership degree greater than 0.

The trapezoidal function can be symmetric or asymmetric, and its mathematical formulation is presented. Additionally, the Gaussian membership function is characterized by its center and width, with a specific exponential expression provided for its calculation. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of these membership functions in fuzzy logic applications, showcasing their definitions, calculations, and characteristics.
