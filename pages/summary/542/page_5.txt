The paper discusses the selection of macro-architecture in cell-based search spaces for neural network models. It highlights the importance of determining both the number of cells and their connections to build effective models. Examples from previous research illustrate different approaches: Zoph et al. (2018) create a sequential model where each cell receives inputs from the two preceding cells, while Cai et al. (2018b) incorporate cells into established architectures like DenseNet. 

The paper argues for the joint optimization of both macro-architecture and micro-architecture to avoid the need for manual adjustments after identifying a well-performing cell. Liu et al. (2018b) introduced a hierarchical search space with multiple levels, including primitive operations and motifs connecting these operations, which can be viewed as a specific instance of the broader hierarchical search framework.

The complexity of the search space significantly impacts the optimization challenge. Even in simpler cases, the optimization remains non-continuous and high-dimensional, as more complex architectures typically yield better performance, increasing design choices. The authors note that many architectures can be represented as fixed-length vectors, allowing for the definition of bounded search spaces while maintaining a finite number of layers.

The subsequent section addresses various search strategies suitable for exploring neural architecture spaces, including random search, Bayesian optimization, evolutionary methods, reinforcement learning, and gradient-based techniques. Historically, evolutionary algorithms have been applied to evolve neural architectures and their weights, demonstrating the ongoing relevance of these methods in architectural search.
