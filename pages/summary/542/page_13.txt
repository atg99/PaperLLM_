The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and the need for a more comprehensive search space that can represent and identify hierarchical structures. Previous works, such as those by Liu et al., have initiated this exploration. The authors highlight that current NAS methods often rely on predefined building blocks, limiting the discovery of novel architectures, which could enhance NAS capabilities.

The performance evaluation of NAS architectures is complicated by various factors beyond the architecture itself, such as the search space, computational budget, data augmentation, and training procedures. The paper notes that improvements in these aspects might overshadow the advantages of the architectures generated by NAS. Therefore, establishing common benchmarks is deemed essential for fair comparisons among different NAS methods.

A significant contribution mentioned is the benchmark proposed by Ying et al., which includes around 423,000 unique convolutional architectures, allowing for low-resource comparisons of search strategies through a pre-computed dataset of training and validation results. The authors also suggest that evaluating NAS within a broader AutoML system, which optimizes hyperparameters and data augmentation alongside architecture search, could yield valuable insights.

Despite the impressive results achieved by NAS, the paper points out that there is still a lack of understanding regarding why certain architectures perform well and the similarity of architectures derived from independent runs. The authors advocate for the identification of common motifs in successful architectures and an exploration of their relevance across different problems.
