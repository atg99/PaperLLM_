The paper discusses Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods, focusing on three key components: search space, search strategy, and performance estimation strategy. 

The search space defines the range of architectures that can be explored, with the inclusion of prior knowledge potentially reducing its size but also introducing bias that may limit the discovery of innovative architectures. 

The search strategy outlines the approach to navigate the often vast search space, balancing the need for rapid identification of high-performing architectures with the risk of prematurely settling on suboptimal designs. 

Performance estimation strategy aims to evaluate the predictive performance of architectures, traditionally through training and validation, which is computationally intensive. Recent research emphasizes developing more efficient estimation methods to facilitate broader exploration of architectures. 

The article is organized into sections discussing search spaces, search strategies, and performance estimation methods, concluding with future directions for NAS. Section 2 specifically addresses common search spaces, starting with chain-structured neural networks as a straightforward example.
