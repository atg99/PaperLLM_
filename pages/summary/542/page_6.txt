The paper discusses the evolution of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and highlights significant milestones in its development, particularly focusing on the use of Bayesian optimization and reinforcement learning (RL) strategies. Beginning with early successes in NAS, such as state-of-the-art vision architectures and the first automatically-tuned neural networks that outperformed human experts, the paper notes that NAS gained traction in the machine learning community around 2017 with the work of Zoph and Le. Their approach utilized extensive computational resources but paved the way for various methods aimed at reducing costs while enhancing performance.

The authors frame NAS as an RL problem, where generating a neural architecture is treated as an agent's action within a defined action space. The agent's reward is determined by the performance of the architecture on unseen data. Different RL techniques are explored, including the use of recurrent neural networks for sampling architectures and Q-learning for selecting layer types and hyperparameters. The paper also presents a simplified interpretation of the architecture sampling process as a stateless multi-armed bandit problem.

Additionally, it discusses an alternative method proposed by Cai et al., which treats NAS as a sequential decision process involving partially trained architectures and function-preserving mutations. This approach employs bi-directional LSTM for fixed-length encoding of architectures and integrates actor networks to decide on actions, trained using the REINFORCE algorithm. The paper also mentions neuro-evolutionary approaches, tracing their origins back several decades, as another avenue for optimizing neural architectures.
