This paper discusses various methods for speeding up performance estimation in Neural Architecture Search (NAS). Key approaches include:

1. **Lower Fidelity Estimates**: This involves reducing training time by using fewer epochs, a subset of data, downscaled models, or lower-resolution images. While this method decreases computational costs, it may introduce bias by underestimating performance. However, as long as the search strategy relies on relative rankings of architectures, this may not pose a significant issue. It is suggested that a gradual increase in fidelity can help mitigate the risk of ranking instability.

2. **Learning Curve Extrapolation**: This technique reduces training time by extrapolating performance from initial training epochs. It allows for the termination of poorly performing architectures early in the search process, thereby speeding up evaluations. Various studies explore how architectural hyperparameters can enhance the prediction of promising partial learning curves.

3. **Weight Inheritance and Network Morphisms**: This strategy involves initializing new models with weights inherited from previously trained models, allowing for faster convergence without starting from scratch.

4. **One-Shot Models and Weight Sharing**: In this method, only a single model is trained, and its weights are shared across multiple architectures, which are considered subgraphs of the one-shot model. This significantly reduces the computational burden.

The paper highlights that while these methods can effectively expedite the performance estimation, they also present challenges in maintaining accuracy within a large search space. Overall, the discussion emphasizes the balance between efficiency and the reliability of performance predictions in NAS.
