Bayesian Optimization (BO) has emerged as a popular method for hyperparameter optimization but has seen limited application in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) due to its reliance on Gaussian processes, which are typically suited for low-dimensional continuous optimization. Some researchers have attempted to adapt BO for architecture search by deriving kernel functions specific to architecture spaces. However, alternative approaches utilizing tree-based models like tree Parzen estimators and random forests have shown greater effectiveness in navigating high-dimensional conditional spaces, achieving state-of-the-art results in optimizing both neural architectures and hyperparameters together. Preliminary evidence suggests these methods may even outperform evolutionary algorithms.

Other strategies include employing Monte Carlo Tree Search to exploit tree structures within the search space and utilizing hill climbing algorithms that incrementally improve architectures based on performance. Additionally, Liu et al. introduced a continuous relaxation method that allows for gradient-based optimization by parameterizing network architectures through convex combinations of operations. This approach enables the simultaneous optimization of network weights and architecture parameters, eventually leading to a discrete architecture.

Performance estimation for architectures is critical, as the simplest approach involves training each architecture from scratch, which can be computationally prohibitive. To address this, methods have been developed to estimate performance using lower-fidelity metrics, thereby accelerating the evaluation process. These strategies are essential for efficiently identifying architectures that maximize performance metrics like accuracy on unseen data.
