The paper discusses the evolution of neuro-evolutionary approaches that utilize genetic algorithms for optimizing neural network architectures and weights. Early methods employed genetic algorithms for both architecture and weight optimization, but as neural architectures grew in complexity, stochastic gradient descent (SGD) became the preferred method for weight optimization due to its superior performance in supervised learning tasks.

Recent advancements in neuro-evolutionary techniques have shifted to using gradient-based methods for weight optimization while retaining evolutionary algorithms exclusively for architecture optimization. These algorithms evolve a population of models, where mutations—such as adding or removing layers and adjusting hyperparameters—are applied to generate offspring from selected parent networks. Different methods vary in their strategies for parent selection, population updates, and offspring generation. For instance, some approaches utilize tournament selection for parent sampling, while others focus on multi-objective optimization. 

The paper also highlights case studies comparing reinforcement learning (RL), evolutionary methods, and random search (RS), revealing that RL and evolutionary methods yield comparable final test accuracies, with evolutionary methods demonstrating better anytime performance and smaller model sizes. Both methods consistently outperform RS, albeit by a small margin. Additionally, there is evidence that evolving large weight sets can be competitive with gradient-based optimization under certain conditions, such as in reinforcement learning tasks, although gradient-based methods remain dominant in supervised learning scenarios.
