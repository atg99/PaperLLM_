The paper discusses one-shot architecture search (NAS) in neural networks, highlighting a model that incorporates multiple candidate operations for each node, such as different convolutional and pooling layers. This approach allows for weight sharing across various architectures, enabling the training of a single supergraph from which subgraphs can be derived for specific architectures. 

Previous methods have either optimized architecture distributions during training or utilized a fixed distribution, with evidence suggesting that the combination of weight sharing and a carefully chosen fixed distribution is sufficient for effective one-shot NAS. The paper also mentions meta-learning techniques, where hypernetworks generate weights for new architectures without needing to train each architecture individually.

However, a limitation of one-shot NAS is the predefined supergraph, which restricts the search space to its subgraphs. Additionally, keeping the entire supergraph in GPU memory limits the size of the supergraph and search space, often leading to smaller, cell-based configurations. Although weight sharing has significantly decreased the computational resources needed for NAS, the paper raises concerns about potential biases introduced when the architecture sampling distribution is optimized alongside the one-shot model. This could result in premature convergence or discrepancies between one-shot and actual architecture performance.
