The paper discusses strategies to accelerate performance estimation in neural architecture search (NAS) by leveraging previously trained architectures. One notable method is network morphisms, which allows for the modification of architectures while maintaining their functionality, significantly reducing training time to just a few GPU days. This approach facilitates the progressive enhancement of network capacity without starting from scratch, although it can lead to overly complex models if strictly applied. To mitigate this, approximate network morphisms are suggested, enabling the reduction of architecture size.

Another prominent technique is One-Shot Architecture Search, which conceptualizes all architectures as subgraphs of a larger supergraph. This method allows for weight sharing among architectures, enabling performance evaluation without separate training, thus speeding up the estimation process. However, this technique introduces bias, often underestimating the actual performance of the best architectures. While it can effectively rank architectures, the correlation between estimated and actual performance remains uncertain.

Various one-shot NAS methods differ in their training approaches. For instance, ENAS utilizes an RNN controller to sample architectures and employs approximate gradients for training. DARTS optimizes the one-shot model's weights by introducing a continuous relaxation of the search space, while SNAS focuses on optimizing a distribution over candidate operations using a differentiable approach. ProxlessNAS addresses memory constraints by binarizing architectural weights, allowing only one edge per operation to remain active, and learning the probabilities associated with these edges. Lastly, Bender et al. propose a method where the one-shot model is trained only once, demonstrating that stochastic deactivation of model parts during training can suffice.
