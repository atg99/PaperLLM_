The paper discusses future research directions in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), highlighting the need to expand beyond the current focus on image classification. While image classification serves as a benchmark due to extensive manual engineering, it limits the potential for NAS to discover fundamentally different architectures. The authors emphasize exploring NAS in less studied domains, such as image restoration, semantic segmentation, transfer learning, machine translation, reinforcement learning, and recurrent neural networks. They also suggest promising applications in generative adversarial networks and sensor fusion.

Another key area for future exploration is the development of NAS methods for multi-task and multi-objective problems, which incorporate resource efficiency alongside predictive performance. This aligns NAS with network compression techniques, indicating that some compression methods can be viewed as NAS strategies and vice versa.

The paper proposes extending reinforcement learning and bandit approaches to create policies based on task properties and resource requirements, as well as enhancing NAS to identify architectures resilient to adversarial examples. Additionally, it suggests the need for broader and more flexible search spaces, as existing cell-based architectures may not generalize well to other domains. The authors call for further studies on NAS in reinforcement learning, noting that while some architectural choices have been made, comprehensive research remains to be done.
