The paper discusses the concept of a cell-based search space for neural architecture design, as proposed by Zoph et al. (2018). This approach involves two types of cells: normal cells that maintain input dimensionality and reduction cells that decrease spatial dimensions. The architecture is formed by stacking these cells, which offers several advantages:

1. The search space is significantly smaller because cells typically have fewer layers than full architectures, resulting in a seven-fold speed-up in optimization while enhancing performance compared to previous methods.

2. The modular nature of cell architectures allows for easier adaptation to different datasets by adjusting the number of cells and filters, demonstrated by the successful transfer of cells optimized on CIFAR-10 to ImageNet.

3. The principle of creating architectures through repeated building blocks has proven effective in various contexts, such as in RNNs and residual networks.

This cell-based methodology has been adopted in numerous recent studies, indicating its effectiveness and versatility in neural architecture search.
