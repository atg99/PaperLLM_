The paper discusses the search space representation used in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) algorithms, specifically focusing on how architectures are encoded. It highlights three main representations: a directed acyclic graph, a string representation of node connections and operations, and a vector representation that captures the probabilities of operations between nodes. 

In the context of NASBench, the paper explains that the goal is to search for a convolutional neural network (CNN) cell with a specific configuration, resulting in a large number of possible architectures. Each architecture undergoes multiple training runs to evaluate its performance, which is then used to rank the architectures based on accuracy.

The paper introduces a metric to evaluate the likelihood that the best architecture discovered through the search process outperforms a randomly searched architecture. This probability is calculated using the rank of the best architecture found relative to the total number of architectures.

Furthermore, the representation of the search space is elaborated upon. Candidate architectures are sampled from a defined neural search space, composed of interconnected nodes, each assigned an operation. The string representation assigns characters to denote connections or operations, while the vectorized representation encodes these relationships as probabilities, allowing for a more nuanced understanding of the architecture's structure.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of effective search space representation in enhancing the efficiency of NAS algorithms.
