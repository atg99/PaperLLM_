The paper evaluates the search phase of Neural Architecture Search (NAS), a method designed to automate the creation of deep learning networks. Traditional comparisons of NAS algorithms focus on their performance in downstream tasks, which overlooks the effectiveness of their search strategies. The authors propose a framework to assess the search phase directly by comparing the quality of solutions generated by NAS algorithms against those produced by random architecture selection.

Key findings include: 
1. State-of-the-art NAS methods generally yield results comparable to random selection.
2. The commonly used weight sharing strategy may obscure the true performance of NAS candidates, negatively affecting the search process.

The authors argue that their evaluation framework is essential for developing NAS strategies that can consistently outperform random approaches. The paper highlights the need for a more nuanced understanding of the search phase in NAS, emphasizing that current evaluation methods do not adequately capture the intricacies of different search strategies.
