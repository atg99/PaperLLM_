The paper critiques the methodology used in Liu et al. (2019b) for selecting optimal architectures in neural architecture search (NAS) by highlighting the potential pitfalls of choosing a single architecture based on limited training epochs. Specifically, it points out that models showing early promise may not maintain that performance after full convergence, suggesting that the best architecture selection after just 300 epochs for PTB and 100 for CIFAR-10, using a single random seed, may not reflect true general performance.

To address the challenges posed by large search spaces, the authors propose utilizing reduced search spaces with known architecture performances to better evaluate search quality. For recurrent neural networks (RNNs), they reduce the number of nodes from 12 to 2, resulting in 32 possible solutions, each trained multiple times to establish a mean performance metric. For convolutional neural networks (CNNs), they leverage the NASBench-101 framework, which offers a structured search space with a significant number of architectures.

In the experimental results, the paper compares several state-of-the-art NAS algorithms—DARTS, NAO, ENAS, and BayesNAS—against a random search policy within standard RNN and CNN search spaces. The experiments reveal that random sampling is robust and often outperforms more sophisticated NAS methods, particularly highlighting the impact of weight sharing on search effectiveness. The RNN results indicate that the random policy consistently yields competitive performance, even surpassing established algorithms in certain metrics. This underscores the importance of evaluating architecture choices comprehensively rather than relying solely on initial performance indicators.
