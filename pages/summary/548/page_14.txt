This paper discusses the evaluation and adaptation of architectures in neural network models, specifically focusing on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The authors emphasize the importance of fair evaluation by using the same model backbone and parameter initialization across all searched architectures. They note that accuracy variations are minimal when evaluated under consistent conditions. 

For RNNs, the methodology involves maintaining the best architecture's connections from the search phase while increasing the hidden state size to enhance capacity. The training process incorporates standard stochastic gradient descent (SGD) initially, transitioning to average SGD for improved convergence. For CNN architectures, RMSProp is employed for optimization, along with techniques like auxiliary heads and cut-out to enhance performance.

The paper also details adaptations made for reduced search spaces in both RNN and CNN contexts. For DARTS, modifications only involve adjusting the number of nodes. In NAO, the initial candidate pool is created by randomly sampling architectures, and the encoder-predictor-decoder network is trained iteratively. ENAS adjustments include reducing sampled architectures and increasing batch sizes.

Additionally, the authors conduct supplementary experiments to investigate the influence of weight sharing in directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) on architecture ranking. They analyze how varying connections affect the dependency of output on node parameters, demonstrating the complexity of architecture evaluation based on shared weights.

Overall, the paper provides insights into the evaluation process, adaptations for reduced search spaces, and the implications of weight sharing in neural architecture search.
