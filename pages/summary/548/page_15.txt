The paper investigates the impact of weight sharing in recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) through experiments in a defined search space. In the RNN analysis, it is shown that the performance of architectures declines as the number of shared weight matrices increases, with the best architectures using no shared weights. This is illustrated in Figure 6, where color coding indicates the extent of weight sharing among different architectures. 

In the CNN space, a similar experiment was conducted using NAS-Bench, revealing a decrease in the Kendall Tau correlation coefficient as weight sharing increases. This indicates that the performance ranking of architectures becomes more disordered when weight sharing is applied, as shown in Table 5.

Additionally, the paper compares random sampling strategies for architecture ranking during training. It highlights that while the DARTS method, which samples multiple architectures, appears to outperform a simpler random sampling approach, this is misleading due to the reliance on early performance as an indicator of final results. The authors argue that their method of sampling one architecture and training it to convergence is a more reliable baseline, as demonstrated in Figure 7, where significant rank variations occur throughout training. The findings suggest that a single random seed may lead to sub-optimal selections, emphasizing the need for robust sampling strategies in neural architecture search.
