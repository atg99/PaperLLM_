The paper presents results on the performance of various neural architecture search (NAS) methods in reduced search spaces for recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Specifically, it compares the mean and best perplexity for RNNs using DARTS, NAO, and ENAS, as well as the mean and best top-1 accuracy for CNNs in the NASBench-101 7-node space.

Key findings include that none of the policies successfully identified the architecture with the best performance. Notably, ENAS consistently converged to the same architecture, indicating its robustness to random seeds. NAO outperformed random sampling on average by maintaining a ranking of architectures, while DARTS failed to discover a top-5 architecture. In terms of CNNs, NAO achieved the highest accuracy of 93.33, but this was still significantly lower than the ground-truth best of 95.06. The ranking of architectures found by all methods did not accurately reflect their true performance, with NAO resembling random search despite its better average performance.

The paper also discusses the impact of weight sharing (WS) on architecture performance. It contrasts results from experiments without WS, which provided the true performance of architectures, against those with WS, where architectures were trained using shared weights. The latter method demonstrated biases due to unbalanced training from ineffective sampling policies. The correlation between architecture rankings with WS and ground-truth rankings was evaluated using the Kendall Tau metric, revealing substantial differences in rankings depending on the training method employed.

Overall, the findings suggest that while some NAS methods can show improved performance over random search, the reliance on weight sharing may hinder the accurate ranking of architectures in terms of their true capabilities.
