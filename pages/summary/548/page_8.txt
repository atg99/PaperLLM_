The paper investigates the impact of weight sharing (WS) on the ranking of architectures within neural architecture search (NAS), particularly focusing on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The findings reveal that the use of WS significantly disrupts the true ranking of architectures, as evidenced by the average Kendall Tau values, which indicate a lack of correlation between WS rankings and actual performance. 

Key observations include:

1. The ranking differences between architectures with and without WS are substantial, with WS consistently failing to produce the true ranking.
2. The average performance of architectures trained without WS is approximately 1% higher than those with WS, demonstrating that WS negatively affects the architecture sampler.
3. For CNNs, the correlation among rankings remains higher compared to RNNs, suggesting that CNNs are generally more robust in this context.
4. There is a clear increase in ranking disorder with greater space complexity, aligning with the amount of weight sharing utilized.
5. The probability of outperforming random search strategies improves significantly when training without WS.

Overall, the results indicate that eliminating weight sharing can enhance the architecture discovery process in NAS, leading to better performance outcomes.
