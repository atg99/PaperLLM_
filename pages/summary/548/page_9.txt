In this paper, the authors analyze the effectiveness of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) algorithms, revealing that their search policies perform no better than random search. They attribute this surprising finding to two main factors: the constrained search space and weight sharing, which disrupts architecture ranking during the search process. This negative impact complicates the overall search and diminishes the performance of the sampled architectures. 

The authors argue that these insights, previously overlooked due to a narrow focus on task-specific results, are crucial for the future development of NAS algorithms. They propose that designing relaxed weight sharing strategies could lead to improvements in NAS methodologies. The conclusions drawn emphasize the importance of understanding the underlying mechanisms of NAS techniques to enhance their effectiveness.
