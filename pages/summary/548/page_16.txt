The paper presents a comparison of state-of-the-art Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods within the NASBench-101 search space, specifically targeting CIFAR-10. Table 6 summarizes the performance of various algorithms based on the number of nodes (n) and their corresponding top-1 accuracy percentages, along with Kendall Tau (K-T) values for one-shot methods.

Key findings include:

1. Sampling-based NAS methods consistently achieve higher mean accuracy with lower standard deviation, indicating easier convergence to local minima but limited exploration of the search space.
2. One-shot methods, such as FairNAS and SPOS, exhibit greater diversity in their solutions, resulting in higher standard deviations but enabling them to identify superior architectures (e.g., FairNAS achieving 94.47 compared to FBNet's best of 93.98).
3. The performance of ENAS improves with an increasing number of nodes.
4. FBNet outperforms DARTS, suggesting that the Gumbel Softmax approach is advantageous.
5. There is significant variance in the performance of these algorithms, which is sensitive to initialization.
6. Notably, even the most effective one-shot algorithms did not identify the overall best architecture with an accuracy of 95.06.

The paper emphasizes the importance of both exploration and exploitation in NAS strategies, highlighting trade-offs between accuracy and variability across different methods.
