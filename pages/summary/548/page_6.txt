The paper evaluates various neural architecture search (NAS) methods, including DARTS, NAO, ENAS, and a random policy, within a constrained search space. Initial results indicate that the random policy performs comparably to established NAS algorithms, with only marginal improvements in top-1 accuracy. Specifically, the best architecture discovered by NAO achieves an accuracy of 97.10%, which is less than 0.5% higher than the random search result of 96.74%. 

The analysis reveals that state-of-the-art NAS algorithms do not significantly outperform random search, particularly in RNN settings. Among the tested methods, ENAS exhibits the lowest variance, suggesting greater robustness, while NAO performs best in CNN settings but poorly in RNN. DARTS demonstrates high sensitivity to random initialization, leading to the largest standard deviation across multiple runs.

To further investigate these findings, the study explores a reduced search space with two nodes, allowing for exhaustive evaluation of all possible architectures. This approach aims to clarify whether the observed performance similarities arise from a constrained search space or the algorithms' failure to identify superior architectures. The results from this reduced space will help determine the upper performance bounds for the evaluated methods.
