The paper by Gruber and Jockisch explores the performance of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs) in the context of motive classification in text. The authors compare these two types of recurrent neural networks, highlighting their strengths and weaknesses in processing sequential data. They reference previous works, including Seidenstücker and Seidenstücker's study on computer evaluation of psychological assessments, and Shrout and Fleiss's research on intraclass correlations for assessing rater reliability, to support their argument. The study is framed within a broader context of content analysis, as noted in the work by Stone et al. on computational approaches. The authors declare no conflicts of interest and affirm that their research adheres to ethical standards. The article is open access, allowing for continued dissemination and citation in academic contexts.
