This paper by Gruber and Jockisch examines the use of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) cells for automated motive classification in text, specifically focusing on achievement motives of hope for success (HS) and fear of failure (FF). The authors discuss the complexity of evaluating picture story exercises, highlighting the need for computer evaluation to enhance human language understanding and practical application.

The paper elaborates on the architectures of GRUs and LSTMs, noting the advantages of each. LSTMs include an input, output, and forget gate, allowing for better handling of long-term dependencies, whereas GRUs feature an update and reset gate, requiring less computational power while maintaining similar performance in certain contexts. The authors suggest that GRUs may be more sensitive to data, while LSTMs may be more specific, leading to varying performances depending on the data characteristics.

The study aims to compare the performance of GRU and LSTM cells in terms of accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity when applied to a dataset of 18,000 coded stories, derived from archival data without demographic information. The hypothesis posits that GRUs will outperform LSTMs in accuracy for low-prevalence content, while LSTMs will show higher sensitivity. The coding process involved training students to ensure consistency in categorizing the stories according to the 11 defined categories of HS and FF.

In summary, the research seeks to enhance the understanding of how different recurrent neural network architectures can be utilized effectively for deep text understanding and motive classification, contributing valuable insights into automated text classification methodologies.
