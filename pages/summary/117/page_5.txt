The paper by Gruber and Jockisch compares the performance of Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) cells in the context of motive classification in text data. The authors highlight that GRU cells tend to exhibit higher specificity due to their exclusion principle, while LSTM cells demonstrate greater sensitivity due to their ability to retain memory. This difference in memory handling may explain why GRUs can outperform LSTMs in certain scenarios, particularly with smaller datasets, as LSTMs are more prone to overfitting.

The study suggests that the choice between GRU and LSTM should be informed by the distribution of the target data and specific classification goals. GRU cells may lead to overclassification but minimize misclassification (alpha error), whereas LSTM cells may capture more information but risk overclassifying (beta error). The authors call for further research to validate these findings with larger and more complex data sets, as well as to explore the applicability of these insights across different contexts beyond motive classification.

The paper also notes that its findings are based on limited data sets, and future studies should consider the implications of these memory architectures in more diverse settings.
