In this study, the authors aimed to automate the classification of motives expressed in stories generated during the Thematic Apperception Test using recurrent neural networks (RNNs). They specifically compared the performance of two types of RNN cells: long short-term memory (LSTM) cells and gated recurrent units (GRUs). Previous research has shown mixed results regarding the superiority of one over the other, prompting the authors to investigate their effectiveness in motive classification.

The study utilized a substantial dataset (N=18000) and employed a 10-fold cross-validation approach. The findings indicated that GRUs achieved a higher overall accuracy (85%) compared to LSTMs (82%) in motive coding. Moreover, GRUs demonstrated greater specificity, meaning they were more effective at correctly identifying the absence of a motive (true negative rate), while LSTMs showed higher sensitivity, excelling in detecting the presence of motives (true positive rate).

A further analysis through a picture-category matrix revealed that LSTMs outperformed GRUs in contexts requiring deep understanding, suggesting that while GRUs may be stronger for general motive classification, LSTMs are better suited for tasks that necessitate a nuanced comprehension of the text.

The authors concluded that neither cell type clearly dominated the other across all aspects of the task, highlighting the potential for future research to explore methods that integrate the strengths of both GRUs and LSTMs for improved performance in text classification related to implicit motives.
