The paper by Gruber and Jockisch compares the performance of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in the classification of motives in text using a recurrent neural network framework. The study employs 10-fold cross-validation across various categories and pictures, revealing how each model performs in terms of classification accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity.

Table 1 presents classification accuracy for both GRUs and LSTMs across different images and categories, highlighting that LSTMs generally outperform GRUs in terms of accuracy for the high success (HS) and failure (FF) scores. For instance, in the HS categories, LSTMs achieve higher accuracy in most instances compared to GRUs. However, GRUs show higher sensitivity in certain categories, indicating their ability to identify more true positives even in cases with lower specificity.

Table 2 evaluates the sensitivity and specificity of both models. The results indicate that LSTMs have a higher specificity in certain situations, particularly where long-term dependencies are crucial for accurate encoding. For example, in scenarios where background knowledge is necessary, LSTMs perform better, while GRUs exhibit greater sensitivity in situations where additional contextual information may lead to confusion.

The discussion emphasizes the potential for automated motive coding using recurrent neural networks. The findings support the hypothesis that LSTM and GRU cells differ significantly in their classification approaches, and their performance varies based on the prevalence of the stimuli being analyzed. Overall, LSTMs tend to excel when the content is frequently represented, suggesting their suitability for complex classification tasks.
