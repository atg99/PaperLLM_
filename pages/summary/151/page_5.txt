The paper discusses the application of logarithmic rules in the context of variational inference, specifically focusing on the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) and its significance in maximizing the likelihood of data. Starting from the logarithm of the probability of data \( p(x_i) \), the authors derive expressions that highlight the relationship between the posterior distributions and the variational approximation.

The key steps include:

1. The transformation of the logarithm of the probability into an expectation involving the approximate posterior \( q(z | x_i) \) and the log probabilities \( p(x_i | z) \) and \( p(z) \).
2. The introduction of the Kullback-Leibler divergence term \( D_{KL}(q(z | x_i) || p(z)) \) as a regularizer, which constrains the approximate posterior to a desired form.
3. The reconstruction term, representing the likelihood of the data given the latent variables, serves to ensure the model can effectively reproduce the input data.

The authors emphasize that by selecting Gaussian distributions for both the latent prior \( p(z) \) and the approximate posterior \( q(z | x_i) \), one can derive a closed-form expression for the loss function. This choice not only simplifies computations but also introduces a form of regularization due to the inherent spread of Gaussian distributions.

Thus, the paper articulates the foundational principles of variational inference, illustrating how the ELBO serves as a practical approach to approximating complex posterior distributions while maximizing the log probability of observed data.
