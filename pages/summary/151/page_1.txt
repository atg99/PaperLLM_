This tutorial by Stephen G. Odaibo focuses on deriving the loss function for the standard Variational Autoencoder (VAE) using variational inference, particularly under the assumptions of a Gaussian latent prior and a Gaussian approximate posterior. The key objective is to maximize an evidence lower bound on the log likelihood of data during training, a necessity due to the intractability of the posterior distribution in Bayesian machine learning.

The tutorial begins with an explanation of Bayes' theorem, which updates beliefs based on new evidence, expressed mathematically as p(z|x) = p(x|z)p(z)/p(x). This foundational concept is critical for understanding the derivation of the variational lower bound. The tutorial emphasizes the importance of deriving these concepts from basic principles, including the conditional probability axiom and joint probability definitions, leading to the formulation of the Kullback-Leibler divergence, which plays a significant role in the VAE loss function.

Overall, the tutorial systematically presents the derivation process, ensuring that the theoretical underpinnings of variational inference and VAE are clearly articulated for readers aiming to deepen their understanding of these complex topics in machine learning.
