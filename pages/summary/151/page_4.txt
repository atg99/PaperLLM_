The Variational Autoencoder (VAE) utilizes a likelihood distribution denoted as p(x|z), parameterized by a neural network with weights collectively represented as θ. The decoder component of the VAE is thus expressed as pθ(x|z). The encoder outputs parameters for the latent distribution, which are sampled to provide input for the decoder. 

The Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence measures the difference between the approximate posterior distribution q(z|x) and the true posterior p(z|x). This divergence is mathematically represented, and through the application of Bayes’ theorem, it can be reformulated. 

The KL divergence can be broken down using logarithmic identities, leading to an expression that separates the components of the likelihood. Notably, log(p(xi)) is treated as a constant and can be factored out, simplifying the calculations. The integral of the probability distribution q(z|x) equals one, which further streamlines the equation.

Ultimately, the relationship established indicates that log(p(xi)) is greater than or equal to the expectation of the log-likelihood of the observed data under the variational distribution, highlighting the foundational principles of the VAE framework in terms of its probabilistic modeling and optimization.
