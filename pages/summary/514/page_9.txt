This paper investigates the application of self-supervised learning techniques to enhance the robustness and uncertainty of deep learning models, particularly in the context of out-of-distribution (OOD) data detection. The authors demonstrate that self-supervised methods can outperform traditional fully supervised approaches, including Outlier Exposure, which requires access to OOD samples for training.

In the experiments conducted on the CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets, the authors applied geometric transformations to outlier images, aligning them more closely with in-distribution data. Results showed that self-supervised techniques, such as RotNet, consistently surpassed fully supervised models in OOD detection. For instance, on the ImageNet dataset, a combination of various self-supervised enhancements, including translation and self-attention, led to significant improvements in the area under the receiver operating characteristic (AUROC) values, achieving an AUROC of 85.7%.

The study concludes that self-supervised learning not only improves robustness against adversarial attacks and label corruption but also enhances OOD detection capabilities, especially on complex datasets like ImageNet. These findings suggest that integrating self-supervised techniques could be crucial for developing more robust deep learning models and improving data representation in future research.
