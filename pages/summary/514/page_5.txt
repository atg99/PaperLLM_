The paper investigates the impact of various image corruptions on classification accuracy using the CIFAR-10 dataset. It compares standard training methods with an auxiliary rotation self-supervision approach. The study employs a corruption matrix to simulate label corruption, adjusting the corruption strength from 0 to 1. 

The methods evaluated include a baseline without correction, termed No Correction, and the state-of-the-art Gold Loss Correction (GLC), which utilizes a two-stage process to estimate corruption probabilities and train a corrected classifier. The GLC requires a small dataset of trusted clean labels for effective training.

Additionally, the paper explores the use of a combined loss function that integrates standard cross-entropy loss with an auxiliary rotation loss, referred to as Rotations. The results show that training with auxiliary rotation prediction significantly enhances robustness, achieving a 5.6% average error reduction on CIFAR-10 and 5.2% on CIFAR-100, translating to an 11% relative improvement over normal training on CIFAR-100. 

The findings underline the effectiveness of self-supervised learning techniques in improving classification accuracy under conditions of label corruption.
