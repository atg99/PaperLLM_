This paper investigates the impact of auxiliary rotation self-supervision on model performance in the presence of label corruption, specifically using CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets. The findings reveal that models trained with auxiliary rotations exhibit significantly lower test error rates compared to those trained normally, especially in scenarios without loss corrections. Notably, on CIFAR-10, the introduction of auxiliary rotations leads to a 26% relative improvement in performance. 

The results, presented in error curves and summarized in a table, indicate that auxiliary rotation prediction not only enhances robustness against label noise but also proves to be more effective than simply increasing the amount of trusted data within the GLC loss correction framework. For instance, the average error with GLC using 5% trusted data is reduced from 14.6% to 10.5% when combined with auxiliary rotations. 

Additionally, while there is a slight increase in error at 0% corruption strength when using auxiliary rotation predictions, the overall trend shows that these predictions contribute positively to performance across various corruption levels. The paper also discusses how self-supervised learning with rotation prediction aids in detecting out-of-distribution examples, suggesting its broader applicability in improving model robustness and reliability.
