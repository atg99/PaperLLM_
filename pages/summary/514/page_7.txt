This section discusses two methods for out-of-distribution (OOD) detection using the CIFAR-10 dataset. 

In the first part, the authors present a multi-class OOD detection approach where a CIFAR-10 classifier is trained to identify OOD examples. They build upon the work of Hendrycks and Gimpel, who demonstrated that the classifier's posterior distribution can help differentiate between in-distribution and OOD examples. The key metric for evaluation is the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), where a score of 50% indicates random chance and 100% indicates perfect performance. The authors introduce an auxiliary self-supervised rotation loss during training, which enhances the classifier's ability to detect OOD examples. The results show that their method achieves an AUROC of 96.2%, outperforming the baseline maximum softmax probability method, which had an AUROC of 91.4%. The improvement suggests that integrating self-supervised rotation prediction with traditional methods can significantly enhance OOD detection performance without requiring additional data.

In the second part, the authors explore one-class learning for OOD detection. They train a model on a single class from a dataset containing multiple classes and use it to detect OOD examples from the remaining classes. This setup presents a challenging scenario since the OOD examples are similar to the in-distribution examples. The authors compare their approach with existing methods such as one-class SVMs and Deep SVDD, which also aim to model the training distribution to identify OOD instances. Overall, the section highlights the effectiveness of self-supervised techniques in improving OOD detection across different scenarios.
