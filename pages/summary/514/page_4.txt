The paper discusses the integration of a rotation-based self-supervised loss into the training of models using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD). By setting λ to 0.5, the total loss combines a cross-entropy loss for classification and a self-supervised loss based on predicting rotations of images. The self-supervised loss is defined as the average cross-entropy between the model's predictions and the true rotation labels for four angles: 0°, 90°, 180°, and 270°. This modification enhances robustness against adversarial attacks, yielding a 5.6% improvement in performance over standard PGD training, particularly as the perturbation distance increases.

The authors also demonstrate that their approach does not rely on gradient obfuscation, as attacks using SPSA did not degrade performance. Furthermore, they explored the combination of their self-supervised method with stronger defenses like TRADES, achieving a 1.22% increase in robust accuracy over TRADES alone.

In addition to adversarial robustness, the paper investigates the models' resilience to common corruptions, highlighting that self-supervised training significantly boosts performance across various corruption types. The model trained with auxiliary rotations improved average accuracy on corrupted images by 4.6%, while maintaining clean accuracy. Notably, there were substantial gains in robustness against specific corruptions, such as 11.4% improvement for glass blur and 11.6% for Gaussian noise.

Lastly, the paper touches upon the challenge of label corruption in training, indicating that prior research has focused on enhancing model robustness against noisy labels. Overall, the findings suggest that self-supervised learning can effectively enhance model robustness without compromising clean accuracy.
