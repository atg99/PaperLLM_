This paper discusses a method for enhancing the robustness of neural networks against adversarial attacks, specifically through the use of self-supervision with auxiliary rotations. The authors present experimental results that indicate their approach improves upon standard adversarial training methods, particularly Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) adversarial training.

Table 1 shows the adversarial accuracy of models under different training regimes, revealing that while normal training yields no adversarial accuracy, adversarial training alone provides some resilience, particularly with 100-step PGD attacks. The introduction of auxiliary rotations further increases adversarial accuracy.

The authors formalize the problem of defending against bounded adversarial perturbations, focusing on minimizing a specific objective function with respect to a model's parameters. They emphasize the importance of PGD as a first-order adversary and detail the mechanics of the PGD algorithm used for training and evaluation.

The experimental setup employs a Wide Residual Network trained with standard hyperparameters and data augmentation techniques. The proposed method includes a self-supervised component that predicts the degree of rotation applied to input images, thereby enhancing representation robustness. The overall loss function during training combines supervised and self-supervised components.

Results indicate that self-supervision through rotations yields significant improvements in adversarial accuracy, particularly against stronger attacks, and suggests that this approach is robust against gradient-free attacks. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of integrating self-supervised learning strategies into adversarial training frameworks.
