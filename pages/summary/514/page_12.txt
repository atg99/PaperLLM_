This collection of papers addresses various advancements in deep learning methodologies and their applications. 

Zagoruyko and Komodakis introduce wide residual networks, which enhance the standard residual network architecture by increasing the width of the layers, facilitating improved training and performance on various tasks.

Zhai et al. propose S4L, a framework for self-supervised and semi-supervised learning that leverages unlabeled data effectively alongside a limited amount of labeled data, leading to better model generalization.

Zhang et al. discuss a theoretically principled trade-off between robustness and accuracy in machine learning models, providing insights into how to balance these two critical aspects during model training.

Zhang and Sabuncu present the generalized cross-entropy loss, a novel loss function designed to improve the training of deep neural networks in the presence of noisy labels, which is a common challenge in real-world datasets.

Together, these works contribute to the ongoing development of more robust, efficient, and effective deep learning models.
