The paper discusses the effectiveness of self-supervised learning in enhancing model robustness and uncertainty estimation, despite its limitations in improving accuracy compared to fully supervised methods. The authors highlight that self-supervised learning can significantly bolster robustness against adversarial attacks, label corruptions, and common input corruptions, which are often overlooked when evaluating models solely based on clean accuracy. They also note that self-supervision excels in out-of-distribution detection, particularly with challenging near-distribution examples, outperforming fully supervised approaches in certain scenarios.

The findings suggest that self-supervised learning should not be merely regarded as a way to bridge the gap to supervised training, but rather as a complementary strategy that provides strong regularization benefits. This perspective opens up new avenues for evaluating self-supervised learning, particularly in terms of robustness and uncertainty, which are crucial for real-world applications. The authors conclude that self-supervised techniques can lead to improved model performance in various dimensions beyond conventional accuracy metrics.
