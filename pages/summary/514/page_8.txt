This paper evaluates various out-of-distribution (OOD) detection methods on CIFAR-10 classes, presenting AUROC values for several techniques including OC-SVM, DeepSVDD, RotNet, DIM, IIC, and their own self-supervised approach. The results indicate that their method, particularly when combined with Outlier Exposure (OE), outperforms fully supervised models across all classes, achieving a mean AUROC of 95.6%.

The authors describe RotNet as a successful self-supervised technique that predicts image rotations to derive representations. Their method builds on this by integrating geometric transformations and employing a deep network to predict these transformations, which helps improve OOD detection. They also reference Golan and El-Yaniv's geometric approach and Deep InfoMax's representation learning, highlighting how their self-supervised one-class OOD detector surpasses previous techniques.

Additionally, the paper discusses the significance of using Outlier Exposure with a diverse dataset to enhance the learning of the supervised baseline, emphasizing the use of Focal Loss to focus on hard examples. The proposed method involves a deep network that predicts rotations and translations, using a WideResNet backbone with specific training parameters. Overall, their findings suggest that self-supervised learning techniques can effectively enhance OOD detection performance.
