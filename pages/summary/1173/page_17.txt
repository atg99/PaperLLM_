The paper presents statistical analysis results comparing noise-based K-means clustering with Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) and traditional K-means across various datasets in different cities. The Wilcoxon test was conducted to evaluate the performance of these clustering methods based on several evaluation metrics: Silhouette Coefficient (SC), Sum of Squared Errors (SSE), Davies-Bouldin Index (DBI), and Partitioning Around Medoids (PBM).

The results indicate that noise-based K-means consistently outperforms FCM in all datasets, with very low p-values suggesting strong statistical significance. The Z-values and ranksum statistics further support this finding, indicating that noise-based K-means provides better clustering results across the evaluated metrics.

Additionally, comparisons between noise-based K-means and traditional K-means also show favorable results for the noise-based approach. Similar trends in statistical significance are observed, with noise-based K-means demonstrating superior performance in terms of SC, SSE, and PBM, although its performance in DBI varies across datasets.

Overall, the analysis underscores the effectiveness of noise-based K-means clustering in handling taxi GPS data compared to both FCM and traditional K-means methods.
