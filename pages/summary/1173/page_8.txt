The paper presents an analysis of taxi GPS data from five major cities: Aracaju (Brazil), San Francisco (USA), Rome (Italy), Chongqing (China), and Beijing (China) to validate the effectiveness of a proposed noise K-means clustering algorithm. The study utilizes various evaluation metrics, including the DB index, PBM index, silhouette coefficient (SC), and sum of squares for error (SSE), to assess the clustering results. A lower DB index indicates better clustering performance, while a higher PBM index and SC signify improved clustering quality. SSE also reflects better clustering with its lower values.

The GPS data is characterized by its spatial distribution across urban areas, including hotspots, which can be challenging to identify. The experimental setup employed VMware with specific hardware and MATLAB for algorithm implementation. Parameters for different clustering algorithms were optimized to achieve efficient performance, with a total of 200 iterations per algorithm conducted independently 20 times. The noise K-means algorithm demonstrated the ability to automatically determine both the number of clusters and the initial clustering centers, showing comparable evaluation results to traditional methods like FCM and K-means. Overall, the findings highlight the potential of the noise K-means algorithm in urban hotspot discovery using taxi GPS data.
