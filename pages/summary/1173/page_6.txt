The paper outlines the steps for implementing a noise-based K-means clustering algorithm, focusing on urban hotspot detection using GPS data. 

Step 1 involves setting the number of clusters K, which is determined based on the number of GPS data points (N). The clustering number is set between √N/2 and √N to adequately represent urban hotspots.

Step 2 describes the optimization of K through a binary inversion method. Here, K is converted from decimal to binary, a binary digit is randomly flipped, and the new binary number is converted back to decimal to yield an updated K.

Step 3 focuses on optimizing the clustering center using the sum of squares for error (SSE). This metric evaluates the clustering's effectiveness, helping to identify the optimal center.

Step 4 entails outputting the optimized clustering center, which is then utilized to address urban road planning challenges by identifying urban hotspots based on the clustering results.

The algorithm's complexity is primarily influenced by the dataset size, with a time complexity of O(n²), indicating that it does not significantly depend on the number of iterations or noise factors.
