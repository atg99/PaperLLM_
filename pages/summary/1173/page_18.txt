The paper presents a novel noise-based K-means clustering algorithm aimed at improving the determination of clustering numbers and the initialization of cluster centers, which are challenges in traditional K-means clustering. This new method was applied to identify urban hotspots in various large cities globally. The effectiveness of the clustering results was evaluated using several metrics, including the Davies-Bouldin index (DBI), PBM index, silhouette coefficient (SC), and sum of squared errors (SSE). 

Statistical analysis through Wilcoxon rank sum testing demonstrated significant differences in urban hotspot distributions among the noise-based K-means, fuzzy C-means (FCM), and conventional K-means methods. However, comparisons between noise-based K-means and K-means plus revealed no significant differences, particularly noted in the case of Roma, Italy, suggesting similar spatial distributions. The findings indicate that the noise-based K-means algorithm successfully captures urban hotspots that more accurately reflect real-world scenarios.

The proposed algorithm has broader applications beyond urban hotspot analysis, including document classification, customer segmentation, ride data analysis, and criminal network analysis. However, the study acknowledges limitations, such as the insufficient amount of GPS data to fully represent urban hotspot distributions and challenges in avoiding specific buildings during analysis. These issues will be addressed in future research.
