The paper compares various clustering algorithms, specifically focusing on the noise K-means clustering method in the context of urban hotspot detection using GPS data from different cities. The results indicate that the noise K-means algorithm has a longer average iteration time due to its need to determine the appropriate number of clusters and initialize cluster centers. However, it outperforms traditional K-means and FCM in identifying urban hotspots and achieving better clustering evaluation results.

The performance of the algorithms is illustrated through average iteration times for different cities, showing that the noise K-means consistently provides better clustering numbers. The SSE convergence curves for the various methods demonstrate that most algorithms, except FCM, converge within approximately ten iterations, validating the effectiveness of the noise-based K-means algorithm for basic partition clustering tasks.

Additionally, the paper employs a visual presentation through the Amap system to intuitively display the urban hotspots identified by the clustering algorithms, further emphasizing the practical application of the noise K-means method in urban analysis.
