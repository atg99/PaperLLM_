The paper examines the effectiveness of various clustering algorithms—Fuzzy C-Means (FCM), K-means, K-means plus, and noise-based K-means—in identifying urban hotspots from taxi GPS data in five cities: Aracaju (Brazil), San Francisco (USA), Rome (Italy), Chongqing (China), and Beijing (China). The results indicate that the clustering outcomes differ significantly across cities, highlighting issues such as local optimum phenomena and uneven distributions of hotspots.

The FCM algorithm exhibited poor clustering results, marked by serious dispersion and local optimum issues, particularly in San Francisco. K-means also showed suboptimal clustering with multiple hotspots clustering together in cities like Rome and Beijing, leading to ineffective urban planning. K-means plus improved clustering outcomes but still faced local optimum issues in Rome. In contrast, the noise-based K-means algorithm provided the best clustering performance, achieving optimal clustering centers and distributions across all cities, thereby effectively capturing urban hotspots relevant to public spaces like shopping areas and parks.

Additionally, a Wilcoxon rank sum test was employed to statistically validate the clustering results. This non-parametric test assesses the significance of differences between clustering evaluations, with results indicating that the noise-based K-means significantly outperforms the other algorithms. The statistical analysis results are detailed in the accompanying tables. Overall, the findings advocate for the noise-based K-means algorithm as a superior method for urban hotspot identification, enhancing the planning of roads and public services.
