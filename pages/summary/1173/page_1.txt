This paper presents a novel K-means clustering algorithm enhanced by a noise algorithm aimed at identifying urban hotspots, crucial for addressing urban congestion issues prevalent in large cities. The traditional K-means algorithm, while valuable for urban road planning, faces challenges in determining the optimal number of clusters and initializing cluster centers. To overcome these limitations, the authors propose a method that incorporates noise to randomly adjust data point attributes, facilitating the automatic determination of cluster numbers and the initialization of centers.

The effectiveness of the proposed algorithm is evaluated using four unsupervised metrics: Davies-Bouldin index (DB), PBM index, silhouette coefficient (SC), and sum of squared errors (SSE). Additionally, a nonparametric Wilcoxon statistical analysis is utilized to assess clustering results' distribution and differences. The algorithm is tested against five taxi GPS datasets from various cities, including Aracaju, San Francisco, Rome, Chongqing, and Beijing, and compared with existing methods such as fuzzy C-means and K-means plus.

Results indicate that the noise K-means clustering algorithm successfully determines the number of clusters and initializes centers more effectively than traditional methods, demonstrating superior clustering performance and accuracy in identifying urban hotspots. This research contributes to the ongoing efforts to leverage information technology in urban development and congestion management.
