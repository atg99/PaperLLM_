This paper presents a novel noise K-means clustering algorithm designed to effectively identify urban hotspots. The key contributions include the development of a clustering method that incorporates a noise algorithm to enhance data point attribution, allowing for automatic determination of cluster numbers and initialization of cluster centers. The algorithm utilizes four unsupervised evaluation metrics—DB, PBM, SC, and SSE—to assess clustering outcomes, alongside non-parametric Wilcoxon statistical analysis to examine the distribution and differences in results.

The noise K-means clustering algorithm operates in three stages. Initially, the noise algorithm enhances data point attribution and outputs clustering results by introducing noise to automatically ascertain the number of clusters and initialize cluster centers. Subsequently, the K-means algorithm refines the generated cluster centers, integrating the noise approach to improve clustering effectiveness. Finally, the algorithm is applied to capture urban hotspots and yield optimal clustering results.

Comprehensive experiments conducted using five sets of taxi GPS data validate the proposed algorithm's effectiveness in addressing the challenges of traditional K-means clustering, particularly in urban road planning contexts.
