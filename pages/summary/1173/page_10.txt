The paper presents a comparative analysis of various clustering algorithms applied to taxi GPS datasets from different cities, specifically focusing on the performance metrics of PBM index and DB index evaluation values. 

In the PBM index evaluation, the results show that the K-means plus algorithm generally outperforms the other methods across multiple cities, including Aracaju, San Francisco, Rome, Chongqing, and Beijing, with maximum, average, and minimum values indicating its effectiveness in clustering. For instance, in Chongqing, K-means achieved a maximum value of 0.0627, while K-means plus reached 0.06133.

The DB index evaluation results similarly highlight the superiority of K-means in most cases, with notable values in cities like San Francisco and Rome. The K-means algorithm consistently shows competitive performance, although FCM also demonstrates strong results in some instances.

Additionally, the paper discusses the average running times of the algorithms, revealing that the noise K-means clustering algorithm requires longer iteration times due to its need to determine the optimal number of clusters and initialize clustering centers. Despite the longer execution time, this algorithm excels in identifying urban hotspots and provides better clustering evaluation results.

Overall, the findings suggest that while K-means and its variants are effective in clustering GPS data, the noise K-means algorithm offers advantages in identifying urban hotspots, despite its higher computational demands.
