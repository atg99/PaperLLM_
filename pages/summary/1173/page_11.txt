The paper presents a comparative analysis of clustering algorithms applied to taxi GPS datasets from various cities, specifically focusing on the evaluation of Sum of Squared Errors (SSE) and the average running time for each algorithm. 

Table 6 summarizes the SSE evaluation values across different locations including Aracaju, San Francisco, Rome, Chongqing, and Beijing. The results indicate that the Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) algorithm consistently yields the highest SSE values across all datasets, suggesting it may be more effective in capturing data variability compared to K-means and its variations. For instance, in Chongqing, FCM achieved an SSE maximum of 151.343, while K-means reached 109.436.

Table 7 presents the average running times for each algorithm, highlighting the efficiency of the standard K-means, which consistently shows the shortest processing times across all datasets. For example, in Aracaju, K-means had an average running time of 2.3773 seconds, significantly less than FCM's 21.7398 seconds.

Additionally, the SSE convergence curves for the different algorithms are illustrated in Figures 4-8, providing visual insights into the performance of each method in terms of convergence behavior over iterations. Overall, the findings suggest a trade-off between clustering accuracy (as indicated by SSE) and computational efficiency, with K-means being faster but potentially less accurate than FCM.
