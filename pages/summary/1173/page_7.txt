The paper presents a Noise-Based K-Means Clustering Algorithm designed to enhance the clustering of taxi GPS datasets. The algorithm begins by initializing a noise radius and recording the number of solutions from the dataset. It establishes a noise reduction rate throughout the iteration process and selects initial solutions randomly, evaluating them using the Sum of Squared Errors (SSE).

Key steps include generating new random values within the noise radius and determining whether these new solutions are optimal based on their differences from current solutions. The algorithm also adjusts parameters based on iteration counts, ensuring that randomness is introduced to the clustering process. The final output includes the number of clusters and new cluster centers, with data points assigned to the nearest centers based on distance.

The clustering process involves obtaining the number of clusters (K) and initial centers using the noise algorithm, which introduces randomness to improve solution quality. The integration of the K-means plus algorithm enhances the sensitivity of cluster center determination, optimizing the clustering centers further. Ultimately, the paper highlights the use of Euclidean distance for similarity measurement and SSE for evaluating clustering results, leading to the identification of urban hotspots within the dataset.
