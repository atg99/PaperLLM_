The paper discusses the advancements in urban transportation management facilitated by the integration of cloud computing, big data, the Internet of Things, and artificial intelligence. These technologies have enhanced the predictability and application of rail transit and urban transportation systems, addressing the challenges of urban congestion in densely populated areas. The study highlights the significant increase in civil vehicle sales from 2010 to 2020, underscoring the growing demand for effective urban transport solutions.

The authors emphasize the importance of utilizing big data as a strategic resource for smart cities. Researchers are leveraging GPS trajectory data to uncover insights related to urban operations, including traffic congestion analysis, crowd movement distribution, travel recommendations, and road planning. Machine learning algorithms, particularly clustering and feature learning techniques, are commonly employed to analyze vehicle trajectory patterns, which include origins and destinations, stops and moves, and moving objects.

The paper references various studies and methodologies aimed at improving traffic management. Notable contributions include models for traffic congestion estimation, such as the Klobuchar correction model, methodologies for assessing congestion between bus stops, and frameworks for quantifying traffic congestion using convolutional neural networks. These efforts collectively aim to enhance urban planning and operational efficiency, ultimately contributing to the development of smarter urban environments.
