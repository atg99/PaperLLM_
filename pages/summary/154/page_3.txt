This paper presents a novel anomaly detection method using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) combined with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, referred to as the VAE-LSTM model. The study focuses on detecting anomalies in time series data, specifically using real-world datasets such as NYC taxi requests, ambient temperature, and CPU utilization from AWS.

The methodology involves training the LSTM model on sequences of embeddings generated from the VAE. The LSTM predicts future embeddings based on past ones, and the prediction error is minimized to optimize model parameters without relying on anomaly labels. For real-time anomaly detection, the model analyzes recent readings, reconstructs data using the VAE decoder, and calculates a score function based on prediction errors. Anomalies are flagged when this score exceeds a defined threshold, which is determined using a validation set.

The model's effectiveness is evaluated against several benchmarks, including VAE, LSTM-AD, and ARMA. Results indicate that the VAE-LSTM model outperforms the alternatives in terms of precision, recall, and F1 score across different datasets, demonstrating its capability to detect anomalies more effectively over longer periods due to its hierarchical structure. Visualizations of the results illustrate the strengths and weaknesses of the various methods, highlighting the high precision of LSTM-AD but its lower recall, while VAE shows good recall but suffers from false positives.

In conclusion, the VAE-LSTM model shows significant promise for anomaly detection in time series data, providing a robust approach that leverages the strengths of both VAE and LSTM methodologies.
