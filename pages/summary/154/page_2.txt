The paper presents a novel anomaly detection algorithm that integrates Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks to effectively identify anomalies in time series data. VAEs are highlighted for their strength in modeling normal behaviors by learning embedding schemes, but they struggle with long-term anomaly detection due to their limited local window analysis. To address this, the proposed method utilizes VAEs as local feature extractors, while LSTMs are employed to capture long-term dependencies in the data.

The algorithm processes time series data in a structured manner, using a sequence of past readings to predict whether an anomaly has occurred. It consists of two main components: a VAE module for extracting local features from short windows of data and an LSTM module for estimating long-term trends. The VAE model is trained in an unsupervised manner, using rolling windows of data to learn a low-dimensional embedding, which is then fed into the LSTM for further analysis.

The training process involves separating the time series into training and testing datasets, with the training set consisting of segments devoid of anomalies. The VAE's encoder and decoder work together to reconstruct the original data from the embeddings, while the LSTM processes the embeddings of multiple windows to predict the next window's embedding. The reconstruction error serves as the anomaly detection score.

The proposed VAE-LSTM model aims to improve the detection of infrequent anomalies by leveraging both local feature extraction and long-term trend analysis, distinguishing it from existing hybrid models typically applied to video analysis. This combination enhances the model's ability to detect anomalies over sequences of consecutive windows in time series data.
