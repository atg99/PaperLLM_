The references listed in the paper encompass a variety of studies focused on anomaly detection techniques, particularly utilizing advanced machine learning methods such as neural networks and variational autoencoders. Key contributions include:

1. Goldstein and Uchida provide a comparative evaluation of unsupervised anomaly detection algorithms for multivariate data, highlighting their effectiveness in diverse applications.
2. Chandola et al. present a comprehensive survey on anomaly detection, reviewing various methodologies and their implications.
3. Ghosh and Schwartzbard explore the application of neural networks for detecting anomalies and misuse in security contexts.
4. Wen and Keyes investigate time series anomaly detection using convolutional neural networks and transfer learning, showcasing innovative approaches in temporal data analysis.
5. Malhotra et al. demonstrate the use of long short-term memory networks for anomaly detection in time series, emphasizing the importance of recurrent architectures.
6. Kingma and Welling introduce auto-encoding variational Bayes, a foundational work for probabilistic models in anomaly detection.
7. An and Cho focus on variational autoencoders for anomaly detection, particularly through reconstruction probability.
8. The works by Suh et al. and Kawachi et al. expand on conditional variational autoencoders and complementary set approaches for supervised anomaly detection.
9. Xu et al. leverage variational autoencoders for unsupervised anomaly detection in web applications, addressing seasonal key performance indicators.
10. Hundman et al. utilize LSTMs and dynamic thresholding for spacecraft anomaly detection, illustrating real-world applications of these methods.

These references collectively contribute to a deeper understanding of the challenges and solutions in anomaly detection across various domains, utilizing both supervised and unsupervised learning techniques.
