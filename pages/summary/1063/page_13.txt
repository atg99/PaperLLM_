The paper investigates the effectiveness of the HUGO steganography analyzer on both real images and those generated by the Steganographic Generative Adversarial Network (SGAN) model. The results show a significant drop in the accuracy of HUGO steganalysis from 0.624 to 0.499 when analyzing SGAN-generated containers, indicating that SGAN can effectively create steganography containers that deceive specific steganalysis algorithms.

Further experiments were conducted using images generated by the Deep Convolutional Generative Adversarial Network (DCGAN). A fixed training set of 160,000 images was utilized, and two experimental conditions were tested: using the same seed value for image generation and tuning the DCGAN for additional epochs. The accuracy results revealed a decrease from 0.982 in the first condition to 0.499 in the second, highlighting the impact of additional tuning on the generator's effectiveness.

Additionally, the paper explores the classification of generated images without steganographic embeddings by training a DCGAN conditional model on the MNIST dataset. The classifier achieved high accuracy on both real and synthetic images, suggesting that it is feasible to develop classifiers that perform well across both domains. However, it also notes that artificial image generation undermines traditional steganalysis techniques.

In the section on information encryption using the SEGAN model, the authors utilize the MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets to assess the model's ability to generate realistic images with hidden information. The experiments focus on evaluating the realism of the generated images and the quality of the encryption. Results demonstrate that the images produced, particularly from the MNIST dataset, exhibit full realism, indicating the potential of SEGAN for effective information encryption.
