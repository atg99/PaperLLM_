The paper outlines a methodology for evaluating the performance of a steganalyzer through a structured experimental protocol. The process begins with independently drawing binary values and message selections to synthesize cover entities. The generated samples are assessed using K-fold cross-validation to ensure that the stego-sample is independent and identically distributed.

To control the diversity of embedded messages, three scenarios are introduced: a fixed message, a pool of messages, and arbitrary messages. This diversity is crucial for validating the performance of the steganalyzer across different message distributions. The authors propose using a generator trained by SGAN to achieve a distribution invariant to the least significant bit embedding of messages from a non-uniform distribution.

In the experiments, both real and synthetic datasets are created. The synthetic dataset is generated using the outlined sampling method, while the real dataset is derived from a reference distribution. Each dataset is used to train separate steganalyzers, which are then combined through weighted majority voting to produce a final output.

The generator and steganalyzer are both designed as one-dimensional convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The generator transforms input noise into a specified output range, utilizing layers for upsampling and activation functions. The steganalyzer processes input samples through convolutional layers, extracting features and producing a confidence score indicating the presence of a message.

Overall, the study aims to explore the transferability of learned features across real and synthetic domains through rigorous validation protocols, ultimately contributing to the understanding of steganalysis in varying contexts.
