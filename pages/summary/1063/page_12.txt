The paper presents findings on the performance of a steganalyser (S3) trained on real and generated images, with a focus on comparing the efficacy of two generative models: SGAN and DCGAN. The architecture of the steganalyser consists of multiple layers, including 2D convolutional layers, max pooling, and fully connected layers, culminating in a sigmoid output. This design achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in steganalysis, aided by a specific filter (F(0)) that enhances training convergence.

The training employs the Adam optimization algorithm with a specified learning rate and binary cross-entropy as the loss function. The experimental setup involves training the SGAN and/or DCGAN to produce images used as containers, followed by training the independent steganalyser on both real and generated images.

Results indicate that while DCGAN-generated images can effectively deceive the steganalyser, SGAN-generated images, designed specifically for this purpose, show only marginal improvements over DCGAN. This suggests potential overfitting of S3 to the real image distribution, resulting in limited generalization to synthetic images. Furthermore, the paper notes an interesting outcome involving the HUGO steganography algorithm applied to SGAN-generated containers, highlighting the relevance of the SGAN model in steganographic applications.
