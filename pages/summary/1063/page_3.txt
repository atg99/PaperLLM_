The research aims to enhance the adaptability of containers used in steganography to effectively deceive steganalysis methods. The work is divided into two primary tasks: 

1. **Adaptive Containers Generation**: This involves creating a model to generate image containers that can integrate with any steganography algorithm. The goal is to produce containers that can mislead any steganalysis approach, ensuring adaptability to various types of analysis.

2. **New Steganography Method**: This task focuses on developing a model for generating images that conceal information within them. The objective is to test the encryption and decryption quality using the MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets. This model differs from the first by not only generating empty containers but also encoding information, akin to visual markers like QR codes.

The paper also discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for this purpose. GANs operate through a two-player minimax game where the generator learns to mimic real data distributions, while the discriminator attempts to distinguish between real and synthetic samples. The training process seeks a saddle point solution, balancing the optimization of both generator and discriminator. The research acknowledges the challenges of training GANs, such as the requirement for a Nash equilibrium and potential issues with the discriminator overpowering the generator during early training stages.

Overall, the study aims to leverage GANs to create adaptive containers and steganography methods that enhance the concealment of information in images while maintaining the quality of the hidden data.
