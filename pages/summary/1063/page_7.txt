The paper discusses the training algorithm for a Steganographic Generative Adversarial Network (SEGAN), detailing its information flow and the steps involved in the training process. The algorithm involves the use of binary keys, messages, and input noise, with the discriminator's loss calculated using cross-entropy. The training is conducted over multiple epochs, where updates to the generator and discriminator models are made based on specific loss functions.

In the experiments section, the focus shifts to the validation of stego-security properties of SGAN-generated one-dimensional vectors. A simpler proof-of-concept experiment is conducted prior to extensive numerical testing on the least significant bit (LSB) embedding within cover images produced by the SGAN. The validation protocol outlines how the generator is assessed after each training iteration, capturing the evolution of the generator's parameters and its output samples.

Overall, the paper presents a structured approach to training SEGANs and evaluates their effectiveness in steganography through rigorous validation methods.
