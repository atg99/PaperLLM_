This paper explores the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the field of steganography, specifically focusing on the generation of secure containers for embedding information. The authors present synthetic images generated for the CIFAR-10 dataset, which, despite their small size, exhibit a high level of realism due to the limitations of current generative modeling techniques.

The quality of encryption is quantitatively assessed through the average percentage of reconstructed pixels, revealing that the proposed model achieves reconstruction quality approaching 1. However, the results indicate that longer messages pose more challenges for encryption compared to shorter ones, as shown in the provided data for various bit lengths across datasets like MNIST and CIFAR-10.

Key contributions of the study include the introduction of a new field for GAN applications in steganography, the implementation of the 1-embedding algorithm to enhance steganalysis security, and the demonstration that both SGAN and DCGAN models can significantly reduce the detection accuracy of steganalysis methods. The paper also proposes a model for generating secure adaptive steganographic containers and outlines strategies to evade steganalysis detection.

The paper culminates in the submission of an article to the NIPS 2016 Workshop on Adversarial Training, which builds upon and extends the findings presented in this work.
