This paper discusses the use of a Steganographic Generative Adversarial Network (SGAN) for steganalysis, specifically focusing on synthetic images and a dataset of celebrities. The dataset consists of 200,000 cropped images, and for the purpose of steganalysis, 10% of the data is set aside as a test set, resulting in 380,000 images for training and 20,000 for testing after applying steganography algorithms.

The SGAN model is structured with convolutional neural network components, specifically a combination of Conv2d, Batch Normalization, and Leaky ReLU layers, forming the basis for both the steganalyser and the image discriminator networks. The generator network employs a fully connected layer followed by several convolutional layers and utilizes the hyperbolic tangent function for output normalization.

Training of the SGAN model was performed using the Adam optimization algorithm with specific parameters, involving updates of weights for the discriminator and steganalyser networks followed by the generator. The paper also introduces an independent steganalyser and a filter specifically designed for steganalysis applications.

The experimental results demonstrate the performance of the SGAN model in generating steganographic images and its effectiveness in steganalysis, as reflected in the k-fold cross-validation metrics such as ROC-AUC and F1-score across different iterations.
