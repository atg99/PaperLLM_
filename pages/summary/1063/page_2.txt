This paper explores the concept of bit-level augmentation in steganography, specifically focusing on how the least significant bits (LSB) of image color channels are utilized to conceal payloads. The traditional LSB method leads to detectable alterations in color statistics, making it vulnerable to machine learning and statistical detection. To mitigate this, the paper introduces the 1-embedding technique, which randomly adjusts pixel values to obscure hidden messages while maintaining the integrity of the last bit.

The paper also discusses advanced steganographic techniques that adaptively modify digital media based on local entropy, targeting complex textures for embedding. It describes a distortion function used to minimize alterations in the cover image while employing coding methods like Syndrome-Trellis Codes (STC). Prominent content-adaptive algorithms discussed include HUGO, WOW, and S-UNIWARD, each utilizing different methods for determining embedding costs.

In the domain of steganalysis, the paper highlights traditional feature extraction methods combined with machine learning classifiers, alongside emerging deep learning techniques using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) that have shown superior detection capabilities compared to conventional methods.

The paper aims to develop an adaptive steganography method that generates stego-containers tailored to the specific detection strategies of adversaries, referred to as Eve, thereby enhancing the overall security of the steganographic process. The goal is to create a system that can dynamically adapt to the characteristics of potential steganalysis approaches.
