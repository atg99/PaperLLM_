The paper discusses the implementation of a Steganography Generative Adversarial Network (SGAN) that utilizes Least Significant Bit (LSB) embedding techniques. The SGAN consists of a generator (G) and a steganalyzer (A), where both networks incorporate Batch Normalization layers to enhance performance. The training process involves a unique loss function that simulates a discriminator's feedback through total variation and L2 loss, without an explicit discriminator.

The generator is tasked with embedding one bit of message per element of the cover vector, achieving a one bit-per-pixel embedding density similar to image processing techniques. The training employs the Adam optimizer with distinct learning rates for the generator and steganalyzer to facilitate a gradual adaptation of the generator to the steganalyzer's feedback.

Validation is performed using two independent steganalyzers: a Random Forest classifier and a 1-dimensional CNN. Hand-crafted features are utilized, derived from an 8-bit reversal procedure to amplify the signal from the stego-embedding. The results indicate that increasing the variety of embedded messages significantly decreases the performance of both steganalyzers, suggesting that the LSB distortion's induced distribution is invariant to changes in message variety.

Further experiments demonstrate that the generator can effectively hide message bits against the Random Forest steganalyzer, even with a low feedback strength. However, when the feedback is insufficient, the generator struggles to maintain an LSB-invariant output distribution, highlighting the importance of the feedback mechanism in generating effective cover vectors.

Overall, the findings reveal that the SGAN can quickly converge to a distribution that is challenging for steganalyzers to detect, particularly when the appropriate balance of feedback strength is maintained during training.
