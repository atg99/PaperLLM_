The paper presents performance metrics for two machine learning models, Random Forest and 1-dimensional CNN, evaluated on cover vectors generated after multiple iterations of training. In Table 1, the Random Forest model shows high performance across various metrics, with the fixed type achieving perfect scores in ROC-AUC, F1-score, accuracy, and out-of-bag (OOB) error. Performance declines with increasing pool sizes, particularly at 4096 and arbitrary types, where metrics drop significantly.

Table 2 outlines the performance of the 1-D CNN, which also demonstrates high initial performance with the fixed type but shows a notable decline as pool sizes increase. The ROC-AUC and F1-scores decrease sharply at larger pools, indicating reduced effectiveness.

Figure 6 illustrates the k-fold cross-validation performance of the Random Forest model on synthetic images produced at different training stages. The findings support the effectiveness of the SGAN model in generating cover vectors that maintain an unconditional distribution invariant to least significant bit (LSB) embedding distortion. Additionally, the embedding approximation provides adequate gradient feedback. The paper concludes by indicating a forthcoming exploration of generating realistic cover images to enhance the security of simple LSB embedding methods.
