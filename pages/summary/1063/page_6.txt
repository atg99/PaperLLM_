The training process for Stochastic mini-batch Gradient Descent in the context of Steganographic Encryption Generative Adversarial Networks (SEGAN) involves specific update rules for its components: the discriminator (D), the steganographic classifier (S), and the generator (G). 

For the discriminator D, the update rule incorporates the gradient of the loss function (ÑGL) which combines the expected log-likelihood of real data and the likelihood of generated data. The steganographic classifier S is updated similarly, focusing on the likelihood of the stego images and the generated images. The generator G is updated with the goal of maximizing the combined error of both D and S, which distinguishes it from traditional GAN models.

The SEGAN model is structured around three main components: Alice (the generator), Bob (the decryption network), and the discriminator. Alice generates images that contain hidden messages, while Bob extracts these messages from the images. The discriminator assesses whether an image is real or generated. This setup can be viewed as an autoencoder where the discriminator serves as a regularization mechanism.

The training process follows standard GAN procedures but includes specific loss functions for each component. The generator's loss (LA) is derived from the standard GAN generator loss, while the encryption loss (LAB) measures the L2 distance between the original and reconstructed messages, ensuring effective message recovery.
