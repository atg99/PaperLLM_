In this paper, the authors address the limitations of existing inpainting methods that primarily focus on reconstructing ground truth images, often resulting in a single optimal output without accounting for diversity in results. Zheng et al. and Zhao et al. propose VAE-based models to enhance diversity but struggle with effectively separating structural and textual information, leading to distorted structures or blurry textures.

The authors introduce a method that leverages a hierarchical vector quantized variational auto-encoder (VQ-VAE) to disentangle structural and textural features. This involves a three-part pipeline consisting of a hierarchical VQ-VAE encoder-decoder, a diverse structure generator, and a texture generator. The hierarchical encoder extracts discrete structural and textural features from the ground truth image, while the decoder reconstructs the image from these features.

The diverse structure generator utilizes an autoregressive network to model the conditional distribution of discrete structural features, allowing for the generation of multiple diverse outputs. The proposed approach aims to improve visual quality in image completion by using novel feature losses evaluated through a pre-trained hierarchical VQ-VAE.

The method's architecture includes vector quantization layers to map the features into discrete representations and employs a straight-through gradient estimator for backpropagation through the quantization process. The total loss function combines reconstruction and commitment losses to ensure stable training. The authors visualize the discrete representations of structural and textural features, highlighting their roles in capturing global and local information, respectively.

Overall, the proposed method seeks to enhance the generation of plausible inpainting results by effectively separating and utilizing structural and textural information.
