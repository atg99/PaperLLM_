The paper addresses the challenges of multiple-solution image inpainting, particularly focusing on the limitations of existing methods that often yield low-quality outputs due to their reliance on parametric distributions for modeling complex image content. Traditional inpainting techniques, such as diffusion and patch-based methods, are unable to generate unique content and often overlook high-level semantics. Recent learning-based approaches employing deep convolutional networks have improved the quality of inpainting by using adversarial learning and contextual features.

The authors propose a novel method based on hierarchical vector quantized variational auto-encoder (VQ-VAE), which distinguishes itself by modeling an autoregressive distribution over discrete latent variables instead of continuous ones. This approach allows for the generation of diverse structural features in an explicit manner, thereby enhancing the quality and structural diversity of inpainted images. The proposed two-stage model consists of a diverse structure generator that samples from a conditional autoregressive distribution to create multiple sets of structural features, followed by a texture generator that uses an encoder-decoder architecture to produce complete images based on these features.

Key contributions of the paper include the introduction of a conditional autoregressive network for generating diverse structures, the incorporation of a structural attention module for texture generation, and the implementation of new feature losses aimed at improving structural coherence and texture realism. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing techniques in terms of both quality and diversity across benchmark datasets such as CelebA-HQ, Places2, and ImageNet.
