The paper presents a novel approach for image inpainting that generates diverse and high-quality results by employing a two-stage hierarchical model based on the hierarchical vector quantized variational auto-encoder (VQ-VAE). The first stage of the model produces multiple coarse inpainting results with distinct structures, while the second stage refines each result by enhancing texture quality.

The authors highlight the challenge of ensuring both the diversity of solutions and the quality of each generated image, as existing methods often lead to distorted structures or blurry textures. To address this, the proposed model separates structural and textural information through the hierarchical architecture of VQ-VAE, which facilitates autoregressive modeling of structural information. This allows for the easy generation of diverse structures.

In the second stage, a structural attention module is integrated into the texture generation network to leverage structural information and capture distant correlations, thereby improving the realism of textures. Additionally, the VQ-VAE is reused to compute two feature losses that enhance structural coherence and texture realism.

Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method not only increases the diversity of inpainting solutions but also significantly enhances the visual quality across various datasets, including CelebA-HQ, Places2, and ImageNet. The authors provide access to their code and models for further research and application.
