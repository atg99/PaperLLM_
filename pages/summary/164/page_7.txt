The paper presents a quantitative and qualitative evaluation of various image inpainting methods, comparing single-solution and multiple-solution approaches on the CelebA-HQ dataset. The metrics used include Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), Inception Score (IS), Modified Inception Score (MIS), and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). The results indicate that the proposed method outperforms both single-solution methods (such as CA, GC, CSA, SF, and FE) and multiple-solution methods (like PIC and UCTGAN) across all metrics, particularly excelling in IS, MIS, and FID, which assess perceptual quality.

In terms of implementation, the model is built using TensorFlow and trained on NVIDIA 2080 Ti GPUs with a specific optimization strategy involving the Adam optimizer and various learning rates. The training process involves sequential training of different components of the model, followed by inference using only the generator components.

Qualitative results demonstrate that while other methods struggle with generating realistic structures or textures, the proposed method effectively synthesizes fine details and maintains structural integrity, showcasing enhanced diversity in outputs. The paper emphasizes the importance of evaluating inpainting methods not only by their accuracy against ground truth but also through perceptual quality metrics that align with human judgment. Overall, the findings suggest a significant improvement in image inpainting capabilities with the introduced method.
