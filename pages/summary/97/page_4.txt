The paper discusses the application of a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) within a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) framework for predicting machine failures. It critiques traditional methods such as Instance-Based Learning (IBL) and Support Vector Regression (SVR) for their limitations in handling noise data and multi-dimensional input variables, respectively. IBL is deemed unsuitable due to its high memory consumption and processing time, while SVR struggles with accuracy as the number of input variables increases, often neglecting the sequence of data which is crucial for prediction.

The paper emphasizes the advantages of deep learning, particularly RNNs, which are designed to capture temporal dependencies in data through their hidden states. RNNs, however, face challenges like exploding and vanishing gradients, which affect their performance in learning long-term dependencies. To address these issues, GRUs utilize two gates—an update gate and a reset gate—to effectively manage data flow during the learning process. This structure allows GRUs to retain important information from previous states while efficiently processing new inputs. The paper concludes by illustrating the GRU architecture, highlighting its potential for enhancing predictive accuracy in machine failure scenarios.
