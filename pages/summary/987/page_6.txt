The paper discusses various pretraining methods for language models, highlighting the importance of the corpus used for effective performance. It categorizes pretraining corpora into general, social media, domain-specific, news, and academic sources, with notable examples such as OpenWebText, C4, and MIMIC-III. The BERT model, for instance, utilized a 16GB dataset from Wikipedia and BookCorpus, while later models leveraged significantly larger datasets like C4 (750GB) and CC-100 (2.5TB) to enhance performance.

The text also outlines the classification of pretraining methods into five types, specifically focusing on Pretraining from Scratch (PTS). Models such as BERT, RoBERTa, ELECTRA, and T5 are mentioned as being pretrained from scratch on extensive unlabeled text, typically involving an embedding layer as part of their architecture. The paper emphasizes the correlation between the size and quality of the pretraining corpus and the resulting model performance, indicating a trend towards using larger and more diverse datasets for training.
