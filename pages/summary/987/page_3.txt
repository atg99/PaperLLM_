In this survey paper, the authors provide a comprehensive review of Transfer-based Pre-trained Language Models (T-PTLMs) and their applications across various fields such as programming, dialogue, networking, academia, and biomedicine. The paper outlines several key components:

1. A brief overview of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL), which is foundational for T-PTLMs.
2. Core concepts related to T-PTLMs, including pretraining methods, tasks, embeddings, and adaptation methods.
3. A new taxonomy categorizing T-PTLMs based on pretraining corpus, architecture, type of SSL, and extensions.
4. A taxonomy for downstream adaptation methods, detailing their characteristics and uses.
5. An overview of benchmarks for evaluating T-PTLMs, both intrinsic and extrinsic.
6. A survey of libraries useful for working with T-PTLMs, including Huggingface Transformers.
7. Future research directions that aim to advance T-PTLMs.

The paper emphasizes the significance of SSL in overcoming the limitations of supervised learning, such as the dependency on labeled data and challenges in data-scarce domains. SSL enables models to learn from vast amounts of unlabeled data, thereby enhancing their generalization capabilities and overall performance on downstream tasks.
