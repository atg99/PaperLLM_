This paper provides an overview of various monolingual Transformer-based Pretrained Language Models (T-PTLMs) across different languages, detailing their pretraining methods and tasks. The models listed include PTT5 for Portuguese, various Swedish models (SweedishBERT, SweedishALBERT, SweedishELECTRA), Polish models (HerBERT, PolishBERT), Czech (RobeCzech), Korean (KLUE-BERT, KLUE-RoBERTa), and Persian (ParsBERT), each with unique corpora and tokenization methods.

The paper highlights the emergence of encoder-decoder models such as MASS, T5, and BART, which utilize a Seq2Seq language modeling approach, allowing for tasks like translation and summarization. It emphasizes the significance of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) in developing T-PTLMs, categorizing it into three main types: 

1. **Generative SSL**: This method involves predicting tokens through various strategies, including next-token prediction, masked token prediction, and reconstructing original text from corrupted input. Notable models using generative SSL include GPT-3, RoBERTa, and BART.

2. **Contrastive SSL**: While no T-PTLMs are solely pretrained with this method, it enhances models like BERT by improving sentence-level semantics. Examples include CERT, Mirror-BERT, and SimCSE, which have shown improvements in performance on language tasks.

3. **Adversarial SSL**: This approach focuses on distinguishing corrupted tokens, with models like ELECTRA and XLM-E employing techniques such as replaced token detection to enhance performance.

Overall, the paper underscores the advancements in T-PTLMs through diverse SSL techniques, contributing to the evolution of natural language processing capabilities.
