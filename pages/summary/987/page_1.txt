The paper "AMMUS: A Survey of Transformer-based Pretrained Models in Natural Language Processing" by Katikapalli Subramanyam Kalyan, Ajit Rajasekharan, and Sivanesan Sangeetha provides a comprehensive overview of transformer-based pretrained language models (T-PTLMs) and their significant impact on various natural language processing (NLP) tasks. The authors trace the evolution of these models from the initial developments of GPT and BERT, emphasizing their reliance on transformers, self-supervised learning, and transfer learning to generate universal language representations from extensive text datasets.

The survey begins with an introduction to self-supervised learning, elaborating on its importance and different types. It then delves into core concepts related to T-PTLMs, including pretraining steps, methods, tasks, embeddings, and downstream adaptation techniques. A new taxonomy of T-PTLMs is presented, categorizing them based on pretraining corpus, architecture, and self-supervised learning types. The authors also review various benchmarks for evaluating T-PTLMs and summarize relevant libraries for practical implementation.

Additionally, the paper discusses future research directions that could enhance the capabilities of T-PTLMs. It aims to serve as a valuable resource for understanding key concepts and keeping abreast of developments in the field. The authors provide a list of T-PTLMs with accessible links for further exploration.
