The paper discusses three primary downstream adaptation methods for utilizing pretrained language models in specific tasks: feature-based, fine-tuning, and prompt-based tuning.

1. **Feature-based Approach**: This method involves generating contextual word embeddings from models like BERT and using these embeddings as input features in task-specific models. Unlike traditional embeddings such as Word2Vec and GloVe, BERT-based embeddings are contextual, address out-of-vocabulary (OOV) issues, and encode more information due to their deep architecture. However, this approach requires training the downstream model from scratch, which necessitates a large amount of labeled data.

2. **Fine-tuning**: This method adapts the pretrained model's weights for specific tasks by minimizing task-specific loss, thereby imparting necessary task knowledge. Fine-tuning enhances model performance by creating distinct clusters for different labels. It typically updates all transformer layers, with higher layers experiencing more significant changes. Different tasks may require task-specific layers, while models like T5, which maintain a consistent input-output format, do not require additional layers. Fine-tuning can take various forms, including vanilla fine-tuning, intermediate fine-tuning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and multi-task fine-tuning. The latter approaches aim to prevent overfitting, especially on smaller datasets, through strategies like adapters or pruning.

Overall, the paper illustrates how these adaptation methods leverage pretrained language models to improve performance on specific tasks while addressing challenges such as overfitting and the need for extensive labeled datasets.
