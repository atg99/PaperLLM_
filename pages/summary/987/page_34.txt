The paper provides a detailed examination of the risks and challenges associated with transformer-based pretrained language models (T-PTLMs). Key concerns highlighted include data leakage, where sensitive information can be extracted from models trained on private data, as demonstrated by the GPT-2 model's ability to generate personal addresses from names. This underscores the necessity for developing advanced methods to assess and prevent data leakage, particularly in sensitive domains like biomedical.

Moreover, the paper addresses the issue of bias in deep learning models, which can perpetuate societal biases present in training data. Recent efforts to mitigate bias include data augmentation techniques and specific approaches to identify bias-sensitive tokens. Continued research is essential to enhance fairness in model decisions.

The paper also discusses the instability of fine-tuning T-PTLMs, noting that different random seeds can lead to significant performance variance. While initial beliefs attributed this instability to catastrophic forgetting and small dataset sizes, new findings suggest that optimization difficulties and generalization issues are the primary causes. Proposed solutions to improve fine-tuning stability include intermediate fine-tuning, mix-out techniques, and careful adjustment of learning rates.

In conclusion, the paper reviews recent advancements in T-PTLMs, covering various pretraining methods, tasks, and adaptation techniques. It introduces a new taxonomy for T-PTLMs and outlines future research directions to enhance these models' capabilities and applications.
