The paper discusses advancements in model fine-tuning techniques in machine learning, particularly focusing on iterative multi-task fine-tuning (MTFT), hybrid MTFT, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and prompt-based tuning.

Iterative MTFT is compared to traditional feature selection, enabling the selection of optimal datasets for model fine-tuning. Mahanjan et al. demonstrated its effectiveness by achieving state-of-the-art results on the clinical STS dataset. However, iterative MTFT is resource-intensive due to multiple training iterations. To address this, hybrid MTFT combines vanilla MTFT with fine-tuning on related datasets using a smaller learning rate, improving efficiency.

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, such as adapters and pruning-based approaches, are explored to minimize the need for separate models for each task. Adapters, developed by Houlsby et al., are small trainable layers that allow for efficient fine-tuning with fewer parameters, enabling performance comparable to fully fine-tuned models with only a fraction of the task-specific parameters. AdapterFusion, proposed by Pfeiffer et al., enhances this by integrating knowledge from multiple tasks, outperforming traditional fine-tuning methods.

Pruning-based fine-tuning capitalizes on the redundancy in deep pre-trained models, allowing for the removal of less critical parameters with minimal impact on performance. Studies show that pruning can significantly reduce model size while maintaining high performance levels.

Lastly, prompt-based tuning addresses the discrepancies between pretraining and fine-tuning objectives by formulating the tuning process as a slot-filling task. This method enhances model performance, especially in few-shot and zero-shot scenarios, by using manually or automatically generated prompts. The paper highlights the evolution from manual prompt creation to automated methods, such as prompt mining and gradient-based search, to improve efficiency and effectiveness in tuning pre-trained language models.
