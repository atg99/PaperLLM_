The paper discusses various types of pretrained transformer language models (T-PTLMs) that enhance the understanding and processing of academic and medical texts. OAG-BERT is highlighted for its unique approach of incorporating entity type embeddings, which augments its training on academic texts with additional context regarding papers, venues, affiliations, and domains. Similarly, medical models like BEHRT and BERT-EHR utilize age and gender embeddings to capture temporal and demographic nuances in patient visit sequences. UmlsBERT emerges as a knowledge-enriched model that leverages UMLS data to improve word representation and semantic associations.

The taxonomy section classifies T-PTLMs based on pretraining corpus, model architecture, self-supervised learning type, and extensions. General models like GPT-1 and BERT are pretrained on diverse corpora, while models like BERTweet are tailored specifically for social media text, demonstrating the importance of corpus type in model efficacy. Language-based models are divided into monolingual and multilingual categories, with the latter showing promise in cross-lingual tasks when trained on both parallel and non-parallel data. The paper emphasizes the ongoing need for specialized models to address the complexities and unique characteristics of different text types across various domains.
