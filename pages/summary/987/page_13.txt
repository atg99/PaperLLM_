The paper discusses various types of embeddings used in natural language processing, focusing on character, sub-word, hybrid, code, and auxiliary embeddings. 

Character embeddings capture the basic building blocks of text, while sub-word embeddings utilize characters and frequently occurring sub-words, generated through tokenizers like WordPiece and Byte Pair Encoding (BPE). These tokenizers create a vocabulary by either augmenting or reducing character sets to fit a predefined size, with considerations for multilingual models requiring larger vocabularies.

Hybrid embeddings, such as those used in CharBERT, combine character and sub-word embeddings to enhance model performance. Code embeddings are specifically relevant in the medical domain, where concepts are represented by standardized codes from ontologies, allowing for effective processing of electronic health records.

Auxiliary embeddings, including position, segment, and language embeddings, provide additional contextual information. Position embeddings are crucial in transformer models, which lack inherent mechanisms for learning token order, while segment embeddings help differentiate tokens from different input sentences. Language embeddings are utilized in cross-lingual models to inform the model about the language of the input, though some models like XLM-R omit them to improve handling of code-switching.

Overall, the choice and implementation of these embeddings are critical for optimizing model performance across various tasks and domains.
