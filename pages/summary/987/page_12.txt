Sub-word Embeddings: Sub-word embeddings, on the other hand, break down words into smaller units—sub-words or frequently occurring segments—allowing the model to represent a broader vocabulary while mitigating the out-of-vocabulary (OOV) issue. Models like BERT, RoBERTa, XLNet, T5, and BART utilize sub-word embeddings, which are generated through tokenizers that create vocabularies based on the frequency of occurrence within the training corpus.

Auxiliary Embeddings: Beyond main embeddings, auxiliary embeddings provide additional contextual information that enhances the model's understanding of the input data. These can include positional embeddings that encode the order of words in a sequence, language embeddings to indicate the language of the text, and other relevant features that support the main input data representation.

In summary, embeddings play a crucial role in transforming input text into a format suitable for deep learning models, particularly in transformer-based pretrained language models. The choice between character, sub-word, or mixed embeddings impacts the model's capability to handle vocabulary size, OOV issues, and the granularity of information captured from the input data.
