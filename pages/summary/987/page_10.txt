The paper discusses various pretraining tasks for language models, comparing their methodologies and efficiencies. 

1. **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)** is a foundational task that predicts masked tokens in a sentence but has limitations: it only utilizes 15% of tokens for training and introduces discrepancies between pretraining and fine-tuning due to the exclusive use of a special mask token.

2. **Replaced Token Detection (RTD)** addresses these issues by identifying replaced tokens instead of masked ones, leveraging bidirectional context and allowing the model to learn from all tokens. RTD consists of two steps: training a generator with MLM and then training a discriminator with RTD loss, which classifies tokens as replaced or not.

3. **Shuffled Token Detection (STD)** is another token-level discriminative task that improves training efficiency by identifying shuffled tokens, enhancing the model's ability to learn coherent sentence representations.

4. **Random Token Substitution (RTS)** allows for identifying randomly substituted tokens without needing a separate generator, thereby reducing computational costs. It substitutes 15% of tokens randomly, achieving performance similar to MLM while requiring less training time.

5. **Swapped Language Modeling (SLM)** replaces tokens with random vocabulary tokens at a lower sampling rate, which helps mitigate discrepancies seen in MLM but is less sample efficient.

6. **Translation Language Modeling (TLM)** extends MLM to cross-lingual scenarios, using parallel sentence pairs to learn cross-lingual mappings. It masks tokens in both sentences and predicts them using context from both.

7. **Alternate Language Modeling (ALM)** focuses on predicting masked tokens in code-switched sentences derived from parallel sentences, further enhancing cross-lingual model training.

Overall, these tasks demonstrate advancements in training language models by addressing previous limitations and enhancing efficiency and performance across various linguistic contexts.
