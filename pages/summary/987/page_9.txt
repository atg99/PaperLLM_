The paper discusses various pretraining methods for language models, focusing on Task Adaptive Pretraining (TAPT) and Knowledge Inherited Pretraining (KIPT). TAPT is noted for its complementary nature to other pretraining techniques, such as PTS and CPT, enhancing model performance when applied sequentially.

KIPT, introduced by Qin et al., combines self-supervised learning with knowledge distillation, allowing new models to leverage information from existing pretrained models. This method is more efficient than traditional self-supervised learning, as it accelerates convergence and improves learning outcomes by integrating knowledge from a teacher model alongside self-supervised learning. KIPT also supports lifelong learning and knowledge transfer, exemplified by the CPM-2 model, which is the first large-scale language model pretrained using this approach.

The paper further explains pretraining tasks, which are self-supervised and designed to provide effective training signals. These tasks should be challenging and relevant to downstream applications. The document highlights two primary tasks: Casual Language Modeling (CLM), which predicts the next word based on context, and Masked Language Modeling (MLM), which utilizes bidirectional context for improved token representation. MLM, first implemented in BERT, masks tokens to enhance the learning process by focusing on predicting masked tokens rather than relying solely on preceding context. This approach has demonstrated significant advantages in model performance.
