The paper discusses the distinctions between Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) and other learning paradigms, emphasizing that SSL requires automatic label generation and aims to provide universal knowledge rather than task-specific knowledge. The primary goals of SSL include learning universal language representations and improving generalization through vast amounts of unlabeled data.

The paper categorizes SSL into three types: 
1. **Generative SSL**: Involves models like autoregressive and autoencoding language models. Autoregressive models predict the next token based on previous tokens, exemplified by GPT-1. Autoencoding models, like BERT and RoBERTa, predict masked tokens using bidirectional context. Hybrid models, such as XLNet, combine both approaches.
2. **Contrastive SSL**: This method involves learning through comparison, as seen in BERT's next sentence prediction (NSP) and ALBERT's sentence order prediction (SOP).
3. **Adversarial SSL**: This approach focuses on identifying modifications in input tokens, with examples including replaced token detection in ELECTRA.

The paper also highlights the significance of pretraining in natural language processing, where models are trained on large volumes of unlabeled text before fine-tuning on specific tasks. This process allows models to learn universal representations, adapt easily to downstream tasks, perform well with limited data, and mitigate overfitting through effective initialization.

Pretraining involves several steps:
1. Preparing an unlabelled text corpus, which can be enhanced by deduplication.
2. Generating a vocabulary using tokenizers like WordPiece or Byte Pair Encoding, resulting in different vocabulary sizes across models.

The discussion underscores the advantages of pretraining, including improved performance on small datasets and reduced training requirements for downstream tasks.
