The paper discusses various methods to optimize deep learning models, particularly Transformer-based Pre-trained Language Models (T-PTLMs), focusing on pruning, knowledge distillation, quantization, parameter sharing, and character-based models.

Pruning involves removing redundant components, such as weights, attention heads, or layers, to enhance model efficiency without significantly affecting performance. This method draws inspiration from biological observations about synaptic reduction during development. Research indicates that many attention heads in T-PTLMs are redundant, and encoder layers can also be dropped during pretraining or inference.

Knowledge distillation is a compression technique where a smaller student model learns from a larger teacher model, aiming to replicate its performance. Different approaches exist, including L2 loss and cross-entropy loss methods to align the student and teacher models. Notable models like DistilBERT and Tiny-BERT have been developed using this technique.

Quantization reduces model size by representing weights with fewer bits, typically down to 8 bits or less. This method is often complemented by pruning. However, quantization is hardware-specific, requiring specialized hardware for implementation. Techniques like mixed-bit quantization and product quantization have been proposed to mitigate performance drops in ultra-low bit models.

Parameter sharing, exemplified by the ALBERT model, reduces the number of parameters through cross-layer sharing and factorized embedding parameterization. This approach allows for significant parameter reduction while maintaining training efficiency.

The paper also addresses character-based T-PTLMs, which utilize character-level embeddings to overcome limitations of sub-word embeddings, such as the inability to encode fine-grained information and susceptibility to noise. Models like CharacterBERT and CharBERT incorporate character and sub-word embeddings, enhancing robustness against typos and improving performance over traditional models.

Lastly, the paper mentions Green T-PTLMs, which focus on adapting general models to specific domains or enhancing them with knowledge from external sources through continual pretraining. Overall, these methods contribute to making T-PTLMs more efficient and effective for various applications.
