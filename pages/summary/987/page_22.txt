The paper discusses advancements in pre-trained language models (PTLMs), particularly focusing on environmentally friendly methodologies and novel approaches to sentence and tokenization-free embeddings. Continual pretraining of models is noted for its high resource consumption and CO2 emissions, prompting the development of "Green models" like GreenBioBERT, exBERT, and E-BERT, which achieve competitive performance while being more sustainable. 

GreenBioBERT enhances the BERT model with domain-specific word embeddings, achieving results comparable to BioBERT, which requires extensive pretraining. exBERT reduces costs by freezing parameters during continual pretraining, while E-BERT integrates Wikipedia2Vec entity vectors, outperforming models that necessitate additional pretraining.

The paper also highlights the limitations of sentence embeddings from traditional T-PTLMs, leading to the emergence of supervised and self-supervised learning approaches. SBERT is introduced as a supervised method that fine-tunes BERT for improved sentence representation, while DvBERT incorporates multi-view learning. Self-supervised methods like IS-BERT, Mirror-BERT, TSDAE, and SimCSE aim to enhance sentence embeddings without relying on labeled datasets.

Additionally, the paper addresses issues with sub-word and character embeddings in existing PTLMs, such as fixed vocabulary constraints and the need for explicit tokenizers, which can hinder performance in certain languages. Tokenization-free T-PTLMs, such as CANINE and ByT5, are presented as solutions to these challenges, operating directly on character sequences or byte-level inputs, thereby reducing parameter requirements and improving robustness against noise.
