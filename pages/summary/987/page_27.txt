The paper discusses various methodologies for evaluating the progress of Task-Specific Pretrained Language Models (T-PTLMs), focusing on prompt mining, generation, paraphrasing, and continuous prompts. Prompt mining involves collecting sentences to create new prompts, while prompt generation utilizes T-PTLMs like T5. Paraphrasing methods, such as back translation and thesaurus equivalences, generate new prompts from seed prompts. Gradient-based search and continuous prompts are also explored, with the latter operating in the embedding space of T-PTLMs, allowing for task-specific vectors to be added independently of T-PTLM parameters. Notably, prompt ensembling, which generates multiple prompts for a single task, has been shown to outperform traditional model ensembling.

The evaluation of T-PTLMs is categorized into intrinsic and extrinsic methods. Intrinsic evaluation focuses on probing the knowledge encoded during pretraining, using tools like LAMA, XLAMA, and others to assess the model's factual and common-sense knowledge. LAMA, in particular, evaluates the model's ability to predict blank tokens in fill-in-the-blank questions based on a corpus of facts. However, LAMA has limitations, including its focus on single-token entities, restrictions on model vocabulary, language constraints, and the simplicity of many fact triples. 

Overall, the paper outlines key concepts and methodologies for evaluating T-PTLMs, emphasizing the importance of both intrinsic and extrinsic evaluations in understanding and improving model performance.
