The paper provides a comprehensive overview of various pretrained transformer models for both social media and multilingual tasks. It categorizes these models based on their architectures, pretraining tasks, corpora, vocabulary sizes, number of languages supported, and parameter counts.

In the section on social media-based transformer pretrained language models (T-PTLMs), several models are highlighted, including HateBERT, RoBERTa-Twitter, and Bertweet, among others. These models leverage large datasets, such as hateful Reddit comments and millions of tweets, for tasks like offensive tweet classification and sentiment analysis.

The multilingual T-PTLMs section discusses models like MuRIL, IndicBERT, and mT5, noting their architectures and the diverse range of languages they support. The models utilize various pretraining tasks and corpora, including Wikipedia and Common Crawl, to enhance their performance across multiple languages.

Key challenges associated with multilingual models are discussed, such as the "Curse of Multilinguality," which refers to the unequal representation of languages due to underrepresented low-resource languages in pretraining datasets. This challenge is compounded by the limited capacity of models, which can lead to performance degradation when additional languages are integrated beyond an optimal threshold. Another identified issue is the "Embedding barrier," which affects the overall performance of multilingual models.

Overall, the paper underscores the advancements in T-PTLMs for social media and multilingual applications while also acknowledging inherent limitations and challenges that researchers must address for improved model efficacy.
