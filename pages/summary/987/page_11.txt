The paper discusses various pretraining tasks for language models, focusing on techniques that enhance cross-lingual understanding and sentence-level semantics. 

1. **Adaptive Language Modeling (ALM)**: The model is pretrained on code-switched sentences, improving the relationships between different languages. ALM demonstrates superior performance compared to traditional Translation Language Models (TLM) as shown by Yang et al. The loss for ALM is calculated based on the probability of predicting masked tokens in a code-switched context.

2. **Sentence Boundary Objective (SBO)**: SBO is a task that predicts masked tokens based on contiguous spans and boundary tokens, differing from Masked Language Modeling (MLM) which masks tokens randomly. SBO is more challenging as it requires predicting entire spans rather than individual tokens, aiding tasks like entity extraction and question answering. The loss for SBO is derived from the probabilities of predicting tokens within a span.

3. **Next Sentence Prediction (NSP)**: NSP focuses on identifying whether two sentences are consecutive, serving as a binary classification task. This training enhances the model's ability to understand sentence relationships, which is beneficial for tasks like question answering and natural language inference. The loss is defined based on the accuracy of predicting sentence continuity.

4. **Sentence Order Prediction (SOP)**: SOP refines the NSP concept by emphasizing sentence coherence. It involves determining whether sentences are in the correct order, with training instances created to ensure balance between swapped and non-swapped pairs. The loss is similarly defined to NSP.

5. **Sequence-to-Sequence Language Modeling (Seq2SeqLM)**: An extension of MLM, Seq2SeqLM is designed for encoder-decoder architectures, predicting masked tokens based on the entire input sequence context. The loss is calculated from the probabilities of predicting masked n-grams sequentially.

6. **Denoising Auto Encoder (DAE)**: DAE reconstructs original text from various forms of corrupted input, providing richer training signals than Seq2SeqLM by focusing on entire sequences rather than just masked tokens. BART is an example of a model utilizing DAE, trained on sentences corrupted through permutation and in-filling. The loss is computed based on the likelihood of reconstructing the original text from its corrupted version.

Overall, these tasks contribute to the development of robust language models that excel in understanding and generating human language across different contexts and languages.
