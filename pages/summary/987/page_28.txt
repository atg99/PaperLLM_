The paper discusses various intrinsic and extrinsic benchmarks used to evaluate language models. It highlights the LAMA probe, which assesses factual and common-sense knowledge, and its extension XLAMA, which supports multiple languages and multi-token entities. Unlike LAMA's Untyped Query (UnTQ), XLAMA utilizes a Typed Query (TQ) approach tailored to relation types. X-FACTR is another multilingual probe that also focuses on multi-token entities. The paper notes that performance varies across languages, with high-resource languages yielding better results, and highlights the potential for improvement through techniques like code-switching pretraining.

Probes such as WDLMPro and WNLaMPro shift the focus from entity relationships to word understanding, using methods like fill-in-the-blank templates and word-definition matching. Extrinsic evaluation is emphasized as crucial for assessing model performance across diverse tasks, with benchmarks providing a standardized approach for comparison. GLUE and SuperGLUE benchmarks are identified as key measures for evaluating natural language understanding, although advancements in model performance have reduced opportunities for further improvement in GLUE. The paper underscores the importance of benchmarks in tracking the development of pretrained language models.
