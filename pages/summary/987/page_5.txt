1. **Vocabulary Size**: The paper notes that multilingual models like XLM-R and mBART utilize large vocabularies to accommodate multiple languages, which can increase the model's size. In contrast, character-based and tokenization-free models may not require such extensive vocabularies.

2. **Pretraining Tasks**: Effective pretraining tasks should challenge the model and enhance its semantic understanding at various levels. The paper highlights the need for tasks that provide ample training signals and are closely aligned with downstream tasks. For instance, SpanBERT's span boundary task relates directly to span extraction, while PEGAUSUS's gap sentence generation is relevant to summarization.

3. **Pretraining Method**: Instead of starting from scratch, the paper suggests using methods like Knowledge Distillation (KD) combined with Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) for more efficient pretraining. It also recommends simultaneous pretraining with general and domain-specific corpora for models operating in specialized fields.

4. **Pretraining Dynamics**: The choice of pretraining dynamics can significantly impact model performance. Techniques such as dynamic masking, larger batch sizes, and increased pretraining steps are advised. Adjusting learning rates based on batch size and layer can improve optimization and convergence.

5. **Pretraining Corpus**: The paper categorizes pretraining corpora into four types, noting that the text characteristics vary significantly across domains. General domain texts tend to be more formal and less noisy, while social media texts are often colloquial and messy. Domain-specific corpora, such as in Biomedical and Finance fields, contain unique terminology that may not appear in general texts. The choice of pretraining corpus should align with the target domain to enhance model effectiveness.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of thoughtful design in the pretraining process to optimize language model performance across various applications.
