The paper introduces the 'backyard dog' problem as a refined approach to face recognition, focusing on a limited group of known individuals, termed 'family members' or 'friends,' while distinguishing them from 'strangers.' Unlike traditional face recognition, which often requires identifying individuals from a vast unknown set, the 'backyard dog' problem simplifies this to a small, known class of identities, facilitating more efficient processing.

The system, referred to as the 'backyard dog,' must not only differentiate between friends and strangers but also verify identities among the family members. This task is framed as a binary classification problem, which is less complex than traditional face recognition challenges that involve a massive number of unknown identities.

The paper also discusses the constraints of this problem, emphasizing the need for timely decision-making within limited hardware resources. A solution leveraging a pre-trained deep learning model is proposed, with the VGG CNN and FaceNet being evaluated as potential candidates. VGG CNN, trained on 2,622 identities, requires extensive preprocessing to compare images, while FaceNet is noted for its various architectures and efficiency in processing. The document highlights the limitations of existing datasets for training face recognition models, underscoring the need for more representative data to enhance performance.
