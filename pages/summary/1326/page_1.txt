The paper addresses the challenge of reducing the depth of pre-trained deep neural networks while maintaining their functionality for specific tasks, particularly in the context of face recognition. The authors focus on the "backyard dog" problem, which involves a network that can correctly identify a limited group of individuals (family members) and distinguish them from non-members (strangers). 

To achieve this, the authors propose a network shallowing algorithm that takes an existing deep learning model and outputs a shallower version. This non-iterative algorithm is based on advanced supervised principal component analysis and aims to produce smaller, specialized networks tuned for specific operational conditions. 

The performance of the proposed method is evaluated through extensive numerical experiments, demonstrating that it can significantly reduce the depth of deep learning networks, albeit with some mild deterioration in performance. The method is generic, applicable to a wide range of feed-forward networks, allowing for the generation of smaller networks from a larger, more universal model. 

Overall, the study highlights the potential for optimizing deep learning models for specific use cases without the need for extensive re-training, thereby addressing the practical limitations associated with data availability and technical constraints in AI applications.
