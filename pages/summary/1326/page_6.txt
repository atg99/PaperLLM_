The paper discusses the construction and operational structure of a 'backyard dog' network derived from a given legacy network. The authors suggest that while all layer types could be evaluated, it is advantageous to first eliminate fully connected layers to facilitate image scaling as an additional hyper-parameter. The post-processing routine consists of three main stages: centralization, spherical projection, and the construction of a new fully connected layer. 

Centralization involves subtracting the mean vector calculated from the training set, while spherical projection normalizes each data vector to unit length. The new fully connected layer produces the output feature vector for the 'backyard dog' network. The paper explains that centralization can be integrated as a network layer, and the spherical projection is a known L2 normalization technique. For the construction of the fully connected layer, advanced supervised PCA is employed.

The output vector interpretation involves identifying a set of identities, categorized into families and non-family members. The network output for an image is compared to the outputs of images within the family to determine if the image belongs to a family member or is classified as a stranger. A decision threshold is established to minimize misclassification errors, specifically the misclassification of family members.

Overall, the paper details the methodology for creating a specialized neural network that enhances the identification of individuals in image datasets while minimizing classification errors.
