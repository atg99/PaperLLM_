The paper provides a comparative analysis of memory requirements and computational resources for various neural network architectures, specifically VGG16, FaceNet, and DeepFace. Key metrics include the number of weights in millions, features passed between layers, and floating point operations (FLOP) required per image processing. 

Table 2 summarizes the architectures' characteristics, showing that VGG16 has the highest weight count at 144 million and a significant FLOP of 15,475 million. FaceNet's configurations vary in weights and FLOP, with NN1 utilizing 140 million weights and 1,606 million FLOP, while other models exhibit lower resource demands. DeepFace aligns closely with VGG in terms of training methodology, utilizing a multi-class setting and evaluating image pairs to determine identity.

The paper highlights differences in training datasets, noting that VGG was developed using the largest dataset, while others from Google and Facebook are not publicly accessible. A comparison of computational efficiency across platforms shows that C++ implementations on the Raspberry Pi are competitive with TensorFlow implementations. 

In conclusion, the authors emphasize the varying interpretations of "deep" networks, as evidenced by the different layer counts across architectures, and provide insights into the practical applications of these networks, particularly in single-core environments.
