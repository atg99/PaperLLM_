The selection process in the optimization algorithm involves comparing the fitness values of parent and offspring vectors, where the member with the lower fitness value advances to the next generation. This process is mathematically represented, ensuring that the most fit individuals are retained.

The Linear Population Size Reduction (LPSR) technique enhances the performance of the SHADE algorithm by dynamically adjusting the population size according to a linear function. At the end of each generation, the population size is recalculated, with a minimum of four members required, and the worst-ranked individuals are removed if the new population size is smaller than the previous one.

The objective of the optimization is to accurately estimate parameters for photovoltaic (PV) cells, ensuring that data at three critical points—open circuit, short circuit, and maximum power points—are satisfied. Unlike past studies that focused only on the maximum power point, this approach incorporates all three points into the optimization objective. For both single and double-diode models, specific parameters such as the diode ideality factor, series resistance, and parallel resistance are optimized, while other parameters are calculated based on established relationships.

The objective function formulation must guarantee that the PV module performance aligns with the I-V characteristics at the specified points. The optimization algorithm aims to minimize errors at these points, with the error at the open circuit point being explicitly defined. This comprehensive approach ensures that the estimated parameters yield current and voltage values consistent with the desired performance characteristics of the PV cells.
