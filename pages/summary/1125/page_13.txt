The paper discusses the parameter estimation techniques for solar cells, specifically focusing on the single and double-diode models. It highlights that the double-diode model offers improved accuracy for parameter optimization compared to the single-diode model. The optimization process involves five key variables (a1, a2, RS, RP, IO1), while two additional variables (IO2, IPV) are derived from the optimized ones. 

Results from the optimization are presented in Table 9, showing the parameters for the double-diode model of the polycrystalline cell KC200GT. The I-V characteristics obtained from different parameter sets demonstrate zero error at crucial points such as open circuit, short circuit, and maximum power points, illustrated in Figure 10. The normalized values of the optimized variables are depicted in a parallel coordinate plot in Figure 11, revealing a wide range for the RP value and a small absolute value for IO1, ranging from approximately 0.01 to 0.9 p.u. 

Additionally, the study includes results for the double-diode model applied to monocrystalline cell SQ85 and thin film cell ST40, with detailed findings presented in the appendix. Overall, the research indicates that the optimization approach is effective across various types of photovoltaic cells, maintaining consistent results regardless of cell type or parameters considered.
