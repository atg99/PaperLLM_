This paper discusses various optimization algorithms applied to estimate parameters of photovoltaic (PV) cells using experimental data and datasheet information. Several algorithms are explored, including the acceleration coefficients PSO, enhanced leader PSO, flower pollination algorithm, improved shuffled complex evolution algorithm, JAYA algorithm, differential evolution with adjustments, chaotic whale optimization algorithm, and imperialist competitive algorithm. These methods utilize data from solar cells under varying conditions and focus on minimizing error functions related to PV module performance.

The paper highlights the limitations in data provided by PV manufacturers and reviews techniques for parameter extraction based on datasheet information. It compares classical methods with metaheuristic approaches, emphasizing the use of the linear population size reduction technique of the success history based adaptive differential evolution (L-SHADE) algorithm for optimizing parameters without prior assumptions about them. 

Key contributions include the formulation of an error function based on three major points of the I-V characteristic curve, the application of analytical and metaheuristic methods to both single and double-diode models, and the demonstration of L-SHADE's effectiveness in providing multiple feasible solutions. The performance of L-SHADE is compared with other well-known optimization algorithms, underscoring its competitive capabilities for PV cell parameter estimation.
