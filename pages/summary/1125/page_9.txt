The paper discusses the calculation of errors at specific operational points for both single-diode and double-diode models in photovoltaic systems. The errors are formulated for the short circuit point and maximum power point using equations derived from established models. The optimization goal is to minimize the aggregate error, which is defined as the sum of the squared errors at these points.

The paper also outlines the parameters for the L-SHADE algorithm, which is used for optimization. Key inputs include the dimension of the optimization problem, initial population size, and maximum fitness evaluations. The algorithm requires specific decision variable ranges for both diode models.

The L-SHADE algorithm's process is detailed through a series of steps, beginning with initialization, where input values and population vectors are defined. The algorithm then enters a loop where it evaluates the fitness function, adapts control parameters, performs mutation and crossover, and selects the best individuals for the next generation. This iterative process continues until the termination criteria based on fitness evaluations are met. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the systematic approach to optimizing photovoltaic models through error minimization and the structured implementation of the L-SHADE algorithm.
