The data presented in Table 7 summarizes the performance of various algorithms—Genetic Algorithm (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Multi-Search Algorithm (MSA), Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO), and L-SHADE—applied to the polycrystalline KC200GT solar cell using both single-diode and double-diode models. Key findings indicate that GA, PSO, MSA, and L-SHADE achieved zero error across all trial runs, while the worst error values for Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) and GWO algorithms were approximately 10^-11, which can be practically considered as zero.

The analysis of optimal cell parameters across 30 trial runs revealed that the targeted performance at short circuit, open circuit, and maximum power points can be accomplished using different configurations of cell parameters, as illustrated in a 3D scatter plot. The total CPU time required for executing the algorithms showed that GWO was the fastest, followed by L-SHADE and PSO. However, GWO and PSO produced more concentrated solutions, indicating limited exploration capabilities compared to other algorithms.

The study emphasizes that relying solely on datasheet information from three major points of a solar cell is insufficient for accurately determining its unique I-V characteristics, rather than claiming superiority among the algorithms.
