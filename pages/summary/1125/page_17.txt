This paper discusses the parameter estimation of photovoltaic (PV) cells using selected evolutionary algorithms, leveraging datasheet information at three significant points on the current-voltage (I-V) characteristic curve: open circuit, short circuit, and maximum power points. The study conducts parameter optimizations using both single-diode and double-diode models for various types of PV cells, providing detailed circuit analyses for each model. 

The L-SHADE algorithm is explored in depth, while results from other algorithms such as Genetic Algorithm (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Artificial Bee Colony (ABC), Multi-Strategy Algorithm (MSA), and Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO) are summarized. The findings indicate that relying solely on these three major points does not yield a unique I-V characteristic, as multiple sets of optimal parameters can satisfy the I-V relationships at these points. Consequently, designers can select optimal parameters from a pool of solutions, emphasizing the need for experimental data at additional points to achieve a unique and accurate I-V characteristic for PV cells. 

The study's conclusions, derived under Standard Test Conditions (STC), are applicable across varying temperatures and irradiance levels, where multiple optimal parameter sets can still conform to the established I-V relationships. The appendix includes a table of optimal parameters for the single-diode model of the Shell SQ85 monocrystalline PV cell, detailing various runs with optimized and calculated variables alongside error measurements.
