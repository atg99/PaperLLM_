This paper focuses on the application of an adaptive differential evolution algorithm, specifically the Linear Population Size Reduction based Success History Adaptive Differential Evolution (L-SHADE) algorithm, to estimate the parameters of solar cells using minimal data from manufacturer datasheets. 

The methodology involves modeling the solar cell using single-diode and double-diode configurations and solving the corresponding non-linear equations derived from the I-V characteristics at key points: open circuit, short circuit, and maximum power points. The L-SHADE algorithm optimizes the parameter estimation process by minimizing the error in the I-V relationships, achieving near-zero error rates. The results demonstrate that the algorithm can yield multiple feasible solutions for the PV module parameters, emphasizing its effectiveness in parameter estimation without requiring prior assumptions.

The introduction highlights the significance of solar energy as a renewable resource in light of fossil fuel depletion and environmental concerns. It outlines the necessity for accurate modeling of solar cells to maximize their efficiency and performance in practical applications. The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the parameter estimation process, showcasing the advantages of using the L-SHADE algorithm in comparison to other metaheuristic approaches.

In conclusion, the proposed method provides a robust framework for estimating solar cell parameters, facilitating better design and optimization of photovoltaic systems with limited data availability.
