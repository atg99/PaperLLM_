The paper presents a novel pruning scheme for convolutional neural networks (CNNs) that addresses inefficiencies encountered when running pruned networks on ASIC and FPGA accelerators. While CNNs excel in computer vision tasks, their large weight storage and computational demands hinder their deployment in embedded systems. Traditional pruning methods have primarily focused on the quantity of pruned weights without considering accelerator architecture, leading to issues such as internal buffer misalignments and load imbalances.

The proposed scheme ensures that an equal number of weights remain for each weight group that corresponds to simultaneously fetched activations. This architecture-aware pruning approach not only resolves the inefficiencies but also enhances accelerator performance, achieving a doubling of efficiency. Despite these constraints, the pruning ratio achieved is comparable to that of existing unconstrained methods across various networks, including AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet, and MobileNet, as well as already pruned slimmed networks.

Additionally, the paper highlights that this pruning technique can reduce the logic complexity of sparse accelerators while improving the efficiency of prior sparse architectures. The pruned models resulting from this research are made publicly accessible, indicating the contribution to the field of deep learning and neural network optimization.
