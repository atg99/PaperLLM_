The paper introduces an accelerator-aware pruning algorithm designed to optimize the distribution of non-zero weights in neural network architectures, specifically targeting accelerator efficiency. This approach focuses on two critical parameters: activation- and weight-fetching groups and the uniformity of non-zero weights across these groups. Unlike previous pruning methods that resulted in irregular weight distributions, the proposed scheme ensures a consistent number of non-zero weights, denoted as Nnon_zero, for all weight-fetching groups. This uniformity facilitates better alignment with accelerator architectures, addressing issues such as misalignment in sparse structures and load imbalance across processing elements (PEs).

The proposed pruning method reduces complexity in accelerator design, particularly in indexing and activation selection logic. By dividing weight-fetching groups into smaller pruning groups, the width of multiplexers (MUXs) for activation selection can be minimized, leading to simpler designs without sacrificing performance. Experimental results indicate that a pruning group size of Ngroup=16 with a 75% pruning ratio maintains CNN performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of the approach in enhancing both efficiency and architectural simplicity. Overall, the algorithm not only resolves inefficiencies of previous sparse accelerators but also contributes to reducing their complexity, making it beneficial across various architectures.
