The proposed pruning scheme aims to optimize the number of parameters (Npar) in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by reducing it from 256 to 64, aligning with a typical pruning ratio of 75%. This reduction eliminates the need for padding zeros, allowing for smaller activation buffers and more efficient square-like memory components. The indexing logic for non-zero weights is simplified through direct indexing, which is more efficient than relative indexing used in previous methods, as it avoids filler zeros and reduces weight storage waste.

The pruning process can be executed in several ways, including one-time pruning, where the least significant weights are pruned simultaneously followed by retraining, and incremental pruning, where weights are pruned one at a time with periodic retraining. Incremental pruning is favored when one-time pruning is insufficient, although it requires longer retraining periods.

The scheme is adaptable to various accelerator architectures, including those that use different weight-fetching methods. For instance, the proposed method can be tailored for the sparse MWSA structure of Cnvlutin, allowing it to exploit both activation and weight sparsity. Additionally, the pruning scheme is applicable to fully-connected layers, where weights can be grouped and pruned along row or column axes depending on the processing element (PE) structure used, either MWMA or MWSA.

Overall, the proposed pruning scheme enhances efficiency and adaptability across different architectures while maintaining effective weight management in CNNs.
