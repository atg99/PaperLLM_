The experimental results indicate that the pruning of convolutional layers maintains performance even under significant weight reduction, with up to 75% pruning achievable without substantial accuracy loss. The retraining process is critical for recovering any accuracy lost during pruning, particularly in deeper networks where the complex architecture aids in maintaining performance. The results also suggest that pruning can lead to slight improvements in accuracy, likely due to regularization effects. In contrast, achieving similar results in shallower networks like AlexNet proves more challenging, though effective retraining strategies can still restore accuracy.

Overall, the findings validate the effectiveness of the proposed pruning method in balancing model compression with performance retention, making it applicable to state-of-the-art CNNs.
