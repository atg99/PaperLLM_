The paper discusses a novel algorithm for pruning in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) that addresses critical parameters in accelerator designs, such as the number of multipliers and the width of the internal weight buffer. The proposed method applies pruning to weight groups aligned with activation groups, ensuring a fixed number of weights remain in each group. This alignment helps resolve misalignment issues, optimizing the use of internal buffers and multipliers. The algorithm also reduces data selection complexity and addresses load imbalance by evenly distributing non-zero weights.

Section II provides an overview of CNNs, detailing their structure, which typically includes multiple convolutional layers and fewer fully-connected layers. Convolutional layers perform most arithmetic operations, and the paper outlines their operational mechanics through a mathematical representation. 

In terms of pruning, the paper reviews existing methods that remove unimportant weights or nodes to minimize storage and operations. It highlights the evolution of pruning techniques, distinguishing between structured and unstructured pruning, and summarizes previous studies that report varying pruning ratios for different layers in networks like AlexNet and VGG16.

The proposed accelerator-aware pruning scheme, detailed in Section III, aims to enhance efficiency while maintaining the integrity of the network's performance. Experimental results supporting the effectiveness of this method are presented in Section IV, followed by concluding remarks in Section V.
