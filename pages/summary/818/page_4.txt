The paper discusses the architecture and efficiency of a sparse channel-axis-parallel convolutional neural network (CNN) accelerator, specifically focusing on the Cambricon-X architecture. It highlights the significance of non-zero weights in processing and how these weights interact with fetched activations. The architecture employs a sparse MWMA (Matrix-Weighted Matrix-Accumulated) structure within processing elements (PEs), where the number of multipliers (Nmul) is less than the number of parallel activations (Npar), thus optimizing performance by ignoring zero weights.

The selection of activations from a larger pool necessitates the use of multiplexers (MUXs), with a complex indexing module (IM) to manage this process. The paper notes that while previous unstructured pruning methods achieved high pruning ratios, they often resulted in a mismatch between the remaining non-zero weights and activations. This misalignment can hinder the efficiency of the MWMA architecture, as it requires simultaneous fetching of activations and weights.

To address this issue, Cambricon-X implements a padding-zero scheme, which, while helpful, introduces inefficiencies by wasting buffer space and requiring a larger number of MUXs and activation buffers. This results in increased chip area usage and potential load imbalance among PEs, as processing times can vary based on the number of non-zero weights in each activation-fetching group.

The paper identifies a gap in existing studies regarding pruning strategies that consider the distribution of non-zero weights across different layers, particularly convolutional layers. It contrasts previous approaches with the proposed scheme, which aims to optimize efficiency by aligning the pruning process with the accelerator architecture, thereby tackling issues of inefficiency and load imbalance while encompassing both convolutional and fully-connected layers.
