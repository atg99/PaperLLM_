The paper presents a study on the classification of renal cancer using fuzzy and neuro-fuzzy techniques. The dataset is divided into 75% for training and 25% for testing, with the training phase consisting of four epochs. The system's ability to accurately classify patients with renal cancer and non-renal cancer is validated, with the training error illustrated.

A comparative analysis of the developed models is conducted to determine which is more effective in classifying inputs, including unseen data. The accuracy of each model is measured by comparing the output of the system with the expected output provided by medical professionals. While no model achieves 100% accuracy, the performance is assessed through tests.

A total of fifty tests were performed on the fuzzy expert system, revealing that 48 out of 50 cases were classified correctly. The results are presented in confusion matrices for multiple folds, illustrating the classification effectiveness across different severity classes. For instance, the first fold shows that 15 cases of the minor class were accurately classified, while in the moderate class, 12 out of 13 were correctly identified, with one misclassified as serious. This detailed evaluation highlights the system's potential in aiding renal cancer diagnosis.
