The paper discusses the structure and functioning of a five-layer Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS), which is designed for recognizing renal cancer. 

Layer 1 involves nodes that adjust function parameters, producing outputs based on the membership functions of fuzzy sets A_i and B_i. Layer 2 consists of fixed nodes that calculate the firing strength of each rule through the multiplication of incoming signals. In Layer 3, the output is normalized by dividing the firing strength of each rule by the total firing strength of all rules, resulting in normalized firing strengths.

Layer 4 features adaptive nodes where the output is determined by the product of normalized firing strength and a linear combination of parameters associated with the nodes. Finally, Layer 5 has a single fixed node that aggregates all incoming signals to provide the system's final output.
