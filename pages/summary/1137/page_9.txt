The paper presents a hybrid neuro-fuzzy method for diagnosing renal cancer, integrating two established soft computing techniques. It details the formulation of mathematical functions representing the impact of smoking on health, with specific functions for acute, moderate, and chronic smoking behaviors. 

A knowledge base is established containing a set of rules formulated in an IF-THEN format, essential for the fuzzy inference system. The rules are aggregated through conjunction or disjunction, with the total number of rules calculated for two layers. For Layer 1, the total rules amount to 36, based on the membership functions of smoking, dialysis, occupational exposure, and genetic factors. Layer 2 generates 864 rules, considering variables such as haematuria, red blood cell count, flank pain, tumor size, Von Hippel-Lindau gene, high blood pressure, and trichloroethylene exposure.

The input variables feed into the system to classify the stages of renal cancer—minor, moderate, serious, and critical—demonstrating the system's applicability in medical diagnostics. The methodology for developing this intelligent system is outlined, emphasizing its potential in improving renal cancer diagnosis.
