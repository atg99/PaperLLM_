The paper discusses the development of an Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) for diagnosing renal cancer. The system utilizes seven input variables and generates 864 rules based on the combinations of these inputs, with Gaussian membership functions applied. The methodology for generating rules is automated during the training phase, ensuring comprehensive coverage of possible input combinations.

The training and testing phases are critical, involving a dataset of 200 cases collected from renal cancer experts. The dataset undergoes 4-fold cross-validation, where three parts are used for training and one for testing in each iteration. This process ensures that 75% of the data is allocated for training while 25% is reserved for testing, allowing for effective validation of the system's ability to classify renal cancer cases accurately. The training process consists of four epochs, aiming to enhance the model's classification performance for distinguishing between renal cancer and non-renal cancer patients.
