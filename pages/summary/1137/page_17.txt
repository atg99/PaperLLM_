The paper presents a comparison of two inference systems for renal cancer detection: a traditional fuzzy inference system (FIS) and an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS). The study utilizes k-fold cross-validation to evaluate the performance of these systems, with performance metrics including sensitivity, specificity, precision, and classification accuracy calculated for four folds.

From the results summarized in Table 25, the ANFIS demonstrates superior performance with an overall sensitivity of 97.61%, specificity of 98.27%, precision of 97.67%, and classification accuracy of 98%. In contrast, the FIS achieves lower metrics, with a classification accuracy of 96%, sensitivity of 95.44%, specificity of 96.48%, and precision of 95.45%. 

The paper cites previous work by Nikita et al., which reported performance metrics of 96% accuracy, 95.5% sensitivity, 96.1% specificity, and 95.8% precision for their FIS. The results indicate that the ANFIS outperforms the existing system, affirming its effectiveness in medical diagnostics for renal cancer.

A bar chart is utilized to visually represent the performance comparison between the two systems, confirming that the ANFIS is more accurate and precise in detecting renal cancer compared to the traditional FIS. Overall, the findings suggest that ANFIS is a more effective tool for renal cancer detection.
