The paper presents an intelligent medical diagnostic system for renal cancer using an Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS). The system's performance is evaluated through accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and precision, achieving results of 98%, 97.61%, 98.27%, and 97.67%, respectively. Comparisons with existing systems indicate that ANFIS outperforms traditional Fuzzy Inference Systems (FIS), as illustrated in a bar chart that visually compares the performance metrics of both models.

The proposed system is particularly beneficial for novice doctors, aiding in the detection and staging of renal cancer, and serves as a training tool in resource-limited areas. Unlike traditional fuzzy logic, which struggles with imprecise data and lacks adaptability, ANFIS integrates neural networks to overcome these limitations, allowing it to handle datasets with missing values.

Future research directions include expanding performance parameters and enhancing input variables for improved classification accuracy in renal cancer diagnosis. The implementation of the proposed system requires only MATLAB software.

The authors collectively contributed to various aspects of the research, and the work was supported by a grant from the Korean government, with no conflicts of interest declared.
