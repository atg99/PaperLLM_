This paper discusses the ongoing care needed for patients diagnosed with renal cancer, emphasizing the importance of regular check-ups to reduce the risk of recurrence. It highlights various techniques used for the detection and diagnosis of renal cancer, with a focus on image processing methods that help identify biomarkers and cancer stages based on kidney images. Input variables crucial for diagnosing renal cancer are identified, including haematuria, red blood cell count, flank pain, tumor size, Von Hippel-Lindau syndrome, high blood pressure, and exposure to trichloroethylene.

Additionally, the paper introduces artificial neural networks (ANNs) as a simulation of the human brain, capable of performing tasks in a parallel and distributed manner. ANNs are utilized in various fields, including medicine. The operational flow of an ANN involves inputting data into neurons, combining these inputs with weights and biases, and applying a summation function followed by an activation function to produce an output.
