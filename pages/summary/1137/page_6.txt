The paper outlines a proposed system for assessing renal cancer through a structured approach involving two layers of input parameters and corresponding output variables. 

In Layer 1, key input parameters include smoking status, dialysis status, levels of OE (unknown parameter), and GOH (unknown parameter). Each of these inputs is categorized by specific ranges, indicating acute, moderate, or chronic conditions for smoking, and similar categorizations for the other parameters.

Layer 2 expands the assessment with additional input variables, such as numerical ranges for an unspecified parameter, RBCC, FP, TS, VHLG, HBP, and TCEE. These inputs are classified as low, normal, medium, or high, contributing to a more detailed understanding of the patient's condition.

The output variables from these layers indicate the presence of renal cancer in Layer 1, while Layer 2 provides a classification of cancer stage, ranging from minor to critical.

Furthermore, the paper includes statistical parameters like mean, standard deviation, and frequency for both layers, enhancing the analysis of input and output variables. This structured approach aims to facilitate accurate diagnosis and staging of renal cancer.
