The section presents a comprehensive overview of related work in the domain of feature representation learning in computer vision, highlighting the importance of modeling invariances. Traditional feature representations, such as SIFT and HOG, as well as learned representations from Convolutional Networks (ConvNets), have long relied on invariance to factors like translations, scaling, and lighting changes through techniques like data augmentation. The authors introduce a novel approach that leverages invariance in self-supervised learning through pretext tasks, which allows for learning representations without relying on specific label distributions. 

Previous research has explored various methods for reconstructing images from intermediate representations, including sparse coding and autoencoders. Recent trends have focused on defining pretext tasks that capture specific data distribution properties, particularly in video data, where tasks include frame ordering and tracking. The paper emphasizes that its approach is distinct in learning representations that are invariant to image transformations, as opposed to merely covariant.

The Pretext-Invariant Representation Learning (PIRL) method is closely related to existing frameworks that utilize contrastive learning and mutual information maximization to achieve invariant representations. PIRL extends the notion of data augmentation to incorporate prior pretext tasks, thereby providing a fresh perspective on combining these tasks with contrastive learning. 

Additionally, the authors note that PIRL diverges from previous predictive learning approaches that focus on reconstructing missing data, instead prioritizing the learning of invariances. The empirical performance of PIRL is demonstrated through its state-of-the-art results on ImageNet classification tasks, indicating its effectiveness in achieving high accuracy with smaller model sizes. The methodology involves applying known image transformations to datasets and training a convolutional network to generate invariant representations through empirical risk minimization.
