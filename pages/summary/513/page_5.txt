The paper presents the performance of the PIRL (Pretext Invariant Representation Learning) model, highlighting its superiority over various self-supervised learning methods across multiple evaluation metrics. The results show that PIRL outperforms all alternative approaches in terms of average precision (AP) measures, achieving a notable 5-point improvement over the Jigsaw pretext task. This underscores the model's effectiveness in learning invariant image representations, which is crucial for robust performance in object detection tasks.

PIRL also surpasses the results of NPID++, demonstrating the advantages of pretext invariance. Remarkably, it outperforms supervised models pre-trained on ImageNet, particularly in conservative metrics like APall and AP75. The authors emphasize that PIRL utilizes the same backbone model and pre-training data without labels, achieving significant improvements compared to previous self-supervised methods that required larger curated datasets or models.

In evaluating the quality of learned representations, the authors trained linear classifiers on fixed representations across four diverse datasets. The results indicate that PIRL enhances recognition accuracy by over 15% compared to Jigsaw on ImageNet, achieving the highest single-crop top-1 accuracy among self-supervised learners using ResNet-50.

The paper further compares PIRL with other models, showing that it consistently outperforms its covariant counterpart (Jigsaw) and approaches fully supervised performance on datasets like Places205 and VOC07. The results on iNaturalist also demonstrate a significant gain. Overall, PIRL establishes itself as a leading self-supervised learning approach, excelling in both accuracy and efficiency while leveraging the same architecture and training conditions as its competitors.
