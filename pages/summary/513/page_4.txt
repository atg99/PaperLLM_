The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of the PIRL (Pretext-Invariant Representation Learning) method, particularly in conjunction with the Jigsaw pretext task. The authors utilize a ResNet-50 architecture to compute image representations, employing specific techniques to extract features from both original and transformed images. The representation for the original image is generated by applying average pooling and linear projection to the res5 features, resulting in a 128-dimensional vector. For the transformed image, they extract patches, compute their representations, concatenate them in random order, and apply a second linear projection to yield another 128-dimensional representation.

The experimental setup focuses on transfer learning across various datasets, particularly for object detection and image classification. The authors evaluate PIRL against several baselines, including the Jigsaw model and NPID, a variant of PIRL, to analyze the impact of pretext-invariance. The experiments reveal that PIRL outperforms these methods, achieving better detection metrics on the Pascal VOC dataset when pre-trained with self-supervised learning techniques. The study underscores the importance of hyperparameters in transfer learning, detailing the training process, data sources, and evaluation metrics used.

Overall, PIRL demonstrates significant improvements in performance without requiring additional pre-training data or modifications to the network architecture, highlighting its effectiveness in self-supervised learning contexts.
