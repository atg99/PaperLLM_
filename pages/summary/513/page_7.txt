The paper investigates the effectiveness of the PIRL framework in learning invariant representations of images, specifically regarding its capabilities against various transformations. The authors conducted an analysis by normalizing representations and calculating L2 distances between original and transformed images. Results indicated that PIRL effectively learns invariant representations, as evidenced by the similarity in distances between original and transformed images. In contrast, Jigsaw representations exhibited greater variability, suggesting a dependency on image transformations.

Further analysis explored the quality of representations across different layers of the convolutional network. While Jigsaw representations improved from the conv1 to res4 layers, their quality diminished at the res5 layer due to covariation with image transformations. Conversely, PIRL representations maintained their invariance in the res5 layer, allowing for a stronger focus on semantic information, thus indicating that res5 is optimal for PIRL representations.

The study also introduced a multi-task baseline that combines NPID and Jigsaw classification to assess the importance of learning invariance to the Jigsaw task. This baseline performed similarly or slightly worse than NPID++, underscoring the significance of Jigsaw invariance for enhanced representation quality.

Additionally, the paper examined the impact of the hyperparameter λ in the PIRL loss function, which balances two NCE losses. The results showed that the quality of PIRL representations is sensitive to λ, with the optimal performance achieved at λ=0.5. Finally, the authors analyzed the effect of varying the number of image transformations, highlighting that both PIRL and Jigsaw performance is influenced by the number of transformations applied.
