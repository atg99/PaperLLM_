The paper "Self-Supervised Learning of Pretext-Invariant Representations" by Ishan Misra and Laurens van der Maaten presents a novel approach to self-supervised learning aimed at creating semantically meaningful image representations without requiring semantic annotations. The authors argue that many existing pretext tasks result in representations that are covariant with image transformations, which can hinder their effectiveness in semantic recognition tasks. To address this, they introduce Pretext-Invariant Representation Learning (PIRL), which focuses on learning invariant representations.

PIRL modifies a common pretext task—solving jigsaw puzzles—to encourage the construction of representations that remain similar for transformed versions of the same image while differing from representations of other images. This approach allows for the retention of semantic information, essential for effective image recognition. The results of the paper demonstrate that PIRL significantly enhances the semantic quality of learned representations, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmarks for self-supervised learning. Notably, PIRL surpasses supervised pre-training in object detection tasks, highlighting its potential for developing robust self-supervised representations that exhibit good invariance properties.
