The paper investigates Pretext-Invariant Representation Learning (PIRL) as a method for generating representations that maintain semantic information despite image transformations used in self-supervised learning tasks. The study shows that PIRL can utilize a large number of permutations in image transformations, specifically achieving significant improvements over the Jigsaw task. The results indicate that increasing the number of negative samples during pre-training positively influences the quality of learned image representations, as evidenced by higher Top-1 accuracy in linear classifiers on ImageNet.

The authors also explore the flexibility of PIRL in conjunction with various pretext tasks, such as the Rotation task, demonstrating that PIRL consistently outperforms existing methods. Combining transformations from both Jigsaw and Rotation tasks further enhances representation quality. The findings suggest that PIRL achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmarks, even surpassing supervised pre-training methods in object detection tasks.

In conclusion, PIRL shows promise for advancing self-supervised learning by leveraging invariance to image transformations while maintaining high performance. Future work will focus on expanding transformation sets and integrating PIRL with clustering-based approaches to potentially enhance image representations even further.
