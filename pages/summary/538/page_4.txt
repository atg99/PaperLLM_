The paper presents Autoregressive Predictive Coding (APC) as a method for unsupervised speech representation learning using a deep LSTM network. The model is trained in an autoregressive manner with a time-shifting factor to encourage the extraction of general structures in speech signals rather than local features. 

In the phone classification task, the paper compares various methods, including Mel features and different configurations of APC with varying depths. The results indicate that the Phone Error Rate (PER) generally decreases with the number of steps to the target until reaching a threshold, after which it begins to increase. 

For speaker verification, APC representations are compared to i-vectors and CPC models. The findings show that APCs outperform both i-vectors and CPC when the number of steps is less than 10, suggesting that APCs capture both phonetic and speaker information effectively. Notably, deeper APCs do not consistently improve performance in speaker verification, with lower layers providing more accessible speaker information.

The discussion emphasizes the potential of APC for transfer learning in speech and audio domains, highlighting its effectiveness in extracting both phonetic and speaker information. The authors suggest future work could involve training APCs on larger, noisier datasets and exploring the combination of representations from different layers to enhance performance in downstream tasks. They also stress the importance of understanding how internal representations transform across layers to improve model interpretability.
