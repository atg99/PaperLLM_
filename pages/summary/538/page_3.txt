This paper investigates the performance of Auto-regressive Predictive Coding (APC) and Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC) models in the context of phone classification, particularly focusing on their ability to capture phonetic content. The authors utilize a multi-layer unidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network with residual connections for APC modeling, while adapting CPC implementations to ensure a fair comparison.

The study employs a set of hyperparameters without tuning them based on downstream tasks, aiming to evaluate their impact on the learned speech representations. The accessibility of categorical information is defined as the linear separability among classes, measured through phone error rates (PER) using linear classifiers. For speaker verification, a Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) model is trained on the speech representations.

Results indicate that the APC model significantly outperforms all CPC variants in phone classification, suggesting that APCs provide more accessible phonetic content despite their simpler architecture. Notably, CPC models that draw negative samples from the same utterance (cpc-n9same) perform better than those drawing from different utterances (cpc-n9all), highlighting the importance of the source of negative samples in learning phonetic features.

Further analysis shows that deeper APC models yield improved representations, particularly for smaller prediction steps. Additionally, the incorporation of non-linear transformations in classifiers enhances performance, yet APC models still maintain a notable advantage over transformed surface features. Overall, the findings emphasize the effectiveness of APC in extracting phonetic information compared to CPC, paving the way for future exploration of deeper architectures and training strategies.
