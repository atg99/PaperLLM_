This paper discusses the modeling of token sequences in language and acoustic feature prediction using two main approaches: Acoustic Predictor Coding (APC) and Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC). The probability of a token sequence is modeled as the product of conditional probabilities given the preceding tokens. The training process minimizes the negative log-likelihood, optimizing parameters associated with a look-up table, a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), and a Softmax layer for language modeling. In the context of speech data, APC directly feeds frames into the RNN without a look-up table, replacing the Softmax layer with a regression layer to predict future frames based on local signal smoothness.

APC aims to predict a future frame n steps ahead, optimizing the model by minimizing the L1 loss between the actual and predicted sequences. In contrast, CPC focuses on learning representations that distinguish a target future frame from randomly sampled negative frames, utilizing a frame encoder, a context encoder, and a scoring function. The model minimizes a contrastive loss to estimate the density ratio between the target frame and negative samples.

The paper also presents experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of the APC representations in capturing phone and speaker information. Using the LibriSpeech corpus for training, the study assesses the robustness of the learned features through phone classification and speaker verification on different datasets, including Wall Street Journal and TIMIT. The goal is to determine how well the representations generalize across domains and retain critical phonetic and speaker characteristics.
