The paper discusses the trade-off between expressiveness and simplicity in the choice of the distribution q(z) for variational autoencoders (VAEs). A Dirac distribution on latent points would lead to high-quality reconstructions but poor generalization. To address this, the authors compare a full covariance multivariate Gaussian with a 10-component Gaussian mixture model (GMM) in their experiments.

In the related works section, the authors highlight various studies that have sought to diagnose and improve the VAE framework, including attempts to augment it for better optimization. They argue that simpler deterministic frameworks, such as deterministic denoising and contractive autoencoders, can perform competitively in generative modeling. However, these earlier models faced challenges like convergence issues and limited scalability, leading to their replacement by VAEs.

The paper also discusses methods to address the aggregated posterior mismatch in VAEs, noting that these often require more complex forms for the prior p(z), which increases computational demands. The authors critique the use of additional VAEs to estimate latent space, as it reintroduces optimization challenges and is less efficient than fitting a simple q(z).

Furthermore, the paper examines adversarial autoencoders (AAEs), which incorporate a discriminator into the encoder-decoder framework, resulting in sharper samples but higher computational costs and training instabilities. Wasserstein autoencoders (WAEs) are introduced as a generalization of AAEs, framing autoencoding as an optimal transport problem, and can be trained using various loss functions.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of simpler deterministic models in generative tasks while addressing the limitations and complexities associated with traditional VAE approaches.
