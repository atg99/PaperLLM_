This paper, presented at ICLR 2020, explores the performance of various generative models, specifically focusing on the GRAE (Generative Relational Autoencoder) framework in comparison to other models like GCVVAE, GVAE, and CVAE. The study evaluates these models based on their ability to generate structured objects, such as mathematical expressions and molecular structures.

Key findings indicate that GRAE achieves the highest average scores for generating valid samples of both expressions and molecules, outperforming other models, particularly in terms of syntactically valid molecules where GRAE's success rate is significantly higher than that of GVAE. The results show that while GCVVAE performs well for simpler tasks like generating equations, GRAE excels in more complex tasks involving molecular structures.

The paper argues that the traditional Variational Autoencoder (VAE) framework has limitations that can be addressed by reframing sampling as noise injection to enhance smoothness. This approach allows for the development of a deterministic autoencoding framework that better captures the latent space, resulting in improved sample quality. The authors highlight the advantages of their method in generating comparable or superior samples while mitigating issues associated with stochastic frameworks.

In conclusion, this research contributes to the understanding of generative modeling by demonstrating that a deterministic approach can effectively bridge the gap between theory and practice, leading to enhanced performance in generating structured objects.
