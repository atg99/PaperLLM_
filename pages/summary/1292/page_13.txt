The appendix discusses the trade-off between reconstruction quality and random sample quality in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) when trained on the MNIST dataset. It highlights that using more Monte Carlo samples during training (k > 1) improves sample quality, as shown by the FID metric, but also increases memory and computation requirements. Consequently, the common practice is to use a single sample (k = 1), which limits performance.

The right side of Figure 3 illustrates the balance between reconstruction loss (LREC) and Kullback-Leibler divergence (LKL). Increasing the weight of LKL enhances random sample quality but degrades reconstruction accuracy. This indicates that imposing structure in the latent space results in a trade-off that negatively affects sample quality.

The appendix further proposes an alternative perspective on enforcing smoothness in the decoder's output by augmenting the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) optimization problem. It introduces a constraint that limits the variation of the decoder output for different encodings, maintaining Gaussianity assumptions for convenience. This leads to a variation of the Regularized Autoencoder (RAE) framework, where the smoothness of the decoder's output is controlled by the chosen form of the posterior distribution. The analysis suggests that the choice of a parametric form for the posterior can significantly influence training outcomes, particularly when considering the isotropic nature of the distribution.
