The paper discusses various regularization schemes for Regularized Autoencoders (RAEs), highlighting Tikhonov regularization as a foundational method related to low-magnitude input noise. The authors explore several alternatives, including instance noise from Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which enhances performance and maintains Lipschitz continuity through gradient penalties and spectral normalization. They question the necessity of explicit regularization in deep networks, given recent successes without it, and propose a baseline framework that omits regularization terms. The authors also derive their RAE framework from first principles by augmenting the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) optimization problem of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), establishing a link between gradient bounding and spectral norm regularization.

In addressing the limitations of VAEs, particularly the detachment from a prior distribution over the latent space, the authors propose ex-post density estimation to fit a density estimator to the latent space. This approach aims to resolve the issues of distribution mismatches while remaining computationally efficient and applicable to various VAE variants. Overall, the paper provides insights into regularization techniques and their implications for latent space modeling in autoencoder frameworks.
