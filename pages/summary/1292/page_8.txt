This paper presents a thorough evaluation of the Regularized Autoencoder (RAE) framework, particularly in the context of generative modeling, and compares it against Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Wasserstein Autoencoders (WAEs). The authors emphasize the importance of assessing generative models through metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and precision-recall scores, revealing that RAEs consistently achieve competitive performance across various datasets. Notably, sampling RAEs exhibit the lowest FIDs when utilizing a modest 10-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for density estimation. The results indicate that RAEs can rival state-of-the-art VAE performance and even challenge Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) despite limited hyperparameter tuning.

The paper also explores the effectiveness of different regularization techniques within RAEs, finding no single method outperformed others consistently. Interestingly, the authors note that RAEs, even without a fixed latent space structure, maintain impressive interpolation quality, as demonstrated through qualitative evaluations. The findings highlight the benefits of ex-post density estimation, which significantly improves sample quality.

Furthermore, the paper investigates the application of RAEs to structured inputs like molecules and arithmetic expressions, utilizing the Grammar VAE architecture for comparison. The Grammar RAEs (GRAEs) outperform both Character VAEs and Grammar VAEs in generating valid samples that adhere to specific structural rules, as evidenced by Bayesian optimization results.

In conclusion, the study strongly supports the potential of the RAE framework as a formidable alternative to VAEs and other generative models, particularly in terms of sample quality and structural generation capabilities.
