The paper discusses the limitations of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) in generative modeling, highlighting the unsatisfactory balance between sample quality and reconstruction quality. Previous research has attributed these issues to simplistic prior distributions and over-regularization from the KL divergence term in the VAE objective. Additionally, the VAE framework allows for trivial solutions that can lead to posterior collapse when using powerful decoders. Training VAEs also involves approximating expectations through sampling, which can increase gradient variance, necessitating careful tuning of hyperparameters. Furthermore, the learned posterior distributions often do not align with the assumed latent priors, negatively impacting sample quality.

In contrast, this work proposes a deterministic alternative called Regularized Autoencoders (RAEs), which aims to simplify the generative modeling process while still achieving a meaningful latent space. The authors argue that training a stochastic encoder-decoder pair in VAEs can be effectively replaced by a deterministic architecture with noise added to the decoder’s input. They explore various regularization methods to replace this noise injection and analyze its impact on performance.

The main contributions of the paper are: introducing the RAE framework as a viable alternative to VAEs, proposing an ex-post density estimation method that enhances sample quality for various generative models without retraining, and conducting a thorough empirical evaluation comparing RAEs to VAEs and other baseline models across standard image datasets and more complex domains like molecule generation. The findings suggest that RAEs can produce equally good or better samples than VAEs and Wasserstein Autoencoders (WAEs), thus providing a simpler and more efficient approach to generative modeling.
