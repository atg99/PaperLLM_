This paper presents a novel approach to modeling latent spaces in the framework of Regularized Autoencoders (RAEs) by utilizing ex-post density estimation, diverging from traditional methods that may constrain the ability to fit high-dimensional data. The authors compare RAEs with Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and their variants, particularly VQ-VAEs, which, despite their name, are deterministic autoencoders requiring complex discrete density estimators. The paper also contrasts RAEs with GLO, noting that RAEs incorporate deterministic encoders alongside decoders, akin to GANs.

The experiments aim to explore three key questions: the comparability of sample quality and latent space structure between RAEs and VAEs, the impact of different regularizations on RAE performance, and the effects of ex-post density estimation on VAEs. Results from experiments conducted on datasets such as MNIST, CIFAR, and CelebA indicate that RAE models demonstrate competitive performance against existing models, with specific regularization techniques (RAE-GP, RAE-L2, RAE-SN) yielding favorable results in terms of sample reconstruction quality, random sample generation, and interpolation quality.

The evaluation metrics, including FID scores, reveal that RAEs either match or exceed the performance of established models across various tasks while maintaining the ability to interpolate effectively despite the absence of explicit priors on the latent space. The findings suggest that RAEs, with their innovative regularization strategies, present a promising direction for further research in latent variable modeling.
