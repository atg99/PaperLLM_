The paper introduces Constant-Variance Variational Autoencoders (CV-V AEs) as a simplified variant of traditional Variational Autoencoders (V AEs) by fixing the variance of the latent distribution across different data points. This modification streamlines the computation of the expected value and simplifies the Kullback-Leibler (KL) loss term, facilitating easier implementation. Despite their application in fields like variational image compression and adversarial robustness, systematic studies on CV-V AEs are limited, which the authors aim to address.

The paper also explores Deterministic Regularized Autoencoders (RAEs), which derive from the concept of V AEs but eliminate noise injection in favor of explicit regularization for the decoder. By transitioning from a probabilistic framework to a deterministic one, the authors argue that RAEs can maintain effective regularization while simplifying the training process. The loss function for RAEs incorporates reconstruction loss, latent space size constraints, and an explicit regularization term, enabling the model to achieve better image quality without the need for Monte Carlo approximations.

Ultimately, while RAEs forego the generative sampling mechanism typical of V AEs, the authors propose a method for regaining this capability through post-hoc density estimation, addressing potential issues with posterior mismatch. This research contributes to a deeper understanding of regularization techniques in neural networks, particularly in the context of generative models.
