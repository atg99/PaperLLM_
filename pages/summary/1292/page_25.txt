The paper presents a comparison of various regularization schemes for Regularized Autoencoders (RAE) across different datasets, including MNIST, CIFAR, and CelebA. The results, summarized in Table 3, indicate that while the different regularization methods yield improvements in reconstruction, random sample quality, and interpolated test samples, the enhancements are marginal. This limited improvement is attributed to the challenges of hyperparameter tuning associated with additional regularization losses.

The authors highlight that Autoencoders (AE) without explicit decoder regularization still perform reasonably well, suggesting that convolutional neural networks may possess some implicit regularization when paired with gradient-based optimizers. This observation leads them to explore combinations of regularizations, specifically focusing on regularizing the decoder while omitting regularization in the latent space, as illustrated by the AE-L2 row in Table 3.

Furthermore, the paper references recent findings in GAN literature that indicate combining multiple regularizations can enhance neural network performance. Despite this, the authors caution that such combinations significantly increase the number of hyperparameters, complicating model training and contradicting the study's aim for simplicity. Their experiments with tuning these hyperparameters did not yield the expected performance boosts, as summarized in the latter part of Table 3.
