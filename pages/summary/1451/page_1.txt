This paper introduces the Unified Pre-trained Language Model (UNILM), designed for both natural language understanding (NLU) and natural language generation (NLG) tasks. UNILM is pre-trained using three types of language modeling tasks: unidirectional, bidirectional, and sequence-to-sequence prediction. It employs a shared Transformer network with specific self-attention masks to manage context for predictions.

UNILM demonstrates superior performance compared to BERT on the GLUE benchmark and outperforms it on question answering tasks like SQuAD 2.0 and CoQA. The model achieves state-of-the-art results on five NLG datasets, including significant improvements in ROUGE-L scores for CNN/DailyMail and Gigaword summarization, an F1 score in CoQA, BLEU-4 in SQuAD question generation, and NIST-4 in DSTC7 document-grounded dialog response generation.

The paper underscores the effectiveness of pre-trained language models in enhancing various NLP tasks and highlights UNILM's unique capability to bridge the gap between NLU and NLG through its unified architecture. The code and pre-trained models are accessible at the provided GitHub link.
