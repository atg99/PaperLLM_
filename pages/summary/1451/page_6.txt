This paper presents an evaluation of various summarization models on the CNN/DailyMail and Gigaword datasets, focusing on both extractive and abstractive summarization methods. The results indicate that the UNILM model achieves superior performance across the board, outperforming other state-of-the-art models. In particular, on the CNN/DailyMail dataset, UNILM surpasses the best extractive model by 0.88 points in ROUGE-L, establishing a new benchmark for abstractive summarization.

The paper details the training process for UNILM, which involves fine-tuning on a non-anonymized version of the datasets for 30 epochs, employing specific hyperparameters such as a masking probability of 0.7 and label smoothing. The evaluation metrics used are the F1 version of ROUGE, and the models are compared based on their performance on different scales of training data. For Gigaword, UNILM shows significant improvement, particularly in low-resource settings with only 10,000 training examples, outperforming the MASS model by 7.08 points in ROUGE-L.

Additionally, the paper touches upon question answering (QA) tasks, distinguishing between extractive and generative QA. Extractive QA is framed as a natural language understanding task, where the model predicts the start and end positions of answer spans within a passage. Overall, the findings underscore UNILM's effectiveness in both summarization and question answering tasks, highlighting its adaptability and robustness in various settings.
