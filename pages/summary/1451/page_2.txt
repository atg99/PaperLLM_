The paper discusses a new model called U NILM, which integrates multiple language modeling objectives into a unified framework using a shared Transformer architecture. This model is designed to enhance the capabilities of both natural language understanding (NLU) and natural language generation (NLG) tasks.

Key comparisons among different language models (LMs) are presented, highlighting the various pre-training objectives such as left-to-right, right-to-left, and bidirectional language modeling. U NILM is characterized by its ability to utilize these different contexts for predicting masked words, thus allowing it to be fine-tuned for a range of tasks.

The advantages of U NILM are threefold: it consolidates multiple LMs into a single model, reducing the need for separate training; it promotes generalization through shared parameters optimized across different tasks; and it facilitates applications in both NLU and NLG, making it suitable for tasks like abstractive summarization and question generation.

Experimental results indicate that U NILM performs competitively with BERT on the GLUE benchmark and excels in extractive question answering tasks. Furthermore, it sets new state-of-the-art benchmarks in several NLG tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness as both a bidirectional encoder and a sequence-to-sequence model. The model's architecture allows for flexible context access through self-attention masks, enabling it to adaptively control the information utilized during training and inference.
