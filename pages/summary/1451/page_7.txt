This paper presents a comparative analysis of various models for extractive and generative question answering tasks using the Stanford Question Answering Dataset (SQuAD) 2.0 and the Conversational Question Answering (CoQA) datasets. 

In the extractive QA domain, results show that UNILM consistently outperforms BERT LARGE and RMR+ELMo across both datasets. For SQuAD 2.0, UNILM achieves an Exact Match (EM) score of 80.5 and an F1 score of 83.4, surpassing BERT LARGE's scores of 78.9 and 81.8. Similarly, on the CoQA dataset, UNILM scores 84.9 in F1, while BERT LARGE scores 82.7.

The paper also highlights the unique characteristics of CoQA, which includes conversational context and free-form answers. The authors modify their models to incorporate conversational history and effectively handle yes/no questions.

For generative QA, the authors adapt UNILM into a sequence-to-sequence model, achieving significant improvements over previous generative methods like Seq2Seq and PGNet. UNILM's performance demonstrates a notable reduction in the gap between generative and extractive methods.

Additionally, the paper explores answer-aware question generation using the SQuAD 1.1 dataset, aiming to generate questions based on given answer spans. The comprehensive experimentation and results indicate UNILM's effectiveness across various QA tasks, solidifying its role as a robust model in both extractive and generative frameworks.
