The paper discusses the design and implementation of a sequence-to-sequence language model (LM), referred to as U NILM, which integrates a bidirectional encoder and a unidirectional decoder. This model is pre-trained for various conditional text generation tasks, notably abstractive summarization. The training process includes a next sentence prediction task to enhance the bidirectional LM's capabilities.

The overall training objective combines several LM objectives: one-third of the time is dedicated to the bidirectional LM, another third to the sequence-to-sequence LM, and the remaining sixths to both left-to-right and right-to-left LM objectives. U NILM is built on the architecture of BERT LARGE, employing a 24-layer Transformer with a hidden size of 1,024 and 16 attention heads, comprising approximately 340 million parameters. Pre-training utilizes datasets such as English Wikipedia and BookCorpus, with a vocabulary size of 28,996 and a maximum input sequence length of 512 tokens. The model employs a token masking strategy, with a 15% masking probability.

For optimization, Adam is used with specific hyperparameters, and the pre-training process spans about 770,000 steps, utilizing substantial GPU resources. 

Fine-tuning for downstream natural language understanding (NLU) tasks involves using U NILM as a bidirectional Transformer encoder, akin to BERT, where the model's encoding vector serves as input to a softmax classifier. In contrast, for natural language generation (NLG) tasks, the model is fine-tuned to recover masked tokens in target sequences, maximizing the likelihood of these tokens given their context.

The experiments conducted cover both NLU tasks, such as those in the GLUE benchmark and extractive question answering, and NLG tasks like abstractive summarization, question generation, generative question answering, and dialog response generation, with a focus on producing concise summaries from input texts.
