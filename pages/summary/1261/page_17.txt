This paper highlights several preprocessing strategies essential for preparing datasets, including test-train splitting, random oversampling, feature selection, k-fold validation, and handling missing values. These techniques aim to improve data quality, address class imbalance, and facilitate effective modeling.

The review identifies stacking as the most effective ensemble method, achieving a performance accuracy of 82.6% in the studies analyzed. Notably, stacking demonstrated perfect prediction rates for liver and diabetes diseases. Despite the frequent use of bagging and boosting, stacking and voting outperformed these methods, with bagging and boosting showing optimal performance in only a minority of cases.

Concerns regarding data leakage in ensemble methods were addressed through various safeguards, such as appropriate cross-validation techniques and feature engineering precautions. These measures are crucial for maintaining the integrity and reliability of predictive results.

The study acknowledges limitations, including a focus on literature from 2016 to 2023 regarding five chronic diseases, the exclusion of other ensemble techniques, and potential biases due to geographic focus. Future research could expand on these areas to enhance the generalizability of findings.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes the ongoing advancements in machine learning and artificial intelligence for early disease prediction, with ensemble methods playing a critical role in improving accuracy and reliability in healthcare analytics.
