The paper "Ensemble Learning for Disease Prediction: A Review" by Mahajan et al. explores the application of ensemble learning techniques in enhancing disease prediction accuracy. Ensemble learning combines multiple classifiers to achieve better performance than individual classifiers. The study focuses on identifying trends in the effectiveness of various ensemble methods—bagging, boosting, stacking, and voting—against five well-researched diseases: diabetes, skin disease, kidney disease, liver disease, and heart conditions.

The authors conducted a systematic literature review, identifying 45 relevant studies published between 2016 and 2023 that utilized at least two of the four ensemble methods. Their findings indicate that stacking, despite being used less frequently (23 times), achieved the highest accuracy in predictions (19 occurrences). Voting emerged as the second most effective method. Stacking consistently outperformed other methods in predicting skin disease and diabetes, while bagging excelled in kidney disease predictions, and boosting performed best for liver and diabetes predictions.

The results highlight the varying performance of ensemble techniques across different diseases, suggesting that stacking generally provides superior accuracy. This review provides insights into current trends in disease prediction models employing ensemble learning, assisting researchers in selecting appropriate models for predictive analytics in healthcare. The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding how different ensemble methods perform against commonly used disease datasets.
