This paper evaluates the effectiveness of ensemble approaches in disease prediction through a comprehensive literature review of 45 studies. The main ensemble methods examined include bagging, boosting, stacking, and voting, which are noted for their ability to reduce bias and variance, thereby enhancing prediction accuracy. The findings indicate that stacking has been utilized in half of the studies and achieved the highest performance accuracy at 82.6%. In contrast, bagging, despite being the most frequently used, yielded the lowest accuracy at 26.8%, while boosting demonstrated better results at 40.5%. The research serves as a guide for scholars considering ensemble methods for their projects and aids in identifying the most effective strategies for disease prediction. The paper asserts that the use of ensemble techniques can significantly improve the performance of base classifiers in this domain.
