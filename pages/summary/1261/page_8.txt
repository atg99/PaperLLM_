The paper discusses various studies that utilize ensemble machine learning approaches to enhance the prediction accuracy of several diseases, including heart disease, chronic kidney disease (CKD), skin diseases, liver disease, and diabetes. It highlights that ensemble methods outperform individual classifiers in predicting the risk of heart disease and CKD, achieving remarkable accuracy rates. For instance, Basar and Akan achieved 100% prediction accuracy for CKD using an ensemble random subspace approach with base learners like k-nearest neighbors and decision trees. Similarly, Verma et al. reported 99.67% accuracy for skin disease prediction through various ensemble techniques.

The paper also emphasizes the application of machine learning techniques for predicting liver disease, where Singh et al. achieved accuracies of 88.55% with random forest and other algorithms. In diabetes prediction, Abdollahi and Nouri-Moghaddam utilized a stacked generalization technique to reach 98.8% accuracy, while Kumari et al. reported significant performance metrics using a combination of machine learning algorithms.

The study reviews a total of 45 publications, summarizing the diseases, references, and the ensemble algorithms employed. It aims to compare different ensemble strategies such as bagging, boosting, stacking, and voting to identify the most effective framework for improving prediction accuracy in complex disease conditions. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of integrating various machine learning models to enhance diagnostic capabilities across different health conditions.
