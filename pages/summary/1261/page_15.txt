The analysis of various ensemble approaches for chronic disease prediction reveals that stacking consistently outperforms other methods, achieving the highest accuracy rates across multiple disease categories. Specifically, stacking demonstrated the best performance in heart disease, skin cancer, liver disease, and diabetes, with accuracy rates reaching up to 100%. In contrast, bagging yielded the lowest accuracy, with only 26.8% of cases showing the best performance. Boosting and voting also showed moderate success, but stacking's ability to combine predictions from diverse models and capture complex relationships positions it as the most effective strategy for chronic disease prediction. Overall, the findings highlight the importance of selecting the right ensemble method based on the specific disease and dataset characteristics to optimize predictive performance.
