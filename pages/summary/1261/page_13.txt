The table summarizes various machine learning models used for clinical data analysis, specifically focusing on diabetes prediction using the Pima Indians Diabetes dataset. Different base learners such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) are combined with ensemble methods like bagging, boosting, and stacking to improve prediction accuracy. 

Key findings include:

- A study utilized SVM with stacking and majority voting, achieving an accuracy of 79% for stacking and 65.1% for voting.
- Another approach combined multiple algorithms (ANN, SVM, KNN, Naive Bayes) with bagging and voting, resulting in an impressive voting accuracy of 98.6%.
- A method employing KNN, Random Forest (RF), Decision Trees (DT), and others with feature selection via genetic algorithms reached a stacking accuracy of 98.8%.
- Lastly, a model using DT, KNN, SVM with various preprocessing techniques achieved a best accuracy of 90.1% through boosting.

Overall, stacking and voting emerged as effective ensemble strategies, with stacking consistently yielding the highest accuracies across different studies.
