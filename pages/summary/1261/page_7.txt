The study investigates the use of ensemble machine learning methods for disease prediction and diagnosis, focusing on heart, skin, liver, kidney, and diabetes disorders. Initially, 1046 articles were identified using specific keywords related to disease prediction and diagnosis with ensemble methods. After eliminating duplicates and filtering for English-language publications, 766 articles remained. A further selection process, which required the use of at least two of the four ensemble techniques—bagging, boosting, stacking, or voting—narrowed the articles down to 45.

The results indicate that ensemble techniques enhance the prediction accuracy of weak classifiers, particularly in heart disease risk prediction. Majority voting was found to significantly improve accuracy, with one study achieving a heart disease risk prediction accuracy of 98.18% and majority voting yielding an even higher accuracy of 98.38%. The data collection process followed the PRISM guideline, illustrating the systematic approach taken in selecting relevant literature for this review.
