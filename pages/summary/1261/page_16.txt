The paper explores the performance of various ensemble machine learning methods—bagging, boosting, stacking, and voting—in the context of disease prediction. It highlights that while these methods enhance prediction accuracy by reducing bias and variance, their interpretability varies. Bagging provides high accuracy but lacks insight into feature relationships, boosting complicates models by emphasizing misclassified samples, and stacking combines multiple models using a metalearner, making it harder to interpret. Voting balances accuracy and interpretability through majority or weighted voting.

The review of 45 articles revealed the use of 23 distinct datasets from sources like Kaggle and UCI, with the UCI Cleveland Heart Disease, UCI Chronic Kidney, UCI Dermatology, UCI Indian Liver Patient, and Pima Indians Diabetes datasets being the most prevalent. Among the ensemble methods, boosting consistently delivered the best accuracy across all datasets, followed by stacking and bagging. Voting was only optimal for two datasets.

The findings suggest that ensemble methods significantly improve the accuracy of disease prediction models, particularly for heart disease, which has received more focus than other chronic diseases such as skin cancer, liver disease, diabetes, and kidney disease. These models can aid healthcare professionals in identifying high-risk individuals, thereby enhancing health outcomes and reducing care costs.
