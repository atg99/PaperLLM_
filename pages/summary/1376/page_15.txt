This paper discusses the application of complex networks and Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) in the management and operation of water distribution and gas transmission systems. 

In the context of water distribution, the systems are characterized as complex networks where nodes represent water sources and demand points, while links include pipes, valves, and pumps. The analysis of these networks, as noted by Herrera et al. and di Nardo et al., enhances operational efficiency. Smart water networks incorporate advanced technologies like sensors and flow meters that contribute to improved hydraulic performance and optimized management, as highlighted by Candelieri et al.

For gas transmission, pipelines and compressor stations form a complex network with underground storage and gas stations as nodes. Smart gas grids enable real-time control to meet fluctuating demand and integrate with electrical power grids, as discussed by Bliek et al. and Brown et al. The paper also touches on a holonic architecture for continuous production complexes, particularly in oil production, where production units collaborate through agent programs for effective management of resources and processes.

Overall, the integration of complex network analysis and MAS offers innovative approaches to enhance the operational efficiency and management of critical utility systems.
