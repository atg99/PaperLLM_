The paper discusses the essential properties and criteria that define agents within multi-agent models. Key properties include:

1. **Location and Situatedness**: Agents exist within and interact with their environment, which is deemed essential.
2. **Mobility**: The ability to travel across networks and machines is optional.
3. **Autonomy**: This is a critical property, defined in absolute terms as self-manipulation of capabilities and in relative terms as independent action without human guidance.
4. **Perception**: Agents must have the ability to perceive their environment through sensors, capturing both instantaneous inputs and historical data, which is essential.
5. **Communication**: Essential for agents to interact with each other and with humans.
6. **Adaptation**: The ability to learn from past experiences and modify the environment is optional.
7. **Reactivity**: Essential for agents to respond promptly to environmental changes.
8. **Pro-activeness**: Optional; agents should have goals and take initiative, involving reasoning and planning.
9. **Rationality**: Optional; agents should act to maximize performance.
10. **Social Interaction**: Essential for agents to engage dynamically with others, influencing their behavior.

The paper also highlights that multi-agent models encompass the representation of individual agents, their interactions, and the environment. The capabilities of agents are shaped by the specific definitions used, which can represent various entities such as humans, machines, or organisms. Key components of these models include communication mechanisms, environmental sensing, and action capabilities, all supported by a knowledge model that contains relevant information for operation.
