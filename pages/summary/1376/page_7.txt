This paper reviews key concepts in the field of Distributed Artificial Intelligence (DAI), specifically differentiating between multi-agent systems (MAS) and agent-based modeling and simulation (ABMS). MAS is characterized by its ability to address complex, distributed problems through collaboration among agents, each capable of planning and communication, thereby achieving solutions beyond individual capabilities. In contrast, ABMS focuses on simulating repeated interactions among agents within a social system, providing insights into individual and collective behaviors, and is applied across various disciplines to explore self-organization and emergent behaviors.

The paper discusses the ambiguity surrounding the definition of an agent, noting that some researchers emphasize the actions of agents as primary characteristics, while others highlight properties like autonomy, social ability, reactivity, and pro-activeness. Agents are viewed as computer systems that autonomously act to achieve their goals and can be seen as intentional systems with mental representations like beliefs and intentions. In ABMS, agents are defined as proactive entities that interact based on simple local rules, leading to emergent system behavior.

The paper also presents a classification of agents based on their properties, tasks, and structures, identifying various types such as collaborative, reactive, and hybrid agents. It highlights the evolving understanding of agent properties, including flexibility and personality traits, and discusses the distinction between reactive and deliberative agents, as well as biological and robotic agents. Overall, the review underscores the complexity and variety of interpretations in the definition and classification of agents within the MAS and ABMS frameworks.
