Hierarchical architectures organize components or functions across multiple levels, with higher levels exerting broader influence and command over lower levels. Traditional hierarchical structures are characterized by rigid command/respond communication, which can limit flexibility and adaptability. In contrast, modified hierarchical architectures promote coordination among subordinates, allowing for interaction within the same level to accomplish tasks without constant oversight.

Heterarchical architectures differ by eliminating direct control, instead distributing supervision across the system. In these architectures, autonomous entities make localized decisions and collaborate using communication protocols, but this decentralized approach can hinder achieving global optimization and introduce unpredictability.

Holonic architectures aim to combine the strengths of both hierarchical and heterarchical systems by utilizing "holons," which function as both independent entities and components of a larger system. This hybrid structure allows for timely responses to disturbances while still considering overarching coordination from a central holon.

The organization of Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) can lead to effective control and optimization through local objectives of agents that negotiate and cooperate towards a global optimum.

The section also discusses representative applications of complex networks and agent-based control, particularly in supply chain and manufacturing networks. Manufacturing processes are complex due to the diversity of functions and resources, necessitating efficient management to maintain product quality. Key requirements include scheduling, processing orders simultaneously, quality assurance, and adaptability to changes. Effective supervision aligned with these dynamic needs is crucial.

Research in complex networks provides a framework for understanding supply chain dynamics, with studies such as Hearnshaw et al. highlighting the importance of complex network theory in analyzing relationships among supply chain actors.
