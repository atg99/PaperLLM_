The paper discusses the critical role of transportation systems and water distribution networks in societal development and citizen well-being. Transportation systems, including roads, railways, and airline networks, are structured as complex networks with nodes representing intersections or stations and links representing the routes connecting them. Common challenges faced by these networks include congestion at both nodes and links, often exacerbated by their scale-free topology.

The concept of smart transportation systems is introduced, aiming to enhance traffic efficiency and reduce issues like congestion through real-time data and alerts. These systems contribute to the development of smart, resilient, and energy-efficient cities. The paper references a proposed integration of energy storage systems within smart transportation frameworks and outlines a hierarchical control architecture for automated highways involving both roadside and vehicle-level management.

Additionally, the paper addresses water distribution systems, emphasizing their importance for public health, food security, and energy supply. The risks associated with these systems, such as pipe bursts and contamination, can lead to significant socioeconomic impacts and health risks for citizens, highlighting the need for effective management and resilience in these infrastructures.
