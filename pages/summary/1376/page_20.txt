The references listed highlight significant contributions to the understanding of complex networks across various domains, including social, biological, and infrastructural systems. Key works include Bornholdt and Schuster's comprehensive overview of graphs and networks, Scott's exploration of social network analysis, and Brintrup et al.'s characterization of supply networks as complex systems. The evolution of random graphs was notably addressed by Erdos and Rényi, while Watts and Strogatz introduced the concept of small-world networks. Barabási's work on scale-free networks has been pivotal in understanding network structure and dynamics.

Research into the functional cartography of metabolic networks by Guimera and Amaral, along with Zio's insights into reliability and efficiency in critical infrastructures, showcases the application of network science to real-world problems. The classification of planar maps and the study of road networks by Strano et al. further illustrate the diverse applications of network analysis. Additionally, community structure in networks has been explored by Girvan and Newman, emphasizing the interconnectedness of social and biological systems.

Emerging issues in animal social networks were discussed by Kurvers et al., while Brentan et al. focused on optimizing water supply networks through community detection. Collaborative prognostics in social asset networks were investigated by Palau et al., indicating the growing importance of network science in various sectors. The emergence of core-periphery structures and centrality measures in networks, as studied by Lee, Verma, Opsahl, and Freeman, provide a deeper understanding of node importance and network dynamics. Overall, these references collectively contribute to the evolving landscape of network science, informing both theoretical and practical advancements.
