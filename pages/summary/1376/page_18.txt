The paper discusses the modeling, validation, and calibration of agent-based models (ABMs) across various scientific disciplines, emphasizing the challenge of effectively processing data while providing feedback to the model. It highlights the potential of supervised, unsupervised, and semi-supervised learning techniques for model calibration, including classification and comparison of key model features within observation windows and defining baselines for predicted behaviors.

Future research is anticipated to explore how agents in multi-agent systems (MAS) can learn from each other, leveraging reinforcement learning where agents alternate between student and teacher roles to enhance system performance. However, challenges remain in applying this approach to complex real-world domains.

The paper outlines key future applications in systems engineering, including:

1. **Cyber-Physical Systems (CPSs)**: These systems integrate physical and digital components to enhance monitoring and control, aiming for optimal decision-making. MAS and complex networks are expected to address challenges in CPSs, particularly in industrial settings like smart electric grids and smart manufacturing.

2. **Digital Twins (DT)**: DTs serve as digital replicas of physical assets, allowing for preemptive performance testing under various scenarios. The complexity of future DTs will necessitate advanced modeling systems that incorporate complex networks, MAS, and visualization techniques to manage large-scale interdependencies.

3. **Blockchain Technologies**: The paper discusses blockchain as a decentralized ledger system, where blocks and transactions can be represented as nodes and links in a complex network. Future research will focus on dynamic complex networks to analyze blockchain, with agent-based systems playing a critical role in synchronization and control.
