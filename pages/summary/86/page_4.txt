The experiments aim to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed model, EA-LSTM, which integrates competitive random search (CRS) for optimizing attention weights in LSTM networks.

The CRS method enhances the LSTM's attention layer by generating optimal parameter combinations through a systematic process involving selection, recombination, and mutation of attention weights. This approach allows for effective exploration of the parameter space, driven by error feedback from the LSTM's predictions. The algorithm iteratively refines the attention weights until the desired set size is achieved, significantly influencing the model's performance.

The collaborative mechanism established between EA-LSTM and CRS facilitates the sharing of parameters and loss feedback, optimizing the training process. The model is trained using mini-batch stochastic gradient descent and Adam optimizer, with attention weights from CRS incorporated prior to LSTM training.

The subsequent sections of the paper detail the datasets used, parameter settings, training results, and comparative analyses against baseline models such as SVR, GBRT, RNN, GRU, and LSTM, as well as other attention-based methods. These comparisons are essential to validate the performance gains achieved by the proposed EA-LSTM model.
