This paper discusses the development and evaluation of an Enhanced Attention Long Short-Term Memory (EA-LSTM) model for time series prediction, focusing on its application to datasets like Beijing PM2.5 and SML2010. The authors conducted a parameter sensitivity analysis, examining the impact of different time steps, hidden units, and batch sizes on the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of predictions. 

The training of the EA-LSTM was done using Competitive Random Search (CRS) over 20 epochs, with results indicating that the model's performance continuously improved without becoming trapped in local minima. Attention weights were visualized using heat maps to demonstrate how the model effectively utilized local information and performed soft feature selection across multiple time steps.

The performance of EA-LSTM was compared against several baseline models, including traditional machine learning methods and other deep learning approaches like SVR, GBRT, RNN, LSTMs, and GRU. The results, averaged from multiple training and testing cycles, showed that the EA-LSTM outperformed these baseline methods on the selected datasets. Additionally, comparisons with the DA-RNN model revealed that the EA-LSTM achieved higher prediction accuracy, suggesting that enhancements in the training methodology for attention layers can be as effective as more complex attention mechanisms.

The study concludes that the use of competitive random search for optimizing attention weights significantly benefits the EA-LSTM model, leading to improved prediction results in time series forecasting tasks.
