The paper discusses the limitations of traditional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, particularly their reliance on gradient-based methods for training. To address these limitations, the authors propose an innovative approach that employs competitive random search, inspired by evolutionary computation and genetic algorithms, to optimize the attention mechanism in LSTMs. This method enhances the global searching ability, enabling the model to avoid local optima and improve prediction accuracy.

The study focuses on time series prediction, which can be categorized into regression and classification tasks. It involves using historical sequences of values to forecast future outcomes. The authors define the input feature matrix and establish a nonlinear mapping function to predict future values based on historical data.

The methodology section introduces the evolutionary attention-based LSTM model, detailing how it integrates an attention layer to manage long-term dependencies and prioritize important data samples. The attention weights are learned through competitive random search, and a collaborative training mechanism is implemented, where prediction errors guide the search process.

To model temporal dependencies effectively, the paper highlights the advantages of LSTMs over traditional methods, emphasizing their gated structure that enables better memory retention and information management. The authors provide the mathematical formulations that underpin the LSTM's calculations, illustrating how attention weights are applied to the input data and how the final predictions are derived.

Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms baseline models in prediction accuracy across various real-time series datasets, validating the effectiveness of using competitive random search in training attention mechanisms within LSTMs.
