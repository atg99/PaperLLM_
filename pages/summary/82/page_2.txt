This paper addresses the need for a comprehensive and accessible introduction to Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, aiming to unify the foundational theories for the Machine Learning community. It emphasizes a clear, consistent notation to facilitate understanding of both the "how" and "why" behind these systems.

The authors begin with RNNs, as LSTMs are a specialized form of RNNs, allowing insights gained from RNN analysis to be applicable to LSTMs. They derive canonical RNN equations from differential equations, highlighting the significance of this approach in understanding neural networks and their application in data processing tasks. The paper argues that differential equations provide a more natural modeling framework for real-world scenarios compared to difference equations.

The document outlines several key objectives for explaining the Vanilla LSTM system and its extensions: to ensure intuitive and complete explanations, provide a general overview of the LSTM system, include illustrative diagrams, describe the system modularly for easy integration, and utilize vector notation for practical implementation.

The structure of the paper includes a principled background on RNN systems, an exploration of RNN unrolling, a discussion on the training mechanism using Back Propagation Through Time, and a methodical construction of the Vanilla LSTM cell. Additionally, it details the Augmented LSTM system and concludes with a summary and suggestions for future work. Overall, the paper aims to fill existing gaps in literature by providing a thorough and intuitive understanding of RNNs and LSTMs.
