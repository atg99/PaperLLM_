This paper discusses the total partial derivatives of the objective function E in relation to the Vanilla LSTM cell. It introduces four key variables: ρ[n], γ[n], α[n], and ψ[n], representing the derivatives with respect to intermediate and border variables. Notably, ψ[n] is crucial as it influences all parameter updates in the Vanilla LSTM network, making it the most significant error gradient sequence.

The backward pass equations are derived using the chain rule, incorporating these derivatives. Key equations include χ[n], which combines the derivative of the output with respect to the value signal, and ρ[n], which relates the value signal to the intermediate variable r[n]. The equations also detail how the derivatives propagate through the network, ultimately leading to the calculation of accumulation derivatives, which are essential for model parameter updates.

To mitigate the risk of excessively large accumulation derivatives during training, the paper mentions that these values are clipped to a range between -1 and 1. This clipping is designed to prevent numerical instability and ensure effective training of the LSTM model. Overall, the paper emphasizes the critical role of these derivatives in the functioning and optimization of Vanilla LSTM networks.
