The Vanilla LSTM network processes the current cell state as a weighted combination of the previous state and new update information. Similar to standard RNNs, it can learn short-range dependencies in training data. However, if the model parameters lead to a situation where the norm of the cell state gradient is less than one, the error gradient may eventually vanish over time, despite positive contributions from the update signals. The paper emphasizes the necessity for all functions, including gate signal functions, to be differentiable during training via Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). The logistic function is introduced as a suitable activation function for the gate signals, providing a continuous and differentiable mapping from the entire real line into the range between zero and one. The logistic function is expressed in various forms, including a re-parameterized version of the hyperbolic tangent, and can be applied element-wise to vector inputs.
