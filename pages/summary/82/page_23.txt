The paper discusses the mechanics of the Vanilla Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, focusing on the computation of control signals at each step indexed by n. The control signals, denoted as gcs[n], gcu[n], and gcr[n], are derived from various data signals available at each step. Specifically, gcs[n] utilizes the previous state signal s[n-1], alongside v[n-1] and current input x[n], while gcr[n] relies on the current state s[n], v[n-1], and x[n]. The equations for calculating these control signals are presented, emphasizing how they are linear combinations of the available data signals.

The paper highlights the importance of these control signals in adapting to the flow of training data and modulating the data signals during inference. This adaptability helps the LSTM model to effectively manage the effects of artificial boundaries created by sequence truncation, enhancing its robustness and output quality.

The section concludes with an overview of how the LSTM cell functions over a series of K steps, detailing the internal state s[n], which remains unobservable externally, and the interplay between data preparation and control mechanisms. The LSTM design allows for candidate data signals to be selectively propagated based on the control signals, with values ranging from 0 to 1 determining the extent of this propagation. Overall, the Vanilla LSTM network is characterized by its structured approach to data management and control, enabling it to perform well in various applications.
