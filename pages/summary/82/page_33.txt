The paper discusses the mechanisms underlying the effectiveness of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in addressing the vanishing gradient problem, particularly when processing long-range dependencies in data. It begins by establishing the relationship between various partial derivatives of state signals across time steps, emphasizing the significance of maintaining these derivatives below unity to prevent diminishing gradients.

The key focus is on the total partial derivative of the objective function with respect to state signals, which reflects the amount of error gradient preserved after multiple time steps. The paper outlines three scenarios that could lead to negligible gradients: a trivial state where the network is inactive, a highly orchestrated condition where the terms cancel each other, and a case where the spectral radius of a specific matrix is less than one, indicating that the error gradient diminishes naturally due to the characteristics of the training data.

Furthermore, the paper highlights the necessity of constraining various parameters and signals within the LSTM architecture to achieve favorable outcomes. Multiple conditions are proposed to ensure the spectral radius remains below one, thereby facilitating effective gradient propagation. The discussion culminates in the observation that if the model is trained to saturate certain signals, such as the control gate signals, it may inadvertently lead to recirculation of the error gradient, potentially undermining the training process.

In conclusion, the work provides a comprehensive analysis of how LSTM networks can effectively mitigate the vanishing gradient problem through careful parameter management and signal conditioning, essential for learning from long-range dependencies in sequential data.
