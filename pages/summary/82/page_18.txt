The paper discusses the stability analysis of a truncated unrolled RNN system, focusing on the Jacobian matrix's behavior and the implications of eigenvalues on gradient propagation. The Jacobian matrix's magnitude is influenced by the product of individual Jacobian matrices, which is affected by the system's design ensuring stability. If all eigenvalues of the weight matrix satisfy stability conditions, the Jacobian remains bounded, leading to vanishing gradients. However, if any eigenvalue fails this condition, gradients may explode, complicating training with Gradient Descent.

The paper highlights two scenarios arising from instability: one where the readout signal saturates, leading to negligible gradients, and another where the state signal remains biased in a quasi-linear region, potentially causing overflow. Both scenarios underscore the challenges of training standard RNNs, which can suffer from vanishing or exploding gradients regardless of stability.

To address these issues, the paper introduces the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, designed to mitigate the vanishing gradient problem by incorporating nonlinear, data-dependent controls. This design modification allows the LSTM to maintain robust gradient flow during training. The LSTM architecture differentiates itself from traditional RNN cells by explicitly separating state updates into components influenced by previous states and inputs, ultimately enhancing the model's ability to learn long-term dependencies without the limitations of standard RNNs.
