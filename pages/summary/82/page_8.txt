The paper discusses the stability conditions for a recurrent neural network (RNN) defined by a specific set of equations. For the system to be stable, every eigenvalue of the matrix ˆW must lie within the unit circle. The paper simplifies the analysis by setting the time step △T to 1 and assuming a diagonal matrix A with large negative entries, which ensures the stability of the system. Under these conditions, the influence of the state signal from memory on the system's trajectory becomes negligible, allowing the system to be reduced to its standard RNN definition.

The stability of the RNN is primarily determined by the matrix ˆW, which can be expressed as a product of two diagonal matrices, leading to a simplified stability analysis. The necessary condition for stability is that the eigenvalues of matrix B must be positive and less than the absolute values of the diagonal entries of matrix A. If these conditions are not met, the system risks becoming unstable, resulting in oscillations or saturation.

The paper also explores an alternative approach by treating the parameters of the RNN as independent and setting Ws to zero, maintaining the same stability requirements. It emphasizes that the RNN essentially implements the backward Euler numerical integration method for a specific ordinary delay differential equation, suggesting the underlying phenomenon being modeled is fundamentally analog in nature.

Additionally, the paper contrasts two modeling directions: starting from a continuous-time domain and moving to a discrete domain, or vice versa. The former is exemplified by modeling audio signals, while the latter is illustrated with written text. Both approaches serve to highlight the advantages and limitations of the models used in understanding the underlying phenomena. The discussion concludes with a note on the necessity to evolve the RNN into more complex architectures, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, to address stability and performance challenges.
