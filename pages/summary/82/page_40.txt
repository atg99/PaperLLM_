The paper provides a comprehensive exploration of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, emphasizing a principled approach rooted in differential equations. It begins by establishing the canonical formulation of RNNs through sampling delay differential equations relevant in various scientific fields. The authors derive the standard RNN formulation by selecting suitable parameters and applying stability considerations, while also explaining RNN unrolling as an approximation of Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) systems.

The training of standard RNNs using Back Propagation Through Time is discussed, highlighting the challenges of vanishing and exploding gradients that complicate the Gradient Descent training process. To overcome these limitations, the authors transition to LSTM networks, detailing their construction from canonical RNNs and providing an in-depth overview of the Vanilla LSTM architecture, including both the forward and backward passes.

A significant contribution of the paper is its pedagogical approach, which clarifies the analysis of RNNs and LSTMs from a Signal Processing perspective, alongside a formal derivation of the RNN unrolling process. The authors introduce two novel extensions to the Vanilla LSTM: convolutional non-causal input context windows and an external input gate. They augment LSTM cell equations with these enhancements and suggest future work to implement the Augmented LSTM in high-performance computing environments, emphasizing its potential applications in language representation for question answering and automated customer support.

The paper concludes by recommending benchmarks against various models, including Vanilla LSTM and Transformers, to assess performance impacts and the effectiveness of the external input gate in refining input sequences. The authors encourage further exploration of the Augmented LSTM in diverse practical domains.
