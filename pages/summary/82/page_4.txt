The paper discusses a system modeled by a nonlinear ordinary delay differential equation (DDE) that incorporates discrete delays, as represented in Equation 9. The equation reflects the time rate of change of a state signal, influenced by three primary components: an analog component based on the state signal itself, a readout component derived from the state signal, and an external input component. Each component includes time-shifted functions that account for delays in their respective signals.

The choice of the hyperbolic tangent as the warping function is justified by its monotonic and negative-symmetric properties, along with its quasi-linear region that can be adjusted for system design. This function ensures that the output signals remain bounded while retaining state information with low distortion, particularly in the small signal regime.

The time delay elements in the model introduce a memory aspect, allowing the instantaneous rate of change of the state signal to include contributions from past values of the state, readout, and input signals. This enhances the model's ability to capture causal and contextual information, making it applicable in various fields such as biology and engineering.
