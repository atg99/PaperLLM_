The paper discusses the derivation of partial derivatives of the objective function E with respect to the parameters of a Vanilla LSTM cell. The equations from 156 to 170 outline the partial derivatives for various weight matrices and biases associated with the input, state, and forget gates for different components of the LSTM cell. 

It then consolidates these derivatives into a total partial derivative of E with respect to the model parameters, Θ, at a given time step n, as shown in Equation 171. Notably, when the LSTM cell is unrolled for K steps, the same parameters are shared across all steps, leading to the total derivative of E being a summation of the contributions from each unrolled step as expressed in Equation 172.

This total derivative is crucial for optimization using Gradient Descent, where it is calculated over batches of training segments. Each segment typically consists of 16 to 128 samples, and the cumulative gradients from these segments are utilized to update the model parameters effectively. While the paper notes that regularization is important in machine learning training, it is not the main focus of the current discussion.
