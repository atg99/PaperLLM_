This section of the paper outlines the derivation of equations essential for training a Vanilla LSTM network cell using Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT). The process begins with the computation of two backward-moving gradient sequences, ⃗χ[n] and ⃗ψ[n], which represent the total partial derivatives of the objective function with respect to the observable value signal and the state signal, respectively. By anchoring the chain rule at the cell's border, the derivation focuses on expressing intra-cell total partial derivatives in terms of ⃗χ[n], thereby reducing the complexity of intermediate variables.

The backward-moving recursion of ⃗χ[n] and ⃗ψ[n] indicates that their values at index n depend on those at index n+1, allowing for the gradual computation of total partial derivatives with respect to model parameters. During inference, only the input signal ⃗x[n] and the value signal ⃗v[n] are accessible, while other intermediate signals remain internal to the cell. The value signal can be transformed into an output signal ⃗y[n] through various decision functions, and input preprocessing techniques can enhance the system's contextual awareness.

The section concludes with the computation of derivatives for the warping functions, which simplifies the training equations and supports modular implementation at each step. This approach not only facilitates training but also enhances the overall efficiency of the Vanilla LSTM cell.
