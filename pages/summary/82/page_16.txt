The section discusses the training difficulties associated with recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on the truncated unrolled RNN systems defined by specific equations. Proposition 1 establishes the equivalence between the standard RNN system and its truncated version. While the truncated system is theoretically stable, practical training issues arise due to "vanishing gradients" and "exploding gradients," especially when dealing with long-range dependencies across many samples.

The training process typically utilizes Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT), an adaptation of the Back Propagation algorithm for sequence data. This involves unrolling the RNN to transform its directed graph into a directed acyclic graph, enabling the application of the chain rule for differentiation. BPTT aims to optimize an objective function based on the readout signal by adjusting the system parameters. 

The objective function often aggregates contributions from individual readout signals, leading to the temptation to isolate gradients. However, this approach overlooks the interdependence of state signals across time steps, which must be considered to accurately compute the total gradient. The section highlights the necessity of recognizing these dependencies to ensure effective training of RNNs.
