This section discusses the unrolling or unfolding of recurrent neural network (RNN) cells, specifically in the context of Equations 30 and 32. The term "cell" refers to the state before computation, and unrolling involves initializing the state signal, s[n], and evaluating the equations over discrete time steps indexed by n. This process allows for the visualization of the sequence of outputs generated by the RNN, as depicted in Figure 2.

The equations are recursive, meaning that the state signal s[n] at any index n incorporates contributions from previous states and inputs for all prior indices k, up to k=0. This characteristic classifies RNNs as Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) systems, which highlights their ability to maintain information over time.

Additionally, the section introduces the vector-valued unit step function, u[n], and the vector-valued unit sample function, δ[n]. The unit step function is defined as a vector that outputs 1 for n≥0 and 0 for n<0, while the unit sample function represents an impulse at n=0. These functions are illustrated in Figure 3 and serve as fundamental components in understanding the behavior of RNNs over time.
