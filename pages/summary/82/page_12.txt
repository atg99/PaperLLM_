The paper presents Proposition 1, which asserts that in a standard RNN system, an appropriate parameterization can optimize the objective function L for an N-sample sequence when certain conditions are met. These conditions include the existence of finite constants M and K_m, where K_m is less than N, and the ground truth output sequence can be divided into independent segment-level subsequences. 

The proof begins by establishing that the optimization of L indicates that the RNN's output ensemble closely approximates the ground truth output ensemble within a specified tolerance. By segmenting both the RNN output and the ground truth in a similar manner, the paper formulates the segment-level output subsequences. It is shown that, due to the mutual independence of these segments, the overall objective function can be expressed as a combination of independent segment-level components.

Furthermore, it is stated that the independence of the segment-level ensembles leads to the independence of their corresponding observable outputs, readout signals, and state signals. This independence imposes a restriction on the initial conditions of the state signal subsequences, ensuring that the initial conditions of one segment do not depend on the samples from another segment. 

In summary, the proposition highlights the sufficiency of a single, reusable RNN cell for optimizing the objective function and inferring outputs from unseen inputs by leveraging the independence of segment-level subsequences.
