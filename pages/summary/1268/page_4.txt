The paper introduces MotherNets, a method for rapid deep ensemble learning that creates a compact representation of an ensemble of neural networks. The process begins by identifying the shallowest network within a cluster of networks, referred to as C, and constructing a MotherNet (Mc) layer by layer, ensuring each hidden layer mirrors the structure of the corresponding layer in C with the fewest parameters. For convolutional networks, the MotherNet is built block-by-block, selecting layers based on the minimum number of filters and filter size within each block.

To effectively handle ensembles with varying sizes and architectures, the paper suggests partitioning the networks into g clusters. Each cluster has its own MotherNet constructed to optimize performance while minimizing computational costs. The clustering is performed using a balanced K-means algorithm that aims to minimize the Levenshtein distance between the vector representations of the networks and their respective MotherNets. This approach ensures that the MotherNet captures significant commonality among the networks, enhancing efficiency in computation and data movement.

Overall, the methodology emphasizes the importance of maintaining structural integrity and minimizing discrepancies across diverse networks to achieve better performance and resource sharing in deep learning tasks.
