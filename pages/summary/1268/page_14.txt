The paper presents algorithms for constructing MotherNets, which are designed to improve the efficiency of ensemble learning in neural networks. Two specific algorithms are outlined: one for fully-connected neural networks and another for convolutional neural networks.

In the case of fully-connected networks, the MotherNet is constructed by identifying the layer with the least number of parameters at each position across the ensemble, ensuring a compact representation. For convolutional networks, the approach is block-based, where the MotherNet is formed by selecting the minimum number of convolutional filters and their sizes from the ensemble for each position in the blocks.

The paper also discusses model covariance and its impact on ensemble predictive accuracy, utilizing Chebyshev’s Inequality to illustrate that lower covariance among models enhances performance. It emphasizes that maintaining high average model accuracy is crucial, as any degradation in model quality can negate benefits from reduced variance.

Furthermore, the paper details the ensemble inference process, highlighting that as the ensemble size increases, the computational and memory demands grow linearly due to the requirement of passing data through each network and maintaining their parameters. Overall, the proposed MotherNets aim to streamline the ensemble learning process while preserving accuracy.
