The results indicate that MotherNets outperform traditional methods such as knowledge distillation (KD), teacher networks (TN), and bagging (BA) in terms of both efficiency and effectiveness. Specifically, MotherNets serve as a foundational network from which ensemble networks can rapidly be trained, leveraging the learned functions for improved convergence and regularization.

The study highlights that BA, while faster, often results in higher test error rates due to its reduced exposure to unique data. In contrast, MotherNets consistently show lower test error rates, approximately 3.6 times faster than BA, and up to 5.5% lower absolute test error rates compared to other methods. The breakdown of training time reveals that while most methods require substantial time for each network, MotherNets allow for quick training after the initial MotherNet has been established.

Furthermore, the paper compares MotherNets to snapshot ensembles (SE), revealing that MotherNets not only provide better oracle test accuracy but also scale more effectively with larger ensembles. As the number of networks increases, MotherNets maintain superior training times, demonstrating a significant advantage in training efficiency.

In conclusion, MotherNets represent a substantial advancement in ensemble learning, facilitating faster training and improved accuracy, particularly as the ensemble size grows. This work has implications for applications requiring efficient and effective deep learning models.
