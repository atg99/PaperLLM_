The paper discusses the limitations of existing methods for training ensembles of deep neural networks, such as speed, accuracy, diversity, and size. Traditional approaches like bagging and knowledge distillation often lead to small ensembles due to high training costs, which restrict their generalization accuracy. For instance, while decision forests can utilize hundreds of models, deep learning ensembles typically consist of only a few networks.

Recent techniques like Snapshot Ensembles and TreeNets attempt to reduce training time by generating multiple networks from a single model. However, these methods compromise accuracy and diversity, as they rely on a single initialization and may not leverage varied architectures. Knowledge Distillation offers a compromise by training a large network and distilling its knowledge to smaller networks, but this still results in high training costs and limited diversity.

The authors propose MotherNets, which allow for rapid training of large ensembles with diverse architectures. MotherNets capture the structural similarities between clusters of networks and are trained on the full dataset. Each target network is then derived from its corresponding MotherNet through function-preserving transformations. This approach promises lower training times and improved generalization accuracy compared to existing fast ensemble training methods while enabling the construction of larger and more diverse ensembles.
