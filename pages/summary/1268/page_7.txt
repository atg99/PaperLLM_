In their experiments, the authors demonstrate that MotherNets provide a superior trade-off between accuracy and training time across various datasets, including SVHN, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100. The training setup involves stochastic gradient descent with specific learning rates tailored for different architectures. Results indicate that MotherNets, especially with a single cluster setup (g=1), outperform existing methods like Knowledge Distillation (KD) and TreeNets (TN) in both speed and accuracy. 

The findings suggest that MotherNets establish a new Pareto frontier in the accuracy-training time trade-off, consistently yielding better performance than traditional methods, while also being more efficient in terms of training duration. The paper highlights the effectiveness of shared layers in enhancing model accuracy, aligning with previous research. Overall, the authors advocate for the adoption of MotherNets in scenarios where rapid ensemble learning is critical.
