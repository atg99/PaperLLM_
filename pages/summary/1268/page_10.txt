The paper introduces MotherNets, a novel approach to deep ensemble learning that aims to minimize model variance while maintaining high accuracy and reducing training costs. The analysis of model variance is conducted by training five models under three conditions: MN (MotherNets), SE (Snapshot Ensembles), and FD (fully independent models). The study measures the variance and covariance of the softmax outputs across test examples, revealing that independent training in FD results in zero covariance and the lowest ensemble variance of 0.0051. In contrast, MN and SE show non-zero covariance but still achieve significant variance reduction compared to a single model, with ensemble variances of 0.0125 and 0.0130, respectively, compared to 0.026 for a single model.

Key takeaways indicate that both MN and SE have similar training speeds to single models, providing substantial variance reduction at minimal cost. MN, in particular, allows for the creation of independent model clusters, further decreasing covariance and ensemble variance. Additionally, MotherNets demonstrate improved individual model accuracy over Snapshot Ensembles, leading to superior overall ensemble performance.

The paper also discusses related ensemble techniques, such as parameter sharing methods like Dropout, which implicitly ensemble networks during inference, and highlights that MotherNets can effectively integrate different architectures. Moreover, it notes that various training efficiency techniques are compatible with MotherNets, which does not alter the core computational components of training.

In conclusion, MotherNets facilitate the training of large and diverse neural network ensembles by leveraging structural similarities to reduce training epochs, thus navigating a new balance between accuracy and training cost.
