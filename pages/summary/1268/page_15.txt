The paper introduces the concept of MotherNets, a method for constructing deep ensemble learning architectures. The algorithm outlined for creating a MotherNet involves assembling convolutional networks block-by-block. The process begins by initializing an empty MotherNet and setting the input/output layer sizes based on the ensemble of networks. The algorithm identifies the shallowest network and its corresponding blocks, selecting the minimum number of filters and filter sizes for the hidden layers from the ensemble.

Additionally, the paper presents shared-MotherNets as a means to enhance efficiency by reducing inference time and memory requirements. In this approach, parameters from the MotherNet are incrementally trained in a shared manner after the hatching process. The shared-MotherNet is initialized with multiple input and output layers, each corresponding to the hatched networks, allowing for simultaneous predictions. Each hidden layer is constructed by consolidating neurons from all hatched networks, reducing multiple copies of neurons to a single entity while summing inputs from various sources. 

The training of the shared-MotherNet is conducted incrementally, allowing for joint training of neurons originating from the MotherNet. This results in an effective ensemble that maintains performance while optimizing resource usage. The paper emphasizes the advantages of using shared-MotherNets in deep learning applications, showcasing a structured method for ensemble learning.
