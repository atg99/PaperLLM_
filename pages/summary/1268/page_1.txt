MotherNets address these challenges by capturing structural similarities among ensemble members, allowing for shared data movement and computation. Initially, a single or a few MotherNets are trained, from which the target ensemble networks are derived. This transfer of function enables much faster convergence during subsequent training phases compared to starting from scratch. 

Additionally, MotherNets utilize clustering to manage the tradeoff between accuracy and training cost, creating separate MotherNets for different architecture clusters. The results demonstrate that MotherNets establish a new Pareto frontier for the accuracy-training cost tradeoff, outperforming existing methods like Snapshot Ensembles and Knowledge Distillation. Specifically, they achieve a 2 to 3 percent reduction in absolute test error rates and up to 35 percent faster training times. These advantages are consistent across various neural architectures and large datasets, indicating the scalability of MotherNets for ensemble learning in deep neural networks.
