The paper presents a series of experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed robust policy gradient (RPG) method compared to a robust policy gradient using linear programming (LP-RPG) and non-robust policy gradient (PG) methods. The experiments involved generating nominal models for various state-action space sizes, with results averaged over 100 runs.

Initially, the focus was on ℓ1-robust Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), where the RPG method was compared with the LP-RPG approach. The LP-RPG method relies on linear programming to compute the robust value function, while RPG uses a regularized formulation of robust value iteration. The results indicated that LP-RPG had significantly higher computational costs, being 1 to 3 orders of magnitude slower than RPG, confirming the theoretical complexities outlined in the paper.

Further comparisons between RPG and non-robust PG across different ℓp-balls demonstrated that RPG maintains similar time complexity to non-robust PG, particularly for p values of 1, 2, and infinity, where regularization quantities can be computed in closed form. For higher p values, a slight increase in running time was noted due to the necessity of binary search for computation.

Overall, the findings suggest that the RPG method is not only effective in terms of performance but also significantly more efficient than LP-RPG, particularly in the context of ℓ1-robust MDPs, with expectations of even greater discrepancies in computational costs for other ℓp-robust MDPs.
