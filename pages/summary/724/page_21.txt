The paper discusses the computational complexity associated with policy gradient methods in the context of non-robust Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and robust policy improvement. It establishes that the complexity of policy gradient for non-robust MDPs is O(S^2A log(SAϵ−1)). It further notes that robust policy improvement is strongly NP-Hard for convex non-rectangular uncertainty sets, even when convex. The paper indicates that the policy gradient method can achieve global optimality given oracle access to the policy gradient in polynomial time, which suggests that computing robust policy gradients must also be NP-Hard.

In section F.1, the paper presents helper results relevant to robust MDPs, specifically focusing on variance and mean function computations. It details that computing certain functions with ϵ-tolerance requires O(S log(Sϵ−1)) for variance and O(S log(ϵ−1)) for mean, utilizing binary search methods. 

The paper defines the occupation measure and provides a mathematical framework for its computation. It establishes that the occupation measure can be approximated with a complexity of O(S^2A + S^2 log(ϵ−1)) and discusses the implications of this approximation in relation to the stochastic matrix properties of Pπ.

Overall, the findings highlight the computational challenges inherent in robust MDPs and provide a structured approach to approximating the occupation measure, which is crucial for understanding policy performance under uncertainty.
