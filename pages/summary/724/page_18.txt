The paper discusses the determination of optimal policies within the context of robust reinforcement learning under uncertainty, specifically focusing on the use of Q-values. It establishes that while an optimal policy typically selects the action with the highest Q-value, this principle does not hold when dealing with s-rectangular or coupled uncertainty sets, where the optimal policy may exhibit stochastic behavior. 

Key findings include the formulation of Bellman recursions for robust Q-values, as presented in Proposition D.1, which outlines the recursion for both s-rectangular and (s, a)-rectangular cases. The robust Q-value is defined through the application of the Bellman operator, incorporating rewards and transition probabilities. This approach is further refined in Corollary D.2, which also presents the recursion for robust Q-values under an ℓp-ball constrained uncertainty set.

The paper then transitions to the evaluation of robust Q-learning equations, examining whether the proposed linear operator is contracting and can be efficiently computed. Proposition D.3 provides the evaluation of this operator for both types of uncertainty sets, indicating the structure of the operator and its dependence on the rewards and transition probabilities.

Overall, the work contributes to the understanding of robust reinforcement learning by detailing the conditions under which deterministic policies may fail and providing mathematical formulations for robust Q-value calculations.
