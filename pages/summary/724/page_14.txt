This paper presents a mathematical framework for analyzing the optimization of policies within a specified uncertainty set. It begins with the establishment of a proportionality coefficient C and outlines the minimization of a function φ, indicating that the optimal value c* is achieved while adhering to a noise constraint. 

The proof of Proposition 4.1 demonstrates that for any policy π within the rectangular uncertainty set ℓp, the utility uπ can be expressed in terms of the gradient of a variance function κq. It highlights that the utility vector uπ is a sub-gradient of κq, leading to the relationship between the utility and the optimal policy's variance.

Additionally, the properties of the variance function κq are noted, specifically its translation invariance along all-ones directions and the unit norm of the balanced-normalized vector up(v).

Following this, Theorem 4.2 characterizes the worst-case model in the context of an (s, a)-rectangular uncertainty set. It establishes a relationship between the worst-case rewards and transition probabilities, showing that they can be expressed in terms of nominal values adjusted by specific components related to the policy's utility. 

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous approach to understanding policy optimization under uncertainty, employing mathematical proofs to validate its propositions and theorems.
