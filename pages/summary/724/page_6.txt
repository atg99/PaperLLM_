The paper discusses the computation of robust Q-values and robust visitation frequencies in the context of uncertainty sets in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). It introduces the worst-case model parameters, denoted as Qπ_U and dπ_U, which represent the robust Q-value and the robust visitation frequency, respectively. The authors define a normalized and balanced robust value function, uπ_U, which is linked to the q-variance function and has a zero mean and unit norm.

The paper presents Theorem 4.2, which establishes the relationship between nominal models and worst-case models in the (s, a)-rectangular case. The worst reward function is shown to be independent of the employed policy, while the worst transition kernel is policy-dependent. This suggests that the worst-case scenario directs the system toward low-reward states.

Theorem 4.3 extends this relationship to the s-rectangular case, where the worst model is influenced by the policy, indicating a more complex dependence compared to the (s, a)-case. The results demonstrate that the worst models can be derived in closed form with a time complexity of O(S^2A log(ϵ^−1)), comparable to non-robust value iteration. However, employing convex optimization methods increases the complexity significantly.

Overall, the findings highlight the intricacies of robust MDPs, revealing how the worst-case models are constructed and the implications of policy dependence in different uncertainty configurations.
