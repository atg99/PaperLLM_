The paper introduces a Robust Policy Gradient (RPG) method that improves upon existing approaches by only requiring the nominal kernel to derive occupation measures, rather than sampling from all models within an uncertainty set. Theorem 5.1 presents the RPG formulation for rectangular ℓp-ball-constrained uncertainty, defining the robust policy gradient as a combination of the standard policy gradient and a correction term that accounts for the worst-case transition kernel.

The correction term is derived from the visitation frequency of the worst kernel and is essential for achieving robustness against transition uncertainties. The authors emphasize the significance of the rank-one perturbation structure of the worst transition kernel, which is guaranteed under ℓp-ball uncertainty conditions. The paper raises an open question regarding the conditions under which the worst transition kernel retains this structure for other convex sets.

Algorithm 1 outlines the implementation of the RPG, detailing the steps to compute policy gradients and update the policy iteratively. The complexity analysis reveals that while computing the RPG is generally more complex than non-robust variants, the additional computational burden is manageable, particularly when estimating the robust Q-value and the compensation term.

The results are generalizable to arbitrary norms, provided the uncertainty set is (s, a)-rectangular, maintaining the rank-one perturbation structure. The paper suggests that further exploration is needed to fully understand the implications of different norm structures on the RPG framework.
