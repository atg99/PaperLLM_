The paper discusses the complexity of evaluating robust Markov decision processes (MDPs) under uncertainty, specifically focusing on the computational complexity associated with the gradient and the robust Bellman operator. It establishes that the total complexity is O(SA) operations, resulting in a final complexity of ˜O(S²A log(ϵ⁻¹)), where ϵ represents the error margin.

The section then generalizes the findings to arbitrary norms beyond the ℓp norm, introducing the concept of (s, a)-rectangular robust MDPs. The uncertainty set is defined with constraints on the reward and transition probabilities, represented by Us_a and characterized by specific bounds on the rewards and transition matrices.

The robust Bellman operator is detailed, showing how to compute the robust value function and identify worst-case scenarios. The worst values are derived from the robust Bellman operator, and the paper explains how the worst kernel remains a rank-one perturbation of the nominal kernel.

The robust occupation measure is computed using established lemmas, and the robust Q-value is derived through a series of iterative calculations. The paper concludes that while the methods for general norms require numerical convex optimization techniques, the focus remains primarily on the ℓp case due to its concrete formulation in the analysis.
