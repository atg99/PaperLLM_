The paper discusses the implications of the (s, a)-rectangularity assumption in reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on the optimization of reward and transition functions under uncertainty. It establishes that the worst-case reward function, R*(s, a), is independent of the policy π and is defined as R*(s, a) = -αs,a for all (s, a) in the set X. In contrast, the worst-case transition function, P*(·|s, a), is dependent on the value function, which varies with policy π. The paper derives the worst-case transition function through optimization, leading to the expression for P*(s'|s, a) in relation to the value function vπ.

The proof of Theorem 4.3 emphasizes that under the s-rectangular case and given an uncertainty set U=Us, the worst model can be expressed in relation to a nominal model. Specifically, it shows that Rπ_U(s, a) = R0(s, a) - αs[πs(a)/∥πs∥^q]^(q-1) and Pπ_U(·|s, a) = P0(·|s, a) - βs[πs(a)/∥πs∥^q]^(q-1). The relationship between the worst-case and nominal models is derived from the definitions of the models and the (s, a)-rectangularity assumption, confirming that the worst reward does depend on the policy π while providing a method for calculating it.
