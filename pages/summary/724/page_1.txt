This paper introduces a novel approach called robust policy gradient (RPG) for efficiently solving rectangular robust Markov decision processes (MDPs) in reinforcement learning. Traditional policy gradient methods often overlook transition uncertainties, leading to challenges in developing robust policies that are computationally intensive to train. The authors provide a closed-form expression for the worst occupation measure and identify that the worst kernel can be viewed as a rank-one perturbation of the nominal kernel. By combining the worst occupation measure with robust Q-value estimation, they derive an explicit form of the robust gradient.

The paper emphasizes the significance of robust MDPs, which aim to optimize returns under the worst-case scenarios, thereby ensuring robustness in uncertain environments. It highlights the computational challenges associated with robust planning, as these problems can be NP-hard. The authors propose that by assuming the uncertainty set is convex and s-rectangular, the complexity can be managed. They also explore the relationship between robustness and regularization in reinforcement learning, suggesting that proper regularization can facilitate robust behavior without the high computational costs typically associated with convex optimization.

In conclusion, the RPG method developed in this work allows for robust policy training with comparable time complexity to non-robust approaches, significantly easing the computational burden and enhancing the practicality of robust reinforcement learning strategies.
