The paper discusses the formulation and optimization of robust policies in the context of uncertainty sets characterized by ℓp-norm balls. It introduces two types of uncertainty sets: the (s, a)-rectangular case and the s-rectangular case, each defined by specific constraints on rewards and transition probabilities. The sets account for linear constraints to ensure non-negativity in the model, which can lead to conservative policies but are suitable for model-free robust learning. The authors explain that the norm-ball structure allows for efficient computation of robust Bellman updates similar to non-robust scenarios, leveraging regularization techniques.

Proposition 3.1 establishes that for any policy, the robust Bellman operator can be expressed in a regularized form, facilitating the derivation of robust policy gradients (RPG) for the defined uncertainty sets. The paper then outlines a robust gradient method, emphasizing the use of projected sub-gradient ascent to optimize the robust return, despite the challenges posed by non-differentiability and potential NP-hard complexity in general convex uncertainty sets.

The latter sections focus on the necessary components to derive RPG, starting with the general framework of policy gradient optimization. The authors reveal that the worst transition kernel can be viewed as a rank-one perturbation of the nominal, which aids in deriving the robust occupancy measure—the visitation frequency associated with the worst kernel. Finally, an alternative definition of robust Q-value is proposed, with a method to estimate it through Bellman recursion, thereby enriching the understanding of robust policy optimization under uncertainty.
