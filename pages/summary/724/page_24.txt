The paper discusses the challenges of generalizing the concept of s-rectangular balls within various norms and metrics, emphasizing the specific properties of the ℓp-norm that facilitate rank-one perturbation proofs. The key property highlighted is 'decoupling,' which allows for the decomposition of norms into sums across components. The authors note that while this holds for ℓp-norms, its applicability to other norms remains uncertain and warrants further investigation.

In the section on generalizing to metrics and divergences, the authors express that extending their Robust Policy Gradient (RPG) method to incorporate distance or divergence-based uncertainty sets presents difficulties. This is primarily because the worst kernel's rank-one perturbation characterization, essential to their method, may not be applicable in certain scenarios, such as with Kullback-Leibler divergence constraints.

The experimental section outlines the methodology and results of their study. The experiments utilized randomly generated transition kernels and reward functions, with a consistent discount factor and noise parameters across varying state and action sizes. Conducted on a high-performance machine, the experiments were primarily coded in Python, with results shared publicly.

The findings indicate that their methods exhibit excellent scalability in large state-action spaces, requiring significantly less time compared to traditional linear programming (LP) methods. The authors observed that while the s-case generally outperformed the sa-case in smaller spaces, this trend reversed with larger sizes due to implementation intricacies and the computational demands of the robust value function evaluation. Specifically, the cost of the s-case increased at a faster rate than the sa-case, ultimately leading to suboptimal performance.

Finally, the paper details the steps for computing RPG using LP, highlighting robust value iteration as the most computationally intensive phase, which involves repeated evaluations of robust Bellman operators through LP formulations.
