The paper discusses the derivation of a robust occupation measure using nominal values, leading to an explicit robust policy gradient (RPG). The focus is on ball-constrained uncertainty, which facilitates an efficient calculation of the robust occupation matrix from the nominal occupation measure. The core contribution is established through Lemma 4.4, which shows that a rank-one perturbation of the nominal transition matrix can relate the corresponding occupation matrices.

The concept of expected transition uncertainty is introduced in Definition 4.5, defining the uncertainty for any policy within a rectangular ℓp-ball-constrained uncertainty set. Theorem 4.6 establishes a relationship between the robust visitation frequency and the nominal visitation frequency, highlighting that the non-robust occupation measure must be penalized by a term that accounts for the uncertainty. This penalty term tends to zero when the robust value function converges.

The paper acknowledges challenges in obtaining a non-biased estimator for the robust occupation measure in model-free learning and suggests that while importance sampling could be used, it introduces issues of variance and bias.

Section 4.3 shifts focus to robust Q-values, defining them in relation to the associated value function and the Bellman recursion. The robust Q-value is shown to be the unique fixed point of a γ-contracting operator. Proposition 4.7 presents a regularized operator equivalent to the Bellman operator, which aids in the efficient computation of the robust Q-value.

Overall, the paper provides a systematic approach to deriving robust measures in the context of uncertain environments, contributing valuable insights into robust reinforcement learning.
