The paper discusses robust policy gradient (RPG) methods in the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with uncertainty. It begins by characterizing the worst-case kernel and introduces the notation for the robust Q-value and occupation measure. The key result demonstrates how the difference between the Q-values can be bounded in terms of the initial value function, the normalized vectors, and the Lipschitz properties of the functions involved.

A significant theorem presented is the RPG theorem, outlining that for any rectangular ℓp-ball-constrained uncertainty, the robust policy gradient can be computed as a combination of the robust occupation measure and the robust Q-value. The paper emphasizes that the complexity of computing these values increases with the level of uncertainty in the MDPs.

In non-robust MDPs, the authors highlight that the most computationally expensive tasks involve calculating Q-values and occupation measures, which require O(S²A log(ε⁻¹)) operations. The total cost of policy gradient computations primarily revolves around these Q-value calculations, with additional terms accounting for approximation errors. The paper concludes with a detailed analysis of the complexity associated with RPG iterations, illustrating how uncertainty structures can affect computational efficiency.
