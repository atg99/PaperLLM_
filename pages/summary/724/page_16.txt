The paper discusses the worst kernel in the context of value functions and policies, defining it mathematically as an optimization problem. The worst kernel is expressed through a minimization of a function related to the value function and policy, specifically denoting the transition probabilities that minimize the expected value under certain constraints.

The optimization problem is reformulated to involve two layers of minimization, focusing on conditions related to the norms of certain variables and their interactions with the policy. The paper establishes a relationship between the optimal policy and the value function, ultimately leading to a definition of the balanced value function.

Additionally, the paper provides a lemma regarding the relationship between occupation matrices derived from two transition matrices that differ by a rank-one perturbation. It proves that the occupation matrix of the perturbed transition can be expressed in terms of the original occupation matrix and the perturbation parameters. This establishes a foundational relationship critical for understanding the dynamics of the transition matrices and their impact on the value functions in the given context.
