The paper presents an evaluation of a new algorithm for optimal classification trees, tested on various datasets, including recidivism and credit risk data. Experiments were conducted on an AWS m5a:4xlarge instance and a personal laptop, comparing the algorithm's performance against baseline methods, specifically CART and BinOCT. The results demonstrate that the new method consistently matches or exceeds the performance of CART, using its objective value as a warm start.

Key findings include the algorithm's efficiency, as it operates on a single thread yet remains competitive with BinOCT, which utilizes eight threads. Notably, while BinOCT is optimized for speed by limiting the search space, it may miss optimal sparse solutions that the proposed method can identify.

The paper also discusses the convergence behavior of the algorithm, highlighting how different scheduling policies affect performance. Using a curiosity-based approach allows the algorithm to find optimal trees more quickly than a lower-bound scheduling method. Scalability analyses indicate that while runtime theoretically increases with the number of features, the algorithm can prune the search space effectively, leading to faster convergence when additional irrelevant features are included.

Overall, the new algorithm shows promise in balancing speed and optimality in classification tasks, outperforming traditional methods in specific contexts while maintaining a manageable computational footprint.
