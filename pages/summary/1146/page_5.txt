This paper discusses the analysis of optimal decision trees, specifically focusing on the permutation of leaves and the implications for classification accuracy and support bounds. It emphasizes that only permutations of leaves need to be considered, as outlined in the referenced corollary.

The leaf accurate support bound establishes that for each leaf in an optimal decision tree, the number of correctly classified samples must exceed a certain threshold. The hierarchical objective lower bound is decomposed into two components: unchanged leaves and those that will be split. This decomposition is expressed mathematically, where the proportion of misclassified data in unchanged leaves and the leaves to be split are defined.

Theorem 3.1 presents a hierarchical objective lower bound, indicating that if a child tree maintains certain unchanged leaves, its objective will not exceed the defined lower bound. This property facilitates a pruning strategy in a sequence of trees, ensuring that as trees are evaluated, the search space can be effectively reduced based on previously established bounds.

Furthermore, Lemma 3.2 extends this concept by introducing a one-step lookahead, allowing for further pruning of child trees if the lower bound for a tree exceeds the current best objective.

The paper also introduces Theorem 3.3 and Theorem 3.4, which provide lower bounds on the support of nodes and classification accuracy, respectively. The first theorem states that the support through each internal node must be at least twice the regularization parameter, establishing a criterion for pruning suboptimal trees. The second theorem discusses the incremental classification accuracy, asserting that optimal trees must maintain certain accuracy thresholds.

Overall, the findings present a robust framework for evaluating and pruning decision trees based on hierarchical bounds, enhancing the efficiency of tree-based classification methods.
