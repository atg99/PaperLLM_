The paper discusses the comparative search spaces of CORELS (Constrained Optimization Rule Lists) and decision trees in the context of binary classification. It highlights that the optimization problem for rule lists is significantly easier due to pre-mined rules, whereas decision trees require evaluating every possible split across multiple variables, making their optimization exponentially more complex. 

Table 1 illustrates the rapid growth of the search space for decision trees as the depth increases, revealing that the search space for trees is vastly larger than that for rule lists. The paper notes that in CORELS, the maximum number of clauses per rule is limited, leading to a manageable number of rules. In contrast, the number of distinct decision trees grows dramatically with depth, emphasizing the complexity involved in decision tree optimization.

The authors introduce a framework for Optimal Sparse Decision Trees (OSDT), which focuses on binary classification but can potentially be extended to multiclass scenarios. The framework represents a decision tree as a collection of leaves, where each leaf corresponds to a classification rule. The paper explores how to efficiently split leaves in the tree to improve classification accuracy, detailing the structure of the leaf set and the process of identifying which leaves can be beneficially split.

Overall, the findings highlight the challenges posed by the search space of decision trees compared to rule lists and propose a structured approach to optimizing decision trees through selective leaf splitting.
