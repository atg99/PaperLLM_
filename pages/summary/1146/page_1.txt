The paper "Optimal Sparse Decision Trees" addresses the long-standing issue of optimality in decision tree algorithms, which have been widely used for interpretable machine learning since the 1980s. Traditional algorithms like CART and C4.5 are often greedy, leading to suboptimal decision trees due to their myopic nature. This lack of optimality is both a theoretical and practical challenge, with previous mathematical programming approaches failing to efficiently solve the optimization problems associated with decision trees.

The authors propose a novel practical algorithm designed for optimal decision trees specifically for binary variables. This algorithm combines analytical bounds to reduce the search space with modern system techniques, including efficient data structures and a custom bit-vector library. The results from their experiments demonstrate significant improvements in scalability, speed, and the ability to prove optimality.

The introduction highlights the increasing importance of interpretable machine learning, particularly in high-stakes decision-making areas such as healthcare, environmental monitoring, credit scoring, and criminal justice. The paper argues that to encourage the adoption of interpretable models over black-box approaches, effective tools for constructing these models must be developed. Despite the historical dominance of greedy algorithms in practice, the authors emphasize the need for optimal decision tree methods to enhance accuracy and interpretability.
