The paper presents a nonparametric model for open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, demonstrating its effectiveness compared to parametric baselines, specifically SegFormer, across various class settings (150 to 847 classes). The results, summarized in Table 4, indicate that the nonparametric approach consistently outperforms the parametric counterpart, achieving higher mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) scores while maintaining comparable or fewer parameters.

Ablative studies are conducted to analyze the impact of different model components on performance, as detailed in Table 5. The training objectives reveal that combining multiple loss functions (LCE, LPPC, LPPD) enhances performance, achieving an mIoU of 46.4%. The study on the number of prototypes per class shows that while increasing the number of prototypes improves accuracy, diminishing returns occur beyond ten prototypes, leading to a recommended K=10 for optimal accuracy and computational efficiency.

Additionally, the momentum coefficient's effect on prototype updating speed indicates that slower updates (values between 0.999 and 0.9999) yield better results, while higher rates lead to performance drops. The choice of distance measure for pixel-prototype similarity also plays a crucial role, with cosine distance outperforming standard Euclidean and Huberized measures.

The conclusion emphasizes the advantages of the nonparametric framework, including explicit prototypical representation, improved generalization, and direct feature embedding optimization. The authors suggest potential avenues for further research, particularly in enhancing interpretability by aligning prototypes with pixel- or region-level observations.
