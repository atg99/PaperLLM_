This paper presents a novel perspective on semantic segmentation by framing existing methods as parametric approaches that utilize learnable class prototypes, specifically through softmax weights or query vectors. The authors identify limitations in these parametric strategies and propose a nonparametric alternative that employs non-learnable prototypes. Instead of assigning a single learnable weight or vector to each class, their model represents classes as sets of non-learnable prototypes derived from the mean features of training pixels. This approach enables dense predictions through nonparametric nearest prototype retrieval, optimizing the spatial arrangement between pixel embeddings and anchored prototypes.

The nonparametric framework is versatile, accommodating an arbitrary number of classes while maintaining a constant number of learnable parameters. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their model across various segmentation architectures, including FCN-based and attention-based models, achieving strong performance on multiple datasets such as ADE20K, Cityscapes, and COCO-Stuff, particularly in scenarios with large vocabulary.

The introduction emphasizes the evolution of semantic segmentation, highlighting the predominance of Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs) and the recent rise of Transformer-based models. It poses critical questions regarding the relationship and differences between parametric and nonparametric mask decoding strategies, suggesting that a deeper understanding of these aspects can influence the design of future segmentation models. Overall, the paper encourages a reevaluation of conventional semantic segmentation methodologies through this prototype-centric lens.
