The paper presents a comparative analysis of a new semantic segmentation approach against several established models across three datasets: ADE20K, Cityscapes, and COCO-Stuff. The quantitative results, as shown in Tables 1 to 3, indicate that the proposed method consistently achieves higher mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) scores while using fewer learnable parameters compared to baseline models, including FCN, HRNet, Swin, and SegFormer. 

Particularly, on ADE20K, the proposed method outperforms the FCN-based models by 1.2% and 1.0% over HRNet, and shows improvements over recent attention-based models with gains of 0.6% and 0.8%. Similar trends are observed on the Cityscapes validation set, where the approach surpasses all competitors by notable margins. On the COCO-Stuff test, the method also establishes a new state-of-the-art with an mIoU score of 43.3%.

Qualitative results highlight the method's ability to handle diverse and challenging scenarios effectively, surpassing Segformer in accuracy. The paper also discusses the scalability of the approach to large-vocabulary semantic segmentation, demonstrating its superiority in managing an increased number of classes without the need for additional learnable parameters, thus showcasing the efficiency of the proposed framework. 

Overall, the findings suggest that the new approach not only enhances segmentation performance across various datasets but also maintains efficiency in terms of parameter usage, making it a compelling option for semantic segmentation tasks.
