The paper discusses a framework for pixel classification using an exemplar-based reasoning approach, which leverages a winner-take-all classification mechanism. It defines a probability distribution for each pixel based on its distance to class prototypes, employing negative cosine similarity as the distance measure. The training objective is founded on cross-entropy loss, which encourages pixels to be closer to their respective class prototypes while distancing them from irrelevant prototypes.

However, the authors highlight limitations in this approach, specifically that it only considers pixel-class distances without addressing the relationships between pixels and their prototypes within the same class. To resolve these issues, the paper introduces a within-class online clustering strategy and additional training objectives that focus on prototype assignments.

The within-class online clustering method allows for the assignment of pixel samples to class-specific prototypes, optimizing the process by updating prototypes based on these assignments. This clustering is scalable, requiring fewer passes over the dataset compared to offline methods. The paper formalizes this process through optimization constraints that ensure unique and balanced assignments of pixels to prototypes.

Additionally, the paper proposes a pixel-prototype contrastive learning strategy based on the assignment probabilities. This strategy aims to maximize the posterior probability of prototype assignments, thereby enhancing the learning process by reinforcing the relationships between pixels and their corresponding prototypes.

Overall, the proposed framework aims to improve pixel classification by refining prototype selection and enhancing the relationships between pixels and prototypes, ultimately leading to more discriminative and accurate representations within the model.
