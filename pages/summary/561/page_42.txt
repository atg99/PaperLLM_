The paper discusses various strategies to enhance the efficiency of hyper-parameter tuning in machine learning. It highlights the challenges posed by small datasets, which can lead to bias and low fidelity. To address this, a transfer series expansion method is suggested, which leverages a set of predictors for faster evaluations. 

An early stopping strategy is also emphasized as a means to conserve training resources. This method involves segmenting the training process and terminating models that do not meet specific performance standards at checkpoints. Common early stopping techniques include median stopping and curve-fitting, which can be integrated with random search and Bayesian methods. Additionally, multi-bandit-based and population-based algorithms represent alternative early stopping approaches that aim to enhance efficiency by eliminating underperforming models, although there is a risk of prematurely discarding models that may improve with further training.

The paper also explores transfer learning, which utilizes insights from prior tasks to optimize performance or resource allocation for a target task. It notes that the target model should resemble the source model for effective transfer learning, making it particularly suitable for hyper-parameter tuning. The straightforward application of this approach involves initializing the model with parameters from similar configurations, allowing for subsequent fine-tuning to improve performance. While transfer learning can help save training time, it may introduce unpredictable noise, unlike the more controlled noise from subsampling and early stopping.
