The paper provides an overview of Bayesian Optimization (BO) focusing on its two main components: the surrogate model and the acquisition function. The BO process is structured into five key steps, starting with the establishment of a prior distribution for the surrogate model, followed by identifying the optimal hyper-parameter set, computing the acquisition function, applying this set to the objective function, and updating the surrogate model based on new results. This cycle continues until an optimal configuration is achieved or resource limits are reached.

The surrogate model may either be a specific analytic function or a non-parametric model, which allows for flexible modeling of the objective function. The acquisition function is crucial for balancing exploration and exploitation by minimizing the loss function to select optimal candidate points. While various probability models can be employed in BO, the Gaussian Process (GP) is the most prevalent due to its nonparametric nature, which allows the number of parameters to adapt based on the dataset size. Additionally, GP uses a multivariate Gaussian distribution as the prior for an infinite number of real-valued variables, enabling effective modeling of complex functions.

Several acquisition functions have been explored in the literature, including the probability of improvement, GP upper confidence bound, and predictive entropy search, with expected improvement being the most commonly utilized. The paper emphasizes the popularity of GP as a default surrogate model due to its flexibility and statistical properties, while noting that further details can be found in the referenced literature.
