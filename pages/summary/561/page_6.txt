In the paper by Yu and Zhu, the authors discuss the challenges associated with learning rate (LR) scheduling in machine learning models. They highlight that traditional LR schedules require users to pre-determine hyper-parameters, which can be difficult and often relies on experience. Additionally, these schedules typically apply a uniform LR across all layers, which may not be optimal.

To address these issues, the authors mention automated hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) and cyclical LR methods, where the LR is updated within a triangular bound that decays cyclically. They also reference a study by You et al. that introduces layer-wise adaptive rate scaling (LARS), allowing for local LRs that are specific to each layer's weights and gradients.

The authors emphasize the importance of selecting an appropriate LR, noting that a small LR can slow convergence while a large one may hinder it entirely. They suggest that the LR schedule should be tailored to the optimizer algorithm and recommend tuning both the LR schedule and the associated hyper-parameters simultaneously. Ultimately, while the optimal LR varies by task, certain guidelines can help in making effective choices.
