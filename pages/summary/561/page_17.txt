Hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) is a critical aspect of machine learning that involves finding the best set of parameters for model training. This paper reviews various algorithms and their applications, highlighting the differences between grid search and random search. Grid search allocates a fixed budget across hyper-parameter sets, which can be inefficient, especially when parameters are not uniformly distributed. Conversely, random search is more effective in discovering optimal configurations, as it allows for a more flexible exploration of the search space. It is particularly advantageous in large datasets and multi-dimensional scenarios due to its ease of parallelization and resource allocation.

While random search can be computationally intensive, it is recommended for the initial stages of HPO to quickly narrow down the search space before applying more refined guided algorithms. It often serves as a benchmark for evaluating the performance of newly developed optimization methods.

Bayesian optimization (BO) is another significant algorithm in HPO, known for its historical roots and effectiveness in global optimization problems. BO employs a sequential model-based approach that balances exploration and exploitation, aiming to minimize the number of trials needed to find the global optimum. This method is designed to iteratively improve results based on accumulated data, making it a powerful tool in the optimization landscape.
