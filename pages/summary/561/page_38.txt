Yu and Zhu discuss the challenges and methodologies in hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) within the context of deep learning. They highlight that while tools like Tune and NNI are convenient for experienced developers, users face limitations such as the necessity for deployment and configuration work, as well as the inability to run extensive trials due to resource constraints. The reliance on command-line interfaces further complicates usability.

The paper emphasizes the evolution of HPO, noting its 20-year history and its growing importance in the era of deep learning. Key challenges in automatic HPO include the infeasibility of traditional search algorithms due to the complexity and number of hyper-parameters, the high cost of evaluating certain model configurations, and the necessity of parallelism to enhance computational efficiency.

In the discussion section, a comparison of various algorithms is presented. Random search is noted for its parallelism and compatibility with early stopping strategies, which enhance efficiency in reducing the search space. Bayesian optimization (BO) is recognized as a classical method that utilizes prior search experiences through a guided approach, effectively balancing exploration and exploitation. The paper underscores the importance of computational efficiency in HPO tasks, particularly for deep learning models, and outlines the advantages and disadvantages of different algorithms in terms of resource savings, accuracy, and applicability.
