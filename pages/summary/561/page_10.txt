The paper discusses the updates of weights and biases in neural networks using specific optimization algorithms, namely Adam, which combines momentum and RMSprop. The equations for updating the velocity of weights and biases (vdw and vdb) are represented with momentum parameters, while the squared gradients (Sdw and Sdb) are updated using RMSprop. Bias corrections are applied to both velocity and squared gradients to account for initialization effects.

The suggested values for the momentum parameter (β1 = 0.9) and RMSprop parameter (β2 = 0.999) are noted, along with a small constant (ε = 10^-8) to prevent division by zero. These default configurations are deemed suitable for most problems, making Adam easy to implement in various deep learning frameworks, such as TensorFlow, Keras, Caffe, MxNet, and PyTorch.

The paper emphasizes the necessity of adjusting the learning rate (LR) alongside the optimizer, highlighting that Adam can serve as a reliable default optimizer due to its effectiveness over RMSprop. It also points out that while Adam and RMSprop can be used interchangeably in many scenarios, stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with momentum may require more time to converge to optimal solutions.

Finally, the significance of hyper-parameters in model training is reiterated, with LR identified as particularly crucial.
