The paper discusses the challenges associated with hyper-parameter selection, emphasizing that the reliance on empirical methods often leads to workable, rather than optimal, parameter sets, which diminishes the credibility of results.

The authors reference the no free lunch theorem, highlighting that no single optimization approach is universally superior, thus underscoring the necessity for expert knowledge in parameter selection to focus efforts effectively. Automated machine learning (AutoML) has emerged as a solution to automate the design and training of neural networks, albeit at the cost of significant computational resources.

Hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) is identified as a critical component of AutoML, tasked with finding the best hyper-parameters necessary for model training. These hyper-parameters, which remain fixed during training, influence the model's architecture and training efficiency. The paper traces the history of HPO back to the early 1990s, noting its increasing importance in the context of advancing machine learning applications.

The authors outline three primary goals of HPO: reducing the manual workload for AI experts, enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of neural network training, and improving the reliability of training outcomes. The necessity for HPO is further amplified by two trends in deep learning: the increasing complexity of models aimed at achieving higher accuracy, and the development of lightweight models that maintain performance with fewer parameters. These trends complicate the empirical tuning of hyper-parameters, reinforcing the essential role of HPO in contemporary machine learning practices.
