The paper provides a comprehensive review of hyper-parameter optimization, emphasizing its significance in both training processes and model structure. Key hyper-parameters discussed include momentum beta, mini-batch size, the number of hidden layers, learning rate decay, regularization lambda, activation functions, and Adam optimizers. 

Among these, the focus shifts to model design-related hyper-parameters: the number of hidden layers, regularization lambda, and activation functions. The number of hidden layers is crucial as it directly affects the network's ability to learn complex features and achieve higher accuracy. Increasing the depth of neural networks, such as transitioning from ResNet-18 to ResNet-200, allows for improved performance. 

The number of neurons in each hidden layer is also essential; insufficient neurons can lead to underfitting, while excessive neurons can cause overfitting and longer training times. The paper suggests specific guidelines for tuning the number of neurons, balancing between input and output layer sizes.

Regularization techniques are highlighted as vital for managing model complexity, particularly in scenarios with limited training data. These methods help prevent overfitting by introducing a regularization term that aids in feature selection. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of carefully selecting hyper-parameters to optimize model performance while maintaining efficiency.
