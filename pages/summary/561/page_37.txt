This paper provides a comprehensive review of hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) algorithms and applications, focusing on two open-source toolkits: NNI Tune and ApplicationToolkit for AutoML, HPO, and model compression. 

NNI Tune supports a wide range of state-of-the-art algorithms with a straightforward implementation process, allowing users to start experiments by importing configuration and search space files. In contrast, the ApplicationToolkit requires users to define a trainable class and make more modifications to their original code, although it does not necessitate individual files for configuration.

Both toolkits allow for visualization of results using TensorBoard, but NNI is noted for its superior web UI, enhancing user experience. Shared advantages of both NNI and Tune include ease of deployment, scalability, and support for advanced hyper-parameter searching and early stopping algorithms, surpassing offerings from services like Google Vizier and SageMaker. 

However, while Tune leverages existing libraries for search algorithms, requiring users to install relevant packages, NNI rewrites most algorithms, which may reduce dependency issues. Despite their strengths, both toolkits are not as user-friendly as closed-source alternatives, which may limit their appeal to some users. Overall, the paper highlights the balance of flexibility and usability in the context of open-source HPO frameworks.
