The paper reviews hyper-parameter optimization, particularly focusing on Gaussian Processes (GP) as surrogate models for Bayesian Optimization (BO). The mean function and covariance function, or kernel function, are crucial for defining the surrogate model's quality. Commonly used mean functions include zero, while the squared exponential and Matérn 5/2 kernels are popular due to their flexibility and ability to incorporate learnable length scale parameters.

GPs allow for uncertainty quantification in predictions, being nonparametric and dependent on input points. However, they face challenges, such as conceptual complexity and poor scalability with high-dimensional data. GPs also have a significant computational cost, with kernel matrix computations being resource-intensive.

Acquisition functions guide the selection of the next sampling point, balancing exploration and exploitation. The expected improvement (EI) is highlighted as a common acquisition function due to its effectiveness and simplicity. The paper emphasizes the importance of kernel choice and acquisition function selection in optimizing hyper-parameters effectively.
