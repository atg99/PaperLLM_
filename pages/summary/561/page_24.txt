The paper by Yu and Zhu discusses the application of algorithms for hyperparameter optimization (HPO), focusing on early-stopping strategies that can enhance computational efficiency. Early-stopping algorithms are likened to those used in neural network training, where they prevent overfitting by allowing users to terminate trials early. This is particularly useful for reallocating computational resources to more promising hyperparameter configurations.

Two main early-stopping strategies are highlighted:

1. **Median Stopping**: This straightforward policy is adopted by frameworks like Google Vizier, Tune, and NNI. It operates without a specific model and is applicable across various performance curves. The decision to stop a trial is based on whether the best objective value at a certain step is worse than the median of previously completed trials at that step. This method does not require users to define hyperparameters.

2. **Curve Fitting**: This method, which involves learning, predicting, and assessing, relies on regressing a performance curve from completed or partially completed trials to predict final objective values. A trial is halted if the predicted final objective value is significantly worse than the best historical performance. This approach often utilizes Bayesian parametric regression and incorporates a combination of multiple learning curves, which are parameterized and weighted to improve prediction accuracy. The process involves fitting curves, filtering outliers, and adjusting weights through Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling.

Overall, these strategies provide systematic ways to optimize HPO processes, allowing for effective early termination of trials based on performance predictions.
