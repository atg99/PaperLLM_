The paper discusses the advantages of mini-batch gradient descent (mini-batch GD) over traditional gradient descent and stochastic gradient descent (SGD). Mini-batch GD accelerates training, especially with large datasets, by reducing noise and increasing convergence probability. The mini-batch size, a crucial hyper-parameter, is recommended to be a power of 2 for optimal memory access, with 32 suggested as a default size. However, oscillation issues can arise with constant learning rates (LR), prompting suggestions to re-optimize mini-batch size and LR after fixing other hyper-parameters.

To address oscillation and improve convergence speed, the paper highlights SGD with momentum. This method enhances standard SGD by using exponentially weighted averages of gradients, thus directing the cost function more effectively. The momentum term, typically set between 0.9 and 0.999, helps reduce oscillation by promoting consistent updates in the same direction.

Additionally, the paper mentions RMSprop as a popular optimizer for deep neural networks, which improves on earlier methods like Adagrad and Adadelta. RMSprop effectively accelerates gradient descent as the steps become smaller, representing an evolution of Rprop for mini-batch updates. Overall, while mini-batch GD remains foundational, more advanced optimizers are increasingly favored in recent research.
