Despite advancements, the inherent complexity and challenges in parallel deployment remain significant hurdles for its widespread application.

Multi-armed bandit algorithms offer a systematic approach to hyper-parameter optimization but face limitations in sampling efficiency. Recent developments, such as the Successive Halving Algorithm (SHA) and Hyperband (HB), have introduced strategies to balance budget constraints and trial numbers more effectively. BOHB combines the strengths of Bayesian optimization with Hyperband to enhance performance through guided search and early stopping. Asynchronous Successive Halving Algorithm (ASHA) further improves distributed implementations, enabling trials to run as resources become available.

Population-Based Training (PBT) presents unique challenges due to its dynamic nature, where constant changes to the computation graph can complicate hyper-parameter optimization. This method struggles to identify optimal hyper-parameter sets as they may not remain stable throughout the training process.

In summary, while grid search remains a practical choice for limited hyper-parameter ranges, random search is favored in initial stages of hyper-parameter optimization for its simplicity. Bayesian optimization has seen significant improvements but still faces challenges in deployment, particularly in deep learning. Multi-armed bandit algorithms and PBT offer alternative strategies, each with their own strengths and limitations in the context of hyper-parameter optimization for deep learning networks.
