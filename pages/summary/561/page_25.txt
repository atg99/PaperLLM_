The paper provides a comprehensive review of hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) algorithms and their applications, focusing on methods that enhance the efficiency of model training in deep learning. It discusses a curve fitting approach for predicting the final objective value during the optimization process, where models are continuously trained and evaluated. If predictive results do not converge, further training occurs until either superior results are achieved or the process is halted. This method is compared to median stopping, highlighting its parameterized nature and the training involved in model building.

The paper also explores bandit-based algorithms, specifically Successive Halving (SHA) and HyperBand, which are recognized for their effectiveness in optimizing hyper-parameters while balancing accuracy and resource consumption. These algorithms utilize a random search sampling method alongside a bandit-based early stopping strategy, significantly improving resource efficiency compared to traditional methods that do not employ early stopping. Overall, the integration of these advanced techniques accelerates the optimization process and aids in identifying state-of-the-art networks.
