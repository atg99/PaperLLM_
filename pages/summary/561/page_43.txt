The paper provides a comprehensive review of hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) methods, particularly in the context of deep neural networks. It begins by discussing the significance of proper initialization in model training, highlighting that well-designed initializations can lead to better performance compared to random initializations. The paper emphasizes the role of transfer learning, especially in scenarios with large datasets like ImageNet, and notes its support by tools such as Google Vizier, which aids in utilizing prior knowledge for model training. 

Meta learning is introduced as a technique for predicting model performance based on hyper-parameter settings through surrogate models, which approximate the relationship between data and various fidelities. The review outlines the evolution of surrogate models, starting from linear regression to more complex models like support vector machines, Bayesian neural networks, and MultiLayer Perceptrons. The choice of surrogate model is crucial, as it impacts prediction accuracy and is influenced by the available sampling data.

The conclusion summarizes the motivations behind the study, particularly the rising use of deep neural networks. It highlights the systematic overview of HPO approaches, the introduction of key hyper-parameters, and a discussion of state-of-the-art algorithms and frameworks. The paper also compares different algorithms and evaluation methods regarding their efficiency for large models and resource consumption, serving as a valuable reference for researchers and practitioners in the field.
