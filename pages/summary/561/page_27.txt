This paper reviews hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) techniques, focusing on algorithms like HyperBand (HB) and its extensions, such as Asynchronous Successive Halving (ASHA) and Bayesian Optimization-HyperBand (BOHB). HB allows users to allocate a maximum amount of resources to trials and determine the proportion of trials to discard based on performance. A key parameter is the resource budget, R, which influences the number of combinations evaluated, with smaller values leading to quicker evaluations but potentially missing optimal configurations.

The paper highlights that HB is beneficial for parallel deployment, as trials are sampled randomly and run independently, improving resource allocation and efficiency compared to traditional random search and Bayesian optimization methods. ASHA enhances HB by addressing straggler issues and optimizing resource distribution without requiring additional hyper-parameters from users. BOHB combines Bayesian optimization with HB, introducing a guided sampling approach to improve trial success rates.

Despite the strengths of HB, the paper notes limitations related to random sampling in the later stages of HPO. It suggests that hybrid methods, particularly BOHB, effectively address these shortcomings and are widely adopted in practical applications. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of HPO methodologies, emphasizing the evolution and effectiveness of these algorithms in optimizing machine learning models.
