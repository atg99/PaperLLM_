The paper discusses hyper-parameter optimization, focusing on learning rate (LR) schedules used in deep learning models. It introduces linear decay and exponential decay as two prominent strategies for adjusting LR over time. 

For linear decay, the learning rate is defined by the equation lr = lr0 / (1 + kt), where lr0 is the initial learning rate and k is the decay rate, with time (t) representing either training time or the number of iterations. The paper highlights that a common practice is to reduce the LR by half every ten epochs or by 0.1 every twenty epochs.

In contrast, exponential decay is characterized by a more rapid decrease in LR initially, followed by a slower decline as convergence approaches, described by lr = lr0 * exp(-kt). The paper notes that similar to linear decay, the parameters can be adjusted based on the number of iterations or epochs, with typical values for epoch drop rate and reduction factor being epochsdrop = 10 and oor = 0.5, respectively.

The text emphasizes that while the initial LR and decay rates play a role, the deep learning model's sensitivity to these parameters is relatively low. The example of EfficientNet is provided to illustrate the complexities involved in selecting appropriate hyper-parameters, underscoring that these choices cannot be made solely by manual design.
