The paper provides an overview of hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) techniques, particularly focusing on surrogate models like Tree Parzen Estimators (TPE). TPE is a probabilistic model that structures the search space conditionally, allowing for the differentiation of hyper-parameters based on their performance. The model expresses the probability of hyper-parameters given the observed outcomes using a tree structure that divides observations into "good" and "bad" based on a defined threshold. This approach enables the maximization of the expected improvement acquisition function, enhancing the efficiency of the optimization process.

The paper notes that TPE has gained popularity over traditional Bayesian methods, especially in deep learning contexts where it accommodates various data types. However, Gaussian Processes (GPs) may outperform TPE in scenarios where hyper-parameters exhibit strong interactions, as TPE does not model these interactions.

Additionally, the paper discusses the importance of incorporating an early-stopping policy in HPO, given the computational demands of training models with different hyper-parameter sets. Early stopping mimics expert behavior to optimize resource allocation by evaluating ongoing trials and determining whether to continue or halt based on performance metrics. This strategy aims to streamline the HPO process, making it more efficient in practical applications.
