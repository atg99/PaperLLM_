The paper discusses hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) techniques, focusing on the limitations of Bayesian Optimization with Hyper-Band (BOHB) and the advantages of Population-Based Training (PBT). 

A key issue with BOHB is the challenge of setting appropriate budgets for optimization, which can hinder efficiency, especially for inexperienced users. The need for users to have prior experience complicates the effective use of BOHB, as improper budget settings can lead to inefficient searches or slow convergence to local optima. The paper highlights that while Bayesian methods may outperform random search in some cases, they can struggle with escaping local optima and require careful budget management, which can be time-consuming.

In contrast, PBT is introduced as a more effective population-based method that combines elements of genetic algorithms, such as initialization, selection, and reproduction. PBT stands out for its ability to adapt hyper-parameters during training and to conduct parallel search alongside sequential optimization. The process involves initializing a population of models with different hyper-parameter settings, evaluating them at regular intervals, and using the performance information to guide hyper-parameter updates through a balance of exploitation and exploration. This method is particularly suitable for deep learning applications due to its computational efficiency and adaptability.

Overall, the review emphasizes the significance of understanding the strengths and weaknesses of different HPO methods, particularly as they apply to the evolving landscape of machine learning and deep learning.
