The paper discusses the comparison and performance of various bandit-based algorithms for hyperparameter optimization (HPO), focusing on BOHB (Bayesian Optimization and Hyperband). It highlights the discard ratio, sampling methods, parallelization, scalability, and the timeline of the algorithms. BOHB utilizes a TPE method with improved multidimensional KDE to effectively handle interaction effects. It merges the advantages of Hyperband, such as budget control, performance monitoring, and computational efficiency, particularly in the early stages of tuning, with the robust final performance and efficiency provided by Bayesian optimization in later stages. Empirical results show that BOHB achieves state-of-the-art performance across various HPO tasks, demonstrating a significant speedup compared to random search, especially under different budget constraints. The findings indicate that BOHB excels with adequate budgets due to its Bayesian guided sampling strategy. The open-source implementation, HpBandSter, is available on GitHub, making BOHB a practical choice for HPO across diverse applications.
