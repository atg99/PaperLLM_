The paper by Yu and Zhu discusses various activation functions used in neural networks, highlighting their characteristics and applications. The sigmoid function is primarily used for binary classification, while the softmax function is suitable for multinomial classification, providing a probabilistic interpretation where the sum of probabilities equals one. The hyperbolic tangent (tanh) function is zero-centered and offers a wider output range compared to sigmoid, making it more efficient for learning, although it also suffers from the vanishing gradient problem. In recent years, the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) has become the most popular activation function, with several variants like leaky ReLU and PReLU gaining traction. Despite the emergence of more advanced functions, ReLU and its variants remain prevalent in neural network architectures.
