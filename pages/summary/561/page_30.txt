The paper discusses Population-Based Training (PBT) as an innovative approach that integrates hyperparameter optimization (HPO) into the training process of large models, such as generative adversarial networks (GANs) and transformer networks. PBT allows for the adjustment of hyperparameters during training, utilizing a warm start method that enhances efficiency compared to traditional HPO methods, especially for models that require long training times to reach convergence.

While PBT shows promise in optimizing adaptive hyperparameters without the need for users to manually choose decay methods, it has limitations. The algorithm lacks theoretical proof of achieving optimal hyperparameter sets, raising questions about whether the configurations it provides are indeed the best possible. Additionally, PBT faces challenges related to the balance between exploration and exploitation strategies and struggles with advanced evolution and mutation decisions. The complexity of defining hyperparameters and the adjustments to the computation graph further complicates its application.

The authors emphasize that algorithms serve as theoretical foundations for HPO, necessitating user decisions on which hyperparameters to focus on, the application of search algorithms, and the training process for deep learning models. This indicates a need for tools that can facilitate these configurations effectively.
