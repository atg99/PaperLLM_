This paper reviews hyper-parameter optimization techniques, focusing on their algorithms and applications in deep learning. It discusses dropout as a method to reduce overfitting by randomly deactivating neurons during training, suggesting a dropout rate between 20% and 50%. An excessive dropout rate may oversimplify the model, while a minimal rate has negligible effects. The paper advocates for larger learning rates with decay and increased momentum during dropout, as fewer active neurons necessitate more updates per batch.

The significance of activation functions in deep learning is emphasized, as they introduce nonlinearity, allowing neural networks to capture complex data patterns. Common activation functions reviewed include sigmoid, hyperbolic tangent, rectified linear units (ReLU), Maxout, and Swish. The paper notes that while sigmoid functions are prevalent due to their bounded output, they have fallen out of favor due to issues such as the vanishing gradient problem and being non-zero-centered, which can hinder the training of deeper networks. The exploration of automatic search techniques for optimal activation functions and their hyper-parameters is also mentioned, underscoring ongoing advancements in the field.
