The paper provides a comprehensive review of various hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) algorithms and their applicability to deep neural networks (DNNs). It evaluates several methods, including Grid Search, Random Search, Bayesian Optimization, Multi-armed Bandit methods, and Population-Based Training (PBT), highlighting their advantages and disadvantages.

Grid Search is noted for its simplicity and ability to run in parallel but struggles with the curse of dimensionality, making it suitable only for a limited number of hyper-parameters. Random Search also allows for parallelism and is easy to combine with early stopping methods, but it exhibits low efficiency and cannot guarantee optimal results, making it more convenient for preliminary stages.

Bayesian Optimization is recognized for its reliability and foundational role in other algorithms, although it faces challenges in parallelism and conceptual complexity. Multi-armed Bandit methods are lauded for their computational efficiency and simple concepts, making them a potential default choice due to their balance between budget and trial numbers.

PBT methods uniquely integrate hyper-parameter tuning with model training, allowing for parallel optimization and a dynamic adjustment of hyper-parameters based on model performance. However, it faces limitations in extending to advanced evolution scenarios and is most beneficial for computationally intensive models.

The paper emphasizes that while these methods are effective, each has inherent limitations when applied to DNNs, necessitating careful consideration when selecting an HPO approach.
