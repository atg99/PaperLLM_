This paper provides a comprehensive review of hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) algorithms and their applications. Key algorithms discussed include Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE), Bayesian Optimization (BO), multi-armed bandit algorithms, and Population-Based Training (PBT).

TPE is highlighted for its suitability with conditional hyper-parameters and its parallelization capabilities, making it effective for tuning convolutional neural networks and neural language models. BO and its variants are widely implemented in various auto-tuning frameworks, including Google Vizier and Sagemaker, indicating ongoing research in this area.

Multi-armed bandit algorithms are noted for their resource efficiency, particularly in massive parallel systems. The Asynchronous Successive Halving Algorithm (ASHA) is mentioned as outperforming many state-of-the-art algorithms in language processing and classification tasks. For sequential and distributed systems, BOHB is recommended for its efficiency and accuracy in tuning neural networks and reinforcement learning algorithms.

PBT is particularly suited for computationally intensive models and nonstationary hyper-parameters, allowing for hyper-parameter tuning during model training, which reduces the costs associated with traditional search methods.

The paper also discusses the evaluation of results in HPO, emphasizing the importance of balancing accuracy and speed. Direct evaluation, while the most accurate, is time-consuming, prompting the exploration of faster methods. Techniques like fidelity reduction and subsampling are proposed as alternatives, though subsampling carries risks of introducing noise and uncertainty due to the variability in optimal hyper-parameter choices across different datasets.

Overall, the paper underscores the significance of selecting appropriate algorithms and evaluation methods to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of hyper-parameter optimization in various machine learning contexts.
