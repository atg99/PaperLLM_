The paper introduces Domain Adaptive Ensemble Learning (DAEL), a novel framework aimed at enhancing the generalization capabilities of a multi-expert network for both unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) and domain generalization (DG). The framework addresses three critical challenges: utilizing pseudo-labels for unlabeled target data, measuring the effectiveness of non-expert ensembles against experts, and leveraging complementary information among domain-specific experts.

DAEL employs a collaborative approach where each source domain is treated as a pseudo-target domain, allowing for effective training of a non-expert ensemble. It utilizes the most confident predictions from experts as pseudo-labels for unlabeled data. Additionally, the effectiveness of the ensemble is evaluated based on its predictions in comparison to expert predictions, incorporating consistency regularization and various input augmentations to enhance learning.

The contributions of the paper include the introduction of DAEL, a simplified yet effective realization for multi-source UDA and DG, the creation of miniDomainNet for rapid experimentation, and the development of Dassl.pytorch, a unified implementation platform for benchmarking. Experimental results demonstrate that DAEL significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods across multiple datasets.

The related work section discusses various UDA techniques, including distance metrics and generative adversarial networks, highlighting how DAEL's multi-source approach distinguishes it from previous methods by enabling collaborative learning among domain-specific classifiers.
