The paper discusses the challenge of generalizing deep neural networks from multiple source domains to a target domain, focusing on two scenarios: multi-source unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) when unlabeled target data is available, and domain generalization (DG) when it is not. The authors introduce a framework called domain adaptive ensemble learning (DAEL) that addresses both problems by creating a model comprised of a shared convolutional neural network (CNN) feature extractor and multiple classifier heads, each specializing in a particular source domain.

DAEL's approach allows each classifier, or "domain expert," to focus on its own domain while leveraging complementary information from other domains in an ensemble manner. This collaborative learning aims to enhance performance on unseen target domains. The framework also addresses critical issues such as scalability, as training multiple models can be computationally intensive, and training challenges stemming from the absence of target domain data or labels.

Extensive experiments conducted on various multi-source UDA and DG datasets demonstrate that DAEL notably improves upon existing state-of-the-art methods in both UDA and DG, often achieving significant performance gains. The study underscores the potential of using domain-specific knowledge collaboratively to mitigate the effects of domain shift in machine learning models.
