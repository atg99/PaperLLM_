The paper presents a comparison of the proposed DAEL (Domain Adaptive Ensemble Learning) method against state-of-the-art techniques on DG (Domain Generalization) datasets PACS and Office-Home. DAEL outperforms traditional methods, achieving an average accuracy of 83.4% on PACS and 66.1% on Office-Home, demonstrating its effectiveness in domain adaptation.

Key design choices in DAEL are evaluated, revealing that a collaborative ensemble approach significantly enhances performance compared to individual expert learning. The ensemble method, utilizing the most confident expert predictions, reduces variance and leads to improved accuracy. The addition of different loss functions enhances individual expert performance, ultimately strengthening the ensemble's predictive capabilities.

The augmentation strategy employed in DAEL involves using weak augmentations for pseudo-label generation and strong augmentations for the ensemble's predictions. This approach mitigates overfitting and has been shown to be vital for maintaining accuracy. Comparisons with baseline models suggest that while strong augmentation plays a role, the core strength of DAEL lies in its collaborative ensemble learning mechanism.

Feature visualization through t-SNE indicates that DAEL achieves better alignment of target features with source features, resulting in clearer class separation compared to conventional methods. Overall, the findings highlight the importance of collaborative learning and effective augmentation strategies in enhancing domain generalization performance.
