Untrained neural networks lack physics priors, leading them to learn approximate physical laws directly from data, which often results in energy conservation errors over time. This paper explores the concept of Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs), inspired by Hamiltonian mechanics, to address this issue. HNNs parameterize the Hamiltonian—a function that relates the state of a system to conserved quantities—using a neural network, allowing it to be learned directly from data rather than being crafted manually.

The authors argue that while traditional approaches to predicting system dynamics rely on discrete time steps, a more effective method involves using differential equations to express dynamics continuously. This method has been under-explored, though Neural ODEs have made strides in this direction. Additionally, existing methods often fail to learn exact conservation laws, leading to drift in predictions. HNNs aim to resolve these problems by ensuring that energy-like quantities are conserved over time.

The paper highlights the historical significance of Hamiltonian mechanics, which provides a unified framework for classical mechanics and has applications across various fields of physics. It introduces the Hamiltonian function, which, when applied, facilitates the accurate prediction of a system's dynamics through the relationships between position and momentum. Overall, the proposed HNN model demonstrates improved performance in conserving energy-like quantities compared to baseline neural networks.
