The paper discusses the performance of a Hamiltonian Neural Network (HNN) in modeling dynamical systems, specifically focusing on its ability to conserve total energy compared to a baseline neural network model. In the two-body problem, the HNN demonstrated significantly lower training and testing losses, as well as a much lower mean squared error (MSE) for energy conservation, suggesting its effectiveness in maintaining energy stability despite deviations from the ground truth orbit.

The authors then extend their investigation to the chaotic three-body problem, noting that preliminary results indicate the HNN outperforms the baseline, although further improvements are required.

In a novel approach, the paper explores training HNNs directly from pixel data, exemplified by a pendulum dataset. Using OpenAI Gym's Pendulum-v0 environment, the authors generated 200 trajectories and processed the pixel observations to create a suitable input for the model. The architecture included a simple autoencoder with fully-connected layers, designed to capture the dynamics of the pendulum while observing both position and velocity.

The loss function for this experiment integrated three components: the HNN loss, a traditional autoencoder pixel loss, and an auxiliary loss for the autoencoder. The results indicate a promising direction for learning Hamiltonians directly from high-dimensional data, marking a significant advancement in the field.
