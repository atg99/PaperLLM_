In Task 3, the focus was on analyzing the performance of Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs) using data from a real pendulum, which was sourced from a study by Schmidt & Lipson. The dataset was more complex due to noise and the presence of friction, which meant that it did not strictly adhere to conservation laws. The objective was to evaluate the effectiveness of HNNs on this real-world data compared to traditional models.

The methodology involved training two types of fully-connected neural networks: a baseline model that outputs the time derivatives of position and momentum directly, and an HNN that estimates these derivatives based on the derivative of a scalar quantity. Both models were trained using a consistent learning rate and optimizer, and they were evaluated based on three metrics: L2 train loss, L2 test loss, and mean squared error (MSE) of the predicted total energy.

Results indicated that the baseline model exhibited a tendency to drift away from the true dynamics over time, whereas the HNN maintained a high level of accuracy. The MSE for the HNN remained stable, contrasting with the rapid divergence observed in the baseline model's performance. Furthermore, the HNN displayed a conserved quantity that closely mirrored the system's total energy, suggesting that it effectively preserved energy over extended periods, unlike the baseline model.

Overall, the findings highlight the advantages of HNNs in modeling dynamics with noisy and biased data, demonstrating their potential for learning conservation laws in real-world scenarios.
