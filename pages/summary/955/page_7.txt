The paper discusses the implementation of an auxiliary loss term in the context of an autoencoder's latent space, specifically aimed at ensuring that the second half of the latent vector closely resembles the derivatives of the first half. This approach is intended to align the latent vector's characteristics with those of canonical coordinates, which is essential for formulating a Hamiltonian. The auxiliary loss does not negatively impact the performance of the autoencoder and is applicable to any autoencoder with an even-sized latent space.

The results section highlights the performance of a Hamiltonian Neural Network (HNN) compared to a baseline model in predicting the dynamics of a pixel pendulum. The HNN effectively conserved a scalar quantity similar to the total energy of the system, enabling it to maintain accurate predictions over longer durations. In contrast, the baseline model exhibited a tendency to decay towards lower energy states. Both models achieved similar training and testing losses, but the HNN significantly outperformed the baseline in terms of energy metrics across various tasks. 

Quantitative results from five tasks demonstrate that while the HNN and the baseline perform comparably in loss metrics, the HNN's energy conservation capabilities lead to markedly better performance, underscoring its potential advantages in dynamic system prediction.
