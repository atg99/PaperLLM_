The paper discusses the application of Hamiltonian mechanics to learn a parametric function for the Hamiltonian, H, in Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs). It highlights that the symplectic gradient, derived from H, allows for the time evolution of a system while preserving total energy. The paper asserts that while Hamiltonian mechanics can model simple systems like mass-spring systems and pendulums, its advantages emerge in complex systems with multiple degrees of freedom, such as celestial mechanics and many-body quantum systems.

The authors propose that HNNs can learn conserved quantities directly from data in an unsupervised manner. During the forward pass, the model outputs a scalar energy-like value from input coordinates, then computes a loss based on gradients of this output concerning the inputs. This approach enables HNNs to learn conserved quantities analogous to total energy, maintaining properties such as reversibility and the ability to manipulate energy levels through integration along the gradient.

The paper details two tasks to investigate the empirical properties of HNNs: modeling a frictionless mass-spring system and a frictionless pendulum. For the mass-spring system, the Hamiltonian is defined, and initial coordinates are sampled with energies in a specified range, while for the pendulum, the Hamiltonian accounts for its nonlinear nature. Both tasks involve constructing training and test sets with added Gaussian noise to assess the performance of the HNNs in learning the dynamics of these systems.
