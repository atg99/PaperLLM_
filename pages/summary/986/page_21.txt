The paper discusses the effectiveness of domain-specific language model pretraining for biomedical natural language processing (NLP). It highlights the risks of overfitting when using original text for relation encoding, particularly when using the [CLS] token, while suggesting that dummification and the use of entity markers yield more reliable results. The authors emphasize the importance of self-supervised learning to address the challenges of obtaining labeled examples in supervised learning.

The paper critiques the assumption that mixed-domain pretraining is beneficial when there is ample in-domain text, as demonstrated in their experiments comparing clinical BERT and PubMedBERT. These findings suggest that related text may not enhance performance when sufficient biomedical text is available. The authors call for a distinction between different types of transfer learning and their applicability in various contexts.

Additionally, the paper addresses the lack of comprehensive benchmarks in biomedical NLP compared to general domains. To contribute to this field, the authors introduce BLURB, a benchmark with thirteen datasets across six tasks, including named entity recognition and question answering. Ultimately, the paper concludes that domain-specific pretraining can lead to superior performance over mixed-domain approaches, establishing new state-of-the-art results in biomedical NLP. The authors also release their advanced biomedical BERT models and introduce the BLURB leaderboard to facilitate ongoing research.
