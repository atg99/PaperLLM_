The paper investigates the effectiveness of BERT models and self-attention mechanisms in named entity recognition (NER) and relation extraction tasks, questioning the necessity of explicit sequential modeling. The authors demonstrate that a linear layer on top of BERT performs comparably to more complex architectures like bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM) for these tasks, indicating that the sequential nature of tagging schemes in NER is less critical when using BERT.

A comparative study of tagging schemes reveals minimal differences in performance across various biomedical NER tasks, suggesting that BERT's self-attention capability diminishes the need for intricate tagging systems. The authors evaluate the impact of entity dummification and relation encoding in relation extraction, highlighting the use of generic tags to anonymize entity mentions to mitigate overfitting risks associated with neural methods.

The study presents a systematic evaluation of different entity marking strategies—dummifying entities, using original text, or adding start and end tags—as well as various relation encoding approaches. The findings emphasize that while neural methods eliminate the need for handcrafted features, they may risk memorizing entity combinations, necessitating techniques like entity dummification to enhance generalization. Overall, the research underscores the shift in NER and relation extraction paradigms due to advancements in BERT and self-attention mechanisms.
