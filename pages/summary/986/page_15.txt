This paper investigates the effectiveness of domain-specific language model pretraining for biomedical natural language processing, particularly focusing on PubMedBERT. The authors compare their results with existing models such as BioBERT, SciBERT, and BlueBERT, finding comparable or superior performance on various tasks. 

An ablation study is conducted to evaluate the influence of different pretraining techniques, particularly the impact of vocabulary choice and whole-word masking (WWM). Results indicate that using an in-domain vocabulary from PubMed significantly improves performance compared to the original BERT vocabulary from Wikipedia and BookCorpus. WWM consistently enhances results, facilitating easier learning due to shorter input lengths.

The study also reveals that pretraining on general-domain text does not provide benefits for biomedical applications, even when using an appropriate vocabulary. In contrast, pretraining from scratch on PubMed yields better performance outcomes with less computational effort. Overall, the findings support the argument that domain-specific pretraining is crucial for effective biomedical NLP applications.
