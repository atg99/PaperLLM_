The paper discusses the pretraining details for various BERT models specifically tailored for biomedical natural language processing. It provides a summary of the vocabulary pretraining corpus and text sizes for models like BioBERT, SciBERT, ClinicalBERT, and PubMedBERT, highlighting their sources and the amount of text used. 

The authors conducted their training on a DGX-2 machine using 16 V100 GPUs, emphasizing that both cased and uncased versions perform similarly, thus focusing on uncased models. They employed whole-word masking with a 15% masking rate for the PubMedBERT model. 

For comparison, the paper references several existing models, indicating that BioBERT and BlueBERT perform continual pretraining from BERT, while ClinicalBERT is pre-trained from BioBERT, sharing the same vocabulary. They utilized BERT-BASE for pretraining PubMedBERT, noting that BERT-LARGE may offer improved performance, which they plan to explore further in future work.

The task-specific fine-tuning utilized the Adam optimizer with a slanted triangular learning rate schedule and a dropout probability of 0.1. The authors reported average performance scores from multiple runs across various datasets, acknowledging variability due to random initialization and dropout. They conducted hyperparameter tuning using a subset of models, opting for a common set of hyperparameters that yielded satisfactory results across both in-domain and out-of-domain scenarios, despite the computational expense of exhaustive tuning.

Overall, the paper provides insights into the methodologies and considerations taken in the development of biomedical language models, emphasizing the importance of pretraining and fine-tuning strategies.
