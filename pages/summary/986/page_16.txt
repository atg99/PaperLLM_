This paper discusses the advantages of domain-specific pretraining in natural language processing, particularly in biomedical contexts. It presents empirical evidence showing that models like PubMedBERT, which utilize a specialized vocabulary from PubMed abstracts, outperform general-domain models such as BioBERT. The figures illustrate how attention mechanisms differ between these models, highlighting that BioBERT fails to focus on relevant words due to its reliance on general vocabulary, which leads to incorrect predictions. The authors also explore the impact of incorporating full-text articles from PubMed Central into the pretraining dataset, noting that while the dataset size increased significantly, it resulted in a slight decline in performance, emphasizing the importance of maintaining domain specificity in training data.
