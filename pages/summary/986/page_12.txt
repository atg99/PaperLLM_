The paper discusses methods for processing and classifying biomedical text using BERT-based models. It outlines various tasks such as featurization for relation instances, sentence similarity, document classification, and question answering, detailing the input transformations, featurizers, and classification layers for each task.

For featurization, relation instances can either use a [CLS] token or concatenate mention representations, with pooling methods applied for entities with multiple tokens. Sentence similarity is approached as a regression task, employing [CLS] and [SEP] tokens to separate sentence pairs. Document classification utilizes a [CLS] token to determine category relevance for text spans. Question answering follows a similar structure to sentence similarity, using appropriate tokens to process questions and reference texts.

The experimental settings involve domain-specific pretraining on a large dataset of PubMed abstracts, where the vocabulary is generated from 14 million abstracts totaling 3.2 billion words. The pretraining follows a standard procedure using a slanted triangular learning rate schedule with the Adam optimizer, conducted over 62,500 steps with a batch size of 8,192.

This structured approach aims to enhance the performance of various biomedical NLP tasks by leveraging the capabilities of BERT in handling complex text representations.
