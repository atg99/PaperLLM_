The paper evaluates the impact of pretraining methods on the performance of PubMedBERT across various biomedical NLP tasks, as summarized in Table 10. The results indicate variations in performance based on the pretraining corpus used: standard PubMed abstracts, a combination of PubMed abstracts and PMC full text, and an extended training on the latter. While some tasks showed improvement with the inclusion of PMC texts, others experienced a decline, attributed to the noisier nature of full texts and the need for additional training cycles to leverage this information effectively.

Additionally, the paper explores the effects of adversarial pretraining, revealing that it generally leads to slight performance degradation, with the exception of sentence similarity tasks. This outcome suggests that adversarial training may be more beneficial with more diverse and out-domain pretraining data. The study also includes an ablation analysis on fine-tuning methods, focusing on task-specific adaptations while maintaining the standard PubMedBERT model. Historically, standard NLP approaches relied on sequential labeling methods, such as conditional random fields, but the current success of neural language models marks a shift in methodology.
