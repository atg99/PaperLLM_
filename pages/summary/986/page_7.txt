The paper discusses the development of a benchmark called BLURB for biomedical natural language processing (NLP), focusing on various tasks such as named entity recognition (NER), evidence-based medical information extraction, relation extraction, sentence similarity, document classification, and question answering. The authors emphasize the importance of selecting appropriate datasets for pretraining domain-specific language models in the biomedical field and highlight the datasets used in BLURB, which include NER datasets like BC5-chem, BC5-disease, and others, along with relation extraction datasets such as ChemProt and DDI.

To evaluate the performance of models on the BLURB benchmark, the authors propose a methodology that involves grouping datasets by task type and calculating the average score for each type to avoid bias towards simpler tasks. The paper also mentions that while previous research has explored complex question-answering tasks, the current focus is on simpler classification tasks for clarity and ease of evaluation. The authors provide a comprehensive overview of the datasets included in the benchmark and aim to support the advancement of research in biomedical NLP by releasing the BLURB benchmark and a corresponding leaderboard.
