The paper discusses the problem formulation and modeling choices for various natural language processing (NLP) tasks within the biomedical domain. It highlights how these tasks can typically be framed as classification or regression problems, with a focus on either individual tokens or sequences as prediction targets. Key tasks such as Named Entity Recognition (NER), PICO element extraction, relation extraction, sentence similarity, document classification, and question answering are examined in detail.

For NER, the task involves recognizing entities in a text span, usually using a sequential labeling approach. The standard tagging scheme is BIO, which differentiates tokens as beginning, inside, or outside of an entity, with variations like BIOUL and IO also considered. Classification can be executed through methods such as linear layers, LSTM, or CRF.

The PICO task is compared to slot filling, where tokens are classified into participant, intervention, and outcome elements, similarly using tagging schemes like BIO and classification methods like linear layers or LSTM.

Relation extraction focuses on identifying binary relations between entity pairs within a text, with various entity and relation representations employed to mitigate overfitting.

Overall, the paper provides a structured overview of standard NLP tasks, their formulations, and the modeling choices that can be made for effective biomedical natural language processing.
