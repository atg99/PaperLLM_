The paper discusses the limitations of continual pretraining in language models, particularly when initialized with suboptimal parameters from general-domain models. It presents evidence that domain-specific pretraining, especially when using in-domain vocabulary, yields significant advantages over mixed-domain pretraining methods. The authors introduce BLURB, a new benchmark designed to improve the assessment of biomedical natural language processing (NLP). They highlight the shortcomings of existing benchmarks like BLUE, particularly its limited coverage of biomedical applications and its mixing of PubMed-based and clinical datasets, which can lead to poor performance in specific tasks. BLURB aims to provide a focused evaluation framework for PubMed-based biomedical applications, facilitating better comparisons and advancements in the field of biomedical NLP.
