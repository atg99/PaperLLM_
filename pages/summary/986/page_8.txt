This paper discusses several biomedical text corpora used for various natural language processing tasks, particularly in the extraction of medical information. 

The NCBI-Disease corpus includes 793 PubMed abstracts with 6892 annotated disease mentions linked to 790 distinct disease entities, utilizing a pre-processed set of train, development, and test splits. The BC2GM corpus focuses on gene mentions in sentences from PubMed abstracts, originally comprising 15000 training and 5000 test sentences, with a pre-processed version used for analysis. The JNLPBA corpus targets entity recognition in molecular biology, encompassing protein, DNA, RNA, cell line, and cell type mentions, with a simplified approach to entity type distinctions.

The Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM) PICO corpus contains PubMed abstracts from clinical trials annotated with Participants, Intervention, and Outcome elements, totaling 4300 training, 500 development, and 200 test abstracts. The F1 score is calculated at the word level for each PIO element, with attention to overlapping elements in the annotations.

For relation extraction, the ChemProt corpus identifies chemical-protein interactions in PubMed abstracts, focusing on five high-level interactions while expanding the training and development sets with false labels for unannotated pairs. The Drug-Drug Interaction (DDI) corpus facilitates research on pharmaceutical interactions, featuring sentence-level annotations of drug interactions.

The paper emphasizes the importance of these datasets in advancing biomedical information extraction through structured annotations and standardized processing methods.
