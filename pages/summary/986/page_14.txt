In this section, the authors evaluate the effectiveness of domain-specific pretraining in biomedical natural language processing (NLP) applications. They compare domain-specific pretraining with mixed-domain pretraining using standard task-specific models across various tasks in the BLURB benchmark. The evaluation covers different pretraining strategies, including vocabulary choices, whole-word masking, and adversarial pretraining.

The results presented in Table 6 indicate that PubMedBERT, which is pretrained exclusively on biomedical text, consistently outperforms other BERT models across most tasks, demonstrating significant advantages particularly over models pretrained with out-domain data. Models like RoBERTa, despite having a larger pretraining corpus, performed poorly in biomedical tasks, similar to the original BERT model. The analysis shows that including mixed out-domain data, even when it is somewhat relevant, does not enhance performance, as seen with ClinicalBERT and BlueBERT. 

BioBERT, which also utilizes PubMed text for pretraining, performs closely to PubMedBERT, but the latter's specific domain-focused pretraining results in better performance across most tasks. The findings highlight the importance of domain-specific pretraining for achieving superior outcomes in biomedical NLP applications.
