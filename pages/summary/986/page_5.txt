The paper discusses the advantages of domain-specific pretraining for biomedical natural language processing (NLP), particularly advocating for the strategy of pretraining from scratch rather than using mixed-domain approaches. The authors argue that given the vast amount of biomedical literature available, particularly over thirty million abstracts in PubMed, a tailored pretraining method is more effective.

Key points include the comparison of vocabularies across different models: BERT, SciBERT, and the authors’ model, PubMedBERT. The analysis shows that standard BERT models lack a sufficient biomedical vocabulary, leading to fragmentation of terms into subword pieces that lack meaningful biomedical context. For instance, the term "naloxone" is broken into four pieces by BERT, while "acetyltransferase" is divided into seven. In contrast, both terms are included in the vocabulary of PubMedBERT.

Additionally, the paper notes that domain-specific pretraining allows for the language model to be trained solely on in-domain data, thus avoiding the need to balance between biomedical and unrelated domains, which can dilute the model's effectiveness for biomedical applications. This focus on in-domain data is posited to enhance model performance in downstream tasks by eliminating issues related to out-of-domain training and vocabulary fragmentation.
