The paper discusses the effectiveness of domain-specific language model pretraining for biomedical natural language processing (NLP). It presents a comparative analysis of average input lengths using general-domain vocabulary versus in-domain vocabulary across various biomedical tasks. Results show that in-domain vocabulary generally leads to better performance metrics across datasets such as BC5-chem, BC5-disease, and others.

In evaluating the impact of pretraining corpora and duration on performance, the study finds that pretraining on Wiki and Books followed by PubMed abstracts enhances results, particularly when the pretraining duration is extended. Specifically, extending pretraining for 60% longer results in improvements that slightly surpass those of the standard PubMedBERT model, which relies solely on abstracts. However, the gains are variable across different tasks, indicating that while some tasks benefit significantly, others show mixed results.

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of using domain-specific data for pretraining in biomedical NLP, suggesting that tailored approaches can yield superior performance compared to general-domain methods.
