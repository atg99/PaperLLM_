This paper evaluates two paradigms for neural language model pretraining: the mixed-domain approach and domain-specific pretraining from scratch. The mixed-domain paradigm, which is commonly used, assumes that out-domain text can enhance pretraining by initializing with a general-domain language model. In contrast, the domain-specific approach utilizes only in-domain text for pretraining and develops its vocabulary from that text. The authors argue that for fields like biomedicine, where there is a wealth of available text, domain-specific pretraining from scratch can significantly outperform the mixed-domain method. 

The paper questions the prevailing assumption that out-domain text is beneficial, noting that mixed-domain pretraining can be seen as a form of transfer learning. Successful transfer learning typically relies on the target data being scarce and the source domain being closely related. However, in domains rich in data, such as biomedicine, the substantial differences between general and biomedical texts may lead to negative transfer, which could impair performance. The findings suggest that relying solely on in-domain text is a more effective strategy for specialized domains.
