The paper discusses the performance of PubMedBERT in biomedical natural language processing through various pretraining and fine-tuning techniques. The results are presented in tables comparing different models and approaches across various biomedical tasks.

Table 11 shows the performance of PubMedBERT with standard and adversarial pretraining on multiple datasets, revealing that adversarial pretraining does not significantly enhance performance in most cases. Notably, BC5-chem achieved the highest F1 score of 93.33, while PubMedQA had the lowest at 55.84.

Table 12 compares the effectiveness of linear layers versus Bi-LSTM networks for task-specific fine-tuning in named entity recognition and relation extraction, with results indicating that linear layers generally perform comparably or slightly better in most tasks.

Table 13 evaluates the impact of different tagging schemes (BIO, BIOUL, IO) on entity-level F1 scores for biomedical named entity recognition. The results show minimal variation among the schemes for BC5-chem, BC5-disease, and JNLPBA, with BIOUL performing slightly better for BC5-chem.

The paper emphasizes the importance of model selection and pretraining strategies in enhancing the performance of neural networks for biomedical NER and relation extraction tasks.
