This paper investigates the effectiveness of domain-specific pretraining for biomedical natural language processing (NLP) by comparing it to the continual pretraining of generic language models. The authors found that pretraining from scratch using domain-specific data significantly surpasses the performance of mixed-domain approaches, challenging the common belief that such mixed-domain pretraining is always beneficial.

To support their investigation, the authors compiled a comprehensive benchmark of biomedical NLP tasks from publicly available datasets, allowing for detailed analysis of different modeling strategies during pretraining and fine-tuning. Their findings indicate that domain-specific pretraining establishes a strong foundation for biomedical NLP, achieving state-of-the-art results across various tasks. Notably, the study also reveals that simpler label representations, such as IO tagging, can be effective for named entity recognition tasks using transformer-based models like BERT, contrary to the conventional use of more complex BIO tags.

To aid further research in biomedical NLP, the authors have made their advanced pretrained models and a benchmark leaderboard publicly available. The paper also outlines the methodology of language model pretraining, focusing on vocabulary generation through subword units using algorithms like Byte-Pair Encoding and WordPiece, and discussing the transformer architecture which underpins modern neural language models. The use of self-supervised learning techniques, such as the Masked Language Model employed by BERT, is highlighted as a significant advancement in pretraining methodologies.
