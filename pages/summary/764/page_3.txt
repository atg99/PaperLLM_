The paper discusses efforts to improve a Transformer model for automatic speech recognition (ASR) using data augmentation techniques due to issues of overfitting observed when training on 281K sentences. Two main augmentation methods were employed: enabling dropout during inference with a pre-trained Jasper model and applying the Cutout technique to perturb the training data. After these augmentations, the dataset was refined to about 2.5 million training examples by removing duplicates and sentences with a word error rate (WER) exceeding 0.5. The effectiveness of these techniques was validated through an ablation study, indicating that sentences generated with dropout and Cutout significantly enhanced performance.

The Transformer model configurations included parameters such as 768 hidden units, 12 attention heads, and a dropout rate of 0.25, trained with the NovoGrad optimizer for up to 300,000 steps. The vocabulary comprised 30K WordPieces from BERT, facilitating the transfer of pre-trained weights. Variations in weight initialization were also explored, comparing random initialization with pre-trained BERT weights. Results showed that models with BERT-initialized encoders outperformed both the baseline Jasper and models with randomly initialized weights, particularly on challenging "other" datasets, although they struggled on "clean" data. Overall, the findings suggest that effective data augmentation and appropriate weight initialization can significantly enhance ASR model performance.
