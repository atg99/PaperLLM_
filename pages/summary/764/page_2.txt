The paper presents a model for automatic speech recognition (ASR) utilizing a baseline model known as Jasper, which is a deep convolutional end-to-end architecture. Jasper processes mel-filter bank features derived from audio inputs and employs Connectionist Temporal Classification loss for training. The specific variant used is Jasper-DR-10x5, comprising 10 blocks of 5 sub-blocks, incorporating techniques akin to DenseNet for improved feature propagation. The training leverages the Novograd optimizer and is implemented in PyTorch within the NeMo toolkit.

For re-scoring ASR outputs, the paper discusses the use of language models (LMs), specifically a 6-gram KenLM and a Transformer-XL model. The LMs estimate the joint probability of sequences in a text corpus, with beam search employed to evaluate hypotheses based on a combination of acoustic and language model scores. The Transformer-XL model complements KenLM, providing additional scoring without replacing it, due to the computational expense associated with its slower inference.

The proposed ASR correction model adopts a Transformer encoder-decoder architecture, commonly utilized in neural machine translation. Key parameters include the number of layers, hidden size, and attention heads. The model employs dropout techniques for regularization and offers two weight initialization methods: random initialization or leveraging pre-trained BERT weights.

Experiments utilize the LibriSpeech dataset, comprising extensive transcribed speech data. The dataset is divided into multiple training parts, with evaluation performed on designated development and test datasets. To train the Transformer ASR correction model, the authors implemented a cross-validation approach, generating ASR predictions from multiple trained Jasper models to create a parallel corpus of predictions and ground truth transcripts.
