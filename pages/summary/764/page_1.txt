In this paper, the authors present a Transformer-based post-processing model aimed at improving the output of automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems. The model functions as a corrective mechanism, translating the potentially erroneous outputs of ASR into grammatically and semantically accurate text. The study emphasizes the importance of data augmentation and the use of pre-trained weights to enhance model performance.

The introduction discusses the evolution of ASR, highlighting the shift from traditional hybrid systems to end-to-end models that streamline training and deployment. While ASR models often utilize external language models for re-scoring, the authors note the limitations of traditional models, which can propagate errors if the initial words are misrecognized. To mitigate these issues, the authors propose a conditional language model that operates similarly to neural machine translation, correcting errors in ASR output.

The authors differentiate their approach from previous work by employing a Transformer architecture, which does not rely on complementary text-to-speech models for data generation. To address overfitting due to limited data, they introduce self-complementary regularization techniques, including augmenting training data with perturbed ASR outputs and initializing model weights with a pre-trained BERT language model.

The model was evaluated on the LibriSpeech dataset, using the Jasper DR 10x5 ASR module as a baseline. Results show that the correction model significantly reduces word error rates compared to both the baseline and re-scoring with a 6-gram language model, approaching the performance of the more advanced Transformer-XL model. The findings demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in enhancing ASR outputs.
