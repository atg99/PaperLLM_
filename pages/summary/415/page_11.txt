The paper discusses the YOLO (You Only Look Once) model, specifically its architecture and the Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) technique used for object detection. YOLO operates on a grid system, dividing the input image into a 3x3 grid and predicting objects across three classes. The NMS process is crucial for refining the detection results by eliminating redundant bounding boxes that either do not contain objects or overlap significantly with others, based on a defined intersection over union (IOU) threshold.

The architecture of YOLO, referred to as Darknet, is designed to process all features of the input image efficiently. It includes two fully connected layers dedicated to bounding box predictions. The model was tested using the Pascal VOC dataset, utilizing parameters such as S=7, B=2, and C=20, resulting in a final output size of 7x7x(2*5+20). This reflects the model's ability to predict bounding boxes and class probabilities within the specified grid cells.
