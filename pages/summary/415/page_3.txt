This thesis by Do Thuan, completed in Spring 2021 at Oulu University of Applied Sciences, explores the evolution of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) algorithm, particularly focusing on YOLOv5 as a state-of-the-art object detection method. Object detection, a key area in computer vision, involves identifying and classifying objects within images. The YOLO algorithm, introduced in 2015, revolutionized this field by framing detection as a regression problem and processing it through a single neural network. This approach led to significant advancements over the past decade.

The research details the progression of the YOLO algorithm through five versions, highlighting that YOLOv5, while not developed by the original creators, outperforms its predecessor YOLOv4 in both accuracy and speed. The study further examines innovative techniques incorporated into YOLOv5 and previous versions. The author utilized the Colab platform to implement object detection using the Global Wheat dataset, which comprises 3432 images of wheat. The results of the YOLOv5 model are evaluated and fine-tuned for enhancement.

Key concepts include machine learning, artificial intelligence, and the use of Python and Pytorch in the implementation of the YOLO algorithm.
