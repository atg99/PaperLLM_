The paper discusses the evolution of the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) and its enhanced version, the Path Aggregation Network (PAN). The original FPN architecture utilizes a top-down pathway, allowing large-scale detectors to access both semantic features from high-level layers and fine-grained features from low-level layers. However, small-scale detectors in FPN primarily rely on semantic features, limiting their effectiveness.

To address this limitation, PAN introduces the concept of concatenating semantic and fine-grained features at high-level layers, thereby improving performance for small and medium-scale detectors. The architecture incorporates bottom-up path augmentation and adaptive feature pooling to facilitate this integration.

The paper highlights a challenge in deep neural networks, particularly those with over 100 layers, where fine-grained features must traverse a lengthy path from low-level to high-level layers. This long pathway can hinder the efficiency of feature utilization within the FPN framework.
