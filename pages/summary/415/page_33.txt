The paper discusses advancements in object detection frameworks, specifically focusing on the modifications made to the PAN (Path Aggregation Network) for YOLOv4. It highlights that in traditional Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN), predictions are made independently at various scales, potentially leading to duplicated predictions and underutilization of feature map information. In contrast, PAN enhances this by integrating all output feature maps through Region of Interest (ROI) Align and fully connected layers using an element-wise max operation, which aggregates the variability of feature maps for improved predictions.

Additionally, the paper describes the head of the YOLOv3 model, which functions as a one-stage detector to make dense predictions. The head generates a prediction vector that includes bounding box coordinates, confidence scores, and class probabilities. YOLOv4 retains the same head as YOLOv3, employing anchor-based detection methods and offering three levels of detection granularity.
