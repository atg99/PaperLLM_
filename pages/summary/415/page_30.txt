The paper discusses the importance of maintaining spatial dimensions and extracting significant features during the processing of input images in object detection models such as YOLO. Traditional max-pooling methods, specifically 3-scale max-pooling, may not adequately retain crucial features, which can hinder training effectiveness. To address this, a new Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) block has been adapted for YOLO, enhancing feature extraction while preserving spatial dimensions.

In the neck feature aggregation phase of YOLOv3, the paper highlights the use of the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) architecture. As images pass through the backbone, they evolve into semantical features, with increased complexity but reduced spatial resolution. This reduction can result in a loss of fine-grained details essential for accurate detection. To mitigate this issue, the FPN employs a top-down approach to combine high-level semantical features with low-level fine-grained features. This integration aids in improving the model's ability to predict small objects effectively, illustrating the significance of feature aggregation in enhancing detection performance.
