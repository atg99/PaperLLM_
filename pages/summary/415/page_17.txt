The paper discusses the use of the k-means clustering algorithm to optimize anchor boxes for object detection in the YOLO (You Only Look Once) framework. Instead of manually selecting anchor boxes, the authors applied k-means clustering on the training set's bounding boxes, focusing on those with similar shapes. They calculated the average Intersection over Union (IOU) with the closest centroid rather than using Euclidean distance. Through experimentation with different values of k, they found that k=5 provided an effective balance between recall and model complexity. Results from the VOC and COCO datasets illustrated this trade-off, displaying five centroids as anchor boxes.

Furthermore, the paper highlights a limitation in YOLOv1, where bounding box predictions can be unstable due to unrestricted placement across the image. This instability arises from random initialization of parameters, causing bounding boxes to be predicted far from their corresponding grid cells. To address this issue, YOLOv2 implemented a sigmoid function to constrain the bounding box center coordinates within the range of 0 to 1, ensuring that predictions are more closely aligned with the responsible grid cells.
