The paper discusses two concepts of architectural object detection, highlighting the commonality in the approach where input images are processed through a feature extractor, known as the Backbone, before being forwarded to the object detector, which includes the Detection Neck and Detection Head. The Detection Neck aggregates features from the Backbone to prepare for the detection process. 

The primary distinction lies in the function of the Detection Head, which is responsible for localization and classification of bounding boxes. Two-stage detectors handle these tasks separately, resulting in Sparse Detection, whereas one-stage detectors, like YOLO, perform both tasks simultaneously, leading to Dense Detection. YOLO, which stands for "You Only Look Once," exemplifies a one-stage detector.

Furthermore, the authors of YOLOv4 conducted extensive experiments incorporating advanced innovations in computer vision across various components of the architecture.
