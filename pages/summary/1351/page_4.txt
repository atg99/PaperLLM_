This paper addresses the challenges associated with the acquisition, transmission, and processing of large volumes of samples that may not be useful, leading to excessive resource consumption. The authors propose semantics-empowered policies aimed at reducing or eliminating uninformative sample updates, thereby enhancing network resource efficiency and scalability.

The evaluation of these policies is based on two scenarios: slowly changing sources and rapidly changing sources, each analyzed with varying probabilities of successful transmission. The performance metrics include average real-time reconstruction error and average cost of actuation error. 

In the case of slow variations, the semantics-aware policy significantly outperforms the change-aware policy, effectively minimizing discrepancies between the original and reconstructed states, even under low channel quality conditions. Conversely, for rapidly changing sources, both semantics-aware and change-aware policies demonstrate strong performance in reconstruction error, with the semantics-aware approach yielding the lowest actuation error while avoiding the transmission of uninformative samples.

The conclusion emphasizes the benefits of goal-oriented data generation and communication policies in remote real-time tracking and actuation, highlighting the potential for reduced error metrics and decreased unnecessary traffic through an understanding of information semantics.
