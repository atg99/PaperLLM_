The stationary distribution of the two-state discrete-time Markov chain (DTMC) is characterized by π0, the probability of the system being synced, and π1, the probability of being in an erroneous state.

The cost of actuation error is a crucial performance metric, recognizing that not all errors carry the same weight. The model captures the costs associated with being in different states during actuation, distinguishing between non-commutative errors, where the cost of transitions is not symmetric. The average cost of actuation error can be expressed as a function of the stationary distribution of the two-dimensional DTMC.

Section 4 introduces goal-oriented sampling and communication policies designed for real-time reconstruction of a Markov source. Several policies are compared, starting with a uniform policy that samples periodically without regard for the source's temporal evolution, which may lead to missed transitions. The age-aware policy triggers sampling based on the age of information, while the change-aware policy prompts updates upon detecting state changes. The semantics-aware policy builds on this by monitoring discrepancies between the receiver and transmitter states to decide when to sample and transmit.

The transition matrix for the DTMC is derived, providing insights into the probability of the system being in an erroneous state. Ultimately, while frequent sampling and transmission could optimize real-time reconstruction, it would also demand more resources, highlighting a trade-off between performance and resource efficiency.
