This paper discusses the pre-training and evaluation of BERTje, a model designed for Dutch natural language processing tasks. The authors employed a masking strategy where 15% of tokens are masked, with 80% replaced by a mask token, 10% by random tokens, and 10% left unchanged. BERTje underwent pre-training for 1 million iterations, with fine-tuning assessed at the 850k iterations checkpoint to understand the impact of training duration on performance.

The evaluation includes several NLP tasks using annotated datasets from three sources. The first source is the Dutch CoNLL-2002 dataset for named-entity recognition (NER), which consists of four classes: person, organization, location, and miscellaneous. The second source is the Lassy Small treebank for part-of-speech (POS) tagging, involving 16 universal POS tags. The third source is the SoNaR-1 corpus, which provides a variety of classification tasks, including token-level NER, coarse and fine-grained POS tags, semantic role annotations, and spatio-temporal relations.

The authors highlight that while these tasks focus on low-level linguistic information, they also sought to evaluate BERTje on a high-level task, specifically sentiment analysis, using the Dutch Book Reviews Dataset, which contains balanced positive and negative reviews.

Results indicate that BERTje, particularly at the 850k checkpoint, outperformed multilingual BERT in NER tasks across the CoNLL-2002 and SoNaR-1 datasets. However, it did not surpass the state-of-the-art score of 90.9% on the CoNLL-2002 test data achieved by a large multilingual BERT model. The findings suggest that a monolingual Dutch BERT model could potentially exceed current performance benchmarks.
