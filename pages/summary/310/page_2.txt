This paper discusses the development of BERTje, a Dutch language model designed to enhance NLP research in Dutch by utilizing a multi-genre dataset for pre-training. The authors highlight that existing models for various languages, including Italian, French, German, Finnish, and Japanese, are often trained on limited datasets, such as Twitter or Wikipedia, which may not provide a comprehensive representation of the language.

BERTje was created based on the BERT BASE architecture, featuring 12 transformer blocks, but it was pre-trained on a diverse and high-quality dataset totaling 12GB of uncompressed text. This dataset includes contemporary and historical novels, a Dutch news corpus, a multi-genre reference corpus, web news articles, and a Wikipedia dump. The authors ensured that the data was cleaned and that there was no overlap with test datasets to maintain quality.

The pre-training process involved modifications to the original BERT objectives. Instead of using the next sentence prediction (NSP) task, which had shown to be ineffective, BERTje employs the sentence order prediction (SOP) task. Additionally, the masked language modeling (MLM) objective was adjusted to mask consecutive word pieces, improving the model's ability to predict words more effectively.

Overall, the paper outlines the methodology behind creating BERTje and its potential to outperform existing models, particularly multilingual BERT, in various Dutch NLP tasks.
