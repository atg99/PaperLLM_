The paper presents BERTje, a Dutch BERT model developed using the transformer architecture to enhance performance on natural language processing (NLP) tasks. Unlike the multilingual BERT model, which primarily relies on Wikipedia text, BERTje is trained on a more extensive and diverse dataset consisting of 2.4 billion tokens. This model demonstrates superior performance over the multilingual counterpart on various NLP tasks, including part-of-speech tagging, named-entity recognition, semantic role labeling, and sentiment analysis.

The introduction outlines a shift in computational linguistics from creating task-specific models to employing large general-purpose language models like BERT. BERT's pre-training involves two main tasks: masked language modeling (MLM), which focuses on learning word embeddings based on context, and next sentence prediction (NSP), aimed at understanding inter-sentence coherence. However, NSP has been critiqued for its ineffectiveness, as it tends to emphasize topic similarity rather than coherence. Subsequent models like RoBERTa and ALBERT have adapted this approach, with RoBERTa omitting NSP and ALBERT replacing it with a sentence order prediction task.

The paper argues for the benefits of monolingual models over multilingual ones, particularly for languages like Dutch, as they are more likely to capture the nuances of general language use rather than being confined to specific domains like Wikipedia. The authors made BERTje publicly available, contributing to advancements in NLP for the Dutch language.
