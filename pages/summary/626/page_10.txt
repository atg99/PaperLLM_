The paper addresses the challenges associated with the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal deep neural networks, particularly in the context of visual question answering (VQA). It highlights how existing models often prioritize certain modalities over others, leading to suboptimal learning outcomes. Key contributions from various studies are discussed, emphasizing the need for better integration and understanding of cross-modal interactions. 

The authors reference foundational works on deep learning architectures, normalization techniques, and multimodal fusion, illustrating the evolution of strategies to improve model robustness and performance. They argue that traditional approaches may overlook the complexity of interactions between different modalities, which can hinder the effectiveness of VQA systems. 

The paper suggests that overcoming these limitations requires innovative methods for combining modalities, as well as adaptive learning strategies that balance the contributions of each modality. By addressing the greedy tendencies in learning, the authors advocate for a more holistic approach to multi-modal deep learning that enhances the understanding and utilization of diverse data sources.
