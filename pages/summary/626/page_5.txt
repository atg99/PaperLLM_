This paper discusses the challenges of greedy learning in multi-modal deep neural networks (DNNs) and proposes methods to characterize and overcome these challenges. The parameters of the uni-modal branches are denoted as θ0 and θ1, while critical parameters from fusion modules are labeled as θ′0 and θ′1. The authors introduce the concept of conditional learning speed, defined as the log-ratio of learning speeds between different modalities after a set number of training steps. This metric serves as an indicator of how effectively the model learns from each modality relative to the other.

The paper further explores the notion of balanced multi-modal learning, suggesting that monitoring the difference in conditional learning speeds can help address imbalances in utilization between modalities. To achieve this, an algorithm is presented that alternates learning from each modality based on their respective learning speeds, allowing for dynamic adjustments to enhance overall learning efficiency.

The re-balancing steps are crucial, as they involve modifying feature maps to accelerate learning from the underutilized modality. The adjustments in these steps are based on the activation statistics from previous training, ensuring that the model learns more effectively from both modalities over time. The authors aim to validate their approach through empirical analysis, demonstrating that the proposed methods can lead to improved performance in multi-modal learning scenarios.
