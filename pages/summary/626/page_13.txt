The supplementary figures in this paper provide insights into the behavior of multi-modal deep neural networks, particularly focusing on the greedy nature of learning when utilizing different modalities, such as color and gray-scale images. 

Figure 7 illustrates the histograms and estimated density functions of two metrics, dutil and dspeed, for models trained on colored and gray-scale versions of the MNIST dataset. The analysis reveals that dspeed is not constrained to a maximum of 1, unlike dutil, indicating that different modalities may influence learning rates in varied ways.

Figure 8 examines the relationship between the L1 regularization weight (λ) and the absolute values of dutil and dspeed. It shows that as log(λ) increases past -5, both metrics tend to rise, suggesting that higher regularization can impact the utilization and speed of learning across modalities.

Figure 9 presents the results of training models with varying values for the imbalance tolerance parameter (α) and the re-balancing window size (Q). The left panel indicates that for Q values other than 50, models maintain relatively balanced utilization rates between modalities. The right panel assesses the models' accuracy, indicating that a Q value of 5 is optimal for the final experiments. Additionally, when α is set to 0.25 times the expected value of dutil, effective control over dutil is achieved, with an optimal α value of 0.1E[bdutil] chosen based on accuracy outcomes.

Overall, these figures support the argument that careful adjustment of hyperparameters can mitigate the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal networks, leading to improved performance and balance in modality utilization.
