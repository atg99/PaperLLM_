The paper discusses the challenges and strategies in training multi-modal deep neural networks (DNNs) while addressing their greedy nature in learning. It compares the performance of various training strategies on different datasets, including Colored-and-gray-MNIST, ModelNet40, and NVGesture. The results, presented in Table 1, show that best uni-modal DNNs outperform vanilla multi-modal DNNs significantly, with the guided training method yielding the highest accuracy across all datasets.

The study emphasizes the importance of balanced modality utilization in multi-modal learning. It introduces the guided and random training strategies, demonstrating that the guided approach leads to better generalization performance compared to the conventional vanilla method and RUBi, a model-agnostic strategy. The guided algorithm reduces the imbalance in modality utilization, as shown by lower values of |bdutil|, and consistently improves generalization performance across all tasks.

The paper concludes that the guided training method effectively mitigates the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal DNNs, resulting in enhanced accuracy and balanced modality usage. The findings suggest that adopting such strategies can lead to more robust and effective multi-modal models.
