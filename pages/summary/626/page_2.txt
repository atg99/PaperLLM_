The paper discusses the challenges associated with the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal deep neural networks (DNNs), particularly how these models often underutilize certain modalities. Through an example from Hand Gesture Recognition, the authors illustrate that a model may heavily rely on one modality, such as depth, while ignoring another, like RGB. This behavior suggests that multi-modal learning can lead to imbalances in how modalities are utilized.

The authors propose a hypothesis called the greedy learner hypothesis, which states that varying learning speeds across modalities contribute to this imbalance. To address this issue, they introduce a metric termed conditional learning speed, which quantifies the learning rate of one modality in relation to others. This metric serves as a proxy for evaluating the conditional utilization rate and guides the development of a new training algorithm called balanced multi-modal learning. This algorithm aims to enhance learning from underutilized modalities and has shown improved generalization performance across three datasets: Colored MNIST, ModelNet40, and NVGesture.

The paper also reviews related work in the field, particularly concerning bias in visual question answering (VQA) and video classification. It highlights existing strategies to mitigate these biases and improve multi-modal learning. The authors emphasize their focus on multi-modal classification tasks, revealing that similar issues of modality underutilization exist. They aim to provide tools for examining and addressing these imbalances.

The problem setup involves a multi-modal dataset with two input modalities. The authors describe their model architecture, which consists of two uni-modal branches connected by intermediate fusion modules. This approach has proven effective, outperforming methods that rely on late fusion, thus reinforcing the relevance of their proposed solutions in enhancing modality utilization in multi-modal DNNs.
