The paper discusses the limitations of current training algorithms in multi-modal deep neural networks (DNNs), particularly their greedy nature, which often leads to underutilization of certain input modalities. The authors propose a method to address this issue by characterizing the learning speeds of different modalities and adjusting the training process accordingly. They derive a conditional learning speed for each modality based on weight and gradient norms, and compute a conditional utilization rate to balance learning across modalities.

Experimental results on ModelNet40 demonstrate that the proposed guided algorithm significantly improves accuracy from 91.21% to 92.45% compared to the vanilla approach. The findings indicate that effective utilization of all modalities enhances generalization in multi-modal tasks, particularly in those requiring cross-modal reasoning, such as visual question answering (VQA). The study underscores the importance of adequate modality utilization in multi-modal learning and contributes to the understanding of greedy learner behavior in these systems.

Overall, the paper provides insights into improving multi-modal DNN performance through better training strategies and highlights the need for tools that facilitate balanced learning from diverse input sources.
