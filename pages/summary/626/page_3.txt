The paper discusses the implementation of a multi-modal transfer module (MMTM) within multi-modal deep neural networks (DNNs) and introduces the greedy learner hypothesis to address challenges in training such networks. The MMTM connects convolutional layers from different uni-modal branches, enabling feature transfer between modalities. 

The process begins by applying global average pooling to feature maps from the uni-modal branches, resulting in two vectors. A fusion mechanism is then employed to generate activations that re-scale the feature maps, allowing information sharing between modalities. The model is trained using stochastic gradient descent to minimize cross-entropy loss for each modality, aiming for high classification accuracy.

The paper further explores the conditional utilization rate to quantify modality imbalance in the DNN. It highlights that each uni-modal branch tends to focus predominantly on its associated input modality, which may lead to suboptimal learning. By modifying the operations within the fusion modules to isolate modality reliance, the authors derive four models to evaluate and compare the accuracy of modality-specific predictions.

The findings underscore the need to address the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal DNNs, which can hinder effective training and utilization of all available modalities.
