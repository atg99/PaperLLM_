The paper discusses the characterization and mitigation of the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal deep neural networks (DNNs). It introduces the concept of conditional utilization rates to measure the relative contributions of different modalities to the model's accuracy. Specifically, these rates quantify how much each modality contributes to improving the model's performance when conditioned on the other modality. The difference in these rates, denoted as dutil(f), indicates the degree of imbalance in the model's utilization of the modalities.

The authors hypothesize that multi-modal learning processes tend to be greedy, meaning that they often rely heavily on one modality that is learned faster, potentially neglecting the contributions of the other modality. This greediness is characterized by the expectation of dutil over multiple training iterations, where higher absolute values indicate a more pronounced reliance on a single modality.

To address this issue, the paper proposes a balanced multi-modal learning algorithm aimed at controlling the learning speed from each modality. The authors define conditional learning speed as a metric to evaluate how quickly the model learns from each modality during training, serving as a real-time proxy for conditional utilization rates. This approach seeks to mitigate the imbalance in modality utilization by ensuring that the learning process does not favor one modality over the other excessively.

Empirical experiments conducted on various multi-modal datasets demonstrate the tendency of current learning processes to produce models with significant imbalance in modality utilization. The proposed algorithm aims to enhance model performance by promoting a more balanced approach to learning from multiple modalities.
