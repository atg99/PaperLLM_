The paper discusses the greedy nature of learning in multi-modal deep neural networks (DNNs) and explores methods to characterize and mitigate this issue. The authors analyze the distributions of two metrics, dutil and dspeed, across models trained on different modalities, revealing that models utilizing two distinct input modalities exhibit asymmetric distributions of dutil around zero. In contrast, models using identical modalities show symmetric distributions, suggesting an imbalance in the reliance on different modalities during learning.

The study also investigates the role of strong regularization, specifically L1 regularization, in influencing the greediness of multi-modal learning. The authors hypothesize that increased regularization encourages a greater imbalance in the utilization of different modalities. Through experiments with varying regularization strengths, they demonstrate a positive correlation between the level of regularization (represented by R(f)) and the degree of greediness in learning, as indicated by the metrics dutil and dspeed. The findings suggest that stronger regularization leads to sparser model parameters and a more pronounced greedy behavior in the learning process. Overall, the paper highlights the importance of understanding and addressing the greedy nature of learning to improve the performance of multi-modal DNNs.
