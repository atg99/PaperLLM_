The referenced works focus on various applications of machine learning and ensemble methods in environmental science, particularly in predicting air quality, climate phenomena, and ecological metrics. Key methodologies highlighted include the use of extremely randomized trees, boosted regression trees, and advanced ensemble learning techniques such as stacking and CatBoost. 

Significant contributions include improved PM2.5 estimates across China, predictions of olive phenology using climate reanalysis, and the estimation of terrestrial latent heat flux over Europe. Other studies evaluate the effectiveness of different machine learning algorithms, such as XGBoost and random forests, in assessing environmental risks like flash floods and air quality. 

The papers also address the importance of feature selection and diversity measures in enhancing model accuracy and reliability. The concept of stacked generalization is emphasized as a method to combine multiple predictive models to improve outcomes in various environmental applications. 

Overall, the body of work underscores the growing intersection of machine learning techniques with environmental monitoring and assessment, aiming to improve predictive capabilities and inform decision-making in climate-related fields.
