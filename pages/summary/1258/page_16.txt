This paper provides a comprehensive overview of machine learning techniques applied in remote sensing, particularly focusing on various algorithms such as stochastic gradient boosting (SGB), support vector machines (SVM), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost). The authors discuss the applications of these techniques in agricultural and environmental monitoring, emphasizing their effectiveness in tasks such as image classification and biomass estimation.

Key references highlight the evolution of remote sensing technology and its integration with machine learning, showcasing various models and methodologies used for vegetation characterization and soil moisture retrieval. The paper also reviews comparative studies on the performance of different machine learning algorithms, such as random forests and support vector regression, in remote sensing applications.

The authors argue that ensemble learning methods, including bagging and boosting, offer significant advantages in handling class imbalance and improving predictive accuracy. They reference foundational works and recent advancements, providing a meta-analysis of the effectiveness of deep learning in remote sensing.

Overall, the paper serves as a valuable resource for understanding the intersection of machine learning and remote sensing, illustrating the potential for innovative applications in environmental science and agriculture.
