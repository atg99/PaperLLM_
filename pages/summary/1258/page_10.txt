The paper discusses the application of various machine learning algorithms for crop yield prediction, highlighting the most commonly used ensemble learning methods. According to the analysis, Random Forest (RF) is the most frequently applied algorithm, followed by Support Vector Regression (SVR) and stacking methods. Notable findings from various studies indicate that ensemble models, particularly RF and XGBoost, demonstrate high accuracy in yield predictions, achieving R2 values ranging from 0.74 to 0.85 and RMSE values between 0.55 and 0.85 t/ha.

Specific studies mentioned include the work of Ruan et al., which identified RF and XGBoost as the most accurate models for predicting wheat yield, and Cao et al., where XGBoost outperformed other algorithms for winter wheat yield estimation. Additionally, Ang et al. found that RF outperformed AdaBoost in predicting oil palm yields using remote sensing data. Kamir et al. reported that SVR was the best single learner for wheat yield estimation, while ensemble techniques did not significantly enhance performance.

The paper also highlights the increasing use of stacking models for yield prediction. Feng et al. demonstrated that stacking algorithms yielded the highest prediction accuracy for alfalfa, while Fei et al. and Li et al. found that stacking improved prediction accuracy across different irrigation scenarios and crop types. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of ensemble learning algorithms in enhancing the accuracy of crop yield predictions.
