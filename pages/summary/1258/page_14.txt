The paper discusses the importance of feature selection in conjunction with ensemble algorithms, which are often viewed as black box models prone to overfitting. It emphasizes the need for identifying significant variables through various feature selection techniques, such as filter, wrapper, and embedded methods, before model training. Studies, including one by Luo et al., demonstrate that combining recursive feature elimination with models like CatBoost can enhance prediction accuracy, as shown by an RMSE of 25.77 t/ha for forest biomass in Jilin province, China. 

The paper also highlights the use of variance inflation factor (VIF) to manage multicollinearity in independent variables, recommending the inclusion of only those with VIF values below 10. Among ensemble methods, tree-based algorithms utilize permutation importance and other metrics like mean decrease in accuracy to identify important variables. Conversely, stacking ensemble methods struggle with variable selection due to their reliance on multiple models, a challenge that has seen limited exploration in existing literature.

Additionally, the paper introduces other ensemble learning algorithms, such as dynamic ensemble learning, which adapts to input patterns to enhance predictions, and blending, which differs from stacking by using a holdout set instead of k-fold cross-validation. 

The discussion extends to deep learning algorithms, which have potential applications in various fields, including agriculture and remote sensing. Current ensemble models predominantly use conventional machine learning methods, but studies indicate that integrating deep learning models can improve prediction accuracy. For instance, ensembles of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have demonstrated high accuracy in tasks like predicting evapotranspiration and mapping landslide susceptibility. The paper concludes by suggesting that future research should explore optimal combinations of deep learning algorithms within ensemble frameworks, particularly emphasizing stacking methods for enhanced predictive performance.
