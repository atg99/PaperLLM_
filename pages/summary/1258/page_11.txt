The paper discusses the estimation of forest structure parameters and biomass, which are essential for understanding carbon stocks and their implications for the carbon cycle and climate change. Various machine learning algorithms and models were evaluated for their effectiveness in predicting forest parameters from remote sensing data. 

García-Gutiérrez et al. found that support vector regression (SVR) with Gaussian kernels outperformed multiple linear regression and other machine learning algorithms in estimating forest parameters in Lugo, Spain. Similarly, Corte et al. demonstrated that SVR provided the most accurate predictions for tree dendrometry parameters from UAV-lidar data, achieving low relative RMSE values.

The paper also highlights the use of ensemble algorithms for forest parameter estimation. Cartus et al. developed a two-stage random forest model using airborne laser scanner data and other remote sensing data, achieving good accuracy in canopy height and growing stock volume estimations. Xu et al. employed a two-stage ensemble approach combining various base learners, resulting in reduced uncertainty in growing stock volume mapping.

Dube et al. utilized stochastic gradient boosting to estimate eucalyptus stand volume in South Africa, achieving higher accuracy than random forest and stepwise regression. Zhang et al. assessed eight algorithms for predicting forest biomass and found that ensemble algorithms like CatBoost yielded the highest accuracy. They also introduced a stacking model to further enhance prediction accuracy.

Ghosh et al. employed stacked ensemble algorithms to predict aboveground biomass in Indian mangroves, noting significant improvements in accuracy. Lastly, Du et al. developed a convolutional neural network model that surpassed traditional machine learning techniques in prediction accuracy, particularly when using a stacking approach.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of advanced machine learning techniques, particularly SVR and ensemble models, in accurately estimating forest structure and biomass parameters.
