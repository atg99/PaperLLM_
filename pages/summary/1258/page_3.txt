The paper discusses various ensemble learning methods in machine learning, focusing on Random Forest (RF), Extremely Randomized Trees (ERT), boosting algorithms, and stacked generalization.

RF utilizes the Gini index for variable importance but overlooks spatial correlations in data. To address this, RF kriging (RFK) combines RF with kriging for improved applications, such as forest biomass mapping and PM2.5 predictions. ERT, another tree-based method, differs from RF by using all samples for training and selecting cutting points randomly, which enhances diversity among tree outputs. Research indicates that ERT may yield more accurate predictions across various applications, including biomass estimation and PM2.5 predictions.

Boosting algorithms transform weak learners into strong ones through iterative adjustments, emphasizing misclassified samples. Notable boosting methods include AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), LightGBM, and CatBoost. XGBoost improves upon GBM with faster processing and regularization techniques, proving effective in mapping species diversity and predicting environmental metrics. LightGBM offers advantages like parallel training and efficient handling of large datasets but struggles with smaller samples. CatBoost excels in managing categorical data and has demonstrated superior performance in biomass estimation and evapotranspiration tasks.

Stacked generalization, or stacking, combines diverse base learners through a meta learner, enhancing predictive performance beyond individual models. Proper design and selection of base learners are crucial for effective stacking, leveraging both precision and diversity to improve outcomes.
