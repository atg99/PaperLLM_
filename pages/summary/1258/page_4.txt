The paper discusses the concept of stacking in machine learning, emphasizing the importance of diversity among base learners. Diversity, defined as the dependence or complementariness among learners, enhances the potential for improved predictive accuracy when diverse learners are combined. Various measures of diversity exist, such as Q-statistics in classification and variance of predictions in regression, but no single measure has proven superior.

The paper notes that while increasing the number of base learners can be beneficial, it does not always correlate with better accuracy and may lead to higher memory and computation costs. Research suggests that using three to four base learners is often optimal for stacking. A typical stacking model consists of two layers, where k-fold cross-validation is implemented to generate verification datasets for the base learners. The predictions from these learners are then utilized as inputs for a meta learner in the second layer. The study highlights that employing class probabilities instead of predicted classes can enhance the effectiveness of the meta learner.

Additionally, the inclusion of engineered features and weighted stacking methods has been shown to improve performance. The paper also presents a literature survey from the ISI Web of Science database, focusing on the intersection of ensemble methods and remote sensing, resulting in 2247 relevant publications from 1979 to 2022, with a notable increase in annual publications from 2015 to 2022.
