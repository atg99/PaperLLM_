The paper presents FactorGCN, a novel graph convolutional network (GCN) framework that enhances node classification tasks through graph-level disentanglement. FactorGCN outperforms existing models in accuracy, achieving 86.57% on the Pattern dataset, indicating its potential as a versatile GCN framework. The method decomposes input graphs into interpretable factor graphs, each representing distinct interconnections among entities, allowing for topology-aware convolutions that yield explainable node features beneficial for downstream applications.

The framework supports multi-relation disentangling, facilitating information propagation in separate spaces. Two new metrics for quantifying graph disentanglement performance are introduced, further validating FactorGCN's effectiveness. The broader impact of this work extends to various applications, including social network analysis and molecular graph classification. It also provides insights into social issues, such as epidemic spread analysis. However, the model is not without limitations, as inaccuracies in the disentangled graphs could lead to biased outcomes. Overall, FactorGCN is positioned as a promising tool in both technical and social contexts.
