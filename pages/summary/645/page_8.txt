The paper presents an evaluation of the FactorGCN method for graph classification, specifically on synthetic graphs and the ZINC dataset. Results indicate that as the number of factor graphs increases, FactorGCN demonstrates significant performance improvements, though excessive factor graphs can complicate disentanglement, leading to diminished returns. 

In synthetic graph experiments, FactorGCN consistently outperformed traditional methods such as MLP, GCN, and GAT across various factor graph counts. For the ZINC dataset, FactorGCN achieved superior results compared to other models, including GatedGCN E, which requires additional edge information, highlighting FactorGCN's effectiveness in disentanglement without such data.

Further evaluations across multiple graph classification datasets confirmed FactorGCN's status as a leading general GCN framework, exhibiting top performance even without its disentangling capabilities. The study also includes an ablation and sensitivity analysis, revealing that the absence of a discriminator negatively impacts performance and that an optimal number of factor graphs slightly exceeding the ground truth enhances results.

Overall, FactorGCN is shown to be a robust and effective method for graph classification tasks, achieving competitive results across various datasets and settings.
