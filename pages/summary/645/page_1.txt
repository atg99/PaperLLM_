The paper presents a novel approach to graph convolutional networks (GCNs) called FactorGCN, which addresses the challenge of disentangling heterogeneous relationships represented by single edges in graphs. Traditional GCNs often overlook the complexity of relationships among nodes, treating them as simple connections. FactorGCN aims to explicitly separate these intertwined relationships by transforming a simple graph into several factorized graphs, each reflecting a distinct latent relationship between nodes.

FactorGCN aggregates node features in each factorized latent space, resulting in disentangled features that enhance performance on various downstream tasks. The authors evaluate FactorGCN using both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in both disentangling relationships and feature aggregation.

The paper highlights the need for disentangling in the structural domain of graphs, contrasting it with previous efforts primarily focused on convolutional neural networks (CNNs). While CNN-based methods have made strides in disentangling features, they are not directly applicable to GCNs due to the differences in data structure. FactorGCN represents a significant advancement in the field by explicitly addressing graph-level disentangling, thus improving the interpretability and performance of GCN models.
