This paper presents a novel approach to enhance the capacity of attention-based Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) through the use of factor graphs. The process begins with the transformation of node features to generate edge coefficient representations, denoted as Eije, using an attention score computed via a one-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). This score is normalized to create coefficients that reflect the connections between nodes in the factor graph.

A significant aspect of the proposed model is its ability to generate distinct factor graphs for better disentanglement. To achieve this, an additional head is introduced in the disentangle layer, which helps prevent the generation of structurally similar graphs that could hinder model performance. The disentanglement is framed as a graph classification problem, where unique labels are assigned to each factor graph, and a discriminator is employed to classify these graphs based on their structural features.

The discriminator architecture consists of a three-layer graph auto-encoder that processes transformed node features and attention coefficients to produce new representations for the factor graphs. A loss function is defined for training the discriminator, focusing on maximizing the correct classification of the graphs based on their labels.

In the subsequent aggregation step, the model aggregates information from the diverse factor graphs. This process mirrors traditional GCN methods, where new node features are computed as a weighted sum of neighboring features, but here it is performed independently for each factor graph. The aggregation is mathematically formulated to ensure that the new features for each node are derived from its connections within the factor graph.

In summary, the paper introduces a framework for improving GCNs by leveraging factor graphs and a structured approach to disentanglement and aggregation, ultimately aiming to enhance model performance on graph-structured data.
