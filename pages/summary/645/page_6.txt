This paper investigates the disentanglement of factor graphs using various graph-based methods, emphasizing the performance of a proposed model, FactorGCN. The authors present qualitative evaluations through visualizations of disentangled factor graphs and correlation analyses of latent features. They highlight the effectiveness of FactorGCN in achieving independent latent features while maintaining strong performance in downstream tasks.

In the qualitative evaluation, the authors provide visual examples of disentangled factor graphs, removing isolated nodes to enhance clarity. They also analyze latent feature correlations, revealing that while traditional models like GCN and MLP yield high performance, their latent features remain entangled. In contrast, GAT produces more independent features but at the cost of performance, whereas FactorGCN balances independence with task performance.

For quantitative evaluation, the paper assesses both downstream task performance and disentanglement using metrics such as Micro-F1 for multi-label classification and mean absolute error for regression tasks. Additionally, they introduce a novel metric, graph edit distance on edge (GED E), which focuses on edge modifications to evaluate disentanglement, allowing for a more nuanced understanding of graph transformations. The authors emphasize the importance of their findings in enhancing the understanding and application of disentangled factor graphs within various contexts.
