The paper presents an evaluation of various graph neural network models on a synthetic dataset, focusing on classification and disentanglement performance. Results demonstrate that the FactorGCN model outperforms others, achieving a Micro-F1 score of 99.5%, which indicates strong classification accuracy alongside effective disentanglement of features. The hidden features from FactorGCN exhibit a block-wise correlation pattern, confirming the disentanglement of latent features.

In contrast, models like MLP and GCN, while achieving good classification scores, fail to disentangle features effectively. The GAT model does attempt disentanglement through multi-head attention but sacrifices performance on the primary task. FactorGCN, however, maintains high performance on both fronts.

The paper also introduces metrics for evaluating performance: the Graph Edit Distance (GED) and the Consistency Score (C-Score), which assesses the stability of matched factor graphs against ground truths. Results from various configurations show that as the number of latent factor graphs increases, FactorGCN's performance improves significantly, although very high numbers of graphs can complicate the task.

The evaluation is further extended to the ZINC dataset, where the model continues to demonstrate superior disentanglement capabilities under conditions where edge type information is hidden. Overall, the findings support the efficacy of FactorGCN in achieving both classification and disentanglement objectives in graph-based tasks.
