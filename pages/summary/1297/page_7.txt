The references provided encompass a range of studies and methodologies focused on recommender systems, particularly exploring advanced techniques such as deep learning, collaborative filtering, and graphical models. Key contributions include:

1. Cheng et al. (2016) proposed the Wide & Deep Learning framework designed to enhance recommendation performance by combining memorization and generalization capabilities.
2. Harper and Konstan (2015) detailed the MovieLens datasets, emphasizing their historical context and significance in the development of collaborative filtering techniques.
3. He et al. (2017) introduced Neural Collaborative Filtering, leveraging neural networks to model user-item interactions effectively.
4. Hidasi and Karatzoglou (2017) focused on session-based recommendations using recurrent neural networks, highlighting the importance of temporal context in user behavior.
5. Various studies (Hu et al. 2008; Pan et al. 2008; Sedhain et al. 2016) examined collaborative filtering methods for implicit feedback datasets and one-class collaborative filtering, emphasizing the adaptation of traditional models to handle different types of data.
6. The significance of linear models and lasso regularization in high-dimensional settings was discussed by Meinshausen and Bühlmann (2006), and further explored by Ning and Karypis (2011) in the context of sparse methods for top-N recommendations.
7. The application of variational autoencoders (Liang et al. 2018) and denoising autoencoders (Wu et al. 2016) illustrates the trend of integrating autoencoder architectures into collaborative filtering frameworks.

Overall, these works collectively advance the field of recommender systems by integrating machine learning techniques and addressing the complexities inherent in user preferences and item characteristics.
