The paper outlines a method involving the processing of tensors in a neural network context, specifically in the framework of Faster R-CNN. The key steps include obtaining and reshaping tensors, namely \( B_t \), \( W_{in} \), and \( W_{out} \), into 3-D formats. 

Experiments are conducted on three datasets: PASCAL VOC 2007, PASCAL VOC 2012, and MS COCO, utilizing VGG16 and ResNet101 as backbone networks. The networks are trained on images with a shorter side of 600 pixels, employing pre-trained models from ImageNet for initialization. The training process involves a mini-batch size of 1 and a total of 140k iterations, with an initial learning rate of 0.001, which decreases after every 50k iterations.

Key parameters are introduced, including \( saT \) and \( T_s \), which filter out small keypoint anchors and define input image size boundaries, respectively. The number of keypoints \( NG \) and the number of channels \( S_k \) for the keypoint-based label tensor are also established, with \( S_k \) set to 16 for optimal anchor mapping. The paper emphasizes that the parameters for K-Faster may differ from those in standard Faster R-CNN, particularly in aspects related to anchor selection and region proposals for classification. The detection performance is primarily evaluated using mean Average Precision (mAP).
