This paper introduces the keypoint-based Faster R-CNN method (K-Faster) for object detection, which enhances the traditional Faster R-CNN by integrating local keypoints to generate bounding boxes known as keypoint anchors. The method aims to address the limitations of standard anchor boxes, which may struggle when errors between predicted and ground-truth boxes are significant. By combining keypoint anchors with traditional Faster anchors, K-Faster effectively detects objects that may not be adequately represented by fixed aspect ratios and sizes.

The keypoint anchors are generated from all possible combinations of keypoints in an image and are processed through a convolutional network according to the Area Ratio of the Anchor to the Image (ARoAI) and the anchor's center coordinates. The approach employs a hierarchical encoding strategy in which lower-index feature maps handle anchors with larger ARoAI and higher-index maps manage those with smaller ARoAI. This dual-anchor mechanism allows for improved region proposals, leveraging local information from keypoints to enhance detection capabilities.

The paper reports extensive experiments conducted on PASCAL VOC 07/12 and MS COCO datasets, demonstrating that K-Faster outperforms traditional Faster R-CNN in terms of mean Average Precision (mAP) and positioning precision of detected boxes. The key contributions of this work include the integration of local keypoints into the Faster R-CNN framework, the development of an area-based encoding technique for heterogeneous anchor distribution, and the overall enhancement of detection performance through the coupling of keypoint and Faster anchors.
