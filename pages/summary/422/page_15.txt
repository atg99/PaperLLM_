The paper presents results from experiments on the PASCAL VOC 2007 test set, comparing various object detection methods. The training data includes both VOC 2007 and VOC 2012 trainval datasets. The results are quantified using mean Average Precision (mAP) across different models and object categories.

Key findings include the performance of two models, K-Faster 16 and K-Faster 101, which achieved mAP scores of 77.2% and 80.5%, respectively. These scores represent improvements of 4.0% and 4.1% over the baseline Faster R-CNN model. The enhancements are attributed to the introduction of keypoint anchors, which provide additional discrimination for object detection. K-Faster models outperformed several state-of-the-art methods, including ION, SSD512, and YOLO v2.

While models like CoupleNet, CEBNet, and MLKP 101 show higher mAP scores, the differences in performance compared to K-Faster are not significantly substantial. CoupleNet achieved an mAP of 82.7% with a 2.2% improvement over R-FCN, whereas K-Faster 101's improvement is greater. CEBNet, despite its complexity and achieving a 5.7% improvement over SSD512, does not match the simplicity and effectiveness of K-Faster.

Visual results indicate the effective detection capabilities of K-Faster 16, illustrating how keypoint anchors enhance detection rates. Overall, the study suggests that the proposed K-Faster models are competitive in the object detection landscape, demonstrating significant improvements while maintaining a simpler architecture compared to some more complex models.
