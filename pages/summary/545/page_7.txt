In the paper "Auto-Keras: An Efficient Neural Architecture Search System," the authors conduct experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of their proposed method, Auto-Keras (AK), in neural architecture search. The experiments aim to address three primary questions regarding the search algorithm's efficacy, the impact of Bayesian optimization and network morphism, and the accuracy of the proposed kernel function in measuring neural network similarity.

The experiments utilize three benchmark datasets: MNIST, CIFAR10, and FASHION, which require different neural architectures for optimal performance. Four categories of baseline methods are compared: straightforward methods (random and grid search), conventional methods (SPMT and SMAC), state-of-the-art methods (SEAS and NASBOT), and variants of the proposed method (BFS and BO). The performance of the deployed system, AK-DP, is also assessed, which incorporates advanced techniques to enhance performance.

The experimental setup includes dividing training data into training and validation sets, using testing data for evaluation, and maintaining consistent training procedures across methods. All methods are executed for 12 hours on a single GPU, with classification error rate as the evaluation metric.

The results indicate that AK-DP achieves state-of-the-art performance across all datasets. Additionally, AK demonstrates the lowest error rates, showcasing its ability to discover effective architectures for both small and large datasets. In contrast, straightforward and traditional methods perform well on MNIST but struggle with CIFAR10 due to their limited search capabilities. The state-of-the-art methods, while effective, are outperformed by AK due to its superior search strategy, which effectively balances exploration and efficiency.

Overall, the findings highlight the advantages of the proposed Auto-Keras system in efficiently searching for and identifying optimal neural architectures, particularly in constrained time and resource settings.
