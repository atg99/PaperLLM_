The paper presents an efficient method for neural architecture search (NAS) utilizing network morphism and Bayesian optimization. Unlike traditional methods that operate in Euclidean spaces, this approach navigates a tree-structured search space where nodes represent neural architectures and edges represent morph operations. The authors identify key challenges, including maintaining tensor shape consistency during morphism operations and the complexity of the search space.

To address these challenges, the authors propose an edit-distance neural network kernel that quantifies the number of operations required to transform one architecture into another. This kernel enables the application of Bayesian optimization, which iteratively updates a Gaussian process model based on existing architectures, generates new architectures using a specially designed acquisition function, and observes their performance.

The paper also introduces a graph-level network morphism to manage changes in architectures at a higher abstraction level than layer-specific morphism. The proposed method is benchmarked against state-of-the-art NAS techniques on datasets such as MNIST, CIFAR10, and FASHION-MNIST, demonstrating superior performance in terms of lower error rates within constrained search times.

Additionally, the authors developed Auto-Keras, an open-source AutoML system based on their method, which features a user-friendly interface and parallel processing capabilities to enhance search efficiency. Key contributions of the paper include the algorithm for NAS guided by Bayesian optimization, extensive experiments validating its effectiveness, and the development of Auto-Keras as a widely adopted tool in the field. 

The problem statement defines the objective of finding an optimal neural network that minimizes a cost function based on training and validation datasets. The search space encompasses all architectures that can be derived through morph operations, making it more expansive than traditional methods with fixed architectures.
