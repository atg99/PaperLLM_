The paper presents theorems establishing the validity of a metric space distance and a kernel in the context of neural architecture search using Auto-Keras. 

Theorem 1 proves that the distance function \(d(f_a, f_b)\) is a metric space distance by demonstrating its properties: non-negativity, definiteness, symmetry, and triangle inequality. Non-negativity is shown by confirming that the distance between any two neural networks is always greater than or equal to zero. Definiteness is established by proving that if the distance is zero, the two networks must be identical. Symmetry is validated as the distance between two networks remains the same regardless of their order. Finally, the triangle inequality is verified through various cases of network layer widths, ensuring that the distance between two networks does not exceed the sum of distances through a third network.

Theorem 2 asserts that the kernel function \(\kappa(f_a, f_b)\) is valid. It references the positive definiteness of the generalized RBF kernel and the ability to isometrically embed any finite metric space into Euclidean space while preserving the kernel's properties. This is supported by Bourgain's theorem, which allows for the distortion of the distance measurements while maintaining positive definiteness.

The paper also details the application of Bourgain's theorem to transform calculated edit distances into a form suitable for Euclidean embedding, which is crucial for Bayesian optimization processes. The algorithm developed takes a metric distance matrix (the edit-distance matrix of neural architectures) and produces vectors in Euclidean space, which enables the calculation of a new distance matrix aimed at minimizing distortion. This process is integrated into the Bayesian optimization framework, allowing for the effective expansion and updating of the distance matrix with new training examples while ensuring the validity of the kernel matrix derived from the distorted distances.
