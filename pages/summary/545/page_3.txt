The paper discusses Auto-Keras, an efficient neural architecture search system that addresses the NP-hard problem of neural network morphism. It introduces a method for comparing two neural networks, fa and fb, by defining an approximated distance metric that combines the edit distances of their layers and skip-connections. The formula d(fa, fb) = Dl(La, Lb) + λDs(Sa, Sb) is proposed, where Dl calculates the edit-distance for morphing layers while ignoring skip-connections, and Ds approximates the edit-distance for skip-connections.

To compute Dl, the paper employs a dynamic programming approach that minimizes the edit-distance through a recursive calculation involving a matrix A. This approach matches layers based on their widths and topological order. The edit-distance function dl calculates the difference in widths of the layers.

For Ds, the paper defines a method to compute the edit-distance for matched skip-connections and maps this problem to a bipartite graph matching scenario, using the Hungarian algorithm to find the optimal matching.

Overall, the paper provides a structured approach to neural architecture search by efficiently calculating distances between neural networks, facilitating the morphing process necessary for optimizing architectures.
