The paper presents a novel method for efficient neural architecture search (NAS) leveraging network morphism and Bayesian optimization, encapsulated in an open-source AutoML system called Auto-Keras. The study evaluates the efficiency of this method, referred to as AK, in comparison to its variants: BFS, which uses network morphism without Bayesian optimization, and BO, which employs Bayesian optimization without network morphism. 

The experiments conducted on the CIFAR10 dataset reveal that AK consistently achieves a lower error rate than BFS while searching a similar number of neural architectures, demonstrating the effectiveness of Bayesian optimization in guiding the search. Furthermore, AK shows a higher training efficiency due to network morphism, enabling it to explore more architectures within the same timeframe compared to BO, ultimately leading to improved performance.

Additionally, the paper visualizes the neural network kernel and performance similarity matrices, indicating a correlation between kernel similarity and performance. The results suggest that the proposed method outperforms traditional hyperparameter tuning and state-of-the-art NAS techniques.

The authors conclude with potential future research directions, including joint tuning of neural architecture and training hyperparameters, as well as designing task-oriented NAS for specific applications like image segmentation and object detection.
