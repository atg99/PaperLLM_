The paper presents a novel method for optimizing acquisition functions in tree-structured search spaces, specifically in the context of Neural Architecture Search (NAS). Traditional acquisition functions are defined in Euclidean space, making them unsuitable for tree-structured optimization. The authors propose using the Upper-Confidence Bound (UCB) as the acquisition function, which balances exploration and exploitation through its explicit balancing factor. The optimization process involves transforming existing neural architectures using network morphism operations, which allows for both expansion and re-evaluation of smaller architectures.

To effectively navigate the tree-structured space, the authors introduce a hybrid optimization method that combines A* search and simulated annealing. The A* algorithm is employed to prioritize the best nodes (architectures), while simulated annealing introduces randomness to prevent the algorithm from getting trapped in local minima. The algorithm iteratively expands architectures, evaluates potential new configurations, and updates the best-found architecture based on the acquisition function's value.

Moreover, the paper addresses the challenge of maintaining tensor shape consistency during the morphing of architectures. The authors propose a graph-level morphism approach, which considers the impact of changes at the layer level on the entire computational graph of the network. This method allows for the insertion and widening of layers while ensuring that input and output tensor shapes remain consistent across the network.

In summary, the proposed method enhances the optimization of neural architectures in a tree-structured search space, balancing exploration and exploitation while ensuring functional integrity of the network through careful morphism operations.
