This paper presents an approach to incremental learning for open-world object detection, focusing on the identification and labeling of unknown objects. The model operates in two main phases: identifying unknown objects at each learning step and progressively integrating them into the existing knowledge base.

Key components of the methodology include an unknown-aware Region Proposal Network (RPN) and an energy-based classification head designed to detect potential unknown objects. The authors emphasize the importance of contrastive learning in the feature space, which allows the model to learn discriminative clusters and add new classes without forgetting previously learned ones.

The paper outlines a systematic approach for managing class prototypes, which are essential for maintaining class separation in the latent space. This involves storing features in fixed-length queues for each class, enabling scalable tracking of feature evolution. The clustering loss is computed after an initial burn-in period to allow feature embeddings to stabilize.

To address the challenge of labeling unknown objects, the authors propose an auto-labeling strategy that utilizes the RPN's capability to generate bounding box predictions. They identify potential unknown objects by selecting background proposals with high objectness scores that do not overlap with any ground-truth objects.

Overall, the proposed method effectively combines incremental learning with open-world detection, enabling the model to adaptively learn from new data while retaining knowledge of existing classes.
