The paper discusses the development of an energy-based model (EBM) for identifying unknown classes in a latent feature space. The model learns an energy function E(F,L) that assesses the compatibility between observed features F and potential labels L. The approach utilizes the Helmholtz free energy formulation to distinguish in-distribution data from out-of-distribution samples, leveraging a relationship between network outputs and Gibbs distribution. A clear separation in energy levels between known and unknown data points is achieved through contrastive clustering, and Weibull distributions are employed to model energy distributions for known and unknown classes.

Additionally, the paper addresses the challenge of catastrophic forgetting when incorporating new classes without retraining from scratch. It reviews various strategies to mitigate this issue, emphasizing the effectiveness of exemplar replay. Recent studies indicate that simple replay methods can outperform more complex solutions in continual learning scenarios. The proposed methodology involves storing a balanced set of exemplars and fine-tuning the model incrementally, ensuring a sufficient number of instances for each class.

The paper also outlines an evaluation protocol to assess the performance of the open world detector in identifying unknowns, detecting known classes, and learning new classes as labeled examples become available.
