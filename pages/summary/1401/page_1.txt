In the paper "Towards Open World Object Detection," the authors introduce a novel problem in computer vision termed 'Open World Object Detection.' This problem focuses on enabling models to identify objects not previously encountered as 'unknown' and to incrementally learn these unknown categories as labels become available, all without explicit supervision.

The authors argue that current object detection approaches assume knowledge of all classes during training, which limits their effectiveness in real-world scenarios where unknown objects may be present. They draw inspiration from human curiosity and developmental psychology, which emphasize the importance of recognizing and learning about unknown entities.

The proposed solution, Open World Object Detector (ORE), utilizes contrastive clustering and energy-based identification of unknowns. The paper outlines a comprehensive evaluation protocol and presents experimental results demonstrating ORE's effectiveness in addressing Open World objectives. Notably, the identification of unknown instances also contributes to reducing confusion in incremental object detection, leading to state-of-the-art performance without additional methodological complexities.

The authors hope their work will stimulate further research in this critical area of object detection, emphasizing the need for models capable of adapting to the vast number of classes present in the open world, beyond the limited annotations in standard datasets.
