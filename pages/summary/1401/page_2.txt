The paper introduces the concept of Open World Object Detection, addressing the limitations of current object detection systems that operate under closed-world assumptions. Existing state-of-the-art detectors often misclassify unknown objects as known classes, leading to false positives. Dhamija et al. highlight this issue, while Miller et al. propose using dropout sampling to estimate prediction uncertainty, marking a notable effort in open set object detection.

The authors argue that the dynamic nature of real-world environments necessitates a more flexible detection model that can learn and incorporate new classes as they are identified. Their proposed methodology, ORE, utilizes contrastive clustering and an unknown-aware proposal network to tackle the challenges of open world detection. This approach not only enhances the detection of unknown objects but also allows the system to incrementally learn new classes when labels are provided.

Key contributions of the paper include the introduction of the Open World Object Detection problem, the development of the ORE methodology, a comprehensive experimental framework for evaluating open world characteristics in detectors, and the achievement of state-of-the-art performance in Incremental Object Detection.

The related work section reviews the evolution of open set classification and detection, highlighting previous efforts to recognize unknown classes and the challenges of adapting classifiers in dynamic environments. The authors note that while existing methods can identify unknown instances, they lack the capability for incremental learning, which their work aims to address. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in object detection, aligning it more closely with real-world applications.
