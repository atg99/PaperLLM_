This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the Open World Object Detection (ORE) framework, focusing on its performance against established benchmarks. The results demonstrate that ORE consistently outperforms the Faster R-CNN baseline across various metrics, including Wilderness Impact (WI), Average Open Set Error (A-OSE), and Mean Average Precision (mAP).

Subsequent comparisons in Table 3 highlight ORE's effectiveness in different settings, including scenarios with incremental class addition from the Pascal VOC 2007 dataset. The results affirm ORE's capability to maintain high performance without necessitating methodological adjustments.

The analysis further delves into the contributions of ORE's components through ablation studies. It reveals that the combination of auto-labeling methodology (ALU) and energy-based unknown identification (EBUI) yields superior performance when compared to their individual use. The integration of contrastive clustering (CC) enhances this performance without compromising the detection of known classes. The sensitivity analysis on exemplar memory size indicates that balanced fine-tuning significantly enhances the accuracy of previously known classes, underscoring the framework’s adaptability and efficiency.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of ORE in incremental object detection tasks, establishing it as a leading approach in the domain. Further explorations and detailed analyses are provided in the referenced sections of the paper.
