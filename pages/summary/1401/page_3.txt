The paper introduces the concept of Open World Object Detection, formalizing it through the definition of known classes Kt and unknown classes U. Known classes are represented in a dataset Dt consisting of input images Xt and their corresponding labels Yt. The model MC is capable of detecting known classes and recognizing unknown instances, which are labeled as zero. This allows for the identification of new classes by human users who can then provide training examples, enabling the model to update incrementally without requiring complete retraining.

The proposed solution, ORE, addresses two significant challenges: identifying unknown instances without explicit supervision and preventing the forgetting of previously learned instances. The authors suggest that clear discrimination in the latent space of the model can facilitate the identification of unknowns and support the learning of new class instances without overlap with existing classes. A key feature of ORE is the implementation of contrastive clustering in the latent space, which helps in distinguishing known from unknown instances.

To manage the identification of unknown instances, the authors propose an auto-labeling mechanism that uses a Region Proposal Network (RPN) to pseudo-label these instances. The separation of auto-labeled unknowns in the latent space allows the model to differentiate between known and unknown classes effectively. The architecture of ORE is based on Faster R-CNN, a two-stage object detector, which is adapted to auto-label and identify unknowns through contrastive clustering.

The contrastive clustering approach aims to keep instances of the same class close together in the latent space while pushing apart instances of different classes. The authors define a contrastive loss function to achieve this separation, using prototype vectors for each known class to guide the clustering process.

Overall, the paper presents a robust framework for Open World Object Detection that enables continual learning and adaptation to new classes while maintaining performance on previously learned instances.
