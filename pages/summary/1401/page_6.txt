The paper presents an Open World evaluation protocol for object detection, specifically utilizing the Open World Object Detector (ORE) that adapts Faster R-CNN for handling known and unknown classes. The tasks are derived from Pascal VOC and MS-COCO datasets, with several classes grouped into sequential tasks, allowing for semantic drift. The training and evaluation splits are clearly defined, with performance metrics including Wilderness Impact (WI) and Absolute Open-Set Error (A-OSE) used to assess how well the model distinguishes between known and unknown objects.

The ORE employs a modified classification head that accounts for a variable number of classes by setting logits of unseen classes to a significant negative value, minimizing their influence during softmax calculations. The model's performance is compared to a baseline Faster R-CNN and an 'Oracle' detector, revealing that ORE significantly reduces WI and A-OSE, thereby effectively managing the presence of unknown classes. 

When comparing incremental learning capabilities, ORE shows improved mean Average Precision (mAP) while maintaining performance on known classes, even as new classes are introduced. The paper provides qualitative results and extensive sensitivity analyses, demonstrating that ORE's design helps mitigate confusion between known and unknown objects, enhancing its effectiveness in both open world and incremental object detection scenarios.
