The paper presents a novel method for time series anomaly detection using a combination of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, Variational Autoencoders (VAE), and Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN). Traditional prediction-based anomaly detection methods, such as those utilizing LSTM, multilayer perceptron, and support vector regression, face challenges due to the increasing complexity of time series data in industrial systems. These methods often struggle to accurately predict anomalies due to the variability in machine behavior influenced by usage and external factors.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a LSTM-based VAE-GAN model that integrates the strengths of both VAE and GAN. The model consists of an encoder that maps input time series to a latent space, a generator that reconstructs the time series, and a discriminator that detects anomalies. By jointly training these components on normal time series data, the model eliminates the need for time-consuming optimization during the anomaly detection phase, thus enhancing detection speed and accuracy.

The contributions of the paper include the introduction of a new anomaly detection method that leverages the mapping capability of the encoder and the discrimination ability of the discriminator. The anomaly score is derived from both the reconstruction difference from the VAE and the discriminator's results, improving the model's ability to differentiate between normal data and anomalies. This approach draws inspiration from successful applications in image processing and demonstrates significant advancements in time series anomaly detection from sensor data.
