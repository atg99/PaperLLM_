The proposed LSTM-based VAE-GAN anomaly detection method serves as a robust framework for monitoring equipment states through time series data. It operates in two main phases: model training, where the normal data distribution is learned, and anomaly detection, where the anomaly scores are computed to identify outliers. By jointly training the encoder, generator, and discriminator, the model leverages their respective strengths, resulting in a more efficient and accurate detection process without the need for extensive optimization during the detection phase.

Experimental results indicate that this method outperforms traditional sample-reconstruction techniques, achieving higher F1 scores on both Yahoo and KPI datasets. Additionally, it demonstrates reduced computational time compared to GAN approaches, due to the streamlined detection process. Although the moving window mechanism allows for repeated calculations of certain anomaly scores, it does not adversely affect detection accuracy. Ultimately, the choice of preprocessing methods, including the moving window, is determined by the specific characteristics of the time series data being analyzed.
