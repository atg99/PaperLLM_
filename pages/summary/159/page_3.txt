The paper discusses the use of time series data for anomaly detection, specifically utilizing two datasets: Yahoo and KPI. A time series is defined as a sequence of data points indexed in time order, with the datasets labeled for anomalies and normal points. The Yahoo dataset, provided by Yahoo Labs, includes both real and synthetic time series data with diverse trends, noise, and seasonality. It consists of 94,866 data points, with 1,669 anomalies, resulting in an anomaly rate of 1.76%. The KPI dataset, sourced from various internet companies, reflects the health status of machines and contains 5,000 points with 38 anomalies, yielding an anomaly rate of 0.76%.

Data preprocessing involved applying min-max normalization to scale values between 0 and 1 and segmenting the time series into training and testing datasets. Anomalies were removed from the training data, while a sliding window technique was used to create sub-sequences.

The paper introduces an LSTM-based VAE-GAN method for detecting anomalies in time series data, which consists of two stages: model training and anomaly detection. The model is trained on normal data to learn its distribution and subsequently assesses the testing data to determine if it aligns with the learned normal distribution, calculating an average anomaly score for each point. The architecture of the proposed method is depicted in the paper.
