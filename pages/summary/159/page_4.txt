The paper presents an LSTM-based VAE-GAN architecture designed to capture the temporal dependencies in time series data. The model is trained exclusively on normal data to learn its distribution. It integrates Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for the encoder, generator, and discriminator components of the VAE-GAN. 

The approach involves dividing the time series into sub-sequences using a sliding window method, where each input to the encoder is a vector representing a sub-sequence. This vector is then encoded into a latent space representation. The generator utilizes this latent vector to produce a corresponding real-time space vector, while the discriminator assesses whether the generated vector aligns with the distribution of the normal training data.

During training, the encoder approximates the posterior distribution and encodes the data sample into a latent representation. The generator reconstructs the input data from this representation. Additionally, a random variable is sampled from a standard normal distribution to aid the generator in producing new samples. The discriminator, equipped with an LSTM followed by a fully connected layer with a sigmoid activation function, learns to distinguish between real and generated data, aiming to minimize the reconstruction error.

Instead of relying solely on VAE reconstruction error, the method employs a reconstruction error derived from the discriminator, leading to improved performance in time series analysis.
