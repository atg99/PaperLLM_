The paper discusses the development and performance of the LSTM-based VAE-GAN, an anomaly detection method utilizing a combination of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and variational autoencoders (VAE). Unlike traditional autoencoders, VAEs model the underlying probability distribution of observations through variational inference. The paper compares LSTM-based VAE-GAN against other LSTM-based methods, including LSTM-AE, LSTM-VAE, and MAD-GAN, all of which utilize LSTM networks with similar parameters.

Results presented in Table 1 indicate that LSTM-based VAE-GAN consistently outperforms the other methods in terms of F1 score across different datasets, demonstrating its superior ability to distinguish between anomalies and normal data points. Specifically, in the Yahoo dataset, LSTM-based VAE-GAN achieved an F1 score of 0.8907, significantly higher than the scores of the other methods.

Additionally, the paper highlights the efficiency of LSTM-based VAE-GAN in the anomaly detection stage. Since this method jointly trains the encoder, generator, and discriminator, it reduces the time needed for anomaly detection compared to GAN-based approaches. Time loss experiments conducted on a high-performance hardware setup show that LSTM-based VAE-GAN requires less time for anomaly detection than MAD-GAN, while still taking slightly longer than LSTM-AE and LSTM-VAE due to the necessity of calculating discrimination results. The findings suggest that LSTM-based VAE-GAN not only provides better performance in detecting anomalies but also offers improved efficiency in processing time.
