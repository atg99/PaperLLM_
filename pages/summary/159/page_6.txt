The paper presents an anomaly detection algorithm that utilizes a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) based Variational Autoencoder-Generative Adversarial Network (VAE-GAN). The algorithm operates in two main stages: training and anomaly detection. During the training stage, the encoder, generator, and discriminator are initialized. The model generates random mini-batches from the training data, encodes them to obtain latent variables, and reconstructs data through the generator. The parameters of the encoder, generator, and discriminator are updated based on gradients derived from the Kullback-Leibler divergence and log probabilities, optimizing for better reconstruction and discrimination.

In the anomaly detection stage, the algorithm calculates the reconstruction difference and discrimination results for the test data. An anomaly score is computed by combining the reconstruction error and discrimination results, and if this score exceeds a predefined threshold, the data point is classified as an anomaly; otherwise, it is deemed normal.

The performance of the LSTM-based VAE-GAN is evaluated using Precision, Recall, and F1 score metrics, comparing it against other reconstruction-based anomaly detection methods, including an LSTM-based autoencoder (LSTM-AE). The results indicate the effectiveness of the proposed method in detecting anomalies in time series data.
