The paper presents a comprehensive review of the application of swarm intelligence (SI) techniques in anomaly detection, particularly in the context of intrusion detection systems (IDS) within cybersecurity. It summarizes various methodologies, their strengths and weaknesses, and highlights the growth trends in publications indexed in databases such as SCIE and Scopus.

Key findings include the observation that SI-based methods are widely applied across different phases of IDS, with a significant focus on intrusion detection and cybersecurity. The review identifies four main cyberspace domains: intrusion detection, cybersecurity, outlier detection, and anomaly detection, noting that most methodologies cover the first two domains comprehensively.

The analysis reveals that while swarm intelligence architectures can be applied across all phases of IDS, previous reviews have often overlooked the potential of various SI models, focusing primarily on a few. The paper includes tables summarizing swarm-based methods used in anomaly detection, detailing their performance metrics, limitations, and the diversity mechanisms employed.

It also emphasizes the need for diverse approaches in future research to address gaps in current methodologies. The study indicates that existing SI-based solutions often do not leverage the full potential of SI techniques for effective anomaly detection, with a particular emphasis on the need for more research into diversity in search methods.

In conclusion, the authors highlight that many aspects of SI techniques remain unexplored, particularly in the context of lightweight anomaly-based IDS for the IoT environment. There is a call for increased awareness and research into the diversity of search mechanisms to enhance detection rates. The authors declare no potential conflicts of interest.
