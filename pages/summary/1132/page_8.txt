The table presents a summary of various swarm-based anomaly detection techniques, highlighting their efficiency, attack types, and limitations. 

1. PSO hybridized with k-means (2010) achieved a detection rate (DR) of 99.13% but did not specify attack types and had a false alarm rate (FAR) of 5.02%. 
2. FA combined with k-means (2012) recorded a true positive rate (TPR) of 80% and a FAR of 20%, noted for lower efficiency compared to other methods.
3. ABC and PSO hybridization (2015) on the KDD Cup'99 dataset achieved a DR of 98.67% for multiple attack types.
4. PSO tuning parameters for MCLP (2015) on KDD Cup'99 had a DR of 99.13% and a FAR of 1.947%, focusing on improving detection efficiency.
5. ACO (2015) used a signature-based methodology, yielding correlation coefficients and normalized mean errors for DoS and DDoS detection.
6. Firefly Algorithm (FA) introduced in 2015 showed a TPR of 77.4% and a low FAR of 0.4% for DoS and DDoS detection.
7. ACO's heuristic function (2016) on KDD datasets reported a DR of 98% and a FAR of 1, emphasizing feature identification for intrusion detection.
8. A time-varying chaos PSO framework (2016) attained a DR of 97.23% and a FAR of 2.41%, claiming superior performance over existing models.
9. ACO's DSNSF model (2016) for DDoS and flash crowds had a TPR of 92% and a FAR of 21%, outperforming other models.
10. Ant Tree miner (2017) achieved over 90% DR with low FAR compared to J48 and Random Tree classifiers.
11. Fuzziﬁed Cuckoo-based Clustering Technique (2017) reported DR of 96.86% and a FAR of 1.297% against state-of-the-art techniques.
12. A hybrid classification method (2018) combining ABC and AFS achieved DR of 99% and an exceptionally low FAR of 0.01%.

Overall, the techniques demonstrate varied effectiveness in detecting different attack types while highlighting the importance of hybrid methodologies and parameter tuning for improved detection rates and reduced false alarms.
