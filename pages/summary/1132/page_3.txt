The paper discusses the increasing threat of cyber-attacks and the inadequacies of current cybersecurity infrastructures, which has led to the development of Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS). IDS are categorized into three classes: signature, anomaly, and hybrid detection systems. This paper specifically focuses on anomaly detection systems, which identify deviations from a normal user profile to detect unknown attacks, particularly insider threats such as credit card theft and account takeover.

Anomaly detection systems operate in two phases: the training phase, where a profile of normal traffic is established, and the testing phase, where this profile is applied to new data. Various architectures for these systems include statistical, data-mining, and machine learning techniques. The paper highlights the use of Fuzzy Q-learning algorithms for protecting wireless nodes against DDoS attacks, as well as a Cooperative IDS model that employs a Cooperative Fuzzy Q-learning optimization technique.

The paper also reviews several machine learning techniques for anomaly detection, including Bayesian networks, neural networks, and support vector machines (SVMs). Bayesian networks are noted for their ability to suppress false alarms but may struggle with speed on large datasets. Neural networks mimic human brain functions and excel in handling uncertain and unreliable data. SVMs are emphasized for their effectiveness in pattern recognition and anomaly detection, particularly through the use of one-class SVMs that focus on specific class examples.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of various anomaly detection techniques, their architectures, and their applications in enhancing cybersecurity.
