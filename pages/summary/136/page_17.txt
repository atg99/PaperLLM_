The paper presents a zero-shot retrieval experiment using the CA V-MAE model on the MSR-VTT dataset, comparing its performance against existing models that were trained on a larger dataset (HowTo100M). Despite being trained on only 2M from AudioSet, the CA V-MAE model achieves comparable visual-audio retrieval results, though it performs worse in the audio-visual direction. The findings suggest that even with significantly less training data, the CA V-MAE model demonstrates competitive performance, highlighting the effectiveness of its design.

The paper also explores the impact of model initialization, revealing that using self-supervised ImageNet pretrained weights consistently enhances performance across different tasks. However, it notes that this initialization is not critical, as the model still performs adequately without it. Furthermore, a comparison of self-supervised and supervised learning weights indicates that while supervised weights improve fine-tuning results, they do not yield better outcomes in linear probing.

Additionally, the paper discusses the chosen masking strategy, employing a 75% masking ratio for both audio and visual inputs, which aligns with previous research suggesting this ratio is effective for such tasks. Overall, the findings underscore the CA V-MAE model's potential in audio-visual tasks while emphasizing the importance of initialization methods and masking strategies in model performance.
