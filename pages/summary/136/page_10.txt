The paper presents CA V-MAE, a model designed for audio-visual self-supervised learning that effectively integrates two frameworks while preventing representation collapse. Key strategies include a multi-stream forward pass, a joint-specific encoder architecture, and masked contrastive learning. From a representation learning standpoint, CA V-MAE creates a coordinated representation suitable for both audio-visual joint event classification and retrieval tasks. The model demonstrates competitive performance, matching or surpassing state-of-the-art (SOTA) models in audio-visual event classification with significantly lower computational resources, and achieving comparable results in retrieval tasks to models trained solely on contrastive objectives. Additionally, CA V-MAE's multi-modal pretraining enhances single-modal representations, setting a new SOTA for audio-based event classification.

Ethically, the research utilizes publicly available YouTube videos and acknowledges potential misuse in surveillance applications. The authors commit to responsible code and model distribution. For reproducibility, the paper details implementation in Section 2.3.1 and Appendix B, with resources available on GitHub. The references cite significant prior works in multimodal self-supervised learning, establishing the context and foundation for CA V-MAE.
