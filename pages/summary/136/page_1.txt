In this paper, the authors present the Contrastive Audio-Visual Masked Autoencoder (CAV-MAE), an extension of the Masked Auto-Encoder (MAE) model to audio-visual multi-modalities. The CAV-MAE integrates contrastive learning with masked data modeling, two prominent self-supervised learning techniques, to develop a coordinated representation of audio and visual data. 

The research highlights the importance of leveraging natural audio-visual correspondences found in videos, which enhances the model's performance in tasks such as audio-visual retrieval. The authors found that the contrastive learning objective not only improves retrieval tasks but also aids in learning a superior joint representation. Their experiments demonstrate that the fully self-supervised pretrained CAV-MAE achieves a new state-of-the-art (SOTA) accuracy of 65.9% on the VGGSound dataset and shows competitive performance on AudioSet for audio-visual event classification against the best supervised models.

The introduction emphasizes the challenge of utilizing large amounts of unlabeled audio-visual data for training, as manual annotation is costly and impractical. The study proposes a novel approach by combining contrastive and masked learning methods, which have not been previously integrated in the context of audio-visual learning. This approach is expected to enhance the model's ability to capture both shared and unique modality information, thereby improving the overall learning and representation of audio-visual data. 

The paper concludes with an invitation to access the code and pretrained models available at the provided GitHub link.
