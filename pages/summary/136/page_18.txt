The paper discusses the performance of audio-visual models initialized with different types of weights and investigates the effects of masking ratios in training. Most existing models rely on ImageNet supervised pre-trained weights, while this study aims to create a fully self-supervised model. The comparison reveals that models initialized with supervised (SL) weights outperform those with self-supervised (SSL) weights in fine-tuning tasks, indicating the value of ImageNet labels as supervision signals.

In terms of masking ratios, the study assesses their impact on audio-visual joint event classification and retrieval tasks. Results show that for joint classification, a smaller masking ratio (50%) yields slightly better performance, though the difference is minor. In contrast, the audio-visual retrieval task suffers from high masking ratios, suggesting that lower ratios are preferable for retrieval without negatively affecting classification tasks, albeit at the cost of increased computation.

The paper presents findings that indicate a masking ratio between 65% and 75% works well across both contrastive learning and masked data modeling frameworks for classification tasks. The masking strategy used is uniform and unstructured, with further exploration into the implications of the heterogeneous dimensions of audio spectrograms. Overall, the study highlights the importance of weight initialization and masking strategies in optimizing audio-visual model performance.
