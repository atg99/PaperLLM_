This paper presents a novel approach called Contrastive Audio-Visual Masked Auto-Encoder (CAV-MAE), which integrates contrastive audio-visual learning with masked data modeling to enhance the learning of joint representations from audio and visual data. The authors propose a framework that tokenizes audio spectrograms and RGB images into square patches, allowing for efficient processing and representation learning.

The framework consists of three primary components: a joint encoder, a joint decoder, and loss functions that include reconstruction loss and contrastive loss. The joint encoder utilizes separate Transformer-based encoders for audio and visual data to produce pooled representations. The contrastive learning aspect aims to leverage the natural pairing of audio and visual information in videos for self-supervised representation learning.

Additionally, the paper discusses the Vanilla Audio-Visual Masked Auto-Encoder (AV-MAE), which serves as a precursor to the CAV-MAE. While previous masked auto-encoder approaches have focused on single modalities, this work is significant as it extends the concept to the audio-visual domain, thereby allowing for the modeling of multi-modal data.

The authors highlight the effectiveness of CAV-MAE in performing well on multi-modal classification tasks and cross-modal retrieval tasks, showcasing its potential for advancing the field of audio-visual representation learning. Overall, this research contributes to the understanding and development of self-supervised learning methods for joint audio-visual representation.
