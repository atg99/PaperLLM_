The referenced papers cover a range of advancements in audio-visual learning, sound classification, and self-supervised learning techniques. 

1. VGGSound introduces a large-scale dataset aimed at improving audio-visual recognition tasks.
2. HTSAT proposes a hierarchical token-semantic audio transformer to enhance sound classification and detection.
3. The work on masked spectrogram prediction presents a self-supervised approach for audio pre-training, aiming to leverage unlabelled data effectively.
4. A sparsely activated model is discussed for multi-modal learning, incorporating text, sound, image, video, and code.
5. BERT's methodology for pre-training deep bidirectional transformers is highlighted for its language understanding capabilities.
6. Dosovitskiy et al. explore the use of transformers for image recognition, emphasizing their efficiency and scalability.
7. Fayek and Kumar focus on large-scale audiovisual learning from weakly labeled data, addressing challenges in data annotation.
8. Masked autoencoders are examined as spatiotemporal learners, enhancing the understanding of temporal dynamics in data.
9. The AudioSet dataset provides a comprehensive ontology for human-labeled audio events, aiding in various audio recognition tasks.
10. Research on multimodal masked autoencoders emphasizes the transferability of learned representations across domains.
11. The Omnimae model proposes a single framework for masked pretraining applicable to both images and videos.
12. Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST) and subsequent papers discuss improvements in audio tagging and self-supervised learning techniques.
13. The scalability of masked autoencoders in vision tasks is highlighted, showcasing their potential for large-scale applications.
14. Hubert focuses on self-supervised learning for speech representation through masked prediction, demonstrating effective encoding of speech data.
15. A recent study on masked autoencoders that listen explores the integration of audio processing with masked learning techniques.

Overall, these works reflect significant strides in leveraging self-supervised learning, multi-modal approaches, and large datasets to advance the fields of audio and visual recognition.
