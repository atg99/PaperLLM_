The paper presents the Contrastive Audio-Visual Masked Autoencoder (CAV-MAE), designed to enhance audio-visual representation learning by integrating contrastive learning with masked data modeling. Previous models, such as the Audio-Visual Masked Autoencoder (AV-MAE), primarily focused on reconstruction tasks, which limited their ability to establish explicit audio-visual correspondence. CAV-MAE addresses this limitation, allowing for a more coordinated joint representation of audio and visual data.

Experimental results demonstrate that CAV-MAE significantly outperforms baseline models that rely solely on either contrastive or masked data modeling objectives. This suggests that the two learning objectives are complementary, leading to a stronger joint audio-visual representation. CAV-MAE achieves a new state-of-the-art accuracy of 65.9% on the VGGSound dataset and shows comparable performance to the best supervised pretrained models on AudioSet. In audio-visual retrieval tasks, CAV-MAE either matches or exceeds the performance of models trained exclusively on the contrastive objective.

Furthermore, the multi-modal pretraining of CAV-MAE enhances single-modal performance, achieving new state-of-the-art results for audio-based event classification on AudioSet-20K and VGGSound. The contributions of this work include extending single-modal MAE to multi-modal AV-MAE, investigating the integration of contrastive learning with masked data modeling, and demonstrating the complementary nature of these objectives in achieving state-of-the-art audio-visual classification results.

The paper also details the pre-processing and tokenization methods used for audio and image inputs, alongside the architecture of the Transformer model employed in CAV-MAE, which includes multi-headed self-attention and feed-forward layers. Overall, CAV-MAE represents a significant advancement in the field of audio-visual learning, providing a robust framework for future research and applications.
