The paper presents a new model called Contrastive Audio-Visual Masked Autoencoder (CAV-MAE), which integrates the strengths of contrastive audio-visual learning and the vanilla audio-visual masked autoencoder (AV-MAE). The AV-MAE processes audio and image inputs by tokenizing them and projecting them into a shared embedding space. It utilizes a joint encoder to handle both modalities but lacks an explicit mechanism to effectively leverage paired audio-visual information, which may hinder its performance.

In contrast, CAV-MAE improves upon this by allowing independent processing of audio and visual tokens through separate Transformer encoders while still enabling multi-stream forward passes to a joint encoder. This design facilitates both contrastive learning and reconstruction tasks. The contrastive learning is achieved through a loss function that encourages the model to learn relationships between audio and visual representations, while the reconstruction task aims to accurately regenerate the original modalities from masked inputs.

The model architecture includes modality-specific embeddings and positional encodings for both audio and visual inputs, ensuring that the unique characteristics of each modality are preserved during processing. By balancing the benefits of both contrastive learning and masked autoencoding, CAV-MAE aims to enhance the understanding of audio-visual correlations, leading to improved performance in tasks involving multi-modal data.
