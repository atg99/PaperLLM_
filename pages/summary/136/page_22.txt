The paper presented at ICLR 2023 investigates the impact of different training masking strategies on audio spectrogram reconstruction and visual sound source localization using the CA V-MAE model. The findings reveal that CA V-MAE outperforms models trained with uniform masking, particularly in time and frequency masking scenarios, indicating its superior ability to leverage global information. While the choice of training masking strategy has a minor effect on downstream classification tasks, it significantly influences reconstruction performance.

In evaluating CA V-MAE for visual sound source localization, the authors found the model's performance to be inconsistent. Although it performs well in audio-visual retrieval, it occasionally mislocalizes sounds to background elements rather than the primary sound source. This issue is attributed to the masked contrastive learning objective, which may cause the model to depend on contextual information rather than accurately identifying the main sound source.

The study also examines the effect of audio-visual pairing information on model performance. By shuffling the original audio-visual pairs in the training dataset, the authors assessed how well the models could leverage audio-visual correlations. Results showed that the CA V model, reliant on audio-visual pairing, experienced a significant performance drop when the training data was shuffled. In contrast, the A V-MAE model showed little impact from shuffling, indicating a reliance on single-modality information. The CA V-MAE model performed similarly to A V-MAE with the shuffled dataset, but demonstrated better performance with the original dataset. These results emphasize the importance of combining contrastive and reconstruction objectives for optimal model performance, as models relying solely on contrastive objectives were less robust and effective.
