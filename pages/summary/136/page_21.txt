The paper presents an analysis of the reconstruction capabilities of a Contrastive Audio-Visual Masked Autoencoder (CAV-MAE) model, focusing on how various masking strategies affect reconstruction performance of audio spectrograms. The study evaluates the mean squared error (MSE) of audio reconstruction under different masking conditions, including time masking, frequency masking, time-frequency masking, and uniform unstructured masking.

Key findings indicate that the difficulty of reconstruction varies with the masking strategy employed. Specifically, time masking is the most challenging, followed by frequency masking, time-frequency masking, and then uniform masking. This suggests that the CAV-MAE model relies heavily on local information for effective reconstruction. Furthermore, the model trained with time-frequency masking outperforms the one trained with uniform masking, particularly in time and frequency masking scenarios, indicating its enhanced capability to utilize global information.

The paper also explores the reconstruction results from the VGGSound dataset, demonstrating the model's strong performance even at high masking ratios (up to 90%), which opens avenues for applications in in-painting and audio enhancement tasks. The results show that structured masking poses greater challenges for reconstruction, resulting in higher MSEs. Overall, the study underscores the significance of the masking strategy in shaping the reconstruction properties of the CAV-MAE model.
