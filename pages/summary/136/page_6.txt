The paper discusses an approach to audio-visual learning by utilizing self-supervised pretraining strategies. It begins by establishing that all models are initialized using ImageNet pretrained weights, specifically from the vision MAE, but unlike previous works that employed supervised weights, this study focuses solely on self-supervised pretrained weights. 

The authors pretrain and compare several models, including single-modal masked auto-encoders (Audio-MAE/Visual-MAE), a contrastive audio-visual learning model (CAV), and various configurations of audio-visual masked auto-encoders (Vanilla AV-MAE, AV-MAE, CAV-MAE, and CAV-MAEscale+). The models are pretrained on the full AudioSet-2M dataset using a consistent pipeline, ensuring the process remains self-supervised by avoiding class balanced sampling.

For evaluation, the pretrained models are fine-tuned on three datasets: AudioSet-20K, AudioSet-2M, and VGGSound, which vary in sample size and domain. The fine-tuning process employs a smaller learning rate for pretrained weights and a larger one for a randomly initialized classification head to preserve learned knowledge.

Key findings indicate that contrastive learning and masked data modeling are complementary. The proposed CAV-MAE, which integrates both objectives, significantly outperforms models relying on either method alone, illustrating the efficacy of combining these self-supervised learning frameworks in audio-visual learning contexts.
