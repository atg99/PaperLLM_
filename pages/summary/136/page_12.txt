The paper discusses advancements in multimodal learning, particularly in audio and visual representation. It references several key studies that contribute to this field. Notable works include the development of contrastive masked autoencoders, which are shown to enhance vision learning capabilities. The Perceiver model introduces iterative attention for general perception, while the Kinetics dataset provides a benchmark for human action recognition in videos. Other significant contributions involve the use of slow-fast auditory streams for audio recognition and large-scale pretrained audio neural networks aimed at audio pattern recognition. The paper also highlights cooperative learning approaches for synchronizing audio and video models, and efficient training methods for audio transformers. Additionally, advancements in masked modeling techniques for both audio and visual data are discussed, showcasing their effectiveness in improving multi-modal representation learning. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of self-supervised learning methods and the integration of audio-visual data for robust learning frameworks.
