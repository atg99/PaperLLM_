This paper discusses advancements in self-supervised learning for audio-visual tasks, emphasizing the integration of audio and visual modalities to enhance representation learning from video data. The authors reference several key studies and methodologies that inform their approach, such as masked autoencoders and contrastive learning techniques, which have proven effective in prior research.

The dataset utilized for experiments includes two significant audio-visual collections: AudioSet and VGGSound. AudioSet comprises over 2 million labeled video clips, allowing for extensive training with a variety of audio events. The subset, AudioSet-20K, offers a more balanced distribution of classes. VGGSound, containing 200,000 clips, is advantageous due to its focus on sound sources. The authors ensure that their pretraining approach remains self-supervised by relying solely on the labels during the fine-tuning phase.

By leveraging these datasets and existing literature, the paper aims to broaden the understanding and capabilities of self-supervised learning in video processing, ultimately aiming for improved performance in audio-visual tasks.
