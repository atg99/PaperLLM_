This paper presents results on audio-visual retrieval tasks using various models evaluated on the AudioSet, VGGSound, and MSR-VTT datasets. The findings highlight the effectiveness of the Contrastive Audio-Visual Masked Auto-Encoders (CA V-MAE) in comparison to traditional methods.

In Table 7, the performance of models with only Masked Data Modeling (MDM) loss shows low retrieval rates, while models with only contrastive loss demonstrate significantly improved results, particularly with varying contrastive temperature values. The CA V-MAE models also show strong performance, especially with a scale factor, indicating that the MAE training objective can enhance retrieval outcomes.

Table 8 focuses on bi-directional retrieval results on the MSR-VTT dataset, where the CA V and CA V-MAE models outperform existing methods in both audio-to-visual and visual-to-audio directions. The results suggest that the MAE objective either maintains or boosts retrieval performance.

Table 9 highlights zero-shot retrieval results, revealing that the CA V-MAE model, trained on a smaller dataset (2M AudioSet), achieves comparable performance to methods trained on the larger HowTo100M dataset, indicating the efficiency of the proposed models.

Overall, the results affirm that the CA V-MAE models not only surpass traditional approaches but also demonstrate robustness in retrieval tasks with limited training data.
