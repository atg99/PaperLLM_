This paper presents an extensive evaluation of a model called CAV-MAE for audio-visual classification and retrieval tasks. The authors conduct ablation studies to assess various design factors affecting model performance on datasets like AudioSet-20K and VGGSound. Key findings indicate that the weight of the contrastive loss significantly influences performance, with optimal values identified. Increasing pretraining epochs and batch sizes consistently enhances results, while normalizing prediction targets provides marginal benefits. Pretraining on larger datasets, like AudioSet-2M, yields better outcomes than smaller in-domain datasets, with the best results achieved through a two-step pretraining process. 

The study also reveals that utilizing outputs from the multi-modal encoder stream during fine-tuning is more effective than concatenating single-modal outputs. When focusing on a single modality, fine-tuning directly on single-modal data is preferable, although the performance gap is minimal for audio. The frame aggregation strategy improves performance without requiring multiple frames as input. In linear probing, CAV-MAE surpasses baseline models. 

In addition, the paper explores the model's capacity for audio-visual retrieval, demonstrating that CAV-MAE effectively learns a coordinated representation that captures correspondences between audio and visual inputs. The authors evaluate retrieval performance using a sample of audio-visual pairs, measuring recall at various ranks based on cosine similarity, confirming the model's robustness in capturing audio-visual relationships. Overall, the findings suggest that CAV-MAE is a promising approach for both audio-visual classification and retrieval tasks.
