The paper presents a comparative analysis of audio-visual classification performance across various models using datasets like AudioSet and VGGSound. The authors highlight the effectiveness of their proposed Contrastive Audio-Visual Learning (CAV) and Multi-Modal MAE models, which utilize self-supervised learning (SSL) for pretraining. Notably, the CAV-MAE model, pretrained with paired audio-visual data, demonstrates significant improvements in single-modal performance, surpassing existing models without requiring supervised pretraining or extensive computational resources.

The results indicate that CAV-MAE achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance in audio-based event classification, with 37.7 mAP on AudioSet-20K and 59.5% accuracy on VGGSound. The authors argue that the multi-modal pretraining allows the model to leverage richer information, as the modalities provide soft labels for each other, enhancing classification performance.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts its approach with previous SOTA models, noting that their fully SSL pretrained CAV-MAE model matches or outperforms these models while utilizing significantly fewer computational resources. The authors emphasize that their model's ability to utilize web-scale unlabeled videos during pretraining distinguishes it from models reliant on ImageNet supervised learning, which often leads to better visual branch performance. Despite the resource constraints, CAV-MAE outperformed existing audio-visual models, particularly on VGGSound, and showed comparable results on AudioSet datasets.
