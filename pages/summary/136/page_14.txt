This paper presents a conference study on the CAV-MAE model, focusing on audio-visual action recognition. The authors emphasize the visual clarity of video clips filtered through a pretrained vision classifier, acknowledging the performance variance due to different dynamic datasets as noted in previous research. To enhance reproducibility, they provide access to training and test sample IDs.

The paper details training parameters, indicating that most experiments utilized four NVIDIA GTX Titan X Pascal GPUs, while a scaled-up version was pretrained on four NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPUs. The CAV-MAE model features approximately 190 million parameters in its full encoder-decoder configuration, with variations for different downstream tasks.

The authors outline their pre-training and fine-tuning hyperparameters in a structured table, detailing aspects such as dataset, optimizer, learning rates, batch sizes, and the use of techniques like class balance sampling and random time shifting.

In the audio-visual action recognition experiments, the study addresses the challenge posed by visual-dominance in existing datasets. The authors conduct experiments on the Kinetics-Sounds dataset, a subset of Kinetics-400, which includes various human action classes. They report on the model's performance, specifically Top-1 accuracy on the validation set, to assess the efficacy of CAV-MAE against other models in the context of audio-visual action recognition without prior pretraining on AudioSet.
