The paper presents an analysis of the performance of the Contrastive Audio-Visual Masked Autoencoder (CA V-MAE) in joint event classification and zero-shot audio-visual retrieval across different training masking strategies and ratios. In the classification task, all masking strategies yield comparable results at a 75% masking ratio on AudioSet-20K and VGGSound. However, structured masking proves to be more challenging for reconstruction, leading to a CA V-MAE model trained with time-frequency masking at a reduced ratio of 50% achieving slightly superior performance.

In the zero-shot retrieval task, the choice of masking strategy significantly influences performance, with time-frequency masking showing the best results in retrieval rates for both audio-to-visual and visual-to-audio tasks. The results indicate that higher frame rates enhance classification performance across datasets, although improvements plateau as the number of frames increases.

Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of masking strategies and frame rates in optimizing audio-visual model performance, providing insights for future research in this domain.
