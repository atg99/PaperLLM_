This paper presents CA V-MAE, a novel model for audio-visual learning that integrates masked data modeling with contrastive learning to enhance audio-visual representation. The evaluation results on datasets such as AudioSet and VGGSound indicate that a contrastive objective is crucial for effective audio-visual retrieval, as models relying solely on masked autoencoders perform near random guessing. The combination of contrastive loss and masked data modeling not only improves classification performance but also maintains strong audio-visual retrieval capabilities. 

The study highlights that scaling batch size and training epochs positively impacts retrieval performance, and that the model exhibits competitive results even when tested on datasets different from the pretraining dataset. This suggests robust audio-visual correspondence and representation transfer. 

The paper also contextualizes its contributions within existing literature on contrastive audio-visual learning and masked auto-encoding, noting that previous works have primarily focused on single-modal scenarios. The authors assert that their approach is innovative as it uniquely leverages audio-visual pair information through a multi-stream joint encoder design.

In conclusion, the findings underscore the complementary nature of masked data modeling and contrastive learning, positioning CA V-MAE as a significant advancement in the field of audio-visual learning.
