The paper introduces the PSANet framework, which enhances global information aggregation through a bidirectional information propagation mechanism. It formulates aggregation as an information flow, represented mathematically to consider the positional relationships between features. The framework simplifies the complex function controlling this flow into adaptive attention weights, allowing it to effectively aggregate global information while emphasizing relevant features. This approach can be integrated into the end of convolutional neural networks to significantly improve semantic segmentation.

Additionally, the paper discusses temporal attention mechanisms, crucial for video processing, which help capture both short-term and long-term dependencies across frames. It critiques traditional methods like RNNs and temporal pooling for their inefficiencies and limitations in modeling temporal relationships. To address these issues, the authors present the Global-Local Temporal Representation (GLTR) model, which employs a dilated temporal pyramid for local context learning and a temporal self-attention module for global interactions. This model aggregates multi-scale temporal cues through dilated convolutions and utilizes self-attention to generate query, key, and value representations from input features. The paper outlines various temporal attention methods, highlighting their capabilities in achieving distinct goals related to temporal context and feature dependency.
