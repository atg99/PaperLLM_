The paper provides an overview of the evolution of attention mechanisms in computer vision, categorizing developments into four distinct phases. 

Phase 1 utilized recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for attention construction, exemplified by the RAM method. In Phase 2, attention mechanisms focused on predicting important regions within data, with DCNs serving as notable examples. The third phase introduced SENet, which developed a channel-attention network that adaptively predicts key features, alongside representative works like CBAM and ECANet.

The fourth phase marks the advent of self-attention, initially proposed for natural language processing and later adapted for computer vision by Wang et al. through the introduction of non-local networks. This phase has seen a proliferation of pure deep self-attention networks, such as visual transformers, demonstrating significant potential to advance attention-based models beyond traditional convolutional neural networks.

The authors aim to classify current attention methods based on data domains. They propose a framework that distinguishes six categories: four fundamental types—channel attention (what to focus on), spatial attention (where to focus), temporal attention (when to focus), and branch attention (which to focus on)—as well as two hybrid categories combining channel & spatial attention and spatial & temporal attention. The paper concludes with a summary of these categories and their associated works.
