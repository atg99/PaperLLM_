The paper discusses advancements in attention mechanisms within computer vision, focusing on two significant techniques: CondConv and dynamic convolution, which enhance network capabilities with minimal computational costs. CondConv utilizes a multi-branch structure and branch attention to improve efficiency.

Dynamic convolution, proposed by Chen et al., introduces an operator that employs multiple parallel convolution kernels, enhancing representational power without increasing the network's depth or width. It incorporates a squeeze-and-excitation mechanism to generate attention weights for the kernels, allowing for dynamic aggregation through weighted summation, which maintains low computational overhead compared to traditional convolutions.

The paper further explores channel and spatial attention mechanisms that merge the benefits of both approaches. The residual attention network (RAN) exemplifies this by integrating attention with residual connections. Each module features a mask branch for learning feature weights and a trunk branch for processing features, enhancing the network's ability to focus on important spatial and channel information.

Despite these advancements, the paper notes that some methods, including RAN, have high computational costs and limited receptive fields. Subsequent works have aimed to improve efficiency and global information capture through techniques like global average pooling and self-attention mechanisms. Overall, the study highlights the evolution and impact of attention mechanisms on the performance of computer vision models.
