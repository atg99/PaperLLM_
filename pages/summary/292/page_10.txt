The paper discusses various spatial attention mechanisms categorized by method and application area, detailing their mechanisms and goals. It presents a table summarizing representative methods, including RNN-based and self-attention-based approaches, highlighting their tasks, attention processes, ranges, and whether they employ soft or hard attention. The goals of these mechanisms range from focusing on discriminative regions and reducing computation for large images to capturing long-range dependencies and adapting neighborhood information.

A specific model, the glimpse network, is introduced, inspired by human visual recognition processes. This model employs a deep recurrent architecture that processes smaller sections of an image, termed glimpses, making it computationally efficient. The glimpse network consists of several components: a context network to initialize the recurrent network, a glimpse network to extract pertinent information, and a recurrent network to aggregate data from multiple glimpses. The model iteratively predicts new object locations and class labels, enhancing its classification performance through focused attention on relevant image parts.
