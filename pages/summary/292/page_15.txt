The paper discusses the advancements of attention mechanisms in computer vision, highlighting their effectiveness compared to traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It emphasizes the success of Vision Transformers (ViT), which outperform CNNs on large datasets such as JFT-300 and ImageNet-21K. Following ViT, numerous transformer-based architectures have emerged, including PCT, IPT, DeepViT, and Swin-transformer, demonstrating strong performance across various visual tasks like image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation.

The paper also introduces GENet, which builds on the concepts of SENet to capture long-range spatial contextual information through a recalibration function. GENet employs a gather-excite mechanism that aggregates input features from large neighborhoods and generates an attention map that scales the input features accordingly. This lightweight module enhances important features while minimizing noise and can be integrated into existing architectures.

Additionally, PSANet is mentioned for its ability to capture long-range dependencies, further illustrating the evolution and impact of attention mechanisms in enhancing computer vision tasks. The paper provides a comprehensive overview of these developments, noting that detailed surveys on vision transformers are available elsewhere.
