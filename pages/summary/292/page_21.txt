The paper discusses various attention mechanisms in computer vision, focusing on their roles in enhancing feature representation in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It specifically highlights two notable modules: the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) and the Bottleneck Attention Module (BAM).

CBAM integrates both channel and spatial attention to effectively guide the network's focus on important features. It employs global average pooling and global max pooling to create channel attention maps, followed by a convolutional layer to generate spatial attention maps. This dual attention mechanism allows CBAM to enhance informative regions while being lightweight enough for integration into existing CNN architectures. However, it notes that the spatial attention may have a limited receptive field due to its convolutional approach.

BAM, proposed concurrently with CBAM, seeks to improve representational capability while maintaining computational efficiency. It utilizes dilated convolutions to expand the receptive field and employs a bottleneck structure to reduce computational costs. BAM processes channel and spatial attention in parallel, summing the outputs after resizing. This module effectively emphasizes features across spatial and channel dimensions but may still struggle with capturing long-range contextual information.

Additionally, the paper introduces spatial and channel Squeeze-and-Excitation (scSE) blocks as an enhancement to the traditional SE block. The scSE blocks aim to incorporate pixel-wise spatial information, which is crucial for dense prediction tasks. It consists of two parallel modules: a channel SE block for channel attention and a spatial SE block that uses 1x1 convolutions for spatial attention. The outputs from these modules are then fused to provide a comprehensive attention mechanism.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of attention mechanisms in CNNs for improving feature representation and identifies areas for further enhancement in current models.
