The paper discusses various attention mechanisms in computer vision, focusing on their effectiveness in enhancing feature representation for tasks such as image segmentation. 

One highlighted approach is coordinate attention, which utilizes pooling kernels to encode spatial information both horizontally and vertically. This method employs a shared convolutional transformation to generate attention vectors, allowing the network to accurately locate targeted objects while maintaining a larger receptive field compared to previous methods like BAM and CBAM. Its lightweight design makes it suitable for mobile networks.

Another significant framework is the Dual Attention Network (DANet), which addresses limitations in encoder-decoder structures by incorporating self-attention mechanisms. DANet utilizes both position and channel attention modules in parallel to capture spatial and channel dependencies. The position attention module aggregates features based on positional similarity, while the channel attention module enhances relevant channels. This dual approach improves feature representation for scene segmentation, although it is computationally intensive for large input maps.

Additionally, the paper introduces Relation-aware Global Attention (RGA), which emphasizes the importance of global structural information through pairwise relations. RGA can be implemented in spatial and channel forms, enabling the computation of attention maps that focus on both spatial and relational information.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of how various attention mechanisms enhance computer vision tasks by improving feature representation and capturing global contextual information.
