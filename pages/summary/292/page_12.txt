Spatial Transformer Networks (STN) are introduced as the first attention mechanism that explicitly predicts significant areas, enabling a network to achieve transformation invariance through an affine transformation model. This model utilizes a learnable matrix to establish correspondences between input and output feature maps, allowing the network to sample relevant regions via bilinear sampling, ensuring end-to-end differentiability.

Deformable Convolutional Networks (Deformable ConvNets) are proposed as an alternative to STNs. They achieve invariance to geometric transformations by dividing convolution into two steps: sampling features on a regular grid and aggregating them through weighted summation. Deformable ConvNets introduce learnable offsets to adaptively sample features, enhancing the receptive field and improving performance in tasks such as object detection and semantic segmentation.

The paper also highlights the emergence of self-attention mechanisms, initially successful in natural language processing, which have shown potential in computer vision. Self-attention addresses the limited receptive fields of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) by capturing global information. It computes attention matrices through linear projections of feature maps, allowing for effective modeling of relationships between different elements. However, the quadratic complexity of self-attention poses challenges, leading to the development of various efficient variants to overcome these limitations. 

Overall, the mechanisms discussed enhance the ability of neural networks to focus on crucial features and understand contextual information, significantly impacting various visual tasks.
