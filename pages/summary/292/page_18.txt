The section discusses two advanced convolution techniques, SKNet and CondConv, which enhance the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) while maintaining computational efficiency.

SKNet is inspired by the adaptive behavior of visual cortical neurons, leading to the development of selective kernel convolution. This method involves three main operations: splitting the feature map into branches with different kernel sizes, fusing the information from these branches through element-wise summation to create a gate vector, and selecting the output feature map based on this gate vector. This adaptive mechanism allows the network to dynamically adjust receptive field sizes, resulting in improved performance without significant computational overhead. SK convolutions can be integrated into any CNN architecture, replacing larger kernel convolutions, and are utilized in models like ResNeSt to further enhance results.

CondConv, proposed by Yang et al., challenges the traditional assumption that all convolution kernels in CNNs are identical. Instead, it introduces a multi-branch operator that adaptively combines multiple convolution kernels. This is achieved by computing a learnable weight vector that determines the contribution of each kernel during convolution. This approach effectively creates an ensemble of multiple experts, allowing for increased representational power without the substantial computational costs typically associated with increasing the depth or width of the network. CondConv is presented as a more efficient alternative to traditional convolution methods.
