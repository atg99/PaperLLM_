The paper discusses various methods for incorporating spatial and temporal attention mechanisms in tasks such as action recognition and re-identification (ReID). It highlights the evolution of attention techniques, particularly focusing on the performance of different models in extracting crucial features from video data.

A summary of the key methods presented includes:

1. **STA-LSTM**: This model predicts spatial and temporal attention separately. The spatial attention is derived from a hidden state through a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) followed by a softmax function, while the temporal attention uses a ReLU activation. This approach emphasizes key points in both domains.

2. **RSTAN**: This method introduces a spatiotemporal attention mechanism that operates in two stages: first applying spatial attention to produce global features for each frame, and then using temporal attention to assess the importance of frame features. The model reshapes input feature maps and employs a series of calculations to derive attention scores, effectively capturing essential features across both spatial and temporal contexts.

3. **STA**: This model addresses the limitations of previous methods that only assigned attention weights to frames. It jointly predicts spatial and temporal attention, capturing intricate relationships between different joints and frames, thereby enhancing performance in ReID tasks.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of joint spatiotemporal attention mechanisms in improving action recognition and ReID outcomes, demonstrating superior representation capabilities in video analysis.
