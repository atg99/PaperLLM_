This survey article reviews the development and application of attention mechanisms in computer vision, inspired by the human ability to focus on salient regions in complex scenes. Attention mechanisms function as dynamic weight adjustments based on image features, enhancing performance across various visual tasks such as image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, video understanding, and more. The authors categorize attention mechanisms into several types, including channel, spatial, temporal, and branch attention, providing a comprehensive overview of each.

The article outlines the evolution of attention mechanisms in computer vision over the last decade, dividing it into four phases. The initial phase began with the introduction of RAM, which integrated deep neural networks with attention mechanisms, utilizing recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for end-to-end learning. The subsequent phase saw advancements like the Spatial Transformer Network (STN), which enabled the selection of important regions through learned transformations.

The authors also emphasize the significant impact of attention mechanisms on enhancing visual task performance and suggest future research directions to further explore this field. A related repository is provided for additional resources on attention mechanisms in vision.
