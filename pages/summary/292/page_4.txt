This paper presents a comprehensive review of attention methods in computer vision, distinguishing itself from previous surveys that primarily focused on natural language processing or specific types of models. Unlike earlier works, this paper classifies attention mechanisms according to their data domain rather than their application field, allowing for a more focused analysis of the mechanisms themselves.

In Section 3, the authors outline a general formulation of attention mechanisms inspired by human visual processing, expressed mathematically as Attention = f(g(x), x). Here, g(x) generates attention corresponding to discriminative regions, while f(g(x), x) processes the input based on this attention. The paper illustrates this formulation with examples, including self-attention and squeeze-and-excitation (SE) attention.

The paper further explores various categories of attention models, beginning with channel attention, which recalibrates the weights of different channels in feature maps to enhance object selection. The seminal work on channel attention, SENet, is detailed, highlighting its use of squeeze and excitation modules to collect global information and capture channel-wise relationships. The formulation of SENet's SE block is provided, emphasizing its role in improving representation ability through attention scaling.

Overall, this review emphasizes the evolution and classification of attention mechanisms in computer vision, contributing to a deeper understanding of their functionalities and applications.
