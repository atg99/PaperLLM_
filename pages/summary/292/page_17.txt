The paper discusses various attention mechanisms in computer vision, focusing on their ability to enhance representation capabilities while managing computational efficiency. One notable method is the Global-Local Temporal Representation (GLTR), which combines local and long-term temporal information to improve feature representation and mitigate noise. However, GLTR's quadratic time complexity restricts its broader application.

To address this, Liu et al. introduced the Temporal Adaptive Module (TAM), which utilizes an adaptive kernel for capturing global contextual information with reduced computational costs. TAM consists of two branches: a local branch that enhances frame-wise features using 1D convolutions and a global branch that generates a channel-wise adaptive kernel from global temporal information. This design allows TAM to effectively capture complex temporal structures in videos while being lightweight enough to integrate into existing 2D CNN architectures.

Additionally, the paper highlights branch attention mechanisms, particularly the Highway Network. This network employs adaptive gating mechanisms to facilitate information flow across layers, addressing challenges in training deep networks. The gating mechanism, which adapts to input, enables efficient routing of information without fixed skip-connections, making it versatile for integration into any CNN.

Overall, the survey emphasizes the significance of attention mechanisms in improving feature representation and computational efficiency in computer vision tasks.
