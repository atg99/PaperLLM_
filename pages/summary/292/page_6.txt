The paper presents a comprehensive overview of various channel attention mechanisms, categorized by their publication date and key objectives, which include emphasizing important channels and capturing global information. The mechanisms are applied across several tasks such as classification, detection, semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, style transfer, and action recognition.

A detailed analysis of specific methods is provided, including Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks (SENet), which utilizes global average pooling followed by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) and sigmoid activation to generate attention maps. Other methods like the Improved Squeeze-and-Excitation Module (ECANet) and the Generalized Second-order Pooling Network (GSoP-Net) are also discussed, highlighting their unique approaches to enhancing channel attention through different pooling and normalization techniques.

The GSoP block, in particular, is noted for its ability to create a covariance matrix to assess channel correlations, followed by row-wise normalization and convolution to maintain structural information, ultimately yielding a c-dimensional attention vector. This process allows the GSoP block to improve global information collection compared to traditional approaches, albeit with increased computational demands.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution and effectiveness of channel attention mechanisms, showcasing their importance in improving performance across various computer vision tasks.
