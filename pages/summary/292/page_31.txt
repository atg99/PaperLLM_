This survey paper examines the application of attention mechanisms in computer vision, highlighting their significance in enhancing model performance across various tasks. Attention mechanisms allow models to focus on specific parts of the input data, improving the relevance of features used in processing.

Key studies referenced include the development of Expectation-Maximization Attention Networks for semantic segmentation, which enhance segmentation accuracy through refined attention techniques. The CCNet introduces criss-cross attention to improve segmentation performance by effectively modeling relationships between pixels. Additionally, the paper discusses the introduction of stand-alone self-attention in vision models, which marks a shift towards more efficient architectures in computer vision.

The survey also covers various architectures that integrate attention mechanisms, such as the Tokens-to-Token Vision Transformer and the Pyramid Vision Transformer, which demonstrate the versatility of attention across different types of visual data. Other notable contributions include the Swin Transformer and the introduction of convolutions in vision transformers, showcasing hybrid approaches that combine the strengths of both methodologies.

The paper emphasizes the evolving landscape of attention in computer vision, presenting a comprehensive overview of recent advancements and their implications for future research and applications in the field.
