The paper discusses various approaches to integrating self-attention mechanisms in deep learning for tasks such as semantic segmentation, object detection, and action recognition. It highlights methods like HamNet, which formulates global relationship modeling as a low-rank completion problem, enhancing interpretability while reducing complexity. EANet emphasizes the importance of focusing on correlations within single samples and introduces external attention mechanisms to minimize computational demands.

Additionally, the paper explores the use of local self-attention as an alternative to convolution operations in neural networks. The SASA model demonstrates that local self-attention can replace spatial convolution, improving computational efficiency and result quality. Concurrently, LR-Net investigates local relationships with a focus on positional embedding and kernel size, while SAN introduces adaptive vector attention for local feature aggregation, proving effective in both image and 3D point cloud processing.

Finally, the paper notes the emergence of vision transformers, particularly the ViT architecture, which leverages multi-head attention to process images similarly to natural language tasks. ViT achieves competitive performance against convolutional neural networks, showcasing the versatility and potential of transformer models in computer vision applications.
