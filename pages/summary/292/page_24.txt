The paper discusses various advanced techniques in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) aimed at enhancing feature representation and improving performance in tasks such as image classification, instance segmentation, and object detection. 

1. **Spatial Relation-Aware Feature**: The paper introduces a formulation for spatial relation-aware features, which combines global average pooling and spatial attention scores to enhance representational power in CNNs. This approach, referred to as RGA, utilizes global relations to generate attention scores for each feature node, facilitating the capture of structural information.

2. **Self-Calibrated Convolutions**: Liu et al. proposed self-calibrated convolutions to increase the receptive field at spatial locations. This method involves splitting the input features, applying average pooling, and then using convolution and bilinear interpolation to model channel relationships, ultimately enhancing network adaptability and achieving superior results in various tasks.

3. **Strip Pooling (SPNet)**: To address the limitations of traditional spatial pooling, Hou et al. introduced strip pooling, which encodes long-range context in both horizontal and vertical directions. This method employs two branches for pooling and uses 1D convolution to capture relationships across different rows and channels, thus producing a comprehensive attention map.

4. **SCA-CNN**: Chen et al. presented SCA-CNN, which integrates spatial and channel-wise attention mechanisms within an encoder-decoder framework for image captioning. The spatial attention model focuses on semantically significant regions, guided by the LSTM hidden state, allowing for a more nuanced understanding of the input image.

Overall, these innovations demonstrate a trend towards leveraging attention mechanisms and advanced pooling techniques to improve the performance and adaptability of CNN architectures in various computer vision tasks.
