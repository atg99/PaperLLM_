This paper provides a survey of attention mechanisms in computer vision, focusing on two notable approaches: the spatiotemporal attention (STA) mechanism and the spatiotemporal graph convolutional network (STGCN).

The STA approach, proposed by et al. [132], generates frame-wise attention maps from input video feature maps without introducing additional parameters. It utilizes l2 normalization to compute attention scores for spatial regions across video frames. By dividing the feature maps and attention maps into local regions, STA captures spatial attention scores and applies l1 normalization to account for temporal relationships. This method effectively reduces the impact of occlusion and is adaptable to variable-length video sequences, making it compatible with various 2D CNN backbones.

On the other hand, STGCN, introduced by Yang et al. [178], aims to model both spatial and temporal relationships within video frames. It constructs a patch graph based on pairwise similarities, incorporating two parallel branches: a temporal graph module and a structural graph module. The temporal module treats each patch as a graph node, using normalized pairwise relations to create an adjacency matrix. STGCN employs graph convolution to aggregate information, capturing spatiotemporal relationships while addressing occlusion issues through pairwise attention.

The paper concludes with thoughts on future research directions, particularly the exploration of necessary and sufficient conditions for attention mechanisms, highlighting the need for further investigation in this area.
