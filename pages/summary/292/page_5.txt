The paper discusses the role of Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks in enhancing visual attention mechanisms within computer vision systems. SE blocks emphasize important channels while suppressing noise, making them valuable for improving model performance. They can be integrated after each residual unit due to their low computational overhead. However, the authors point out limitations of SE blocks. The global average pooling in the squeeze module is noted as too simplistic for capturing complex global information, while the fully-connected layers in the excitation module can complicate the model.

To address these shortcomings, subsequent research has led to advancements in the design of attention mechanisms. For instance, GSoP-Net introduces a global second-order pooling (GSoP) block to enhance the squeeze module, allowing for the capture of high-order statistics alongside global information. This improvement aims to overcome the limitations of first-order statistics utilized by SE blocks. The paper highlights the evolution of these mechanisms and their significance in refining visual attention in computer vision applications.
