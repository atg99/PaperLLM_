The paper discusses the challenges of reproducibility in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) research, particularly on benchmarks like CIFAR-10 and PTB. It highlights that earlier NAS results often lacked reproducibility due to high computational demands, although recent advancements have reduced these costs. Despite some authors releasing their code, many significant works, including one closely related to this study, have not, leading to a lack of exact reproducibility across recent publications.

The authors present a summary in Table 5 of the appendix, which shows that none of the 12 reviewed papers achieved exact reproducibility, and many only reported the performance of a single architecture from one run, which complicates the assessment of novel contributions. They commend DARTS for acknowledging its reliance on random initialization and employing multiple runs for better results. 

The methodology section introduces a NAS algorithm that integrates random search and weight-sharing, applicable to arbitrary search spaces represented as directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). Using DARTS as a framework, the authors detail the process of sampling architectures, which involves selecting nodes and corresponding operations within the defined search space.

The algorithm operates by:
1. Defining decisions needed for each node in the DAG.
2. Identifying possible choices for each decision.
3. Uniformly sampling from the options for each node as it progresses through the architecture.

By training shared weights across different architectures and updating them through back-propagation, the authors evaluate various sampled architectures based on their performance on a separate dataset, ultimately selecting the best-performing architecture. This approach aims to enhance both the efficiency and effectiveness of NAS while addressing reproducibility concerns.
