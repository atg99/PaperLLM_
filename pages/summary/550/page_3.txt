The paper discusses the inadequacies in existing comparisons between NAS methods and traditional hyperparameter optimization techniques, particularly random search. It highlights that many studies do not effectively compare with random search using partial training, which is significantly faster than full training methods. The authors argue for the use of Adaptive SHyperband (ASHA) as a competitive baseline, as it has demonstrated superior performance in hyperparameter optimization.

The complexity of NAS methods arises from the need to adapt search techniques for NAS-specific search spaces, which often involve discrete hyperparameters represented in a directed acyclic graph (DAG) format. Various methods such as evolutionary approaches, Bayesian optimization, gradient-based techniques, and reinforcement learning introduce significant complexity without a definitive superior method. To mitigate this, the authors propose using random search to sample architectures, simplifying the search process.

The paper also examines the evaluation methods tailored for NAS, such as network morphisms, hypernetworks, and weight-sharing, which aim to enhance the efficiency of architecture evaluation. While these methods can reduce evaluation costs significantly, they often sacrifice fidelity to actual performance. Among these, weight-sharing is identified as particularly effective, requiring less computational overhead and yielding competitive results.

The authors draw inspiration from previous work that demonstrated the efficacy of random search combined with shared weights in differentiating between architectures. They aim to enhance this approach by simplifying training routines and extending experimental validation beyond previous benchmarks, particularly addressing the limitations encountered in prior studies.
