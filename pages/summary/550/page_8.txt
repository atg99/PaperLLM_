The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of weight-sharing methods for architecture search using the CIFAR-10 benchmark, focusing on the performance of various configurations in two stages of search. In stage one, shared weights are trained through random search with different parameters. The best architectures from this stage are then retrained from scratch for 600 epochs in stage two.

Table 2 summarizes the results of the best architectures after retraining across different settings. Notably, Random (5), which increased the proxy network size and the number of evaluated architectures, yielded the best performance with a test error of 2.83. The authors highlight that while DARTS showed reproducibility with an average test error of 2.94, there was instability in architecture rankings between evaluations at 100 and 600 epochs, indicating potential variability in performance.

Table 3 further explores this instability by comparing the test errors of DARTS architectures retrained for 100 and 600 epochs. The results reveal significant fluctuations in ranking, emphasizing the need for caution when interpreting performance signals during architecture search. The authors conclude that their approach of retraining architectures for a longer duration ensures more reliable evaluations across multiple trials, particularly for DARTS and Random (5) methods. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of thorough retraining in assessing the effectiveness of neural architecture search methods.
