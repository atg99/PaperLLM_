This section presents a comparison of various neural architecture search (NAS) methods and manually designed networks on the CIFAR-10 benchmark, detailing their test errors, parameters, search costs, and methodologies. The results are categorized into manually designed networks, published NAS methods, and the evaluated methods, with all models trained using cutout for consistency. 

Key findings include:

- The test errors for methods like Shake-Shake and PyramidNet are reported, indicating their performance in comparison to NAS approaches.
- Notable NAS methods include NASNet-A, AmoebaNet-B, ProxylessNAS, GHN, SNAS, and ENAS, each showing varying test errors and requiring different search costs measured in GPU days.
- The DARTS method is highlighted for its variance in test error based on the number of trials and epochs used, with the authors emphasizing the instability in architecture selection during evaluations.
- The authors conducted independent runs and broad reproducibility experiments, specifically for DARTS and ENAS, revealing discrepancies in reported results and emphasizing the importance of longer training durations for stable outcomes.
- The impact of meta-hyperparameters on achieving competitive performance through random search with weight-sharing is discussed, detailing adjustments made across multiple trials to optimize the architecture evaluation process.

Overall, the paper underscores the complexities and variabilities involved in NAS, particularly in reproducibility and the influence of training practices on performance outcomes.
