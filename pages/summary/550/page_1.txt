This undermines the credibility of the findings and complicates the process of comparing different NAS methods.

In this paper, Liam Li and Ameet Talwalkar propose new baselines for NAS grounded in two key observations: NAS can be viewed as a specialized hyperparameter optimization problem, and random search is a competitive method for hyperparameter optimization. They evaluate both a traditional random search with early-stopping and an innovative random search with weight-sharing on two established NAS benchmarks, PTB and CIFAR-10. 

The results indicate that random search with early-stopping performs comparably to the ENAS method, which is a prominent NAS approach. Furthermore, the weight-sharing variant outperforms the early-stopping method, achieving state-of-the-art results on PTB and highly competitive results on CIFAR-10. 

The authors emphasize the need for more rigorous baselines in NAS research, as many existing methods have not been adequately compared to leading hyperparameter optimization strategies. They also highlight the issue of complexity in NAS methods, which often lack clarity regarding the essential components that contribute to their success. Lastly, the paper addresses the reproducibility crisis in NAS, calling for standardized practices to enhance the reliability of experimental results. This work contributes to the ongoing discourse on improving NAS methodologies and fostering reproducibility in the field.
