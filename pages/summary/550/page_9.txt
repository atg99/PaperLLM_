The paper investigates the reproducibility and performance of random search with weight-sharing in neural architecture search (NAS), specifically using the CIFAR-10 dataset. Results from six independent runs show an average test error of 2.92, with some variance observed across runs. Additional evaluations indicate that while two out of five new runs matched the performance of the first, the others underperformed yet still surpassed the results of the ASHA method. This highlights the high variance inherent in random search with weight-sharing.

The conclusion emphasizes the need for better baselines to assess NAS methods, suggesting that a multiplicative speedup metric relative to standard hyperparameter optimization approaches would be beneficial. The paper calls for ablation studies to isolate the effects of individual NAS components, given that random search with weight-sharing performs comparably to established NAS methods like ENAS, SNAS, and DARTS. 

Furthermore, it stresses the importance of reproducibility in scientific research, noting that NAS results are often reported from single runs rather than the standard ten independent runs used in traditional optimization studies. This lack of reproducibility complicates the evaluation of NAS methods and necessitates either increased computational resources or the development of more accessible benchmarks.

The authors acknowledge contributions from various individuals and institutions, and their work was supported by multiple grants, underscoring the collaborative effort behind the research.
