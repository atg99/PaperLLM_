However, ASHA demonstrated a more stable performance due to its systematic evaluation of over 700 architectures, yielding a best architecture with a competitive average error of 3.03, closely matching the performance of DARTS and surpassing random search baselines. 

For the CIFAR-10 benchmark, the architecture search space included various convolutional operations, and the evaluation process was divided into two stages: a proxy network for initial searches and a proxyless network for final evaluation. The ASHA method outperformed previous random search strategies, achieving results comparable to established methods like DARTS and SNAS. 

The findings suggest that the gap between state-of-the-art Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods and standard hyperparameter optimization is narrower than previously thought. The study also highlights the implications of meta-hyperparameter tuning on search efficiency and reproducibility, indicating that future work could focus on leveraging larger proxyless networks to further enhance performance.
