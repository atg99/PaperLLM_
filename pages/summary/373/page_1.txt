This study explores the chemical composition and bioactivity of essential oils and ethanolic extracts from Ocimum basilicum L. and Thymus algeriensis Boiss. & Reut., both sourced from the Algerian Saharan Atlas. It addresses the growing demand in the pharmaceutical and food industries for natural compounds with bioactive properties, aiming to replace synthetic alternatives.

The research employs various methods to analyze the plants' extracts. Total flavonoid and phenol contents were quantified using the aluminium chloride and Folin-Ciocalteu methods, respectively. Essential oils were extracted via hydrodistillation and analyzed using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS). The study assessed the antioxidant potential through multiple assays, including DPPH radical-scavenging and ABTS radical-scavenging tests. Antimicrobial activities were evaluated against a range of pathogens, while anticancer effects were measured using the MTT assay to determine the lethal doses resulting in a 50% reduction in cancer cell growth.

Results indicated a rich flavonoid content in the ethanolic extracts, with the essential oil from Ocimum basilicum containing 26 unique compounds, primarily linalool and linalyl acetate. The essential oil from Thymus algeriensis comprised 29 compounds, with α-terpinyl acetate being the most abundant. Both plants exhibited moderate antioxidant activity and varying levels of antimicrobial efficacy. Notably, the essential oils demonstrated anticancer potential against human cancer cell lines, with LD50 values ranging from 300 to 1000 μg/mL.

In conclusion, this research highlights the potential of Ocimum basilicum and Thymus algeriensis as sources of bioactive compounds, supporting their application in food and pharmaceutical industries.
