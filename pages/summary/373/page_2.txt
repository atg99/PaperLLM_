The study investigates the chemical composition and biological activities of essential oils and ethanolic extracts from Ocimum basilicum and Thymus algeriensis, two plants from the Lamiaceae family, cultivated in the Algerian Saharan Atlas. These plants are known for their medicinal properties, particularly their antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anticancer activities, which have significant implications for nutraceutical and pharmaceutical applications.

Ocimum basilicum, commonly known as basil, and Thymus algeriensis, a type of thyme, have been utilized in traditional medicine for various ailments, including gastrointestinal and respiratory issues. The research highlights the presence of various bioactive compounds within these plants, such as flavonoids, phenolic acids, and terpenes, which contribute to their health benefits.

The methodology involved harvesting the plants during their flowering stage, preparing ethanolic extracts through a standardized protocol, and measuring the total phenolic content using the Folin-Ciocalteu method. The yields of the extracts were found to be 20.16% for O. basilicum and 15.78% for T. algeriensis.

Overall, the findings underscore the potential of these plant extracts as sources of bioactive compounds that could be harnessed for health-related applications, reinforcing the importance of traditional medicinal plants in modern science.
