The study investigates the antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anticancer activities of extracts and essential oils from two plants. The antioxidant capacity was assessed using the ascorbic acid equivalent antioxidant capacity (AEAC) through two assays: the reducing power assay and the phosphomolybdenum assay. The reducing power was determined by mixing the extracts with distilled water and FeCl3, measuring absorbance at 700 nm, while the phosphomolybdenum assay involved heating the extracts with a reagent solution and measuring absorbance at 695 nm.

For antimicrobial activity, the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) was determined for eight microorganisms, including various gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria and fungi. The microdilution method was employed to evaluate the efficacy of the extracts against these pathogens, with ampicillin and amphotericin B serving as positive controls.

The anticancer activity was evaluated using the MTT assay on several cancer cell lines. Cells were treated with different concentrations of the extracts, and viability was assessed by measuring absorbance at 570 nm. Growth inhibition was calculated, and the results were expressed as the concentration required for 50% growth suppression (LD50).

Data were analyzed statistically, with results reported as mean ± standard deviation. The study also included measurements of total phenolic and flavonoid contents of the extracts, which were quantified using the Folin-Ciocalteu and AlCl3 methods, respectively. Overall, the findings suggest that the extracts possess significant antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anticancer properties.
