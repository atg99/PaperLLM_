The paper discusses various analytical methods for estimating the total flavonoid and phenolic content in plant extracts, isolation of essential oils, and evaluation of their antioxidant activities. 

Total flavonoid content was measured using the aluminium chloride colorimetric method, where a diluted extract was mixed with a 2% AlCl3 methanol solution, incubated, and then analyzed at 430 nm. Results were expressed as rutin equivalents per gram of dry weight.

For phenolic content analysis, High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) was employed, utilizing a C-18 column and a gradient elution method. Various phenolic compounds were identified using retention times and UV spectra compared to pure standards, with quantities reported in micrograms per gram of extract.

Essential oils were extracted via hydrodistillation from dried plant material and subsequently analyzed using Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS). This involved comparing retention times and linear retention indices against standards and a mass spectral library for identification.

Antioxidant activities were assessed through several assays. The DPPH radical scavenging capacity was measured by mixing extracts or oils with a DPPH solution and calculating the scavenging effect. The ABTS assay involved creating an ABTS radical cation and measuring the inhibition percentage after adding the test samples. Lastly, the reducing power was determined using a method involving potassium ferricyanide and trichloroacetic acid, with absorbance measurements taken post-reaction.

Overall, the study provides a comprehensive analysis of the chemical composition and antioxidant potential of specific plant extracts and essential oils.
