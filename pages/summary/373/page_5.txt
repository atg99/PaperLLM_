The study investigates the phenolic and flavonoid content, essential oil yield, and chemical composition of ethanolic extracts from Ocimum basilicum and Thymus algeriensis. The findings indicate that O. basilicum contains higher phenolic (226 mg/g) and flavonoid (213 mg/g) content compared to T. algeriensis, which has 125 mg/g and 118 mg/g, respectively. HPLC analysis identified 15 phenolic compounds, with O. basilicum showing high levels of rutin, epicatechin, and vanillic acid, while T. algeriensis had epicatechin as the predominant compound.

The essential oil yields were 1.05% for O. basilicum and 0.51% for T. algeriensis, with 61 compounds identified overall. O. basilicum's essential oil, classified as a linalool-linalyl acetate chemotype, predominantly contains linalool and linalyl acetate. In contrast, T. algeriensis's essential oil is characterized by α-terpinyl acetate, neryl acetate, and α-pinene, with a unique composition that does not include thymol.

The study also assessed antioxidant activities using the DPPH assay, demonstrating the potential of both plants for antioxidant applications. Overall, the research highlights the significant phytochemical properties of O. basilicum and T. algeriensis, contributing to their potential uses in food and medicinal industries.
