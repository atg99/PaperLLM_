The study evaluates the antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anticancer activities of ethanolic extracts (EE) and essential oils (EO) from Ocimum basilicum and Thymus algeriensis. The antioxidant capacity was measured using various assays, with the EE of O. basilicum showing the highest antioxidant effectiveness (IC50 of 3.657 µg/mL) compared to the EO of T. algeriensis (IC50 of 1.387 µg/mL). The phosphomolybdenum assay indicated that O. basilicum oil had the highest antioxidant equivalent to ascorbic acid and surpassed vitamin E in antioxidant capacity.

For antimicrobial activity, both plant extracts and essential oils exhibited superior effects against gram-positive bacteria, with essential oils demonstrating greater potency than the ethanolic extracts. Moderate antifungal activity was noted against Candida species. The presence of compounds like quercetin and rutin in the extracts is highlighted as a contributing factor to their antimicrobial properties.

The anticancer potential was assessed on five human cancer cell lines, revealing antiproliferative effects from both EE and EO. Overall, the study underscores the significant bioactive properties of O. basilicum and T. algeriensis, supporting their potential use in health-related applications.
