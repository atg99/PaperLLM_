This study investigates the chemical composition and bioactivity of essential oils and ethanolic extracts from Ocimum basilicum and Thymus algeriensis, cultivated in the Algerian Saharan Atlas. The lethality of these substances was assessed using LD50 values, which indicate the concentration required to reduce cancer cell survival by 50%. Doxorubicin served as a positive control, showing LD50 values between 1-20 μg/mL across various cancer cell lines. The essential oils demonstrated significantly greater efficacy in inhibiting cancer cell growth compared to ethanolic extracts, attributed to their rich phytochemical constituents, including 1,8-cineole and linalool, as well as polyphenols like quercetin. 

HPLC analysis revealed a high flavonoid content in the ethanolic extracts, while GC-MS identified linalool and linalyl acetate as major components in O. basilicum oil, and α-terpinyl acetate and neryl acetate in T. algeriensis oil. The study also found diverse antimicrobial activities against a range of pathogens, with essential oils showing enhanced potency against gram-positive bacteria. Moderate antioxidant activities were noted, suggesting potential applications in pharmaceuticals and food technology. 

The research was funded by The Deanship of Scientific Research at The University of Jordan and The University of Laghouat, with all authors contributing to the study design, experimentation, and manuscript preparation. Data supporting the findings are available upon request, and ethical approval was not required as the study did not involve human participants.
