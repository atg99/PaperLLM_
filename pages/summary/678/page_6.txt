The paper presents a study on the classification of cancer and normal cell images using a deep learning approach. It details the organization of the dataset into training and testing subsets, specifically employing a 7-fold cross-validation strategy. For cancer cell images, a total of 41 subjects contributed 7049 images, while 20 subjects provided 3176 normal cell images. The testing dataset included 12 subjects with a total of 436 images.

Image preprocessing involved resizing the input images to 224x224 pixels to align with the VGG16 model requirements, followed by normalization using the mean and standard deviation of the training set's RGB values.

To address the challenge of limited dataset size and the issue of class imbalance, the study employed various data augmentation techniques. These included flipping, cropping, and rotation to create diverse copies of the original images, enhancing the model's generalization capabilities. The paper emphasizes that training with augmented images can reduce error rates and improve overall model performance, particularly in the context of the imbalanced CNM-C 2019 dataset. The augmentation process is executed dynamically for each image, generating multiple augmented versions to support robust training.
