The paper discusses the limitations of existing methods for classifying Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL) cell images, primarily due to the use of small datasets and conventional machine learning algorithms. These approaches often lead to overfitting and suboptimal performance, as they rely on handcrafted features that may not be ideal for classification. The datasets in the literature range from 19 to 958 images, which constrains the effectiveness of the models.

The paper highlights the advantages of deep learning-based methods, particularly deep neural networks, which can automatically extract relevant features through convolutional filters, addressing the shortcomings of predefined feature sets. However, the performance of these models is also contingent on the size of the dataset, which is a challenge in medical image analysis. To mitigate the effects of limited data, techniques such as transfer learning and data augmentation are suggested.

Several studies are referenced, illustrating different approaches to classifying ALL subtypes using deep learning. For example, Rehman et al. employed a pre-trained AlexNet on a dataset of 330 images, while Ahmad et al. utilized 903 images and several data augmentation techniques to improve classification outcomes. Despite these advancements, many studies still rely on small datasets (24 to 903 images), which can lead to biased classification results.

The paper also notes the challenges in classifying ALL cell images due to their high intraclass homogeneity and low interclass separability. It points out that using pre-trained models, which require input images to conform to specific dimensions, may alter the morphology of the ALL cells, potentially affecting classification accuracy. Overall, the need for larger datasets and more robust algorithms is emphasized to enhance diagnostic accuracy in ALL classification.
