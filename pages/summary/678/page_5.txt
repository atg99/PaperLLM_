The paper discusses the development of a robust classifier for Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL) cell images using the C-NMC 2019 dataset, which consists of 10,661 single-cell images from 76 subjects. The dataset includes 7,272 images from 47 patients with ALL and 3,389 from 26 healthy subjects. Previous models for ALL classification, such as those by Kasani et al. and Mourya et al., utilized larger datasets but did not account for subject-level variability, which is crucial for enhancing classifier performance. This study addresses this limitation by employing a seven-fold cross-validation strategy that ensures distinct training and testing datasets based on subject data, thus preventing overlap.

The proposed model integrates an attention module with a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to refine feature extraction and improve classification accuracy by addressing morphological similarities in cell images. This approach is novel as it represents the first application of an attention module in CNNs specifically for ALL classification. The dataset underwent preprocessing to standardize image quality, and various data augmentation techniques were applied to prevent overfitting and maintain balance. Overall, the study aims to create a reliable computer-aided design system for differentiating between normal and malignant cells in microscopic images.
