The paper discusses a model based on VGG16 architecture for Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL) classification, emphasizing its effectiveness and simplicity. The dataset distribution before and after data augmentation is detailed in Table 2, showing a significant increase in the number of cancer and normal cell images across multiple folds. For cancer cell images, the total increased from 7049 to 14,098, while normal cell images rose from 3176 to 12,704.

The choice of VGG16 is motivated by two main factors. First, its design utilizes smaller kernel sizes and fewer layers compared to other architectures like VGG19, which helps in reducing the number of hyper-parameters. This design consists of convolutional layers with 3x3 filters and max pooling layers that effectively reduce image size while preserving important features. Second, VGG16's ability to extract features is particularly beneficial for classifying ALL cell images, as it retains essential information necessary for identifying cell textures.

Overall, the proposed model leverages the strengths of VGG16 to create a straightforward yet powerful approach for ALL classification, aiming for high accuracy with reduced computational complexity. The architecture is visually represented in Figure 4 of the paper.
