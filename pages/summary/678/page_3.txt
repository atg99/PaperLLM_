The paper presents a novel approach for classifying acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) and healthy cell images using a convolutional neural network (CNN) trained from scratch, as opposed to the transfer learning methods utilized in prior studies. The authors argue that training a CNN specifically on cell images can lead to better model performance. To enhance dataset quality and diversity, various preprocessing techniques, including data augmentation and class balancing, were applied to mitigate overfitting.

The literature review highlights several conventional machine learning algorithms for blood cell segmentation and classification. Notable contributions include Joshi et al.'s method using histogram equalization and K nearest neighbor clustering, and Mohapatra et al.'s fuzzy logic-based color segmentation approach. Other methods discussed involve the use of support vector machines (SVM), principal component analysis (PCA), and various feature extraction techniques, demonstrating a range of strategies and datasets employed in leukemia detection.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of tailored CNN architectures and comprehensive preprocessing methods to improve the classification accuracy of ALL and healthy cells, while critically examining existing methodologies in the field.
