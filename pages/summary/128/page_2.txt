The motivation stems from the need to model multi-modal distributions effectively, which traditional approaches like Gaussian-Softmax and the Grifﬁths-Engen-McCloskey (GEM) distribution struggle to achieve due to their inherent biases and sampling issues.

The Dirichlet distribution, as a conjugate prior to multinomial distributions, offers advantages in capturing multi-modality through controlled hyper-parameters, unlike the softmax function applied to Gaussian distributions. The paper highlights the deficiencies of both Gaussian-Softmax, which fails to produce high probabilities at the simplex vertices, and GEM, which suffers from a rich-get-richer phenomenon leading to component dominance.

The DirV AE leverages the composition of Gamma random variables to approximate the inverse Gamma cumulative distribution function, showing improved log-likelihood compared to previous methods such as Weibull and softmax Gaussian approaches. Additionally, the paper addresses component collapsing issues prevalent in existing VAEs. The authors argue that both Gaussian VAE and SBV AE face challenges with decoder weight and latent value collapsing, respectively. The DirV AE, however, mitigates these issues, ensuring effective utilization of latent dimensions without collapsing, leading to enhanced qualitative and quantitative performance.

In conclusion, the DirV AE offers a robust framework for modeling complex distributions while resolving significant challenges faced by prior models, establishing itself as a promising alternative for tasks requiring rich latent representations.
