This paper presents an evaluation of various Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) on the MNIST and OMNIGLOT datasets, focusing on metrics such as negative log-likelihood (Neg. LL), negative Evidence Lower Bound (Neg. ELBO), and reconstruction loss. Lower values in these metrics indicate better performance. The results show that the GV AE and its variants, such as GV AE-Softmax and DirV AE, exhibit varying performance across datasets. The DirV AE demonstrates more interpretable latent dimensions compared to GV AE-Softmax and SBV AE, which often lack significant semantic information.

Qualitative results highlight the latent dimension-wise reconstructions, with DirV AE producing more meaningful outputs. The paper also discusses a significant issue known as component collapsing, particularly in GV AE, where decoder weights approach zero, leading to ineffective learning as latent dimensions lose influence over the decoder. This phenomenon is termed decoder weight collapsing to emphasize its source. Overall, the findings indicate that while certain VAEs perform well, issues like component collapsing can hinder their effectiveness.
