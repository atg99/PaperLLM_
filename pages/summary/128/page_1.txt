The paper presents the Dirichlet Variational Autoencoder (DirV AE), which introduces a Dirichlet prior for continuous latent variables that resemble categorical probabilities. The authors employ a stochastic gradient method to infer DirV AE parameters, approximating the Gamma distribution, a part of the Dirichlet distribution, with the inverse Gamma CDF. They address component collapsing issues by identifying two sources: decoder weight collapsing and latent value collapsing, demonstrating that DirV AE avoids these issues while Gaussian VAE and Stick-Breaking VAE do not.

Experimental results indicate that DirV AE achieves superior log-likelihood for latent representation compared to baseline models, and it offers more interpretable latent values without collapsing. Additionally, the learned latent representations from DirV AE yield the highest classification accuracy in semi-supervised and supervised tasks on datasets like MNIST, OMNIGLOT, and SVHN. The paper also shows that DirV AE enhances topic models, delivering better performance in most cases.

The introduction contextualizes the relevance of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) in deep generative models, highlighting limitations in existing approaches that use Gaussian priors. The authors argue for the necessity of modeling the Dirichlet prior explicitly due to its capacity to represent multi-modal distributions, which cannot be achieved with Gaussian distributions and softmax functions. Overall, the DirV AE provides a significant advancement in the field by effectively modeling latent variables with Dirichlet distributions.
