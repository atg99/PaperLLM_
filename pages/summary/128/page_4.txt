This paper, presented at ICLR 2019, explores the approximation of probability distributions, specifically the Weibull and Dirichlet distributions, within the context of variational autoencoders (VAEs). It highlights the similarities between the Weibull and Gamma distributions, citing prior work by Zhang et al. (2018) that utilized the Weibull distribution as a posterior for the Gamma distribution. The authors note that while the Weibull distribution offers advantages such as closed-form KL divergence and a simple reparametrization trick, its exponential component complicates optimization due to sensitivity to changes in its shape parameter k.

The paper also discusses the approximation of the Dirichlet distribution using a softmax Gaussian distribution, referencing works by MacKay (1998) and Srivastava & Sutton (2017). This involves a softmax Laplace approximation that relates the Dirichlet parameters to Gaussian parameters.

The authors then outline their model, which consists of a generative sub-model and an inference sub-model. The generative model, specifically the DirV AE, assumes a Dirichlet prior for the latent variable z, contrasting with the standard Gaussian distribution used in other models. The inference model employs a probabilistic encoder with a Dirichlet approximating posterior, where the parameters are derived through a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to ensure positivity.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the innovative use of Weibull and Dirichlet distributions in VAEs, providing a framework for improved inference and generative modeling.
