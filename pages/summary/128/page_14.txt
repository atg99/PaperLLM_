The paper presents a comparative analysis of various Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) on the MNIST and OMNIGLOT datasets, focusing on metrics such as negative log-likelihood (Neg. LL), negative evidence lower bound (Neg. ELBO), and reconstruction loss. The results indicate that lower values in these metrics are indicative of better performance. 

For the MNIST dataset with K=50, the GV AE model demonstrates strong performance with a Neg. LL of 94.54, while the best-performing model, SBV AE-Kuma, achieves a Neg. LL of 99.27. In contrast, for the OMNIGLOT dataset with K=100, the DirV AE-Learning model yields the lowest Neg. LL of 84.42. The paper also discusses the optimization of objective functions for labeled and unlabeled instances in a semi-supervised classification context, highlighting the integration of class label variables with latent variables.

The experimental setup involved consistent network structures across GV AE, SBV AE, and DirV AE, including hidden layers and latent dimensions. The training process utilized a weighted sum of the labeled and unlabeled objectives with specific hyper-parameters and was conducted using the Adam optimizer. Additionally, the paper includes visualizations of decoder weight behavior and latent embeddings, further illustrating the models' performance.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of the GV AE and its variants in handling semi-supervised learning tasks, with detailed attention to the architectural choices and training methodologies employed.
