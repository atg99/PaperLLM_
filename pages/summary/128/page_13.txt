In this paper, the authors provide detailed experimental settings to support their findings presented in Section 4 of the original paper. They utilize several benchmark datasets including MNIST, MNIST with rotations, OMNIGLOT, and SVHN with PCA transformation. MNIST consists of 60,000 training samples and 10,000 testing samples of handwritten digits, while OMNIGLOT contains 24,345 training samples and 8,070 testing samples of handwritten characters. The SVHN dataset is processed with PCA into 500 dimensions.

The datasets are divided into training, validation, and testing subsets. For MNIST, a 50-dimensional latent variable is used with a specific architecture for the encoder and decoder, while OMNIGLOT utilizes a 100-dimensional latent variable. Key parameters include gradient clipping, ReLU activation functions, Xavier initialization, and the Adam optimizer with specified learning rates. The prior distributions for the Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) are also detailed.

The authors benchmark their method against a baseline of 20 normalizing flows and provide visualizations of latent embeddings. They introduce DirV AE-Learning, which optimizes hyper-parameters through various stages, showing improvements in marginal log-likelihood, ELBO, and reconstruction loss across both datasets. Finally, they describe a semi-supervised classification task using a VAE model structure that incorporates separate random variables, highlighting differences in model adaptations for the task.
