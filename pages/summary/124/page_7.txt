The paper discusses the performance of NV AE (Neural Variational Autoencoder) across various datasets, including MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CelebA, with a focus on the impact of temperature in the prior on image quality and diversity. The findings indicate that NV AE can generate high-quality, diverse samples even at lower temperatures, particularly excelling in hair details. This improvement is attributed to scaling down the standard deviation of Normal distributions in the conditional prior. 

The authors note that using single batch statistics for batch normalization (BN) layers during sampling enhances both diversity and quality, aligning with observations from previous models like BigGAN and DCGAN. However, this approach ties sample quality to other data points in the batch. To mitigate this issue, the authors propose a method of readjusting running mean and standard deviation in BN layers by sampling the generative model multiple times, thus allowing for improved final sampling without dependency on other samples.

The paper highlights the need for further investigation into the effects of BN on Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), as instance normalization did not yield similar results. The authors emphasize the importance of their findings for future work in the field.
