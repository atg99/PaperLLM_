The references listed cover a range of significant advancements in deep learning and neural network architectures. Key contributions include the introduction of deep residual learning, which enhances image recognition capabilities through skip connections, as outlined by Kaiming He and colleagues. MobileNetV2, developed by Mark Sandler and team, focuses on efficient model design using inverted residuals and linear bottlenecks, optimizing performance for mobile applications.

The concept of weight normalization introduced by Tim Salimans and Durk P Kingma accelerates training processes in deep networks. Additionally, Prajit Ramachandran and co-authors explore the search for effective activation functions, while Mingxing Tan and Quoc Le present EfficientNet, a model that rethinks scaling for convolutional networks.

Generative modeling is advanced through several works, including the introduction of residual flows by Ricky T. Q. The Glow architecture by Durk P Kingma and Prafulla Dhariwal employs invertible convolutions for generative tasks, while Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) are enhanced with techniques like the VAMP prior and mutual posterior-divergence regularization.
