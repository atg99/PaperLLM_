This paper presents Nouveau VAE (NVAE), a deep hierarchical variational autoencoder designed to enhance generative modeling capabilities, particularly for high-quality image generation. The authors highlight key differences between NVAE and traditional classification networks, emphasizing the importance of retaining input information. They discuss the unique responses of variational autoencoders (VAEs) to over-parameterization, noting that while powerful encoders can improve model performance by narrowing the amortization gap, over-parameterizing decoders may negatively impact test log-likelihood.

The study identifies challenges in training deep hierarchical VAEs due to the unbounded Kullback-Leibler divergence in the variational lower bound, leading to instability. To address these issues, the authors propose using depthwise convolutions to expand the receptive field without significantly increasing parameters, and they argue that batch normalization (BN) is crucial for the success of deep VAEs. Additionally, they introduce a residual parameterization of the approximate posterior parameters and emphasize the importance of spectral regularization for stabilizing training.

The contributions of the paper include the introduction of NVAE, a novel architecture utilizing depthwise convolutions, a new approach to parameterizing approximate posteriors, stabilization techniques for training, solutions to reduce memory usage, and demonstrations of NVAE's ability to produce state-of-the-art results on large image datasets. The authors claim that NVAE is the first successful application of VAEs to images as large as 256x256 pixels.

The paper also situates NVAE within the broader context of related work, contrasting it with VQ-VAE-2, which, although effective, does not optimize data log-likelihood in the same way. NVAE is shown to outperform previous models, including IAF-VAEs and BIVA, in generating high-quality images, while maintaining a focus on the design of neural networks rather than solely hierarchical complexity. The background section reviews the foundational concepts of VAEs, their hierarchical extensions, and bidirectional encoder networks, setting the stage for the innovations introduced by NVAE.
