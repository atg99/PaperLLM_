The paper discusses the performance and components of the NV AE model, particularly in generating high-quality images and optimizing its architecture through various experiments. In the reconstruction results, NV AE demonstrates its capability to produce diverse and high-quality samples from the CelebA HQ dataset, outperforming other models like MaCow and Glow. 

Ablation studies are conducted to analyze the effects of different components on the model's performance, focusing on normalization and activation functions, residual cells, and the impact of residual distributions. The findings indicate that switching from weight normalization (WN) to batch normalization (BN) enhances training, especially with the ELU activation function. Additionally, using depthwise separable convolution in the generative model yields better performance than regular cells, though the latter is preferred in the bottom-up model due to its lower memory usage.

The role of residual distributions is evaluated, revealing minimal impact on active latent variables and reconstruction loss, yet a slight improvement in the KL term and test log-likelihood. Furthermore, the necessity of spectral regularization (SR) and squeeze-and-excitation (SE) is affirmed, as their removal results in decreased performance. Overall, the results provide insights into optimizing NV AE through careful selection of components, ultimately contributing to its generative capabilities.
