The paper discusses the design of residual cells for a Neural Variational Autoencoder (NV AE) model, focusing on generative and encoder components. It highlights the architecture of the residual cells, particularly the use of BN-Swish convolution layers combined with depthwise separable convolutions, which help in reducing parameters and computational complexity. The generative model's residual cell expands the channel size before applying convolution, while the encoder model retains the channel size but employs regular convolutions to optimize performance.

The authors note that while batch normalization (BN) has been excluded in some state-of-the-art models due to its negative impact during evaluation, they address this issue by adjusting the momentum hyperparameter and applying regularization on BN scaling parameters. They find that the combination of BN and the Swish activation function outperforms previous methods using weight normalization and ELU activation.

Furthermore, the paper introduces the Squeeze and Excitation (SE) mechanism to enhance the performance of Variational Autoencoders (V AEs). The final cell design incorporates additional BN layers, Swish activation, and SE, drawing inspiration from MobileNetV2.

To mitigate high memory requirements associated with depthwise convolutions, the authors employ mixed-precision training using the NVIDIA APEX library, achieving a significant reduction in GPU memory usage. Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of depthwise convolutions in generative models while adapting the architecture for encoder efficiency.
