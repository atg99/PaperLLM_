The paper discusses techniques to enhance the efficiency and stability of training deep hierarchical Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), particularly addressing memory usage and optimization challenges associated with the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term.

To optimize memory usage during the backward pass, the authors propose fusing Batch Normalization (BN) and the Swish activation function. This approach allows for the storage of only one feature map instead of two, leveraging gradient checkpointing. The implementation of this method results in an 18% reduction in memory usage while effectively doubling training throughput from 34 images per second to 64 images per second.

The paper identifies challenges in training deep hierarchical VAEs due to the unbounded nature of the KL divergence from the approximate posterior to the prior distribution. This issue is exacerbated when using separate neural networks to model these distributions, particularly with many latent variable groups. To stabilize training and improve KL optimization, the authors propose two main approaches:

1. **Residual Normal Distributions**: This method parameterizes the approximate posterior distribution relative to the prior, allowing for a smoother adjustment between them. By ensuring that the approximate posterior moves in relation to the prior, the KL divergence is more manageable. The authors argue that this parameterization simplifies the minimization of the KL term compared to predicting absolute parameters.

2. **Spectral Regularization (SR)**: To further stabilize training, the paper introduces a method to regularize the Lipschitz constant of the encoder network. By minimizing the Lipschitz constant, the changes in the encoder's output can be controlled, which helps keep the latent codes bounded and facilitates stable KL minimization.

Additionally, the paper mentions enhancing the expressiveness of approximate posteriors through normalizing flows applied within the encoder network. This allows for more flexible distributions without significantly increasing sampling time.

The experimental section evaluates the proposed NVAE model on various image datasets, detailing quantitative and qualitative results, along with ablation studies to assess the impact of different components of the model.
