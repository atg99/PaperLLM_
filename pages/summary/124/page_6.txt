The paper presents a comparison of the NV AE model against state-of-the-art likelihood-based generative models across various datasets, including MNIST, CIFAR-10, ImageNet, CelebA, CelebA HQ, and FFHQ. The performance is evaluated using bits per dimension (bpd) and negative log-likelihood in nats for MNIST, with lower values indicating better performance. The results show that NV AE, both with and without flow, outperforms existing non-autoregressive models and significantly narrows the gap with autoregressive models.

Specifically, NV AE achieves competitive results on CIFAR-10, improving the state-of-the-art performance from 2.98 to 2.91 bpd. Notably, it ranks second on ImageNet, outperforming several other models across the datasets. The architecture of NV AE, which incorporates a hierarchical structure and residual cells, plays a crucial role in its effectiveness, allowing it to perform well even without flow components.

The qualitative results highlight the model's ability to generate high-quality samples, particularly in challenging datasets like CelebA HQ. The paper emphasizes the importance of network design in variational autoencoders (VAEs) and suggests that well-structured networks can effectively address statistical challenges inherent in generative modeling.
