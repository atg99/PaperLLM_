This paper builds on the findings of Whalen and Pandey, which highlight the effectiveness of using a single base learner in ensemble learning, and the advantages of utilizing different feature sets for various base learners. The authors propose a method to quantify ensemble diversity by examining the differentiation between base learners based on the learning algorithm and feature set used.

Each base learner is characterized by a vector that captures four dimensions: the sentiment classification approach (lexicon-based or machine learning), the specific algorithm employed (e.g., SVM, SentiWordNet), and two feature dimensions. The first feature dimension pertains to the document representation method, such as term presence or word embeddings, while the second dimension relates to the n-gram range, type of word embedding, or specific sentiment lexicon.

To quantify diversity, the authors calculate the degree of differentiation between each pair of base learners across these dimensions and normalize the results. This approach contrasts with traditional methods that focus on prediction errors of base learners and emphasizes the importance of selecting relevant meta-features for measuring diversity.

The computational efficiency of this method is notable, as it allows for diversity quantification without storing or directly comparing individual predictions of the base learners. This enables the calculation of diversity metrics prior to model deployment, reducing the need for retraining models that are ultimately not selected.

Mathematically, the diversity measure is formulated as a function of the number of candidate learners and the dimensions of diversity. A value of 1 indicates complete diversity among selected learners, while a value of 0 signifies uniformity across all dimensions. The accuracy of the ensemble can also be represented in relation to the chosen base learners.

Overall, this work provides a structured approach to enhancing ensemble learning through the quantification of diversity, which can lead to improved performance in sentiment classification tasks.
