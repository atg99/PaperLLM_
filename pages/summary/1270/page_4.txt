Ensemble learning involves combining multiple learning algorithms to create a system of diverse base learners, often referred to as a multiple classifier system. Two seminal papers from 1990 established that ensemble configurations can enhance performance. Schapire demonstrated that a strong classifier can be formed by integrating several weak classifiers, while Hansen and Salamon empirically validated that the prediction error of an ensemble can be lower than that of the best individual classifier.

For an ensemble to outperform its members, it is essential that the base learners are both accurate and diverse, meaning they should perform better than random guessing and make different errors on new data. Dietterich identified three main reasons why ensembles can achieve lower generalization error: statistical, computational, and representational. These concepts highlight the advantages of leveraging multiple perspectives in learning algorithms to improve overall predictive performance.
