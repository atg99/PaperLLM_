The paper discusses the selection of an optimal set of base learners for ensemble modeling through a specific optimization problem. The goal is to maximize the ensemble's performance by utilizing a combination of parameters, denoted as ϕ1 and ϕ2, which are tuned based on a small test problem with three candidate models. The selection criteria depend on the accuracy of the models and their diversity. 

When the models have equal accuracy but differ significantly, the one with the highest accuracy is chosen. Conversely, if the accuracies are similar, a larger number of models are preferred. For example, if accuracies are 0.6, 0.6, and 0.9, the highest accuracy model is selected. However, if the accuracies are 0.8, 0.8, and 0.9, all models are included. If all models are diverse but equally accurate, all should be selected to maximize the ensemble size. If one model is distinctly different, that model and the highest accuracy model are chosen to enhance diversity. The parameters ϕ1 and ϕ2 were determined to be 0.45 each.

The optimization model was implemented in an MS Excel spreadsheet and solved using a genetic algorithm with specific hyperparameters. The study contrasts the performance of ensembles formed from both machine learning models and lexicon-based models, noting that the former consistently outperformed the latter. This allows for an assessment of the ensemble favorability model under varying conditions of accuracy and diversity. Two types of outputs are utilized: discrete outputs and probabilistic outputs, which are combined using plurality voting.
