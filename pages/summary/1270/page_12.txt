The paper discusses various methods for enhancing the diversity and performance of ensemble models through the clustering and pruning of base learners. The first method involves partitioning base learners into clusters based on their prediction similarities, followed by pruning each cluster to improve ensemble diversity. Giacinto et al. utilized hierarchical agglomerative clustering to group classifiers that made similar errors on a validation set and selected a representative learner from each cluster based on dissimilarity to other clusters. Majority voting was then used for aggregating predictions, with the number of clusters optimized for maximum validation accuracy.

The second method focuses on ranking-based approaches where base learners are sorted based on evaluation metrics, and a set number is selected for the ensemble. One approach, termed orientation ordering, computes a signature vector for each learner, representing their classification accuracy. The ensemble's signature vector is averaged, and a reference vector orthogonal to it is used to rank learners. Only those with angles less than π/2 radians relative to the reference vector are included. This method prioritizes learners that accurately classify instances that the ensemble misclassifies.

Rooney et al. introduced a composite metric that combines a learner's validation accuracy with its contribution to ensemble diversity, weighted by a user-defined parameter. The paper compares two diversity measures: the proportion of models with uncorrelated errors relative to a learner and the normalized variance of a learner's predictions compared to the ensemble's average predictions. The top K base learners with the highest composite metrics are selected for the ensemble.

The fourth category encompasses various other pruning methods, including statistical approaches that retain only classifiers performing significantly better than others. Onan's comparative analysis of homogeneous and heterogeneous ensemble models, particularly in text classification, revealed that heterogeneous ensembles, especially those optimized through evolutionary algorithms, outperform traditional homogeneous models like AdaBoost and Bagging. This highlights the effectiveness of diverse ensemble configurations in improving predictive performance.
