The paper discusses the stacking ensemble method, which utilizes multiple base learners to improve classification performance. In this method, predictions from each base learner are integrated into a training dataset for a meta-learner, allowing it to enhance its predictive accuracy. The authors reference Ting and Witten's recommendation to use predicted class probabilities from base learners instead of discrete class labels, which increases the feature count based on the number of classes. They also mention a one-versus-all approach for multi-response regression, leading to the development of StackingC.

Additionally, the paper addresses ensemble pruning, a process aimed at improving the efficiency and predictive performance of an ensemble by reducing the number of base learners. It highlights the computational challenges associated with large ensembles and the complexity of selecting optimal subsets of base learners. The authors categorize ensemble pruning methods into four groups, with the first being search-based methods that use heuristics to explore potential combinations of base learners. The paper underscores the necessity of these methods given the exponential growth of possible subsets as the number of base learners increases.
