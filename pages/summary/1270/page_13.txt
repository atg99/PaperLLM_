The research by Pandey indicates that a voting ensemble using a greedy selection method of base learners can match or surpass the performance of a stacking classifier that employs all available base learners across four data sets. Caruana's stochastic iterative ensemble selection method consistently outperformed the greedy approach, while a hybrid of cluster-based ensemble selection and stacking performed similarly or slightly worse. The most effective strategy involved aggregating results from base learners of the same algorithm before stacking, suggesting that diversity in the selection of base learners may enhance performance.

In the realm of sentiment analysis, ensemble learning is less explored compared to other fields, yet studies show that ensembles and hybrid models often yield superior results over individual classifiers. For instance, Wilson's research reported a significant accuracy boost of 23% to 96% with boosting methods. Prabowo and Thelwall found that a hybrid model combining lexicon-based and rule-based approaches with an SVM classifier outperformed individual classifiers in three out of four scenarios.

Common techniques in sentiment analysis include stacking and weighting various classifiers, with meta-learning approaches generally outperforming simpler weighting methods. Omar's study demonstrated that stacking ensembles using naive Bayes, logistic regression, and SVM as meta-learners outperformed individual models, with logistic regression yielding the highest F-measure for sentiment classification. Similarly, Tsutsumi's comparison of several classifiers with ensemble methods revealed that all ensemble strategies surpassed their individual components, with SVM stacking achieving the best outcomes.

Conversely, Wang raised concerns about selecting base learners in stacking and instead investigated bagging, boosting, and random subspace methods. Their findings indicated that the random subspace approach with SVM as the base learner excelled in six out of ten cases, performing comparably in the others. Additionally, some researchers have effectively enhanced stacking by manipulating input data, specifically addressing feature dimensions, as seen in Xia's work.
