In this section, the paper discusses two types of ensemble learning methods: independent and dependent paradigms. Independent base learners are constructed separately, allowing for parallel processing, while dependent ones are built sequentially. 

Output aggregation methods for ensembles are categorized mainly into weighting and meta-learning approaches. Weighting methods include simple averaging and weighted averaging. Simple averaging computes the arithmetic mean of the predictions from all base learners, suitable when their performances are similar. In contrast, weighted averaging assigns importance to each learner's prediction based on their performance, particularly useful when there are significant differences in accuracy among learners. However, empirical evidence suggests that simple averaging does not necessarily lead to inferior performance compared to weighted averaging.

Voting methods are analogous to averaging but apply to categorical outputs from classification algorithms. Majority voting selects the class predicted by more than half of the classifiers, while plurality voting returns the class with the most votes, regardless of majority. Weighted voting assigns a contribution to each classifier based on its performance, with optimal weights derived from the model's accuracy in relation to the validation set. However, since base learners are often highly correlated, this assumption may not hold. Alternative methods for weight assignment include the Dempster-Shafer theory and entropy concepts, but determining effective weights remains computationally challenging.
