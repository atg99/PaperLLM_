The paper discusses various methods for selecting base learners to form an ensemble aimed at maximizing performance metrics. These methods are categorized into greedy and stochastic approaches. Whalen and Pandey introduced a greedy method that selects the top performing classifiers, while Caruana et al. proposed a stochastic method that involves iteratively choosing a subset of candidate learners to enhance ensemble performance. Both approaches require a predetermined ensemble size and primarily focus on the accuracy of individual classifiers, ignoring their collective impact on ensemble performance.

Additionally, evolutionary algorithms like genetic algorithms and particle swarm optimization have been employed for base learner selection, with objectives related to overall ensemble performance. However, using meta-learning algorithms increases computational costs due to the need for repeated training and prediction during iterations.

Some researchers have emphasized the importance of both accuracy and diversity in selecting base learners, noting the lack of a formal definition for model diversity. Kadkhodaei and Moghadam quantified diversity using entropy, maximizing it by ensuring an even split in votes among classifiers. Gu et al. reviewed multi-objective selection techniques, highlighting the trade-off between accuracy and diversity, which are often viewed as conflicting objectives. In this context, Pareto optimal solutions are sought, where improvements in one metric typically lead to declines in the other.

The paper also mentions clustering-based methods that utilize unsupervised learning techniques as part of a two-stage selection process. Overall, the discussion underscores the complexity of ensemble selection and the need for balancing accuracy and diversity to achieve optimal performance.
