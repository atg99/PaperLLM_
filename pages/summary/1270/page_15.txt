The methodology section of the paper outlines the experimental framework for evaluating sentiment classification models using both lexicon-based and machine learning approaches. The study begins with the implementation of four pre-trained lexicon-based models—Vader, Pattern, SentiWordNet, and the Hu and Liu opinion lexicon—alongside six machine learning algorithms: Naive Bayes, Support Vector Machines, logistic regression, feedforward neural networks, convolutional neural networks (CNN), and long short-term memory networks (LSTM). The first three machine learning models utilize nine different bag-of-words representations, while the CNN and LSTM models leverage end-to-end trained word embeddings. In total, the candidate base learners consist of 42 models, evaluated across ten train-test splits, with 20% of data used for testing.

The selection of base learners for the ensemble model is complex, given the vast number of combinations possible from the 42 models. The paper discusses two selection approaches. The first is a greedy method that selects the five models with the highest mean test accuracy from the experimental runs. The second approach aims to maximize a composite measure of favourability, which values both accuracy and diversity among the selected models, aligned with Condorcet's Jury Theorem. The favourability measure incorporates average accuracy, diversity, and the proportion of models selected, with weights assigned to reflect the relative importance of these criteria. This dual approach ensures a balance between performance and diversity in the ensemble model.
