The cited works collectively focus on enhancing ensemble learning techniques, particularly stacking and selection methods. Seewald (2002) discusses improving stacking efficiency while addressing weaknesses in the approach. Tsoumakas et al. (2008) provide a taxonomy and review of ensemble selection strategies, highlighting the importance of selecting diverse classifiers. Whalen and Pandey (2013) analyze ensemble classifiers in genomics, emphasizing their comparative performance. Caruana et al. (2004, 2006) explore ensemble selection from model libraries and strategies to maximize their effectiveness.

Ledezma et al. (2010) introduce GA-stacking, an evolutionary approach to stacked generalization, while Ordóñez et al. (2008) apply genetic algorithms to optimize classifier ensembles. Chen et al. (2014) and Shunmugapriya & Kanmani (2013) utilize optimization algorithms like ant colony and artificial bee colony methods for configuring stacking ensembles. Onan (2018) proposes a particle swarm optimization technique for text classification using stacking methods.

Gu et al. (2015) present a multi-objective approach to ensemble generation, and Sesmero et al. (2015) discuss generating heterogeneous classifier ensembles through stacked generalization. Lastly, Kadkhodaei & Moghadam (2016) introduce an entropy-based method for selecting the best base classifiers in ensemble setups. Together, these studies contribute to the development of more effective and efficient ensemble learning strategies.
