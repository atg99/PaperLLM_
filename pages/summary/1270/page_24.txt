In this paper, the effectiveness of various ensemble configurations for sentiment classification was examined. A comprehensive review of ensemble techniques, particularly in sentiment analysis, was conducted, followed by a methodology that allowed for the comparative evaluation of different homogeneous ensemble methods. Six ensemble configurations were tested, which combined discrete and scoring model outputs using simple voting, weighted voting, and meta-learning. These were evaluated alongside three ensemble selection methods, including a greedy approach and two variants of a newly introduced favourability model, across four diverse datasets.

The findings revealed median performance enhancements of up to 5.53% over the best individual model across all validation datasets. Notably, trends emerged that may benefit future research, with meta-learning utilizing scoring or probabilistic outputs consistently matching or surpassing the performance of other configurations. Additionally, ensembles leveraging scoring outputs outperformed those using discrete outputs in nine out of twelve instances.

The paper contributes to the limited application of ensemble learning techniques in sentiment classification by providing a comparative evaluation of various ensemble methods on benchmark datasets. It emphasizes the importance of selecting suitable base learners, proposing a novel selection approach based on a favourability metric that combines ensemble diversity, accuracy, and size. This method quantifies diversity through the differentiation of base learners regarding their learning algorithms and feature sets, allowing for efficient pre-deployment assessment without the need for storing individual predictions. The proposed approach aims to facilitate more effective ensemble model construction in future sentiment analysis research.
