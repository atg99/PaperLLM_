The study investigates the effectiveness of an ensemble model selection approach compared to the best individual model performance. Despite a lower average accuracy of base learners, the ensemble configuration yielded a slight increase in median accuracy, indicating the potential of combining ensemble diversity metrics with meta-learning methods. However, there was no direct correlation between the ensemble favourability scores and the actual performance of the ensemble models. 

The most favourable base learner set did not always lead to the best ensemble performance, suggesting that metrics like ensemble size, diversity, and accuracy alone are insufficient predictors of performance. Notably, the Twitter dataset showcased the best improvement over the best base learner, achieved by a diverse ensemble generated through a greedy approach. This indicates that a revised weighting of the favourability metrics may enhance prediction accuracy.

The Twitter dataset also uniquely demonstrated that a lexicon-based model was competitive with machine learning models, allowing for high ensemble diversity without sacrificing accuracy. The study highlighted the importance of setting thresholds on base learner accuracy, which could further refine the ensemble selection process.

Additionally, the effectiveness of output types and combination methods revealed a consistent trend across datasets, with meta-learning approaches outperforming others in most cases. Overall, the complementary model selection strategy improved median performance across all datasets, affirming the value of ensemble methods in enhancing predictive accuracy.
