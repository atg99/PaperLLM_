The paper discusses advancements in ensemble learning for sentiment analysis, highlighting the use of various feature sets and base learners. Xia et al. found that ensembles utilizing different feature sets and classifiers surpassed individual classifiers, with meta-learning approaches outperforming simple methods like voting or averaging. Araque et al. supported these findings, indicating that a combination of diverse features yielded the best performance.

Further complexity is introduced with models that include additional selection layers. Hassan et al. developed a two-stage ensemble framework for Twitter sentiment analysis, which effectively identified sentiment extremities over time. Khan et al. proposed a semi-supervised framework that improved performance on benchmark datasets through revised SentiWordNet scores using a multi-objective model selection algorithm.

Additionally, Lam and Kai introduced a meta-learning ensemble model that selects algorithms based on dataset metadata, enhancing classification accuracy compared to individual learners. The paper notes a limited exploration of ensemble learning in sentiment analysis, particularly regarding the selection of suitable base learners and the comparative evaluation of various combinations of methods and data sets. These gaps are addressed in the subsequent sections of the study.
