The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of various feedback data sets from a South African retail bank, focusing on customer service through over 2,500 text messages. The data sets are diverse, covering multiple domains and varying in class distribution, document length, and vocabulary size. 

Table 1 outlines the characteristics of the data sets, including PL04 Movie Reviews, Yelp Business Reviews, Twitter, and an SMS Banking Survey. Each data set's class distribution (positive, negative, neutral), average document length, and unique token count (vocabulary size) are detailed in Table 2. For instance, the PL04 data set consists of 2,000 documents evenly split between positive and negative sentiments, while the SMS data set has a higher prevalence of negative sentiments.

The study further explores the selection of base learners through a genetic algorithm, identifying two base learners for the PL04, Yelp, and Twitter data sets, while three base learners were chosen for the SMS data set. These base learners represent the best-performing models across various categories, including machine learning, deep learning, and lexicon-based approaches. 

The results from eighteen experiments, each repeated ten times to ensure reliability, are presented with a focus on accuracy metrics across different ensemble configurations. The performance is illustrated through box plots, highlighting the median performance of the best base learner. The findings emphasize the importance of model diversity and accuracy in analyzing customer feedback, providing insights into the effectiveness of different machine learning strategies in processing sentiment analysis from diverse data sources.
