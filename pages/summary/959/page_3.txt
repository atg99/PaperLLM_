This paper discusses the application of Lagrangian mechanics in deriving the dynamics of physical systems through the formulation of the action, defined as the integral of kinetic and potential energy over time. The Lagrangian is expressed as L=T-V, leading to the Euler-Lagrange equation, which describes the system's path. The authors propose a numerical approach to derive the dynamics from a black box Lagrangian, using a vectorized form of the Euler-Lagrange equation and matrix operations to compute changes in coordinates.

The paper also reviews related works that integrate physics-inspired biases into neural networks. Previous studies have focused on specific domains, such as molecular dynamics and robotics, while others, like Interaction Networks, generalize physical interactions through graph-based message passing. Recent advancements have demonstrated the learning of invariant quantities by approximating Hamiltonians with neural networks. This paper aligns with those efforts but aims to learn Lagrangians, providing flexibility in modeling kinetic energy.

Additionally, it highlights the "Deep Lagrangian Networks" (DeLaN) framework, which learns Lagrangian systems under specific assumptions about kinetic energy representation. While effective for certain dynamics, this approach may not apply to all systems, such as charged particles in magnetic fields or relativistic objects. The paper situates its contributions within this broader context of Lagrangian-based methodologies in physics and machine learning.
