The appendix presents a JAX implementation of the Euler-Lagrange equation, specifically focusing on a differentiable Lagrangian function that takes three vectors as input and produces a scalar output. The dynamics of a system, represented by the acceleration vector q_tt, is calculated using the pseudo-inverse of the Hessian of the Lagrangian with respect to the velocity vector q_t and the gradient of the Lagrangian. This approach allows for the efficient computation of gradients when called within a loss function.

An example is provided featuring a ball falling under gravity, with the Lagrangian defined to incorporate kinetic and potential energy terms. The derivatives of the Lagrangian are computed, leading to a clear expression for the particle's acceleration, demonstrating that it accelerates downwards while maintaining a constant velocity in the orthogonal direction.

Furthermore, the appendix discusses the initialization of parameters within a Lagrangian Neural Network. Traditional initialization methods like Kaiming and Xavier were deemed inadequate due to the nonlinearity of the optimization objective. As a solution, an empirical optimization of the KL-divergence of the parameter gradients was performed, resulting in a new initialization scheme tailored to the specific needs of Lagrangian Neural Networks. This empirical approach was refined across various network architectures to derive an effective initialization strategy.
