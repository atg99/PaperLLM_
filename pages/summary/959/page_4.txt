The paper discusses methods for solving the Euler-Lagrange equations using JAX, highlighting the challenges associated with computing the inverse Hessian of a neural network and performing backpropagation. It notes that the matrix inverse operation scales with the cube of the number of coordinates, yet can be efficiently implemented in JAX. The authors provide their code on GitHub.

In the training phase, the authors employed a four-layer neural network with 500 hidden units, a decaying learning rate starting at 0.001, and a batch size of 32. A novel initialization scheme optimized for LNNs was utilized. The choice of activation function was also critical due to the need for a non-zero second-order derivative. After testing various functions, softplus was selected as the best performer.

The experiments included training on a dataset of simulated double pendulum trajectories, where both LNN and baseline models achieved similar final losses, but the LNN exhibited superior energy conservation, maintaining energy discrepancy at only 0.4% compared to 8% for the baseline. 

Another task involved modeling a relativistic particle in a uniform potential, where the Lagrangian was derived from special relativity principles. The paper suggests that traditional models, such as Hamiltonian Neural Networks and DeLaN, may struggle with this system due to their assumptions about the nature of observables and the order of the kinetic term.

Overall, the findings indicate that LNNs outperform traditional models in terms of energy conservation in dynamic systems.
