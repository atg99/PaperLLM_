The Lagrangian is a function that typically represents the difference between kinetic and potential energy. In this paper, we propose a method for learning Lagrangians from data using neural networks, which allows for the modeling of complex physical systems that are not constrained to canonical coordinates.

Our approach addresses the limitations of existing models like Hamiltonian Neural Networks and Deep Lagrangian Networks by enabling the learning of Lagrangians in arbitrary coordinate systems. This flexibility is crucial because many real-world datasets do not conform to the strict requirements of the Hamiltonian framework. By learning the Lagrangian directly, we can ensure that energy conservation is upheld while accommodating a broader range of dynamical behaviors.

We demonstrate the efficacy of our proposed model through experiments showing that it can outperform baseline neural networks in terms of energy conservation and can effectively model physical systems that other approaches struggle with. Furthermore, we extend our method to continuous systems and graph-based representations, showcasing its versatility. 

In summary, our work contributes to the field of physics-informed neural networks by providing a robust framework for learning Lagrangians, thus enhancing the ability of neural networks to understand and predict the dynamics of complex physical systems.
