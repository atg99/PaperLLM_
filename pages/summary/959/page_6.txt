The paper introduces Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNN), a new class of neural networks designed to learn arbitrary Lagrangians. Unlike traditional models that focus on Hamiltonians and require canonical coordinates, LNNs excel in scenarios where such coordinates are either unknown or difficult to compute. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of LNNs in conserving total energy within complex physical systems, specifically using the double pendulum as a case study. The model successfully learns non-trivial canonical momenta, where Hamiltonian learning often faces challenges. 

Additionally, the paper presents a Lagrangian Graph Network that accurately simulates the 1D wave equation under periodic boundary conditions. This network approximates a finite difference operator and maintains energy conservation across the simulated material. The results show the model's capability to track wave dynamics over time, as illustrated through graphical representations of relative amplitude and total energy predictions.

In conclusion, the authors emphasize the advantages of LNNs in learning Lagrangians and their potential applications in various physical systems. Acknowledgments are given to colleagues for discussions and feedback that contributed to the research.
