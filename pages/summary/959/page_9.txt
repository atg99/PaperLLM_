In this study, the authors conducted 2500 optimization steps on a multilayer perceptron (MLP) with a fixed depth and width, varying the initialization variances across layers while keeping biases at zero. They explored 200 random hyperparameter configurations, varying the number of hidden nodes from 50 to 300 and hidden layers from one to three. Using symbolic regression tools, specifically eureqa, they derived an equation to predict the optimal initialization variance based on hyperparameters. The derived equation indicated that the initialization variance is influenced by the number of nodes and the layer type. For example, the first layer's variance is set at a constant, while subsequent hidden layers have variances that depend on their respective indices. The model was trained with 100 nodes in four layers, where weight matrices were initialized from Gaussian distributions with specified variances. Each layer's weight matrix dimensions were carefully structured to facilitate the training process.
