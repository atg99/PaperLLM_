In this paper, the authors introduce Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNNs), a novel approach to modeling physical systems by parameterizing Lagrangians using neural networks. Traditional neural network models often struggle with the underlying symmetries of physical systems, particularly conservation laws like energy and momentum. LNNs address this challenge by not requiring canonical coordinates, making them effective in scenarios where canonical momenta are difficult to determine. 

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of LNNs through experiments on a double pendulum and a relativistic particle, showcasing their ability to conserve energy while conventional methods lead to dissipation. Additionally, the paper discusses the application of LNNs to graphs and continuous systems via a Lagrangian Graph Network, specifically illustrating its effectiveness on the one-dimensional wave equation.

Overall, LNNs provide a robust framework for integrating physical principles into neural network models, allowing for accurate long-term predictions of dynamic systems without the limitations of previous approaches that rely on Hamiltonians.
