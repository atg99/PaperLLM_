This paper explores the performance of Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNN) and Lagrangian Neural Networks (LNN) in learning dynamics from systems with random initial conditions. The findings indicate that HNNs struggle without canonical coordinates, while LNNs can accurately learn system dynamics even in the absence of this prior knowledge. The paper presents results showing that LNNs can match the performance of HNNs when trained on canonical coordinates.

The authors extend their discussion to the application of Lagrangian Graph Networks to various systems, including those with disconnected coordinates, such as graph-like and grid-like systems. They propose a method to model these systems by learning a Lagrangian density, which is summed to form the total Lagrangian. This approach is inspired by previous works on Hamiltonian Graph Networks, focusing on 1D grids with locally-dependent dynamics.

The continuous material is represented by quantities at grid points, with dynamics modeled by treating adjacent grid points as independent coordinates. The total Lagrangian is calculated by summing the Lagrangian densities over connected coordinates. The paper outlines the mathematical framework for solving dynamics by inserting the Lagrangian into a specific equation, noting the efficiency of calculating the Hessian matrix due to its sparse structure.

The authors also discuss how the model can be adapted for different connectivity patterns and how the Lagrangian density can be modeled using a multi-layer perceptron (MLP). The paper concludes by considering the implications of this framework for modeling the 1D wave equation, emphasizing the potential for efficient computation in dynamic systems.
