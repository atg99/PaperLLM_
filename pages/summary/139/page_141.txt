This section discusses the advantages of incorporating latent random variables in modeling structured sequences, particularly in the context of speech. Chung et al. (2015) highlight that the variational recurrent neural network (VRNN) can effectively model natural speech sequences using a simple Gaussian output function, whereas standard RNNs struggle to produce reasonable samples. While RNNs with more complex output functions, like Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs), can generate better samples, they often introduce high-frequency noise, a problem not seen with VRNNs.

The discussion extends to the superiority of deep variational autoencoders (DVAE) over traditional dynamic Bayesian networks (DBNs) and state-space models (SSMs) due to their deep nonlinear processing capabilities. Chung et al. further emphasize that the VRNN explicitly models dependencies between latent variables over time, unlike simpler models such as Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) and Kalman filters, while still accommodating non-linear dynamics.

The section concludes by underscoring the flexibility of generative models derived from the general framework, noting that various models like STORN, VRNN, and SRNN exemplify this versatility, allowing for a range of applications and adaptations in generative modeling.
