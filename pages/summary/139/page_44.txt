The section discusses the operation modes of Deep Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs), emphasizing the distinction between driven and predictive modes. It notes that while models can transition from driven to undriven modes by replacing control inputs with previously generated outputs, the literature lacks comprehensive exploration of these modes within the context of DVAEs. The behavior of a model can vary significantly depending on the mode, analogous to open-loop and closed-loop control systems.

Additionally, the paper addresses the parameterization of DVAEs using Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and feed-forward Deep Neural Networks (DNNs). The factorization approach allows for various conditional independence assumptions, which influence how past information is utilized—either requiring accumulation of past data or adhering to a first-order Markovian relationship. The choice of network architecture directly affects the implementation of these dependencies, leading to a diverse family of distributions based on both the independence assumptions and the network types used.

The section concludes with an example demonstrating specific conditional independence assumptions, illustrating how the generation of outputs depends on past observations and control inputs. This highlights the need for careful consideration of these relationships in DVAE design.
