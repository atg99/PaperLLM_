The paper discusses Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), which were introduced by Kingma and Welling as well as Rezende et al. in 2014. VAEs extend the concept of traditional Autoencoders (AEs) by incorporating a probabilistic framework. Unlike AEs, where the decoder outputs a direct value, VAEs produce parameters of a probability distribution for the output, allowing for the generation of new data from latent variables. This probabilistic approach applies to both the encoding and decoding processes.

The paper highlights various applications of VAEs, including image generation and transformation, as well as modifications of existing data. It also notes their utility in processing speech and music signals, referencing several studies that demonstrate these applications. Moreover, VAEs can serve as prior distributions in more intricate Bayesian models, contributing to tasks like speech enhancement and source separation.

To clarify the discussion, the paper separates the VAE's generative model (decoder) from its inference model (encoder). The generative model is defined using a multivariate Gaussian distribution characterized by a mean vector and a covariance matrix, allowing for a structured representation of the latent space and output generation.
