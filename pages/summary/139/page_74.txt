In this section, the generative model STORN is discussed, focusing on its graphical representation and the relationship between its components. The model highlights the dependence of the hidden state ht on the sequence of past observations and latent variables, denoted as ht = ht(x1:t-1, z1:t). A Dirac probability distribution is utilized to represent ht as a deterministic function of these inputs. 

The paper presents equations that demonstrate the marginalization of the joint density pθ(x1:T, z1:T, h1:T) with respect to h1:T, resulting in the conditional distribution pθ(x(x1:T|z1:T). This distribution reveals that the densities of data sequences factor across time, although the current output relies on the entire history of past outputs and latent variables, encapsulated in ht(x1:t-1, z1:t). 

An alternate representation of STORN is suggested, omitting the internal deterministic aspects for clarity. The discussion draws on previous insights, particularly from Bayer and Osendorfer (2014), emphasizing the model's structure and its implications for understanding the relationships within the data.
