This section discusses the architectures and decoding processes of Dynamic Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs), emphasizing the components of the encoder and decoder networks. Notably, none of the DVAEs operate in a driven mode, meaning they do not utilize an external input. Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) are primarily employed for high-level feature extraction and as combiner functions, while recurrent networks, specifically Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Bidirectional LSTM (BRNN), are used to accumulate temporal information.

The Dynamic Kalman Filter (DKF) incorporates a combiner function (CF) and a gated transition function (GTF) to facilitate the dynamical model, with further details provided in the original DKF paper and Appendix B. The internal state vector in the Variational Recurrent Neural Network (VRNN) and Sequential Recurrent Neural Network (SRNN) is shared between the encoder and decoder. The section also distinguishes between causal and noncausal versions of the Recurrent Variational Autoencoder (RVAE), with the noncausal architecture illustrated in the accompanying figure.

Output variance parameters are log-parameterized to ensure stability. The discussed architectures are consistent across experiments conducted on speech and human motion data, albeit with varying layer dimensions. For speech data, the observed, latent, and RNN internal state vectors have dimensions of 513, 16, and 128, respectively, while for human motion data, the dimensions are 96, 10, and 64. Additional implementation details, such as the number of layers and units per layer, are specified in the appendices.
