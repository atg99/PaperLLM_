paper discusses the behavior of the latent vector of a speech spectrogram generated by a Stochastic Recurrent Neural Network (SRNN) trained in generation mode with scheduled sampling. The key components analyzed include the mean value of the posterior distribution, the log-variance, the sampled latent vector, and the KL divergence term of the variational lower bound (VLB). 

The findings indicate that the latent vector dimensions associated with very small weights significantly influence the computation of the mean and log-variance. Visualizations of the output linear layers support this observation. 

Comparison between the SRNN model trained in generation mode and teacher-forcing mode reveals that the former exhibits less posterior collapse and a greater number of active dimensions. This suggests that while generating data vectors, the model relies on less accurate past values, resulting in a trade-off between accuracy and reliability in the generation process.
