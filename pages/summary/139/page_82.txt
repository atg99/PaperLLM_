In this section, the paper discusses the inference model related to a data sequence and its joint distribution. The joint distribution of variables is marginalized with respect to the hidden states, leading to equations that express the conditional densities over time. The analysis reveals that while there is a factorization of the conditional densities, the variables xt and zt are not conditionally independent given the state ht, due to a direct dependency of zt on xt.

The paper introduces an alternative expression for the joint distribution pθ(x1:T,z1:T) that abstracts away from the internal state, focusing instead on the dependencies among the free random variables. This is represented through equations that detail the factorization into conditional probabilities based on previous observations.

Further, the paper compares the exact posterior distribution of the Variational Recurrent Neural Network (VRNN) to that of the STORN model, noting that both follow a similar factorization structure. The approximate posterior distribution qφ is mentioned as having been selected by Chung, indicating a specific choice made in the modeling process. The overall inference model reflects complex interactions among the variables while maintaining a structured approach to their dependencies.
