The paper discusses the comparison between Generalized Recurrent Neural Networks (GRNN) and State Space Models (SSM), highlighting their structural similarities while noting key differences in their internal state representations. In GRNN, the internal state is deterministic, while in SSM, it is stochastic. The paper emphasizes the importance of model initialization, particularly at the first time frame, where the initial state z0 can be defined in several ways, including assigning a deterministic value or using a prior distribution.

The inference process in SSM involves estimating the state vector sequence from the observed data vector sequence. The choice of Gaussian distributions for this inference is noted as advantageous since it simplifies the process. The paper indicates that the distributions used are part of the exponential family, allowing for both exact and approximate inference algorithms to be applicable based on the specific characteristics of the state and observation dimensions.

The section concludes by introducing Kalman filters as a classical method within SSMs, which have been effectively utilized across various applications, particularly when both the observation and state transitions are linear functions.
