This section provides a brief overview of various models related to variational approximations and generative modeling. Gu et al. (2015) introduced a sampling-based approach that optimizes the observed data marginal likelihood within a variational framework for estimating generative model parameters. Other applications of Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) methods in variational approximations are discussed in works by Maddison et al. (2017), Le et al. (2018), and Naesseth et al. (2018).

The Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) proposed by Hafner et al. (2018) closely resembles the Variational Recurrent Neural Network (VRNN) model, differing primarily in its use of an external input to compute hidden states. This model facilitates learning dynamics and planning for synthetic agents in reinforcement learning settings, allowing for multi-step predictions.

Denton and Fergus (2018) presented a model for Stochastic Video Generation (SVG) that aligns with the STORN model, focusing on stochastic video generation and multi-frame prediction. Their inference model shares similarities with STORN, and they propose a "learned prior" for the generative distribution of latent variables.

Lastly, the Factorized Variational Autoencoder (FVAE) introduced by Deng et al. (2017) merges Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) with tensor factorization, which incorporates data dynamics modeling through a discrete time dimension. Temporal patterns in this model are sampled from a standard log-normal distribution.
