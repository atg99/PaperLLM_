Dynamical Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs) face a common challenge during training and testing due to the use of different modes. Teacher-forcing is typically employed during training, but when generating data or compressing it, the generation mode is necessary. This discrepancy can lead to a performance drop, as the training and testing configurations do not align. To mitigate this, it is advantageous to adopt the generation mode during training to match the practical use case. This involves substituting the prior input with the predicted values in the Variational Lower Bound (VLB) equations. Experimental benchmarks indicate that this approach enhances performance significantly compared to the mismatched configuration, although it still does not reach the performance level observed when teacher-forcing is consistently applied.

DVAEs are characterized by stochastic relationships among control variables, latent variables, and observed variables. A crucial aspect of a DVAE model is the decoding link, where each latent variable is the parent of the corresponding observed variable, represented graphically by an arrow connecting them. This structural relationship is fundamental to DVAEs, allowing for effective modeling of complex data distributions.
