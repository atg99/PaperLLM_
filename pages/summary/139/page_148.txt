DVAEs, or Deep Variational Autoencoders, differ from traditional VAEs by utilizing a sequence of latent vectors z1:T instead of a single latent vector. This approach addresses the encoding capacity challenges associated with long data sequences, as it allows the model to capture time-varying aspects of the data more effectively. The authors reference Li and Mandt (2018), emphasizing that this time-local reconstruction enhances the model's ability to handle complex sequences.

The paper argues that posterior collapse in DVAEs is unlikely caused by limited latent capacity; rather, it may arise from excessive capacity in the latent vector sequence. This hypothesis is supported by experimental observations indicating that inactive components in the latent sequence could be redundant. Consequently, optimizing the dimensions of the latent vectors to match the data while managing computational complexity is a critical challenge in DVAEs.

Furthermore, the authors note that even in high-capacity autoregressive DVAE models, such as SRNN and VRNN, there is no assurance that the autoregressive component will not dominate the information capture, potentially sidelining the latent vector sequence. This highlights an unresolved issue in DVAE research.

The section also briefly mentions improved inference models and algorithms, indicating that the learning process within the VAE framework is based on amortized variational inference techniques, which are crucial for effective model training and inference.
