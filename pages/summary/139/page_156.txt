The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in disentangling and controlling speech production factors within the context of source coding, particularly focusing on Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Deep Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs). It highlights that while identity can be exchanged between sentences while maintaining phonetic content, critical questions regarding the effects of latent variable size and the relevance of extracted factors remain unanswered. Hsu et al. (2017b) emphasize the lack of attempts to learn disentangled and interpretable representations from sequential data without supervision. 

The paper reviews models such as SRNN, VRNN, and SVAE, noting that they offer robust frameworks for sequential data representation but fall short in ensuring the disentanglement of independent attributes in the latent space. Additionally, the interpretability of learned latent variables is questioned, as they often require manual inspection or labeled data. 

The authors argue that while current models provide a strong mathematical foundation, further work is necessary to address the disentanglement challenge, particularly for DVAEs. They suggest that integrating ideas from existing structured or hierarchical models could lead to solutions. 

Furthermore, the paper points out the limited research on the practical applications of VAEs and DVAEs in source coding, particularly regarding data compression issues like quantization and bitrate for data transmission or storage. The phenomenon of latent variable "vanishing" is briefly mentioned as a concern in this area.
