The paper discusses the simplification of the general form of Deep Kalman Filters (DKF) as presented in equation (4.24). The likelihood function L(θ, φ; x1:T, u1:T) is expressed in terms of two expectations and a KL divergence term. While the KL divergence can be computed analytically, the two expectations are intractable, which poses a challenge for approximation.

Previous works by Krishnan et al. indicated the use of stochastic backpropagation, referencing the reparameterization trick from Kingma and Welling and Rezende et al. However, due to the dynamic nature of DKF, the sampling procedure for stochastic backpropagation is more complex than for standard Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). Specifically, an analytical form for qφ(zt|x1:T, u1:T) is not available, and only qφ(zτ|zτ−1, xτ:T, uτ:T) can be derived.

To address this, the authors propose using the chain rule and a "cascade trick" to develop and approximate the intractable expectations. The approach involves breaking down the first expectation into a series of nested expectations, allowing for iterative sampling of qφ(zτ|zτ−1, xτ:T, uτ:T) from τ=1 to t. Each expectation can then be estimated using Monte Carlo methods.

This methodology enables the variational lower bound (VLB) to be made differentiable with respect to parameters θ and φ, allowing for optimization through gradient ascent techniques. Overall, the paper details a structured approach to tackle the challenges of expectation approximation in the context of DKFs.
