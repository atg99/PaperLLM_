The paper discusses the concept of feedback recurrent autoencoders (FRAE) as a nonlinear predictive coding scheme that employs recurrent connections in both the encoder and decoder, along with a feedback link from the decoder to the encoder. This design draws on the predictive coding principle prevalent in source coding theory, as articulated by Gersho and Gray. In this framework, the encoder generates a latent code that captures only the residual information necessary for reconstructing data from its deterministic internal state, which is influenced by prior data vectors. 

FRAE is linked to the predictive mode of dynamic variational autoencoder (DVAE) models, as previously outlined and implemented in various autoregressive DVAE frameworks. Additionally, the feedback connection mirrors the closed-loop coding principle, allowing the decoder to utilize quantized previous data to predict the current data vector, even though this connection was not explicitly mentioned by Yang et al. 

The exploration of nonlinear predictive coders utilizing DVAEs is still in its early stages. Yang et al. highlight the absence of a standard autoencoder architecture for temporally correlated data with variable lengths and long-range dependencies, such as video, speech, and text. The challenge lies in capturing correlation information across different time scales in a sequential manner. This sentiment aligns with Chen et al., who express enthusiasm for extending the learning of lossy codes to data forms that incorporate temporal aspects.
