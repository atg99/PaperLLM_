The paper discusses the implementation of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), focusing on the optimization of the variational lower bound (VLB). The Monte Carlo estimate of the VLB is expressed as a combination of log probabilities and the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the variational distribution and the prior. To optimize this objective, stochastic or mini-batch gradient descent is typically employed.

A significant challenge arises when computing the gradient with respect to the variational parameters, as the sampling operation is not differentiable. The reparameterization trick, introduced by Kingma and Welling, addresses this by transforming the sample using a differentiable function of a standard Gaussian variable, allowing the gradient to be computed effectively.

This results in a differentiable Monte Carlo approximation known as the stochastic gradient variational Bayes (SGVB) estimator. The gradient of the SGVB estimator provides an unbiased estimate of the exact VLB gradient, enabling efficient computation even with a limited number of samples. It is recommended to use a single sample with sufficiently large mini-batches for gradient descent. The training procedure for VAEs is now commonly implemented in deep learning frameworks like TensorFlow and PyTorch.
