The paper discusses the transformation of deterministic recurrent neural networks (RNNs) into generative recurrent neural networks (GRNNs) by introducing stochasticity in the output layer. In this process, a probabilistic observation model is defined, allowing the output sequence to consist of distribution parameters rather than deterministic outputs. This is achieved through recursive relationships where the hidden state is updated based on the input and previous hidden state, and the output is modeled as a Gaussian distribution characterized by mean and variance parameters derived from the hidden state.

The generative aspect of RNNs enables them to capture dependencies in sequential data, making them suitable for tasks where the output is not simply a direct mapping from the input. The choice of distribution can be tailored to the specific characteristics of the data, with alternatives to Gaussian distributions such as mixture models being suggested for improved modeling accuracy.

Overall, the transition from deterministic to generative models marks a significant advancement in the capabilities of RNNs, allowing them to better handle the complexities of real-world datasets where inherent uncertainty is present.
