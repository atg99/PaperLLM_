The paper discusses the implementation of a Deep Variational Autoencoder (DVAE) using speech data, focusing on two models: the Deep Kalman Filter (DKF) and STORN.

In the DKF section, the generation process involves a gated transition function that combines linear and nonlinear estimates of the mean vector for the latent variable zt. Specifically, it utilizes a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for both the mean estimates and the variance calculation. The generation of the observed variable xt is also handled by an MLP. For inference, the model employs a Deep Kalman Smoothing (DKS) approach, utilizing a backward LSTM and various MLPs for estimating the necessary functions.

In the STORN section, the prior distribution of the latent variables z1:T is established as an independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) standard Gaussian, allowing for straightforward sampling. The function for the hidden state ht is created by concatenating MLPs followed by a forward LSTM, while the generation of xt is also performed by an MLP based on ht.

Overall, the paper outlines the structured implementation of these models, emphasizing the use of MLPs and LSTMs to manage the complexities of speech data generation and inference.
