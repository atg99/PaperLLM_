The section discusses advancements in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) that enhance their application to Deep Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs). A critical observation is that traditional VAEs blend local statistics, such as texture, with global structural information, which is not ideal for disentangled latent factors. Subsequent research has proposed mixing VAE latent representations with more advanced decoders that utilize local pixel correlations through convolutive or autoregressive methods. For instance, Chen et al. (2017) introduced an autoregressive conditional density approach for 2D image modeling, where the model captures local statistics through an autoregressive network (typically an RNN), while the global structure is encoded in the latent variable z.

However, a challenge arises known as latent variable vanishing or posterior collapse, where the autoregressive model captures most of the data structure, leaving the latent variable underutilized. To mitigate this, Chen et al. (2017) recommend constraining the autoregressive window size, thereby limiting the decoder's power and allowing for better utilization of the latent variable. Additionally, the incorporation of hierarchical latent structures and different levels of feature maps from convolutional neural networks (CNNs) is suggested as a way to enhance the model's performance while addressing the latent variable collapse issue.
