The section discusses the FHVAE model, which captures temporal information in data by segmenting sequences into fixed-size segments, each represented by two latent variables: z and v. The variable z captures segment-level information, while v captures sequence-level information. This model is particularly effective for speech signals, where segments of around 200 milliseconds correspond to syllables. Here, z typically encodes phonetic details, and v encodes speaker characteristics.

The FHVAE model is closely related to DSAE, which also incorporates a sequence-level latent variable while maintaining a time-frame resolution for the dynamic latent variable z. The equations provided illustrate the segmental modeling approach, indicating how segment data is processed through an LSTM network. Each segment has a unique pair of latent vectors, allowing for many-to-one encoding and one-to-many decoding. The prior distribution for z is isotropic Gaussian and independent of both the segment and sequence, while the distribution for v is influenced by a sequence-level latent variable w. The notation was adjusted in this context to avoid confusion with time indexing.
