This section discusses various models that extend traditional approaches in state-space modeling (SSM) using deep neural networks (DNNs). The first model mentioned is an extension of the switching Kalman filter, which integrates a DNN-based observation model, highlighting the structured variational autoencoder (SVAE) framework proposed by Johnson et al. This framework improves inference efficiency through a structured mean-field approximation, though it lacks specific temporal equations, making it less connected to the primary review.

Archer et al. introduce a black-box deep SSM that enhances computational efficiency by utilizing a multivariate Gaussian approximate posterior with a block tridiagonal inverse covariance matrix. Their fast inference algorithm is applicable to a broad range of SSMs and was tested primarily on various linear and nonlinear models, demonstrating its effectiveness in recovering Kalman filter solutions. Notably, their approach emphasizes the inference model being deep while the generative models remain non-deep. Subsequent work has further developed these ideas, particularly in the context of online learning and real-time applications.
