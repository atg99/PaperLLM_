The paper discusses an inference model within the context of generative models, specifically focusing on the relationships between latent variables and observed data over time. It begins by outlining the joint distribution of variables, emphasizing the factorization and dependencies present in the model. The equations (9.6) and (9.7) illustrate how the generative process can be represented, highlighting that while there is a temporal structure, there is no conditional independence of the observed data given the latent states.

The inference model is described with a particular focus on the SRNN, where the exact posterior distribution of latent states depends on both the previous latent state and the entire sequence of observations. The authors reference work by Fraccaro et al. (2016), which proposed an approximate posterior distribution that mirrors the structure of the exact posterior. This methodology highlights the importance of considering both past and future observations through a gated backward RNN, allowing for effective integration of information across time steps.

The backward RNN is parameterized to capture these dependencies, with its internal state denoted accordingly. Overall, the paper emphasizes the intricate relationships between latent variables and observations, providing a framework for understanding the dynamics of these dependencies in generative models.
