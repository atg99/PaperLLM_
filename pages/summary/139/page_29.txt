The paper discusses Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and their inference model, focusing on the variational distribution qφ(zn|xn) and the optimization of the variational lower bound (VLB). VAEs utilize amortized variational inference techniques, where the generative model pθ(x,z) is defined with a decoder neural network pθ(x|z). To learn the generative model parameters θ, the inference model qφ(z|x) must approximate the intractable exact posterior pθ(z|x).

The inference model is typically represented using an encoder neural network, with a common choice for the approximate posterior being a Gaussian distribution. This is expressed mathematically, indicating that qφ(z|x) is a product of independent Gaussian distributions for each entry of the latent vector. The functions µφ and σφ, which represent the mean and variance, are determined by the encoder, a deep neural network parameterized by weights and biases φ.

The training of VAEs, as outlined by Kingma and Welling (2014) and Rezende et al. (2014), involves optimizing the VLB using stochastic gradient-based methods, ensuring the effective learning of both the generative and inference models.
