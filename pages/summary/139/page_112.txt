The paper discusses various models related to STORN, VRNN, and SRNN, highlighting their capabilities in addressing scalability issues with long training sequences. One model, the Deep Recurrent Attentive Writer (DRAW), proposed by Gregor et al. in 2015, is a dual model of VRAE that encodes static yet structured data, typically low-resolution images, through a sequence of latent vectors. DRAW constructs images iteratively, using a decoder RNN and a canvas matrix to capture the differences between the target image and the current draw, integrating deep learning with predictive coding.

The inference model of DRAW employs an encoder RNN that processes inputs from the target image, previous outputs, and decoder results, thus enabling closed-loop predictive coding. Additionally, DRAW incorporates a selective attention mechanism to focus on critical observation parts, although further details on this mechanism are not provided in the review.

Another model mentioned is the Neural Adaptive Sequential Monte Carlo (NASMC), introduced by Gu et al. in 2015. This model merges an infinite-order Markovian model on latent variables with a comprehensive observation model akin to that used in STORN and VRNN. A variant of NASMC simplifies the Markovian model to an i.i.d. model, making it similar to STORN. The inference model aligns with VRNN's structure and is parameterized by an RNN. NASMC's unique contribution lies in its integration of the DVAE inference framework with sequential Monte Carlo sampling, enabling a systematic approach to learning the generative model's parameters and sampling from the posterior distribution.
