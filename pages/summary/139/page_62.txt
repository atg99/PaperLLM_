In their 2017 paper, Krishnan et al. introduced a generative model called the Deep Markov Model (DMM), presented in an undriven mode by removing the variable u1:T. They significantly enhanced their previous work by detailing a new series of inference models that either depend or do not depend on the latent variable zt−1, while also varying the reliance on observed data. They examined dependencies on both past and present data sequences (x1:t) and the complete data sequence (x1:T). A key advancement was the introduction of an inference model that aligns with the exact posterior distribution, expressed as qφ(zt|zt−1,xt:T). For a complete data sequence, this leads to the formulation:

qφ(z1:T|x1:T) = T ∏ t=1 qφ(zt|zt−1,xt:T).

This model is termed the Deep Kalman Smoother (DKS), as it integrates information from past states (zt−1) and current and future observations. The paper focuses on the DKS inference equations, which utilize a backward recurrent neural network (RNN) on the observed data (xt), augmented by a layer that merges the RNN output with zt−1. The key equations include:

gt = g(zt−1, xt), 
gt = 1/2 tanh(Wzt−1 + b) + gt−1,
[µφ(gt), σφ(gt)] = ez(gt),
qφ(zt|gt) = N(zt; µφ(gt), diag{σ²φ(gt)}).

Here, ez represents a combining network parameterized by φz, while µφ(gt) and σ²φ(gt) are derived from affine transformations of gt. The recursive nature of these equations allows gt to be interpreted as a deterministic function of zt−1 and xt:T, facilitating further insights into the model's behavior.
