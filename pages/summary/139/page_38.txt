State Space Models (SSMs) are structured in a way that they can be represented as linear-Gaussian linear dynamical systems (LG-LDS). The key equations governing these models involve matrices and vectors that define the relationship between the state at time t, the previous state, and the input. The inference process for these models utilizes the Kalman filter, which provides a closed-form solution when considering causal inference, i.e., using past and present observations at each time step. 

In contrast, when a complete sequence of observations is utilized, the method is known as the Kalman smoother. The main goal in practical scenarios is to estimate a "clean" state vector trajectory from noisy observations. 

The Kalman filter operates through an iterative process comprising two main steps: prediction and update. In the prediction step, the predictive distribution of the state is computed based on past observations. This involves integrating over past states to yield the current state distribution. The update step employs Bayes' rule to incorporate the current observation, resulting in the filtering distribution that can be updated recursively. In linear-Gaussian cases, this filtering distribution is also Gaussian, and its parameters can be computed through recursive relations. Thus, the Kalman filter serves as a powerful tool for estimating hidden states in dynamic systems from observed data.
