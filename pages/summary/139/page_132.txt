The study investigates the performance of different autoregressive models—SRNN, VRNN, and STORN—on speech data, noting that SRNN performs best with 8 active dimensions out of 16, while STORN is less efficient, having only 4 active dimensions. In contrast, nonautoregressive models like DKF, DSAE, and RVAE maintain all 16 dimensions as useful for encoding, indicating no posterior collapse. The analysis reveals that the active and inactive dimensions remain consistent across different examples, and dimensions that experience posterior collapse are invariant over time. This suggests that the architecture's time invariance and the characteristics of the generative distributions contribute to the observed patterns of dimensional collapse. The findings highlight the importance of model architecture in influencing the effectiveness of latent representations in speech data processing.
