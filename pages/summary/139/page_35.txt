The paper discusses the relationship between General Regression Neural Networks (GRNN) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), highlighting a key difference: while the GRNN has a deterministic evolution of its internal state (denoted as ht), the latent state (z) of the VAE is stochastic and independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.). The generation of the output vector (xt) in GRNN is stochastic, but the evolution of ht remains deterministic.

The paper formalizes the relationship between ht and the input sequence (u1:t) by expressing ht as a function of this input. In predictive mode, the output distribution is conditioned on ht derived from previous outputs. The stochastic version of the RNN can be trained using statistical criteria, such as maximum likelihood, focusing on maximizing the log-likelihood of observed data over training sequences.

Furthermore, the paper introduces State Space Models (SSMs), which are commonly used to model dynamical systems across various fields. It presents a framework for discrete-time, continuous-valued SSMs, defining the evolution of the latent state (zt) and the generation of the output (xt) using Gaussian distributions parameterized by functions of previous states and inputs. This framework allows for a structured approach to modeling and predicting time-dependent data.
