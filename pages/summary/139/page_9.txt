This paper introduces the concept of deep dynamical Bayesian networks (DDBNs), which are a specific type of deep generative model (DGM). DDBNs integrate Bayesian frameworks for both data generation and modeling the underlying data structure, applicable in tasks like data denoising and transformation. A critical aspect of modeling is the need for the probability density function (pdf) to closely resemble the true pdf of the data, which is typically unknown. To achieve this, models are trained using maximum likelihood estimation, although this process can be complex for DGMs due to the involvement of deep neural networks (DNNs) in generating pdf parameters.

The discussion starts with Bayesian networks (BNs), which are probabilistic models that represent dependencies among random variables through conditional pdfs and can be illustrated via directed acyclic graphs. These dependencies often reflect a hierarchical generative process. Dynamical Bayesian networks extend BNs by incorporating temporal dependencies, making them suitable for modeling dynamical systems and data sequences. They manifest as repeated BNs over time, demonstrating a structure of dependencies across discrete time slices. Notably, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and state-space models (SSMs) serve as specific instances of dynamical BNs, with temporal dependencies modeled through deterministic recursive processes or first-order Markovian processes.
