In this section, we introduce Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and their training methodology, focusing on approximate posterior distribution estimation through variational methods, as detailed in prior works by Kingma and Welling, and Rezende et al. An extensive tutorial on VAEs is also referenced.

To clarify the principles underlying VAEs, we begin with a discussion of autoencoders (AEs). An AE is a deep neural network designed to reproduce an input vector x at its output. During training, the target output matches the input, and at testing, the output is an approximation of the input. The AE typically has a diabolo shape, comprising an encoder on the left that compresses the input into a lower-dimensional latent representation z at the bottleneck layer, and a decoder on the right that reconstructs the input from this latent space. The process is deterministic, meaning that a specific input vector results in a consistent output.
