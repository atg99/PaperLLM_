The paper discusses the Recurrent Variational Autoencoder (RVAE) model introduced by Leglaive et al. in 2020 for speech enhancement applications. The RVAE utilizes a Gaussian noise model combined with nonnegative matrix factorization of variance within a Bayesian framework. Parameters of the RVAE are estimated offline using a large dataset of clean speech signals through the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) methodology, specifically maximizing the variational lower bound (VLB). A variational expectation-maximization (VEM) algorithm is then employed to estimate additional parameters from noisy speech signals, leading to the derivation of probabilistic Wiener filters for speech enhancement.

The RVAE model is specifically designed to represent speech signals in the short-term Fourier transform (STFT) domain, handling sequences of complex-valued vectors. Consequently, the observation model adopts a multivariate zero-mean circular complex Gaussian distribution, as opposed to the standard multivariate real-valued Gaussian distribution, highlighting its suitability for modeling complex-valued data in speech signals.
