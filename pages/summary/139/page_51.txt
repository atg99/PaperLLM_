DVAEs extend the standard Variational Autoencoder framework by incorporating dependencies on past latent vectors in both inference and generation processes. The key aspect of DVAEs is that the latent variable zt at time t depends on the entire sequence of past latent variables z1:t−1. This dependency can be modeled using either a single recurrent neural network (RNN) or two distinct RNNs, allowing for a shared structure in the encoder and decoder.

The internal states of the encoder and decoder are denoted as ht and gt, respectively, with the possibility of them being the same. This shared framework leads to parameter sharing between the encoder and decoder, which is a departure from traditional VAEs, where such sharing does not typically occur.

Training a DVAE involves maximizing the Variational Lower Bound (VLB), which is adapted from the standard VAE to account for data sequences. The extended VLB incorporates the expectations over the latent variables z1:T given the observed sequences x1:T and control variables u1:T. This allows for a structured approach to evaluate the model's performance and optimize it effectively.

In summary, DVAEs represent a significant advancement in the modeling of sequential data by leveraging past information in both the inference and generation stages, resulting in a more coherent and integrated framework for understanding dynamic systems.
