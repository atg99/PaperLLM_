Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) address the challenge of intractable log-marginal likelihood computations resulting from the nonlinear relationships between latent and observed variables. This complexity arises because the observed variables are generated through a deep neural network (DNN) from the latent variables, making the conditional probability pθx(x|z) a nonlinear function of z. A common method to tackle this issue is to maximize a lower bound on the log-marginal likelihood, which relies on the posterior distribution of the latent variables or its approximation.

This approach leads to the expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm and its variants, such as Monte Carlo EM and variational EM, when the posterior distribution is intractable. VAEs utilize variational inference techniques, which involve defining a variational family of probability density functions (pdfs) over the latent variables z. The decomposition of the log-marginal likelihood reveals that the evidence lower bound (ELBO) can be expressed as the sum of the ELBO and the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the variational distribution q(z) and the true posterior pθ(z|x).

The ELBO is defined such that it is always less than or equal to the log-marginal likelihood, with equality holding when the variational distribution matches the exact posterior. The EM algorithm iteratively maximizes the ELBO, alternating between the E-step, where the best variational approximation of the posterior is found, and the M-step, where the parameters θ are optimized. This iterative process ultimately seeks to improve the approximation of the true posterior distribution.
