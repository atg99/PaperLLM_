The Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is a generative model that consists of two main components: an encoder and a decoder. The encoder maps observed data into a latent space, while the decoder reconstructs the data from this latent representation. The encoder outputs parameters for a probability distribution, typically a Gaussian, which captures the uncertainty in the latent space representation.

Mathematically, the VAE defines the joint distribution of the observed variable x and the latent variable z as the product of the conditional distribution of x given z and the prior distribution of z. The prior is usually a standard normal distribution. The decoder network, parameterized by weights and biases, models the conditional distribution as a function of the latent variable z, allowing for flexible data generation.

The VAE framework was established by Kingma and Welling in 2014, providing a systematic approach to learning complex data distributions. This model is applicable to various types of data, enabling it to handle different conditional distributions beyond the Gaussian case.
