The section discusses the inference model used in STORN, specifically addressing the challenges and adjustments made to the approximate posterior distribution qφ as presented by Bayer and Osendorfer. The authors note that the inference relies on a forward RNN, but there is a discrepancy in the generation and inference process due to a one-step lag between the generated xt+1 and the inferred zt. This lag is problematic as it results in inferior inference performance.

To rectify this issue, the authors propose a revised notation for the generation process, leading to a more accurate formulation of the inference equations. The equations detail the computation of the internal state gt of the inference RNN, which is viewed as a deterministic function of the input sequence x1:t. The model is expressed compactly, emphasizing that the inference distribution qφ(z1:T|x1:T) is a product over time steps, which highlights the recursive nature of the model.

However, it is acknowledged that this inference model does not align perfectly with the exact posterior distribution pθ(z1:T|x1:T) at various points in the process, indicating potential limitations in the model's accuracy. The section concludes by referring to a graphical model representation that illustrates these relationships.
