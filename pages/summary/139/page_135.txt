The paper discusses experiments comparing autoregressive and nonautoregressive models in the context of 3D human motion data. The findings suggest that the difference in performance between these models is more pronounced than in previous experiments with speech data, likely due to the smoother trajectories of the motion data. The SRNN model consistently outperformed others, aligning with results from speech data analysis. STORN showed improved efficiency in this context, while DKF performed poorly compared to other nonautoregressive models, only marginally better than the vanilla VAE.

In terms of generating 3D human motion sequences, example videos are available online. The paper also explores the impact of scheduled sampling during training. Results indicate that the SRNN model trained with scheduled sampling performs better than when trained in teacher-forcing mode, although the improvement over the vanilla VAE is limited. The authors suggest that further research into more sophisticated scheduled sampling strategies could enhance performance and bridge the gap between ground-truth and generated data.
