The paper discusses the implementation of Deep Kalman Filters (DKFs) through the framework of Deep Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs). It highlights the advantages of the VAE methodology in addressing challenges related to computational complexity and model parameter estimation in previous approaches. The authors outline the structure of the generative equations used in DKFs, noting that the state dynamics are modeled using deep neural networks (DNNs).

In presenting the generative model, the paper references earlier works by Krishnan et al., which employed multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) for state transition modeling. The authors detail the architecture of the DNNs, including the use of gated linear combinations and various activation functions, which enhance the model's flexibility in handling both linear and nonlinear transitions.

Additionally, the paper draws parallels between the stochastic state of the state-space model (SSM) and the latent state in the VAE context, suggesting that the choice of probability distribution for the state variable does not significantly impact the overarching analysis. The authors advocate for the use of Gaussian distributions in this discussion for comparative clarity with other models. Overall, the paper sets the stage for a deeper exploration of DVAE models, emphasizing their generative and inference components while indicating the optimization strategies employed in model training.
