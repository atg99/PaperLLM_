The paper discusses Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and their relationship with State Space Models, highlighting their ability to transform input vector sequences into output vector sequences, potentially using an internal state vector. The distinction between driven and undriven modes is made, with driven mode involving external inputs and undriven mode lacking external inputs. The basic structure of a single-layer RNN is outlined, including the equations for updating the internal state and producing the output, incorporating weight matrices and nonlinear activation functions. The paper extends this concept to more complex architectures, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), which introduce additional internal variables known as gates. The representation provided is described as a generic or high-level model for RNNs of varying complexities, including multi-layer configurations. Overall, the paper emphasizes the flexibility and adaptability of RNNs in modeling sequences.
