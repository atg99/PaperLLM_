In this section, the authors discuss the results of their experiments on speech data using a scheduled sampling approach to address challenges in model training. They began with a Sequential Recurrent Neural Network (SRNN) model trained in teacher-forcing mode and then fine-tuned it by gradually replacing the ground-truth input with generated speech signals over 500 epochs, resulting in the SRNN-GM model. Additionally, they evaluated the initial SRNN in generation mode, referred to as SRNN-TF-GM, which yielded poor results due to train/test mismatch.

The findings indicate that while SRNN-GM improves performance compared to SRNN-TF-GM, it still lags behind models like DKF and RVAE. This suggests that leveraging predictive links in scenarios without access to ground-truth values is more challenging than in teacher-forcing configurations.

The authors also visualize the learned latent vector's behavior, showcasing the mean and log-variance trajectories of the latent vector as well as the KL divergence term for a sample sentence. They note that certain dimensions of the latent vector maintain a consistent profile in both mean and log-variance, indicating stable representations during the training process.
