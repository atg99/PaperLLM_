The paper discusses the behavior of the latent vector in a speech spectrogram analyzed through a Stochastic Recurrent Neural Network (SRNN) trained with teacher-forcing. It presents four key components: the mean value of the posterior distribution, the log-variance, the sampled latent vector, and the KL divergence term of the Variational Lower Bound (VLB). 

The mean of the posterior distribution is close to zero, and the log-variance is significantly lower than one, indicating that certain dimensions of the latent vector samples appear as noise with minor fluctuations. This suggests that these dimensions are noninformative. Conversely, the "active" dimensions exhibit larger fluctuations, indicating they carry informative content.

The inactivity of some dimensions is indicative of the posterior collapse problem, where the posterior distribution approaches the prior distribution, resulting in noninformative latent variables. This is evidenced by the small and steady KL divergence values for these dimensions compared to the active ones. The paper notes that this issue has been extensively documented in the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) literature, citing several key studies on the subject.
