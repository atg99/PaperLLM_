The paper discusses the definition and functioning modes of Dynamical Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs), particularly focusing on autoregressive DVAEs. It highlights that DVAEs can be utilized for various tasks, such as data generation, analysis-synthesis through encoder-decoder chaining, and data transformation by altering latent vector sequences. 

A key aspect addressed is the dichotomy in generation modes for autoregressive DVAEs, specifically the distinction between "teacher forcing" and "generation mode." In teacher forcing, the model uses actual past observed vectors to generate the current vector, while in generation mode, it relies on previously generated vectors. The paper emphasizes the importance of differentiating between ground-truth observed values and their modeled versions produced by the DVAE. 

The issue of using either ground-truth or generated past vectors is a well-established challenge in recursive models, including Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs); however, it remains underexplored in the DVAE literature. The terminology of teacher forcing, originating from RNN research, is adopted in this context to describe the training configuration where the model is optimized to fit the entire sequence of observed data. This setup is noted to be impractical during the actual generation process.
