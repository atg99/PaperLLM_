In variational inference, the goal is to find an optimal variational distribution that approximates the true posterior distribution as closely as possible. When the exact posterior is intractable, variational methods optimize a lower bound on the marginal likelihood, leading to an efficient approximation of the posterior. 

The approach often involves iteratively updating the parameters of the variational distribution to minimize the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the variational distribution and the true posterior. This optimization can be computationally intensive, particularly in high-dimensional settings, but it allows for a more accurate representation of the posterior than simpler mean-field approaches.

In summary, variational inference provides a framework for approximating complex posterior distributions through constrained optimization, with the mean-field approximation offering a simpler solution while more flexible parametric forms can capture additional dependencies among latent variables.
