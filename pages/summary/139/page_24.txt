In this section, the paper discusses learning through variational inference, particularly in the context of variational autoencoders (VAEs). The generative model is defined, highlighting that the marginal likelihood pθ(x) is derived from the integral of the conditional distribution pθ(x|z) and the prior pθ(z). Unlike probabilistic principal component analysis (PPCA), the posterior distribution in VAEs cannot be analytically determined and requires approximation.

The training objective involves minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the true data distribution p⋆(x) and the model distribution pθ(x). This is mathematically expressed as minimizing DKL(p⋆(x) || pθ(x)), which corresponds to maximizing the expected log-likelihood of the model parameters given the training data. Since the true distribution is unknown, the optimization is performed on a training dataset X, where samples are assumed to be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) according to p⋆(x). The intractable expectation in the KL divergence is approximated using a Monte Carlo estimate, allowing for practical maximization of the log-likelihood of the model parameters.

In summary, the section outlines the principles of variational inference as a method for training generative models with latent variables, emphasizing the challenges posed by intractable marginal likelihoods and the use of empirical risk minimization for practical optimization.
