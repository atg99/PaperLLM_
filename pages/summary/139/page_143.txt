The paper discusses the differences between Dynamic Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs) and standard Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), particularly in terms of their inference model structures and the computation of the Variational Lower Bound (VLB). It emphasizes that while the structure of the exact posterior distribution can guide the inference model design, it is not essential to replicate it, especially if it leads to noncausal processing. Simplifying these dependencies can reduce computational complexity, which is crucial for online applications.

Further, the paper highlights that the recursive nature of DVAEs necessitates a different sampling approach compared to VAEs, which can become computationally intensive when using Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). The discussion also touches on the flexibility in implementing DVAEs, noting that various model representations can achieve the same compact representation, crucial for understanding probabilistic dependencies among variables. However, the optimization process targets a specific model rather than exploring all potential models that fit the compact relationships, thus emphasizing the importance of the chosen representation in guiding the optimization process within recurrent architectures.
