The paper discusses the computational processes involved in recurrent neural networks and state space models, specifically focusing on the two key steps: prediction and update. These steps are essential for calculating model parameters at time t−1 and involve basic matrix and vector operations. The mean vector of the filtering distribution is highlighted as a linear representation of the observation vector, serving as the state estimate.

In the context of noncausal models, a similar predictive and update process occurs, but it incorporates both forward and backward recursion, resulting in a smoothing distribution. While the paper acknowledges the relevance of the Kalman filter and smoother, it notes that a detailed discussion of these topics is outside its scope.

The paper also touches on nonlinear dynamical systems, often referred to as nonlinear Kalman filters, which have been extensively explored prior to the deep learning era. It mentions various extensions to the Kalman Filter designed to address nonlinearities, such as the extended Kalman filter and the unscented Kalman filter. However, the focus remains on deep variational autoencoders (DVAEs), with a review of nonlinear Kalman filters deemed unnecessary for the paper's objectives.
