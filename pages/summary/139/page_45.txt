Dynamical Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) utilize recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to model dependencies among random variables over time. The paper discusses two implementations of a given factorization of these models. The first implementation uses a single internal state variable, while the second employs two distinct internal state variables to generate outputs.

In the first implementation, the RNN's internal state variable, denoted as ht, captures historical information and is used to generate both the latent variable zt and the observed variable xt. The model assumes Gaussian distributions for the variables, leading to equations that express how ht is computed from previous inputs and states, and how it influences the distributions of zt and xt.

The equations illustrate that the parameters of the conditional distributions are derived from non-linear functions implemented through deep neural networks (DNNs). This framework highlights the flexibility in modeling temporal data and the importance of RNNs in capturing complex dependencies. Overall, the paper presents a structured approach to understanding the dynamics of VAEs through graphical models and RNN implementations.
