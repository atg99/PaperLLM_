In this section, the paper introduces the concept of Dynamical Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs), emphasizing the significance of both compact and developed graphical representations. The compact form encapsulates various implementations, while the developed form delineates a specific subset of models that facilitate optimization. The authors highlight that higher-order temporal dependencies may not be explicitly evident in the developed form, as these can be encoded within internal state variables. 

The paper further discusses the inference model within the DVAE framework, outlining the challenges posed by intractable posterior distributions due to nonlinearities and temporal dependencies. To address this, an inference model is defined to approximate the posterior distribution, enabling the inference of latent sequences and parameter estimation for the generative model. Similar to standard VAEs, this inference model employs deep neural networks (DNNs) for parameter generation.

Additionally, the authors touch on the concept of D-separation within Bayesian networks, which provides a methodology for expressing the general form of posterior distributions, despite the inherent computational challenges. This approach is crucial for understanding the underlying dynamics of DVAEs and their applications.
