In this section, the paper discusses the relationship between different deep learning models, specifically the Deep Kalman Filter (DKF), Variational Autoencoder (VAE), and deep Stochastic State Models (SSM). It establishes that the DKF observation model aligns with the VAE decoder when the latent variable z is of reduced dimension compared to the input x. Consequently, the DKF can be interpreted as a VAE decoder that incorporates a temporal (Markovian) model for the latent variable.

Furthermore, a deep SSM is characterized as a fully stochastic variant of a deep Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), where stochasticity is introduced at both the observation and internal state levels. The deterministic internal state of the RNN is replaced with a stochastic state in the SSM. The paper summarizes these relationships by equating DKF, deep SSM, Markovian VAE decoder, and fully stochastic RNN.

The inference model is then defined by identifying the structure of the SSM/DKF posterior distribution pθ(z1:T|x1:T,u1:T). Using the chain rule, the distribution is expressed as a product of terms involving conditional probabilities. D-separation simplifies these terms, indicating that the node zt−1 blocks past information from affecting zt, which leads to the conclusion that the distribution can be rewritten as pθ(zt|zt−1,xt:T,ut:T). This results in a structured representation of the posterior distribution across time steps.
