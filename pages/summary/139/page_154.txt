In this section, the paper discusses improvements and extensions to Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), specifically focusing on Deep Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs). It highlights the relationship between hierarchical latent variable models and DVAEs, noting that DVAEs are characterized by timely ordered latent and observed variables, distinguishing them as a specific case of structured VAEs. 

The authors draw a parallel between DVAE and hierarchical VAE literature, particularly regarding the design of inference models. They point out that the D-separation methodology, a crucial tool for guiding inference model design, has not been systematically applied in DVAE models nor mentioned in hierarchical VAE papers. Instead, authors often select models based on intuitive structures, such as top-down versus bottom-up inference, without emphasizing the D-separation principles.

A key strategy discussed is the concept of module sharing, where the inference model's variable dependencies reflect those of the generative model. This approach can enhance training speed and model fitting, as evidenced in the VRNN model. The authors illustrate this with an example from Salimans (2016), where the inference model structure mirrors the generative model's exact posterior distribution, showcasing that module sharing can coexist with maintaining the integrity of the posterior distribution structure.
