This flexibility is particularly beneficial for DVAEs, as it enables the integration of past and present data in a causal manner, enhancing the model's ability to process sequential data.

The iterative amortized inference technique introduced by Marino et al. provides a framework where the approximate posterior parameters are refined iteratively, leveraging both the current estimates and the gradient of the variational lower bound (VLB). This approach aligns with the principles of the Bayes rule, contrasting with traditional bottom-up models that only map observed data to posterior distributions. 

Semi-amortized VAEs further address the limitations of standard variational inference by combining an encoder network for initial estimates with stochastic variational inference for refinement, thus promoting a more nuanced approach to posterior estimation.

Overall, these advancements in variational inference techniques significantly enhance the performance and applicability of DVAEs in modeling complex sequential data, demonstrating their potential in dynamic systems and offering improved estimation capabilities.
