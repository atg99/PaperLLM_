The discussion addresses the posterior collapse problem in variational autoencoders (VAEs), particularly in natural language processing contexts. This issue arises when a latent vector z fails to effectively encode information from long input sequences, resulting in the autoregressive decoder relying on the RNN internal state vectors instead. Various strategies have been proposed to mitigate this problem.

Bowman et al. (2016) introduced two approaches: first, applying annealing to the KL term of the VAE, gradually increasing its influence during training to ensure z initially encodes data information before being disentangled. Second, they suggested weakening the decoder by masking parts of the word embedding sequence during training. Yeung et al. (2017) proposed a more complex method by adding an extra latent variable to selectively activate portions of z, allowing more efficient use of the latent representation.

He et al. (2018) took a heuristic approach by aggressively training the encoder before each decoder iteration, helping the encoder better align with the evolving posterior distribution. Further solutions have been explored by Lucas et al. (2019), Razavi et al. (2019), and Dai et al. (2020), contributing to more effective and disentangled latent representations.

Overall, while existing methods have improved the situation, there remains potential for further advancements in addressing the posterior collapse issue in VAEs.
