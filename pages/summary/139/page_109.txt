The paper discusses various models related to variational autoencoders, with a focus on the Variational Recurrent Autoencoder (VRAE) introduced by Fabius and Amersfoort in 2014. In the VRAE framework, a data sequence is represented by a single latent random vector rather than a sequence of latent vectors. The generative model is expressed compactly, utilizing a forward recurrent neural network (RNN) to derive the hidden state and subsequently predict the next data point based on the previous context and the latent vector. The inference model similarly employs a forward RNN to process the entire sequence, yielding parameters for the latent vector.

The paper also mentions a related model by Babaeizadeh et al. in 2018, which predicts multiple data points from both past context and a unique latent vector. This model's inference method aligns with that of the VRAE, but it contrasts with a baseline model that uses a frame-by-frame latent vector. 

Furthermore, the Factorized Hierarchical Variational Autoencoder (FHVAE) proposed by Hsu et al. in 2017 aims to learn disentangled and interpretable latent representations from sequential data without supervision. The paper highlights these developments as significant contributions to the natural language processing community, particularly in the context of dynamic variational autoencoders.
