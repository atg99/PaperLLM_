In this section, the focus is on generative models, particularly causal models, which are used to order dependencies during the generation process. A causal model is defined as one where the distribution of the generated variable at time t relies solely on its past values and/or the values of other variables at time t and earlier. In contrast, an anticausal model depends on future time indices, while a noncausal model incorporates past, present, and future values of the conditioning variables.

The discussion highlights that causal modeling is predominant in the Deep Variational Autoencoder (DVAE) literature, although noncausal modeling exists, with the RVAE model being a notable example. The section emphasizes that the methodology for causal and noncausal modeling is fundamentally similar. 

In causal DVAEs, the joint distribution of latent and observed sequences is factorized over time using the chain rule, assuming that the observed variables depend causally on the input sequence. This principle underpins the structure of DVAEs, laying the groundwork for further exploration in subsequent sections.
