The paper discusses the implementation of Disentangled Sequential Autoencoders (DSAEs) for processing speech signals. The model employs a Gaussian distribution for the dynamical model, represented as pθz(zt|z1:t−1), with parameters derived from an LSTM network. The observation model, pθx(xt|zt,v), also follows a Gaussian distribution, with parameters generated by a feed-forward deep neural network (DNN).

The LSTM architecture allows for a virtually infinite-order temporal model, enhancing the capabilities compared to traditional first-order Markov models like the Dynamic Kalman Filter (DKF). This extension demonstrates the potential for incorporating complex transition dynamics and emission distributions into Kalman filters via deep learning techniques, as noted by Krishnan et al. in their 2015 study.

The inference model for DSAE is articulated through the posterior distribution of latent variables, pθ(z1:T,v|x1:T), which is decomposed into the product of the prior pθ(v|x1:T) and the conditional distribution of latent variables given the observed data. This formulation enables the effective learning of the model by optimizing a bound on the likelihood of the data, showcasing the advantages of deep learning in enhancing traditional statistical models.
