The experiments utilized the Wall Street Journal dataset, focusing on the speaker-independent medium vocabulary subset, specifically the si_tr_s for training, si_dt_05 for validation, and si_et_05 for testing. The preprocessing involved applying the short-time Fourier transform to the raw speech waveform sampled at 16kHz, resulting in 513-dimensional discrete Fourier spectra.

For the training phase, speech utterances of 0.8 seconds were extracted, leading to the creation of 46,578 training sequences and 7,775 validation sequences. Each training sequence was represented as a 513 by 50 power spectrogram. The testing phase utilized STFT spectrograms of variable lengths, typically exceeding 2.4 seconds, after removing silence portions.

The modeling approach included a zero-mean circular complex Gaussian distribution for the complex-valued STFT coefficients, while the DVAE output was derived from the squared magnitude of the STFT spectrogram. This setup facilitated the reconstruction of the output speech signal through inverse STFT using the combined magnitude and phase spectrograms, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed method in processing speech data.
