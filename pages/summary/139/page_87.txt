The paper discusses Stochastic Recurrent Neural Networks (SRNN), highlighting their graphical model and distinguishing features compared to Variational Recurrent Neural Networks (VRNN). The SRNN maintains a deterministic internal state (ht) while integrating a latent state (zt) at the dx level, which allows for a clearer separation between deterministic and stochastic layers. Fraccaro et al. (2016) introduced an explicit temporal model for zt, establishing a first-order Markovian dependency that is combined with the internal state ht, contrasting with the implicit temporal dependency seen in VRNN.

The equations presented describe the mean and variance of the distribution for zt, which are influenced by both the previous latent state (zt-1) and the internal state (ht). The graphical representation of SRNN shows the relationship between these states, emphasizing that the feature extraction process is simplified, with parameters represented as a union of those related to the internal and latent states.

The SRNN employs a two-layer feed-forward network for dx and dz, while the function dh utilizes a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) RNN, enabling the model to capture long-term information effectively. The unfolding of recurrence is applied to represent ht as a function of preceding inputs, thereby clarifying the model's structure. Overall, the SRNN framework presents advancements in modeling temporal dependencies in stochastic processes.
