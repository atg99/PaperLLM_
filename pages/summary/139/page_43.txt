The paper discusses the definition and framework of Dynamical Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs), presenting a factorized joint distribution of observed variables and latent variables over time. The equation p(x1:T,z1:T|u1:T) is introduced, generalizing previous formulations and illustrating the alternate sampling of latent variables zt and observed variables xt. The text emphasizes the initialization of the model and how different models in the literature make varying conditional independence assumptions to simplify dependencies in the distributions.

Key assumptions include that the observed variable xt depends only on the latent variable zt, and the latent variable zt depends solely on its previous state zt-1 and input ut. The concept of driven and predictive modes is outlined, where a DVAE is considered in driven mode when the input sequence u1:T influences the generation of either or both xt and zt. In predictive mode, prior observations x1:t-1 are used for generating new observations or latent variables, reflecting feedback mechanisms akin to autoregressive processes commonly analyzed in control theory and time-series analysis.

Overall, the paper establishes a comprehensive framework for understanding the operation of DVAEs, highlighting their dual capabilities in both driven and predictive modes, which can be utilized independently or concurrently.
