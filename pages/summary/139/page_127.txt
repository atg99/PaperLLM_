The paper discusses various models for speech dynamics, particularly focusing on the differences between DSAE, DKF, RVAE, and STORN in terms of their inference structures and performance metrics.

DSAE is presented as an enhancement of DKF, incorporating a sequence-level variable and infinite-order temporal dependencies. However, its performance does not surpass that of DKF, which is attributed to the discrepancies in the inference model. DSAE's reliance on all observed variables for inference leads to the omission of necessary previous latent variables, while also introducing unnecessary dependencies, unlike DKF which aligns with the exact posterior distribution.

The RVAE model exhibits competitive performance, with SI-SDR scores marginally lower than DKF and DSAE, but a superior PESQ score in its noncausal form. This success is attributed to its efficient inference model, which captures the entire sequence of observations effectively, despite lacking a predictive link and dynamical model for the latent vector.

Conversely, STORN's disappointing results stem from its inference model not adhering to the exact posterior distribution structure, failing to utilize essential latent and observed variables, which diminishes its performance in the speech analysis-resynthesis task. Overall, the paper suggests that aligning inference models with the true posterior structure could enhance the performance of models like DSAE and STORN.
