The introduction outlines the integration of deep Bayesian networks (BNs) and deep neural networks (DNNs) to create deep dynamical Bayesian networks (DDBNs). This combination leverages the explainability of Bayesian models while utilizing the powerful representation capabilities of DNNs. DDBNs can be viewed as either dynamic versions of deep BNs, incorporating temporal dependencies, or deep versions of dynamical BNs, merging DNNs with traditional dynamical approaches. Despite their potential for effectively modeling dynamical systems and data sequences, DDBNs face challenges in model training complexity and cost due to their intricate structures.

The introduction also discusses variational inference, particularly its application to deep BNs leading to the development of variational autoencoders (VAEs). VAEs facilitate efficient inference and training by connecting latent variables and unsupervised representation learning. In this context, it is assumed that observed data is generated from unobserved latent variables, typically of lower dimensionality, which encode the information of the observed, potentially high-dimensional data. This framework is supported by foundational works in the field, highlighting the evolution and significance of these methodologies.
