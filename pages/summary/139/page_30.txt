The section on Variational Autoencoder (VAE) training discusses optimization techniques for learning the parameters of both the generative and inference models. The training process involves cascading the encoder and decoder networks, with parameters θ and φ estimated jointly from the training data X. This approach contrasts with the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm, which optimizes the Variational Lower Bound (VLB) in two alternating steps. He et al. (2018) noted that this joint training method may yield suboptimal results.

The VLB is expressed in terms of reconstruction accuracy and regularization. The reconstruction term measures the average accuracy of the encoding-decoding process, representing the mean-squared error between the original data and the decoder's output when a Gaussian generative model is used. The regularization term ensures that the approximate posterior distribution is close to the prior distribution, promoting a disentangled representation of the data where each entry of z captures different characteristics.

While regularization terms can often be analytically expressed, the expectation in the reconstruction accuracy term is typically intractable and is approximated using Monte Carlo methods with samples drawn independently. This combination of terms aims to balance reconstruction quality and regularization to enhance the model's performance.
