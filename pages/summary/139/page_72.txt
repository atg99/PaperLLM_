The paper discusses Stochastic Recurrent Networks (STORN), introduced by Bayer and Osendorfer in 2014, as a model that integrates both deterministic and stochastic components. Specifically, STORN combines an internal deterministic state (ht) with an internal stochastic state (zt). The authors focus on the undriven and predictive modes of STORN, which utilize the previous observation (ut=xt−1) for generating predictions, while noting that these models can also operate in a driven mode with external inputs.

The generative model of STORN is articulated through a series of equations. The observation model for ht is defined by a function that combines weighted inputs from the previous observation, the stochastic state, and the previous hidden state. The output distribution parameters, µθx(ht) and σθx(ht), are derived from ht using a separate output function. The probability of the current observation xt given ht is modeled as a Gaussian distribution, which is consistent with the structure of a basic single-layer Gaussian Recurrent Neural Network (GRNN). The key distinction in STORN is the inclusion of the stochastic state zt as an additional input alongside the deterministic state ht.
