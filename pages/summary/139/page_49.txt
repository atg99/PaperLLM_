D-separation plays a crucial role in understanding the relationships between variables in a probabilistic graphical model, as it allows us to determine when two sets of variables are independent given a third set. This independence implies that the joint distribution of two variables can be expressed as the product of their conditional distributions given the third variable.

In the context of Dynamical Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs), the design of the approximate posterior distribution, denoted as qφ, can benefit from this principle. It is proposed that qφ should mirror the structure of the exact posterior distribution to maintain variable dependencies and ensure that it is informed by the same data. However, a notable issue arises from the early literature on DVAEs, where the formulation of qφ was often made without consideration of the exact posterior structure, leading to arbitrary choices.

In contrast, more recent DVAEs have begun to align the structure of qφ with that of the exact posterior distribution, indicating a shift towards more informed modeling practices. This paper will further explore this evolution through a detailed examination of various DVAE models in subsequent chapters.

The paper also discusses the general form of the exact posterior distribution, emphasizing the complexity of dependencies within the model. Specifically, it highlights that the latent variable at time t, zt, is influenced not only by previous latent variables but also by the entire sequence of observed variables, underscoring the intricate nature of causal and noncausal inference in these models.
