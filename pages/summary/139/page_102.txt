The simplification derived from D-separation allows for a clearer understanding of how the entire sequence of observations is utilized to estimate the object representation, which in turn updates the object dynamics.

Li and Mandt (2018) introduced two models for the approximate posterior distribution qφ. The first, known as the "factorized" model, simplifies the inference by treating the latent vector dynamics in an instantaneous manner, which is less effective and leads to poorer performance. In contrast, the "full" model provides a more integrated approach, considering how content influences dynamics, and aligns more closely with the exact posterior distribution.

The paper emphasizes that the full inference model is crucial for capturing the complexities of the data, enabling more accurate estimations of the latent states and object representations over time. Detailed equations for the full inference model are provided in the supplementary materials, illustrating its robustness compared to the factorized approach.
