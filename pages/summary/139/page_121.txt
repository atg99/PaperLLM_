This paper presents a study on human motion modeling using the H3.6M dataset, which includes 15 scenarios performed by 7 actors. The authors followed a similar approach to previous work, utilizing data from 5 actors for training and 1 actor for testing. This resulted in 88,952 training sequences and 13,838 test sequences, with a validation set comprising 1,920 sequences selected from Actor 5 to streamline computation.

The 3D human motion data was modeled using a Gaussian generative conditional distribution, employing an identity covariance matrix. The models were implemented in PyTorch and trained using the Adam optimizer, with specific learning rates for speech and motion data. Early stopping was applied to prevent overfitting, and a warm-up procedure for the KL regularization term was used during the initial epochs of training.

After training, the models were evaluated on the test set, with performance metrics for speech data including the scale-invariant signal-to-distortion ratio. The methodology emphasizes a systematic approach to training and evaluation, ensuring robust performance assessment of the DVAE models.
