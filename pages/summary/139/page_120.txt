The paper discusses the experimental protocol for utilizing a Variational Deep Autoencoder (DVAE) in analyzing power spectrograms and 3D human motion data. The power spectrogram entries are modeled using a Gamma distribution, specifically with a shape parameter of 1 and a scale parameter of σ²θx,f,t(·). This choice supports the use of the Itakura-Saito divergence in the reconstruction term of the Variational Lower Bound (VLB), as highlighted by Girin et al. in 2019. The DVAE models are adaptable in terms of the conditional probability density function of xt, allowing for the Gamma distribution to replace the Gaussian distribution typically used, which is suitable for speech and audio applications. The linear layer responsible for estimating the distribution parameters contains 513 output units corresponding to the log-variance parameters.

For the 3D human motion data experiments, the H3.6M dataset is employed, renowned for its extensive application in various domains such as video prediction and human pose estimation. This dataset comprises multi-view recordings of 11 professional actors engaged in 17 scenarios, captured at a resolution of 50 Hz using four calibrated cameras. The 3D coordinates of 32 human skeleton joints are extracted and centered relative to the pelvis joint. Each data frame is structured as a 96-dimensional vector by concatenating the 3D coordinates of the joints, with sequences of T=50 vectors representing a 2-second duration. These sequences are derived from the original H3.6M data by applying a sliding window technique, resulting in matrices of 3D joint coordinates sized 96×50 for analysis.
