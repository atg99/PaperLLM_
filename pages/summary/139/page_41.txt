The paper discusses the concept of Dynamical Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs), focusing on their generative modeling capabilities in a driven mode. In this mode, the model incorporates an input sequence of observed random vectors, denoted as u1:T, while x1:T represents the output sequence. The goal is to model the joint distribution pθ(x1:T,z1:T,u1:T). However, the primary interest lies in modeling the conditional distribution pθ(x1:T,z1:T|u1:T), where the input sequence is considered deterministic and the output sequences are stochastic.

The structure of dependencies in the generative model is described, indicating that DVAEs can be viewed as hierarchical models with ordered observed and latent variables, reflecting temporal dependencies. The joint distribution for the observed and latent sequences is typically expressed using the chain rule, emphasizing that various orderings of random vectors can be selected. This choice of ordering is crucial, as it leads to different practical implementations and sampling processes in DVAEs. The paper sets the stage for further exploration of these concepts and their implications in subsequent sections.
