The generative model discussed focuses on capturing the dynamics of objects through the latent variable zt while the observations xt are directly influenced by the actions at. This approach leverages the disentanglement concept, allowing for a clearer representation of the model's dynamics. 

The joint distribution of the random variables can be expressed as a product of conditional distributions, highlighting the independence of features a1:T given the state sequence z1:T, and the independence of observations x1:T given the features a1:T. This factorization is crucial for understanding the relationships among the variables in the model.

The paper references the limitations of classical linear dynamical systems (LDS) in handling abrupt changes in trajectories. To address this, Fraccaro et al. proposed a switching strategy that involves defining LDS parameters at each time step as a linear combination of predefined matrices or vectors, with coefficients estimated using an LSTM network. While this is an interesting development, it is not the primary focus of the current review of the KVAE model. Additionally, a similar transition model was independently proposed by Karl et al. as part of a deep variational Bayesian filter, extending the class of state-space model-based deep variational autoencoders.
