This section discusses variational inference and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), focusing on their ability to generate new data from latent variables while achieving disentangled latent representations. Disentangled representations allow for better interpretability and control over data generation. The process of discovering latent space structure occurs during model training, with inference defined as estimating latent variables from observed data.

In deep Bayesian networks, the exact posterior distribution is often intractable, leading to the use of a parametric approximate posterior distribution implemented via a deep neural network. The model parameters are then estimated by linking the inference model (encoder) with the generative model (decoder) and maximizing the variational lower bound (VLB) of the log-likelihood function over a training dataset.

The VAE methodology promotes deep unsupervised representation learning alongside efficient inference and parameter estimation within a Bayesian framework. The foundational works by Kingma and Welling (2014) and Rezende et al. (2014) have significantly influenced the machine learning field, with VAEs being utilized in various signal processing applications, including image and speech signal generation and transformation. The concept of using neural networks for inference dates back to earlier studies by Hinton et al. (1995) and Dayan et al. (1995), although their training algorithms differ from those used in VAEs.
