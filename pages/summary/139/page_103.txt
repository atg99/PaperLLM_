The paper introduces the Disentangled Sequential Autoencoders (DSAE) model, detailing its formulation through equations that depict the relationships between latent variables and observed data over time. The model utilizes bidirectional generative functions, denoted as →gv and ←−gv, to encode the input sequence xt and its past and future states. The latent variable gv is constructed from these encodings, and the model infers its distribution through a normal distribution parameterized by means µφv and variances σφv derived from gv.

Additionally, the model employs a similar approach for another latent variable zt, using bidirectional functions →gz and ←−gz. The total distribution of the sequence z1:T is represented as a product of individual distributions qφz(zt|v,x1:T), indicating a factorized approach.

The paper critiques the prior approximations of the posterior distribution, noting that neither the factorized nor full approximations accurately capture the dependencies of the exact posterior distribution. The authors illustrate the full inference model with a graphical representation.

In the training section, the authors outline the derivation of the Variational Lower Bound (VLB) for the DSAE model, emphasizing a methodology consistent with other models by injecting the generative model and approximate posterior into the VLB formulation. They also reference inconsistencies in previous works regarding the inference model parameters, particularly in the input structure for the inference process of zt, which they clarify in their equations.
