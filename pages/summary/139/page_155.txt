The discussion focuses on the design and challenges of Deep Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs), emphasizing the need for an inference model that aligns with the exact posterior while sharing components with the generative model. A significant challenge in DVAEs is disentangling different factors of data variation, particularly when these factors exhibit varying dynamics over time. To address this, the paper suggests employing hierarchical modeling with different time resolutions for latent variables, allowing for a clear separation between data dynamics and more static factors like speaker identity or scene objects.

Models such as the DSAE and FHVAE are mentioned as examples that utilize latent variables at various levels, including sequence, segment, and frame levels, to effectively manage phonetic and speaker/session information in speech signals. The authors propose that imposing a suitable prior distribution on the latent factors can aid in better capturing these dynamics, which may differ from the observed data dynamics.

Despite the advancements, the literature reveals a gap in understanding the explainability of the extracted latent factors in DVAE models. Limited experiments are available, with some notable studies demonstrating the effectiveness of swapping latent factors between data sequences, particularly for speech signals. Additionally, other research on disentanglement in videos has shown promising results using adversarial training techniques, further underscoring the potential for separating content from dynamics in complex data structures.
