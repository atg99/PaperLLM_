The paper discusses the framework for Recurrent Variational Autoencoders (RVAEs), focusing on their generic mathematical formulation. The model is represented by equations that define the relationships between latent variables and observations over time. Three scenarios are explored: an instantaneous model that uses only the current latent state, a causal model that incorporates past and present latent states, and a noncausal model that considers the entire sequence of latent states.

To adapt the model for real-valued observations, the authors suggest modifications to the Gaussian distribution parameters in the equations. The latent state variables are assumed to be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) with a standard Gaussian distribution, resulting in no explicit temporal dependencies for these states, though the observations may reflect past and future values.

The authors highlight that previous work by Leglaive et al. implemented the causal and noncausal models using forward and bidirectional Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), respectively. However, detailed implementation equations were not provided in that work. The paper aims to present those equations for clarity and comparison with other models, emphasizing the use of real-valued observations and the potential for different distributions under certain conditions.
