The paper provides a comprehensive overview of various models and their applications, particularly in the context of Dynamic Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs). It begins with a brief tour of related models, including DKF, STORN, VRNN, and SRNN, highlighting their unique features and contributions. The experimental section details the architecture of DVAEs, the protocols employed, and presents results from speech data and 3D human motion data, concluding with insights drawn from these findings.

The discussion emphasizes the fundamental motivations behind DVAEs, showcasing their flexibility and the potential for improvements and extensions from Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). It also explores perspectives on source coding, suggesting broader implications for the field. The paper includes acknowledgments and appendices that provide technical details on the implementation of various models with both speech and motion data, offering a rich resource for researchers interested in these methodologies.
