The paper discusses the concept of Dynamic Variational Autoencoders (DVAEs) and their relationship with standard Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and first-order state space models (SSMs). A key aspect is the identification of temporal links, where at least one element from previous time steps influences either the latent variable z or the observation x at the current time step. The "minimal DVAE" is characterized as a combination of a first-order SSM and a VAE, specifically illustrated by the Deterministic Kalman Filter (DKF) model.

DVAEs can incorporate various temporal dependencies, which can be first-order or potentially infinite, depending on the architecture used—ranging from feed-forward networks like Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and even Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) with temporal convolutions. The paper highlights that Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs), utilizing dilated convolutions, perform competitively with RNNs for sequence modeling tasks.

Additionally, the paper defines two operational modes for DVAEs: the driven mode and the predictive mode. The driven mode occurs when an external input influences the latent or observation variables, while the predictive mode involves using past observations to affect the current variables. The implementation of these modes can occur through teacher forcing or generation mode, with consistency between training and testing modes generally leading to improved performance.
