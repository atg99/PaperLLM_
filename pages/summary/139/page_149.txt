This gap can hinder the performance of generative models since it relates to the difference between the variational lower bound and the intractable log-marginal likelihood we aim to optimize.

To address this issue, researchers have proposed more sophisticated inference models. One approach is using normalizing flows, which create complex approximate posterior distributions through a series of invertible transformations applied to a simple initial distribution. This method allows for the construction of highly expressive models, making it a specific case of hierarchical modeling. Additionally, mixtures of simpler distributions can be formed by introducing auxiliary latent variables and marginalizing them, further enhancing the expressiveness of the approximate posterior.

Another strategy involves modifying the inference algorithm directly, as suggested by Marino et al. These advancements aim to mitigate the amortization gap and improve the performance of learned generative models.
