The paper presents a comparison of MixMatch, a semi-supervised learning method, against several baseline methods on the CIFAR-10 and SVHN datasets. The key focus is on the test error rates as the number of labeled datapoints varies from 250 to 4000. Results indicate that MixMatch significantly outperforms other methods, achieving a competitive error rate of 6.24% with 4000 labels on CIFAR-10, compared to 4.17% for fully supervised training. Notably, with only 250 labels, MixMatch reaches an error rate of 11.08%, while the next best method, Virtual Adversarial Training, has an error rate of 36.03%, highlighting MixMatch's sample efficiency.

The study employs several baseline methods, including Mean Teacher, Virtual Adversarial Training, and MixUp, all re-implemented under consistent conditions for fair comparison. The experiments involved creating multiple splits for each labeled datapoint count to ensure reliable results.

In addition, the paper explores the performance of MixMatch on a larger model with 26 million parameters, comparing it against results from prior work. MixMatch continues to demonstrate strong performance, matching or exceeding previous benchmarks.

The findings underscore the effectiveness of MixMatch, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data, which is a critical aspect of semi-supervised learning. The results suggest that MixMatch achieves high accuracy with fewer labeled examples, making it a valuable method in the field.
