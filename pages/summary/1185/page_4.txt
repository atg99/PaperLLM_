The paper presents Algorithm 1, known as MixMatch, which processes batches of labeled and unlabeled data to enhance semi-supervised learning. The algorithm takes as input a batch of labeled examples \( X \) and a batch of unlabeled examples \( U \), along with parameters such as sharpening temperature \( T \), number of augmentations \( K \), and the Beta distribution parameter \( \alpha \) for MixUp.

The process begins with data augmentation applied to both labeled and unlabeled examples. For each labeled example \( \hat{x}_b \), \( K \) augmentations are generated for each unlabeled example \( \hat{u}_b \). The average predictions of these augmentations are computed and then sharpened to reduce entropy, effectively creating a more confident label distribution. This sharpening is achieved by adjusting the temperature of the categorical distribution, which influences the model's predictions towards lower entropy as \( T \) approaches zero.

Following augmentation and sharpening, the algorithm combines both labeled and unlabeled data, shuffling them to ensure randomness. MixUp is then applied to create new labeled and unlabeled examples. This modified MixUp approach maintains the order of examples in the batch to enable the appropriate computation of loss components. The output of the algorithm consists of processed labeled examples \( X' \) and unlabeled examples \( U' \) with guessed labels, aiming to improve the model's performance in semi-supervised learning tasks.
