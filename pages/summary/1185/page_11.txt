The references listed span various significant contributions to the fields of deep learning and semi-supervised learning, with a focus on techniques that improve model performance and generalization. 

Nissim and Stemmer (2015) discuss the generalization properties of differential privacy, emphasizing its implications for learning algorithms. Odena (2016) explores semi-supervised learning through generative adversarial networks, which has become a pivotal method in the field. Oliver et al. (2018) provide a realistic evaluation of deep semi-supervised learning algorithms, highlighting the challenges and potential of these methods in practice.

Papernot et al. (2016, 2018) contribute to the understanding of knowledge transfer and scalable private learning, respectively, indicating the intersection of privacy and machine learning. Pu et al. (2016) introduce a variational autoencoder approach for deep learning, while Rasmus et al. (2015) propose ladder networks for semi-supervised learning, showcasing innovative architectures.

Sajjadi et al. (2016) focus on regularization techniques for enhancing semi-supervised learning, and Salakhutdinov and Hinton (2007) apply deep belief networks to learn covariance kernels, bridging deep learning with Gaussian processes. Salimans et al. (2016) improve GAN training techniques, which are crucial for generative models.

Simard et al. (2003) provide best practices for convolutional neural networks in visual document analysis, indicating the practical applications of these models. Tarvainen and Valpola (2017) highlight the benefits of mean teacher models for consistency in semi-supervised learning.

Verma et al. (2019) introduce interpolation consistency training, while Zhang et al. (2018) investigate weight decay regularization mechanisms. Zhang et al. (2017) present mixup, a novel approach that moves beyond traditional empirical risk minimization. Finally, Zhao et al. (2015) discuss stacked what-where autoencoders, and Zhu et al. (2003) examine semi-supervised learning using Gaussian fields, reflecting the diversity of methods and approaches in the ongoing development of machine learning techniques.
