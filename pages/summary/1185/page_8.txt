This paper presents an ablation study of the MixMatch algorithm, assessing the impact of various components on its performance in semi-supervised learning, specifically on the CIFAR-10 dataset with 250 and 4000 labels. The study examines several modifications, including the use of mean class distribution with multiple augmentations versus a single augmentation, the effects of temperature sharpening, and the implementation of an exponential moving average (EMA) of model parameters for label guessing. The results indicate that each component significantly contributes to MixMatch's overall performance, particularly in the 250-label scenario.

Furthermore, the paper explores the concept of privacy-preserving learning, emphasizing the importance of generalization without overfitting to training data. The authors employ the PATE framework, which allows a student model to be trained using public unlabeled data supplemented by labeled data from an ensemble of teachers with access to private data. The effectiveness of MixMatch in this context is highlighted, showing that it can achieve higher accuracy with fewer labeled instances than the existing V AT baseline, thereby offering stronger privacy guarantees.

In summary, the findings underscore the efficacy of MixMatch in enhancing performance in semi-supervised learning while also addressing privacy concerns, marking it as a significant advancement in the field.
