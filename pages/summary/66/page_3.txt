The paper presents an analysis of walking and downstairs movement data captured along the x, y, and z axes. Figures 10 to 12 illustrate the walking data, showing consistent peak spacing across the axes, albeit with varying intensities and ranges. In contrast, Figures 13 to 15 depict the downstairs data, characterized by regular peaks on the x-axis, significant spikes on the y-axis, and a z-axis profile that is less aggressive than that of jogging but still similar.

The paper outlines a feature generation and data transformation method, crucial for creating training batches. Each class is segmented into approximately 200 data points, representing about 10 seconds of data, with a step size of 20. This segmentation allows the training network to learn the features associated with each class based on labeled training data. The method employs a windowing technique to extract segments and labels, facilitating model training.

Data processing involves splitting the dataset into training, testing, and validation sets to optimize model performance. The paper emphasizes the importance of balancing training and testing set sizes to manage variance in parameter estimates and performance statistics. It recommends an 80:20 split for training and testing, followed by a further split of the training data into validation sets. The outlined steps include random sampling of training data to evaluate model performance and iterating through different training/testing ratios to achieve optimal results.
