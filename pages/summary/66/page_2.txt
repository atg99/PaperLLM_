The paper presents a detailed analysis of motion data collected from users engaged in different activities: jogging, sitting, and standing. The analysis focuses on the three axes of movement—x, y, and z—and highlights distinct patterns in the data.

For the jogging activity, the y-axis data shows notable fluctuations, particularly when compared to the walking class, with values ranging from 0 to 20 and an average gravitational pull of 9.8 m/s² for upright positions. The z-axis data for jogging exhibits greater fluctuations into the negative range, reflecting the increased force exerted during this activity.

In contrast, the sitting activity displays a stable x-axis plot, with values around 3, while the standing class is near zero. This difference is attributed to device orientation, enabling the network to distinguish between sitting and standing based on relative magnitudes. The y-axis data for sitting is slightly more stable than that of standing, but overall, distinguishing between these two states is challenging due to minimal motion.

The standing activity is characterized by a stable x-axis near zero and a y-axis at approximately 9.8 m/s², indicating the influence of gravitational forces. The z-axis data for standing is similar to walking but lacks the pronounced peaks associated with lateral device movement.

Overall, the findings suggest that while there are identifiable patterns in motion data across different activities, particularly between jogging and walking, distinguishing between sitting and standing remains complex due to their similar characteristics.
