Their advancements, including the C3k2 block and C2PSA module, contribute to superior performance compared to older models like YOLOv3u, which suffers from inefficiencies due to its outdated architecture.

In terms of computational efficiency, YOLOv10n stands out for its speed and low GFLOPs count, making it suitable for real-time applications despite being slightly slower than YOLO11 variants. YOLOv9 models, while accurate, exhibit slower inference times, limiting their practicality in time-sensitive scenarios. The size of the models also plays a crucial role, with smaller models like YOLOv9t performing well on limited datasets, highlighting the importance of model size relative to dataset complexity.

The analysis emphasizes the challenges YOLO models face in detecting small objects and handling varied rotations, suggesting the implementation of oriented bounding boxes in models like YOLO11 OBB and YOLOv8 OBB for improved accuracy.

Overall, the findings underscore the significance of dataset size and diversity in training, which greatly influences model performance. The evolution from YOLOv8 to YOLO11 marks a shift towards greater efficiency and accuracy, establishing YOLO11 as the preferred choice for modern detection tasks across diverse applications.
