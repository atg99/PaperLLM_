The paper presents an analysis of YOLO models' performance on a traffic signs dataset, specifically focusing on metrics such as mean Average Precision (mAP) and computational efficiency. Figures 8 and 9 illustrate the comparison of mAP50 and mAP50-95 scores, as well as precision versus recall based on model size, highlighting the trade-off between accuracy and inference times. 

Overall performance metrics indicate that YOLO11m stands out as the top performer, achieving a mAP50-95 score of 0.795, an inference time of 2.4ms, and a model size of 38.8Mb with a GFLOPs count of 67.9. Following closely are YOLO11l and YOLOv10m, with slightly lower mAP50-95 scores and varying inference times and sizes. The findings emphasize the effectiveness of the YOLO11 and YOLOv10 families in accurately detecting traffic signs while maintaining computational efficiency, outperforming other YOLO families in both accuracy and efficiency on this dataset. 

The paper also references results from the Africa Wildlife Dataset, suggesting a broader evaluation of YOLO models across different contexts.
