This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the evolution of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) algorithm, focusing particularly on YOLO11, the latest iteration. It evaluates the performance of pre-trained YOLO models across three diverse custom datasets, applying consistent hyperparameters to ensure a fair comparison. Key performance metrics analyzed include speed, efficiency, accuracy, and computational complexity, measured through GFLOPs and model size. The study also discusses the practical applications of different YOLO versions, highlighting their strengths and limitations.

The paper is structured into several sections: Section 2 reviews related work, Section 3 describes the datasets and experimental setup, Section 4 presents experimental results and discussions, and Section 5 concludes with insights derived from the research.

The paper references various studies that have examined YOLO's evolution and performance. While some reviews focus on earlier versions and specific applications, they often overlook the latest iterations, including YOLO11, and neglect essential performance metrics such as Recall and Precision, as well as computational efficiency beyond FPS. The current study distinguishes itself by offering a thorough comparison of YOLO11 against its predecessors, examining a range of performance metrics beyond the commonly used mAP and FPS, thereby providing valuable insights for both researchers and industry practitioners in selecting appropriate YOLO models for specific tasks.
