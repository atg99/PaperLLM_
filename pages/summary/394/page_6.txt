The paper discusses advancements in the YOLO (You Only Look Once) object detection architecture, focusing on YOLOv9, YOLOv10, and YOLO11. YOLOv9 introduces features like Programmable Gradient Information (PGI), which optimizes learning by selectively enhancing informative gradients, and GELAN (Gradient Enhanced Lightweight Architecture Network), improving computational efficiency and parameter utilization. The architecture also incorporates CSPNet and ELAN modules, enhancing gradient flow and learning efficiency.

YOLOv10 builds on these innovations with an improved CSP-Net backbone, PAN (Path Aggregation Network) for multiscale feature fusion, and a One-to-Many Head for generating multiple predictions, enhancing training accuracy. It eliminates Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) during inference to reduce latency. YOLOv10 also incorporates NMS-Free Training and optimizes various components for better efficiency and accuracy.

YOLO11, the latest version, features the C2PSA (Cross-Stage Partial with Self-Attention) module, which improves contextual understanding and accuracy, particularly for small objects. It replaces the C2f block with C3k2 to enhance efficiency without sacrificing performance.

The paper also details the hardware and software setup used for training 23 models across five YOLO versions, utilizing NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs and undersampling techniques for balanced datasets. This comparative analysis ensures consistent hyperparameters across the experiments.
