This paper presents a comprehensive benchmark study of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) models, focusing on YOLO11 and its predecessors from YOLOv3 onward. The evaluation utilizes three distinct datasets: Traffic Signs, African Wildlife, and Ships and Vessels, which pose different challenges in object detection. A range of performance metrics, including Precision, Recall, Mean Average Precision (mAP), Processing Time, GFLOPs count, and Model Size, are employed to assess each model's strengths and weaknesses.

The findings indicate that YOLOv9 excels in accuracy but has difficulties with small object detection and efficiency. In contrast, YOLOv10, while exhibiting lower accuracy due to architectural choices affecting overlapping object detection, excels in speed. The YOLO11 family, particularly YOLO11m, demonstrates superior overall performance, achieving mAP50-95 scores of 0.795, 0.81, and 0.325 across the datasets, along with an average inference time of 2.4 ms, a model size of 38.8 MB, and around 67.6 GFLOPs.

This research highlights the evolution of YOLO models and provides critical insights for both industry and academia, aiding in the selection of the most suitable YOLO algorithm for various applications and informing future developments in object detection. The paper underscores the advancements in YOLO's architecture and its implications for real-time object detection across diverse contexts.
