The paper analyzes the performance of various YOLO models on different datasets, specifically focusing on their accuracy, precision, recall, computational efficiency, and overall effectiveness in detecting small and tiny objects. 

In the Africa wildlife dataset, larger models like YOLO5ux and YOLO11x showed suboptimal accuracy, likely due to overfitting, while YOLOv10x performed well due to its modern architecture. The Ships and Vessels dataset revealed moderate effectiveness across the models, with mAP50-95 scores ranging from 0.273 to 0.327, indicating challenges in detecting smaller objects and those with varying rotations.

Accuracy disparities were observed, particularly with mAP50-95 versus mAP50, highlighting the difficulties YOLO models face at higher IoU thresholds. YOLO11x achieved the highest accuracy, with mAP50 and mAP50-95 scores that illustrate the robustness of the YOLO11 family. Conversely, models like YOLOv3u-tiny and YOLOv5n exhibited the lowest accuracy, suggesting limitations in their architectures.

In terms of precision and recall, YOLOv5ux led with a precision of 0.668 and recall of 0.555. Smaller models like YOLOv9m and YOLOv8m also performed well, while YOLO11 models struggled with false positives and lower metrics. Computational efficiency was highlighted, with YOLOv3u-tiny achieving the fastest processing time at 2 ms, while YOLOv9e was the slowest.

Overall, YOLO11s and YOLOv10s demonstrated strong accuracy with efficient processing times, whereas YOLOv3u, YOLOv8x, and YOLOv8l did not meet expectations despite their larger sizes. The findings suggest that the YOLO11 family significantly enhances detection capabilities for small objects while ensuring efficient processing, contrasting with the underperformance of YOLOv9 models in challenging datasets.
