The paper presents a comparative analysis of various models based on their precision, recall, computational efficiency, and overall performance using the Africa wildlife dataset. YOLOv10n, with a compact size of 6.55Mb, achieves high precision (0.932) and recall (0.908), outperforming larger models like YOLOv3u and YOLOv5ul, which have lower scores (0.91 and 0.88 for YOLOv3u, 0.916 and 0.881 for YOLOv5ul) despite their sizes of 204.86Mb and 106.85Mb, respectively. This discrepancy is attributed to potential overfitting issues in larger models.

In terms of computational efficiency, YOLOv10n and YOLOv8n are the fastest, processing images in 2ms and 1.8ms with GFLOPs counts of 8.2 and 19.1. Conversely, YOLOv9e is the slowest at 11.2ms with a GFLOPs count of 189.3. The analysis highlights a trade-off between model size and processing efficiency, where larger models require more time and hardware resources.

Overall, YOLOv9t and YOLOv9s demonstrate superior performance across all metrics, achieving high accuracy while maintaining smaller sizes, low GFLOPs, and short inference times, indicating the effectiveness of YOLOv9's design in balancing performance and efficiency.
