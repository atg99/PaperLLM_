In summary, the research indicates that trust in autonomous systems, including vehicles and airplanes, is a multifaceted issue influenced by factors such as previous experiences, cultural backgrounds, and system reliability. Payre et al. found that higher trust levels correlate with slower reaction times in emergency situations, which poses risks. Akash et al. demonstrated that trust diminishes in faulty scenarios but can recover with repeated positive experiences, revealing cultural differences in trust levels. Daziano et al. established a growing consumer interest in self-driving cars, as demonstrated by their willingness to pay more for various automation levels. Additionally, studies on autopilot systems show that failures, whether manual or automatic, significantly affect trust, with automatic failures leading to greater distrust. Strategies for improving trust in air traffic management systems were proposed, emphasizing the need for thorough training and gradual transitions to new technologies. Overall, these findings underscore the importance of addressing trust issues to enhance the acceptance and functionality of autonomous systems.
