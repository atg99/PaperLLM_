The paper reviews the existing literature on trust in autonomous systems, particularly focusing on self-driving cars. It highlights key findings from various studies that indicate how knowledge, past performance, and safety concerns influence user trust in these technologies. For instance, training and education can enhance trust, while knowledge of risks, such as driving in adverse conditions, may lead to skepticism. Surveys reveal that consumers generally appreciate the ease of use of self-driving cars but express concerns about safety, hacking, and legal implications.

The paper also addresses the impact of design similarities between self-driving cars and conventional vehicles on trust levels. Concerns about over-trust are noted, emphasizing the need for consumer education on when to regain manual control. Additionally, demographic factors, such as cultural backgrounds, affect trust perceptions, as seen in comparative studies between Indian and American subjects regarding autonomous aircraft.

The conclusion underscores the necessity for further research to address existing gaps in understanding trust in automation, particularly the translation of findings into computational trust models that machines can comprehend. The paper suggests that new methodologies can pave the way for more effective autonomous systems.
