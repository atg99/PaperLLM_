The paper by Dassonville et al. explores the concept of trust within teleoperation systems, wherein human operators control machines remotely. The authors identify three key components of such systems: the master universe (the operator's environment), the slave universe (the environment where the machine operates), and the space in between (data transmission and control systems). Through a simulated experiment, the authors found that while literature students exhibited greater self-confidence in operating the system, both literature and scientific students displayed similar levels of trust in the system.

Moray and Idnagaekri analyze the relationship between trust in autonomous systems and the degree of human supervision. Their findings indicate that increased reliance on these systems can lead to reduced monitoring, which may result in malpractice when trust is misplaced. Another study by Dzindolet et al. examines how different malfunction types in autonomous systems affect trust, revealing a significant decline in trust following continuous failures but not after discrete malfunctions.

Merritt emphasizes the importance of individual differences in human behaviors in human-automation interactions. Through an X-ray screening simulation, the study shows that personal characteristics can influence trust levels in autonomous systems, suggesting these factors should be accounted for in future trust analyses.

In the context of human-robot interaction, Murphy et al. investigate the role of trust in autonomous rescue robots deployed in crisis situations. They conclude that victim trust is essential for the robots to effectively aid in rescue operations. Additionally, they highlight that lower failure rates in robotic systems can enhance reliability and, consequently, trust.

Parasuraman and Miller delve into the impact of etiquette on trust in human-robot interactions, arguing that respect and prescribed behaviors significantly influence perceptions of autonomous robots. Their experiments demonstrate that etiquette plays a crucial role in fostering trust and reliability in these systems.
