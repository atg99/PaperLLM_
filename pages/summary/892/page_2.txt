The paper presents a novel interpretation of neural style transfer by framing it as an optimal transport problem, which seeks to find a transformation that aligns two high-dimensional distributions. The authors argue that traditional methods, such as WCT and AdaIN, primarily focus on matching covariance matrices without adequately preserving image structure or considering content loss. 

By treating the features of content and style images as samples from Multivariate Gaussian distributions, the authors derive a closed-form solution called Optimal Style Transfer (OST). This solution not only aligns the style but also minimizes the difference between the transformed features and the original content features, thereby enhancing structural preservation compared to existing methods.

The contributions of the paper include:
1. A theoretical interpretation of neural style transfer as an optimal transport problem, relating it to previous methods like AdaIN and WCT.
2. The introduction of OST, a unique closed-form solution that incorporates content loss into the style transfer process.
3. Demonstrating that OST yields visually appealing results while maintaining better structural integrity of the content images.

The paper also reviews related works, categorizing them into image optimization and model optimization approaches, as well as discussing universal style transfer methods that do not require training on specific style images. The authors emphasize the efficiency of their approach compared to traditional back-propagation methods, which tend to be time-consuming.
