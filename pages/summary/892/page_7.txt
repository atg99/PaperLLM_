The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various style transfer methods, including Gatys, Patch Swap, AdaIN, AdaIN+, WCT, and the authors' proposed method. The analysis focuses on both qualitative and quantitative results, primarily through user studies and loss evaluations.

The user study involved 50 testers assessing stylized images generated from 16 content images and 35 style images, resulting in a total of 560 images. The scoring system ranged from 1 (worst) to 5 (best), and the results indicated that the proposed method consistently achieved the highest average score of 3.07, surpassing all other methods.

In addition to user feedback, the paper evaluates content and style losses based on the metrics established by Gatys. The results, summarized in Table 2, show that the proposed method maintains lower content loss and comparable style loss when contrasted with WCT, while other methods exhibited higher style losses. This suggests that while the proposed method preserves content effectively, it also adheres to style requirements.

Figures in the paper illustrate various aspects of the methods, including the effectiveness of different whitening techniques and the impact of linear interpolation on preserving structure in stylized images. The authors also demonstrate the versatility of their method through additional results and its integration with semantic style transfer.

However, the paper acknowledges limitations, particularly in video style transfer, where frame-by-frame jittering persists. This issue is attributed to both feature transformation and inherent information loss from deep encoder networks.

Overall, the findings confirm that the proposed method enhances the quality of style transfer, achieving better visual results while addressing some of the limitations present in existing techniques.
