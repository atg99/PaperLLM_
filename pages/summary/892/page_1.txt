This paper presents a novel approach to universal style transfer, which aims to minimize losses in feature space without relying on predefined styles. The authors propose a theoretical framework that interprets the style transfer process as an optimal transport problem. They demonstrate how their formulation relates to existing methods like Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN) and Whitening and Coloring Transform (WCT). 

The authors introduce a closed-form solution named Optimal Style Transfer (OST), which incorporates content loss as defined by Gatys. This method is shown to preserve better structural integrity and yield visually appealing results. The OST approach is characterized as simple yet effective, with the authors providing both quantitative and qualitative evidence of its advantages over existing techniques.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of a theoretical analysis of feature transformation in style transfer, hoping to inspire future research in the field. The authors utilize a multi-scale encoder-feature transform-decoder framework, employing different layers of the VGG network for encoding and training decoders to convert features back to images. Overall, the work contributes to the understanding and application of universal style transfer, offering a new solution that enhances prior methods. The code for the proposed method is made available for public access.
