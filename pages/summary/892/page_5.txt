In the experimental results, the authors compare their method against several established techniques, including Gatys, Patch Swap, AdaIN, AdaIN+, and WCT, both qualitatively and quantitatively. The processing speeds of these methods are highlighted in Table 1, demonstrating that their approach is significantly faster than Gatys and Patch Swap, while still maintaining competitive performance compared to AdaIN, AdaIN+, and WCT.

Qualitative comparisons reveal that their method outperforms Gatys, which, while foundational, struggles with achieving stylization due to its iterative nature. In contrast, their closed-form solution provides better stylization while preserving content structures. Similarly, when compared to Patch Swap, their method proves to be more effective in achieving stylization, as Patch Swap tends to merely reconstruct features rather than transfer style effectively.

The authors also find that AdaIN and AdaIN+ fall short in producing visually pleasing results because they assume independence among CNN feature channels, neglecting their correlations. In contrast, their approach acknowledges these correlations, leading to superior outcomes.

When comparing with WCT, the authors note that while WCT's ZCA whitening method can preserve content structure, it lacks constraints on the final transformed feature. Their method addresses this by minimizing the difference between the content feature and the transformed feature, resulting in better structural preservation.

Finally, the authors discuss the flexibility of their method, allowing for adjustments in the degree of style transfer through a linear combination of the original content and transformed features. Overall, their approach demonstrates significant advancements in the field of neural style transfer, providing both efficiency and quality in the resulting images.
