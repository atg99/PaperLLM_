The paper discusses a novel approach to universal style transfer (OST) using a pipeline that involves feature extraction, transformation, and image reconstruction. The process begins by extracting features from both content and style images using a pre-trained encoder, followed by a feature transformation to generate stylized features. The decoder then reconstructs the stylized features back into an image. While previous methods like AdaIN and WCT have been utilized for style transfer, they often fall short in achieving visually pleasing results due to their limitations in handling channel correlations.

The authors propose to reformulate neural style transfer as an optimal transport problem, modeling the features of content and style images as samples from multivariate Gaussian distributions. They establish a transformation matrix that aligns the features of the content and style distributions while considering their covariance matrices. This leads to a more effective way of transforming features, as it addresses the shortcomings of existing methods by ensuring that the transformation satisfies necessary conditions for matching the covariance matrices of the two distributions.

The paper highlights that while AdaIN focuses on matching means and ignores channel correlations, WCT achieves a better match by considering the covariance structure. The proposed method aims to enhance the theoretical understanding of feature transformations in style transfer, potentially leading to improved visual results.
