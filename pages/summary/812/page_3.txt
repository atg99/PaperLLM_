This paper discusses a novel approach for channel pruning in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) called Variational CNN Pruning, which eliminates the need for a retraining stage typically required by existing pruning methods. By employing a variational Bayesian framework, the method reformulates the Batch Normalization (BN) layer, extending its scale factor to include a shift term, termed channel saliency. This allows for the estimation of the distribution of channel saliency through variational inference, facilitating the pruning of redundant channels based on this distribution.

The authors highlight two significant issues with traditional BN usage: the independence of the scale and shift parameters, which can lead to inappropriate channel pruning decisions, and the dynamic nature of these parameters during training. To address this, they propose a revised formulation of the BN layer that combines the scale and shift terms, thereby allowing the channel saliency to be directly utilized for pruning decisions.

The approach leverages variational inference to approximate the posterior distribution of channel saliency, making the method more stable and interpretable. This is achieved by defining a parameterized distribution to approximate the true posterior, thus avoiding computationally intractable integrals. Overall, Variational CNN Pruning presents an innovative solution to enhance efficiency in CNNs while maintaining performance.
