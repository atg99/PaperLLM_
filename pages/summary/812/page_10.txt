The referenced papers focus on various techniques for compressing deep neural networks to enhance efficiency and performance. Key contributions include filter level pruning methods for network compression, as highlighted by Luo et al. in their work on Thinet, and practical guidelines for efficient CNN architecture design presented by Ma et al. in ShuffleNet V2. Variational dropout techniques discussed by Molchanov et al. and structured Bayesian pruning methods by Neklyudov et al. further emphasize the importance of sparsity in network design.

Several studies, such as those by Tung and Mori on in-parallel pruning-quantization and by Wang et al. on tensor ring nets, explore innovative approaches to network compression through quantization and structured sparsity. The significance of neuron importance score propagation for pruning networks is presented by Yu et al., while techniques for clustering convolutional kernels, as shown by Son et al., contribute to reducing computational demands.

Other notable contributions include Rastegari et al.'s work on binary convolutional networks and Peng et al.'s filter group approximation method, both of which aim to facilitate extreme network compression. Overall, these studies collectively advance the field of deep learning by providing essential methodologies for optimizing neural network architectures while maintaining performance.
