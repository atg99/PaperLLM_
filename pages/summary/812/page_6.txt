This paper investigates the impact of a variational pruning method on deep learning models, specifically focusing on VGG-16, DenseNet-40, and various ResNet architectures. The results are presented in two tables summarizing model accuracy, pruned channels, parameters, and FLOPs on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets.

In Table 1, the performance of different ResNet architectures on CIFAR-10 is detailed. The base models demonstrate high accuracy, with VGG-16 at 93.25% and ResNet-164 at 93.58%. After applying pruning, the accuracy slightly decreases for most models, with VGG-16 dropping to 93.18% while achieving a significant reduction in parameters (62%) and FLOPs (39.10%). The pruning method is particularly effective for ResNet-164, which sees a 74% reduction in channels while maintaining 93.16% accuracy.

Table 2 highlights results on CIFAR-100, showing similar trends. Although VGG-16's accuracy improves post-pruning, DenseNet-40 experiences a decline from 74.64% to 72.19%. ResNet-164 also shows a decrease in accuracy from 75.56% to 73.76%, but with a notable reduction in parameters and FLOPs.

The findings suggest that the proposed variational pruning method effectively reduces model complexity while retaining competitive accuracy, particularly in deeper models. The results indicate that as model depth increases, the benefits of pruning become more pronounced, allowing for substantial reductions in redundancy without significantly compromising performance.
