This paper addresses significant challenges in deep neural network pruning, particularly focusing on the inefficiencies and instabilities of existing methods. Current approaches often require multiple iterations of pruning and retraining, which are time-consuming and impractical. Additionally, the layer-alternative parameter optimization can lead to instability, causing drastic changes in hidden layer parameters, resulting in arbitrary channel removals that are not interpretable and can degrade model performance.

To overcome these issues, the authors propose a novel method called variational CNN pruning, which redefines the channel pruning problem within a Bayesian probabilistic learning framework. This method aims to provide a more compact, stable, interpretable, and flexible compression of neural networks. By operating directly on a redefined scaling factor in Batch Normalization without introducing extra parameters, the approach simplifies integration into existing deep learning frameworks. The authors extend the scaling factor to include a bias term, termed channel saliency, and treat it as a random variable to model its probabilistic distribution.

The proposed method allows for the pruning of unimportant channels based on this distribution, utilizing stochastic variational inference to estimate the channel saliency distribution. The benefits of this Bayesian framework include no need for additional parameters, elimination of retraining steps for improved computational efficiency, and enhanced stability and interpretability of the optimization process. Experimental results demonstrate that the method can prune up to 74% of channels with minimal accuracy loss on CIFAR-10 and achieve better performance than the baseline while removing about 40% of channels in ResNet-50 on ImageNet.

The paper also reviews related work in the field, categorizing existing methods into tensor decomposition, network quantization, non-structured pruning, and structured pruning. While various techniques have been proposed to enhance computational efficiency and reduce model size, many involve complex operations or lead to accuracy loss. The authors position their approach as a more efficient and effective alternative to existing pruning methods.
