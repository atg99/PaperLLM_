The paper discusses a method for addressing unsolvable inference problems by transforming them into a tractable optimization problem. The primary goal is to estimate the posterior distribution by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between an approximate posterior distribution \( q_\phi(\gamma) \) and the true posterior \( p(\gamma|D) \). This minimization is equivalent to maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), which consists of an expected log-likelihood term \( L_D(\phi) \) and a KL divergence term.

The expected log-likelihood serves as a reconstruction term aimed at maximizing the model's predictive probability, while the KL divergence acts as a regularization term that imposes a sparsity penalty on the channel saliency \( \gamma \). By optimizing these two terms, the model can effectively balance performance and compression.

To compute the gradient of the expected log-likelihood, the Reparametrization Trick is introduced, allowing for unbiased and differentiable estimation through a Monte Carlo approach. The model parameters, including the neural network weights \( w \), are optimized in an end-to-end manner to obtain an approximate distribution of channel saliency and the weights.

To ensure sparsity in the approximate distribution \( q_\phi(\gamma) \), a fully factorized Gaussian distribution is utilized, with a prior distribution also modeled as a Gaussian with a mean of zero. This choice encourages the channel saliency parameters toward zero, facilitating the pruning of ineffective channels. The KL divergence can then be computed tractably, allowing for effective optimization.

Key reasons for the chosen prior distribution include its sparsity-inducing properties, the lack of distribution gap between the approximate and prior distributions, and the tractability of the KL divergence calculation. The paper concludes that while other sparsity-inducing distributions can be considered, they may complicate the calculation of KL divergence due to intractable integrals.
