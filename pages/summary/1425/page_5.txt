This paper presents a novel approach to visual question answering (VQA) that involves reading text from images, specifically focusing on book covers. The study evaluates the performance of various methods, particularly highlighting the effectiveness of the BLOCK+CNN+W2V model. The authors provide examples of successful and failed attempts to answer questions about book titles, authors, and publication years, demonstrating the challenges posed by diverse text layouts, font styles, and question variations.

The results indicate that while traditional features like BLOCK and CNN alone are insufficient, combining these with word embeddings significantly improves accuracy, particularly for genre-related questions. The study also analyzes the performance of different OCR techniques, noting that TextSpotter outperforms others in recognizing book titles and author names.

Error analysis reveals that failures often arise from complex cover designs and text recognition difficulties, emphasizing the need for better handling of cluttered backgrounds and varied font styles. The paper concludes by advocating for further research in this area, as the dataset created serves as a valuable resource for advancing VQA methodologies.
