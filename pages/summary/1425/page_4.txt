The paper presents a novel approach to answering visual questions by reading text in images, focusing on a structured methodology to process and analyze visual data. The baseline approach involves detecting text blocks from images, representing them using a five-dimensional vector based on their coordinates, and employing Optical Character Recognition (OCR) engines—specifically Tesseract, CRNN, and VGG deep text spotter—to extract textual information. Performance is evaluated through recall metrics for recognizing book titles and author names, with results indicating that the text spotter outperforms the other engines. 

Following OCR, Named Entity Recognition (NER) is applied to classify identified entities in the text, such as person names and publication years. This involves tagging the text blocks with relevant information based on detected entities, including editions when applicable.

The paper further introduces a trainable OCR-Visual Question Answering (OCR-VQA) model. It utilizes bidirectional long short term memory (BLSTM) for question representation and VGG-16 pretrained CNN features for image representation. The model integrates multiple feature types, including word embeddings and NER tags, into a composite vector, which is processed through a fully connected feedforward network to predict various outputs related to the visual questions posed.

The results section promises a detailed analysis of the approach's performance on a dataset known as OCR-VQA –200K, utilizing different feature sets to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed model.
