This paper investigates the linguistic feature encoding capabilities of BERT across its layers using probing tasks. The tasks are categorized into surface, syntactic, and semantic features, assessing aspects such as sentence length, word presence, syntactic tree depth, and subject-verb agreement. The methodology involves evaluating both trained and untrained versions of BERT to understand the hierarchy of linguistic signals.

Results indicate that BERT effectively encodes surface information in its lower layers, syntactic information in the middle layers, and semantic information in the upper layers. Notably, the untrained model outperformed the trained version in predicting sentence length, suggesting that training may shift the model's focus from basic surface features to more complex structures.

Additionally, the study extends previous research on subject-verb agreement by analyzing each BERT layer's performance in relation to the number of intervening nouns (attractors). The findings highlight BERT's capability to learn syntactic structures, reinforcing its effectiveness in linguistic tasks. Overall, the paper contributes to our understanding of how BERT processes and represents various linguistic features across its architecture.
