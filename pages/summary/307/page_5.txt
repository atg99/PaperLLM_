The paper investigates the compositional structure of representations learned by BERT, focusing on how different role schemes influence the model's performance on natural language processing tasks. Using Tensor Product Decomposition Networks (TPDN), the authors analyze how well these networks can approximate BERT's representations based on various role schemes. These schemes include left-to-right and right-to-left indices, bag-of-words, syntactic tree positions, and random binary trees.

Results indicate that BERT's middle layers are effective at capturing syntactic features, with specific attention given to layer #8, which manages long-distance dependencies better than the preceding layer. The Mean Squared Error (MSE) results show that a tree-based role scheme best approximates BERT's representations across most layers, suggesting that BERT implicitly encodes tree-like structures despite its reliance on attention mechanisms.

The study also includes a case analysis of dependency trees derived from self-attention weights, revealing that BERT accurately captures determiner-noun and subject-verb dependencies. The findings emphasize the importance of deeper layers in BERT for competitive performance in NLP tasks and underscore its ability to model complex syntactic relationships.
