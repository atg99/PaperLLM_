The paper investigates the structural understanding of language that BERT, a language representation model, acquires through its training. BERT, which stands for Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers, has shown exceptional performance in various language understanding benchmarks, prompting the authors to explore the nature of the representations learned at different layers of the model.

The authors conduct a series of experiments to demonstrate that BERT captures language structure in a hierarchical manner. They find that the lower layers of BERT primarily encode phrase-level information, while the intermediate layers incorporate syntactic features, and the higher layers focus on semantic features. Notably, the study highlights that BERT relies on deeper layers to effectively manage long-distance dependencies, such as subject-verb agreement.

Additionally, the authors use probing tasks to further analyze BERT's learned representations, revealing that BERT's structure can be seen as compositional, resembling classical tree-like structures commonly found in linguistic theory. The findings not only enhance the understanding of BERT's success but also provide insights into its limitations, contributing to the broader field of neural network interpretability in natural language processing.
