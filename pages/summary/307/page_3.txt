The paper investigates the linguistic structures learned by BERT's representations, focusing on the span representations from different layers of the model. The authors utilize the bert-base-uncased variant of BERT, which contains 12 layers, each with a hidden size of 768 and 12 attention heads. They analyze the activation of the first input token at each layer to derive BERT representations.

The study builds on prior work by Peters et al. (2018), which demonstrated that LSTM-based models can capture phrase-level information. The authors question whether BERT, trained with a different objective, retains similar capabilities and whether this information is distributed across multiple layers. They compute span representations through a concatenation of the first and last hidden vectors from each layer, along with their element-wise product and difference.

Using t-SNE for visualization, they find that BERT captures phrase-level information predominantly in the lower layers, with this information becoming less distinct in higher layers. Their k-means clustering analysis corroborates this observation, revealing that lower layers better encode phrasal information, as indicated by the Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) metric results.

Additionally, the paper discusses probing tasks, which serve to uncover the linguistic features encoded in neural models. By setting up classification tasks to predict linguistic phenomena based on features from the model's output, the authors aim to further elucidate the linguistic capabilities of BERT's representations.
