The paper "What does BERT learn about the structure of language?" by Ganesh Jawahar, Benoît Sagot, and Djamé Seddah explores the linguistic knowledge acquired by the BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) model during its training. The authors investigate how BERT captures various aspects of language structure, including syntax and semantics, by analyzing its internal representations.

Through a series of experiments, the authors demonstrate that BERT effectively learns to represent grammatical structures, such as part-of-speech and syntactic dependencies. They also show that BERT's representations can distinguish between different linguistic phenomena, indicating its ability to grasp complex language rules.

The findings suggest that BERT not only excels in specific language tasks but also possesses a robust understanding of the underlying structure of language, which contributes to its performance in various natural language processing applications. The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding these learned representations to enhance the development of future language models.
