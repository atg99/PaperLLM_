In the paper by Manchev and Spratling, the authors present a comparison between two learning algorithms, Target Propagation Through Time (TPTT) and Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT), focusing on their respective training costs for synthetic problems. The training cost is averaged over 100 iterations, and the results demonstrate that TPTT converges significantly faster than BPTT, as illustrated in Figure 4.

Furthermore, the authors build on the findings of Lillicrap et al. (2016), who showed that random feedback alignment can achieve comparable synaptic weight updates to those produced by backpropagation after a few training epochs. The current analysis extends this concept to examine the transition matrix updates for TPTT and BPTT, specifically focusing on the updates to the recurrent weight matrix Whh. The authors compute the update term for the TPTT-trained network using a defined equation and simultaneously calculate an update term through backpropagation, thereby enabling a direct comparison of the two approaches. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the efficiency of TPTT in terms of convergence speed and provides a detailed analysis of weight updates and layerwise errors in the context of recurrent neural networks.
