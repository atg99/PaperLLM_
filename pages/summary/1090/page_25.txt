This paper explores the implementation of target propagation (TP) in recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on the DTP modification and its impact on accuracy. The findings indicate that target propagation tends to outperform backpropagation through time (BPTT) in simple RNNs, although the TPTT network does not match the performance of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks. The authors suggest that this discrepancy may be attributed to the inherent advantages of the LSTM architecture, implying that a direct comparison would necessitate an LSTM model trained with target propagation.

The paper references previous work by Wiseman et al. (2017), noting TPTT's superior performance in four out of five tested problems. The authors propose future investigations into TPTT's efficacy in language modeling tasks, including potential modifications for next character prediction, and express interest in the performance of TPTT-trained LSTM networks in these contexts.

An alternative update rule is introduced to differentiate between the original TPTT and the TPTT hx configuration, where both hidden and input weight matrices are updated using local targets. Experimental results reveal that the TPTT hx network generally outperforms the original TPTT, except in the adding problem, suggesting that avoiding the chain rule through hidden weights enhances stability and convergence in deeper networks.

Despite improvements, the adding problem remains challenging for the TPTT hx network, which, even with manual adjustments to learning rates, fails to achieve results on par with backpropagation. Overall, the study emphasizes the potential of target propagation in RNNs while acknowledging the need for further research to fully exploit its capabilities.
