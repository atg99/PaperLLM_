The paper discusses the application of Target Propagation Through Time (TPTT) in training Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), specifically comparing it to Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). The training utilized Nesterov's Accelerated Gradient with a momentum of 0.9, starting with short sequences of 10 time steps and progressively increasing the sequence length. However, the BPTT-trained Simple Recurrent Network (SRN) struggled with longer sequences, failing to solve most problems, including the 3-bit Temporal Order Problem, which it only managed at the initial sequence length.

To address the issues of vanishing gradients, the study tested an SRN variant with orthogonally initialized weight matrices, which help preserve gradient norms during backpropagation. The networks underwent extensive training, processing 2 million examples over 100,000 iterations. The performance was evaluated using a cross-entropy cost function for classification tasks and Mean Squared Error (MSE) for regression tasks, with specific success criteria defined for each.

Results were recorded every 100 iterations on a validation set, with a success criterion established at a validation error below 0.0001. The paper compares the performance of BPTT and TPTT networks, providing details on hyperparameter optimization for each method and the performance metrics achieved. The findings indicate the effectiveness of TPTT in handling longer sequences, contrasting with the limitations observed in BPTT training.
