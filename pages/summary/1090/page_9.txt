In the paper "Target Propagation in Recurrent Neural Networks," the authors discuss the limitations of backpropagation through time (BPTT) in learning from recurrent networks, particularly when dealing with discrete error signals that exhibit zero gradients almost everywhere. This situation makes it difficult for BPTT to effectively train networks, while target propagation through time (TPTT) can manage these discrete non-linearities by allowing discrete transmissions between units. This method enhances the biological plausibility of models by employing spike coding rather than firing rate coding, which aligns more closely with biological processes.

The authors highlight that TPTT does not require synchronized forward and feedback phases, enabling more flexible computational dependencies and potential parallel execution. They compare their findings to previous work by Wiseman et al. (2017), which reported disappointing results for target propagation in recurrent settings. The current paper, however, asserts that their TPTT approach generally outperforms BPTT in recurrent network training.

Additionally, the authors draw parallels between TPTT and synthetic gradients, where updates are made without relying strictly on a global error signal, allowing for asynchronous updates within the network. This results in a Decoupled Neural Interfaces (DNI) architecture, which provides significant flexibility in training by reducing the constraints of sequential error propagation.

Overall, the paper presents a compelling argument for the advantages of target propagation in recurrent neural networks, emphasizing its potential for improved learning and biological relevance compared to traditional backpropagation methods.
