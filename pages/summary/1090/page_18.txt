In their study, Manchev and Spratling investigate the effects of Gaussian noise on the adding problem within a network framework, specifically examining a modified version of the Temporal Progressive Training Technique (TPTT). The parameters for the experiment were set to specific values, and the network was run for a substantial number of iterations, allowing for the analysis of the cost function over time.

The authors highlight that the dependency between the update phases G(·) and F(·) is not as rigid as previously thought. This assertion was supported by experiments that manipulated the probability of executing each update phase, revealing that the network's performance on synthetic problems was minimally affected by the strict alternation of phases. The results indicated that while precise synchronisation of updates leads to a slight advantage, the two phases can operate independently, suggesting potential for parallel computation in TPTT implementations.

Additionally, the authors reference previous research indicating that recurrent networks can generalise beyond the training example lengths, further enriching the understanding of network capabilities in handling temporal tasks. This generalisation ability is significant for improving network performance in various applications.
