In the paper by Manchev and Spratling, the authors discuss the limitations of backpropagation through time (BPTT) in the context of neural networks, particularly concerning biological plausibility. They argue that BPTT requires neurons to have a symmetric backward connectivity pattern, which is considered impractical in biological systems. This is highlighted by the fact that credit assignment is not an issue for the Simple Recurrent Network (RNN) model since it operates with a single neuron and consistent synapses across time steps. However, more complex RNN architectures, such as higher-order RNNs and recurrent multilayer perceptrons, do face credit assignment challenges.

The authors also raise concerns about the biological feasibility of BPTT due to its reliance on complex temporal indexing and the necessity for the network to store and match time-indexed error signals and activity patterns. Additionally, the continuous value computations in backpropagation and the precise timing of forward and backward passes are seen as unlikely to occur in biological neural networks.

To address these issues, the authors propose a new model called target propagation through time (TPTT). This model focuses on setting local targets for neuron activations rather than computing gradients at each time step. The method aims to optimize synaptic weights locally by determining targets that would lead to a decrease in global error. The paper outlines how to set these local targets, particularly emphasizing the simplicity of setting targets for the final time step and the need for an inverse function to establish targets for earlier time steps. Overall, the proposed TPTT approach seeks to offer a more biologically plausible alternative to traditional backpropagation methods in recurrent networks.
