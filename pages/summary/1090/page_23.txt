This paper introduces Target Propagation Through Time (TPTT), a novel algorithm for training recurrent neural networks that utilizes a difference target propagation approach. TPTT generally outperforms traditional backpropagation (BPTT) in terms of training efficiency and is particularly effective in addressing the vanishing and exploding gradient problems, making it advantageous for deeper networks. The authors demonstrate that TPTT not only improves convergence speed but also enhances performance over longer sequences by minimizing the distance between hidden layer outputs and local targets, thus avoiding the chain rule through recurrent weights.

The paper argues that TPTT is more biologically plausible than BPTT, as its feedback connections resemble the structure of neurons with distinct dendritic functions. This biological motivation is supported by evidence from previous studies suggesting that incorporating neurobiological mechanisms can enhance computational capabilities. Additionally, TPTT is better suited for handling discrete error signals, suggesting potential for adaptation in networks with discretized layers.

The authors also address the use of orthogonal initialization, which was chosen to maintain architectural consistency between BPTT and TPTT during experiments. They raise the question of how orthogonal initialization relates to biological plausibility, noting a lack of literature on this topic. They suggest that careful initialization could serve as a form of pre-training, potentially aligning weights before learning begins.

Overall, the findings indicate that TPTT provides significant advantages in training recurrent neural networks, and the authors highlight several avenues for future research, including the application of TPTT to networks with discrete layers and further exploration of initialization strategies.
