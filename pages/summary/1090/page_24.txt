Manchev and Spratling explore the effects of different initialization techniques in training networks, specifically comparing identity initialization to orthogonal initialization in the context of target propagation through time (TPTT). They note that while identity initialization may yield better performance on tasks like addition, a deeper examination of initialization methods and the biological relevance of orthogonal initialization is warranted for future studies.

Jaderberg et al. (2017) characterize target propagation as a restrictive training method due to its reliance on sequentially generated local targets. They propose a solution involving the unrolling of a Simple Recurrent Network (SRN) over a defined number of time steps, allowing the network to operate as two separate computational graphs. This configuration enables asynchronous operation, potentially allowing for parallel training of TPTT networks, which could enhance training efficiency.

Further, the authors discuss the possibility of removing the approximation of the G(·) phase in favor of random feedback alignment, which could simplify training and provide insights into the differences between plastic and fixed feedback synaptic weights. They reference Lee et al. (2015), who found that injecting random noise can stabilize learning, particularly in addition tasks, while Ororbia et al. (2018) highlight challenges with difference target propagation in selecting effective local targets, leading to performance issues.
