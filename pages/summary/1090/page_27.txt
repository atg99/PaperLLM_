The paper discusses the effectiveness of Target Propagation (TP) in Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) compared to Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). It presents experimental results on various problems, including temporal order, 3-bit temporal order, addition, and random permutation tasks, highlighting the performance and convergence times of TP and BPTT under different settings.

Figure A1 illustrates the impact of Gaussian noise on the addition problem, while the parameters for the network are specified as i=0.2, f=0.01, g=0.05, with the network running for 100,000 iterations. The cost function values are averaged over 100 iterations.

Table A3 summarizes the convergence times needed for BPTT and TP training methods across different problems. It compares the best-performing BPTT models with the TP models using common and best hyperparameters. Notably, the results show that BPTT generally requires more iterations for convergence compared to TP, indicating TP's potential efficiency in training RNNs.

Overall, the findings suggest that TP could be a viable alternative to BPTT, particularly in scenarios involving noise and varying problem complexities. The paper emphasizes the importance of hyperparameter tuning in achieving optimal performance in RNN training.
