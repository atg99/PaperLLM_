The paper investigates the effectiveness of Target Propagation in Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) by comparing it to Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) in various temporal order problems. Key findings are presented in two figures and a table. 

Figure 8 illustrates the impact of a randomized transition between updating the parameters of two networks, G(·) and F(·), on the performance of RNNs in temporal order tasks with a specified maximum time step (tmax). Results show that constant alternation between the two phases leads to different cost changes, affecting the networks' accuracy.

Table 4 presents the accuracy of BPTT and Target Propagation Through Time (TPTT) networks on multiple tasks, with the TPTT network generally outperforming BPTT in temporal order problems. For instance, the TPTT network achieved an accuracy of 45.76% on the temporal order problem compared to 26.67% for BPTT. In the 3-bit temporal order task, TPTT reached 28.36% while BPTT only achieved 13.65%. Notably, the TPTT network demonstrated a perfect accuracy of 100% on the random permutation task.

The experiments involved training both TPTT and BPTT networks on synthetic problems with sequences of length 100 and testing their performance on longer sequences of length 200. The findings suggest that TPTT may offer better generalization capabilities compared to BPTT, particularly in tasks requiring temporal order recognition.
