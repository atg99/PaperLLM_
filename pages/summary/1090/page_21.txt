The paper discusses the performance of a Target Propagation Through Time (TPTT) trained Simple Recurrent Neural Network (SRNN) on the MNIST sequence of pixels problem, using 1,000 test images selected from the dataset. The training was conducted over a maximum of 375,000 iterations, with a hyperparameter space for the learning rates constrained to specific values. The optimal learning rates were found to be 10^7 for input, 10^2 for feedback, and 10^8 for output.

Results indicated that the TPTT-trained SRNN outperforms traditional Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) trained SRNNs using ReLU and tanh activations. However, it did not achieve the same level of performance as LSTM or Identity Recurrent Neural Networks (IRNN) when the number of neurons in the hidden layer was the same. The paper references previous findings suggesting that larger networks trained with backpropagation do not typically perform worse due to the method's tendency to disregard excess parameters, raising concerns about TPTT's performance with an increasing number of hidden neurons. Performance data for the comparison networks was derived from Le et al. (2015), with maximal accuracy and changes over time presented in accompanying tables and figures.
