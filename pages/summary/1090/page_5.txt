The paper discusses a method for target propagation in recurrent neural networks (RNNs), focusing on optimizing the hidden state over time. The central idea is to define a function G that approximates the inverse of the RNN's activation function F, allowing for the setting of local targets to guide the optimization process. 

The model employs a linearly corrected formula for target propagation, influenced by the work of Lee et al. (2015). This approach stabilizes the optimization by ensuring that as the predicted hidden state approaches the target, the current hidden state also converges towards the predicted target. The formula for the corrected target is defined as ^ht, which is a combination of the current hidden state ht and the outputs of the function G.

The configuration of G is specified as a function that takes the inputs xt+1 and ht+1, producing an output that incorporates learned weights and bias terms. This leads to the final equation for the upstream targets, which integrates the approximated inverse function into the target propagation framework. 

Overall, the proposed method enhances the stability and performance of RNNs by refining the target setting process through local optimization techniques.
