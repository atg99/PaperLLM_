In their analysis, Manchev and Spratling present Figure 6, which illustrates the changes in layer-specific costs for three different TPTT-trained networks tackling various problems: the addition problem, the permutation problem, and the 3-bit temporal order problem, all for T=30. Each row of the figure consists of three plots: the first shows the cost related to the update rule for Vhhandch, the second corresponds to the cost for learning Whhandbh, and the third depicts the change in global error E. 

The findings indicate that the networks generally succeed in approximating the inverse effectively. However, a notable trend is observed where the difference between local targets and feedforward activations diminishes at the lower network levels, which may hinder the learning capacity of the network, akin to the vanishing gradient problem. Despite this challenge, TPTT-trained networks demonstrate superior performance over their BPTT-trained counterparts in three out of the four synthetic problems analyzed, suggesting a more robust learning capability in the context of TPTT.
