In contrast, the TPTT model proposed by Manchev and Spratling utilizes real-valued feedback connections, allowing for more nuanced adjustments to synaptic weights. This mechanism facilitates the assignment of credit or blame based on locally computed errors, driving synapses towards long-term potentiation or depression without relying on the non-local transmission of information characteristic of traditional backpropagation methods. 

While some argue that the local target-setting process in TPTT resembles weight transport, it is posited that the adaptability of feedback connections in the human brain supports the model's biological plausibility. Evidence suggests that adaptable feedback connections outperform random feedback alignment techniques, highlighting the potential advantages of the TPTT framework. 

However, the weight transport resolution offered by TPTT does not extend to simple recurrent neural networks (SRNNs), as they represent a temporally indexed version of a single neuron. Nonetheless, target propagation may enhance biological plausibility in recurrent networks with long-range connections, akin to the architecture of the human visual system, which relies on complex recurrent networks for information processing. 

The discussion also highlights that within-group weight transport issues arise when employing recurrent connections in a dynamic visual signal processing context. Although target propagation can mitigate these concerns, the challenge of handling complex temporal indexing remains, limiting TPTT's current applicability in this domain. Additionally, the reliance on continuous values in TPTT contrasts with the binary weight adjustments seen in conventional backpropagation, which may pose further challenges in aligning the model with existing frameworks.
