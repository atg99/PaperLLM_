The paper discusses the application of Target Propagation in Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and presents experimental findings regarding the angle between updates derived from Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) and Target Propagation Through Time (TPTT). The angle measurement is utilized to assess the orthogonality of update vectors across various problems: temporal order, 3-bit temporal order, and random permutation.

In the experiments, it was found that the angle between the update matrices initially approached 90 degrees, indicating orthogonal updates. As training progressed, this angle decreased, particularly in the 3-bit temporal order problem where it fell below 45 degrees. Notably, during the random permutation problem, the angle temporarily increased before peaking near 89 degrees, suggesting that TPTT may have identified a more efficient path to a local minimum, which was supported by a shorter convergence time.

Additionally, a modified network was introduced to track layer-specific cost functions throughout training, providing further insights into the learning dynamics of TPTT. Results from three TPTT networks, each tackling different problems with a time span of T=30, were presented, emphasizing the clarity of the plots while confirming consistent behavior across deeper networks. Overall, the findings highlight the dynamics of update vectors in TPTT and its potential advantages in RNN training.
