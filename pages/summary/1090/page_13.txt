The paper explores the effectiveness of Target Propagation (TPTT) in training Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) compared to Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). It presents a comparison of convergence rates across different problems, highlighting the number of iterations required for each method to reach convergence. 

The results show that TPTT performs well on the Temporal Order, 3-bit Temporal Order, and Random Permutation tasks using the same hyperparameters. However, the addition problem proved to be the most challenging, with TPTT needing manual adjustments to learning rates to improve performance, yet it still fell short of BPTT's results.

A significant advantage of TPTT is its ability to address the vanishing and exploding gradient issues commonly encountered in RNNs. The paper illustrates that while the spectral radius of the hidden-to-hidden matrix in BPTT decreases over time, leading to ineffective learning, TPTT maintains stability, allowing for continuous weight updates and effective cost reduction.

Additionally, TPTT demonstrated a faster convergence speed than BPTT across most tasks, except for the addition problem where TPTT struggled. The paper concludes that TPTT shows promise in improving RNN training efficiency, particularly in classification tasks, although it may require further adjustments for complex problems.
