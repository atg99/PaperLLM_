The paper by Manchev and Spratling investigates the performance of two learning mechanisms, Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) and Truncated Backpropagation Through Time (TPTT), in training neural networks on temporal order problems. The authors present results in Table 1, which outlines the maximal depth (sequence length) achieved by various models under different conditions. Each model begins training with an initial sequence length of T=10, and if successful, the length is increased by 10 until failure occurs, with the highest T achieved recorded as tmax.

Key findings indicate that TPTT generally outperforms BPTT in terms of achieving greater depths in three out of four tested scenarios. For instance, in the temporal order problem, the models trained with TPTT exhibited higher tmax values compared to those trained with BPTT. Figure 3 provides a visual comparison of the two training mechanisms, illustrating changes in synaptic weight updates and cost during training with a sequence length of T=100. The results underscore the efficacy of TPTT in facilitating training on more complex temporal tasks, suggesting that it may be a preferable alternative to BPTT for certain applications in sequence learning.
