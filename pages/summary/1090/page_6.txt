In the paper by Manchev and Spratling, the authors describe a two-phase operation for a neural network after local targets are established. In the first phase, the network updates the parameters Vhh and ch using gradient descent to minimize the mean squared error (MSE) between the approximated state and the actual values at time t-1. The learning rate for this phase is denoted as βg.

In the second phase, the network focuses on updating the feedforward parameters, specifically Whh and bh, with the goal of reducing the global error by aligning outputs closer to the target values. The learning rate for these updates is denoted as βf. Notably, the output parameters Why and by are updated directly with respect to the error E, as they are not affected by vanishing or exploding gradients. 

The paper also discusses two methods for updating the input feedforward parameters Wxh. One method aligns with the update approach for Why and by, while the alternative method mirrors the updates for Whh, aiming to bring the hidden layer outputs closer to local targets. The experiments conducted utilize a combination of these methods to evaluate performance.
