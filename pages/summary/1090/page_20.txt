In the study by Manchev and Spratling, the performance of different neural network training methods, specifically LSTM networks, is evaluated across various synthetic problems and the MNIST digit classification task. The results for the synthetic problems, as shown in Table 5, indicate that LSTM networks trained with specific learning rates generally outperform networks trained using backpropagation through time (BPTT) and target propagation through time (TPTT) in three out of four tasks. The learning rates were optimized through a grid search, revealing that the LSTM architecture's inherent advantages contribute significantly to its performance, rather than solely the optimization technique used.

Additionally, the MNIST dataset, consisting of 60,000 training and 10,000 test images of handwritten digits, was employed to assess the networks' capabilities in handling long-range dependencies, as each image's 784 pixels are presented sequentially to the network. The training setup for the MNIST classification task closely mirrored that of previous studies, utilizing mini-batches of 16 images and a training limit of 1,000,000 iterations. The hidden layer contained 100 neurons, and a grid search was conducted to optimize the network parameters, albeit with a limited sample size due to the dataset's computational demands. Overall, the findings suggest that LSTM networks are effective for complex sequence learning tasks, particularly those involving long-range dependencies.
