The paper discusses the architecture and functionality of the Residual Extended Skip (RES) block and the Wide Context (WC) block, both of which are integral to enhancing segmentation in cancer detection. 

The RES block focuses on generating middle-level features from low-level features, effectively mitigating information degradation. It accommodates high size variations in cancer regions by employing a residual extended skip mechanism that performs contextual aggregation across multiple scales, ensuring scale invariance. This design increases the valid receptive field, enhancing the BU-Net's segmentation capabilities.

The WC block consists of two parallel connections, each containing two convolution layers with varying filter sizes. This strategic combination of filter sizes in both connections optimally extracts features that contribute significantly to performance. The WC block also plays a crucial role in extracting contextual information necessary for distinguishing between various cancer sub-classes and facilitates feature aggregation at transition levels, improving the reconstruction of segmented regions.

Overall, both blocks are designed to optimize feature extraction and contextual understanding, which are vital for accurate cancer segmentation.
