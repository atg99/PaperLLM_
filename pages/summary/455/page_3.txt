The paper introduces BU-Net, a novel network architecture aimed at improving brain tumor segmentation. BU-Net is built upon the U-Net framework and incorporates two innovative modules: the Residual Extended Skip (RES) module and the Wide Context (WC) module. These enhancements are designed to facilitate better contextual information and feature aggregation. The RES module specifically addresses the conversion of low-level features to middle-level features, which is vital for accurately segmenting brain tumors that can vary significantly in appearance. Additionally, it increases the valid receptive field, overcoming limitations present in previous models. To tackle issues arising from class imbalance in pixel representation, BU-Net employs two combined loss functions.

The effectiveness of BU-Net was evaluated using two benchmark datasets, BraTS 2017 and BraTS 2018. The BraTS 2017 dataset includes images from 285 glioma patients, categorized into high-grade and low-grade gliomas, with expert-labeled ground truth for training data. The validation dataset includes images from 46 patients but lacks publicly available labels. The classes labeled in this dataset include enhancing tumor, necrosis and non-enhancing tumor, edema, and healthy tissue. The BraTS 2018 dataset mirrors the BraTS 2017 dataset in terms of training images and labeling procedures, with the addition of a new validation dataset containing images from 66 patients of unknown grade. BU-Net has demonstrated promising results compared to existing state-of-the-art segmentation techniques in this domain.
