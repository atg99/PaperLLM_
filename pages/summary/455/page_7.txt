The section discusses the challenges of brain tumor segmentation, particularly focusing on the issue of class imbalance in MRI data. The distribution of classes in the BraTS training data reveals that healthy tissues cover 98.46% of the area, while edema, enhancing tumors, and non-enhancing tumors occupy significantly smaller portions (1.02%, 0.29%, and 0.23% respectively). This disparity adversely affects segmentation performance.

To tackle this problem, the BU-Net model employs a customized loss function that combines weighted cross-entropy (WCE) and Dice loss coefficient (DLC). The WCE aims to classify tissue cells according to their class, while the DLC focuses on maximizing the overlap between ground truth and predicted segmented regions. The total loss function is represented as Ltotal = WCE + DLC.

The effectiveness of the BU-Net model is evaluated through both quantitative and qualitative analyses. The Dice score, a common metric in previous studies, is used for quantitative performance evaluation, allowing for comparison with state-of-the-art methods. The model was tested on the BraTS 2017 HGG dataset, which consists of 210 cases, with 80% allocated for training and 20% for testing, as defined by the BraTS challenge.
