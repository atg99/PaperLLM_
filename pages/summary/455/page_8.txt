The paper presents BU-Net, a novel architecture for tumor segmentation in MRI scans, and compares its performance with existing techniques such as U-Net, ResU-Net, and others. BU-Net demonstrates significant improvements over its baseline U-Net, achieving gains of 7%, 6.6%, and 8.5% for whole, core, and enhancing tumor segmentation, respectively. The Dice scores indicate that BU-Net outperforms four state-of-the-art methods for high-grade glioma (HGG) data.

In experiments using the BraTS 2017 dataset, BU-Net achieved a Dice score of 0.901 for whole tumors, 0.867 for core tumors, and 0.835 for enhancing tumors, surpassing the best results from existing literature. Specifically, BU-Net outperformed ResU-Net by 0.3% for enhancing tumors and 0.5% for core tumors, and improved by 1.6% over NovelNet for whole tumors. This suggests that BU-Net is effective in identifying small tumor regions.

Further evaluation on the BraTS 2018 dataset showed BU-Net achieving Dice scores of 0.901, 0.837, and 0.788 for whole, core, and enhancing tumors, respectively. The results affirm BU-Net's superior performance compared to both U-Net and other state-of-the-art methods, indicating a high intersection over union for tumor area identification.

Qualitative analysis complements the quantitative findings, showcasing visual comparisons between ground truth and BU-Net predictions. The results reveal that BU-Net closely aligns with the ground truth, in contrast to U-Net, which incorrectly segments unwanted regions as edema. This visual analysis underscores BU-Net's capability to accurately identify tumor regions, particularly necrosis, where U-Net falls short. Overall, BU-Net demonstrates notable advancements in MRI tumor segmentation, highlighting its potential for clinical applications.
