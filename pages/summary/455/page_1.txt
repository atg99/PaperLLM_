The paper presents BU-Net, a modified U-Net architecture designed for the semantic segmentation of brain tumors, which is crucial for effective treatment and diagnosis. The authors emphasize the challenges in brain tumor segmentation and the need for enhanced performance in this area. BU-Net incorporates residual extended skip (RES) connections and wide context (WC) features, along with a customized loss function, to improve the extraction of diverse features and increase the valid receptive field. This approach facilitates better contextual information aggregation, which leads to improved segmentation outcomes.

BU-Net was specifically evaluated on high-grade glioma (HGG) datasets from the BraTS 2017 Challenge, focusing on three key tumor sub-regions: tumor core (TC), whole tumor (WT), and enhancing core (EC). The performance of the proposed architecture was quantitatively assessed using the dice score metric, demonstrating superior results compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques. The findings suggest that BU-Net can significantly contribute to advancements in bioinformatics and medical research related to brain tumor segmentation. 

The introduction highlights the prevalence of malignant brain tumors, particularly gliomas, and the importance of high-quality image processing for their diagnosis. MRI serves as the primary tool for monitoring these tumors, employing various modalities to achieve comprehensive visualization.
