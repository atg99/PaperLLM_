The paper discusses a parameter-efficient approach to transfer learning in natural language processing (NLP) using adapter modules integrated within the Transformer architecture. The adapter modules are designed to be added after the multi-headed attention and feed-forward layers of each Transformer layer, allowing for task-specific adaptations without the need for extensive retraining of the entire model.

The architecture includes a bottleneck design, significantly reducing the number of parameters needed for adaptation. Each adapter first reduces the dimensionality of the output, applies a nonlinearity, and then projects it back to the original size. This design allows for tuning with only a small fraction of the parameters compared to full fine-tuning, typically around 0.5-8% of the original model's parameters. The inclusion of skip-connections within the adapter modules aids in maintaining performance while keeping the architecture efficient.

The paper presents experimental results demonstrating that adapter tuning achieves performance comparable to full fine-tuning on the GLUE benchmark, with only about 3% of the parameters being trained. This effectiveness is further validated across 17 additional classification tasks and in SQuAD question answering, indicating that adapter-based tuning effectively focuses on the higher layers of the network.

The experiments were conducted using the pre-trained BERT model, following standard training procedures that optimize using the Adam algorithm. The findings suggest that adapter modules provide a compelling solution for efficient transfer learning in NLP, allowing for both flexibility and reduced computational requirements.
