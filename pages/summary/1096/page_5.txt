The paper discusses parameter-efficient transfer learning for natural language processing (NLP), highlighting the effectiveness of adapter tuning compared to full fine-tuning of models like BERT. The results from various tasks on the GLUE benchmark demonstrate that adapter tuning, which uses significantly fewer parameters, achieves comparable performance to full fine-tuning. For instance, using only 1.3% of the parameters, adapter tuning scored 80.0% overall accuracy, while full fine-tuning used 100% of the parameters and achieved 80.4%.

The experiments also included various datasets beyond GLUE, showing that while the BERT BASE model's performance improves with fine-tuning, adapter tuning maintains competitive accuracy with a drastically reduced number of trained parameters. The analysis indicates that tuning fewer layers can lead to performance drops on certain tasks, whereas adapters consistently deliver good results across multiple tasks.

Figures in the paper illustrate the trade-offs between the number of parameters and performance, revealing that adapter tuning not only requires fewer resources but also achieves efficiency by maintaining high accuracy. Specifically, the study found that tuning just the top layer of BERT resulted in diminished performance compared to using adapters. This indicates that adapter tuning is a viable strategy for achieving strong performance in NLP tasks while being resource-efficient. Overall, the findings suggest that adapter tuning is a highly effective approach for transfer learning in NLP, producing compact models that closely match the performance of larger, fully fine-tuned models.
