This paper discusses parameter-efficient transfer learning for natural language processing (NLP), focusing on the use of adapters within Transformer networks. The authors highlight the benefits of fine-tuning pre-trained models, such as BERT, which has led to state-of-the-art results in various tasks like question answering and text classification. However, they note that traditional fine-tuning requires new network weights for each task, which can be inefficient.

The paper explores multi-task learning (MTL) where tasks are trained simultaneously, leveraging shared network parameters to improve performance across various NLP applications. Despite its advantages, MTL necessitates concurrent access to all tasks during training.

Continual learning is presented as an alternative, focusing on learning from a sequence of tasks. However, it faces challenges like catastrophic forgetting, where models lose previous task capabilities upon retraining. Techniques exist to mitigate forgetting, but they are not as effective as the proposed adapter approach, which scales better with a smaller parameter footprint.

In the vision domain, fine-tuning pre-trained models has become standard practice, and the paper references the use of convolutional adapter modules that facilitate incremental learning. The authors argue that their bottleneck adapters are significantly smaller than those used in concurrent works, while maintaining performance.

The paper also acknowledges related work such as Projected Attention Layers (PALs) and empirical comparisons with other methods, emphasizing the unique contributions of their adapter framework. Acknowledgments are given to contributors for valuable discussions.
