The study investigates the efficiency of parameter usage in transfer learning for natural language processing by comparing different methods of fine-tuning. It highlights the use of adapters, which are smaller modules added to the base model, against traditional full fine-tuning of the top layers. The findings indicate that adapters can achieve performance comparable to full fine-tuning while significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters required.

Results across various tasks, including GLUE and SQuAD, show that adapters maintain high accuracy while training fewer parameters. For instance, on the SQuAD validation set, adapters of size 64 achieved an F1 score of 90.4%, closely trailing the full fine-tuning score of 90.7%, with adapters of size 2 still attaining a respectable 89.9%.

The analysis suggests that the flexibility and efficiency of adapters make them a promising approach for parameter-efficient transfer learning in NLP, allowing for effective model adaptation across different tasks without the overhead of extensive parameter tuning.
