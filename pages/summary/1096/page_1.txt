The paper presents a novel approach to transfer learning in natural language processing (NLP) using adapter modules, addressing the inefficiencies of fine-tuning large pre-trained models for multiple downstream tasks. Traditional fine-tuning requires a new model for each task, which leads to excessive parameter usage. In contrast, adapter modules are compact and allow for parameter sharing by adding only a few trainable parameters per task while keeping the original model's parameters fixed. 

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach by applying it to the BERT Transformer model across 26 diverse text classification tasks, including the GLUE benchmark. Their results show that adapter modules achieve near state-of-the-art performance, with a minimal increase of 3.6% in parameters per task, compared to 100% parameter training required for full fine-tuning. The paper emphasizes the advantages of compact and extensible models that can be incrementally trained for new tasks without losing performance on previously learned tasks. This method provides a practical solution for applications such as cloud services, where models need to adapt to a stream of incoming tasks efficiently.
