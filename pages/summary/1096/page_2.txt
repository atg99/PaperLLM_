The paper discusses an innovative approach to parameter-efficient transfer learning in natural language processing (NLP) through adapter tuning. Traditional methods like feature-based transfer and fine-tuning require new weights for each task, but adapter tuning significantly reduces the number of parameters trained per task while maintaining performance. The proposed method involves adding adapter modules between layers of a pre-trained network, allowing for minimal additional parameters—two orders of magnitude fewer than fine-tuning—while achieving comparable results.

Adapter tuning operates by defining a new function that integrates both the pre-trained parameters and task-specific parameters. This design allows the model to retain knowledge from previous tasks without interference, a key advantage over multi-task and continual learning systems. The adapters do not interact, and the shared parameters remain fixed, preserving the model's memory of earlier tasks.

The paper highlights the effectiveness of the adapter tuning method through experiments on a variety of text classification tasks, demonstrating that it nearly matches the performance of fully fine-tuned models while using only a fraction of the task-specific parameters. This efficiency is particularly beneficial for cloud-based services where multiple models are trained sequentially.

The proposed bottleneck adapter module enhances the architecture of Transformer networks, which are already state-of-the-art in various NLP applications. The adapter modules are characterized by a small number of parameters and near-identity initialization, ensuring stable training and minimal impact on the original model. Overall, adapter-based tuning provides a scalable solution for training NLP models across diverse tasks efficiently.
