The paper discusses parameter-efficient transfer learning in natural language processing (NLP) using a method called adapter tuning, which aims to achieve performance comparable to full fine-tuning while using significantly fewer parameters. The authors focus on the GLUE benchmark, evaluating the pre-trained BERT LARGE model, which has 330 million parameters. Their experiments involve hyperparameter tuning for adapter size and learning rates, revealing that adapters achieve a mean GLUE score of 80.0, slightly lower than the 80.4 score from full fine-tuning, while requiring only 1.3 times the parameters of BERT, as opposed to 9 times for full fine-tuning.

In addition to the GLUE benchmark, the authors test adapter performance on various text classification tasks with diverse characteristics. They conduct a large-scale hyperparameter search using the Neural AutoML algorithm to ensure their BERT-based models are competitive. The results show that adapter tuning is nearly equivalent to full fine-tuning, with adapters requiring significantly fewer parameters (1.19 times the parameters across all tasks) compared to the variable fine-tuning approach, which requires 9.9 times the parameters.

The paper further explores the trade-off between adapter size and model performance, indicating that smaller adapters introduce fewer parameters, potentially impacting performance. Overall, the findings highlight the efficiency of adapter tuning in achieving high performance with reduced parameter counts in NLP tasks.
