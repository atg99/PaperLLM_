The paper discusses the implementation of a variable length genetic algorithm for efficient hyperparameter optimization in deep learning. It presents experimental results comparing the performance of classical genetic algorithms with different population sizes under a 30-hour time constraint. The findings, illustrated in three plots (a, b, c), show the fitness levels of individuals across generations for population sizes of 50, 20, and 10, respectively. The experiments indicate that a larger population size leads to fewer generations being produced, as exemplified by the algorithm halting in the second generation with a population of 50 due to the limited time for evaluation. Each model was assessed over 4000 iterations, resulting in the evaluation of approximately 75 models within the given time frame. The study highlights the trade-offs between population size and the number of generations in the optimization process.
