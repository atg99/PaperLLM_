The paper discusses an efficient hyperparameter optimization method in deep learning using a variable length genetic algorithm. A key aspect is the weight initialization scheme for deeper models, where a new model builds upon the best model from a previous phase by transferring some weights while initializing others randomly.

The genetic algorithm's settings include parameters such as population size, number of generations, number of phases, mutation rate, and survival chances for less fit individuals. In the experiments, 50% of the fittest individuals are retained for the next generation, with a survival rate of 20% for less fit individuals and a mutation rate of 20%. Notably, the method does not define a fixed number of phases but instead employs a stopping condition.

The stopping condition is triggered when there is no improvement in validation accuracy despite adding more layers. Specifically, if the best fitness in the new phase falls more than 0.01 below the previous phase's best, the evolutionary process halts, and the individual with the best fitness is selected for further training. For example, the process concluded at Phase 14, where the best fitness was 0.8070, compared to 0.8233 at Phase 13.

The results indicate that 20 individuals were evolved over 5 generations in each phase, with each trained for 5 epochs to assess fitness. The evolutionary process stopped at Phase 14 as the stopping condition was met. An increase in convolutional layers showed significant initial improvements in model accuracy, although this enhancement diminished in later phases, highlighting the impact of convolutional layers on model performance.
