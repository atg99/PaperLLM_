The paper discusses an efficient hyperparameter optimization method in deep learning using a Variable Length Genetic Algorithm (VLGA). It illustrates the evolutionary process and when the algorithm terminates based on fitness thresholds, as shown in the figures. The authors implemented large scale evolution on a public framework, testing various population sizes (30, 50, and 80). The findings indicate that while large scale evolution can yield good models with sufficient resources, it performs poorly under constraints. Specifically, with a limited setup of one GPU for 30 hours, the fitness of individuals plateaued, particularly with smaller population sizes.

The paper compares VLGA with random search, traditional genetic algorithms, and large scale evolution, demonstrating that VLGA achieved a test accuracy of 88.92% within 25 hours. In contrast, large scale evolution produced the lowest accuracy under similar time constraints due to its reliance on mutation alone, lacking the crossover mechanism that could enhance model diversity. Although classical genetic algorithms yielded relatively high accuracy, they require fixed layer configurations, which may not be suitable for all problems. VLGA, by contrast, adapts to varying layer counts, thus offering better model performance when computational resources are limited. Overall, the study emphasizes the practicality and effectiveness of VLGA in environments where resources are constrained.
