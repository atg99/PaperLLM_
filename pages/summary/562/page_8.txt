The paper discusses an efficient approach to hyperparameter optimization in deep learning using a variable length genetic algorithm. It highlights the significance of the search space, which encompasses all potential solutions for constructing convolutional neural networks (CNNs). A smaller search space may expedite the search process but can limit model diversity, while a larger search space, although more comprehensive, demands greater computational resources.

The search space in the experiments includes various hyperparameters such as the number of output feature maps, convolutional filter sizes, activation function types, pooling types, skip connections, batch normalization, and the total number of layers. As the model's depth increases, the search space expands exponentially, complicating the search process. Notably, the study incorporates pooling types in the search space, a deviation from previous works. The initial model configuration with two layers presents 156,800 combinations, which skyrockets to approximately 10^35 with 14 layers.

The paper details the experimental setup, noting that the method is implemented in Python with Keras, utilizing a single GPU. To enhance efficiency, the fitness of each model is evaluated based on test accuracy after five training epochs. Additionally, weight transfer is employed to optimize time; if a model progresses to a new generation, its trained weights are retained and integrated into the new models, which are then trained for another five epochs to assess their fitness.
