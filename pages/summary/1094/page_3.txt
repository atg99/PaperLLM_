The paper by Kim et al. discusses the evolution of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and highlights their significance in medical imaging. Unlike fully connected layers, CNNs incorporate at least one convolutional layer, making them more effective for image processing tasks. The authors refer to these CNN models as backbone models and provide a summary of the five most prominent models in chronological order.

The evolutionary timeline begins with LeNet, developed in 1998, followed by AlexNet in 2012, which marked a turning point by winning the ImageNet challenge. VGG, introduced as the first deep model, and GoogLeNet, known for its innovative inception block concept, further advanced the field. However, very deep networks faced challenges, such as the vanishing gradient problem, which was addressed by ResNet through the use of residual blocks with skip connections.

The paper also details the calculation of parameters per filter and emphasizes the use of the Softmax function in the classifier head, except for LeNet-5, which employs the hyperbolic tangent function. The models' architectures and their respective parameters are summarized in a table, providing insights into their structure and performance on different datasets, including MNIST and ImageNet.
