In this paper, Kim et al. explore the application of transfer learning (TL) using pretrained convolutional neural networks (CNN) for medical image analysis. The emergence of deep learning algorithms, particularly CNN, has transformed image analysis, enabling automated processes that outperform traditional handcrafted methods. Despite their efficacy, CNNs require substantial training data, which poses challenges in the medical field due to limited cohort sizes and the high costs associated with expert-annotated datasets.

To address the data scarcity issue, researchers have turned to TL techniques, which leverage knowledge gained from related tasks to enhance performance on new tasks. The paper reviews various TL approaches, including unsupervised TL and generative adversarial networks (GANs), and highlights recent advancements in the field. The authors aim to provide a comprehensive survey of TL applications in medical image analysis, offering an overview of case studies, actionable recommendations for leveraging TL, and a compilation of publicly available medical datasets.

The structure of the paper includes a background section on TL principles, a description of literature selection protocols, analysis of results, critical discussions, and a conclusion summarizing lessons learned. The authors emphasize the significance of TL in improving medical image classification while also presenting foundational concepts related to TL and CNNs.
