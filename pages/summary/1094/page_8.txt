The analysis of the literature revealed a substantial focus on various transfer learning (TL) configurations, with a notable diversity in methodologies. The most frequently employed model was Inception, recognized for its efficient use of parameters due to the incorporation of 1x1 filters, which serve to reduce computational demands. Despite the advantages of ResNet in terms of error rates and parameter efficiency, AlexNet and VGG remained popular, possibly due to their straightforward architecture.

The findings indicated no significant correlation between dataset size and performance metrics like accuracy and AUC, suggesting that larger datasets do not inherently lead to better outcomes. Furthermore, the study highlighted the lack of a consensus on optimal fine-tuning practices, with various studies adopting inconsistent methodologies. While fine-tuning from scratch is common, it is often computationally expensive and does not consistently enhance performance. Therefore, beginning with a feature extractor approach and gradually fine-tuning layers as needed is advised.

The paper also pointed out the need for further investigation into how different medical data characteristics might influence the effectiveness of TL approaches, as existing literature has not adequately addressed this. Overall, the survey underscores the complexity of applying TL in medical imaging and the necessity for standardized methodologies to enhance the reproducibility and efficacy of results.
