The paper by Kim et al. provides a comprehensive review of transfer learning (TL) in medical image classification, highlighting its significance in addressing data scarcity and optimizing resource usage. The authors analyzed 425 peer-reviewed articles, narrowing their focus to 121 studies that met their criteria. They categorized the studies based on backbone models and TL methodologies, including feature extraction, hybrid approaches, fine-tuning, and fine-tuning from scratch.

The findings reveal that a majority of studies evaluated multiple models, with deep models like Inception being the most prevalent. In terms of TL methods, many studies benchmarked various approaches to determine optimal configurations, with feature extraction and fine-tuning from scratch being the most commonly used. The review emphasizes the effectiveness of TL in overcoming challenges posed by limited datasets and urges practitioners to utilize deep models as feature extractors to enhance computational efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.

The authors conclude that transfer learning has proven beneficial in medical image analysis, encouraging the adoption of advanced deep learning models to improve performance while managing costs and time effectively.
