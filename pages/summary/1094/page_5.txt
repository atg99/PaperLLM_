The paper by Kim et al. presents a systematic review of studies focusing on transfer learning in medical image classification. Initially, 467 papers were retrieved from PubMed and Web of Science, with 42 duplicates removed. After screening, 189 studies were excluded, leading to 236 full texts being assessed, from which 114 were disqualified, resulting in 121 studies for further analysis. The studies were categorized based on their backbone models and types of transfer learning, with an emphasis on data characteristics and model performance.

The review highlights a notable increase in publications on transfer learning for medical image classification since 2016, following the success of AlexNet in 2012. Although there was a slight decline in publications in 2020, this was likely due to delays in the indexing process.

A significant portion of the studies (57) evaluated various backbone models. Notably, a benchmark study indicated that VGG19 achieved the highest accuracy of 89.3%, which is atypical as deeper models like Inception and ResNet often outperform shallower models. Comparisons among models revealed that Inception generally provided superior performance over VGG, while ResNet50 was reported to achieve better accuracy than several other models. The findings underscore the evolving landscape of model performance in medical image classification, with implications for future research and application.
