The paper by Kim et al. analyzes various models and transfer learning (TL) approaches utilized in medical imaging studies. The most frequently employed model was Inception, which was used in 26 studies, followed by AlexNet with 14 and VGG with 10. ResNet and Inception-ResNet were less common, while a few studies utilized models like LeNet5, DenseNet, and CheXNet.

In terms of TL strategies, 46 models were evaluated, with the feature extractor method being the most popular (38 studies), followed by fine-tuning from scratch (27). The latter, despite being resource-intensive, updates the entire model, whereas feature extraction significantly reduces computational costs. A hybrid feature extractor approach was also noted for its efficiency.

The analysis covered a variety of human anatomical regions, primarily focusing on breast and skin cancer. Different imaging modalities were highlighted, including CT and MRI, known for their capacity to produce 3D data, and digital microscopy for whole slide images of tissue samples. Most studies focused on binary classification, with a majority of them using datasets ranging from 0 to 600 samples.

Lastly, performance metrics such as the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and accuracy were visualized against data size and image modality, indicating the importance of normalized data quantities for fair comparison across studies.
