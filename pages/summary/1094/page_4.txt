This paper by Kim et al. explores transfer learning (TL) with convolutional neural networks (CNN) in the context of medical image classification. TL allows the transfer of knowledge from pre-trained CNN models, typically trained on natural images, to new tasks in the medical domain. The authors focus on homogeneous TL, where both source and target tasks involve image analysis, utilizing CNNs pretrained on ImageNet data.

The study identifies two primary TL approaches: feature extraction and fine-tuning. The feature extraction method involves freezing convolutional layers while attaching a separate classifier, such as SVM or Random Forest. In contrast, fine-tuning updates the entire parameter set, which is more resource-intensive. The authors categorize these approaches further, resulting in four distinct TL methods.

The methodology section details the selection process for relevant publications, which were retrieved from PubMed and Web of Science databases. Specific inclusion criteria were established, focusing on the use of CNNs for image analysis and the presence of terms related to transfer learning. A thorough screening process was conducted to eliminate irrelevant or duplicate studies, ensuring that only experimental studies were considered.

The analysis covers eight properties of 121 research articles, including CNN model types, performance metrics, TL methods, fine-tuning ratios, data modalities, subject areas, data quantity, and the number of classification categories. This comprehensive survey aims to provide insights into the effective application of TL with CNNs in medical imaging.
