The paper by Kim et al. discusses the application of transfer learning (TL) in medical image classification, emphasizing the limited use of native 3D convolutional neural networks (3D-CNNs) compared to 2D and 2.5D models. Although studies show that 3D-CNNs can outperform their 2D counterparts, resource limitations have constrained their use. The authors highlight the scarcity of data in medical image analysis and the reliance on publicly accessible datasets, which promote reproducibility in research. They note that most studies provided minimal details on hardware setups and performance metrics, hindering the assessment of TL efficiency.

The study focuses solely on TL for image classification, excluding other applications such as object detection and segmentation. It also does not evaluate custom CNN architectures or vision transformers, which are gaining traction in image analysis. The authors conclude by offering practical recommendations for selecting and tuning CNN models, suggesting the use of deep CNN models like ResNet or Inception as feature extractors, and advocating for gradual fine-tuning to optimize performance while conserving computational resources. Additionally, they present a structured table of publicly available medical datasets categorized by various attributes to assist researchers in their work.
