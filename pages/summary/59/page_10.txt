The cited works encompass a range of significant contributions to the fields of computer vision, deep learning, and action recognition. Key papers include the ImageNet challenge by Russakovsky et al., which set benchmarks for large-scale visual recognition. Sak et al. introduced Long Short-Term Memory networks for acoustic modeling, highlighting advancements in handling sequential data. Schuldt et al. focused on human action recognition using local SVMs, while Sermanet et al. presented Overfeat, integrating recognition, localization, and detection through convolutional networks.

Simonyan and Zisserman's two-stream convolutional networks and their very deep convolutional networks further advanced video action recognition and image classification respectively. The UCF101 dataset by Soomro et al. provided a benchmark for human action classification in videos. Venugopalan et al. explored video-to-language translation using deep recurrent networks, and Villegas et al. investigated video sequence prediction by decomposing motion and content.

Wang et al. proposed a hybrid fully convolutional network for actionness estimation, while Wu et al. modeled spatial-temporal clues for video classification. The Convolutional LSTM network by Xingjian et al. was introduced for precipitation nowcasting, demonstrating the versatility of deep learning in temporal tasks. Xu et al. and Ng et al. contributed to instance segmentation and video classification, respectively, showcasing the evolving landscape of deep learning applications in processing and understanding video content.
