The paper discusses a novel approach to enhancing the training of deep recurrent neural networks (RNNs) through the use of a context bridge module (CBM) and an overlap coherence training scheme. The authors highlight the challenges associated with training deep RNNs on high-dimensional long sequences, particularly the computational complexity that arises as model depth increases. To address this, they propose a method that incorporates a temporal dropout rate to decouple the learning of representation and temporal information, thereby improving the model's generalization ability.

The context bridge module allows the model to function as a general version of stacked RNN/LSTM architectures. By adjusting the output of the representation unit, the model can simulate conventional RNNs or deeper architectures, demonstrating its versatility. A toy experiment is introduced to illustrate the effectiveness of the context bridge, where the model successfully learns spatial and temporal information from video classification tasks.

The overlap coherence training scheme is presented as a solution to the limitations of sampling methods used in training RNNs. While traditional approaches sample short clips from long sequences, potentially losing important temporal information, the proposed method introduces overlaps between clips to maintain a smooth flow of information. This allows the model to leverage both short-term and long-term dependencies effectively.

Overall, the paper presents a compelling argument for using the context bridge module and overlap coherence training scheme to enhance the training of deep RNNs, making it more efficient and capable of capturing complex temporal relationships in high-dimensional data.
