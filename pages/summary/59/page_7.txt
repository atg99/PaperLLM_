The paper presents video prediction results evaluated on the KTH dataset, comparing various methods including ConvLSTM, MCnet, and a proposed deep RNN framework utilizing a CBM (Convolutional Block Module). Key metrics used for evaluation are PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio) and SSIM (Structural Similarity Index). The results indicate that the proposed method outperforms the original MCnet and ConvLSTM models, achieving improvements of 1.6% in SSIM and 1.8% in PSNR for 10-frame predictions, and 2.6% in SSIM and 2.1% in PSNR for 20-frame predictions.

The paper further discusses the architecture of Polygon-RNN models of varying depths, emphasizing the performance benefits of deeper networks. The analysis shows that the deep CBM model significantly outperforms shallow RNNs, achieving a competitive edge over existing models like Polygon-RNN++. The study highlights the importance of model depth and the effectiveness of the CBM in optimizing training for action recognition and anticipation tasks.

The findings suggest that deeper models, particularly with the integration of CBM, not only achieve lower training loss but also higher IoU (Intersection over Union) values in performance evaluations. Overall, the research underscores the advantages of utilizing deep architectures and the innovative design of the CBM for enhanced video prediction and action recognition tasks.
