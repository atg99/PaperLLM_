The paper presents a novel approach to address the challenges associated with training deep recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for visual sequence tasks. The authors propose an Overlap Coherence Training Scheme aimed at reducing the training costs typically incurred due to long sequences. This scheme involves randomly sampling long sequences into overlapping short clips, which act as a communication bridge to facilitate smoother information propagation between adjacent clips. By simplifying the original Markov process, the training complexity is significantly reduced while maintaining temporal coherence among the clips.

The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of the proposed framework through extensive experiments across various tasks, including action recognition, anticipation, auxiliary annotation, and video future prediction. Key findings include that deep RNNs benefit from increased depth compared to shallow networks, the Context Bridge Module (CBM) enhances performance over traditional RNN structures like LSTM, and the overlap coherence training scheme enables training on high-dimensional sequential inputs on standard computing devices. Notable performance improvements are reported, such as over 11% relative gains in action recognition tasks, a 14.7% increase in IoU for the Polygon-RNN task, and a 2.4% enhancement in PSNR and SSIM metrics for video future prediction.

The paper also reviews related work in visual sequence tasks, highlighting the limitations of previous methods, including pooling approaches and 3D convolutional networks. It discusses the exploration of deep RNN structures and the importance of depth in capturing hierarchical features, underscoring the need for a framework that balances optimization and performance for high-dimensional inputs like videos.

In conclusion, the proposed deep RNN framework, characterized by the CBM and the overlap coherence training scheme, demonstrates significant potential for advancing the capabilities of RNNs in visual sequence modeling.
