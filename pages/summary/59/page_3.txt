The paper introduces a novel architecture called the Context Bridge Module (CBM) designed to effectively capture both temporal and representation information flows in deep recurrent neural networks (RNNs). Traditional deep RNNs face challenges due to the difficulty of co-adapting parameters to handle high-dimensional visual tasks, which can lead to ineffective training. The authors argue that separating these two types of information flow can simplify the training process.

In the CBM architecture, the representation flow is managed by a conventional CNN structure that extracts features from individual inputs without recurrent operations, while the temporal flow is processed through an RNN structure. This separation allows the two branches to learn independently, thereby easing the complexity of co-adaptation. The representation unit (R) acts as a bridge for the representation flow, while the temporal unit (T) encodes sequential inputs.

The paper also discusses the implementation of a Temporal Dropout (TD) scheme, which aims to decouple the learning of representation flow from temporal flow at the beginning of training. By controlling the back-propagation of gradients through the TD mechanism, the authors suggest that the model can develop a more effective neural representation before fully integrating the temporal learning process.

In summary, the authors propose the CBM as a solution to the challenges faced by deep RNNs in high-dimensional tasks, emphasizing the benefits of independently processing representation and temporal information flows to enhance training efficiency.
