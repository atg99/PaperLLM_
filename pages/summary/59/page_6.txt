The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various deep recurrent neural network (RNN) architectures for action recognition and anticipation tasks on the UCF-101 and HMDB-51 datasets. The study compares the performance of different models, including 1-layer and 15-layer versions of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, Convolutional LSTMs (ConvLSTMs), and Convolutional Block Memory (CBM) networks. 

Key findings indicate that while the 15-layer ConvLSTM model shows lower accuracy in action recognition compared to the 1-layer LSTM, the 15-layer CBM model outperforms the shallower models with significant relative improvements of 12.2% on UCF-101 and 11.7% on HMDB-51 for action recognition. For action anticipation, both deep models improve performance, with the CBM achieving 88.6% relative improvements on UCF-101.

The study also explores replacing the VGG backbone with InceptionV1, validating the versatility of the proposed deep RNN framework. Results show that the deep CBM model continues to outperform shallow models, achieving a 5.3% improvement for action recognition.

In a separate evaluation on the Kinetics dataset, the deep CBM without a backbone demonstrates competitive performance, achieving substantial improvements over conventional shallow models. The study further extends the application of the deep RNN framework to the Polygon-RNN model for instance segmentation tasks on the Cityscapes dataset, highlighting the adaptability of the architecture.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of deep RNNs, particularly the CBM architecture, in enhancing action recognition and anticipation tasks while also being applicable to auxiliary tasks like instance segmentation.
