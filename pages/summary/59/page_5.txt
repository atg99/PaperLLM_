The paper introduces a novel training approach for deep recurrent neural networks (RNNs) aimed at enhancing the performance of models in sequence tasks, particularly in video action recognition and anticipation. The authors propose an overlap coherence loss, which encourages outputs from overlapping sections of different clips to remain coherent. The training objective combines this new loss with the original task loss, allowing for better information transfer across clips.

Key aspects of the training scheme include random sampling for data augmentation, which enhances generalization, and an innovative initialization method that uses hidden states from earlier trained clips to facilitate smoother information flow. This approach also addresses the vanishing/exploding gradient problem by shortening sequences, thus making training more manageable.

The effectiveness of the proposed scheme is demonstrated through a toy experiment involving an image sequence of cats and dogs, where the model successfully predicts the distance of dog images from a cat image, even when the cat image appears significantly earlier in the sequence.

The paper further evaluates the deep RNN framework against conventional shallow RNNs like LSTM on various sequence tasks, particularly using the UCF-101 and HMDB-51 datasets for action recognition. The deep RNN architecture comprises 15 layers with convolutional kernels to preserve spatial features, contrasting with the linear kernels of shallow networks. The implementation details include video frame resizing, color augmentation, and the use of batch normalization.

Overall, the results indicate that the deep RNN framework outperforms shallow counterparts, confirming its superiority in handling high-dimensional inputs and complex sequence tasks.
