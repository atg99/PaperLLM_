In the WD setting, where the camera moves parallel to the ground, both models generate plausible outputs. However, CONV-AR-VAE struggles with maintaining object consistency, particularly with the blue object, while NeRF-VAE provides consistent predictions and accurately infers scene geometry. In the OOD setting, where the camera is lifted and oriented downward, CONV-AR-VAE fails significantly, producing distorted colors and unrealistic outputs. In contrast, NeRF-VAE successfully renders plausible scene geometry from these OOD viewpoints.

Further investigations involve generalization on the CLEVR dataset, where NeRF-VAE again outperforms CONV-AR-VAE, demonstrating superior predictions as the camera approaches objects. When the number of visible objects exceeds what the model was trained on, NeRF-VAE with an attention-based scene function reconstructs all objects accurately, while the MLP variant misses some.

The paper highlights the challenges posed by degenerate orientations in the GQN data, where camera views are restricted to a plane. This limitation affects the neural network's ability to handle varying viewpoints. To mitigate rendering artifacts, the authors suggest removing certain orientation inputs during training, which does not negatively impact visual output quality.

Overall, NeRF-VAE demonstrates a clear advantage in generalization capabilities over CONV-AR-VAE, particularly in handling OOD camera views and varying object counts. The findings indicate that NeRF-VAE is more robust in rendering consistent and plausible scene representations across diverse scenarios.
