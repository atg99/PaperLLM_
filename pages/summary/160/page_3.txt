The paper presents NeRF-VAE, a generative model that integrates geometry awareness into 3D scene representation. The model employs an encoder to approximate the posterior distribution over a latent variable z using a set of context images and corresponding camera positions. This latent variable defines specific scenes represented by a conditional scene function G, which is utilized by the NeRF renderer to generate images from various viewpoints.

In the training phase, NeRF-VAE optimizes a variational objective that combines reconstruction error and KL divergence to learn the parameters of the scene function and the encoder. The encoder, implemented as a ResNet, processes multiple context views, producing outputs that are averaged to form the approximate posterior over z, modeled as a diagonal Gaussian.

To enhance inference efficiency, the model adopts amortized inference, contrasting with traditional NeRF, which requires scene-specific parameter estimation. Additionally, it employs an iterative refinement process to address the amortization gap, where initial guesses for posterior parameters are iteratively improved through sampling and gradient evaluation.

The decoder’s reliance on geometric structure differentiates NeRF-VAE from standard VAEs, which typically lack such explicit geometric considerations. The paper suggests potential for future exploration into more advanced techniques for context encoding.
