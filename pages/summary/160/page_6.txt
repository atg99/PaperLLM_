The paper presents NeRF-VAE, a geometry-aware generative model for 3D scenes that aims to improve upon the limitations of the original Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) framework. The authors conduct experiments to analyze the model's performance in terms of Mean Squared Error (MSE) when varying the number of training and context views. Results indicate that NeRF-VAE consistently outperforms NeRF when fewer training views (less than or equal to 6) are used, whereas NeRF requires a significantly larger number of views (around 100) to achieve comparable error rates.

The analysis reveals that NeRF's error distribution is wide and prone to large errors when trained with fewer views, while NeRF-VAE maintains a concentrated error distribution across different context view counts. This indicates that NeRF-VAE captures scene structure more effectively, even with a simpler architecture. The authors highlight that NeRF struggles with interpolation between limited training views, leading to catastrophic failures in predictions, whereas NeRF-VAE remains robust across varying error levels.

In a comparative study with a convolution-based generative model, CONV-AR-VAE, the authors note that while both models can synthesize novel views from few input images, NeRF-VAE's geometry-informed decoder offers superior generalization capabilities to out-of-distribution camera positions and orientations. This suggests that the explicit incorporation of 3D geometry in NeRF-VAE enhances its performance in generating realistic 3D scenes.

The paper concludes that while NeRF-VAE shows promise in handling fewer views effectively, there is potential for further improvement through a more expressive scene function and enhanced inference mechanisms.
