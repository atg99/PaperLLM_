The paper presents NeRF-VAE, a geometry-aware 3D scene generative model that utilizes variational autoencoding techniques to generate and reconstruct scenes from latent variables. The study focuses on the performance of NeRF-VAE on the CLEVR and GQN datasets, comparing it with the CONV-AR-VAE model through quantitative metrics such as Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence.

Key findings indicate that NeRF-VAE variants, particularly those employing iterative inference and MLP-based scene functions, exhibit improved reconstruction performance while maintaining low KL values, especially on the simpler CLEVR dataset. In contrast, the attentive models, although achieving lower reconstruction errors in high KL regimes, indicate a higher capacity for modeling complex data. The CONV-AR-VAE struggles with modeling CLEVR data, achieving high KL values despite a high MSE threshold.

On the more complex GQN dataset, characterized by high-frequency textures and partial visibility, MLP scene functions without iterative inference yield lower KL values but higher MSE. Attentive models can achieve lower reconstruction errors at similar KL levels, demonstrating a peaked posterior for certain latent variable values. Utilizing iterative inference enhances performance for both decoders, allowing for the best trade-off between KL and MSE, although CONV-AR-VAE is noted to have limited generalization capabilities for out-of-distribution views.

The paper further illustrates NeRF-VAE's ability to learn an unconditional prior over scenes, generating diverse samples that closely resemble the training data distributions. An example showcases the model's proficiency in inferring a scene from a single context image, predicting a complete view of a partially visible object while reflecting uncertainty about its exact shape through predictive variance in depth estimation. Overall, NeRF-VAE demonstrates significant advancements in 3D scene generation and reconstruction.
