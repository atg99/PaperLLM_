The paper presents NeRF-VAE, a geometry-aware scene generative model that integrates NeRF as a decoder within a variational autoencoder (VAE) framework. This model is characterized by its view-consistency and ability to generalize across different camera settings, which distinguishes it from convolutional models like GQN. NeRF-VAE effectively infers scene structure from limited views due to its learned prior over scene functions, overcoming the limitations of NeRF where insufficient views can lead to inconsistent scene interpretations.

Despite its advantages, NeRF-VAE has limitations, particularly regarding its per-scene expressivity. While NeRF dedicates its capacity to a single scene, NeRF-VAE divides its capacity between shared across-scene information and per-scene latent information, limiting the expressivity for individual scenes. However, the low-dimensional latent variable presents opportunities for future research, including scene interpolation, dynamic scene extensions, and adaptive latent representations.

In conclusion, while NeRF-VAE shows promise in geometry-aware scene generation, its capacity limitations necessitate further exploration to enhance its expressivity and adaptability.
