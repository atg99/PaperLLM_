The paper discusses the ongoing challenges in diagnosing and treating metastatic breast cancer (MBC), which remains the most common malignant cancer globally and a leading cause of cancer-related death in women. Despite advancements in diagnostic and therapeutic methods, approximately 10 to 50% of breast cancer patients may eventually experience metastasis, complicating prognosis and treatment due to heterogeneity in primary tumor subtypes. Accurate diagnosis, particularly through the staging of breast cancer and the identification of axillary lymph node (ALN) metastases using sentinel lymph node (SLN) biopsies, is crucial yet hindered by subjective evaluation techniques reliant on pathologists.

The paper highlights the evolution of digital pathology as a solution to these issues, emphasizing the benefits of advanced scanning technologies and the potential for remote consultations. However, challenges remain, including the need for manual inspections and the limited availability of well-trained pathologists and advanced equipment, particularly in developing countries. 

To improve diagnostic accuracy for metastatic cancer detection, the paper proposes employing analytic approaches such as deep learning and machine learning, specifically focusing on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). CNNs have shown promise in extracting features from medical images, including those used in cancer diagnosis. The paper underscores the potential of the EfficientNet architecture, which utilizes a compound scaling method to enhance performance while minimizing computational demands. Despite its advantages, there is a noted gap in research regarding EfficientNet's application to medical images, which motivates the study's exploration of this area, particularly addressing the issue of image resolution disparity that affects CNN performance in medical imaging.
