The paper discusses the challenges of applying convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to small resolution medical images and proposes strategies to enhance the performance of the EfficientNet model for breast cancer (BC) diagnosis. Traditional cropping methods often lead to the loss of critical features, especially since cancer cell indicators are typically concentrated in the center of images. To address these issues, the authors introduce three main strategies: a new cropping method called Random Center Cropping (RCC) that preserves image resolution and important features, reducing the downsampling scale of EfficientNet to better accommodate small resolution images, and incorporating attention and feature fusion mechanisms to enrich semantic information.

The study's contributions include being the first to explore EfficientNet for metastatic breast cancer classification, the introduction of RCC for improved data augmentation, and technical enhancements that significantly boost EfficientNet's performance. The results indicate that the boosted EfficientNet-B3 model achieves an accuracy of 97.96% and an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 99.68%, demonstrating its effectiveness in utilizing CNNs for BC diagnosis.

The benchmark dataset used is the Rectified Patch Camelyon (RPCam), where the performance of the boosted EfficientNet is compared to other state-of-the-art models like ResNet50 and DenseNet121. The findings reveal that while the basic EfficientNet may perform well on training data, it tends to overfit, whereas the boosted model mitigates this issue through enhanced training diversity. The boosted model shows significant improvements in accuracy, AUC, sensitivity, specificity, and F1-Measure, confirming the advancements made in the study.
