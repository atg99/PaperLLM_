The paper discusses the impact of various strategies on the performance of the EfficientNet model in cancer classification tasks. It highlights the effectiveness of Random Center Cropping (RCC), Reducing Downsampling Scale (RDS), Feature Fusion (FF), and Attention mechanisms in enhancing feature representation and performance metrics such as Accuracy (ACC) and Area Under the Curve (AUC).

1. **Influence of Random Center Cropping (RCC)**: RCC significantly improves model performance, as evidenced by an increase in AUC from 99.24% to 99.54% and ACC from 97.01% to 97.57%. This enhancement is attributed to increased diversity in training images, which helps mitigate overfitting.

2. **Influence of Reducing Downsampling Scale (RDS)**: RDS yields modest performance improvements (0.35% in ACC and 0.19% in AUC) by providing larger feature maps. The lower resolution of the RPCam dataset compared to EfficientNet-B3's input requirements results in smaller features that can hinder performance. RDS shows improved results when combined with RCC.

3. **Influence of Feature Fusion (FF)**: FF, which integrates low-level and high-level features, demonstrates significant performance boosts with the highest AUC and a notable increase in ACC. The combination of FF with RCC and RDS leads to the highest ACC and a strong AUC outcome, showcasing FF's adaptability.

4. **Influence of Attention Mechanism**: The attention mechanism is crucial for enhancing the model's focus on cancerous tissues while suppressing background noise. The integration of attention improves both ACC and AUC metrics. When combined with RCC, FF, and RDS, attention results in notable performance gains, increasing ACC from 97.59% to 97.85% and AUC from 99.58% to 99.68%. The minor performance difference between models using all methods and those using RCC, RDS, and FF alone can be attributed to RDS's retention of low-level information, similar to the effects of FF and attention.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of these strategies in improving the performance of cancer classification models, with particular focus on the synergistic effects of combining them.
