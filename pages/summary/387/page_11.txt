The paper discusses the development and validation of a boosted EfficientNet-B3 model for the digital diagnosis of metastatic breast cancers (MBCs). The model's performance was assessed using five indicators: Area Under the Curve (AUC), Accuracy (ACC), Sensitivity (SEN), Specificity (SPE), and F1-Measure, all derived from True Positive (TP), True Negative (TN), False Positive (FP), and False Negative (FN) values. AUC serves as a comprehensive measure of model performance, with higher values indicating better outcomes.

The implementation utilized the PyTorch deep learning framework, leveraging four GTX 2080Ti GPUs for training over 30 epochs with the Adam optimizer. Data augmentation techniques, including a random cropping method (RCC) and image flipping, were employed to enhance dataset diversity while preserving image resolution. The initial learning rate was set at 0.003, with adjustments made at specific epochs to optimize training.

The study concludes that the proposed boosted EfficientNet architecture effectively addresses the challenge of small image resolutions in medical imaging, demonstrating significant performance improvements over traditional models such as ResNet50 and DenseNet121. The findings suggest that these methods may also enhance diagnostic capabilities in other medical conditions and models in the future.
