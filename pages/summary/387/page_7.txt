The paper discusses the enhancement of the EfficientNet model for classifying lymph node metastases in breast cancer using the Rectified Patch Camelyon (RPCam) dataset. It identifies challenges related to the downsampling scale, as the RPCam dataset has a lower resolution compared to standard datasets, leading to potential loss of low-level features essential for accurate classification. To address this, the authors propose reducing the downsampling scale and integrating an attention mechanism via a Squeeze-and-Excitation block, which focuses on the most informative central features of images.

The study highlights the significance of combining low-level and high-level features through a technique known as Feature Fusion (FF) to improve classification accuracy, especially given the complex texture contours present in RPCam images. Experimental results demonstrate that the modified EfficientNet-B3 model outperforms other architectures like ResNet50 and DenseNet121, while also mitigating overfitting issues.

The authors acknowledge limitations, including the exclusive use of the RPCam dataset and the potential for improved classification with multiple training sources. They also suggest that their model could be adapted for other biomedical diagnostic applications with further modifications. Future work may explore different feature fusion strategies and attention mechanisms to enhance performance further.
