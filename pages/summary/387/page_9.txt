The paper discusses the architecture and enhancements of the boosted EfficientNet-B3 model, focusing on its ability to classify images effectively. The main building block of this architecture is the MBConv layer. Initially, images are processed through several convolutional layers to extract features. An attention mechanism is then employed to enhance the significance of relevant features while suppressing irrelevant background information. The model also utilizes a feature fusion (FF) mechanism to maintain low-level information during classification.

To improve performance, the authors adjusted the downsampling scale by modifying the stride of the convolution layer. After conducting experiments with various downsampling scales, they found that a scale of 16 yielded the best results, leading to a feature map size of 6x6, which is larger than the original scale of 32.

The attention mechanism is further explained through a Squeeze-and-Excitation block. This involves a Squeeze operation to pool global features, followed by an Excitation operation that learns channel weights using fully connected layers. The output is obtained by multiplying these learned weights with the original feature maps.

The FF technique consists of four steps: saving features from specific convolutional layers, applying the attention mechanism to these features, combining low-level and high-level features, and classifying the fused features. This integrated approach aims to enhance the model's classification accuracy by effectively utilizing both attention and feature fusion mechanisms.
