The study presents a boosted version of the EfficientNet-B3 model, achieving an impressive accuracy of 97.96% ± 0.03% and an AUC value of 99.68% ± 0.01%. This performance indicates that the model could serve as an efficient, reliable, and cost-effective tool for medical institutions in relevant domains. The authors, J.W. and H.Z., were instrumental in the conception and design of the experiments, while the data analysis was a collaborative effort involving all authors. The research did not receive external funding, and there were no conflicts of interest declared. Data related to the research is available upon request. The paper references various studies related to breast cancer detection and treatment methodologies, emphasizing the relevance and potential impact of their findings in the field of medical diagnostics.
