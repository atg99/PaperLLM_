The paper discusses the performance of the Boosted EfficientNet-B3 model in cancer detection using the Rectified Patch Camelyon (RPCam) dataset. The results indicate that Boosted EfficientNet-B3 surpasses other architectures, such as ResNet50 and DenseNet121, in various metrics including accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and F1-Measure, while utilizing fewer parameters and computational resources. Specifically, the model's accuracy improved from 99.24% to 99.68% on the testing dataset, and it showed more than a 1% increase in sensitivity, specificity, and F1-Measure compared to its baseline.

The paper also highlights the overfitting issues faced by ResNet50 and DenseNet121, reinforcing the effectiveness of the EfficientNet-B3 architecture. To further enhance performance, the authors implemented four strategies: Random Center Cropping (RCC), Reduce the Downsampling Scale (RDS), Feature Fusion (FF), and Attention mechanisms. Ablation studies demonstrated that these strategies significantly enhance the model's generalizability and accuracy, particularly RCC, which helps retain critical information from the dataset.

The findings confirm the potential of Boosted EfficientNet-B3 and its strategies to improve state-of-the-art backbone networks, suggesting future applications and expansions of these methods in cancer detection tasks.
