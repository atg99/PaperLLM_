The paper discusses the Rectified Patch Camelyon (RPCam) dataset, which consists of digital histopathology images of lymph node sections from breast cancer. These images are 96 by 96 pixels in size and contain three RGB color channels. The dataset includes binary labels indicating the presence (1) or absence (0) of breast cancer metastasis, with an imbalance of 130,908 positive images and 89,117 negative images. Key pathological features for cancer classification are located within a central 32 by 32 pixel area of the images.

To enhance the dataset and improve model generalization, the authors implement a Random Center Cropping (RCC) technique. This process involves initially padding each image with 8 pixels, enlarging the image to 112 by 112 pixels. Random cropping is then applied to restore the image to 96 by 96 pixels while ensuring the integrity of the critical 32 by 32 pixel central area is preserved. This method aims to provide informative patches for training while reducing background noise, thereby potentially enhancing the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) used for feature extraction and cancer detection.
