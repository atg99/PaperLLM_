The first strategy, Random Center Cropping (RCC), is designed to retain the integrity of critical central areas in images while simultaneously enriching the dataset by randomly selecting peripheral pixels. This approach ensures that essential features are not compromised and allows for deeper network architectures to be employed.

The second strategy involves reducing the downsampling scale to better align with the lower resolution of the RPCam datasets, which is significantly smaller than typical image inputs used in other models. This adjustment is critical as it helps maintain more low-level features during the feature extraction process, which is vital for accurate detection.

Attention mechanisms and feature fusion (FF) are the other two strategies proposed to further improve accuracy. These methods focus on enhancing the model's ability to discern discriminative features relevant to metastasis, thereby refining the detection process.

The study emphasizes that while previous works have primarily utilized large resolution datasets for breast cancer mass detection, its approach is novel in that it targets lymph node metastases in a lower resolution context. The research aims to leverage the capabilities of EfﬁcientNet in this new application, potentially paving the way for improved diagnostic tools in breast cancer detection and treatment.
