The paper "Theory of Deep Convolutional Neural Networks: Downsampling" by Ding-Xuan Zhou addresses the theoretical underpinnings of deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs), particularly focusing on their approximation capabilities. The motivation stems from the successful applications of deep learning across various fields, necessitating a robust theoretical framework for structured deep neural networks.

The author introduces a downsampling operator to mitigate the challenges associated with analyzing networks that increase in width due to convolutions. This downsampling technique is shown to effectively approximate ridge functions, suggesting that DCNNs possess advantageous modeling capabilities. Furthermore, the paper establishes that the output of any multi-layer fully-connected neural network can be represented by a downsampled DCNN with comparable free parameters, indicating that the approximation ability of DCNNs is at least on par with that of fully-connected networks.

Additionally, the paper presents a theorem for approximating functions on Riemannian manifolds, which illustrates that DCNNs can effectively learn manifold features from data. Overall, this research contributes to the understanding of DCNNs by providing a theoretical foundation for their approximation abilities and highlighting their structural advantages in deep learning applications.
