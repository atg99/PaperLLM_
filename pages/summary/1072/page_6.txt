The paper discusses the construction and approximation capabilities of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in representing functions, particularly ridge functions. It begins with a mathematical formulation where the last layer of the network is constructed based on previous layers using specific filter lengths and operations defined by matrices and bias vectors. The function approximation is approached using a well-known scheme from approximation theory, which allows for estimating errors in approximation.

Key points include the establishment of the relationship between the output of the network and the input through the use of bias vectors and filter masks, which are chosen to ensure that the network can approximate target functions effectively. The paper demonstrates how to derive bounds on the total number of computation units and free parameters required for achieving a desired approximation accuracy.

Additionally, the paper highlights that the parameters can be optimized based on the properties of the input data, specifically mentioning the potential advantages of utilizing sparse characteristics in the input for better performance in approximating ridge functions.

The final sections introduce the realization of fully-connected networks through deep CNNs, emphasizing the connection between inner products and convolutional factorizations, which facilitates the representation of complex functions in a more structured manner.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive examination of how deep CNN architectures can be structured and optimized for effective function approximation, with specific mathematical underpinnings supporting the theoretical claims.
