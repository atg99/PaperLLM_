This paper by D.-X. Zhou presents significant results regarding the approximation capabilities of downsampled deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs). The main findings can be summarized as follows:

1. **Composed Mapping**: The paper introduces a composed mapping \( A_{q,p}^{w,b} \), which is utilized to express the last layer of a downsampled DCNN. This mapping facilitates the understanding of how the network processes input data through multiple layers and downsamplings.

2. **Hypothesis Space**: The hypothesis space \( H_{w,b,J,s} \) is defined, which encompasses functions generated by the DCNN structure, indicating the network's ability to model a variety of functions based on its design and parameters.

3. **Ridge Function Approximation**: The first major result demonstrates that DCNNs can effectively approximate ridge functions of the form \( g(\xi \cdot x) \), where \( \xi \) is an unknown feature vector and \( g \) is an univariate function. The theorem states that if \( g \) is Lipschitz continuous, the approximation error can be controlled, with specific parameters influencing the accuracy.

4. **Dimension-Free Rates**: The paper highlights that the approximation rates achieved by DCNNs do not depend on the dimension of the input space, contrary to findings in approximation theory for fully-connected networks. This suggests that DCNNs may be more efficient in certain contexts, particularly for ridge functions.

5. **Realizing Fully-Connected Networks**: The second key result establishes that any output function from a deep fully-connected neural network with ReLU activations can be replicated by a downsampled DCNN. This finding reinforces the idea that DCNNs possess at least the same approximation capacity as fully-connected networks.

6. **Application to Riemannian Manifolds**: The final result extends the approximation capabilities of DCNNs to functions defined on Riemannian manifolds, showing that the rates of approximation depend on the manifold's dimension rather than the ambient space.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the advantages of DCNNs, particularly in terms of their approximation capabilities and efficiency when compared to traditional fully-connected neural networks. The results suggest potential applications in various fields, including machine learning and functional approximation.
