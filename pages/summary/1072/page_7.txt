The paper presents a proof of Theorem 2 using an inductive construction method for deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs). It starts with the input layer and assumes the existence of filter masks and bias vectors for the previous layer. The construction of the current layer involves creating a specific sequence of filter masks from a convolutional matrix, ensuring that the output matches the desired fully connected network structure.

The proof elaborates on the process of defining the sequence of weights and applying a lemma to establish the existence of the necessary filter masks. It ensures that the parameters used in the DCNN are bounded, completing the induction proof for Theorem 2.

Furthermore, the paper utilizes Theorem 2 to establish Theorem 3, which addresses the approximation of functions on Riemannian manifolds using a three-layer neural network. This part references prior work that describes the construction of the network and its performance metrics, including bounds on approximation errors. The authors conclude that the total number of free parameters and network width necessary for achieving a specific approximation accuracy can be quantified, thereby proving Theorem 3.
