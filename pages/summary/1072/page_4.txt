This paper discusses the theoretical framework and approximation capabilities of downsampled Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNNs). It presents a model with a specific architecture characterized by depth J, downsampling, and uniform filter lengths. The authors derive conditions under which the model can approximate functions with a specified accuracy ε. They establish that achieving this accuracy requires a depth of order O((d+mε^(-m/2))^2/s) and free parameters of order O((d+mε^(-m/2))^2), where d is the input dimension and m is a measure of complexity.

The paper compares its results against classical literature on function approximation using shallow and multi-layer networks. It references foundational works that established the universality and efficiency of neural networks, particularly noting developments related to the ReLU activation function. The authors highlight that existing results often assume specific conditions on activation functions, which are not met by ReLU.

Notably, the paper contrasts its findings with those of Yarotsky (2017), demonstrating that DCNNs can achieve similar approximation accuracies with significantly fewer parameters as the input dimension d increases. This is particularly advantageous, as increasing dimensionality typically complicates approximation tasks and requires more complex models.

The paper concludes by discussing the implications of these approximation rates for generalization error bounds in DCNN-based learning algorithms, emphasizing the need to consider the hypothesis space defined by the filter masks and bias vectors, which differs from traditional kernel methods. The analysis of convolutions in DCNNs is set to follow, promising further insights into their operation and efficiency.
