This paper discusses the approximation capabilities of deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) compared to fully connected neural networks. It builds on prior literature from the late 1980s that established the foundational theory of function approximation using neural networks. The paper emphasizes the unique structure of DCNNs, which utilize sparse convolutional filter masks instead of fully connected matrices, thus introducing a more efficient parameterization.

Key contributions of the paper include:

1. **Introduction of Downsampling**: The authors propose a downsampling operation within DCNNs to manage the width of the networks, moving away from a linearly increasing width structure. This is motivated by wavelet literature.

2. **Theorems on Approximation**: The paper presents a theorem demonstrating that DCNNs can better approximate ridge functions than fully connected networks for certain classes of functions. 

3. **Realization of Fully Connected Outputs**: It is shown that the output of a multi-layer fully connected neural network can be replicated by a uniform DCNN, indicating that DCNNs possess at least equivalent approximation capabilities.

4. **Function Approximation on Riemannian Manifolds**: The authors prove that DCNNs can effectively learn features of data represented on Riemannian manifolds, expanding their applicability in various contexts.

Overall, the findings suggest that DCNNs not only match the approximation abilities of fully connected networks but may also surpass them in specific instances, particularly when utilizing structured functions. The incorporation of downsampling further enhances the efficiency of DCNNs in function approximation tasks.
