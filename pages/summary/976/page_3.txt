The paper is structured into several sections, beginning with an introduction to key terminologies in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Natural Language Generation (NLG) in Section 2. Section 3 provides a historical perspective on NLP, highlighting its applications and recent advancements. Section 4 focuses on the datasets utilized in NLP and the various methodologies employed, while Section 5 addresses evaluation metrics and the challenges faced in the field. The paper concludes with a summary in Section 6.

In Section 2, the paper differentiates between two main components of NLP: Natural Language Understanding (NLU) and Natural Language Generation (NLG). NLU is described as the capability of machines to comprehend natural language, enabling them to analyze and extract meaningful information such as concepts, entities, emotions, and keywords. This functionality is particularly relevant in customer service applications, where understanding user-reported issues—whether spoken or written—is essential.

The section also emphasizes the importance of linguistics, which examines the meaning, context, and variations of language. It introduces crucial terminologies across different levels of NLP, starting with phonology, which deals with the systematic organization of sounds in language. The term phonology is derived from Greek, reflecting the study of voice or sound, and is foundational to understanding language structure and meaning in NLP.
