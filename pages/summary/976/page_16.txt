The paper discusses the development and implementation of natural language processing (NLP) systems in the medical field and their applications in various sectors. The lexicon for these systems was created using resources such as MeSH, Dorland's Illustrated Medical Dictionary, and general English dictionaries. The Centre d'Informatique Hospitaliere at the Hopital Cantonal de Geneve has been working on an electronic archiving environment that incorporates NLP features, initially archiving patient records and later adapting the LSP-MLP for the French language. The system, known as RECIT, employs Proximity Processing to analyze medical sentences and convert free text into a language-independent knowledge representation.

Additionally, Columbia University developed MEDLEE, which extracts and encodes clinical information from narrative reports into structured formats. 

The paper also highlights recent NLP projects by various companies. RAVN Systems launched the ACE Powered GDPR Robot, an AI-driven tool designed to facilitate compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) by automating document analysis and data classification. This system helps organizations manage sensitive personal data efficiently and respond to data requests without labor-intensive processes.

Capital One introduced Eno, a natural language chatbot that allows customers to interact via text. Eno offers a human-like conversational experience for inquiries about savings and other banking services, distinguishing itself from other chatbots by prioritizing user privacy and avoiding platforms that may compromise sensitive information. 

Overall, the paper underscores the significance of NLP in enhancing data management and user interaction across different domains, especially in healthcare and finance.
