The paper discusses the issue of ambiguity in natural language, which can arise at syntactic, semantic, and lexical levels. Syntactic ambiguity allows a sentence to be parsed in multiple ways, while semantic ambiguity involves misinterpretation of word meanings. Lexical ambiguity refers to a single word having multiple meanings. Each type of ambiguity can often be resolved through an understanding of the complete context. Various methods to address ambiguity include Minimizing Ambiguity, Preserving Ambiguity, Interactive Disambiguation, and Weighting Ambiguity. Notable research has focused on preserving ambiguity, aligning with the goals of minimizing or removing ambiguity and incorporating statistical elements.

Additionally, the paper introduces Natural Language Generation (NLG), which is the process of creating meaningful text from internal representations. NLG is part of Natural Language Processing and involves four phases: identifying goals, planning how to achieve those goals, evaluating the situation and communication resources, and realizing the plans in text form. It contrasts with language understanding and requires both a speaker (application) and a generator (program) to produce coherent and relevant phrases.
