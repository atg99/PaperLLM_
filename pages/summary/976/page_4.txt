Phonology is defined as the study of sounds in language, focusing on the organization and behavior of these sounds within linguistic systems. It encompasses the semantic role of sound in encoding meanings across various human languages. 

Morphology, on the other hand, examines the structure of words and their smallest meaningful units called morphemes. For instance, the word "precancellation" can be broken down into three morphemes: the prefix "pre," the root "cancella," and the suffix "-tion." Morphemes maintain consistent meanings across different words, allowing individuals to decipher unfamiliar terms. Lexical morphemes are standalone words with meaning, while grammatical morphemes, such as suffixes like "-ed" and "-ing," modify these lexical morphemes. Bound morphemes are categorized into inflectional and derivational types, where inflectional morphemes alter grammatical categories and derivational morphemes change the semantic meaning of words.

At the lexical level, both humans and natural language processing (NLP) systems comprehend the meanings of individual words through various processing methods, including part-of-speech (PoS) tagging. This involves assigning the most probable PoS based on context, allowing for better semantic understanding. Lexical analysis breaks text into components like paragraphs and sentences, aligning words with their appropriate PoS tags. Techniques such as stop word removal, stemming, and lemmatization enhance text analysis by focusing on meaningful content. Stop words, which are frequent and carry little meaning, are removed, while stemming reduces words to their root forms, and lemmatization retrieves the original form of words without removing suffixes.
