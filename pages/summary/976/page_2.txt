The introduction of this paper defines language as a system of rules and symbols used for communication, highlighting the role of Natural Language Processing (NLP) in facilitating interaction between humans and computers without requiring specialized knowledge of machine languages. NLP, an intersection of Artificial Intelligence and Linguistics, aims to enable computers to understand and generate human language, encompassing two main components: Natural Language Understanding and Natural Language Generation. Linguistics is described as the science of language, covering various aspects such as phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, and pragmatics. 

The paper emphasizes the contributions of theorists like Noam Chomsky to the field of syntax and outlines key tasks in NLP, including Automatic Summarization, Co-Reference Resolution, Discourse Analysis, Machine Translation, Morphological Segmentation, Named Entity Recognition, Optical Character Recognition, and Part of Speech Tagging. Many of these tasks have practical applications, and while they are interrelated, they are often treated as distinct for ease of use. 

The paper also aims to explore the history of NLP, its advancements, and current projects, while addressing datasets, methodologies, evaluation metrics, and challenges within the field. The objectives are to provide insights into important NLP and NLG terminologies, review historical progress, and discuss ongoing developments and challenges in NLP.
