The paper discusses the evolution of Natural Language Processing (NLP) from the 1990s to the early 2000s, highlighting significant trends and developments. Early work emphasized heavy evaluation methods and user modeling, as noted by Young and Chase and Wahlster and Kobsa. Cohen et al. introduced a compositional theory of tune interpretation, while McKeown showcased the effectiveness of rhetorical schemas in text production. Key topics such as word sense disambiguation and probabilistic networks were also identified as critical areas for future research.

As NLP progressed into the early 2000s, various methodologies emerged to tackle the complexities of natural language data. Neural language modeling became prominent, with Bendigo et al. proposing feed-forward neural networks to predict the next word based on previous words. Collobert et al. introduced multitask learning for parts-of-speech and named entity recognition, while Mikolov et al. advanced word embeddings to improve text representation beyond traditional sparse models.

The introduction of neural networks marked a transformative phase for NLP, particularly through sequence-to-sequence frameworks proposed by Sutskever et al. These innovations significantly enhanced NLP capabilities, leading to applications of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in tasks such as sentiment analysis, text classification, and machine translation. The paper underscores the growing importance of neural networks in NLP, noting that discussions and implementations of these technologies gained momentum by 2013, fundamentally reshaping the field.
