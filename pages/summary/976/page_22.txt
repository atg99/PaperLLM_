Naive Bayes is a probabilistic algorithm grounded in Bayes' Theorem, used for predicting text tags such as in news articles or customer reviews. It calculates the probability of each tag based on prior knowledge, returning the tag with the highest probability. While Naive Bayes is effective for common NLP tasks like segmentation and translation, it faces challenges, such as the risk of zero probabilities when encountering unseen words in test data.

Hidden Markov Models (HMM) operate by transitioning between states to generate output symbols. Despite the complexity of potential states and symbols, the internal workings remain concealed. HMMs are particularly useful in speech recognition, aligning output sequences with phonemes, and are also applicable in bioinformatics for tasks like multiple sequence alignment. They can be utilized in various NLP tasks, including word prediction and intrusion detection.

Neural networks have revolutionized NLP since 2010 by enabling the learning of multilevel features. They are primarily used for word embedding, allowing words to be represented as vectors that reveal semantic similarities. Applications of neural networks in NLP include information retrieval, text summarization, classification, machine translation, sentiment analysis, and speech recognition. Initial focus on feedforward and convolutional neural networks has transitioned to recurrent neural networks, particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, which effectively capture contextual relationships among words.
