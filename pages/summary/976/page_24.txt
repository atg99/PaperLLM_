The paper discusses the limitations of the BLEU score in evaluating the quality of generated sentences in natural language processing (NLP). It highlights that BLEU treats all words equally, leading to misleading scores when sentences contain similar structures but differ in meaning. To improve evaluation, the paper suggests using a combination of uni-grams, bi-grams, and n-grams, as well as setting limits on word counts based on their appearance in reference phrases to reduce repetition.

Furthermore, the paper introduces the GLUE benchmark, which assesses NLP models across multiple tasks, promoting generalization beyond singular tasks. GLUE consists of nine diverse datasets that evaluate models on various language understanding tasks, such as sentiment analysis and textual entailment. The overall performance score is the average of scores across these tasks, emphasizing the model's ability to generalize knowledge of language.

The paper also addresses the challenges in NLP, including the complexity of contextual words, synonyms, homonyms, and the difficulties posed by sarcasm and irony. These issues complicate the design of algorithms and models, as they require a deeper understanding of language nuances, which remains a significant hurdle in advancing NLP applications.
