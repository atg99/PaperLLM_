The paper discusses various challenges and advancements in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Natural Language Generation (NLG). It highlights the complexities of informal language, idiomatic expressions, and culture-specific terminology, which complicate the development of universally applicable models. The authors emphasize that while models may perform well within specific domains or geographic areas, broader applications face significant hurdles, including misinterpretation of words and phrases across different contexts, such as education, health, and law.

The paper notes the advancements in autocorrect and grammar correction technologies but points out that understanding writer intent, especially with sarcasm and informal language, remains a major challenge. It acknowledges the progress in widely used languages but calls for models that cater to diverse linguistic and technological needs.

Additionally, the paper introduces the emerging field of Visual Commonsense Reasoning, which seeks to enhance NLP by extracting information from visual media. Research efforts, such as those by Wen and Peng, and others, are mentioned as promising avenues for improving cross-media retrieval and understanding.

In conclusion, the paper serves three main objectives: to provide insights into key NLP and NLG terminologies for newcomers, to review the history and recent developments in NLP, and to discuss datasets, methodologies, and evaluation metrics. It aims to motivate both new and experienced researchers to delve deeper into the evolving landscape of NLP and its applications.
