Text categorization involves processing large volumes of data, such as official documents and news articles, and assigning them to predefined categories. Systems like The Carnegie Group's Construe streamline this process by automating categorization tasks, which traditionally required human indexers. Applications of text categorization include routing trouble tickets and filtering email spam.

Spam filtering, a specific application of text categorization, employs various machine learning techniques to classify emails as spam or not. Techniques include Rule Learning, Naïve Bayes, Memory-based Learning, Support Vector Machines, Decision Trees, and Maximum Entropy Models. Naïve Bayes is particularly favored for its effectiveness despite its simplicity. Two primary models used in text categorization are the Multi-variate Bernoulli model, which considers whether words are present, and the Multi-nomial model, which accounts for the frequency of word occurrences.

Information extraction focuses on identifying and extracting relevant phrases from text, such as names, places, and dates, which are essential for various applications. Overall, text categorization and information extraction play crucial roles in managing and interpreting large datasets.
