Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are designed to process sequential data, making them well-suited for applications in natural language processing (NLP), time series analysis, and more. A notable variant of RNNs is Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), which effectively retains important information over longer periods while discarding irrelevant data. Another simplified version, the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), has demonstrated superior performance in various tasks compared to standard LSTMs. 

Attention mechanisms have further advanced NLP by enabling networks to focus on relevant information based on current states and annotations. The introduction of transformers has significantly impacted the field, although they face limitations due to fixed-length context in language modeling. To address this, Transformer-XL was proposed, allowing for the learning of longer-term dependencies beyond fixed word lengths. Additionally, the Compressive Transformer introduces an attentive sequence model that compresses memories for long-range sequence learning. Recent explorations into deep learning applications in NLP, as discussed by Otter et al., highlight ongoing advancements in the field.
