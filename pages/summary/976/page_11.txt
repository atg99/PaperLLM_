This paper discusses the significant advancements in Natural Language Processing (NLP) facilitated by models such as BERT and various tools that have contributed to the field. Key NLP tools include Sentiment Analyzers, Parts of Speech (POS) Taggers, Chunking, Named Entity Recognition (NER), Emotion Detection, and Semantic Role Labeling. 

Sentiment analysis, as described by Nasukawa et al. (2003), involves extracting sentiments related to specific topics through feature term extraction and sentiment association, utilizing a sentiment lexicon and pattern database to evaluate sentiments on a scale from -5 to +5. The paper notes the predominance of tagsets developed for Indo-European languages, highlighting a gap in research for Asian and Middle Eastern languages.

Research has been conducted on creating POS taggers for languages like Arabic, Sanskrit, and Hindi. For example, Zeroual et al. (2017) and Tapswi & Jain (2012) discuss the development of rule-based taggers and treebank techniques for Arabic and Sanskrit, respectively. Diab et al. (2004) applied a supervised machine learning approach with Support Vector Machines (SVMs) to tag parts of speech in Arabic.

Chunking is addressed as a method for extracting phrases from unstructured text, emphasizing the importance of recognizing phrases like "North Africa" as single entities. Various researchers have utilized CoNLL 2000 shared task data for chunking, employing features like words and POS tags.

Named Entity Recognition (NER) is explored as a technique for identifying and classifying entities such as locations and organizations. The challenges posed by internet slang and informal language are noted, with Ritter (2011) proposing a restructured NLP pipeline to improve NER performance in social media contexts.

Lastly, emotion detection is examined, particularly in the context of mixed languages such as Hinglish. Sharma (2016) categorized sentences based on emotions using machine learning techniques to analyze conversational patterns. The paper underscores the evolving landscape of NLP and the necessity for research across diverse languages and informal contexts.
