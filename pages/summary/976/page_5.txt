The paper discusses various levels of Natural Language Processing (NLP), each focusing on different aspects of language understanding. 

At the lexical level, tokenization involves identifying individual words, with stemming reducing words to their root form, while lemmatization aims to return the correct base form based on context. 

The syntactic level follows, where parts of speech (PoS) tagging allows for the grouping of words into phrases and clauses, ultimately forming sentences. This stage analyzes grammatical structure, emphasizing the importance of word order and the retention of stop-words, as altering these can change meaning. It highlights the relationship between words, exemplified by the contrasting meanings of the sentences "Ram beats Shyam in a competition" and "Shyam beats Ram in a competition."

Next, the semantic level focuses on determining the meaning of sentences. Unlike humans, machines require logical structures to understand interactions among words. This level also addresses semantic disambiguation, clarifying words with multiple meanings based on context, such as the word "bark."

Finally, the discourse level extends beyond individual sentences to analyze relationships and coherence across multiple sentences. It examines the logical structure of texts, focusing on elements like anaphora and coreference resolution to maintain meaning and coherence throughout the discourse.

Overall, the paper outlines the hierarchical structure of NLP, emphasizing the importance of each level in achieving accurate language comprehension.
