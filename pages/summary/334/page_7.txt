This paper presents a study on using the Generative Pre-trained Transformer 2 (GPT-2) model for automated program repair (APR) in JavaScript. While existing web-based APR tools focus on specific types of bugs, the authors propose a data-driven approach that emphasizes the importance of patch correctness, ensuring that generated fixes closely match developer solutions. The research involved creating a dataset from GitHub commits, resulting in 18,736 JavaScript files and 16,863 training samples. The GPT-2 model was trained on correctly fixed code snippets to predict patches for 1,559 buggy programs. It successfully repaired 126 programs on the first attempt and generated correct patches in 269 instances with multiple attempts. This suggests that although GPT-2 was designed for natural language processing, it can also learn to code and repair programs. The authors indicate that larger models may yield better results and express intentions to explore this further in future work. The research was supported by various national programs and scholarships.
