The paper by Lajkó et al. details the use of the GPT-2 architecture for automated program repair (APR). It presents a study showing the effectiveness of GPT-2 in generating patches for bugs, quantified through success rates in various candidate patch selections. The results indicate an accumulation of effectiveness across multiple generations of patch candidates, with exact matches (EM) and edit distances (ED) reported for each generation.

The research also discusses existing datasets used in APR, highlighting prominent collections such as Defects4J, ManyBugs, and Bugs.jar, which were utilized to build their training dataset. The paper references notable benchmarks like CodeXGLUE, where other models, including CoTexT, achieved state-of-the-art performance in code repair tasks.

Despite not achieving the highest performance metrics, the authors claim their work is pioneering in applying GPT-2 for this specific task. The paper contrasts the capabilities of GPT-2 with other models like CodeGPT, which excelled in code completion tasks but were not used for APR in prior studies.

The authors acknowledge the broader landscape of APR tools, including GenProg, PAR, and Nopol, which have historically contributed to the field. They emphasize the need for innovative approaches, particularly using generative models like GPT-2, to enhance the automation of bug fixing in software engineering. Overall, the paper contributes to the ongoing exploration of generative models in solving practical programming challenges.
