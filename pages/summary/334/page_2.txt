The paper by Lajkó et al. presents an innovative application of the GPT-2 model for Automated Program Repair (APR) specifically targeting JavaScript, a programming language that has seen increasing adoption for both client-side and server-side development. The authors highlight the absence of prior research using GPT-2 for this purpose, despite the popularity of JavaScript in the programming community.

The background references the CodeXGLUE benchmark established by Tufano et al. in 2019, which has facilitated rapid advancements in model architectures for various programming languages. Current top-performing models in the APR domain include CoTexT, PLBART, and DeepDebug, achieving accuracy rates between 18% and 23%. However, none have utilized GPT-2 for bug fixing tasks.

The research involved mining a dataset of 18,736 bug-fixing commits from GitHub, which was preprocessed and divided into training, testing, and validation sets. The experiments demonstrated that GPT-2 could successfully repair 126 programs on the first attempt and generated correct patches in 269 cases when given multiple opportunities.

Despite being an earlier version of the GPT model, GPT-2 showed promising results, indicating potential for further improvements with more advanced models. The authors acknowledge resource limitations during their experiments, which restricted the exploration of larger models. They emphasize that their training data and methodology are reproducible for future research.

The paper is structured to provide a comprehensive overview of the research, detailing the dataset and model in Section 2, followed by preprocessing and training steps in Sections 3.1 and 3.2, and the patch generation process in Section 3.3. Evaluation and analysis are covered in Section 4, with related work discussed in Section 5, culminating in a conclusion.

The authors have made their dataset and trained models available on GitHub, promoting ongoing research in the field of Automated Program Repair for JavaScript.
