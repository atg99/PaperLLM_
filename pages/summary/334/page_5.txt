The paper discusses the application of the Generative Pre-trained Transformer 2 (GPT-2) for automatic program repair in JavaScript. The authors evaluate the effectiveness of GPT-2 in generating patches for buggy code by analyzing the edit distance between developer-created patches and those generated by the model. They conducted experiments where patches were generated multiple times for each bug, observing that the model successfully produced the correct patch nearly 50 percent of the time in the first generation.

The findings indicate that while the accuracy of the first generated line was approximately 8.08 percent, considering the first five lines increased this to 12.89 percent. However, generating more lines did not significantly improve accuracy, suggesting that excessive generations may not yield new correct patches due to parameter settings in the GPT-2 generation function.

Qualitative evaluations revealed that many generated patches were nearly identical to the expected results, with some differences in whitespace that affected compilability. The model demonstrated an ability to accurately generate complex patches, including for loops and human-readable error messages. The paper concludes that the parameter settings of the GPT-2 model, particularly the number of generations and line outputs, can be optimized for better developer supervision and manual evaluation of generated patches. The authors have made their results available in a GitHub repository for further exploration by researchers.
