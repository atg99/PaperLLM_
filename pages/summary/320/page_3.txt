The paper introduces a novel model named VLNœBERT, which integrates vision and language for navigation tasks, specifically addressing the challenges in tasks like REVERIE. The architecture leverages a multi-layer Transformer to encode both linguistic instructions and visual observations, enabling the agent to navigate through an environment based on natural language commands. 

The navigation task is defined as requiring the agent to interpret a sequence of instructions while observing its surroundings and deciding on actions to transition between states. The state representation includes navigational history and spatial positioning, allowing the agent to navigate a connectivity graph effectively.

The paper revisits the BERT model, highlighting its capability of capturing dependencies within language through a multi-head self-attention mechanism. The self-attention process involves transforming input features into queries, keys, and values, which are processed to derive attention outputs that are then concatenated and projected to maintain dimensional consistency.

Overall, VLNœBERT aims to seamlessly integrate navigation and remote referring expression tasks within a single framework, enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of vision-and-language navigation systems.
