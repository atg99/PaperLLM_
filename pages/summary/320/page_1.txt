In this paper, we introduce VLNœBERT, a recurrent vision-and-language BERT model specifically designed for navigation tasks. Our approach integrates a recurrent function into the BERT architecture to maintain cross-modal state information, allowing the model to effectively manage history-dependent attention and decision-making processes. This innovation enables VLNœBERT to operate within the constraints of a partially observable Markov decision process, crucial for navigating complex environments based on human instructions.

Through extensive experiments on the R2R and REVERIE datasets, we demonstrate that our model achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming more complex encoder-decoder frameworks. Additionally, the versatility of our approach allows it to be generalized to other transformer-based architectures, facilitating pre-training and enabling the simultaneous resolution of navigation and referring expression tasks.

In summary, VLNœBERT represents a significant advancement in the application of vision-and-language BERT models to navigation tasks, addressing the unique challenges posed by partially observable environments and improving upon existing methodologies in the field.
