The paper presents an enhancement to Vision-and-Language BERT (V&L BERT) by incorporating recurrence to better handle time-dependent inputs, particularly for visual language navigation (VLN) tasks. The authors conduct ablation studies to evaluate the impact of different configurations of V&L BERT on navigation performance, comparing baseline models and various modifications across seen and unseen validation scenarios. Results indicate that the proposed model, VLNœBERT, achieves state-of-the-art performance on the R2R and REVERIE datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in learning navigation skills.

Key findings include that models initialized with pre-trained weights (OSCAR and PREVALENT) converge faster and achieve higher success rates than randomly initialized models. The paper emphasizes the advantages of pre-trained visiolinguistic knowledge for enhancing navigation learning, with VLNœBERT showing significant improvements in training efficiency and performance metrics such as SPL (Success weighted by Path Length). 

The authors conclude that the integration of recurrence into V&L BERT allows for improved handling of partially observable Markov decision processes, facilitating better learning of downstream tasks. They suggest future work could extend the model's application to various navigation scenarios and tasks requiring sequential decision-making, such as dialog navigation and action anticipation in robotics.
