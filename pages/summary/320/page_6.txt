This paper presents a comparison of various agents' performances on the R2R and REVERIE benchmarks, specifically focusing on navigation and remote referring expression tasks. The results are summarized in two tables, highlighting key metrics such as Success Rate (SR), Oracle Success Rate (OSR), and Success Path Length (SPL) across different validation and test splits.

In the R2R benchmark, the proposed VLNœBERT model, initialized from OSCAR, outperforms previous methods across all dataset splits, demonstrating significant improvements compared to both random initialization and other fine-tuned models like PRESS and PREV ALENT. The findings indicate that leveraging pre-trained general vision-linguistic knowledge substantially enhances navigation learning, leading to a notable increase in both SR and SPL metrics.

For the REVERIE benchmark, VLNœBERT also exhibits improved generalization to unseen data, with SR for navigation and object grounding increasing significantly in comparison to previous models. The results suggest that the model effectively balances accuracy and efficiency in navigation tasks, while also showcasing a reduced performance gap between unseen validation and test splits, indicating stronger generalization capabilities.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of pre-training and recurrent architectures in enhancing the performance of navigation agents, particularly in complex environments. The results advocate for the effectiveness of integrating pre-trained models directly into navigation networks rather than solely using them as feature extractors.
