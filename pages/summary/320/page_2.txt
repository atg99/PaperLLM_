In this paper, the authors address the challenges of memory consumption and computational efficiency in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) tasks by introducing a novel model called VLNœBERT. This model is designed to operate effectively on long visual and textual sequences during training without incurring excessive GPU memory usage. The key innovation of VLNœBERT lies in its recurrent architecture, which enhances the ability to learn from history-dependent state representations without relying on external memory buffers or recurrent modules like LSTMs.

To optimize memory usage, VLNœBERT employs a self-attention mechanism that treats language tokens as keys and values while excluding them as queries during navigation. This approach is inspired by the cross-modality encoder used in LXMERT, allowing the model to be trained on a single GPU without compromising performance. Furthermore, VLNœBERT retains the multi-task learning capability of its predecessor V&L BERT models, making it adaptable for various vision and language tasks beyond navigation.

The performance of VLNœBERT was evaluated using two distinct datasets: R2R and REVERIE, which differ in visual clues, instructions, and objectives. The model, initialized from a pre-trained V&L BERT and fine-tuned on these datasets, achieved state-of-the-art results. Notably, on the R2R test split, it improved the Success Rate by 8% and obtained a Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) of 57%. In the REVERIE dataset, it achieved 23.99% navigation SPL and 13.51% Remote Grounding SPL, demonstrating its strong generalization capabilities and potential for integrating VLN with other vision and language tasks.

The paper also reviews related work in the field of VLN, highlighting various approaches that have been explored to enhance visual-textual correspondence and navigation efficiency. Previous methods include cross-modal attention techniques, self-supervised learning strategies, and multi-task navigation models. The authors emphasize the significance of their recurrent integration into BERT, which distinguishes VLNœBERT from prior models by enabling direct training for navigation tasks.

In conclusion, VLNœBERT represents a significant advancement in the integration of recurrent mechanisms within vision-and-language models, offering improved efficiency and performance for navigation tasks while maintaining the versatility to address additional challenges in the field.
