This paper presents a novel model, VLNœBERT, designed for visual language navigation (VLN) and referring expression grounding (REF). The model achieves notable performance metrics, including 24.62% success rate (SR) and 19.48% success per location (SPL) for navigation, and 12.65% RGS and 10.00% RGSPL for REF, surpassing previous benchmarks. Notably, the model's performance improves significantly on test splits, indicating the effectiveness of a BERT-based approach for multi-task learning in VLN and REF.

The authors argue that while prior methods exhibited higher object success rates (OSR) likely due to longer search times, they struggled with lower SR, suggesting inefficiencies in navigation stopping points. The findings indicate that the pre-trained model, PREV ALENT, which was trained on lower-level instructions, positively impacts navigation and object grounding tasks, possibly due to structural priors aiding in learning.

Visualization of language attention weights reveals that VLNœBERT effectively tracks navigation progress, with attention shifting from the beginning to the end of instructions as the agent moves. This suggests a strong alignment between state representation and instruction completion.

An ablation study highlights the impact of various network components on performance. The integration of V&L BERT significantly enhances the model's capabilities, particularly when fine-tuned for navigation. The study shows that simply using pre-trained V&L BERT does not fully exploit its potential, emphasizing the importance of learning recurrent structures within the model. Additionally, using averaged visual attention weights and enhancing state representation through visual-textual matching contributes to improved outcomes.

The paper also addresses the challenges of applying BERT for self-attention due to high memory costs. It demonstrates that re-attending language features at each step increases memory consumption and diminishes performance, as it dilutes valuable information from the entire instruction set. Overall, the results advocate for a multi-functional framework that leverages pre-trained models while maintaining efficient resource use during training.
