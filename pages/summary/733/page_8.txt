The paper presents an upper bound on the performance of the policy \(\pi_{\text{ALG}}\) returned by PACLE, involving three key components: optimization error, uncertainty error, and model mis-specification error. The optimization error \(C(T)\) decreases with iterations and is defined as \(C(T) = 4Hq \log|A| / T\). The mis-specification error \(E_{\text{msp}}(\nu)\) is the sum of stage-wise errors influenced by the choice of radii for the critic. The uncertainty error \(U(\pi; \alpha)\) is defined in terms of cumulative covariance matrices and reflects the amount of information from the dataset in relation to the policy.

The main result, Theorem 1, provides a guarantee under Assumption 1 regarding the data collection process. It establishes that after a specified number of rounds, the PACLE procedure yields a policy \(\pi_{\text{ALG}}\) such that the difference in value between \(\pi_{\text{ALG}}\) and any comparator policy \(\pi\) is bounded by the uncertainty term, the mis-specification error, and the optimization error, with high probability.

This guarantee highlights a trade-off between the sub-optimality of the comparator policy and its associated uncertainty, indicating that while the optimization error can be minimized, the uncertainty increases logarithmically with the number of iterations. Notably, the guarantee remains valid for all stochastic policies, showcasing the robustness of PACLE compared to previous work, which typically focused solely on the optimal policy.
