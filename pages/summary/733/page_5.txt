In this section, the paper explores various assumptions regarding action-value function classes, laying the groundwork for the Bellman restricted closedness assumption, which is central to the analysis. The discussion begins with the least restrictive assumption, which posits that the Markov Decision Process (MDP) allows for a linear representation of action-value functions across all policies. However, this linearity assumption is shown to be insufficient for effective learning, as recent studies indicate that it may require an exponential number of samples to outperform a random policy.

To achieve polynomial complexity in sample size and running time, stronger assumptions are necessary. The analysis hinges on bounding the Bellman error in relation to the sup-norm deviation from linearity, leading to the introduction of the Bellman restricted closedness assumption. This assumption evaluates how well the action-value function aligns with the Bellman evaluation operator, facilitating the analysis of least-squares policy evaluation.

Additionally, the paper compares these assumptions to the low-rank MDP assumption, which has been explored in recent research. The low-rank condition is defined by the existence of reward parameters and mappings that satisfy specific criteria across states and actions.

A proposition is presented, illustrating the hierarchical relationship among the three assumptions: low-rank MDPs are a strict subset of those satisfying Bellman restricted closedness, which in turn are a strict subset of linear action-value function MDPs. This nested relationship underscores the increasing restrictiveness of these assumptions.
