In this paper, the authors present a new algorithm for offline reinforcement learning (RL) that employs actor-critic methods while integrating the principle of pessimism. This approach addresses the theoretical gaps in understanding actor-critic methods in offline settings, where learning occurs without further exploration of the environment. The proposed algorithm allows for the use of a broader range of scenarios, specifically when the Bellman evaluation operator is closed concerning the action value function of the actor's policies, extending beyond traditional low-rank Markov decision processes (MDPs).

The authors demonstrate that their algorithm is computationally efficient, requiring only the solution of a series of second-order programs. They establish an upper bound on the suboptimality gap of the policy produced by the algorithm, which is influenced by the data coverage of any comparator policy, whether it is data-dependent or not. This upper bound is complemented by a minimax lower bound that is nearly tight, differing only by logarithmic factors.

The paper also discusses the challenges of offline policy learning, particularly the risk of maximization bias leading to overly optimistic value estimates. The authors argue that incorporating pessimism mitigates this issue by penalizing policies that may be uncertain or misleadingly valued due to statistical errors. They outline two methods for implementing pessimism: penalizing policies that deviate significantly from the dataset-generating policy or penalizing the value functions of poorly covered policies. The authors opt for the latter method in their algorithm.

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding and practical application of offline actor-critic methods in RL, providing theoretical guarantees that enhance the reliability of learned policies.
