This paper introduces a method for incorporating pessimism into the optimization of weight vectors in reinforcement learning. The approach involves perturbing the weight vector \( w_h \) such that \( w_h = \xi_h + b w_h \), with \( \xi_h \) representing a pessimism vector that adheres to a user-defined bound. The critic in the algorithm utilizes a dataset \( D \), a policy \( \pi \), and a set of tolerance parameters and weight radii to solve a convex optimization problem. This problem is characterized by a linear objective and quadratic constraints, making it a specific type of second-order cone program solvable with standard convex solvers.

The algorithm consists of two main components: the actor and the critic. The critic's role is to solve the optimization program to determine the optimal weight vector \( w \). The actor employs a mirror descent algorithm based on Kullback-Leibler divergence to update the policy using an exponentiated gradient rule. This results in a simplified update method when the Q-values from the critic are compatible with the policy space. The paper identifies that this compatibility allows for maintaining a linear action-value function even amidst pessimistic perturbations.

The mirror descent algorithm achieves significant online regret rates, which depend on the learning rate and the specific analysis employed. The results indicate potential for improved regret rates through further research. Overall, the proposed method enhances the efficiency and statistical performance of policy updates in reinforcement learning contexts.
