The paper discusses the challenges of implementing pessimism in function approximation within offline reinforcement learning (RL). Key issues include accurately estimating uncertainty, as underestimating it may not resolve coverage problems while overestimating can lead to overly conservative policies. The introduction of pessimism can complicate the value function class, necessitating additional model assumptions that prior work often oversimplified.

The paper highlights the role of actor-critic methods, which combine policy and value-based approaches, in addressing these challenges. The authors aim to determine whether actor-critic methods provide theoretical advantages in offline RL. Their main contribution is demonstrating that by decoupling policy optimization from policy evaluation, the integration of pessimism becomes more straightforward.

The research focuses on policy learning with linear function approximation in an offline context, utilizing a dataset comprising state-action pairs and corresponding rewards and successor states. The authors allow for a general dependence structure among samples, enabling data collection from various policies or even adversarially.

To find the optimal policy amidst uncertainty, the authors propose an actor-critic procedure that iteratively optimizes a lower bound on the value of the optimal policy. The method involves constructing a family of statistically plausible Markov decision processes (MDPs) for any given policy, which leads to a convex program that provides a lower bound estimate for the value function. This framework then facilitates the maximization of these bounds over a set of policies, effectively integrating the actor-critic methodology where the critic evaluates policies pessimistically while the actor maximizes performance.
