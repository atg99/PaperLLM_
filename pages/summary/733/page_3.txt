This paper presents a computationally efficient implementation of an actor-critic framework utilizing a pessimistic approach in the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). The authors demonstrate that the sequence of estimated policies produced by the actor aligns with online learning guarantees based on a series of pessimistic MDPs identified by the critic. The introduction of pessimism is a focal point, achieved through direct perturbation of the value functions without adding pessimistic bonuses or altering the class of functions and policies. This approach offers the advantages of maintaining existing model assumptions and complexity, enabling the derivation of tight confidence intervals and minimax optimal estimation bounds.

The structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 delves into MDPs and the foundational assumptions for the analysis. Section 3 introduces the Pessimistic Actor Critic for Learning without Exploration (PACLE) algorithm. Section 4 presents the main results, including an upper bound for the PACLE algorithm and a minimax lower bound. Section A outlines the proof of the upper bound, with detailed proofs for both main results provided in the appendices. The paper concludes with a discussion in Section 5.

The notation section summarizes key terms used throughout the paper, including definitions related to Euclidean balls and upper bounds. The background on finite-horizon MDPs is briefly introduced, highlighting the components such as the state space, action set, reward functions, and transition dynamics, which are crucial for understanding the proposed algorithm and its implications.
