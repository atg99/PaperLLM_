The paper discusses the development of the PACLE procedure, an actor-critic method aimed at finding near-optimal policies in the offline reinforcement learning setting. This method introduces pessimism in the value function evaluation by the critic, ensuring a lower bound on the true value function with high probability. The actor maximizes these lower bounds through a form of mirror ascent. A key aspect of PACLE is its use of direct perturbations in a linear function approximation scheme, which avoids additional model assumptions and maintains computational tractability. Unlike previous methods that present computationally intractable algorithms, PACLE involves solving a low-dimensional second-order cone program, making it more efficient.

The paper acknowledges that while the approach is promising, several questions remain for future research, including numerical studies to assess PACLE's practical performance and extending the analysis to more complex function classes. The authors also suggest exploring the application of their ideas to the more challenging exploration setting. The research received support from various NSF grants and other institutions.
