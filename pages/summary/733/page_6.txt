The paper introduces the Pessimistic Actor-Critic for Learning without Exploration (PACLE), focusing on a specific actor-critic algorithm designed for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). The analysis begins by establishing that any assumptions made regarding Bellman restricted closedness also apply to MDPs that meet the more stringent low-rank MDP condition.

The critic component of PACLE, detailed in Section 3.1, aims to generate pessimistic value function estimates for a given policy. Instead of using Monte Carlo methods with importance sampling—which can lead to significant estimation errors due to unknown distributions—the approach utilizes a least-squares temporal difference method. This method is adjusted to provide lower bounds on the true value function, thereby ensuring that the action-value function remains linear and addressing potential statistical errors from a poorly covered dataset.

The critic's goal is to minimize an expression that estimates the value function Vπ(s1) for the policy π at the initial state s1, employing a regression approach that incorporates pessimistic perturbations. These perturbations are introduced through a cumulative covariance matrix, which grows with the number of samples and reflects the information contained in the dataset at each time step. The paper defines norms based on this covariance matrix to facilitate the regression problem solved in a backward manner from time step H to 1.

In summary, the PACLE algorithm aims to provide a reliable framework for policy evaluation and action selection in MDPs, leveraging pessimistic estimates to mitigate the risks associated with exploration in learning scenarios.
