The collection of references highlights key advancements in reinforcement learning, particularly in policy optimization and evaluation within Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). 

Antos et al. (2007, 2008) focus on fitted Q-iteration and Bellman-residual minimization, presenting methods to learn near-optimal policies using limited sample paths. Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis (1995, 1996) provide foundational texts on dynamic programming and neuro-dynamic programming, essential for understanding reinforcement learning frameworks.

Recent works, such as by Bhandari and Russo (2020) and Buckman et al. (2020), explore convergence properties and the significance of pessimism in policy optimization using fixed datasets. Chen and Jiang (2019) discuss information-theoretic aspects of batch reinforcement learning, emphasizing theoretical underpinnings.

Duan et al. (2021) investigate risk bounds and Rademacher complexity in batch settings, while also addressing off-policy evaluation challenges in their earlier work (2020). The theoretical framework for bilinear classes is presented by Du et al. (2021), aiming to enhance generalization in reinforcement learning.

Several studies focus on robust algorithms and regularization techniques, including Farahmand et al. (2016) on regularized policy iteration and Farajtabar et al. (2018) on doubly robust off-policy evaluation. The works of Haarnoja et al. (2017, 2018) contribute to the development of soft actor-critic methods, integrating maximum entropy principles into reinforcement learning.

Overall, this compilation underscores the evolution of reinforcement learning methodologies, highlighting both theoretical advancements and practical implications in policy optimization and evaluation within continuous action spaces.
