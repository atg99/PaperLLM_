The paper discusses the implications of uncertainty in determining optimal policies within a reinforcement learning framework. It posits that if the uncertainty U(π*; α) of the optimal policy π* is large—potentially infinite—then traditional bounds may not be effective. However, if a near-optimal policy π+ exists and is well-represented in the dataset, Theorem 1 ensures that the value of the learned policy can be close to the optimal value with high probability.

The paper contrasts this approach with traditional analyses that only consider the optimal policy, which lack meaningful guarantees. It references similar findings from other works in the literature, highlighting the relevance of near-optimal policies. Theorem 1 also allows for a family of results based on the choice of the critic's radii, which impacts both the approximation power and the complexity of the function class used.

The paper then establishes a lower bound on the performance of any policy, indicating that the uncertainty function U(π; pd) is a necessary component for any learning procedure. It presents Theorem 2, which provides an information-theoretic lower bound for low-rank MDPs under certain assumptions, showing that the results remain tight up to logarithmic factors.

In comparing this work to existing literature, the authors note that their findings improve upon previous results by closing gaps in upper and lower bounds and accommodating a broader class of problems, particularly with the inclusion of low-rank MDPs. The paper emphasizes that the guarantees derived from their analysis are more robust than those provided by prior studies, owing to refined methods for certifying optimality.
