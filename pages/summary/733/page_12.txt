This collection of references comprises significant contributions to the field of reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on off-policy evaluation and policy optimization. 

Jaques et al. (2019) explore off-policy batch deep reinforcement learning, emphasizing the integration of implicit human preferences in dialogue systems. Jiang and Huang (2020) introduce the minimax value interval approach for off-policy evaluation and policy optimization, addressing challenges in these areas. Jiang et al. (2017) discuss PAC-learnability in contextual decision processes with low Bellman rank, contributing to the theoretical understanding of reinforcement learning frameworks.

Jiang and Li (2016) present a doubly robust method for off-policy value evaluation, enhancing the reliability of value estimates. Jin et al. (2021) introduce the Bellman eluder dimension, providing new classes of reinforcement learning problems and sample-efficient algorithms. Jin et al. (2020a) demonstrate provably efficient reinforcement learning with linear function approximation, while Jin et al. (2020b) investigate the efficiency of pessimism in offline reinforcement learning.

Kakade's works (2001, 2003) delve into policy gradients and sample complexity within reinforcement learning. Kallus and Uehara (2019) tackle off-policy evaluation challenges, proposing double reinforcement learning strategies to improve efficiency. Khodadadian et al. (2021) analyze the linear convergence of natural policy gradient algorithms, furthering understanding of optimization techniques.

Kidambi et al. (2020) propose a model-based approach to offline reinforcement learning, while Konda and Tsitsiklis (2000, 2003) provide foundational insights into actor-critic algorithms. Kumar et al. (2019, 2020) focus on stabilizing off-policy Q-learning and introduce conservative Q-learning methods, addressing the stability and reliability of learning processes.

Lan (2021) presents policy mirror descent for reinforcement learning, achieving linear convergence and new sampling complexities. Laroche et al. (2019) emphasize safe policy improvement through baseline bootstrapping, enhancing the safety of learning algorithms. Lastly, Levine et al. (2020) offer a comprehensive tutorial and review on offline reinforcement learning, outlining open problems and future directions. Liao et al. (2020) contribute to batch policy learning in average reward Markov decision processes, expanding the scope of learning methodologies.

Together, these works represent a rich tapestry of research aimed at advancing reinforcement learning, particularly in off-policy contexts, providing both theoretical foundations and practical algorithms.
