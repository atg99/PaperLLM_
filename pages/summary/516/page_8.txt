The paper presents data2vec, a framework for self-supervised learning applicable across speech, vision, and language modalities. It emphasizes the robustness of predicting targets from the average of multiple layers rather than solely the top layer, especially in speech and NLP tasks, while still noting a slight advantage for vision when predicting from multiple layers.

The study highlights the significance of contextualized target representations, revealing that restricting the teacher model's self-attention to a portion of the input during pre-training enhances downstream performance. The findings indicate that larger context sizes correlate with improved accuracy, with the best results achieved when the entire input is accessible.

Additionally, the paper evaluates different features derived from the teacher model as targets. It concludes that the output from the feed-forward network (FFN) yields superior performance compared to the self-attention block, which is attributed to the bias in self-attention features that do not effectively represent the input. The FFN features incorporate information from previous steps, mitigating this bias and leading to better model efficacy.
