data2vec introduces a unified framework for self-supervised learning across speech, vision, and language modalities. The model operates in two modes: teacher and student. In teacher mode, it generates representations of the complete input, which serve as targets for the learning task. In student mode, the model predicts these representations from a masked version of the input. The teacher's weights are an exponentially moving average of the student's weights, allowing for effective learning across diverse input types.

This approach simplifies various modality-specific methods by leveraging continuous and contextualized target representations through self-attention, thus enhancing the richness of the learned representations compared to fixed or local-context targets used in prior works. Experimental results demonstrate that data2vec achieves state-of-the-art performance in multiple modalities, outperforming previous benchmarks in image classification and speech recognition, as well as achieving superior results on the GLUE benchmark for natural language understanding.

The paper also discusses related work in self-supervised learning, highlighting the evolution of techniques in computer vision and natural language processing. It contrasts data2vec with existing methods that focus on visual tokens or isolated input representations, emphasizing its unique capability to predict latent representations that are contextualized and informed by the entire input. This positions data2vec as a versatile and effective model for advancing self-supervised learning across various domains.
