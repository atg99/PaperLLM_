The paper presents data2vec, a self-supervised learning framework applicable across speech, vision, and language modalities. Unlike previous NLP methods that predict discrete tokens, data2vec generates continuous and contextualized representations, allowing for flexibility in target adaptation and context utilization. This approach contrasts with models like BERT, which rely on fixed embeddings.

In the realm of speech, existing models such as wav2vec 2.0 and HuBERT focus on predicting discrete speech units. In contrast, data2vec predicts contextualized latent representations directly, avoiding quantization. The paper also discusses the importance of multimodal representation learning, stating that while data2vec does not engage in multimodal training, it aims to unify self-supervised learning objectives across modalities for enhanced future representations.

The training process for data2vec involves predicting model representations of complete input data from a partially masked view. This is achieved through a two-mode approach: a student model encodes the masked input, while a teacher model, using an exponentially moving average of weights, encodes the unmasked input. The architecture employs the Transformer model, with modality-specific encoding techniques for vision, speech, and text.

In the training phase, parts of the input are masked, and the model is tasked with predicting the original unmasked representations based on the encoded masked sample. This method leverages self-attention to incorporate contextual information, highlighting a key difference from BERT. Overall, data2vec aims to enhance self-supervised learning across various modalities by providing a flexible and context-aware framework.
