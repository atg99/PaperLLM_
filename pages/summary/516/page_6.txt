The paper presents data2vec, a self-supervised learning framework applicable across speech, vision, and language domains. In the speech processing section, the authors pre-train data2vec using 960 hours of Librispeech data, evaluating its performance on automatic speech recognition with varying labeled data amounts from 10 minutes to 960 hours. Results indicate significant improvements in word error rates (WER), especially with limited labeled data, demonstrating that rich contextualized targets enhance performance without needing discrete speech units.

In the audio event classification task using the AudioSet dataset, data2vec achieves a mean average precision (mAP) of 34.5, outperforming other models that utilize similar pre-training and fine-tuning data setups. The training process incorporates techniques such as DeepNorm and linear softmax pooling, contributing to stability and effectiveness.

For natural language processing, data2vec adopts a training setup similar to BERT, pre-training on the Books Corpus and English Wikipedia. This versatile framework exhibits strong performance across different modalities, highlighting its potential for advancing self-supervised learning in diverse applications. Overall, the findings suggest that leveraging contextualized targets during pre-training can lead to improved outcomes in various tasks.
