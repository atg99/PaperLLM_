The paper introduces data2vec, a novel framework designed for self-supervised learning applicable to speech, natural language processing (NLP), and computer vision. The authors argue that while self-supervised learning has advanced significantly across these modalities, existing algorithms are often tailored to individual modalities, leading to specific designs and biases. This fragmentation hinders the development of a unified learning approach.

Data2vec addresses this issue by employing a consistent learning method across different modalities. The framework predicts contextualized latent representations from a masked view of the input data, utilizing a standard Transformer architecture in a self-distillation setup. Unlike traditional methods that focus on modality-specific targets, data2vec aims to capture information from the entire input, resulting in a more holistic representation.

The authors present experimental results demonstrating that data2vec achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on major benchmarks in speech recognition, image classification, and natural language understanding. The paper emphasizes the potential of a unified learning algorithm to simplify multi-modal learning and enhance models' comprehension of the environment.

In conclusion, data2vec represents a significant step toward general self-supervised learning, with the promise of improving the effectiveness of multi-modal models. The authors provide access to models and code to facilitate further research and application in this area.
