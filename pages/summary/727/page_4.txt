The paper discusses a novel approach to Multiagent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) by introducing a Policy Gradient Algorithm called Meta-MAPG. This algorithm extends the previously established Meta-PG framework by incorporating the learning dynamics of other agents, which is crucial for improving adaptation performance in competitive environments.

The authors highlight that prior work, such as LOLA and Meta-PG, simplified the learning dynamics by treating other agents as external factors. This assumption overlooks the sequential dependencies present in MARL, where the policies of all agents interactively influence each other's learning processes. Consequently, this can lead to biased optimization and suboptimal strategies during training.

Through a probabilistic model perspective, the paper illustrates how Meta-MAPG accounts for the impact of a meta-agent's behavior on the future policies of its peers. By doing so, it enhances the adaptation performance of the meta-agent compared to Meta-PG. The authors provide empirical evidence, demonstrating that Meta-MAPG achieves smoother convergence to winning strategies in a stateless zero-sum game, while Meta-PG exhibits performance degradation due to biased updates.

The paper concludes by presenting algorithms for both meta-training and meta-testing, indicating that Meta-MAPG requires centralized access to other agents' policy parameters during training. However, it also outlines a decentralized approach for situations where such access is not feasible. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of integrating peer learning dynamics in MARL frameworks for achieving better performance in complex multi-agent environments.
