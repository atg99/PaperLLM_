The paper presents a novel policy gradient algorithm, Meta-MAPG, aimed at enhancing learning processes in multiagent reinforcement learning (MARL) by directly modeling the interactions and learning dynamics of all agents within a unified objective function. This work builds upon the meta-policy gradient theorem established by Al-Shedivat et al. in 2018, expanding its application to the complexities of multiagent environments where agents continuously adapt.

The authors conduct a theoretical analysis of Meta-MAPG, highlighting an additional term that accounts for the influence of one agent's learning on others, a concept derived from previous research by Foerster et al. This analysis reveals that Meta-PG is a special case of Meta-MAPG under the assumption of independent learning among agents—an assumption that often fails in practice and can lead to detrimental divergences in learning outcomes. The paper addresses this issue through a correction for the meta-gradient, ensuring more effective learning in dynamic environments.

Empirical evaluations across various multiagent domains—including mixed incentive, competitive, and cooperative settings—demonstrate that Meta-MAPG consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods. The results indicate a significant improvement in the ability of agents to adapt when encountering novel agents during the learning process.

Additionally, the paper discusses the inherent non-stationarity in MARL, explaining how the constantly evolving policies of agents create a non-stationary environment from each agent's perspective. This is framed within the context of stochastic games, where interactions among agents are modeled as a Markov chain of joint policies, emphasizing the complexity introduced by each agent's adaptive learning.

Overall, this work provides a theoretically sound and empirically validated framework for advancing multiagent reinforcement learning, paving the way for more robust and adaptive agent interactions in complex environments.
