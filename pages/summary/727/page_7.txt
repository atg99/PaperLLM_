The results indicate that Meta-MAPG maintains its effectiveness in multi-agent settings, demonstrating the algorithm's robustness. In these scenarios, the meta-agent can adapt its strategies based on the behaviors of multiple peers, showing improved performance over baseline methods.

Overall, the paper presents a novel policy gradient algorithm, Meta-MAPG, designed for multiagent reinforcement learning. The findings illustrate that Meta-MAPG can effectively adapt to various environments, manage non-stationarity by adjusting trajectory counts, and perform well with decentralized meta-training. Furthermore, the algorithm shows promising generalization capabilities across different distributions and scenarios, including competitive settings like rock-paper-scissors. The results confirm that Meta-MAPG outperforms traditional methods, showcasing its potential for real-world applications in dynamic multi-agent systems.
