The paper presents a novel policy gradient algorithm called Meta-MAPG designed for multiagent reinforcement learning (MARL), focusing on the concept of "learning to learn." The experimental setup involves a meta-agent that adapts its policy based on the learning dynamics of peer agents, which are initialized from a distribution of policies. The implementation employs LSTM networks for policy adaptation and utilizes generalized advantage estimation along with DiCE for computing peer learning gradients.

The study assesses the performance of Meta-MAPG in various environments, including repeated matrix games and the RoboSumo domain, highlighting the challenges posed by complex observation and action spaces. In particular, the paper investigates the iterated prisoner’s dilemma (IPD) to explore the necessity of accounting for both an agent's learning and that of others. The results demonstrate that the meta-agent effectively infers the behavior of its peers, adapting its strategy to maximize rewards by encouraging cooperation while adjusting to the evolving policies of its counterparts.

The findings indicate that performance tends to decline as joint policy updates increase, as agents learn from each other, often leading to mutual defection. However, the meta-agent's ability to shape the peer agent's future policies towards cooperation allows it to gain an advantage, showcasing the efficacy of considering both personal and peer learning in MARL contexts. Overall, the research emphasizes the importance of adaptive strategies in dynamic multiagent environments.
