Meta-MAPG is a novel meta-learning algorithm designed for multiagent reinforcement learning, focusing on how agents can quickly adapt to changing policies within a shared environment. The performance of Meta-MAPG improves with an increasing number of trajectories between policy updates, demonstrating its capability to handle varying levels of complexity in multiagent settings. 

The algorithm incorporates both own learning and peer learning gradients, which are essential for optimal adaptation. An ablation study showed that omitting either gradient results in suboptimal performance, highlighting the necessity of both components for effective learning. In cooperative environments, such as the 2-Agent HalfCheetah scenario, the influence of peer learning does not significantly enhance performance since agents inherently adjust their policies for mutual benefit due to joint rewards. 

Overall, Meta-MAPG consistently outperforms baseline methods, including Meta-PG, LOLA-DiCE, and REINFORCE, particularly in scenarios with multiple agents. This study provides insights into the dynamics of learning in multiagent systems and the importance of adaptive strategies in non-stationary environments.
