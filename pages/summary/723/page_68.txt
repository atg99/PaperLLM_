The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan discusses the upper bound on the smoothness of a function L with respect to a specific set of parameters. It establishes that by choosing a certain value for T, they can satisfy an inequality that ensures optimality in the context of their analysis. The authors derive that this choice leads to a bound expressed in terms of the size of the state and action spaces, ultimately simplifying to a manageable expression.

In the section concerning the proofs, specifically Lemma 15, the authors build on previous definitions of compatible function approximation. They define an error function L(w) that quantifies the discrepancy in approximating values through a vector w. The minimizer of this function is shown to be proportional to the update direction of the Natural Policy Gradient (NPG). The authors also highlight the implications of their findings for the Softmax policy parameterization, noting that while the derived minimizer may not be unique, it serves as a critical solution to the least-squares problem associated with their analysis.

In essence, the paper rigorously explores mathematical constructs related to function approximation in reinforcement learning, providing insights into optimal parameter choices and the behavior of the associated error functions.
