The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan discusses several lemmas related to iterative processes in a mathematical context. 

In Lemma 49, it is established that for any state s where the set Is is non-empty, there exists an iteration Ta+ for any action a+ in Is+ such that for all t greater than Ta+, the function ϕ(t)(a+ | s) exceeds ϕ(t)(a | s) for all actions a in the set B0(a+). This conclusion is based on the properties of the set B0(a+) and the existence of a specific iteration ta that allows for the desired inequality.

Lemma 50 addresses the behavior of the function ϕ(t)(s; a) for actions in the sets Is+ and Is− as t approaches infinity. For actions in Is+, it is shown that ϕ(t)(s; a) is bounded from below and strictly increasing after a certain time T1. Conversely, for actions in Is−, ϕ(t)(s; a) is strictly decreasing, suggesting that it approaches either negative infinity or a constant limit. The proof employs a contradiction method to demonstrate that if an action in Is− were to remain above a certain threshold for t greater than T1, it would contradict the established behavior of ϕ(t)(s; a).

In summary, the paper rigorously proves properties of iterative processes over defined sets of actions, establishing bounds and convergence behaviors that are crucial for understanding the dynamics of the system being studied.
