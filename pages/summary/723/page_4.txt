In this paper, Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan explore the limitations and advancements in first-order and quasi second-order methods for policy optimization in reinforcement learning. They establish that a certain dependence is unavoidable for first-order methods, even with exact gradients. The authors then focus on the Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) algorithm, presenting a significantly improved iteration complexity result for achieving an epsilon-optimal policy, which is independent of the number of states, actions, or distribution mismatch coefficients. Their proof builds on existing analyses, particularly extending a mirror descent approach to address the non-concavity of the policy optimization problem.

The paper highlights the benefits of the NPG algorithm, particularly its dimension-free convergence rate, which enhances the performance over standard gradient ascent methods. This aspect is especially relevant given the popularity of NPG and its variants, such as the Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) algorithm.

Furthermore, the authors discuss function approximation within policy gradient methods, emphasizing the need for average case guarantees rather than worst-case scenarios inherent in approximate dynamic programming. They review existing literature that provides provable guarantees for these methods, noting the distinction between approaches that utilize problem-dependent parameters and those that work with restricted policy classes. The authors contribute by offering performance bounds with a reduced dependence on density ratios, thus refining the understanding of approximation and estimation errors in the context of the natural gradient method. Their findings underscore the potential of supervised learning techniques in addressing approximation issues in reinforcement learning.
