The section discusses log-linear policy classes and their application in soft policy iteration within the context of policy gradient methods. It defines a log-linear policy for any state-action pair, characterized by a feature mapping. The policy is expressed in terms of the exponential of a linear combination of the features, normalized by a partition function.

The paper introduces compatible function approximation, which provides a centered version of the feature mapping, allowing for a more accurate representation of the policy’s advantage function. The natural policy gradient (NPG) update rule is presented, emphasizing that it minimizes a quadratic error related to the advantage function using the centered features.

A variant called Q-NPG is also introduced, which differs from NPG by not centering the features and instead minimizing the error concerning the action-value function. The authors highlight the relationship between NPG/Q-NPG and incremental soft policy iteration, indicating that both can be viewed as iterative updates to the policy based on minimizing specific error metrics.

The equivalence of NPG and Q-NPG is noted, particularly in scenarios where the compatible function approximation error is zero, resulting in equivalent policy updates. This section underscores the theoretical framework for understanding these policy gradient methods and their iterative nature in improving policies through soft updates.
