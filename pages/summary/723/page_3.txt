The paper discusses the iteration complexities of various policy gradient methods in the context of reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on algorithms that utilize exact gradients in a tabular setting. It presents a summary of different algorithms including Projected Gradient Ascent on Simplex, Policy Gradient with softmax parameterization, and Natural Policy Gradient (NPG), highlighting their respective complexities.

The iteration complexities are expressed in terms of the number of iterations required to achieve a policy that minimizes the difference between the optimal value function and the learned value function for a given starting state. Notably, NPG demonstrates a significant advantage as it does not depend on the distribution mismatch coefficient, which is crucial for convergence.

The paper emphasizes the importance of using a log barrier regularizer to improve convergence rates, particularly in avoiding issues with vanishing gradients near suboptimal policies. This regularization technique is discussed in the context of its practical relevance and its relationship to relative entropy regularization, which is commonly used in practice.

Finally, the authors highlight that the convergence rates of the algorithms depend on the optimization measure's coverage over the state space, as indicated by the distribution mismatch coefficient. This analysis provides insights into the efficiency and effectiveness of various policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning.
