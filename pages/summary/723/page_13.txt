The paper discusses the theory behind policy gradient methods in the context of non-convex optimization. It highlights the importance of finding first-order stationary points, which can indicate near-optimal parameter settings. The authors emphasize that the convergence of algorithms, such as projected gradient ascent, can be assessed by quantifying their rate of convergence to these stationary points.

A key result presented is Lemma 4, which establishes that direct policy parameterization exhibits a notion of gradient domination. This lemma, utilized in the analysis of the policy improvement algorithm CPI, indicates that the performance measure associated with a policy can be optimized under different state distributions. Specifically, it shows that a small gradient magnitude in feasible directions suggests near-optimal performance, provided that the state distribution used for optimization sufficiently covers that of the optimal policy.

The significance of this result is linked to Bellman and Dreyfus's theorem, which asserts the existence of a single policy that optimally addresses all starting states. Additionally, the exploration challenges are encapsulated in the distribution mismatch coefficient, underscoring the complexities involved in achieving optimal policy performance across varying state distributions.
