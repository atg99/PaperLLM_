This paper discusses the theoretical foundations of policy gradient methods within reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on the projected gradient ascent algorithm. The algorithm is defined by the update rule which involves projecting onto a feasible set in the Euclidean norm. Theorem 5 establishes that the algorithm achieves a specific level of performance, quantified in terms of the value function, under certain conditions related to the number of iterations and the dimensions of the action and state spaces.

The authors provide a proof that utilizes established iteration complexity results of projected gradient ascent. They demonstrate that, under the defined conditions, the policy becomes ε-stationary, indicating that the gradients with respect to feasible directions are small. This result assures that the best policy found over a specified number of iterations is close to optimal, emphasizing that the average gradient norms can be leveraged to ensure policy effectiveness.

Moreover, the paper discusses the challenges posed by vanishing gradients and saddle points in the context of sparse-reward Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). It highlights that a policy may exhibit zero gradients even when it is significantly suboptimal, particularly when it fails to visit rewarding states. The authors illustrate this with a specific MDP example where many policies have negligible gradients yet remain far from optimal in terms of value.

In conclusion, the paper underscores the importance of considering the distribution of states when evaluating optimality in policy gradient methods, suggesting that conditions on state distributions are essential for ensuring that stationary policies are indeed optimal.
