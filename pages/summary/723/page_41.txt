The paper presents significant advancements in the theory of policy gradient methods, particularly focusing on function approximation and sample size issues. It introduces the first provable results that allow for average case approximation errors without explicit worst-case dependencies on the state space. The authors characterize an approximation and estimation error decomposition, indicating that effective function approximation hinges on good conditioning related to exploration and low distribution-shifted approximation error. 

The work highlights that employing stochastic projected gradient ascent is sufficient for accurate policy optimization. However, it also points out the need for future research to enhance sample efficiency and address polynomial dependencies through techniques such as variance reduction and data re-use.

Several promising research directions are identified, including the removal of density ratio guarantees in prior algorithms and understanding robust representations against distribution shifts. The authors express hope that combining policy optimization with exploration methods can enhance robustness while reducing the requirement for a well-conditioned initial distribution.
