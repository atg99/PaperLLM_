The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan explores the on-policy approach in reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on the fitting distribution d(t). It allows for a starting state-action distribution, enhancing flexibility in policy optimization. The authors introduce a smoothness assumption on the policy class, defining it in terms of the log probability function of actions given states. They highlight that the tabular softmax policy and log-linear policies exhibit this smoothness under certain conditions.

The authors define a state-action measure based on a comparator policy, emphasizing the need for bounded estimation errors in the NPG algorithm's iterates. They outline two critical types of errors: excess risk, which relates to the boundedness of the estimation error in relation to the optimal policy, and transfer error, which concerns the expected loss of the algorithm's iterates compared to the comparator policy.

The analysis aims to ensure that the estimation errors decrease as the number of samples increases, leading to improved policy performance over time. These findings contribute to understanding the robustness and efficiency of reinforcement learning algorithms in various policy classes.
