The paper discusses the theoretical foundations of policy gradient methods in the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). It begins with a mathematical proposition outlining a bound on the difference between optimal value functions and value functions at specific time steps. The analysis utilizes various lemmas and propositions to derive conditions under which the bound holds, particularly focusing on the relation between trajectory lengths, the discount factor, and the state transition matrix.

A critical aspect of the discussion involves the formulation of the policy in a direct parameterization manner, emphasizing how actions are chosen based on the state. The transition probabilities are defined for different states, detailing how the policy affects the movement from one state to another within the MDP framework. The paper also references specific equations that illustrate the probabilities associated with various actions and states, providing a structured view of how policy gradients operate within the defined MDP.

Overall, the paper aims to establish a rigorous understanding of the mechanics behind policy gradient methods, reinforcing the importance of theoretical bounds and state transitions in optimizing policy performance.
