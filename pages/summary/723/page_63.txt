The paper discusses the theory of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, focusing on the convergence properties of certain gradients. It establishes key results using lemmas that provide insights into the behavior of the value function and the gradients associated with actions in a given state.

One critical point made is that as the denominator approaches one, the maximum of the function approaches infinity, which is significant for understanding the behavior of the policy gradient. The paper also highlights that the sum of gradients equals zero, leading to a relationship between the parameters at different times, indicating stability in the policy's performance over time.

The proof structure is inductive, demonstrating that if a certain condition holds at time t, it continues to hold for subsequent times. This is important for establishing the long-term behavior of the policy being evaluated.

The paper introduces sets that partition actions based on their performance over time, allowing for a clearer analysis of which actions improve or degrade the policy's effectiveness. The results imply that certain actions will consistently lead to better outcomes as time progresses, reinforcing the notion of convergence in policy gradient methods.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous mathematical framework for understanding the dynamics of policy gradients, emphasizing the importance of both the value function and the behavior of action gradients over time.
