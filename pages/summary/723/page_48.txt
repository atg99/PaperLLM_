The section discusses proofs related to first-order optimality in constrained optimization, specifically focusing on the definition of first-order stationarity and its implications. First-order stationarity is defined as a state where the gradient of the value function is negatively correlated with any feasible direction of movement, indicating that the policy is optimal under the given constraints.

The authors present Proposition 37, which establishes conditions under which the projected gradient update leads to a first-order stationary point. The gradient mapping is defined and involves a step size that adjusts based on the smoothness of the value function. The proof leverages results from previous theorems, showing that if the gradient norm is below a certain threshold, the update rule guarantees convergence towards optimality.

Theorem 5 further elaborates on the properties of the gradient mapping, confirming that it maintains smoothness across all states. The results indicate that the convergence rate of the algorithm is related to the difference between the optimal value and the initial value, as well as the number of iterations, establishing a clear link between the mathematical framework and practical optimization outcomes in constrained environments.
