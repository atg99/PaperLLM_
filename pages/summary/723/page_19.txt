The paper discusses the theory behind policy gradient methods, specifically focusing on the concept of approximate first-order stationary points in the context of an entropy-regularized objective. Theorem 12 establishes that if the regularization is sufficiently small, the approximate first-order stationary points can be considered approximately globally optimal.

The theorem outlines conditions under which the value function of a policy, represented as V_π(ρ), approaches the optimal value function V*(ρ) within a bounded difference determined by the regularization parameters. Here, it is crucial to demonstrate that the maximum advantage function A_π(s; a) can be bounded appropriately for all states, which is essential for proving the theorem's claim.

In the proof, the performance difference lemma is employed to relate the value functions and show that the difference between them can be expressed as a function of the maximum advantage across state-action pairs. The analysis then focuses on establishing bounds on the advantage function, particularly when A_π(s; a) is greater than zero. By utilizing the policy gradient expression, the paper derives conditions that lead to the conclusion that the advantage function is appropriately bounded, thus supporting the theorem's assertion.

Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of regularization in achieving optimality in policy gradient methods and provide a rigorous mathematical framework to support these claims.
