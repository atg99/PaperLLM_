This paper discusses the theoretical underpinnings of policy gradient methods, particularly focusing on the Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) approach. It references prior works that have contributed to the understanding of policy gradient methods and their convergence properties, including Bhandari and Russo's global convergence results and Liu et al.'s analysis of the Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) algorithm. Liu et al. integrate concepts from mirror descent and neural network approximation but do not address cases involving incomplete policy classes.

The paper emphasizes the significance of the Q-NPG algorithm, which is closely related to the NPG method but utilizes function approximation for the Q-function rather than advantages. The context is framed within a finite Markov Decision Process (MDP), characterized by finite state and action spaces, a transition model, a reward function, a discount factor, and a starting state distribution.

A deterministic stationary policy is defined, alongside a stochastic policy, and how these policies influence the trajectory distribution is explored. The value function for a policy is introduced as the expected discounted sum of future rewards, setting the foundation for analyzing policy performance within MDPs. The paper provides a comprehensive theoretical framework that aims to bridge gaps in existing literature regarding policy gradient methods and their applications in reinforcement learning.
