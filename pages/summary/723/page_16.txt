The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan discusses the challenges of vanishing gradients in the context of chain Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). Proposition 6 highlights that for specific parameter conditions, the k-th order derivatives of the value function exhibit small norms, indicating that the gradients may vanish at suboptimal parameters. This suggests that existing results on escaping saddle points in non-convex optimization do not guarantee global convergence, particularly when higher order derivatives are small.

Remark 7 emphasizes that in certain scenarios, such as random exploration strategies, sample-based gradient estimates can become zero, leading to numerical instabilities even when exact gradients are computed. Remark 8 clarifies that the lower bound presented does not contradict the upper bound established in Theorem 4, as a distribution mismatch coefficient can be infinite in specific cases, affecting the policy suboptimality bounds.

Remark 9 contrasts this lower bound with information-theoretic lower bounds, indicating that the findings apply specifically to policy gradient approaches. The results underscore that a lack of effective exploration can hinder policy gradient algorithms, as small gradients could result from either near-optimal policies or insufficient visits to beneficial states.

The paper also shifts focus to the softmax policy parameterization, noting that while it generally presents a non-concave optimization problem, global optimality can be achieved under certain regularity conditions. This section reinforces the complexities involved in achieving optimal policies within the framework of MDPs.
