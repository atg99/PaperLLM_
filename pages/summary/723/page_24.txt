The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan discusses the challenges of function approximation in reinforcement learning, specifically in the context of policy optimization. The authors aim to derive agnostic results that perform comparably to the best policy within a given class or another comparator policy. They focus on the policy optimization problem while highlighting the importance of distribution adjustments in optimization, akin to approaches used in tabular scenarios.

The authors introduce a variant of the Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) update rule, detailing how it connects to compatible function approximation as established by previous work. They present a regression problem for finding a minimizer that aids in approximating the advantage function using features derived from the policy’s log probabilities. Additionally, they explore a variant called Q-NPG that utilizes Q-values instead of advantages.

Key findings include the effectiveness of NPG updates amid statistical estimation errors and approximation errors from parameterized function classes. The paper presents a novel decomposition framework for understanding these errors and discusses the implications for both log-linear and more complex policy classes, such as neural networks. Notably, the results offer provable approximation guarantees without explicit worst-case dependencies on the state space, marking a significant contribution to the field. The common policy classes discussed are characterized by differentiable functions, exemplified by the tabular softmax policy.
