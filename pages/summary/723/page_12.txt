The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan introduces the concept of the distribution mismatch coefficient, which quantifies the difficulty encountered by policy optimization algorithms in maximizing a given objective function V. This coefficient is defined as the componentwise division of two distributions, where one represents the initial state distribution and the other measures the sub-optimality of the policy being evaluated. The authors denote this coefficient as dπ(ρ, σ) and often select π to be the policy that maximizes V over a certain class of policies.

The paper also establishes a notation convention for denoting optimal policies and their corresponding values and advantages. The focus shifts to a constrained tabular parameterization, where the authors describe a simple first-order method that employs gradient ascent updates directly on the policy simplex, projecting back onto the simplex if constraints are violated. This method is recognized as projected gradient ascent, with the state-action probabilities serving as parameters.

The authors assert that the value function Vπ(σ) exhibits a Polyak-like gradient domination condition, which is instrumental in deriving convergence rates for the algorithm. They draw parallels to existing literature on non-convex optimization, noting that their approach offers insights into runtime limitations for algorithms that depend on the curvature of saddle points.

In summary, the paper presents a framework for understanding the challenges of policy optimization through the lens of distribution mismatch and gradient domination, providing foundational insights that connect to broader optimization principles.
