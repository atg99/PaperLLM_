The paper presents a theoretical framework for policy gradient methods, specifically focusing on the differentiation of the state-action transition matrix with respect to policy parameters. The transition matrix, denoted as eP(θ), represents the dynamics of the system under a given policy θ. By applying differentiation, the authors derive expressions that relate the gradient of the transition matrix to the derivatives of the policy function.

The key result is that the first derivative of the transition matrix can be expressed in terms of the gradient of the policy and the transition probabilities. This relationship is crucial for optimizing policies in reinforcement learning. The authors also establish bounds on the derivatives of the transition matrix, indicating that the norm of these derivatives can be controlled, which is essential for stability in policy updates.

Furthermore, the paper introduces the Q-function, Qθ(s0; a0), which represents the expected return of taking action a0 in state s0 under policy θ. The Q-function is related to the transition dynamics and the reward structure of the environment, and it is reformulated using the transition matrix and the reward vector.

Overall, the paper rigorously develops the mathematical foundations of policy gradient methods, providing insights into the behavior of the transition dynamics and the implications for policy optimization.
