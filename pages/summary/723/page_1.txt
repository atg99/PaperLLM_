The paper "On the Theory of Policy Gradient Methods: Optimality, Approximation, and Distribution Shift" by Agarwal et al. provides a comprehensive theoretical analysis of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). The authors address fundamental questions regarding the convergence properties of these methods, including whether they can achieve global optimality and how they handle approximation errors when using a limited class of parametric policies.

The study distinguishes between two types of policy parameterizations: "tabular" methods, which guarantee global convergence to the optimal policy, and parametric policy classes such as log-linear and neural networks, which may not encompass the optimal policy. For the latter, the authors present agnostic learning results. A key contribution of the work is the introduction of average-case approximation guarantees, which sidestep the limitations of worst-case scenarios often associated with large state spaces by linking the analysis to supervised learning under distribution shift.

The paper emphasizes the relationship between estimation error, approximation error, and exploration, articulated through a defined condition number. The findings highlight the flexibility and applicability of policy gradient methods in various settings, underlining their significance in the reinforcement learning landscape.
