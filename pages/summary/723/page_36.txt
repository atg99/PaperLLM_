The paper discusses the NPG (Natural Policy Gradient) algorithm, presenting a lemma known as the "regret lemma" that aids in understanding the update rule of NPG. The update rule is formulated as a progression from the current policy parameter to the next, incorporating an arbitrary bounded sequence of weights. The authors define a function as smooth if its gradient difference is bounded by a smoothness parameter, leading to a Taylor series expansion that provides insights into the behavior of the function under updates.

The NPG Regret Lemma considers a comparison policy and a state distribution, with the premise that the log-probability of actions given states is a smooth function. The lemma establishes an error term related to the difference between the expected value of the comparison policy and the updated policy. The results indicate that the minimum regret over time is bounded by a combination of factors including the number of actions, the smoothness parameter, and the error term. The proof leverages the smoothness condition to derive bounds on the regret, confirming that the NPG algorithm can achieve desirable performance under the specified conditions.
