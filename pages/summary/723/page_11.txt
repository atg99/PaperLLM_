The paper discusses the theory behind policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, focusing on key concepts such as state visitation distribution and the performance difference lemma. It defines the discounted state visitation distribution for a policy and presents the general form of the policy gradient. The paper highlights the importance of differentiable parameterization of policies and contrasts two gradient expressions, noting that one is applicable only under certain constraints. Additionally, it introduces the performance difference lemma, which quantifies the difference in value between two policies at a given state. This lemma is a fundamental tool in understanding the behavior of policy gradients and is accompanied by a proof for completeness. Overall, the paper provides a formal framework for understanding how policy gradients can be used to optimize reinforcement learning strategies.
