The paper presents an algorithm for sampling in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and provides an unbiased estimate of the action-value function Q(s, a). The algorithm is structured to begin with a starting state-action distribution and involves sampling state-action pairs over time, with a certain probability of either acting according to a policy or accepting the sampled pair. The termination of the sampling process leads to the calculation of the undiscounted sum of rewards.

A key point discussed is that for "linear" MDPs, as demonstrated in the works of Jin et al. and others, the bias is zero when the features of the linear MDP are utilized. This allows for model misspecification without significant approximation errors in certain cases.

The paper also contrasts the proposed approach with existing algorithms like POLITEX and EE-POLITEX, highlighting that the assumptions made in this work are less stringent, only requiring a good relative condition number for one policy. The analysis further distinguishes between regret bounds in average reward settings and suboptimality bounds in discounted settings.

The section on Q-NPG sample complexity introduces an episodic sampling oracle, which facilitates unbiased sampling of the action-value function. The authors provide a sample-based version of the Q-NPG algorithm that employs stochastic projected gradient ascent. A corollary establishes that under certain conditions, the sample complexity of the Q-NPG algorithm is manageable, with expected sample usage scaling with the number of iterations and gradient steps. The expected length of each episode is also factored into the total sample estimates.
