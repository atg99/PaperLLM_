The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan explores the action-value function Q and the advantage function A in the context of reinforcement learning. The Q-value function is defined as the expected sum of discounted rewards obtained by taking action a in state s, while the advantage function represents the difference between Q and the value function V. The primary objective of the agent is to maximize the expected value from the initial state by optimizing a policy.

The authors reference the Bellman and Dreyfus theorem, which asserts the existence of an optimal policy that maximizes the value function across all states. The study focuses on ascent methods for optimizing the value function over a class of parametric stochastic policies. Three types of policy parameterizations are considered: 

1. **Direct Parameterization**: Policies are represented directly with constraints ensuring non-negativity and normalization across actions.
2. **Softmax Parameterization**: This approach uses the softmax function to convert real-valued parameters into probabilities, ensuring the policy is stochastic.
3. **Restricted Parameterizations**: This includes classes of policies that may not encompass all stochastic policies, such as log-linear and neural policy classes.

The authors emphasize that the value function V is non-concave in the parameters for both direct and softmax parameterizations, which complicates optimization since standard convex optimization techniques cannot be applied. They formally illustrate this with a lemma and provide an example to demonstrate the non-concavity of the optimization problem.
