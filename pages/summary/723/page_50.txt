In this section of the paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan, the authors examine the value function V(s0) in a Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, particularly focusing on its dependence on parameters related to state and action. The value function is expressed as a mathematical expectation involving rewards over time, emphasizing that it only relies on specific parameters when transitioning between states.

The authors introduce key terms, including the largest and smallest probabilities across states, denoted as 𝜈p and 𝜈 respectively. They proceed to prove Proposition 6, which establishes bounds on the value function and derivatives under certain conditions. Specifically, they show that for a given range of parameters, the value function remains bounded and that the difference between the optimal value function and the approximated value function is also constrained.

Additionally, they define a matrix M and its properties to facilitate bounding the expected rewards and derive relationships involving normalized discounted probabilities of reaching certain states in k steps. The paper outlines recursive relations for these probabilities, providing a foundation for further analysis of the value function's behavior in the MDP context. 

Overall, the results contribute to understanding the efficiency and limitations of policy gradients in MDPs, highlighting the interplay between parameters and the resulting value functions.
