The paper discusses the theory behind policy gradient methods in optimization, specifically focusing on the second-order derivatives of the objective function. It presents a mathematical formulation showing that the Hessian matrix for the policy gradient is smooth and provides conditions under which this smoothness holds. 

Key results include the demonstration of the smoothness of the objective function, indicated by the property that the second derivatives vanish for different state variables. The proof establishes that the function is 2-smooth, which is essential for ensuring convergence in optimization algorithms.

The paper also references standard optimization results from previous works by Ghadimi and Lan, as well as Beck, which are applied to the context of gradient descent algorithms. It outlines assumptions necessary for these results, such as the proper and closed nature of the function being minimized and the convexity of its domain. 

A critical definition introduced is the gradient mapping, which is a tool for understanding the behavior of the optimization process, particularly when projecting onto a convex set. Theorems presented confirm that the sequence generated by the gradient descent algorithm is non-increasing and that the gradient mapping approaches zero, indicating convergence to an optimal point.

Overall, the paper rigorously establishes foundational concepts for policy gradient methods while leveraging existing optimization theorems to support its claims.
