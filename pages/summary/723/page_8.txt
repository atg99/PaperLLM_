The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan discusses the concept of concentrability in the context of approximate dynamic programming methods. The authors highlight that while traditional bounded error assumptions are stringent, concentrability allows for sharper bounds on average case function approximation errors if the concentrability coefficient is bounded. Key references (Munos, 2005; Szepesvári and Munos, 2005; Antos et al., 2008; Lazaric et al., 2016) provide sample size and error bounds based on this bounded coefficient, which is crucial for data collection policies that induce concentrability.

The paper distinguishes between the concentrability coefficient and the distribution mismatch coefficient, noting that the latter does not impose restrictions on Markov Decision Process (MDP) dynamics. It critiques existing methods that still impose such constraints and discusses the average case guarantees for the MD-MPI algorithm under bounded concentrability, emphasizing the relevance of function approximation errors in these guarantees.

The authors also address computational and statistical complexities in function approximation, leveraging results from stochastic approximation literature to propose a linear time method for the critic estimation step in the natural policy gradient algorithm. Their approach, which involves streaming updates based on stochastic optimization, contrasts with the Least Squares Temporal Difference methods used in previous works.

Finally, the paper references concurrent work by Bhandari and Russo that discusses conditions for global convergence to the optimal policy, noting limitations in applicability to certain algorithms. Overall, the authors provide a comprehensive analysis of the implications of concentrability in the design and guarantees of approximate dynamic programming methods.
