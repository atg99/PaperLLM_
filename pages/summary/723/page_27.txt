The paper discusses the theory of policy gradient methods, emphasizing the roles of excess risk and transfer error in the context of the Q-NPG algorithm. The excess risk arises from the discrepancy between the estimated parameter w(t) and the true parameter w(t)?, while transfer error pertains to the limitations of the best linear fit to approximate Q-values. 

To formalize these concepts, the paper introduces Assumption 6.1, which defines conditions for estimation and transfer errors. It establishes that for a fixed state distribution and comparator policy, the estimation error is bounded, indicating that the difference in loss between the estimated and true parameters is limited. The bound is expected to improve with more samples, specifically as O(1/sqrt(N)), where N is the number of samples. The transfer error is also bounded, defining it as the discrepancy in loss when evaluated against a specific state-action measure derived from the comparator policy.

Furthermore, Assumption 6.2 introduces the concept of a relative condition number, which assesses the relationship between different state-action distributions and their impacts on the performance of the policy gradient methods. This condition number is crucial for ensuring that the assumptions hold in practical applications.

Overall, the paper provides a structured approach to understanding the limitations of policy gradient methods in terms of estimation and transfer errors, setting the groundwork for further exploration of their implications in reinforcement learning.
