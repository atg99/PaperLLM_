The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan discusses the convergence properties of a softmax parameterization in a reinforcement learning context. It presents a framework for updating parameters using a gradient-based approach, specifically through the softmax function.

The authors derive an update rule for the parameter s at iteration t+1, which is influenced by the gradient of a value function V(t) with respect to the parameter. They reference Lemma 40 to establish a relationship between the gradient and the softmax parameterization, leading to an expression that connects the updated parameter to its previous value.

The analysis includes a reference to the smoothness of the function and the conditions under which gradient ascent will effectively decrease the function value. The paper states that under certain assumptions, the update rule ensures that the value function at the next iteration is less than or equal to the current value function, thereby supporting convergence.

The authors further establish that the limits of the value function V(t)(s) and action-value function Q(t)(s;a) exist as the number of iterations approaches infinity. They introduce constants V(1)(s) and Q(1)(s;a) that represent these limits and define sets based on the relationship between Q(1)(s;a) and V(1)(s). The existence of a threshold T0 is asserted, beyond which the action-value functions converge to their limits, demonstrating the stability of the system.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for understanding the convergence of value functions in reinforcement learning scenarios under specific parameterization and update conditions.
