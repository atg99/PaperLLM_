This section discusses related work in the context of policy gradient methods, emphasizing their theoretical foundations and empirical developments. The authors highlight a gradient domination property, which states that any first-order stationary point of a policy's value is nearly optimal, referencing previous results by Scherrer and Geist, as well as Kakade and Langford. They propose framing this as a Polyak-like property to leverage advancements in non-convex optimization for reinforcement learning.

The paper connects to existing literature on the global convergence of policy gradients in Linear Quadratic Regulators, citing Fazel et al. The authors mention the empirical study by Ahmed et al., which underscores the importance of entropy-based regularization in policy optimization, aligning with their own use of log barrier regularization.

For the natural policy gradient algorithm, connections to Even-Dar et al. and Geist et al. are noted, particularly regarding online regret guarantees in changing Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and the equivalence to the natural policy gradient with softmax parameterization. The authors also reference the MD-MPI algorithm, which shares similarities with their work and offers a comparative analysis of convergence rates.

The discussion then shifts to iterative algorithms that constrain updates to be close to previous policies, such as CPI and PSDP. They highlight the improved iteration complexity of PSDP over CPI, as shown in prior studies. Lastly, the authors introduce approximate dynamic programming, encompassing various methods like fitted value iteration and generalized policy iteration, while discussing their reliance on the concentrability coefficient.
