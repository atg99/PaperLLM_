In this paper, Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan derive a formula for A(s; a) and provide a proof for it, highlighting the relationship between gradient ascent convergence and the regularization parameters used in optimization. The key result is presented in Corollary 13, which discusses iteration complexity when employing log barrier regularization. The authors emphasize the necessity of balancing the regularization parameter with the desired accuracy for achieving global optimality, regardless of the initial state distribution.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts the log barrier regularization with the more traditional entropy regularization. They note that while the entropy regularizer is less severe in penalizing small probabilities, the log barrier regularization exhibits a more aggressive behavior, particularly in its impact on relative entropy. This aggressive nature is crucial for ensuring significant probabilities for actions with positive advantages, thereby facilitating polynomial convergence rates in optimization. The authors also express uncertainty regarding whether a polynomial convergence rate can be achieved with the entropy regularizer, leaving this as an open question for future research.
