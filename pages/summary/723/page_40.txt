In this paper, Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan examine the convergence properties of policy optimization techniques in both tabular and function approximation contexts. They define the relative condition number at iteration t, which relates to the convergence behavior of the optimization process. The authors demonstrate that the non-convex nature of the policy optimization problem is not the main obstacle for typical policy gradient approaches, as indicated by their global convergence results that highlight the favorable characteristics of the underlying optimization problem.

However, the study also reveals that insufficient exploration can lead to convergence towards sub-optimal policies, a phenomenon observed in practical applications. This issue is linked to the conditioning of the optimization problem. The authors argue that standard policy gradient methods may struggle with convergence, particularly when faced with distribution mismatch, while natural policy gradient methods exhibit faster convergence in tabular settings when using exact gradients.

In summary, the paper emphasizes the importance of exploration strategies in policy optimization and illustrates how different approaches can lead to varying levels of convergence efficiency. The findings suggest that while traditional policy gradient methods can be slow under certain conditions, natural policy gradient methods provide a more robust solution in tabular scenarios.
