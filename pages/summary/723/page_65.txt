The paper discusses the theoretical foundations of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning. It introduces the concept of Zt, defined as the sum of the gradients of the value function V at specific time steps t0, with respect to policy parameters θ for actions a0. For non-empty sets T(t), Zt is expressed as a combination of the gradients over time, indicating the relationship between the value function and policy parameters.

The analysis reveals that as time approaches infinity, the limit infimum of Zt tends toward negative infinity. This outcome suggests that for any non-empty set T(t), the gradients of the value function with respect to the policy parameters exhibit a specific behavior that influences the overall dynamics of the learning process.

The paper emphasizes the monotonicity of the policy parameters, demonstrating that the gradients are negative, which reverses the sign of certain inequalities. This leads to a contradiction regarding the lower bounds of the policy parameters, thus establishing crucial insights into the convergence properties of policy gradient methods.

Overall, the findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the theoretical underpinnings of policy gradient approaches, shedding light on their behavior and implications in reinforcement learning contexts.
