The paper discusses the theory behind policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, emphasizing the advantages of parameterizing policies unconstrainedly compared to direct parameterization. While this approach allows for the use of standard optimization algorithms, it introduces challenges, particularly as the optimal policy tends to a deterministic state when parameters increase indefinitely.

Three algorithms are analyzed: the first employs direct policy gradient ascent, the second introduces a log barrier regularizer to prevent excessive parameter growth and promote exploration, and the third examines the natural policy gradient, demonstrating a global optimality result that does not rely on distribution mismatch or dimensionality issues.

Specifically, for the softmax parameterization, the gradient is affected by the exponential scaling of parameters, leading to nearly deterministic policies with gradients approaching zero. Despite this, the paper establishes that gradient ascent can asymptotically converge to the global optimum under certain conditions. The update rule for gradient ascent is defined, and a theorem is introduced that guarantees global convergence given that the optimization distribution remains strictly positive across all states.

The paper raises an open question regarding the implications of relaxing the strict positivity condition on convergence, as it could potentially hinder the learning rate for certain states. Furthermore, the authors highlight the complexities in proving convergence due to the limitations of applying the gradient domination lemma to the softmax parameterization.

Overall, the findings underscore the nuanced behavior of policy gradient methods and the importance of parameterization in achieving effective optimization in reinforcement learning contexts.
