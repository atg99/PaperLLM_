The paper discusses the theory of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the characteristics of stochastic policies and their implications for optimization. It establishes that if a state is reachable under a given policy, it remains reachable under any stochastic policy, allowing for the application of zero derivative conditions at each reachable state.

To minimize the loss function L, the paper sets the condition that the gradient of the value function concerning the policy is zero, leading to a state-dependent offset. This results in updates to the policy parameters and action probabilities, where the normalization factor ensures that the updates are invariant to specific choices of the constant offset. The authors choose this offset to be zero for simplicity.

The paper also includes a section on smoothness proofs, which are essential for establishing convergence guarantees in the context of smooth, non-convex optimization. Key definitions include the Hadamard product and the diagonal matrix representation of vectors. A smoothness lemma is presented, demonstrating that the gradient of the function related to the policy parameters is bounded by the distance between parameter vectors, facilitating the analysis of convergence in policy updates.

Overall, the paper provides a foundational understanding of policy gradient methods, emphasizing the importance of stochastic policies and smoothness in optimization processes.
