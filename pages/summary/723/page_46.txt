The work of Szepesvári and Munos presents finite time bounds for sampling-based methods, which are crucial for improving the performance of value iteration in finite time. Williams' contributions from the early 1990s laid the groundwork for statistical gradient-following algorithms, enhancing connectionist approaches in reinforcement learning. Furthermore, the exploration of function optimization through these algorithms is highlighted, showcasing their applicability in various contexts. Recent developments by Yang and Wang introduce sample-optimal parametric Q-learning, emphasizing the effectiveness of linearly additive features in reinforcement learning. Together, these works illustrate the evolution and optimization of learning algorithms within the reinforcement learning framework.
