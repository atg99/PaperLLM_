The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan addresses the technical challenges in demonstrating the convergence of a sequence V(t)(s) in a specific optimization framework. The authors establish that this sequence is pointwise monotone increasing, leading to the existence of a limit V(1)(s) through the monotone convergence theorem. They define limiting sets for each state based on the relationship between the limiting quantities V(1)(s) and Q(1)(s;a). A key challenge is to show that the set Is+ is empty for all states, which would imply that V(1)(s) equals the optimal value V?(s). The proof employs a contradiction approach, relying on the divergence of parameters associated with actions in the presence of a non-empty Is+.

The authors propose a regularization approach to ensure polynomial convergence rates, countering the rapid deterministic behavior of policies under softmax parameterization that can lead to slow convergence. They introduce a log barrier regularized objective, incorporating relative entropy to manage the distribution of probabilities. This regularization is distinct from traditional entropy regularizers. The paper concludes with the policy gradient ascent updates for the proposed regularized objective, aiming to optimize the convergence dynamics effectively.
