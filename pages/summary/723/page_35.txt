The paper discusses the theory behind Policy Gradient methods, specifically focusing on a Sample-based Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) algorithm. The algorithm is designed to optimize policy parameters using stochastic gradient descent (SGD) while maintaining an unbiased estimate of the advantage function. 

In Algorithm 4, the process begins with the initialization of policy parameters. For each time step, the algorithm iterates through a series of SGD updates. These updates involve calling a previous algorithm to obtain state-action pairs and an unbiased estimate of the advantage function, which is crucial for calculating the update direction. The update is performed using a projection to ensure the updated parameters remain within a defined constraint set.

The expected number of samples required by the NPG algorithm is derived, indicating that it is influenced by both the number of iterations and the expected episode length. The proof asserts that the update direction utilizes an unbiased estimate of the true gradient of the loss function, ensuring that the sampling procedure leads to a conditionally unbiased estimate.

The paper further provides a general analysis of the NPG method, followed by a specialization that aids in proving the main theorems presented in the section. This structured approach underlines the algorithm's effectiveness and theoretical foundations in optimizing policy gradients.
