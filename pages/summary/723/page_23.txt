The paper discusses the theory behind policy gradient methods, focusing on the proof of Theorem 16, which pertains to the performance difference lemma. The proof establishes that the difference between the value of a state under an optimal policy and a policy at time t can be expressed in terms of expected values involving Kullback-Leibler divergences and log partition functions.

The authors utilize Lemma 2 to derive an equation that relates these values, emphasizing the closed-form updates from Lemma 15. They also apply Lemma 17 to bound the expected log partition function, leading to a relationship between the values across different time steps. The proof concludes by leveraging these relationships to demonstrate the performance bounds of the policy gradient methods.

In the latter section, the paper transitions to discussing function approximation and distribution shift, highlighting that the parametric policy classes considered may not encompass all stochastic policies, nor guarantee the inclusion of an optimal policy. This introduces complexity compared to the tabular results, emphasizing the challenges faced in practical applications of policy gradient methods.
