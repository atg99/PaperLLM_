The paper discusses the theoretical foundations of policy gradient methods, focusing on the computation of higher-order partial derivatives of a matrix M with respect to policy parameters. 

Lemma 39 establishes that the k-th order partial derivatives of M can be expressed in a specific form involving coefficients and monomials. The proof begins by simplifying the expression for the first derivative and utilizes the chain rule to extend this to higher-order derivatives.

The derivation involves the transition matrix P, which depends on the policy parameters, and it shows how the structure of these derivatives can be expressed through an inductive process. The base case for k=1 is demonstrated, and the inductive step is outlined, showing that if the hypothesis holds for k-1, it can be extended to k.

Key points include the dependence of the derivatives on the structure of the transition matrix and the conditions for the indices in the monomials that arise in the expression for the derivatives. The proof relies on maintaining specific relationships between the indices to ensure the correctness of the derived expressions.
