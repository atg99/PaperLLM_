The paper discusses the theoretical framework for policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on establishing bounds for the gradient norms. It derives a bound on the maximum gradient norm given certain parameters and conditions, including the number of actions and the horizon of the policy. The authors present mathematical expressions that relate the gradient norm to various factors, including the discount factor and the horizon length.

The key results include a requirement that the gradient norm does not exceed a specific threshold, which leads to a derived condition on the number of actions (k) that can be optimally selected. The paper concludes that for maintaining the desired gradient norm, the value of k must be limited by a function of the horizon (H) and other logarithmic factors.

Furthermore, the optimal policy is indicated to always select the first action, yielding a specific discounted reward. This reward is mathematically expressed and highlights the relationship between the policy and the expected returns in the context of reinforcement learning. The findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the constraints and performance of policy gradient methods, essential for developing efficient algorithms in this domain.
