The paper discusses the theoretical aspects of policy gradient methods, focusing on the convergence properties of action probabilities in various states as time progresses. Key lemmas (44-50) establish that for actions in certain sets, the probabilities approach zero or are bounded as time approaches infinity. 

Lemma 43 asserts the existence of a threshold \( T_1 \) beyond which the value function \( V(t)(s) \) becomes close to a limit \( V(1)(s) \). It demonstrates that for actions in sets \( I_s^- \) and \( I_s^+ \), the advantage function \( A(t)(s;a) \) behaves differently, being less than a negative bound for \( I_s^- \) and greater than a positive bound for \( I_s^+ \).

In Lemma 44, the smoothness of the value function with respect to parameters is confirmed, leading to the conclusion that the derivative of the value function with respect to policy parameters approaches zero for all states and actions. This implies that the probabilities of actions in set \( I_s^+ \) converge to zero, while the sum of probabilities in \( I_s^0 \) approaches one.

Overall, the lemmas provide a rigorous foundation for understanding the behavior of policy gradient methods, emphasizing the convergence of action probabilities and the role of the advantage function in determining optimal actions.
