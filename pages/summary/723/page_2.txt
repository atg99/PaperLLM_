The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan addresses the theoretical convergence properties of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, particularly within the framework of discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). While a substantial amount of empirical research has been conducted on these methods, their convergence to globally optimal solutions and their behavior in terms of approximation errors and finite sample sizes have not been thoroughly explored.

The authors aim to provide a robust theoretical foundation for first-order and quasi second-order policy gradient methods, focusing on both tabular policy parameterizations and function approximation scenarios. In the tabular case, they analyze three algorithms: projected gradient ascent, gradient ascent with a softmax parameterization, and natural policy gradient ascent. They present upper bounds on the number of iterations required to achieve an epsilon-optimal policy when exact policy gradients are available.

One of the key contributions is the establishment of global convergence guarantees for these methods. The authors demonstrate that first-order stationary points of the value function can lead to approximately optimal policies under certain conditions, enabling a global convergence analysis informed by non-convex optimization principles. Furthermore, they provide the first global convergence guarantees for the softmax parameterization of policies, showing that the policy gradient algorithm asymptotically converges to an optimal policy despite the challenge posed by the deterministic nature of the optimal policy in this context.

Overall, this work enhances the theoretical understanding of policy gradient methods, aligning their convergence properties with those established for iterative value function-based algorithms.
