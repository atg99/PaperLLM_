The paper by Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, and Mahajan advances the understanding of convergence rates in the context of natural policy gradients (NPG) and their relationship to trust region policy optimization (TRPO). The authors utilize a proof strategy inspired by the online regret framework for changing Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) as established by Even-Dar et al. in 2009. They demonstrate that their approach yields a faster convergence rate compared to previous analyses by Even-Dar et al. and Geist et al. in 2019.

A key component of their analysis is the Improvement Lemma, which establishes a lower bound for the performance improvement of the iterates generated by NPG updates. This lemma shows that for any starting state distribution, the difference in value functions between consecutive iterations is non-negative, indicating that the updates lead to performance improvements.

The proof relies on the property that the logarithm of the partition function, logZt(s), is non-negative, which is derived using Jensen's inequality. The authors further relate the performance differences to the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the distributions of the policy at consecutive iterations.

Through these contributions, the authors clarify the theoretical underpinnings of NPG updates and their efficiency, enhancing the understanding of policy optimization methods in reinforcement learning.
