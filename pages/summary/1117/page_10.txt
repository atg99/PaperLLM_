The paper discusses several scenarios related to water resource management, including long-term planning, reservoir operation, wastewater treatment, and model calibration. Each scenario highlights the consequences of previous decisions on costs, resource impacts, and environmental quality.

For long-term water resource planning, adjustments in source capacities may be needed based on demand shortfalls or supply excesses, affecting costs and resource impacts. In reservoir operations, decisions regarding water storage must balance hydropower generation against irrigation and environmental needs, particularly in response to spillage levels. Wastewater treatment decisions involve managing treatment levels to meet water quality standards while considering cost implications. In model calibration, adjustments to parameters like runoff curve numbers or roughness coefficients are necessary to accurately reflect observed conditions.

The iterative process of selecting and evaluating trial solutions, informed by environmental models and prior experience, is essential for improving outcomes. However, the paper argues that this informal optimization approach may not yield the best results due to the vastness of the solution space in environmental problems, which often contains many potential solutions that are not practical. The likelihood of finding optimal or near-optimal solutions is low, as only a small fraction of the available options are typically explored. This underscores the need for more systematic and comprehensive optimization strategies in environmental management.
