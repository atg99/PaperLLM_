The paper discusses the intuitive application of evolutionary algorithms (EAs) in optimizing solutions for environmental problems, highlighting the distinction between informal optimization processes, which rely on personal knowledge and intuition, and formal EA-based processes that utilize evolutionary operators for automatic adjustments. EAs are versatile, capable of handling both discrete and continuous decision variables, unlike most traditional optimization methods that primarily focus on continuous variables. This adaptability is crucial for real-world applications where decision variables can be categorical or subject to practical constraints. 

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the straightforward nature of constraint handling in EAs, often achieved through penalty functions that convert constrained optimization problems into unconstrained ones by incorporating penalties for constraint violations. The review also mentions alternative techniques for constraint handling, such as restricted tournament selection in multi-objective optimization, illustrating the flexibility and effectiveness of EAs in various optimization scenarios.
