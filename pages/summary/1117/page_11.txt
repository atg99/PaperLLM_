The paper discusses the challenges associated with solving complex environmental problems, particularly the difficulties inexperienced analysts face when applying domain knowledge. It highlights the impracticality of complete enumeration of potential solutions due to prohibitively long simulation times, even if each simulation run only takes one second. For instance, the water distribution system problem's total simulation time is estimated at approximately 2x10^70 years. 

The authors argue that informal optimization methods are unlikely to yield near-optimal solutions, while the computational intractability of evaluating every possible solution underscores the necessity of formal optimization methods. These methods can significantly improve solution efficiency, yielding enhancements of 10% to 50% over informal trial-and-error approaches. Furthermore, formal optimization provides a systematic approach to tackling environmental issues with conflicting objectives, especially in complex scenarios that are not well understood.

The paper also includes a table illustrating the vast search spaces for several environmental problems, emphasizing the theoretical infinity of search space sizes for continuous decision variables. The examples provided showcase the extensive scale of decision variable options and potential solutions, reinforcing the argument for adopting formal optimization techniques in environmental problem-solving.
