The paper discusses the challenges associated with optimization problems, particularly in decision-making contexts involving multiple variables and constraints. It emphasizes that the number and range of decision variables, as well as the level of discretization, significantly impact the complexity of the search space. A larger search space can complicate the identification of optimal solutions, especially when the feasible region—where all constraints are satisfied—is small. 

The paper illustrates this with a wastewater treatment case, where certain variable combinations may yield lower costs but violate water quality constraints. Therefore, both the size of the search space and the feasible region are critical in finding optimal solutions.

Moreover, the paper examines the properties of the fitness landscape, which represents the relationship between decision variables and objective function values. A smooth fitness landscape is easier to navigate, while a rugged landscape, characterized by many local optima, poses significant challenges for identifying the global optimum. Real-world problems often exhibit complex fitness landscapes with non-linearities and discontinuities, particularly when derived from intricate models.

The presence of multiple local optima can provide decision-makers with options but complicates model calibration, as it becomes difficult to ascertain the correct model parameters. The paper highlights that the choice of error measures and data subsets for calibration can further influence the fitness landscape, affecting the determination of the best model representation of physical processes.
