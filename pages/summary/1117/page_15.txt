The paper discusses the complexities involved in multi-objective optimization, particularly in the context of decision space and objective space mapping. It highlights that the optimization process is influenced by various factors, including the statistical properties of data and the division of this data into calibration and validation subsets. 

Figures presented illustrate the relationship between decision space and objective space, emphasizing that both objectives should be minimized. The paper further explains the fitness landscapes associated with each objective and the concept of a Pareto front, which represents the set of non-dominated solutions in a two-objective optimization scenario.

The difficulty of navigating the fitness landscapes to locate globally optimal solutions is primarily determined by the size of the search area, which is influenced by the number of decision variables and their respective ranges. This complexity underscores the challenges faced in optimizing systems, such as water distribution networks, where multiple objectives must be balanced.
