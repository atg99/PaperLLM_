The paper discusses the limitations of traditional optimization algorithms, such as gradient-based and random search methods, which are constrained by their fixed searching behaviors that are not adaptable to varying fitness landscapes. Gradient-based methods work best in smooth landscapes with a single basin, while random searches are suited for rough and unstructured spaces. In contrast, evolutionary algorithms (EAs) offer flexibility in their searching behavior, allowing for a tailored balance between diversification and intensification based on specific problem characteristics.

A significant advantage of EAs is their compatibility with existing simulation and integrated assessment models. This is achieved through a two-way coupling process where the optimization algorithm sets decision variables for the simulation model, which in turn evaluates objective functions and constraints, returning the results to the algorithm. This integration enables systems that can be simulated to also be optimized using EAs, allowing for comparison between informal optimization outcomes and those derived from the EA process. Such comparisons enhance user trust in the optimization results, as highlighted by Smith et al. in 2015.
