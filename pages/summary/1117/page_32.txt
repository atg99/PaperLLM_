The paper discusses the computational challenges faced in optimization processes, particularly when dealing with large and complex search spaces and uncertainties. These challenges necessitate the use of simulation models that often require extensive computational resources. To enhance efficiency, strategies such as using surrogate models, parallel computing, and pre-emption techniques are suggested. Additionally, the size of the search space can be reduced through problem reformulation.

The paper also highlights the difficulty in adjusting the searching behavior of evolutionary algorithms (EAs) due to the unknown properties of the fitness landscape. While sensitivity analysis is commonly used to determine parameters for searching behavior, dynamic adjustments can also be made during the search process to optimize performance.

Finally, the implementation of EAs is described as a black box approach, where the search algorithm and simulation model interact through an I/O interface. This method allows for the integration of various algorithms and programming languages, facilitating a flexible and efficient optimization workflow.
