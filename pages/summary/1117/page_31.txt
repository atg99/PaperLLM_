This paper discusses different approaches to Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEAs), particularly highlighting simplified strategies that focus exclusively on non-dominated solutions. Algorithms such as PAES and PADDS are mentioned as examples that utilize crowding distance to guide the selection process when preferences among non-dominated solutions arise. 

The paper emphasizes that the number of Pareto-optimal solutions tends to increase with the addition of more objective functions. This phenomenon occurs because existing Pareto-optimal solutions remain optimal even as new objectives are introduced, which expands the Pareto front in the objective space. Consequently, as the number of objectives increases, a larger proportion of feasible solutions become Pareto-optimal. This increase presents a challenge for MOEAs, limiting their effectiveness in accurately approximating high-dimensional Pareto fronts, often restricting their application to problems with two or three objectives.

Further, the paper outlines challenges associated with using Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs), particularly regarding computational efficiency. Since EAs operate on populations and require multiple iterations, the computational demand escalates with the size of the population and the number of iterations, especially when objective function evaluations involve complex environmental models. This aspect becomes a significant concern when applying EAs to complex, real-world problems. 

Overall, the paper provides insights into the dynamics of MOEAs and the inherent challenges in their computational implementation.
