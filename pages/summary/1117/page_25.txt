The paper discusses the mechanisms of selection, crossover, and mutation in evolutionary algorithms (EAs) used for optimization. It describes how selection processes, including tournaments and elitism, ensure that high-quality solutions are preserved and promoted during the search for optimal solutions. Crossover is illustrated through a single-point crossover operator, which creates offspring by swapping genes between two parent solutions, contributing to both intensification and diversification based on the positions of the parents. 

Mutation is introduced as a method to enhance diversity by randomly altering some offspring, enabling the algorithm to avoid local optima. The application of these heuristic operators is effective for both continuous and discrete problems. In continuous scenarios, model parameters are adjusted within their feasible ranges, while in discrete problems, such as pipe sizing in water distribution systems, decision variables are restricted to specific available options. The paper cites foundational works on genetic algorithms by authors such as Holland and Goldberg, providing a comprehensive overview of the topic.
