The paper discusses the advantages of evolutionary algorithms (EAs) in multi-objective optimization. EAs allow for the simultaneous handling of multiple objectives without merging them into a single fitness landscape, thereby preserving insights into trade-offs and approximating Pareto fronts in a single run. They are also conducive to parallel computing, enabling faster evaluations compared to traditional methods that operate serially. 

EAs produce a variety of near-optimal solutions, facilitating decision-making by allowing for consideration of factors beyond the mathematical formulation of the problem. This approach enhances the control of decision-makers, who can sift through large solution spaces to identify viable candidates for further analysis. 

Moreover, EAs increase trust in optimization results as they utilize outputs from established environmental simulation models, mimicking the processes of experienced modellers. This iterative improvement process reduces the black-box perception often associated with optimization, contrasting with traditional methods like linear programming that may rely on oversimplified models. However, the trust in these solutions can be influenced by the decision-makers' familiarity with the simulation model, especially if the outcomes differ from expectations.
