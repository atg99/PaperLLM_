The paper discusses the penalizing approach to handling constraints in optimization, particularly through the use of penalty functions. This method allows for the integration of constraint violations into the objective function, enabling the removal of constraints from the optimization formulation. The user must often determine the appropriate penalty function and weights through trial and error.

In exploring multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs), the paper highlights that while their search mechanisms are based on the principles of single-objective evolutionary algorithms, they differ significantly in fitness evaluation. MOEAs require a complex fitness assignment process to unify multiple objective functions into a scalar fitness value for ranking and selecting solutions. 

The fitness assignment relies on the concept of dominance, where candidate solutions are ranked according to their dominance strength in the objective space. The paper outlines various strategies for this ranking, such as dominance depth, dominance rank, and dominance count. A high-level pseudo-code is provided, illustrating the process of generating an initial population, updating it through heuristic operators, and assigning fitness values based on dominance and density until stopping criteria are met. The final output consists of non-dominated solutions and their fitness functions.
