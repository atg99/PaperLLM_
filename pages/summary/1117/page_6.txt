The paper provides an introductory overview of the necessity for optimization in environmental simulation models, which play a crucial role in decision-making across various domains, including environmental regulation, land use management, natural hazard management, and more. It highlights the extensive applications of these models, such as managing reservoir systems, air pollution, and groundwater, among others. 

The core argument is that environmental models are essential for reproducing the dynamics of real-world systems and testing various management scenarios. To effectively support decisions, model inputs must align with proposed alternatives, and outputs must correspond to relevant environmental outcomes. The paper defines key terms, such as objectives (desired outcomes), decision variables (available alternatives), and constraints (limitations like pollution levels).

An example illustrates the application of these concepts in designing water distribution systems aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions and costs while ensuring adequate water delivery. The paper emphasizes that the optimization process involves identifying the best decision variable values to achieve the desired outcomes while adhering to constraints, thus providing solutions to environmental challenges.
