It outlines how to structure these problems to facilitate the application of optimization techniques and provides an overview of evolutionary algorithms (EAs), their implementation, and the challenges faced in real-world scenarios.

The discussion is organized around five classes of environmental problems, each illustrated with specific examples:

1. **Mitigation of Global Climate Change Impacts**: This example focuses on optimizing water distribution systems to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, highlighting the need for modifications in engineered systems to mitigate climate change.

2. **Regional Resource Consumption in Urban Systems**: Here, the long-term planning of water resources is discussed, emphasizing the balance between water supply security and the social and environmental impacts of resource extraction.

3. **Natural Resource Management**: This section illustrates the operation of reservoir systems through optimization to achieve favorable economic, social, and environmental outcomes within watersheds.

4. **Pollution Management**: The determination of wastewater treatment levels is presented, where optimization seeks to identify cost-effective treatment strategies that ensure the environmental health of river systems.

5. **Environmental Model Development**: This example involves model calibration, utilizing optimization to align model outputs with measured data.

Overall, the paper aims to make the principles of optimization and EAs accessible to a broad audience, asserting their applicability across various environmental decision-making contexts.
