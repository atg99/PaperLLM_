The paper discusses the Non-Dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II), introduced by Deb et al. in 2000. It explains how solutions in a population are organized into multiple fronts based on dominance; the first front consists of non-dominated solutions ranked as 1, while subsequent fronts are ranked accordingly based on their dominance status relative to previous fronts. Solutions on the same front are considered equally fit. 

The paper also compares different fitness assignment strategies used in multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (EAs), including dominance rank and dominance count. Dominance rank assigns fitness based on the number of solutions that dominate a given solution, while dominance count is based on how many solutions a given solution dominates.

Furthermore, it emphasizes the importance of diversity not only in the decision-variable space but also in the objective space to ensure a uniform distribution of optimal solutions along the Pareto front. The concept of "crowding distance" is introduced as a measure to estimate solution density in the objective space, which helps in promoting the spread of solutions. In bi-objective optimization, crowding distance is calculated using the surrounding solutions to determine the relative fitness of solutions on the same front.

The paper concludes that when multiple solutions receive the same rank, secondary fitness metrics like crowding distance can be employed to differentiate between them, thus enhancing selection processes in EAs.
