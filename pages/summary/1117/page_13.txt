The translation of environmental and water resources management issues into optimization problems can be challenging due to their complex and poorly defined nature. Engaging stakeholders in the formulation of these problems is essential, as it allows for a collaborative approach to establish objectives, decisions, and constraints. 

Optimization problems can be represented geometrically through a "fitness landscape," which illustrates the relationship between the objective function and decision variables. For single-variable problems, this landscape is a nonlinear line, while for two variables, it forms a plane. In higher dimensions, it becomes a hyperplane that is difficult to visualize. 

When focusing on a single objective to maximize, the optimization aims to locate the highest point in the fitness landscape, known as the "global optimum." This process entails identifying the combination of decision variable values that yields the maximum objective function value, analogous to finding the "highest hill" in the landscape.
