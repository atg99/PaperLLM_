Single Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) and other metaheuristics optimize objective functions through iterative updates of decision variable values, typically using a population of solutions rather than a single solution. These algorithms employ heuristic operators, often inspired by natural processes, to guide the optimization process. Two main philosophies underpin these operators: evolutionary computation, exemplified by Genetic Algorithms (GAs), and swarm intelligence, represented by Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO).

EAs utilize a variety of operators that work collectively to adjust decision variables based on feedback from previous iterations. This process seeks to balance exploration (diversification) and exploitation (intensification) of the search space, which is modulated by parameters specific to the operators used. 

The optimization process starts with a randomly distributed initial population, focusing on exploration in early iterations to identify promising regions of the solution space. As the algorithm progresses, the focus shifts to exploitation within these regions to pinpoint the optimal solution. The algorithm continues until predefined stopping criteria are met, ultimately returning the best solution and its fitness value. 

This structured approach allows EAs to effectively navigate complex optimization landscapes through a combination of biological inspiration and strategic search techniques.
