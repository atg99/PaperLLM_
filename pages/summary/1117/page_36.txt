The paper discusses the application of evolutionary algorithms (EAs) in optimizing complex environmental decision-making processes. It highlights a method where trial solutions are evaluated through simulations, with the next solution selection driven by nature-inspired strategies, such as the survival of the fittest and ant foraging behavior. This approach enables the discovery of solutions that either enhance environmental performance at the same cost or achieve desired outcomes at lower costs, rather than resulting in poorer environmental results for higher expenses.

While EAs offer significant advantages, the paper cautions that they may not always be the optimal choice. For problems that can be linearized or require rapid solutions, traditional methods like Linear Programming may be more effective. The authors emphasize the importance of selecting the appropriate optimization method based on the problem's characteristics. EAs are particularly advantageous for large, complex, and non-linear issues, providing decision support in iterative exploratory contexts. By minimizing computational demands, EAs allow for a broader exploration of options and trade-offs, enabling modellers to concentrate on critical aspects of the problem.

The paper concludes with acknowledgments to contributors and reviewers who enhanced the manuscript's quality and clarity.
