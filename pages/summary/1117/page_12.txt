The section outlines the process of formulating and solving optimization problems, emphasizing the similarities to informal optimization processes. It identifies three key components essential for formal problem formulation: 

1. **Objective Functions**: These represent the goals of the optimization, such as maximizing system performance or minimizing environmental impact.
   
2. **Decision Variables**: These are the manipulable values that influence the objective functions, which can be either discrete or continuous.

3. **Constraints**: These are limitations placed on decision variables to ensure feasible solutions and to avoid undesirable outcomes.

A solution is defined as a specific set of decision variable values, with feasible solutions adhering to all constraints. The evaluation of solutions is performed through the objective function, which reflects environmental outcomes linked to the decision variables.

The formal representation of optimization problems is provided, where the objective function is maximized subject to constraints. This includes the use of inequality constraints and bounds on decision variables. The text also notes that minimization problems can be reformulated from maximization problems, and single-objective optimization can be expanded to multi-objective optimization by incorporating additional objective functions.
