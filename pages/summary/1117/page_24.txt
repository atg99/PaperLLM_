The paper discusses the performance of evolutionary algorithms, illustrating their functionality through figures that depict the evolution of a population of solutions. Initially, a random population is shown, followed by an evolved population that has identified regions of attraction, culminating in a final population that converges to the global optimum.

In the section on Genetic Algorithms (GAs), the author explains the process of updating the population through a series of operators. Each solution in the population is likened to a chromosome, with individual decision variables representing genes. The algorithm employs a selection operator to choose parent chromosomes based on their fitness, which is determined by an objective function. 

The selection process follows the "survival of the fittest" principle, utilizing methods like the "Roulette wheel" selection, where fitness levels determine the probability of selection for breeding. This selection is followed by crossover and mutation operations to generate offspring, which then form the next generation of the population. The overall process emphasizes the iterative nature of GAs in optimizing solutions.
