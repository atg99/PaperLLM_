The paper illustrates the dynamics of pheromone concentration in a discrete optimization scenario involving two decision variables, X1 and X2, each with nine possible values. Initially, all options are equally favored, but as iterations progress, paths leading to better solutions accumulate more pheromone. This positive feedback mechanism mimics natural processes and ultimately results in the globally optimal solution being identified with higher pheromone levels in the final iteration.

In practice, the selection of decision variable options during each iteration is influenced by the pheromone concentrations, commonly implemented through methods like biased roulette wheels. The algorithm may also incorporate a preference for locally optimal solutions, which affects the probability of path selection. The interplay between pheromone levels and local preferences is crucial in guiding the optimization process toward effective solutions.
