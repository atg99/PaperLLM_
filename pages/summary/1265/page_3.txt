The paper introduces BoostForest, a novel ensemble learning approach that utilizes bootstrap sampling to create replicas of the training set and trains a BoostTree on each replica. BoostForest employs only one hyper-parameter, the number of BoostTrees, and demonstrates superior performance compared to classical ensemble methods such as Random Forest, Extra-Trees, XGBoost, and LightGBM. The framework is flexible, allowing for various base learners, including BoostTrees, decision trees, and neural networks.

The effectiveness of BoostForest was evaluated through experiments focusing on six key questions regarding its generalization performance, convergence speed, base learner complexity, integration of different base learners, impact of various node functions, and capability to handle large datasets. The experiments were conducted on 34 real-world datasets, encompassing a range of feature counts and sample sizes. Categorical features were converted to numerical ones, and numerical features were scaled appropriately.

BoostForest's design allows it to leverage the strengths of different models while maintaining simplicity in its hyper-parameter tuning, making it a promising addition to the field of ensemble learning.
