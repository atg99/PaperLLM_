The paper presents an analysis of the generalization performance of various ensemble learning models, specifically focusing on the number of base learners and the complexity of the base learner models. The results are derived from five repeats of 2-fold cross-validation across multiple classification and regression datasets.

In the classification task, the average accuracies for different models such as RandomForest, Extra-Trees, XGBoost, LightGBM, and BoostForest are reported. The performance is evaluated with varying numbers of base learners, showing that as the number of base learners increases, classification accuracy tends to improve across different datasets, particularly highlighted in the QSAR and BA datasets.

For regression tasks, the average Root Mean Square Errors (RMSE) are presented, indicating similar trends where increasing the number of base learners generally leads to lower RMSE values, thereby enhancing predictive accuracy.

Additionally, the paper explores the impact of the maximum number of leaves in the trees of the ensemble models. It shows a correlation between the complexity of the model (measured by the number of leaves) and the classification accuracy and RMSE, with optimal values yielding the best performance.

The complete results for both classification and regression tasks are provided in supplementary figures, indicating a comprehensive evaluation across 15 datasets. Overall, the findings suggest that both the number of base learners and the model complexity significantly influence the performance of ensemble learning methods.
