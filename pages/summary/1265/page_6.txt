The study fixed the number of base learners in BoostForest at 100 while tuning other parameters through grid-search and inner 5-fold cross-validation. Results demonstrated that BoostForest consistently achieved the highest classification accuracy across 15 datasets, with improvements noted as the maximum number of leaves per tree increased. In regression tasks, BoostForest also yielded the lowest RMSE on 11 datasets.

The effectiveness of the BoostForest strategy was further validated by comparing it to CART, showing that CARForest outperformed CART on 28 of 30 datasets. This suggests that the integration method for BoostTrees can be extended to other base learners for enhanced performance. 

Additionally, the study explored the use of more complex regression models like ELM and SVR within the BoostTree framework. The results indicated that BoostForest-ELM and BoostForest-SVR outperformed their respective baseline models, confirming the potential of using diverse regression techniques within the BoostForest ensemble learning approach. 

Overall, the findings underscore the superiority of BoostForest, particularly with BoostTree as the base learner, in both classification and regression tasks compared to other methods.
