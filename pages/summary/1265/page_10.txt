The paper discusses the implementation details of a method aimed at reducing computational costs in the BoostTree framework, particularly for numerical feature splitting. The process involves identifying the minimum and maximum of each selected numerical feature and extracting 100 evenly spaced values between them to determine the optimal split. 

The loss function for BoostTree-ELM remains consistent with that of the original BoostTree, as both ELM and Ridge Regression (RR) share the same objective functions. In contrast, the BoostTree-SVR loss function requires modification to align with SVR's loss function. The modified loss function incorporates regularization coefficients and is expressed in terms of summations over specific indices, reflecting both the training data and the SVR models for left and right child nodes.

The study utilized 33 publicly available datasets from the UCI Machine Learning Repository and MNIST for experimentation. The source code is made publicly accessible on GitHub. The research received multiple grants from various organizations in China, highlighting its academic support. Author contributions are outlined, indicating collaboration in analysis, manuscript writing, and supervision, with a declaration of no competing interests. References to foundational texts and recent studies in the field are also provided, showcasing the work's context within existing literature.
