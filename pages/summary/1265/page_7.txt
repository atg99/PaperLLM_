This paper introduces BoostTree, a novel tree model that integrates Gradient Boosting Machines (GBMs) into a single decision tree framework. BoostTree operates by training either linear or nonlinear functions at each node, and for a given input, it traverses down to a leaf, aggregating outputs from all node models along the path to generate a prediction.

To enhance performance and mitigate overfitting, particularly with small sample sizes, the authors propose BoostForest, which combines multiple BoostTrees. This ensemble method employs bootstrap sampling to create replicas of the training set, training a BoostTree on each one. BoostForest features a single hyperparameter, the number of BoostTrees, and is adaptable to various types of base learners, including decision trees and neural networks.

The performance of BoostForest was evaluated on 14 datasets, demonstrating superior accuracy in classification tasks and lower root mean square error (RMSE) in regression compared to traditional ensemble methods like Random Forest, Extra-Trees, XGBoost, and LightGBM. Notably, while BoostForest excelled on large datasets, it required dimensionality reduction techniques, such as PCA, to perform optimally on high-dimensional datasets like MNIST and RLCT.

The paper discusses the potential for replacing Random Forests in existing models like Deep Forest with BoostForests to improve performance. Future research directions include enhancing BoostForest for high-dimensional data and applying it to complex problems in various fields such as biology and healthcare.

In summary, this research presents BoostTree and BoostForest as effective tools for ensemble learning, showcasing their advantages over conventional methods and indicating paths for further investigation and application.
