The paper discusses the hyper-parameter tuning and performance evaluation of BoostForest and its comparison with four baseline algorithms: Random Forest, Extra-Trees, XGBoost, and LightGBM. Hyper-parameters were selected through grid-search and cross-validation methods, with early stopping for boosting iterations and out-of-bag error for Random Forest and Extra-Trees. The BoostForest model employed 100 base learners, with parameters including the minimum samples per leaf and a regularization coefficient.

Performance measures included classification accuracy for classification tasks and root mean squared error (RMSE) for regression tasks. BoostForest outperformed the baselines on 25 out of 30 datasets, with statistical significance confirmed via t-tests and Benjamini Hochberg correction. Specifically, it significantly outperformed Random Forest and Extra-Trees on 25 datasets, and XGBoost and LightGBM on 22 datasets each.

The study also analyzed the impact of varying the number of base learners on performance, demonstrating that BoostForest achieved the highest classification accuracy on 14 of 15 datasets and the smallest RMSE on 10 regression datasets. The results indicated that BoostForest converges effectively within 50 BoostTrees.

Additionally, the paper examined the effect of base learner model complexity, noting that increasing complexity reduces bias but raises variance. The findings suggest that BoostForest is user-friendly, requiring only the specification of the number of BoostTrees, and is capable of parallelization due to its training method.
