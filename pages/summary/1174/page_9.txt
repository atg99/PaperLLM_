The paper presents a comparative analysis of a novel algorithm's clustering cost against traditional methods, specifically focusing on the k-means objective. The results demonstrate that the proposed algorithm consistently outperforms previous works cited as [9] and [19] in terms of cost across various datasets, including bank, census, and diabetes, with specific costs reported in Table 1. The experiments involved sub-sampling data and adjusting fairness parameters to achieve a balance of 0.5.

The analysis included a runtime evaluation, revealing that the algorithm efficiently processes large datasets, such as the census1990 dataset with 500,000 points and 13 features, in under 30 minutes. The paper notes that despite using an iterative rounding method, the linear programming solutions were predominantly integral, minimizing the need for extensive computation. In contrast, attempts to frame the problem as an integer programming challenge resulted in significantly longer solution times.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed algorithm in achieving fair clustering while maintaining lower costs compared to existing methodologies.
