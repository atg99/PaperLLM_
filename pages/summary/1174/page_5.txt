The paper presents a proof related to the F AIR(k,p)-CLUSTERING problem and its implications for the F AIRp-ASSIGNMENT problem. Initially, an algorithm A is applied to an instance I, yielding a solution (S, φ) with a performance guarantee of Lp(S; φ) ≤ ρ · OPTvnll(I) ≤ ρ · OPTfair(I). The solution S serves as the set of facilities for a new instance J of the F AIRp-ASSIGNMENT problem, where another algorithm B is executed to obtain a λ-violating solution ˆφ. This leads to the conclusion that (S, ˆφ) satisfies specific properties.

The lemma asserts that OPTasgn(J) ≤ (ρ + 2) · OPTfair(I), establishing a relationship between the optimal assignments and the fair clustering solution. The proof utilizes the optimal solution (S*, φ*) of I with Lp(S*; φ*) = OPTfair(I) and demonstrates the existence of a new assignment φ' that adheres to required conditions and maintains a cost bound of Lp(S; φ') ≤ (ρ + 2) · OPTfair(I).

Key claims support this lemma: Claim 4 confirms that φ' meets the required conditions, while Claim 5 employs triangle inequality to relate the distances and validate the cost bound. The paper then outlines an algorithm for the F AIRp-ASSIGNMENT problem, culminating in Theorem 6, which asserts the existence of a (4∆ + 3)-violating algorithm for this problem, supported by a natural LP-relaxation. Overall, the work provides significant insights into the relationship between clustering and assignment problems in fair contexts.
