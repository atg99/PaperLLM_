This paper presents fair k-means and k-median algorithms that operate in any metric space and can accommodate an arbitrary number of overlapping groups. Unlike previous studies, such as those by Ahmadian et al. and Bercea et al., which focus on specific clustering objectives and constraints, this work emphasizes a straightforward yet powerful framework capable of handling any ℓp-norm objective, specifically addressing the issue of overlapping groups.

The authors situate their research within the broader context of fairness in algorithm design, which has garnered significant attention in recent literature. They highlight various studies that explore fairness through disparate impact across different problems, including classification, ranking, and clustering. Notably, Chierichetti et al. first tackled the concept of disparate impact in clustering for two groups, while subsequent works extended this to more groups. Additionally, they reference approaches that define fair clustering based on the representation of groups and the selection of cluster centers.

The paper delves into the preliminaries of the V ANILLA(k,p)-CLUSTERING problem, where the goal is to open a subset of facilities and assign clients to these facilities to minimize the total distance, without fairness considerations. The authors define the FAIR(k,p)-CLUSTERING problem, which introduces fairness constraints by considering multiple overlapping groups and fairness vectors that guide the clustering process.

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of fair clustering by presenting algorithms that are both effective and applicable in diverse settings, addressing a gap in existing literature regarding overlapping groups and fairness in clustering objectives.
