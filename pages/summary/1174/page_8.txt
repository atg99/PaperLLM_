The paper presents a comparative analysis of a fairness-focused clustering algorithm (ALG) against a traditional k-means clustering approach (VC). The evaluation is conducted using three datasets: bank, census, and credit card, with specific parameters set to δ=0.2 and k=4. The balance metric, which measures fairness across clusters, is plotted on the y-axis. The goal is to achieve a balance of at least 0.8, indicated by a dotted line.

Results show that ALG maintains a minimum balance of 0.75 for the census dataset, while VC can drop to a balance of 0 for the bank dataset. The maximum additive violation of ALG is recorded at 3, which is significantly better than the theoretical bound of 4∆+3, demonstrating its effectiveness across varying values of δ and k. In contrast, VC exhibits considerable unfairness.

Cost analysis reveals that the cost of ALG remains within 15% of the vanilla clustering cost for k≤10 across all datasets. Notably, for the credit card dataset, despite the high unfairness of the VC solution, the cost of fairness for ALG is only 6%, indicating that the vanilla centers are positioned favorably even though they are unfair. Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed algorithm effectively balances fairness with cost efficiency in clustering tasks.
