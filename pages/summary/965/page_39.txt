The paper outlines a method for estimating the transition probabilities and rewards in a Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework. The focus is on inferring approximations of the conditional probability p(s′|s, a) and the reward function r(s, a) based on a sequence of observed states, actions, and rewards, represented as ht = (s0, a0, r0, s1, a1, r1, ..., a t−1, rt−1, st).

To estimate the reward function r(s, a), the paper introduces the set X(s, a), which contains indices k where the state-action pair (sk, ak) matches (s, a). The values of the rewards corresponding to these indices, rk1, rk2, ..., rk#X(s,a), are independent samples of the random variable r(s, a, w). The mean reward r(s, a) can then be estimated using an unbiased estimator given by the formula:

ˆr(s, a) = Σk∈X(s,a) rk / #X(s, a)

This approach allows for effective estimation of the reward function based on empirical data collected from the MDP.
