The goal of reinforcement learning (RL) is to develop policies that maximize the expected return from each state in a given environment. The theorem presented states that there exists an optimal policy π∗ within the policy space Π, such that for any state s in the state space S, the expected return Jπ∗(s) equals the maximum return achievable by any policy π in Π, denoted as Jπ(s). This optimal policy can be found under certain mild assumptions regarding the functions f, r, and Pw.

In the context of reinforcement learning, the challenge lies in constructing an approximate optimal policy ˆ π∗ that closely aligns with the true optimal policy π∗, as measured by specific metrics. If the underlying dynamics of the environment, represented by f, r, and Pw, were known, one could theoretically resolve the optimal control problem directly. However, since these elements are typically unknown, the key question arises: how can one effectively tackle the combined problem of inference and optimization to identify the optimal policy? This paper assumes that the mild conditions for the existence of the optimal policy will consistently hold.
