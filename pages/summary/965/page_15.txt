The paper discusses various frameworks and approaches within reinforcement learning, highlighting key distinctions between multi-agent and single-agent environments. In a multi-agent framework, the environment consists of multiple intelligent agents, and it can be simplified to a single-agent framework by treating the states of other agents as unobservable. Game theory plays a crucial role in analyzing interactions among these agents.

The paper contrasts single-state environments, where optimal policy computation often involves maximizing a stochastic function, with multi-state environments that introduce additional complexity. It also differentiates between multi-objective reinforcement learning agents, which handle multi-dimensional reinforcement signals, and single-objective agents, which focus on a single goal.

Furthermore, it introduces the concept of risk-averse reinforcement learning agents, whose objective shifts from maximizing expected cumulative rewards to maximizing the minimum possible cumulative reward, reflecting a focus on minimizing potential losses. This overview encapsulates the essential themes and arguments presented in the paper.
