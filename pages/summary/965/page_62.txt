The paper discusses the concept of a contraction mapping within the context of a specific mapping denoted as H. It establishes that H is indeed a contraction mapping by demonstrating that the difference between two mappings K and K within a bounded set B(S × A) is bounded by a contraction factor γ. 

The argument is structured around the use of the supremum norm, showing that for any states s and actions a, the mapping satisfies the condition of contraction. Specifically, it uses an inequality to express that the maximum deviation between the outputs of the mapping H for different inputs is controlled by the maximum deviation of the mapping K, multiplied by the contraction factor γ. 

The paper also notes an additional assumption regarding the positivity of rewards, which is crucial for maintaining the validity of the contraction mapping property. This formalization is essential in establishing the stability and convergence properties of the mapping in the given context.
