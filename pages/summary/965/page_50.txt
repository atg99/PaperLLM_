In the described algorithm, the model ˆQN is generated through a process that iterates beyond one level (N > 1). It computes the expected value QN(s, a) by considering the rewards r(s, a, w) and the maximum expected future rewards derived from a previous iteration ˆQN−1. The training set TS is constructed from state-action pairs and their corresponding rewards, incorporating the maximum future reward estimates.

The motivation behind this approach is the assumption that ˆQN−1 serves as a sufficiently accurate approximation of QN−1, allowing it to be treated as equivalent. The paper suggests a mapping of various components, such as states and actions to inputs and outputs, and the reward function to a specific notation. By applying a supervised learning (SL) algorithm to the constructed training set, a model of QN is produced.

The algorithm concludes when the iteration count N reaches a predetermined threshold, at which point the policy ˆµ∗N(s) is derived as the action that maximizes the approximated Q-value ˆQN(s, a), serving as an approximation of the optimal policy µ∗(s).
