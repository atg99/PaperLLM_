The paper discusses the challenges of inferring the optimal policy ˆµ∗ in scenarios with very large or infinite state-action spaces, moving beyond the previously considered discrete and manageable spaces. In such cases, traditional tabular representations of ˆµ∗ and ˆQN-functions are insufficient, necessitating the use of function approximators to effectively represent these functions. It emphasizes the importance of these approximators being able to generalize well across the entire state-action space based on the information from ht. The paper highlights the extensive literature on function approximators within reinforcement learning and introduces 'fitted Q-iteration' as a key algorithm. This algorithm computes the ˆQN functions from ht by addressing a series of batch mode supervised learning problems, thereby adapting to the complexities of larger state-action spaces.
