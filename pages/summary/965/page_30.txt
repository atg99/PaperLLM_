The paper discusses the concept of optimal stationary policies in decision-making processes. It presents a theorem stating that a stationary policy µ∗ is considered optimal if it maximizes the expected value function Q(s, a) for a given state s. The value function Jµ∗(s) is defined as the maximum expected value that can be achieved from state s.

Additionally, the paper introduces the concept of N-optimal stationary policies, denoted as µ∗N. These policies are optimal when there are exactly N steps remaining, meaning they also maximize the expected value function QN(s, a) at that point in time. However, it is noted that an N-optimal policy is generally suboptimal compared to the overall optimal policy, as indicated by the relationship Jµ∗(s) being greater than or equal to Jµ∗N(s). This highlights the potential limitations of N-optimal policies in achieving the best possible outcome over a longer horizon.
