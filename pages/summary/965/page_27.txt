The paper discusses QN-functions and the Bellman equation in the context of dynamic programming. QN-functions are defined as a recurrence relation that maps state-action pairs to real numbers, with a base case of Q0(s, a) set to zero. Specifically, QN(s, a) is computed using the expected value of the reward function r and the maximum of the QN function from the previous iteration. 

The Q-function, on the other hand, is defined as the unique solution to the Bellman equation, which also incorporates the expected reward and the maximum Q-value for subsequent actions. 

A key theorem presented in the paper asserts that the sequence of QN-functions converges to the Q-function in the infinite norm, indicating that as the number of iterations increases, the difference between QN and Q approaches zero. This establishes the foundational relationship between QN-functions and the Q-function within dynamic programming frameworks.
