The paper discusses the transition from an inference problem to one of computational complexity in the context of the fitted Q-iteration algorithm. When only a limited number of one-step system transitions are available, the primary challenge lies in inference. However, as the number of transitions increases, specifically denoted as M, the computational complexity of the fitted Q-iteration algorithm escalates, growing at a rate of M log M when integrated with tree-based methods. This suggests that beyond a certain threshold of transitions, the problem shifts towards computational complexity. The paper poses an open research question regarding the potential of utilizing algorithms that possess inferior inference capabilities compared to the fitted Q-iteration algorithm yet are less computationally intensive, aiming to address the challenges posed by computational complexity.
