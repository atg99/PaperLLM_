The paper discusses several challenges in designing intelligent agents, focusing on three main difficulties. 

First, the inference problem arises when the dynamics of the environment and the reward mechanisms are not fully known. Agents must develop policies that can infer effective control actions based on limited information gathered through interactions with the environment.

Second, computational complexity is a concern as agents need to process historical observations efficiently within constraints of time and memory. This necessitates the design of policies that can manage and utilize past data effectively.

Lastly, there is a tradeoff between exploration and exploitation. To maximize rewards, agents should favor actions that have previously yielded positive outcomes. However, to identify these actions, they must also explore new options that have not been tested. This challenge is closely related to the inference problem and is often referred to as the dual control problem in classical control theory.
