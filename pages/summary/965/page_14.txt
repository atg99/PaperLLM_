The paper discusses various characterizations of reinforcement learning (RL) problems, highlighting key distinctions that shape the nature of these problems. It categorizes RL environments into stochastic and deterministic types, where stochastic environments involve random disturbances drawn from a conditional probability distribution, while deterministic environments do not include randomness. The paper also distinguishes between partial and full observability, indicating that a partially observable environment provides limited information about the state compared to a fully observable one.

Furthermore, it addresses the difference between time-invariant and time-variant dynamics. Time-invariant dynamics maintain consistent relationships over time, while time-variant dynamics incorporate time as a variable influencing state transitions. The paper contrasts continuous dynamics, which involve smooth changes over time, with discrete dynamics that focus on distinct state transitions. Lastly, it differentiates between finite and infinite time of interaction, which impacts the learning process and strategies employed in RL. Overall, these characterizations are essential for understanding the complexity and design of RL algorithms.
