The paper discusses the challenges faced by agents with limited computational resources in estimating the structure of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). It highlights that both the number of operations needed to estimate the MDP structure and the memory requirements increase linearly with time, leading to potential issues after a certain period of interaction. 

To address these problems, an algorithm is proposed that allows for updating the MDP structure at any time without the number of operations depending on time, and with memory requirements that do not grow with time. The algorithm initializes various counters and reward structures to zero at time zero. For each subsequent time step, the algorithm updates the count of state-action pairs, increments rewards based on received feedback, and calculates transition probabilities without the need for additional memory.

This approach effectively mitigates the impact of limited computational resources by ensuring that the operations and memory usage remain constant, regardless of the duration of interaction.
