The paper defines the history of an algorithm at stage k as Fk, which includes the elements R0 to Rk, e0 to ek, and other variables α0 to αk and η0 to ηk-1. It establishes three key conditions for the algorithm's convergence to a target R∗ with probability 1. 

Firstly, the expected value of ηk given Fk is zero, indicating that the algorithm's errors are centered around zero. Secondly, there are constants A and B such that the expected squared error, conditioned on the history, is bounded by A plus a term proportional to the squared norm of Rk, ensuring that the errors do not grow too large. Lastly, the nonnegative sequence αk(e) must diverge when summed over k, while the sum of their squares converges, which suggests that the algorithm incorporates sufficient variability in its updates to ensure convergence.

This framework provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for the algorithm's reliability and effectiveness in reaching the desired outcome.
