The paper presents a solution to a reinforcement learning problem using a recursive formula for the Q-values, defined as QN(s, a). The equation incorporates the immediate reward r(s, a) and the discounted future rewards, where γ represents the discount factor. The Q-values are calculated iteratively, with the initial condition set to zero for all state-action pairs.

The table outlines the computed Q-values for various states (s) and actions (a) across different iterations (N). For instance, QN(1, -1) and QN(2, -1) yield zero for the initial iterations, indicating no immediate reward or future value from those actions. In contrast, QN(2, 1) and QN(3, 1) demonstrate increasing values as the iterations progress, reflecting the accumulation of future rewards as influenced by the discount factor.

The results highlight the impact of action choices on the eventual Q-values, particularly emphasizing how future rewards can accumulate through strategic actions over time. This structured approach provides a clear view of the relationship between states, actions, and their corresponding Q-values in a reinforcement learning context.
