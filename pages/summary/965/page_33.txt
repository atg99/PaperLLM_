The paper presents a pragmatic model-based approach for designing optimal policies denoted as π∗. It emphasizes the importance of inferring a model of the environment based on available information, which allows for the computation of a stationary policy that is optimal in relation to this model. To address the exploration-exploitation tradeoff, the approach involves randomizing the stationary policy, enabling the selection of actions based on this randomized strategy.

The design of the functions π∗ is structured around three sequential tasks. The first task is the "system identification" phase, where the goal is to estimate an approximate system dynamics function (denoted as ˆf), an approximate probability distribution (ˆP), and an approximate reward function (ˆr) based on the information available at time h. This foundational step is crucial for developing effective policies that can adapt to the modeled environment.
