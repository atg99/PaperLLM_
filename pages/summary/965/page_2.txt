This paper provides a comprehensive overview of the reinforcement learning (RL) framework, beginning with an introduction to its foundational concepts. It characterizes RL problems by identifying key components such as states, actions, and rewards, which are essential for understanding how agents learn to make decisions. The primary goal of reinforcement learning is to develop strategies that maximize cumulative rewards over time.

The paper distinguishes between RL problems with small state-action spaces and those with large state-action spaces, highlighting the challenges and methodologies specific to each scenario. In small state-action spaces, the learning process can be more straightforward, allowing for quicker convergence. Conversely, large state-action spaces introduce complexities that necessitate more sophisticated approaches to ensure effective learning.

Finally, the paper discusses the convergence of Q-learning, a popular RL algorithm, detailing the conditions under which it is guaranteed to converge to an optimal policy. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding different RL problem characteristics and the implications for developing effective learning algorithms.
