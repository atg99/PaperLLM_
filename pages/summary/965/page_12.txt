The paper discusses the balance between exploration and exploitation in reinforcement learning (RL) agents. Agents must utilize their existing knowledge to maximize rewards while also exploring new actions to improve future decision-making. A key challenge is that focusing exclusively on either exploration or exploitation can hinder performance. The agent needs to try various actions, progressively favoring those that yield the highest expected rewards, which requires repeated trials, especially in stochastic environments.

Additionally, the exploration phase must be conducted safely to avoid unacceptable states that could jeopardize the agent's integrity. This can be managed by assigning a reward of negative infinity to such states, framing the challenge as one of exploration-exploitation. Furthermore, the paper highlights the complexity of operating in adversarial environments, where other players may actively undermine the agent's objectives, necessitating strategic adaptation.
