The paper discusses the extension of the Q-learning algorithm through the integration of parametric function approximators for the Q-function, denoted as ˜Q(s, a, θ). The desired update for this function is defined by the equation δ(st, at) = rt + γ max a∈A ˆQ(st+1, a, θ) - ˆQ(st, at, θ), which is applied after observing the state-action-reward transitions (st, at, rt, st+1). The update mechanism for the parameters θ is specified by the equation θ ← θ + αδ(st, at) ∂˜Q(st, at, θ)/∂θ, where α represents the learning rate. This approach allows for more flexible and efficient learning in environments where the state-action space is large, leveraging the benefits of function approximation to generalize learning across similar states and actions.
