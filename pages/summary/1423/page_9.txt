The paper presents a comparative analysis of various models designed for Direct Answer (DA) evaluation in vision-language tasks, particularly focusing on A-OKVQA. Several models, including Pythia, ViLBERT, LXMERT, KRISP, and GPV-2, were fine-tuned on A-OKVQA and evaluated based on their performance in both DA and Multiple-Choice (MC) settings. GPV-2 emerged as the top performer across all categories, attributed to its extensive learning of concepts.

The study also highlights the impact of ground-truth rationales on model performance. When GPV-2 was tested with these rationales, significant improvements were observed, indicating their utility in enhancing accuracy. However, the results suggested that the performance boost was not solely due to the rationales containing answers, as replacing the answers with a placeholder still yielded better results than without rationales.

Further analysis examined how different models performed based on answer frequency and knowledge types. The findings indicated that GPV-2, along with ClipCap and GR-GPT, excelled in predicting less common answers, while traditional discriminative models like LXMERT struggled with these cases. The paper concludes that the differences in performance across models provide insights into their strengths and weaknesses in handling various question types within A-OKVQA.
