The model described in the paper is trained autoregressively, generating output tokens conditionally based on input tokens, and is supervised using cross-entropy loss targeted at the next sequence token. During inference, the model is prompted with an image prefix and question tokens to predict the next likely token, repeating this process until the end-of-sequence token is produced. The text output is then decoded, excluding the end-of-sequence token. While beam search is an alternative for text generation, it was found to yield poorer results due to the brevity of the answers, which typically range from 1 to 3 words.

The model was fine-tuned over 10 epochs, selecting the checkpoint that achieved the best F1 validation score for generated answers, utilizing COCO pre-trained weights from the ClipCap authors. The pre-trained model employed CLIP ViT-B/32 features and produced 10 image prefix tokens, with GPT-2 also fine-tuned for the specific task.

For rationale generation, the process closely mirrored that of answer generation, with the ground-truth answer replaced by a ground-truth rationale. In this case, beam search was used during generation, which proved to be more effective for the longer rationale outputs.
