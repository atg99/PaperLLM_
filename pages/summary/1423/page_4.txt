The paper discusses the development of the A-OKVQA dataset, focusing on the processes for filtering images, collecting questions, and gathering rationales. Images with more than three objects were deemed "interesting" for training and validation, while a ResNet-50 classifier was employed to identify interesting images in the test set, achieving 78% accuracy. This filtering resulted in 23.7K unique images.

Question collection involved 437 crowd-workers who underwent a qualification task to ensure the questions required image analysis, commonsense reasoning, and originality. To reduce redundancy, images were clustered based on visual features, and workers were assigned similar images sequentially. A database of previously written questions was maintained to encourage diversity, and automated checks using Pythia filtered out trivial questions. Ultimately, 60% of post-qualification questions were excluded, culminating in 37,687 questions.

Rationales were collected for each question, where workers explained the correctness of answers in concise terms, ensuring high-quality output through qualification processes. The final dataset comprises 24,903 Question/Answer/Rationale triplets, with a distribution of 17.1K for training, 1.1K for validation, and 6.7K for testing, adhering to COCO 2017 splits.

The dataset demonstrates a long-tail distribution of answer options, with significant overlap between training and validation/test sets, indicating some similarity in answers but also highlighting the need for models to generate out-of-distribution responses. Compared to other datasets, A-OKVQA features a greater number of questions while maintaining diversity and includes rationales, setting it apart from similar datasets like OK-VQA and VCR.
