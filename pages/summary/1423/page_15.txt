The paper details the dataset collection process, highlighting the quality control measures taken to ensure high standards in the questions generated. Approximately 60% of questions were rejected due to various flaws, primarily focusing on four categories: questions that required only recognition of common objects, counting specified objects, did not necessitate image analysis, or merely inquired about the color of identifiable objects. Examples of these flawed questions are illustrated in Figure 4.

The data collection interface utilized by crowdworkers is described, showcasing the structured approach to question generation. Workers received clear instructions and examples of acceptable and unacceptable questions. After composing a question, they were prompted to check for similarity to existing questions in the dataset, which encouraged originality. Workers were required to write questions for only two out of four images to avoid producing unsuitable questions. This approach aimed to enhance the quality of the dataset by allowing workers to skip images they found challenging.

Additionally, the rationale writing process is outlined, where workers confirmed the correctness of answers before providing succinct explanations. This step ensured that any discrepancies in answers led to the removal of conflicting questions from the dataset.

For the large-scale pre-trained models discussed in Section 5.2, the vocabulary was generated from the training set, including all correct answers and choices appearing in at least three questions, culminating in a vocabulary of 10,424 answers. This meticulous approach reflects the authors' commitment to maintaining high-quality data for their research.
