The paper presents a comparative analysis of various models' performance on a question-answering task categorized by answer frequency and knowledge type. The models evaluated include VilBERT, LXMERT, KRISP, ClipCap, GR-GPT, and GPV-2. 

In terms of answer frequency, GPV-2 consistently outperforms other models, achieving the highest accuracy across all categories, particularly for answers that appear 201 times or more. The results indicate that while GPV-2 leads in overall performance, models like KRISP excel in specific knowledge types, particularly in Commonsense Knowledge where it surpasses VilBERT and LXMERT. 

A detailed analysis of prediction overlap reveals that only a small percentage (5.85%) of questions were correctly answered by all models, highlighting significant variability in performance. The paper also notes that 30.96% of questions were not correctly answered by any model, underscoring the challenges faced in this domain. 

Further exploration of mistake patterns shows that even the lowest performing model (GR-GPT) answered questions correctly that the best model (GPV-2) did not, indicating different models have unique strengths and weaknesses. A majority voting approach among the models yielded a lower accuracy than using GPV-2 alone, emphasizing the latter's superior performance. 

Finally, qualitative analysis identified specific reasoning shortcomings in the models, demonstrating the need for improvement in addressing various knowledge types. Overall, the findings suggest that while GPV-2 is the most effective model, there remains considerable room for advancement in multi-modal question-answering systems.
