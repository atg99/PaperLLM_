The paper discusses the limitations of existing Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets and presents the A-OKVQA dataset as an improvement. Existing datasets like KVQA and OK-VQA primarily focus on knowledge retrieval from fixed sources or simple lookup questions, which do not require significant reasoning. OK-VQA, while an advancement, still leans towards basic knowledge queries that lack complexity.

The authors highlight that their dataset, A-OKVQA, emphasizes common-sense knowledge and reasoning, requiring deeper cognitive engagement to answer questions. It includes rationale annotations that help assess the reasoning capabilities of knowledge-based VQA systems. The paper also compares A-OKVQA to other datasets like S3VQA, Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR), and VisualCOMET, noting their specific focuses on human intentions or scene graphs, which differ from A-OKVQA's broader object-centric approach.

The authors reference the CLEVR dataset as an example of visual reasoning that, despite its structured questions, lacks realism and richness. They argue that A-OKVQA's reliance on real images and free-form language enhances its applicability and challenge.

Furthermore, the paper touches on the intersection of knowledge and commonsense reasoning in natural language processing, contrasting it with the question-answering capabilities of existing models. The dataset is built from the COCO dataset, known for its diverse and visually rich images, making it suitable for generating complex questions that require external knowledge. Overall, A-OKVQA aims to push the boundaries of VQA by integrating reasoning and common-sense knowledge into the question-answering process.
