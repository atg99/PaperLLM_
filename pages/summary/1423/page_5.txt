The paper presents an analysis of the A-OKVQA dataset, focusing on the types of knowledge required to answer questions within it. A-OKVQA comprises 24,903 questions, with a significant proportion requiring commonsense knowledge, visual knowledge, and knowledge from established knowledge bases. The dataset's unique feature is its requirement for broader knowledge types compared to existing VQA datasets like FVQA, OK-VQA, and S3VQA, which often emphasize fixed knowledge bases or factoid knowledge.

The authors compare A-OKVQA to several other VQA datasets, noting that A-OKVQA has the longest average question length and the highest number of unique words, indicating its complexity. A quantitative analysis of a subset of 1,000 questions reveals that the predominant knowledge types needed include commonsense knowledge about human behavior, visual knowledge, knowledge from databases, and physical knowledge acquired through everyday experiences.

Overall, the findings suggest that A-OKVQA not only tests factual knowledge but also emphasizes the importance of commonsense reasoning and visual interpretation in answering questions. This positions A-OKVQA as a versatile dataset for advancing the field of visual question answering.
