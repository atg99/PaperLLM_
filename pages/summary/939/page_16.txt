The referenced studies explore various applications of machine learning and statistical methods in medical diagnosis and prognosis. Chang et al. (2016) compared computer-aided diagnosis for thyroid nodules against radiologist assessments, highlighting the potential of machine learning in ultrasound image analysis. Several studies utilized support vector machines (SVM) for cancer detection and prognosis, including microcalcifications (El-Naqa et al., 2002), breast cancer (Polat & Güneş, 2007; Akay, 2009), and lymph node metastasis in esophageal and gastric cancers (Wang et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2011). 

Research also focused on gene expression data, employing SVMs for cancer classification (Brown et al., 2000; Furey et al., 2000), with Golub et al. (1999) and Pomeroy et al. (2002) demonstrating molecular classification's efficacy in cancer outcomes. Studies on neuroimaging and psychiatric disorders utilized SVM to identify biomarkers (Orrù et al., 2012; Costafreda et al., 2009), while predictive models for heart disease and sepsis outcomes leveraged various machine learning techniques (Palaniappan & Awang, 2008; Jaimes et al., 2005).

Other notable works include decision tree analyses for predicting clinical outcomes in mesothelioma (Brims et al., 2016) and Crohn's disease (Dias et al., 2017), as well as algorithms predicting drug response in epilepsy (Silva-Alves et al., 2017). The application of machine learning extended to genomic data for predicting Alzheimer’s disease (Wei et al., 2011; Kim & Kim, 2018) and understanding gene-nutrient interactions related to breast cancer (Naushad et al., 2016).

Epidemiological studies utilized machine learning for mapping disease distribution and environmental suitability for diseases like Ebola and Zika (Messina et al., 2016; Pigott et al., 2014). Overall, these studies illustrate the significant potential of machine learning techniques in enhancing diagnostic accuracy, prognostic predictions, and understanding disease patterns in various medical fields.
