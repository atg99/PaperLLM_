This paper discusses the integration of advanced machine learning algorithms into epidemiologic research, highlighting both the potential benefits and existing challenges. Machine learning has the capacity to lower entry barriers for epidemiologists and revolutionize research methodologies. However, many traditionally trained epidemiologists lack the necessary skills to fully utilize these technologies due to the continued reliance on closed-source programming languages like SAS and Stata. 

Logistical issues surrounding the sharing of de-identified patient data and the accumulation of large healthcare datasets pose significant barriers compared to the private sector. Furthermore, the class imbalance often present in healthcare and surveillance data can complicate the application of standard machine learning algorithms. Despite the methodological similarities between machine learning and statistical methods, the general lack of familiarity with these algorithms among epidemiologists hinders their practical adoption.

The authors argue that while machine learning algorithms can provide new solutions to problems inadequately addressed by classical methods, they cannot compensate for poor study design or data quality. Bridging the cultural and technical gaps between machine learning and epidemiology is crucial for enhancing the integration of these approaches into epidemiologic research.
