2. **Super Learner and Targeted Maximum Likelihood Estimation**: van der Laan and colleagues present the Super Learner, a machine learning ensemble method that enhances the robustness of causal estimates, which is further explored in applications such as HIV-1 drug resistance prediction.

3. **Propensity Score Methods**: Several studies focus on the use of propensity scores to reduce bias in observational studies. Rosenbaum and Rubin's foundational work on subclassification is complemented by newer approaches utilizing machine learning techniques for propensity score estimation, as discussed by Westreich et al. and others.

4. **Causal Inference Techniques**: The papers explore various methods for causal inference, including recursive partitioning for heterogeneous effects and Bayesian networks for causal discovery, as detailed by Athey, Imbens, and others.

5. **Applications in Health and Epidemiology**: Several studies apply these statistical methodologies to real-world health issues, such as vaccine safety during pregnancy, the impact of childhood adversities on mental health, and the long-term effects of early sexual initiation on health outcomes.

6. **Machine Learning in Medical Diagnostics**: The application of machine learning techniques, including support vector machines, in distinguishing between tumor types and treatment effects in medical imaging is highlighted, illustrating the intersection of advanced statistical methods and healthcare.

Overall, these references underscore the evolution of statistical methodologies in addressing complex causal questions in various fields, particularly in epidemiology and medical research.
