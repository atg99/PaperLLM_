The paper discusses the application of model reduction techniques and neural networks to parametric partial differential equations (PDEs). It highlights the importance of controlling approximation errors in the context of neural networks approximating functions derived from these PDEs. 

Theorem 3.4 is referenced, establishing a method to control the error between the neural network output and the PCA approximation. The relationship between these errors is expressed mathematically, showcasing bounds on the error terms, which depend on Lipschitz continuity and the properties of the involved functions.

The authors proceed to approximate a function ϕ with a neural network χ, emphasizing that ϕ is Lipschitz continuous. They transform the components of ϕ into a suitable domain, allowing the construction of neural networks that approximate each component within a specified error margin. The parameters for these networks are derived from the dimensions and desired accuracy, ensuring that the overall neural network χ maintains a close approximation to ϕ.

An important set A is defined, which contains inputs where the transformed function remains bounded. The approximation error ePCA is analyzed, and its expectation is decomposed into contributions from set A and its complement. The first term of this decomposition is shown to be bounded by the error tolerance τ, utilizing the Lipschitz property of GY and the closeness of χ to ϕ.

Overall, the paper demonstrates how neural networks can effectively approximate functions related to parametric PDEs while maintaining control over the approximation errors through careful mathematical formulation and analysis.
