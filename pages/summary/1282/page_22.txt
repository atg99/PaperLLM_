The paper discusses model reduction and the application of neural networks for solving parametric partial differential equations (PDEs), specifically focusing on the Poisson equation. It highlights the challenges associated with using deep neural networks, particularly their tendency to struggle with optimization as the complexity increases, which can result in suboptimal solutions. The authors compare their method with that of Chkifa, noting that while linear maps improve consistently with reduced dimensions, neural networks face difficulties due to local minima and potential overfitting.

The study employs a model where the right-hand side of the Poisson equation is represented by a summation involving independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) sequences. The solution operator is characterized as linear, and a Taylor series representation is provided to demonstrate the relationship between the input and the solution. The authors emphasize the importance of incorporating prior knowledge about the solution map, such as linearity, to simplify the optimization process and enhance the approximation scheme.

Figures included in the paper illustrate the performance of the proposed methods, showing relative test errors in relation to the amount of training data and the reduced dimension. The findings suggest that while additional data can improve neural network performance, it may not guarantee the discovery of the optimal solution. Overall, the paper advocates for a structured approach that leverages the inherent properties of the problem to achieve better results in solving parametric PDEs.
