The paper discusses model reduction and the application of neural networks for parametric partial differential equations (PDEs). It presents Lemma B.1, which establishes a relationship between the eigenvalues of a non-negative, self-adjoint, compact operator C on a separable Hilbert space H and the sum of these eigenvalues over a collection of d orthonormal elements. The proof demonstrates that for any arbitrary set of orthonormal elements, the sum of their associated eigenvalues is bounded by the maximum eigenvalue, leading to an inequality that is fundamental in understanding the behavior of the operator C.

The paper also introduces Theorem 3.4, which relies on a Monte Carlo estimate of the Hilbert-Schmidt distance between the operators C and CN, as defined in the context. Lemma B.2 further asserts that there exists a constant Q, dependent solely on a parameter ν, such that the expected value of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm difference between CN and C scales inversely with N, thereby providing a quantitative measure of the approximation error in the context of model reduction.

Overall, the results emphasize the importance of spectral analysis in the reduction of complex systems described by PDEs and the effectiveness of using neural networks in this context.
