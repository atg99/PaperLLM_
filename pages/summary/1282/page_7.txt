The paper discusses a computational methodology for function approximation that combines Principal Component Analysis (PCA) with neural networks. The authors introduce an approximation framework where the mapping is denoted as ΨPCA, representing a composition of dimension reduction via PCA and a neural network approximation. The practical implementation replaces the theoretical mapping with a data-driven approximation, referred to as ΨNN, using neural networks as the approximation for the unknown function.

The authors highlight that while PCA is a straightforward and effective method for dimension reduction, it is not always easily inverted, unlike other methods rooted in neural networks. They note that despite the empirical success of deep autoencoders, these methods currently lack a theoretical foundation for function spaces, which limits their applicability to the goals of this study.

The paper emphasizes the importance of dimension reduction in both input and output spaces for enabling effective function space algorithms, particularly those utilizing neural networks. The authors express the potential for nonlinear dimension-reducing maps, such as deep autoencoders, which could enhance the approximation of nonlinear operators in future research.

In the second section, the authors detail the application of PCA within a real, separable Hilbert space, supported by a probability measure with a finite fourth moment. They outline the training process based on a finite collection of independent and identically distributed samples drawn from the measure. The methodology is applied in two contexts: first, to the input space and second, to the output space derived from the operator Ψ. The subsequent analysis aims to explore the implications of this approach further.
