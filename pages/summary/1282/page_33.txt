The paper presents a theorem, Theorem C.1, which establishes conditions under which a certain limit involving a functional Ψ converges to zero. Specifically, it asserts that if there exists a probability p in the interval (0,1) such that the sequence of norms of functions φj belongs to the space lp(N;R), then the limit of the supremum of the difference between Ψ(ξ) and K, as K approaches infinity, equals zero. The proof employs linearity and the Poincaré inequality to derive a Lipschitz estimate, leading to the conclusion through the application of Stechkin’s inequality.

In Appendix D, the authors discuss the relative test error during the training of neural networks for linear elliptic problems. They note a tendency for overfitting when using a larger number of training samples in conjunction with a high-dimensional parameterization, as the underlying map is linear while the neural network is non-linear. This suggests that simpler neural network architectures may yield better results, although the authors do not pursue this avenue further. Instead, they emphasize the advantage of incorporating prior knowledge about the problem, particularly linearity, which is illustrated in the figures presented. The figures (D.1 and D.2) depict the relative test errors for varying numbers of training samples across different epochs.
