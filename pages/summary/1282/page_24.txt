The paper discusses the application of model reduction techniques and neural networks to parametric partial differential equations (PDEs), specifically focusing on the Darcy flow problem with piecewise constant coefficients. Figures presented in the paper illustrate the relationship between training data, resolution, and reduced dimensions in terms of relative test errors. 

In Figure 4.7, panel (a) indicates how errors change with varying resolution while maintaining a fixed number of training examples (N=1024). Panel (b) maintains a constant mesh size of 421x421 and explores the error relative to reduced dimensions. Panel (c) specifically examines the neural network approach, showing that increased training data leads to a reduction in error for fixed mesh and reduced dimensions.

Figure 4.8 contrasts online and offline computation times for the same problem setup, highlighting the efficiency of the method on a single GPU (GTX 1080 Ti).

The paper emphasizes that increasing the amount of training data can significantly reduce approximation errors, aligning with the theoretical framework presented in Theorem 3.1. This theorem suggests that the effectiveness of reduced dimensions relies on the properties of the probability measure and its pushforward. Therefore, the size of the training dataset should scale with the number of reduced dimensions to ensure that errors associated with finite data approximation are comparable to those from the finite-dimensional approximation.

Additionally, the paper compares the proposed method with the reduced basis (RB) method utilizing Principal Component Analysis (PCA), implemented through a Galerkin projection to expand the solution in the PCA basis.
