The paper presents a proof involving the properties of specific maps and their Lipschitz continuity. It begins by defining the operator C(uj) as the tensor product uj⊗uj for j in the set {1,...,N} and C(u) as u⊗u for any u in a Hilbert space H. The expectation of C(u) under a distribution ν is shown to equal C, and it is noted that the expected squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm of C(u) is finite.

Using Jensen's inequality, the paper establishes bounds on the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of C, leading to the conclusion that the difference between the expected norm and the norm of C is related to the average of the norms of the individual C(uj). This forms the basis for setting Q as the difference between the expected squared norm of C(u) and the squared norm of C, completing the proof.

Additionally, Lemma B.3 is introduced to estimate the Lipschitz constants of the maps FX, FY, GX, and GY, demonstrating that these maps satisfy global Lipschitz conditions. If a function Ψ is locally Lipschitz, it is shown that another function ϕ derived from Ψ retains the local Lipschitz property, with bounds established based on a symmetric, increasing function L.

The proofs for the Lipschitz properties of FY and GX utilize the eigenvectors of empirical covariance matrices, employing Parseval's identity to relate differences in the maps to the norms of the inputs. Overall, the paper underscores the mathematical relationships between various functions and their continuity properties in the context of Hilbert spaces.
