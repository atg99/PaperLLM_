The paper discusses the mathematical formulation of non-centered covariance operators within the context of Hilbert spaces, particularly focusing on the existence and uniqueness of a self-adjoint, non-negative, trace-class operator C. This operator is defined through its relation to expected values involving random variables drawn from a distribution with a finite second moment. The paper establishes that C can be expressed as the expected value of the outer product of random variables, leading to the derivation of its trace and implications for eigenvalues and eigenvectors.

In the infinite data setting, the authors introduce the optimal projection space Vd, which spans the first d eigenvectors of C. The relationship between Vd and finite approximations derived from data, denoted as Vd,N, is explored, indicating that the quality of these approximations is influenced by the decay of the eigenvalues of C. The Karhunen-Loeve Theorem is referenced to further elaborate on the representation of random variables in terms of their eigenstructure.

The paper also transitions into the discussion of neural networks, characterizing them as nonlinear functions defined through compositions of affine maps and nonlinear activations. The focus is on the architecture of neural networks, particularly those using the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation function. The parameters of the network, including weights and biases, are learned through regression problems using stochastic gradient methods. The authors emphasize the empirical success of neural networks as effective regressors and interpolators for high-dimensional data, while acknowledging the need for a comprehensive theoretical framework.
