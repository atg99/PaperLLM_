The paper presents a proof that generalizes a previously established result, focusing on bounding the average excess error between two quantities related to subspaces in a Hilbert space. The authors start by deriving a bound on the average excess error using specific lemmas, including one based on Fan's Theorem. They demonstrate that the expected value of the difference between the empirical average and the theoretical average can be controlled through the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.

Key steps in the proof involve manipulating the properties of orthogonal projection operators and applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities to obtain bounds on the expected values. The relation between the eigenvalues of two operators, CN and C, is examined, leading to the conclusion that the average excess error remains bounded under certain conditions. 

The authors further utilize Fan's Theorem to relate the sum of eigenvalues of C to the maximum of a functional over subspaces, establishing connections between theoretical constructs and empirical observations. The results culminate in an estimate for the expected value of the empirical average, taking into account both the Monte Carlo error and the eigenvalue contributions from the tail.

Overall, the proof intricately combines theoretical results with practical implications, grounding the argument in established mathematical principles while advancing the understanding of error bounds in the context of subspace projections in Hilbert spaces.
