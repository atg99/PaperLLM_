The paper by K. Bhattacharya, B. Hosseini, and others focuses on the approximation of nonlinear operators, particularly in the context of the Burgers' equation. The authors note that their method facilitates the handling of non-Lipschitz input-output maps, which is a significant advancement in the field. They provide numerical examples involving both linear and nonlinear operators, highlighting that while a neural network can approximate a linear operator effectively, traditional optimization methods may struggle to identify the optimal solution. This suggests a potential advantage in incorporating known properties, such as linearity, into the parameterization process.

The authors employ a consistent neural network architecture across their computational examples, utilizing a 5-layer dense network with specified layer widths and the SELU nonlinearity. They outline the training process using stochastic gradient descent with Nesterov momentum, emphasizing the importance of learning rates and the benefits of initializing with pre-trained networks for improved convergence.

The study examines second-order elliptic PDEs, particularly those representing various scientific applications, and evaluates different input-output maps generated by the solution operator. They present numerical results that validate their theoretical developments and demonstrate the effectiveness of their methodology in both Lipschitz and non-Lipschitz settings. Additionally, the paper addresses the one-dimensional viscous Burgers' equation, detailing its weak solutions and the flow map's properties at a fixed time.

Overall, the findings illustrate the advantages of the proposed neural network approximation over traditional linear methods, particularly in complex nonlinear scenarios like the Darcy flow problem and the Burgers' equation, where the latter exhibits significant performance advantages.
