In this paper by K. Bhattacharya, B. Hosseini, et al., various probability measures denoted as µG, µL, µP, and µB are introduced and illustrated through representative samples. These measures are utilized in different subsections to model inputs and analyze specific problems. The authors explore the Lipschitz continuity of functions and derive inequalities related to the integral of the differences between transformed functions, which leads to critical results regarding the approximation of parametric partial differential equations (PDEs).

The core contribution of the paper lies in presenting a novel methodology for approximating parametric PDEs. The authors advocate for constructing neural network architectures as mappings between Hilbert spaces before discretization, contrasting with traditional methods that discretize first. This approach ensures that the resulting numerical methods exhibit uniform properties concerning the mesh size used in discretization.

The paper includes numerical experiments that validate both theoretical results and practical applicability beyond theoretical confines. Specifically, the authors demonstrate the robustness of their method against various discretizations while approximating the input-output relationships defined by different classes of parametric elliptic PDEs, including the Darcy model for porous media flow and the parabolic Burgers’ equation. Subsequent subsections provide detailed numerical results for Lipschitz maps, the Darcy flow problem, and associated flow maps, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed methodology in practical scenarios.
