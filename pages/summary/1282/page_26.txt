This paper presents a data-driven methodology for model reduction and neural networks applied to parametric partial differential equations (PDEs), specifically focusing on Burgers' equation and Darcy flow problems. The authors analyze the performance of their approach by examining relative test errors across different mesh resolutions and reduced dimensions.

The findings indicate that when using a small mesh for training, errors increase with larger meshes due to interpolation issues; however, this increase is minimal. Conversely, training on larger meshes results in negligible error increases on smaller meshes, suggesting that the neural network captures intrinsic properties of the solution operator that are independent of discretization.

For Burgers' equation, the input-output mapping is demonstrated to be nonlinear and locally Lipschitz, although the precise Lipschitz constant is unknown. The numerical results validate the efficacy of the methodology, showing that the neural network outperforms linear mappings due to the stronger nonlinearity of the solution operator in this case. The paper also notes that increasing the reduced dimension does not always lead to improved error rates, as it complicates the optimization problem. However, increasing the training dataset helps mitigate this issue, as illustrated by the results showing the relationship between error and reduced dimension improves with more training data.

In conclusion, the proposed methodology effectively learns mappings between separable Hilbert spaces and exhibits mesh-independent behavior, making it a promising tool for parametric PDEs. The authors establish consistency with PCA in globally Lipschitz forward maps, reinforcing the robustness of their approach.
