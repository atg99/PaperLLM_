The paper discusses the performance of a method applied to the Darcy flow problem with log-normal coefficients, as illustrated in Figure 4.6. Using 1024 training examples, panel (a) presents relative test errors based on mesh resolution, while panel (b) maintains a fixed 421x421 mesh to analyze error as a function of reduced dimension. Panel (c) focuses on results from a neural network, keeping the mesh fixed to evaluate error with varying training data amounts.

The authors reference a power series that is summable in the Hilbert space H1 0(D;R), establishing the analyticity of the input-output map Ψ, which is crucial for their method's validation. They utilize the method of Chkifa through truncation of the power series, highlighting that this approach requires solving the Poisson equation, which they compare against their method using the same number of data points.

While Chkifa's method shows superior performance for a fixed number of PDE solves, the authors note that their method has the advantage of requiring no knowledge of the model or the Poisson equation, relying solely on training data.

In the context of Darcy flow, the mapping Ψ from L∞(D;R+) to H1 0(D;R) is considered, with the solution operator being nonlinear and locally Lipschitz. Despite the complications arising from this nonlinearity, the authors assert that their methodology maintains competitive numerical results. They observe that error is invariant to mesh refinement and that while the neural network outperforms the linear approach, increasing the reduced dimension does not consistently lead to improved error due to optimization complexity. This is confirmed in the analysis presented in panels (b) of Figures 4.6 and 4.7.
