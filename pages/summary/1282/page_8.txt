The paper discusses the application of non-centered Principal Component Analysis (PCA) in the context of model reduction for parametric partial differential equations (PDEs). It begins by establishing the framework for PCA, emphasizing the use of a standard non-kernelized approach to facilitate the construction of a decoder. 

Key concepts include the definition of the empirical projection error, which measures the discrepancy between the original data and its projection onto a finite-dimensional subspace, as well as the empirical covariance operator that underpins PCA. The covariance operator is shown to be a self-adjoint, trace-class operator, with eigenvectors and eigenvalues that characterize the data distribution.

The paper outlines the formation of PCA subspaces and presents a minimization problem to find the subspace that minimizes the projection error. It introduces the PCA encoder, which maps data from the Hilbert space to coefficient representations in a lower-dimensional space, and the PCA decoder, which reconstructs data from these coefficients. 

The relationship between the encoder and decoder is established, demonstrating that the composition of these two operations approximates the identity mapping in the context of a d-dimensional subspace. Finally, the paper hints at extending the discussion to a more general setting by considering the projection error in an infinite data scenario, setting the stage for further quantitative analysis in subsequent sections.
