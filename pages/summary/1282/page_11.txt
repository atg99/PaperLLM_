The paper by K. Bhattacharya, B. Hosseini, et al. presents a novel methodology that distinguishes itself from traditional Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) through its approach to coefficient formation. Unlike RBMs, which utilize an intrusive method that requires knowledge of the underlying partial differential equations (PDEs), the proposed method employs regression or interpolation of latent representations derived from the available dataset. This non-intrusive technique allows for application in situations where the PDE mapping is unknown, making it suitable for black-box models.

The authors relate their approximate solution operator to Taylor approximations, where a specific form of input is analyzed. They demonstrate that if the solution operator is analytic, a Taylor expansion can be utilized to approximate the operator by truncating it to a finite subset of indices. However, this traditional method necessitates knowledge of the PDE, contrasting with their approach.

In the section on approximation theory, the authors establish a significant result proving that for any given error margin, an epsilon-approximation of the solution operator can be achieved by carefully selecting PCA truncation parameters, data amounts, and a sufficiently robust neural network architecture. They define the components involved in their approximation process and present Theorem 3.1, which asserts that under certain conditions, it is possible to ensure the approximation error remains below a specified threshold. This theorem is built upon further results, including Theorem 3.5, which is addressed under the assumption of a globally Lipschitz map. The findings are significant for the development of data-driven methods that do not rely on explicit PDE knowledge.
