The paper presents Theorem 3.5, which establishes conditions for the existence of a zero-extended stacked neural network that approximates a measurable, globally Lipschitz map between real, separable Hilbert spaces. Given a probability measure supported on one of the spaces, the theorem provides a framework to control the approximation error through the selection of parameters related to the dimensions of the spaces, the size of the dataset, and the error tolerance. 

Specifically, the theorem highlights that the neural network's parameters depend on the dimensions of the spaces, the dataset size, and an error parameter, with the goal of ensuring that certain intrinsic properties of the measures remain small. The approximation error is analyzed in terms of expectations over the dataset, leading to a bound that combines contributions from both the neural network approximation and the finite-dimensional approximations of the spaces involved. The proof involves estimating this error and indicates that as the error parameters approach zero, the complexity of the network may increase, necessitating further investigation into the computational implications of this growth.

The results underscore the interplay between the choice of parameters and the effectiveness of the neural network in approximating the desired map, with the theorem providing a structured approach to achieving accurate approximations in this context.
