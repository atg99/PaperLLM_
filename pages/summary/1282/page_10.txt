The paper discusses the use of model reduction and neural networks for approximating solutions to parametric partial differential equations (PDEs). It highlights the challenges in achieving effective neural network architectures for this purpose, referencing various theoretical frameworks for their approximation capabilities.

In Section 3, the authors focus on a specific class of neural networks, specifically those utilizing the ReLU activation function. They define a set of neural networks that map input vectors to output vectors, with constraints on the number of non-zero weights and biases, which indicates the model's complexity. They also introduce zero-extended neural networks to manage the non-compactness of latent spaces after applying Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction.

The paper compares their approach to existing methodologies, particularly in the context of PCA. It establishes a clear representation of the approximation function that emerges when PCA is employed, contrasting it with the general setting involving arbitrary encoders. The authors denote the relationship between the input data and the output space using coefficients derived from the PCA eigenvectors, which parallels classical reduced basis methods.

Overall, the paper presents a framework that integrates neural networks with model reduction techniques, emphasizing the importance of structured approximations in solving parametric PDEs efficiently.
