The paper discusses the integration of model reduction techniques with neural networks for solving parametric partial differential equations (PDEs). It highlights various methodologies and architectures employed in prior studies, specifically addressing their limitations, particularly concerning robustness to mesh refinement. Existing approaches often rely on neural networks that map discretized values, which may not yield consistent results across different discretizations, necessitating architecture overhauls.

The authors note that while some recent works have started addressing these issues by introducing architectures based on neural network approximation theorems, they still face challenges with mesh-dependent parameters. An alternative method focuses on parametrizing the solution via neural networks, akin to collocation methods in numerical PDE solutions, where the neural network approximates the solution using spatial or spatio-temporal variables as inputs. This method requires retraining for new input parameters, which can be computationally expensive and cannot be fully data-driven due to the need for knowledge of the underlying PDE.

The paper also references the study of neural networks as a flow of time-dependent PDEs and touches upon earlier works that explored learning PDEs from data. However, these approaches often consider fixed discretizations, limiting their applicability to infinite-dimensional spaces.

The historical context of model reduction methods is provided, tracing back to the 1970s with the development of reduced basis methods (RBMs) that represent PDE solutions using known basis functions. This foundational work in engineering has led to further exploration of approximation theory and rates of convergence for RBM approximations, indicating a rich background for the ongoing research in this domain.
