This paper discusses the performance of a neural network-based method for solving Darcy flow problems compared to traditional reduced basis methods (RBM). It highlights the efficiency of the proposed method in terms of online and offline runtimes, particularly when transferring solutions across different mesh sizes. The authors note that while both methods yield similar error rates, the neural network significantly reduces the time required for online computations. Specifically, the RBM takes approximately 28 hours to compute 5000 test solutions, while the neural network achieves this in just 28 minutes, with further reductions possible through parallelization.

The paper emphasizes that the neural network, despite having the highest offline cost, is more practical due to its low online cost. In contrast, the linear map method offers a slightly lower online cost and comparable offline costs, making it suitable for linear operators and scenarios where some approximation error is acceptable.

The authors also address the limitations of existing image-to-image regression methods, which do not scale well with mesh size, and suggest the potential for developing neural networks that maintain performance independence from mesh size. Lastly, the paper indicates that the proposed method can effectively transfer learned solutions between different meshes through interpolation or sub-sampling, contingent on a smoothness assumption regarding the PCA basis, which remains an area for future research.
