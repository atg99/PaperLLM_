This paper addresses the critical issue of disinfection in public spaces to combat the spread of infectious diseases, particularly in light of the COVID-19 pandemic. The authors propose a robotic solution designed to disinfect walls and other surfaces, emphasizing the need for the robot to effectively follow walls while maintaining a specified distance. This capability is essential for operation in unknown environments and for dealing with uncertainties.

To achieve this, the study introduces two fuzzy logic systems: a non-singleton Type 1 Fuzzy Logic System (T1-FLS) and a non-singleton Interval Type 2 Fuzzy Logic System (IT2-FLS). The performance of these systems in enabling wall-following behavior was evaluated through simulations that considered various wall configurations. The results indicate that the IT2-FLS outperforms the T1-FLS, showcasing its potential for real-world applications in wall disinfection tasks.

The paper reinforces the importance of robotic solutions in reducing human exposure to pathogens during disinfection processes and highlights the effectiveness of fuzzy logic systems in achieving robust wall-following behavior for disinfection robots.
