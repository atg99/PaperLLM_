The paper discusses a novel method for a wall disinfection robot that maintains a specified distance from walls during disinfection tasks. Effective sterilization relies on the distance, as the intensity of the disinfectant agent—whether liquid, gas, or ultraviolet light—depends on it. The proposed method utilizes online path planning based on range sensor information, employing fuzzy logic to establish wall-following behavior. Two types of fuzzy logic systems are introduced: a Type 1 Fuzzy Logic System (T1-FLS) and an Interval Type 2 Fuzzy Logic System (IT2-FLS), allowing users to adjust the lateral distance without redesigning the system.

Simulations demonstrated that both fuzzy systems could effectively follow walls, with IT2-FLS outperforming T1-FLS significantly. The simulations used realistic parameters, suggesting that the results would translate well to real-world applications. Future work is proposed to explore adaptive parameters for the disinfection mechanisms based on distance and to conduct real-world trials with the robot. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of robot-aided solutions in reducing the spread of infectious diseases through effective surface disinfection.
