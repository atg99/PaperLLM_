The findings indicate that the proposed fuzzy logic systems (FLSs) successfully enabled the robot to follow walls effectively across various configurations of lateral distance (dT). The average lateral errors observed were minimal, suggesting the FLSs' robustness and generalization capabilities. 

In the validation phases, the performance of the FLSs was rigorously tested against various wall arrangements, establishing their suitability for wall-following tasks, particularly for disinfection robots. The IT2-FLS demonstrated superior performance compared to the T1-FLS, particularly in maintaining desired distances from walls. This aspect is critical for the functionality of disinfection robots, though future assessments are recommended to include additional performance indicators such as time and energy efficiency.

The study highlights the advantage of the proposed FLSs over traditional methods, which often require retraining or redesign when the lateral distance is adjusted. The flexibility of the proposed systems allows for easy modifications to the lateral distance without the need for redesign, making them particularly effective for dynamic disinfection requirements. However, some deviations were noted in concave wall regions, indicating potential areas for further improvement in the wall-following algorithm.
