The paper discusses the development and architecture of an Interval Type 2 Fuzzy Logic System (IT2-FLS) designed for wall-following behavior in robotics. IT2-FLSs are acknowledged for their complexity and higher computational requirements compared to Type 1 Fuzzy Logic Systems (T1-FLSs), yet they offer improved performance.

The architecture of the IT2-FLS includes a fuzziﬁcation layer where inputs are processed using input membership functions. Unlike T1-FLSs, which rely on crisp values, IT2-FLSs utilize a Footprint Of Uncertainty (FOU) defined by two fuzzy sets: the Lower Membership Function (LMF) and the Upper Membership Function (UMF). This allows for a range of activation degrees rather than a single value, leading to enhanced decision-making capabilities.

The fuzziﬁed inputs for the IT2-FLS are represented as ranges for the inputs: ed, ˙ed, and df. Each input generates two crisp values during the fuzziﬁcation process. The paper also includes diagrams illustrating the architecture and membership functions of the IT2-FLS, highlighting the various fuzzy labels and the normalization of the membership functions based on the robot's hardware capabilities. 

Additionally, the IT2-FLS employs the same rule base as the T1-FLS for inferencing, but it processes two firing strengths for each rule due to the consideration of LMFs and UMFs, thereby enhancing the robustness of the system.
