The paper discusses the performance comparison of two fuzzy logic systems (FLSs) used for wall-following behavior in robots, particularly in scenarios involving dents in wall surfaces. Two cases are analyzed: a negative dent commonly found near doors and a positive dent created by structural elements like pillars. The results indicate that the robot equipped with the Interval Type-2 Fuzzy Logic System (IT2-FLS) exhibited smoother trajectories compared to the Type-1 Fuzzy Logic System (T1-FLS) in both cases, with average lateral errors of 0.02 m and 0.05 m for IT2-FLS, versus 0.03 m for T1-FLS in the respective cases.

In a subsequent validation phase, the performance of both FLSs was assessed against complex wall arrangements. The robot effectively followed these arrangements for 200 seconds, and it was found that the IT2-FLS consistently produced smoother paths, with a mean lateral error of 4.9 cm compared to 6.1 cm for the T1-FLS. Statistical analysis revealed that the IT2-FLS significantly outperformed the T1-FLS, with a paired t-test showing a significant difference (t(11)=7.19, p=0.000) and a large effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.86). The findings affirm that the IT2-FLS is superior for wall-following applications, particularly for scenarios involving wall irregularities, highlighting its potential use in robotic disinfecting applications.
