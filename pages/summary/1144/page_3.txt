This paper addresses the challenges in developing wall-following capabilities for mobile robots, particularly in the context of wall disinfection. Traditional machine learning methods, including various optimization techniques applied to Type 1 Fuzzy Logic Systems (T1-FLSs), require extensive training tailored to specific conditions, limiting their generalization and flexibility. While several studies have explored T1-FLSs based on expert knowledge to improve wall-following performance, they still face constraints related to maintaining a specific lateral distance from walls and necessitate redesigning membership functions for distance adjustments.

The authors propose a novel approach that utilizes both T1-FLS and Interval Type 2 Fuzzy Logic Systems (IT2-FLS), focusing on enhancing the robot's ability to maintain a target distance from walls while allowing for easy adjustments without requiring redesign. The flexibility of this method is highlighted as a significant improvement over existing systems, enabling better adaptability for wall disinfection tasks. The paper systematically evaluates the performance of both FLS types in maintaining the target distance during wall following, contributing valuable insights to the field. Subsequent sections detail the proposed system, the FLS designs, and the validation process, culminating in a comparison of T1-FLS and IT2-FLS effectiveness.
