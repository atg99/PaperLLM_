The paper discusses the formulation of interval Type 2 fuzzy sets within a fuzzy logic system (FLS), focusing on the derivation of firing strengths and the implications of fuzzy rules. The firing strengths, denoted as Ri, are calculated using minimum fuzzy operators, which serve as t-norm and t-conorm operators. Each rule generates a range for these firing strengths rather than a single crisp value.

The outputs from the fuzzy rules are represented through lower and upper membership functions (LMFs and UMFs), which are clipped according to the firing strength. This results in interval Type 2 fuzzy sets as outputs, specifically denoted as ˜mw0(w) and ˜mv0(v). The aggregation of these fuzzy sets uses the fuzzy max operator, leading to the creation of interval Type 2 fuzzy sets that require type reduction for defuzzification. 

To achieve this, the paper employs the Karnik-Mendel algorithm, which transforms interval Type 2 fuzzy sets into interval-valued Type 1 fuzzy sets. The resulting outputs, cw and cv, are defined by their lower and upper limits, estimated through specific formulas that consider the number of samples across the output's universe of discourse. The switch points for the outputs are iteratively determined, allowing for a comprehensive approach to fuzzy inference and defuzzification.
