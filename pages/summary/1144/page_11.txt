In the first phase of validation, the robot maintained a distance of 0.5 m from the wall and operated at a maximum speed of 1 m/s. For the right-angle corner scenario (case "a"), the robot displayed effective navigation by adjusting its velocity and angular position as it approached the corner, resulting in a minimal average absolute displacement error (E) of 0.04 m for T1-FLS and 0.02 m for IT2-FLS, indicating better performance for the latter.

In the second scenario (case "b"), where the robot followed the outer surface of a wall with a right-angle corner, both FLSs successfully adjusted the robot's trajectory, maintaining low errors (E of 0.03 m for T1-FLS and 0.02 m for IT2-FLS). This confirmed their capability to adapt to changes in wall direction.

The evaluation of wall-following behavior along curved walls (cases "c" and "d") showed that both FLSs effectively managed the curvature, with E values of 0.07 m and 0.05 m for T1-FLS and IT2-FLS, respectively, when following from the inside, and 0.06 m and 0.04 m, respectively, when following from the outside. The IT2-FLS consistently exhibited superior performance, characterized by lower peaks of lateral error compared to T1-FLS.

Overall, the findings validated the effectiveness of the proposed FLSs in guiding mobile robots through complex wall-following scenarios, essential for successful disinfection operations.
