The paper discusses the challenges associated with disinfecting public spaces to control the spread of infectious diseases, particularly emphasizing the role of robots in this process. It identifies that indirect transmission often occurs through contaminated surfaces, such as walls in high-traffic areas like hospitals and transit stations, necessitating frequent disinfection. While teleoperated robots have been developed to assist in medical care during outbreaks, their requirement for continuous human operation limits their effectiveness for autonomous disinfection tasks.

To address the need for autonomous wall disinfection, the paper highlights the importance of wall-following capabilities in robots. Traditional line-following methods are impractical for larger, public environments, and existing navigation techniques like Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) have limitations, particularly in less feature-rich areas like hospital corridors. The authors argue for the superiority of robots equipped with online path planning and decision-making abilities over those relying on offline methods.

The paper reviews various approaches to wall-following behavior, including fuzzy logic systems and PID controllers, noting their limitations in maintaining a consistent distance from walls or adapting to non-straight surfaces. Additionally, it references machine learning methods, specifically neural networks, which have been studied for wall-following behavior but lack real-world verification through experiments or simulations.

In conclusion, the authors advocate for the development of autonomous robots capable of effective wall disinfection, highlighting the need for innovative navigation and control methods that ensure safe and efficient operation in public spaces.
