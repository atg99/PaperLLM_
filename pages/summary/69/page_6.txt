This paper discusses the numerical evaluation of nonlinear first-order non-homogeneous delay differential equations (DDEs) represented by Equations 11, 12, and 14. A standard approach involves discretizing the equations in time to compute input and state signal values at each time sample for numerical integration. The backward Euler discretization is applied, leading to a series of equations that describe the evolution of the system over discrete time steps.

By setting the delay parameter equal to the sampling time step, the paper outlines a procedure for storing and updating values of the readout signal in memory. This results in a transformation of the continuous equations into a discrete form, ultimately leading to a nonlinear first-order non-homogeneous difference equation. The discretization process converts continuous time measurements into discrete indices, simplifying the equations.

The final form of the equation is expressed in terms of sequences, allowing the representation of the system's dynamics through a recursive relationship. The paper introduces a matrix formulation for the system's state, where the evolution of the state vector is influenced by previous states and input signals, encapsulated in the defined matrices and functions. The approach emphasizes the stability and applicability of the backward Euler method for solving such equations numerically, while also allowing for the inclusion of additional time-delayed terms as necessary for the specific problem context.
