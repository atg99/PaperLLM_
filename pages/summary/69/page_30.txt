The paper discusses the total partial derivatives of the objective function E in the context of the Vanilla LSTM cell, defining derivatives with respect to intermediate variables such as ρ, γ, α, and a crucial border variable ψ. The variable ψ is significant as it represents the total partial derivative of E concerning the state signal at a specific index n, influencing all parameter updates in the LSTM network, thereby serving as the essential error gradient sequence.

The backward pass equations are derived using the chain rule, incorporating these derivatives. Key equations are presented, illustrating how the derivatives interact to compute the gradients necessary for updating the model parameters. The total partial derivatives with respect to the model parameters at step n are linked to accumulation derivatives, emphasizing their role in training. To mitigate potential numerical issues caused by excessively large accumulation derivatives, the paper notes that these derivatives are clipped to a range between -1 and 1. This clipping is crucial for maintaining stability during the training process, ensuring the effective functioning of the control and data warping functions.
