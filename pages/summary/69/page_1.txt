The paper "Fundamentals of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Network" by Alex Sherstinsky, published in the Elsevier journal "Physica D: Nonlinear Phenomena," aims to clarify the foundational concepts of RNNs and LSTMs, addressing gaps in the existing literature. While LSTMs have been extensively covered in various resources, many lack comprehensive explanations of training formulas and the rationale behind techniques such as "unrolling" an RNN.

The tutorial derives the canonical RNN formulation from differential equations, provides a precise justification for the unrolling technique, and discusses the challenges of training standard RNNs. It transitions to the "Vanilla LSTM" network through logical arguments, presenting all relevant equations and detailed descriptions of the LSTM's components. The paper emphasizes an unconventional yet accessible notation and approach, aiming to enhance understanding.

The introduction highlights the significant impact of LSTMs in various applications, including language modeling and machine translation, and addresses the common challenges practitioners face when seeking to understand these networks. The author notes that existing resources often leave critical questions unanswered, prompting a need for a self-contained primer that clearly explains the Vanilla LSTM computational cell alongside well-structured schematics and formulas. This work is intended for practitioners and researchers looking to deepen their understanding and implement the augmented LSTM model effectively.
