The paper discusses the fundamentals of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, presenting a principled approach based on differential equations common in various scientific fields. It establishes that the canonical formulation of RNNs can be derived from sampling delay differential equations, leading to a standard RNN formulation through parameter selection and stability considerations. The authors detail the unrolling of RNNs and address the challenges of vanishing and exploding gradients in training via Back Propagation Through Time.

The paper also outlines the transition from standard RNNs to LSTMs, highlighting the structural enhancements that improve robustness. It includes a comprehensive overview of the Vanilla LSTM network, detailing its components and the forward and backward passes. The authors emphasize their pedagogical approach, which provides clarity on RNN and LSTM concepts from a Signal Processing perspective.

Notably, the paper introduces two extensions to the Vanilla LSTM: convolutional non-causal input context windows and an external input gate, which are integrated into the LSTM equations. For future work, the authors recommend implementing the Augmented LSTM in high-performance environments and benchmarking its effectiveness in practical applications like language representation for question answering and customer support automation. They stress the importance of evaluating the impact of the new features compared to existing models, including Vanilla LSTMs and Transformers.
