This paper discusses various aspects of recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on their architecture, independence assumptions, and the challenges associated with approximating infinite impulse response (IIR) systems with finite impulse response (FIR) models. 

Remark 1 highlights that the existing propositions and proofs do not encompass advanced RNN architectures like Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Attention Networks, which are increasingly relevant in the field.

Remark 2 emphasizes that while segment-level state signal subsequences are independent, input signal subsequences from different segments do not require independence. It is noted that dependencies in input signals can be managed through appropriately trained hidden layer weights, preserving the independence of state signal subsequences.

Remark 3 points out that no method for converting IIR to FIR is universally optimal. The effectiveness of an FIR approximation depends on how well it fits the specific data distribution and performance criteria of the application. Unrolling RNNs for a finite number of steps can lead to artificial discontinuities in the output sequence. However, if the underlying data consists of independent segments, unrolling can help align the model outputs with the inputs, akin to the Sampling Theorem in signal processing.

Remark 4 discusses the challenges of adhering to the independence assumptions of Proposition 1 in real-world applications, where subsequences may exhibit dependencies. It suggests that empirical experimentation and statistical analysis can help identify suitable initial conditions for subsequences to achieve a more favorable subdivision into independent segments.

Overall, the paper underscores the delicate balance between theoretical assumptions and practical modeling considerations in the application of RNNs to various tasks.
