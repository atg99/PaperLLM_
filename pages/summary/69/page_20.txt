The paper discusses the derivation of a modified recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture, focusing on the stability and gradient issues often encountered in standard RNNs. It begins with the observation that the weight matrix W in Equation 96 is a diagonal matrix with positive fractions, allowing for the simplification of the state update equation. By setting Ws to the identity matrix, the state equation can be expressed as a direct function of the previous state, ensuring that contributions from past states remain fractional and thus stable.

The paper highlights the importance of controlling the update term, which is influenced by both the state signal from the previous time step and the current input signal. It identifies a potential problem where the connection between states through the weight matrix can lead to vanishing or exploding gradients. To mitigate this, a control readout gate, gcr[n], is introduced to modulate the readout signal, effectively controlling the amount of the readout signal that contributes to the current state.

Furthermore, the paper introduces a control input gate, gcx[n], which allows for a more flexible integration of the external input signal into the update process. The update term is redefined to accommodate both the control readout and control input gates, leading to a more dynamic composition of the update signal.

The dynamic range of the cell's observable value signal is maintained through a saturating warping nonlinearity, Gd(z), which processes the aggregated input signals to produce the final update candidate signal. The final state update equation incorporates these modifications, demonstrating how the architecture can effectively manage the contributions of previous states and inputs while maintaining stability and mitigating gradient issues.
