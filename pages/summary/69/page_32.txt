The paper discusses the advantages of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks over standard Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) in terms of gradient descent training. It emphasizes that the gates in LSTMs contribute to better numerical stability for the gradients, which is crucial for effective optimization. The analysis focuses on the intermediate gradient sequences and their ability to maintain information flow over long sequences, which is essential for mitigating gradient decay.

Equations presented in the paper illustrate the relationships between various gradient sequences, highlighting that both border and intermediate sequences depend on each other. The detailed derivations show how changes in one gradient sequence affect others, underscoring the complex interdependencies within the LSTM architecture. The paper concludes that understanding these relationships is vital for enhancing the training process of LSTM systems, making them more robust against issues like vanishing gradients that typically affect standard RNNs.
