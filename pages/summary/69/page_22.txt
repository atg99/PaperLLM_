The Vanilla LSTM network processes the current cell state as a weighted combination of the previous cell state and the current step's historical and novel update information. Similar to standard RNNs, the Vanilla LSTM can learn short-range dependencies from subsequences in the training data. However, if the model parameters lead to a condition where the gradient signal norm ∥⃗ gcs[n]∥ is less than one, the error gradient may vanish over time, despite non-zero contributions from the update candidate signal and reset gate signals. The gate signals ⃗ gcs[n], ⃗ gcr[n], and ⃗ gcu[n] need to be differentiable for training with Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). The logistic function is utilized as a suitable activation function for the gate signals, mapping inputs from the range of negative to positive infinity into the range of zero to one, ensuring the necessary properties for effective training. This function can be applied element-wise to vector inputs.
