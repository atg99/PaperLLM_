In this section, the paper derives the Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) framework from the perspective of differential equations. It begins with a general nonlinear first-order non-homogeneous ordinary differential equation that describes the evolution of a state signal vector over time. The equation is expressed as the sum of a vector-valued function of time and a constant vector.

The function representing the state signal is specified in a canonical form, which includes an input signal vector. This formulation is relevant in various fields, such as physics and biology. The paper also discusses how analog quantities can be transformed into discrete representations through sampling, particularly in the context of processing video signals.

A specific case of the function is presented as an "Additive Model," which sums multiple vector-valued functions of time. This model is significant in brain dynamics research, as it accounts for the rate of change in neuronal activities. The Additive Model has been adapted in numerous ways, including the integration of delays and constraints that reflect biological processes, and has applications in areas like vision, decision-making, and memory.

Furthermore, the paper highlights that the Additive Model generalizes the Hopfield model, linking biological plausibility to applications in physics and engineering. A detailed formulation of a saturating Additive Model is provided, with specific definitions for its constituent terms, illustrating how the readout signal vector is a warped version of the state signal vector. The section concludes with a discussion on the choice of nonlinear activation functions used in the model.
