This section discusses the challenges associated with training recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on the truncated unrolled RNN system. Proposition 1 outlines that the equations provided define this truncated system, which theoretically maintains stability. Despite this stability, RNNs face significant practical issues during training, primarily the "vanishing gradients" and "exploding gradients" phenomena. These issues become more pronounced when the target subsequence dependencies span a large number of samples, necessitating a wider unrolled RNN model to effectively capture these long-range dependencies.

The training of truncated unrolled RNNs typically employs Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT), a variant of the back propagation algorithm adapted for sequence data. BPTT involves unrolling the RNN for a finite number of steps, transforming the directed cyclic graph of the RNN into a directed acyclic graph. This allows the application of back propagation techniques to RNNs, leveraging the existence of an equivalent feedforward network for finite steps.

As a supervised training algorithm, BPTT uses data pairs to optimize an objective function that depends on the readout signal at specific indices. The optimization process often involves gradient descent methods, with BPTT facilitating the calculation of gradients through repeated application of the chain rule. The section highlights the importance of measuring total gradients with respect to both the readout signal and the state signal, as the dependencies between these signals can affect the overall objective function.

The overall objective function is typically defined as the sum of contributions from the readout signal at each index. However, direct application of the chain rule to the readout signal alone can overlook essential components of the gradient, particularly due to the interdependencies of state signals across time steps. The relationships between state signals at consecutive time steps are critical for accurately capturing the dynamics of the RNN during training.
