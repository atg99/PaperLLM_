This section focuses on deriving the equations essential for training a Vanilla LSTM network cell using Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT). The process begins with calculating two gradient sequences, ⃗ χ[n] and ⃗ ψ[n], which represent the total partial derivatives of the objective function E with respect to the observable value signal ⃗ v[n] and the state signal ⃗ s[n], respectively. The approach of "anchoring" the chain rule at the cell's border is strategically chosen to adhere to modular design principles, simplifying the equations by reducing the number of intermediate variables.

The backward-moving gradients ⃗ χ[n] and ⃗ ψ[n] are recursively defined, showing that their values at a particular index depend on those at the subsequent index, thereby facilitating the computation of total partial derivatives with respect to model parameters. Intermediate gradient sequences, such as ⃗ αcs[n], ⃗ αcu[n], ⃗ αcr[n], and ⃗ αdu[n], are created to allocate contributions from signals at each step to the objective function's total partial derivatives. Summing these contributions across all steps generates the overall derivatives needed for parameter updates.

During inference, only the input signal ⃗ x[n] and value signal ⃗ v[n] are observable, while other intermediate signals remain internal. The value signal can be transformed into an output signal ⃗ y[n], which may involve linear transformations and softmax operations. Additionally, input signals can undergo preprocessing to enhance contextual attention, with a non-causal input filter discussed in a later section.

The section concludes with the derivation of derivatives for warping functions, setting the groundwork for the training equations and highlighting the benefits of using ⃗ χ[n] for a modular and simplified implementation of the Vanilla LSTM cell.
