The paper presents Proposition 1, which establishes that a single reusable RNN cell can effectively optimize the objective function L for a sequence of N samples and infer outputs from unseen inputs. The proposition is based on the assumption that there exists a parameter set Θ for which L is close to an optimal value, and that the ground truth output sequence can be divided into independent segment-level subsequences. 

The proof outlines that when L is optimized, the observable outputs of the RNN approximate the ground truth outputs within an acceptable tolerance. The outputs are segmented similarly to how the ground truth is segmented, leading to independent segment-level components of L. This independence allows for the assumption that the output subsequences are also independent across different segments.

Furthermore, the proof emphasizes the invertibility of certain functions and the independence of random variables associated with different segments. It concludes that the initial conditions for the state signal subsequences of one segment should not depend on samples from another segment, ensuring the mutual independence of these subsequences. This independence is crucial for the effective functioning of the RNN in processing and inferring outputs from segmented data.
