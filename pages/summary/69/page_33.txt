The paper discusses the behavior of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in relation to the vanishing gradient problem, particularly focusing on how the partial derivatives of the objective function can be preserved across many time steps. The key equation (Equation 180) outlines how the partial derivatives can be extrapolated from one time step to another, emphasizing that the preservation of error gradients is critical for effective training. The paper identifies conditions under which the gradients can diminish, specifically when the spectral radius of the matrix Q_k−1, k;˜Θ and the diagonal of the state control signal gcs[k] are kept below unity. 

Three cases are presented to explain when the gradients may become negligible: when the network is in a trivial state, when the signals perfectly cancel each other out, and when the spectral radius condition is satisfied. The authors argue that the most favorable scenario for LSTM networks occurs when Q_k−1, k;˜Θ < 1, which can be achieved through various signal and parameter constraints.

The analysis concludes that as the difference between time indices increases, the contributions of the Q terms become less significant, leading to a natural decay of error gradients, provided that the elements of gcs[n] remain fractional. If the model is trained to saturate gcs[n] at 1, the error gradients could be recirculated, complicating the training process. The paper highlights the importance of managing signal magnitudes to prevent issues related to vanishing gradients, thereby enhancing the LSTM's capability to learn long-range dependencies effectively.
