The paper discusses the mechanics of a recurrent neural network (RNN) system that incorporates control gates to modulate the contributions of previous state signals and current update signals. Specifically, the state signal at the current step is computed as a blend of the retained state from the previous step and the update information derived from the readout signal and the current input signal. This blending is regulated by two gate signals: the "control state" gate (gcs) and the "control update" gate (gcu). These gates allow for fine-tuned adjustments on how much of the previous state and current update information is integrated into the state signal, with their values constrained between 0 and 1. 

The paper also highlights the flexibility of RNNs in terms of processing direction and the potential benefits of non-causal processing, which can enhance performance in applications like audio, speech, and text analysis by utilizing future input data for context. The importance of these control mechanisms in managing the flow of information in RNNs, particularly in Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, is emphasized as a significant area of research.
