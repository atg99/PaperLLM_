The paper discusses the stability of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and the challenges posed by vanishing and exploding gradients during training. It begins by analyzing the Jacobian matrix's behavior in relation to the stability of the RNN system when long-range interactions are present. Specifically, if all eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix satisfy the stability condition (0 < µi < 1), the magnitude of the Jacobian decreases exponentially with increasing distance between samples. Conversely, if any eigenvalue violates this condition, the Jacobian can grow exponentially, leading to saturation or overflow in the readout signal.

To address these challenges, the paper introduces the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, designed to mitigate the vanishing gradients problem. The LSTM architecture incorporates nonlinear, data-dependent controls that help maintain gradient flow, thus allowing for effective training. The LSTM modifies the traditional RNN structure by separating the update mechanisms for the cell's state and readout signals, thereby enhancing robustness and versatility.

Overall, the paper emphasizes that while standard RNNs struggle with gradient-related issues, LSTMs provide a viable solution by ensuring stable and efficient training, ultimately improving the model's ability to infer outputs over long sequences.
