The paper addresses the need for a comprehensive and unified reference for understanding Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks within the Machine Learning community. It aims to serve as an introductory guide for students and practitioners, emphasizing a clear and consistent notation to enhance understanding of both the theoretical and practical aspects of these systems.

The focus begins with RNNs, as LSTMs are a specialized form of RNNs. The authors derive the canonical RNN equations from differential equations, arguing that this approach provides a clearer understanding of the underlying principles and facilitates the transition to LSTM architecture. They highlight the importance of differential equations in modeling neural networks and the advantages they offer for addressing real-life scenarios compared to difference equations.

The document outlines several key requirements for its content, including intuitive notation, completeness in covering inference and training equations, a general approach to the Vanilla LSTM system, illustrative diagrams, modularity for integration into broader architectures, and the use of vector notation for ease of implementation.

The organization of the paper includes a principled background on RNNs, a formal explanation of RNN unrolling, a discussion on training mechanisms through Back Propagation Through Time, and the construction of the Vanilla LSTM cell. Subsequent sections delve into the details of the Vanilla LSTM and its augmented versions, concluding with a summary and suggestions for future research.

Overall, the paper aims to provide a thorough understanding of RNNs and LSTMs, filling a gap in existing literature by addressing the complexities and nuances of these critical components in machine learning.
