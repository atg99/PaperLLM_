This study compares the effectiveness of Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN in detecting cracks in images. The following key conclusions were drawn:

1. Faster R-CNN produced more complete detection bounding boxes than Mask R-CNN, particularly in scenarios where joint training posed challenges for bounding box regression. Faster R-CNN exhibited a higher score threshold, and both models demonstrated improved crack detection with a limited dataset of over 130 images. However, detection of complex cracks proved sensitive to learning rates, with the least favorable results occurring at a learning rate of 0.0002.

2. Faster R-CNN outperformed YOLOv3 in detecting both single and complex cracks, achieving favorable results for bright, dark, straight, deep, and shallow cracks within the dataset used.

3. Both models require larger datasets for accurately detecting complex cracks, as the current dataset predominantly featured simple transverse cracks.

4. The accuracy and loss metrics for Faster R-CNN showed little variation across five different learning rates, similar to Mask R-CNN.

5. At learning rates above 0.005, Faster R-CNN exhibited higher accuracy than Mask R-CNN, while Mask R-CNN performed better at rates below this threshold. Additionally, Faster R-CNN maintained a lower loss value compared to Mask R-CNN.

Future research will focus on optimizing the use of limited labeled data to enhance detection outcomes, exploring options such as pre-trained models, active learning, and data augmentation. The authors also express intent to investigate advanced algorithms to further improve detection results.
