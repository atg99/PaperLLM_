The paper compares the performance of Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN in detecting cracks of varying types and sizes, using different learning rates. In Figures 9 and 10, detection results for deep and shallow cracks are presented alongside results for straight and bending cracks. Faster R-CNN shows consistent performance for horizontal straight cracks, with a higher score-thr, while its sensitivity to learning rate changes affects the detection of bending cracks. Specifically, a learning rate of 0.02 yields the best results for detecting all cracks, but lower rates result in partial miss detections.

Mask R-CNN demonstrates better efficacy in detecting simple transverse cracks compared to bending ones, with a learning rate of 0.01 producing the most complete detection. The results indicate that both models excel at detecting simpler crack forms, but struggle with more complex crack shapes, suggesting a need for additional training data for optimal performance.

Further evaluation using the CRACK500 dataset confirms that Faster R-CNN maintains strong generalization capabilities, while Mask R-CNN is slightly less effective but still functional. The authors highlight the efficiency of migration-learning based finetuning methods, suggesting that improved results could be achieved with minimal training on the CRACK500 dataset.
