The paper discusses the performance of two deep learning models, Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN, in detecting cracks of varying characteristics, including depth and shape. The authors present results from experiments using different learning rates to assess the models' sensitivity and detection capabilities.

For deep and shallow cracks, Faster R-CNN demonstrated superior detection performance compared to Mask R-CNN. The learning rates tested ranged from 0.02 to 0.0002, with Faster R-CNN effectively detecting both types of cracks across most rates, though it showed some limitations with very low learning rates. In contrast, Mask R-CNN was more sensitive to learning rate adjustments, leading to missed detections, particularly at lower rates.

The experiments also compared the detection of straight versus bending cracks. Faster R-CNN maintained consistent performance in detecting straight cracks, while it struggled with bending cracks as the learning rate decreased. Mask R-CNN exhibited better results for straight cracks but faced challenges with bending cracks, particularly at lower learning rates, resulting in poor detection rates.

Overall, the findings indicate that while both models can detect simple cracks effectively, more robust training data may be necessary to enhance their performance on complex crack shapes. The results underline the importance of selecting appropriate learning rates for optimizing detection accuracy in crack analysis.
