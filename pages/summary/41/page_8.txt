The paper presents a comparative analysis of Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN in detecting cracks using various learning rates. Figure 6 illustrates the accuracy (acc) and loss for both models across five different learning rates: 0.02, 0.01, 0.005, 0.0025, and 0.0002. The results indicate that Faster R-CNN performs consistently well on single crack detection, with optimal performance observed at a learning rate of 0.005. However, its sensitivity to learning rate adjustments is more pronounced for bifurcated cracks, where detection is less effective, particularly for vertical cracks.

Mask R-CNN also shows improved detection of single cracks with adjusted learning rates, though its performance deteriorates at very low rates, leading to overfitting. The detection of bifurcated cracks similarly declines with lower learning rates, with transverse cracks being detected better than vertical ones. Overall, both algorithms demonstrate effectiveness in single crack detection but struggle with complex bifurcated cracks, suggesting a need for larger datasets for better training. The findings highlight the importance of selecting appropriate learning rates in enhancing crack detection capabilities in structural assessments.
