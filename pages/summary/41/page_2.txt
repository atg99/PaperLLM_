This paper discusses advancements in crack detection and quantification using deep learning and convolutional neural networks. It highlights various methodologies proposed by researchers to improve the accuracy and efficiency of detecting cracks in concrete structures, including roads, bridges, and tunnels. 

Key contributions include the introduction of a crack evaluation framework using Mask R-CNN by Kim et al., which detects and quantifies cracks through morphological operations. Kalfarisi et al. presented a unified framework for quantitative assessment using reality mesh-modeling technology. Wu et al. utilized photogrammetry to create mesh models for visualizing and evaluating cracks, while Peng et al. focused on hybrid feature learning for bridge crack detection. 

Other notable methods include deep learning approaches for microcrack identification by Guo et al., and structured light and depth camera techniques for structural damage quantification by Bang et al. The paper also mentions various enhancements made by researchers such as Han et al.'s pixel-level semantic segmentation network and Li et al.'s multilayer feature fusion for tunnel defect detection.

Additionally, it details classification functions for different defects, with Trana et al. developing a method for detecting and classifying asphalt pavement cracks, and Liu et al. creating a model that integrates pixelwise and region-based deep learning for comprehensive pavement inspection. 

The paper emphasizes the need for further exploration of crack detection methods tailored to the unique characteristics of road images, despite the success of models like Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN in general vision tasks. The study aims to validate its proposed model through experimental results.
