The paper investigates the performance of Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN in detecting road cracks under varying conditions, specifically focusing on cracks with and without sunlight interference, as well as deep and shallow cracks. 

In the analysis of cracks with sunlight interference, Faster R-CNN demonstrated effective detection across different learning rates, particularly excelling at identifying well-lit cracks. However, it struggled with poorly lit cracks when the learning rate was set to 0.0002, leading to missed detections. The study also found that the direction of the cracks did not significantly affect detection results, as a transverse single crack was chosen for comparison.

Similarly, Mask R-CNN showed varying performance based on learning rates. At higher rates, it successfully detected well-lit cracks, but at lower rates, particularly 0.0002, the model faced overfitting, resulting in poor detection outcomes. For cracks without sunlight interference, Mask R-CNN performed adequately at rates of 0.02, 0.01, and 0.005, but struggled at lower rates.

When examining deep and shallow cracks, Faster R-CNN maintained satisfactory detection results across different learning rates, with minor misses at extreme rates. In contrast, Mask R-CNN was more sensitive to learning rate changes, with optimal detection at 0.02 for deep cracks and 0.005 for shallow cracks, while lower rates led to significant missed detections and overfitting.

Overall, the findings indicate that Faster R-CNN generally outperformed Mask R-CNN in detecting both deep and shallow cracks, demonstrating robustness to learning rate variations, whereas Mask R-CNN exhibited sensitivity, leading to inconsistent detection results.
