The paper discusses a method for masking active and inactive frequency bands in audio samples, particularly focusing on sound events. The activation matrix A is utilized to determine whether a frequency band is active based on a threshold value λ. The study identifies three key parameters—position, length, and quantity—represented by λ, l, and MN, which must be tailored to the specific sound class conditions. 

Using the UrbanSound8k dataset, the authors perform experiments to create two distinct datasets: one masking active frequency bands and the other masking inactive bands. Results indicate that the model trained on the masked active frequency band dataset demonstrated a significant drop in classification accuracy to 78.3%, compared to the original dataset. Conversely, the model trained on the inactive frequency band dataset experienced only a 3.4% accuracy decrease, maintaining most of its performance. This suggests that active frequency bands, as defined in the study, play a crucial role in sound classification, highlighting their importance despite potential deviations in their artificial definition.
