The paper presents a novel temporal-frequency attention-based convolutional neural network model (TFCNN) aimed at enhancing environmental sound classification. The study demonstrates that applying frequency attention significantly improves the model's accuracy in distinguishing various sound events, such as wind and helicopter noises, particularly in urban environments. This improvement is evidenced by clearer feature distributions, as shown in Figure 10, where the model effectively emphasizes relevant time frames and frequency bands while suppressing background noise.

The TFCNN is compared against state-of-the-art models on the UrbanSound8K and ESC-50 datasets. The results indicate that TFCNN outperforms several single-feature models, which rely on two-dimensional feature maps or original waveforms for classification. When compared to multi-feature models, TFCNN shows competitive performance with models like M-LM-C CNN, while also maintaining lower complexity due to its single feature representation and simplified architecture.

Overall, the paper concludes that TFCNN effectively captures critical time-frequency features, achieving a balance between accuracy and model complexity, which positions it as a competitive approach in the field of environmental sound classification.
