The paper presents a novel architecture termed TFCNN, designed for audio classification by leveraging two attention spectrograms, SF and ST. The proposed method includes a channel combination strategy, which concatenates the two spectrograms into a two-channel output, and utilizes a network architecture comprising six convolutional layers, three pooling layers, and two fully connected layers. Each convolutional layer employs batch normalization and ReLU activation, while dropout is applied to mitigate overfitting.

The feature processing involves segmenting the log-Mel spectrogram into 64-frame sub-segments with a 50% overlap, ensuring that the label for each segment aligns with the original audio. During training, these segments are fed into the network, with the final classification determined through a probabilistic voting strategy based on predictions from all segments.

The experimental evaluation utilizes the ESC-50 and UrbanSound8K datasets. The ESC-50 dataset includes 50 categories of audio, totaling 2.8 hours of sound, while UrbanSound8K comprises 10 categories with 8732 short audio clips, totaling approximately 7.3 hours. The experiments employed tenfold and fivefold cross-validation strategies and utilized categorical cross-entropy as the loss function. The implementation was conducted using TensorFlow on a hardware setup featuring an NVIDIA GTX 1080 GPU and an Intel Core i7 CPU.
