The paper discusses the application of the Harmonic-Percussive Source Separation (HPSS) algorithm to process Log-Mel spectrograms for environmental sound analysis. The HPSS algorithm, originally proposed by Driedge, separates audio signals into harmonic and percussive components, allowing for a clearer representation of frequency distribution and activity within the sound data. The harmonic spectrogram effectively illustrates frequency bands, while the percussive spectrogram reveals a distinct vertical structure that differentiates between relevant sound events, such as gunshots, and background noise.

The study introduces a temporal-frequency attention mechanism to address the complex time-frequency structure of environmental sounds, which often include both meaningful sound frames and irrelevant noise. The temporal attention mechanism is designed to focus on relevant time frames, suppressing silent or non-informative segments. Concurrently, the frequency attention mechanism enhances the significance of active frequency bands that carry distinguishing information while reducing the weight of less informative bands.

The methodology involves standardizing the Log-Mel spectrogram and applying the HPSS algorithm to derive harmonic and percussive spectrograms. Convolution operations with specific kernel sizes extract nonlinear features from these spectrograms, resulting in one-dimensional matrices that represent frequency and temporal weights. The final step normalizes these matrices using the Softmax function to produce frequency and temporal weight matrices, which are essential for the subsequent classification tasks within the proposed TFCNN model.
