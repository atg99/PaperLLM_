The paper discusses the analysis of frequency bands across different sound categories and their impact on classification. It highlights that each sound category exhibits distinct frequency activation patterns. For instance, the "car horn" shows a scattered activation across frequency bands, while the "siren" predominantly activates in the middle and low frequency regions. In contrast, the "drilling" category demonstrates higher activation across nearly all frequency bands. To further investigate the influence of these varying activation values on classification, the authors propose a masking method to selectively ignore specific frequency bands, as illustrated in their results. The frequency activation matrix for different sound categories is presented to support their analysis.
