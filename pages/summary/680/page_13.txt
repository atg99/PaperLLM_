The paper presents a comparison of various models for environmental sound classification, specifically using the Urbansound8K and ESC-50 datasets. It highlights the effectiveness of different feature representations and architectures in achieving sound classification accuracy. The models evaluated include convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with varying input types such as raw audio data, log-Mel spectrograms, and Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs). 

Key findings indicate that the Two-Stream CNN achieved the highest accuracy on the UrbanSound8K dataset at 95.8%, while the TSCNN-DS model reached 97.2% accuracy. The proposed TFCNN model, which utilizes log-Mel features, achieved an accuracy of 84.4% on ESC-50 and 93.1% on UrbanSound8K, demonstrating competitive performance with fewer parameters. This work emphasizes the importance of model architecture and feature selection in improving environmental sound recognition tasks. 

The references cited provide a comprehensive background on existing methodologies and advancements in sound classification, showcasing the evolution of techniques from classical machine learning approaches to deep learning frameworks. The paper contributes to the ongoing research in the field by presenting empirical results and comparisons that guide future developments in environmental sound classification.
