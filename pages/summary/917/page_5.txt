The paper discusses the differences between two proxy-based loss functions, Proxy-NCA and Proxy-Anchor, in the context of deep metric learning. It highlights how each method handles proxies and embedding vectors during training, focusing on the gradient dynamics associated with positive and negative examples.

In Proxy-NCA, the gradients for positive examples are uniform, regardless of their difficulty, while negative examples are influenced minimally by a select few proxies. Conversely, Proxy-Anchor adjusts the gradient scales based on the relative hardness of positive examples, exerting stronger influences on harder positives. Additionally, Proxy-Anchor considers the interactions among negative examples more comprehensively, leading to a more nuanced gradient effect.

The paper also compares the training complexities of various loss functions. The proposed Proxy-Anchor loss has a complexity of O(MC), similar to Proxy-NCA. In contrast, SoftTriple loss, which employs multiple proxies, scales to O(MCU2). Pair-based losses, such as Contrastive and Triplet losses, exhibit higher complexities of O(M2) and O(M3), respectively, making them less efficient as the dataset size increases.

The authors conclude that Proxy-Anchor maintains competitive complexity while enhancing performance through its more sophisticated handling of gradients. Experimental results on benchmark datasets are presented to validate the efficacy of the proposed method and to analyze the impact of hyperparameters and embedding dimensions.
