The paper discusses the limitations of pair-based losses in metric learning, which utilize data pairs during training but suffer from high complexity and slow convergence due to the exponential growth of tuples with increased training data. Many pair-based methods require careful hyperparameter tuning and may lead to overfitting, as they often rely on local pairwise relations. 

To mitigate these issues, the paper introduces Proxy-based losses, which utilize proxies—representatives of subsets of training data—to reduce training complexity significantly. Proxy-NCA is highlighted as the first proxy-based loss, simplifying Neighborhood Component Analysis by associating data points with a smaller number of proxies. Other methods like SoftTriple and Manifold Proxy loss build on this concept but still face limitations, as they do not leverage the rich data-to-data relations available in pair-based methods.

The authors propose a new metric learning loss called Proxy-Anchor loss, which combines the advantages of both proxy-based and pair-based methods. This loss allows for faster convergence while still enabling interactions between data points through gradients, thereby enhancing the quality of the learned embedding space.

The paper also reviews Proxy-NCA loss in detail, explaining its mechanism and showing how it encourages proximity between data points and their corresponding proxies. The gradient calculations for Proxy-NCA demonstrate how the loss function works to pull positive pairs together while pushing negative pairs apart, ultimately achieving faster convergence with lower complexity.
