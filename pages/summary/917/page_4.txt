The paper discusses the limitations of Proxy-NCA loss, particularly in handling fine-grained data-to-data relationships due to its reliance on proxies for embedding vector associations. While Proxy-NCA maintains low training complexity, it fails to exploit the intricacies of data interactions, limiting the performance of embedding networks.

To address these limitations, the authors introduce Proxy-Anchor loss, which incorporates proxies as anchors for entire data batches. This method allows data samples to interact through these anchors, enhancing the learning process. The loss function is designed to pull together the hardest positive examples while pushing apart the hardest negative examples, utilizing a Log-Sum-Exp formulation that ensures varying degrees of influence based on the relative hardness of examples. Key parameters, such as scaling factor α and margin δ, control the strength of these interactions, ultimately improving the quality of the learned embedding space.

The authors highlight that Proxy-Anchor loss offers a significant advantage over Proxy-NCA by actively considering relative hardness in its gradients, thereby providing richer supervisory signals during training. This results in a more flexible and generalizable embedding network capable of exploring a larger area of the embedding space. The paper concludes with a complexity analysis of the training process, underscoring the efficiency and effectiveness of Proxy-Anchor loss in producing discriminative embeddings.
