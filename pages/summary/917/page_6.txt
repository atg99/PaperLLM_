The paper presents a detailed evaluation of various image retrieval methods on the CUB-200-2011 and Cars-196 datasets, focusing on the performance of the Proxy-Anchor loss. The results are summarized in Table 2, which shows the Recall@K percentages for multiple models with different embedding dimensions and configurations. The models assessed include Clustering, Proxy-NCA, Smart Mining, and several others, with the Proxy-Anchor method demonstrating superior performance across all settings, particularly in the challenging CUB-200-2011 dataset.

The datasets used for evaluation include CUB-200-2011, Cars-196, Stanford Online Product (SOP), and In-shop Clothes Retrieval. The training and testing splits are specified, detailing the number of images utilized from each dataset. The implementation details highlight the use of the Inception network with batch normalization as the embedding network, alongside the AdamW optimizer for training.

In terms of methodology, the paper describes the proxy setting where a single proxy is assigned per semantic class, initialized to ensure uniform distribution. The image processing techniques involve random cropping and horizontal flipping during training, with standardized sizes for input images.

The comparative analysis indicates that the Proxy-Anchor loss method outperforms previous approaches, achieving significant improvements in recall metrics, particularly with a 2.7% increase in Recall@1 on CUB-200-2011, thereby underscoring the efficacy of the proposed model. The results suggest that the Proxy-Anchor method represents a notable advancement in image retrieval performance.
