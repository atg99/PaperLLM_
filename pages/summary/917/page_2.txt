This paper presents a novel metric learning loss called Proxy-Anchor loss, which combines the strengths of both proxy-based and pair-based loss methods while addressing their limitations. Traditional pair-based losses, such as Triplet loss and Contrastive loss, associate data points based on their proximity in the embedding space but often do not utilize the entire batch effectively. On the other hand, proxy-based losses like Proxy-NCA focus on data-to-proxy relations, which can overlook rich data-to-data interactions.

The proposed Proxy-Anchor loss utilizes each proxy as an anchor, associating it with all data in a batch. This approach allows the loss to pull data points of the same class closer to the proxy while pushing others away, thus accounting for the relative hardness of each data point based on its proximity to the proxy. This design not only speeds up convergence but also enhances robustness against noisy labels and outliers, eliminating the need for hyper-parameter tuning in tuple sampling.

The contributions of this work are threefold: it introduces a loss that captures rich data-to-data relations, achieves state-of-the-art performance on four public benchmarks for metric learning, and demonstrates faster convergence than existing methods such as Proxy-NCA and Multi-Similarity loss.

The paper also categorizes existing metric learning losses into pair-based and proxy-based methods, highlighting their respective approaches and limitations. Pair-based losses focus on relationships among data pairs, while proxy-based losses simplify training by using fewer proxies but often miss out on detailed data interactions. The Proxy-Anchor loss aims to bridge this gap, enhancing both performance and efficiency in metric learning tasks.
