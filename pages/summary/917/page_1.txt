The paper introduces a new metric learning loss called Proxy Anchor Loss, which aims to combine the strengths of pair-based and proxy-based methods while addressing their limitations. Pair-based losses provide fine-grained semantic relations between data points but suffer from high training complexity and slow convergence. In contrast, proxy-based losses enable faster convergence but do not fully leverage the rich relationships between data points.

Proxy Anchor Loss utilizes proxies to enhance convergence speed and resilience against noisy labels and outliers. Additionally, it allows embedding vectors to interact through gradients, facilitating the exploration of data-to-data relations. The authors evaluate their method on four public benchmarks, demonstrating that networks trained with Proxy Anchor Loss achieve state-of-the-art performance and converge more quickly compared to existing methods.

The introduction highlights the importance of learning a semantic distance metric for various applications, including image retrieval and face verification. The paper critiques existing pair-based losses for their computational inefficiency and the necessity of tuple sampling techniques, which can lead to overfitting. In contrast, proxy-based losses alleviate these issues by learning proxies as representatives of data subsets, thus reducing training complexity.

Overall, Proxy Anchor Loss represents a significant advancement in deep metric learning, effectively balancing the trade-offs between speed and the richness of data relations.
