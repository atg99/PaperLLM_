In this section, the authors discuss improvements to residual networks (ResNets), which are built from stacking residual building blocks (ResBlocks). Each ResBlock allows for the propagation of input data through a learnable residual mapping function, denoted as F. The structure of a ResBlock includes ReLU activation functions, which can hinder information flow by zeroing out negative signals, particularly at the start of training. 

The original ResNet architecture, while effective, may impose too many activation gates on the main information path, affecting signal propagation. In contrast, the pre-activation ResNet redesign moves batch normalization (BN) and ReLU layers to the beginning of the block, allowing for more direct signal flow but resulting in a lack of overall signal normalization, which complicates learning as more blocks are added.

The authors highlight the trade-offs between the original ResNet and the pre-activation version. While the original ResNet may restrict signal flow due to excessive gating, the pre-activation design can lead to unregulated signal propagation, which introduces issues like unnormalized signals and challenges in learning identity mappings. Both designs present unique challenges that need to be addressed for optimal performance in deep learning tasks.
