The paper presents Improved Residual Networks (iResNet), which enhance the performance of traditional ResNet architectures across various depths. The results indicate that iResNet consistently reduces top-1 error compared to baseline ResNet models, achieving a notable 1.93% improvement on the 200-layer network. The authors demonstrate that while increasing depth typically leads to optimization issues for ResNet, iResNet continues to improve performance, showcasing better training convergence and requiring fewer epochs to surpass ResNet's results.

Figures illustrate the training and validation curves across different depths (50, 101, 152, and 200 layers), highlighting iResNet's superior optimization capabilities. Even at extreme depths of 302 and 404 layers, iResNet maintains performance without convergence issues, unlike the baseline ResNet, which fails to achieve good results at these depths. The findings suggest that the only limitations for iResNet are related to computational resources rather than architectural constraints. Overall, iResNet offers significant advancements in deep learning model efficiency without increasing complexity.
