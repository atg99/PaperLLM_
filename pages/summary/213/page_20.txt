The paper presents a comparison of a novel network architecture called ResMax, which incorporates a projection shortcut, demonstrating significant performance improvements over previous methods in a 50-layer deep network context. The authors acknowledge skepticism regarding the practicality of extremely deep networks, such as those exceeding 400 or 3000 layers. However, they argue that depth is essential for learning powerful representations, and what may seem impractical today could become commonplace in the future. 

The authors draw a parallel to the human brain, which efficiently processes information with a vast number of neurons and connections while using minimal energy. They suggest that future neural networks could mimic this capability by dynamically adjusting their architecture based on the complexity of tasks and input data. This adaptability could lower computational demands and energy consumption while allowing for limitless learning potential, ultimately moving closer to achieving human-level intelligence.
