In the appendix of the paper "Improved Residual Networks," additional explanations and detailed experimental setups are provided. The authors emphasize that their models are trained from scratch on six datasets, except for object detection where pre-training on ImageNet is utilized. They adopt a specific weights initialization method for all convolutional layers.

The appendix includes a table detailing the number of bottleneck blocks for various depths of ResNet-based architectures on ImageNet, ranging from 50 to 404 layers. This table highlights the varying complexity of each stage across different network depths.

A key finding presented is the comparison of convergence speeds between original ResNets and the proposed iResNets. The authors note that original ResNets exhibit slow convergence at the beginning of training, particularly as the depth of the network increases. In contrast, iResNets demonstrate a more rapid convergence, maintaining stability and lower fluctuations in validation accuracy throughout training. This greater stability is illustrated in the training curves, which show smoother descents in validation accuracy for iResNets compared to the baseline.

Furthermore, the iResNets consistently achieve significant improvements in validation results and display enhanced training set convergence compared to original ResNets, suggesting more efficient optimization. The authors propose that incorporating additional regularization techniques, like dropout, could further enhance the performance of iResNets and indicate plans for future exploration in this area.
