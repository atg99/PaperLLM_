The paper discusses improvements to Residual Networks (ResNets) with a focus on bottleneck building blocks and projection shortcuts across various depths on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet datasets.

In Table 14, a comparison of projection shortcuts on ImageNet shows performance metrics for ResNet versions and the proposed ResMax model. The ResMax model achieves a top-1 accuracy of 22.85%, slightly lower than the baseline ResNet models, but maintains the same parameter count and GFLOPs.

The paper highlights three key differences in the proposed projection shortcut compared to prior work. First, it employs max pooling instead of average pooling, enhancing translation invariance and local information pooling. Second, it utilizes a 3x3 kernel for pooling, aligning with the size of the spatial convolution, which facilitates more effective element-wise addition between the projection shortcut and main block. Third, the proposed shortcut is integrated across all stages, unlike previous models that limited its use to the last stages. The authors argue that these modifications contribute to improved recognition performance, supported by additional motivations outlined in the main paper.
