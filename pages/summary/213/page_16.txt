The paper presents a comparison of various ResNet architectures, including iResNet, across different depths (50, 101, 152, and 200 layers) using the ImageNet dataset. It includes training and validation curves that illustrate the top-1 error rates for both ResNet and iResNet models. The results indicate that using a 320x320 crop size for validation can significantly enhance performance, demonstrating the advantage of the fully convolutional design of ResNets.

In addition to image classification, the paper explores iResNet's application in video recognition on datasets such as Kinetics-400 and Something-Something-v2. It highlights the training methodology, which involves using 16-frame clips with specific frame-skipping strategies to accommodate the temporal dynamics of video data. The architecture is adapted for video input, maintaining a spatial size of 224x224, with random cropping techniques employed to achieve varied input sizes.

Overall, the findings suggest that iResNet outperforms traditional ResNet models in both image and video recognition tasks, as reflected in the lower error rates and effective learning curves presented in the study.
