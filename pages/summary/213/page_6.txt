The paper introduces a novel architecture called ResStage, which aims to enhance information propagation in deep neural networks. Unlike the original approach, ResStage eliminates the first batch normalization (BN) in the first Middle ResBlock since the signal is already normalized by the Start ResBlock. The architecture is characterized by a fixed number of ReLU activations in the main propagation path—specifically four—regardless of network depth. This design choice prevents the degradation of the signal as it passes through multiple layers.

The network is structured into stages, with each stage comprising three components: an introduction, body, and conclusion. The End ResBlock of each stage features a BN and ReLU, which stabilize the signal for the subsequent stage. Additionally, the Start ResBlock includes a BN layer to normalize the signal before combining it with the projection shortcut, which also provides normalization. This systematic approach facilitates better learning and optimization, enabling training of extremely deep networks while allowing dynamic selection of ResBlocks during learning.

The paper also discusses an improved projection shortcut mechanism compared to the original ResNet architecture. The traditional method uses a 1x1 convolution with a stride of two to match dimensions, resulting in a significant loss of information as it skips 75% of feature map activations. The authors argue that this approach lacks a meaningful criterion for selecting which activations to retain. The proposed modifications lead to improved performance in the experimental results while maintaining comparable model complexity to previous architectures.
