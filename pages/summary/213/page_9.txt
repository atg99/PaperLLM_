The paper presents improvements to residual networks through the introduction of two architectures: ResGroup and ResGroupFix. The key innovation is the ResGroup block, which shifts the focus from the conventional 1x1 convolution, typically associated with bottleneck architectures, to a 3x3 convolution that utilizes a larger number of channels. This change enhances the network's ability to learn spatial patterns, an essential feature for image classification tasks.

Table 1 outlines the configurations of the proposed architectures compared to the standard ResNet-50, highlighting differences in layers, parameters, and computational requirements. Notably, the ResGroup block is designed to allocate more channels to the 3x3 convolution, thereby increasing its capacity for detecting spatial features. This design choice is argued to significantly improve performance metrics, as shown in subsequent experiments.

The experimental section of the paper evaluates the proposed architectures across six datasets, with ImageNet being the primary focus. The results include top-1 and top-5 error rates, demonstrating the effectiveness of the ResGroup approach compared to both the baseline ResNet and ResNeXt models. Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed modifications lead to better accuracy in image classification tasks, validating the architectural changes introduced in the study.
