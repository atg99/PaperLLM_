The paper presents an improved architecture for Residual Networks, termed ResStage, which addresses limitations in the original and pre-activation ResNet designs. The authors argue that the original architecture suffers from a lack of non-linearity between successive 1x1 convolutions in its projection shortcut, which restricts learning capability. The proposed ResStage architecture introduces a more effective arrangement of residual blocks, ensuring stability through batch normalization before each main stage and incorporating at least one non-linearity at the end of each stage.

The ResNet-50 serves as a case study, demonstrating the structure of the proposed architecture, which divides the network into distinct stages characterized by changes in output spatial size or channel numbers. Each stage consists of a Start ResBlock, several Middle ResBlocks, and an End ResBlock, with varying designs tailored to their positions within the stage. Importantly, while the configuration is modified for better performance, the overall model complexity remains unchanged, maintaining the same number of components and parameters as the original and pre-activation architectures.

In conclusion, the proposed ResStage architecture enhances the learning potential of Residual Networks without increasing their complexity, providing a more effective framework for deep learning tasks.
