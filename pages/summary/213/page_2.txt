The paper addresses the degradation problem observed in deep networks, where increasing layers can lead to worse performance. The authors argue that while ResNets introduced skip connections to help mitigate this issue, the problem persists, particularly with deeper networks. They propose a novel architecture design that separates the network into stages and utilizes different building blocks based on their position within each stage. This structure aims to enhance information propagation throughout the network.

Furthermore, the authors highlight the importance of projection shortcuts in maintaining signal integrity and minimizing information loss. They introduce a parameter-free improved projection shortcut that significantly enhances performance. Additionally, they revise the bottleneck building block of ResNet to increase the number of spatial channels, allowing for more effective learning of spatial features while controlling the overall parameters and computational cost.

In summary, the key contributions of the paper include a new network architecture that enhances information flow, an improved shortcut projection to reduce information loss, and a building block design that increases spatial learning capacity, all leading to better performance in deep residual networks.
