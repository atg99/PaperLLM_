The paper introduces an improved projection shortcut for Residual Networks (ResNets), addressing the challenges of noise and information loss in the original projection shortcut. The proposed method separates spatial and channel projections, utilizing a 3x3 max pooling with stride 2 for spatial projection and a 1x1 convolution followed by batch normalization for channel projection. This approach enhances the selection of feature map activations by focusing on the highest activations, thereby minimizing information loss and improving signal integrity through the network.

The authors highlight three main motivations for this improvement. First, the new projection shortcut reduces information loss, aiding in the flow of information across ResBlocks. Second, incorporating max pooling at the Start ResBlock of each stage enhances the network's translation invariance, leading to better overall recognition performance. Third, the method combines "soft downsampling" from the 3x3 convolution with "hard downsampling" from max pooling, providing complementary benefits for classification and spatial context retention.

Importantly, the proposed shortcut does not increase the number of parameters in the model and only marginally raises the computational cost by adding three max pooling layers. The authors refer to the enhanced architecture as the improved residual network (iResNet) and emphasize its effectiveness based on experimental results.
