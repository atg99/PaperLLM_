The paper investigates the effectiveness of different convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures in recognizing handwritten digits from the MNIST dataset. Specifically, the study focuses on variants of CNNs with three layers (CNN_3L) and four layers (CNN_4L), analyzing six configurations for the three-layer architecture and five for the four-layer architecture. Key parameters such as the number of feature maps, stride sizes, padding, dilation, and receptive fields are varied across these cases.

The recognition process involves several steps: collecting MNIST images, splitting them into training and test datasets, preprocessing the data, normalizing it, batching for training, training the CNN models, and finally, evaluating recognition accuracy and processing time.

Experiments were conducted using MATLAB 2018b on a personal computer equipped with an Intel i7 CPU, 16 GB memory, and an NVIDIA GTX 1060 GPU. The MNIST dataset contains 60,000 training images and 10,000 testing images, all normalized to a size of 28x28 pixels.

Data preprocessing techniques such as scaling, noise reduction, and skew estimation were employed to enhance recognition performance. The CNN architectures utilized convolution layers with various kernel sizes to extract features from the images.

Results indicated that different configurations of the CNN architectures significantly impacted recognition accuracy and processing time. The highest accuracy achieved was 99.76% for both CNN_3L and CNN_4L architectures, with specific configurations yielding optimal results. Overall, the study highlights the importance of training parameters, gradient descent optimizers, and CNN architecture in improving handwritten digit recognition.
