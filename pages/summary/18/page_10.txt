The paper discusses a convolutional neural network (CNN) model developed for handwritten digit recognition, focusing on optimizing recognition accuracy and processing time through various learning parameters. The Adam optimizer is highlighted for its fast convergence and learning speed, with specific equations provided for calculating the first-moment value and variance of gradients, leading to parameter updates.

The experimental setup involves creating and analyzing different CNN architectures, specifically three-layer (CNN_3L) and four-layer (CNN_4L) variants. A total of six cases for the three-layer architecture and five cases for the four-layer architecture are examined, differing in feature maps, stride sizes, padding, dilation, and receptive fields.

The recognition process follows a systematic approach: collecting MNIST handwritten digit images, dividing them into training and test sets, applying pre-processing techniques, normalizing the data, batching the training dataset, training the CNN model, and finally analyzing recognition accuracy and processing time across the variants. The experiments were conducted using MATLAB 2018b on a personal computer equipped with an Intel i7 CPU and NVIDIA GTX GPU, utilizing the standard MNIST database comprising 60,000 training images and 10,000 test images.

Data pre-processing is emphasized as crucial for effective recognition, involving techniques such as scaling, noise reduction, and skew estimation. The images were normalized to a fixed size of 28x28 pixels, with convolution layers employing 28 kernels of sizes 5x5 and 3x3 to extract features from the images. Overall, the study investigates the impact of training parameters, optimizers, and CNN architecture on handwritten digit recognition performance.
