The paper discusses key components of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), specifically focusing on the activation layer, classification layer, and gradient descent optimization algorithm. 

In the activation layer, various functions such as sigmoid, ReLu, and Softmax are explored, with the ReLu function being preferred due to its non-linearity, speed, and alignment with human neural behavior. It outputs zero for negative inputs and retains positive inputs, enhancing the model's performance.

The classification layer is the final layer in CNN architecture, functioning as a fully connected feed-forward network. It combines features from previous layers to predict classes for input images, employing the Softmax activation function to classify these features based on the training dataset.

The gradient descent optimization algorithm is highlighted as a crucial tool for enhancing neural network performance. It updates the network's weights and biases to minimize a cost function, thus improving learning efficiency. The paper categorizes gradient descent into three types: batch, stochastic (SGD), and mini-batch gradient descent, with SGD noted for its speed and efficiency in deep learning applications. The update mechanism for learning parameters is outlined, emphasizing the role of the learning rate and momentum factor in adjusting weights based on the gradient of the loss function.
