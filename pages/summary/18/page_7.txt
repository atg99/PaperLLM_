The paper discusses key parameters of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), focusing on dilation and pooling layers. Dilation is crucial as it affects the receptive field of a CNN, allowing for an increased field-of-view without altering the feature map. However, excessively large dilation can lead to increased computational demands, which may slow down processing times. The relationship between dilation, weights, and inputs is expressed in specific equations.

Pooling layers, placed between convolutional layers, serve to reduce input dimensionality and computational complexity. They help in feature selection and mitigate overfitting by retaining only essential values from non-overlapping sub-regions of the input images. The paper highlights max-pooling as a preferred method due to its efficiency in retaining maximum information, which enhances convergence speed and generalization. An example illustrates how max-pooling transforms a 4x4 output into a 2x2 output by selecting the maximum values from each sub-region.
