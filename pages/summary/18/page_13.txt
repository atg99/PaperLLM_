The paper investigates the recognition accuracy of MNIST handwritten digits using various optimizers, including stochastic gradient descent with momentum, Adam, Adagrad, and Adadelta. The results indicate that the CNN architecture with three layers (CNN_3L) achieves the highest accuracy of 99.89% when paired with the Adam optimizer, which is noted for its ability to compute adaptive learning rates and facilitate fast convergence. 

The findings suggest that while increasing the number of layers to four (CNN_4L) could enhance the model's capacity, it may lead to overfitting, resulting in reduced accuracy. To mitigate overfitting, techniques such as dropout, which randomly excludes certain neurons from the training process, are recommended. The study emphasizes the importance of optimizing CNN parameters to maximize recognition accuracy.

Additionally, the proposed CNN model is compared with other approaches in the field, demonstrating superior performance without the need for ensemble architectures, which tend to increase computational costs and complexity. The proposed model's accuracy of 99.89% positions it favorably against other contemporary methods, confirming its effectiveness in handwritten numeral recognition on the MNIST dataset.
