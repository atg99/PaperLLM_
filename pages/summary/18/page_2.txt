This paper discusses the advantages of deep neural networks (DNNs) over shallow neural networks (SNNs), particularly focusing on convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It highlights that CNNs, which consist of multiple hidden layers, are more effective in automatically detecting important features from images without requiring separate feature extraction processes, unlike SNNs that depend on handcrafted features. The paper emphasizes the efficiency of CNNs in various applications such as image classification, object recognition, and natural language processing.

CNNs are inspired by human visual perception and can learn to recognize objects through exposure to numerous examples, similar to how children learn. The paper lists notable CNN architectures like GoogLeNet, AlexNet, VGG, and ResNet, illustrating their capacity for hierarchical feature learning. CNNs require less pre-processing, can achieve high recognition accuracy even with limited training data, and are robust against variations in input images such as rotation and translation.

In contrast, SNNs struggle with complex pattern recognition and require a smaller volume of data, often leading to dependency on expert feature extraction. The paper also notes that CNNs have been successfully applied to handwritten digit recognition tasks, achieving accuracy rates of 98% to 99% on the MNIST dataset. An ensemble model combining multiple CNNs was utilized for further recognition experiments. Overall, the paper underscores the evolution and effectiveness of CNNs in modern recognition systems.
