The paper presents a detailed analysis of the configuration and performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with varying layer architectures aimed at improving recognition accuracy for the MNIST handwritten digit dataset. The study examines multiple cases of CNN architectures, specifically focusing on the number of layers, hyper-parameters such as kernel size, stride, dilation, and padding.

Key findings include:

1. **Receptive Field Impact**: The research highlights that a receptive field close to the input size correlates with higher recognition accuracy. Conversely, a significant gap between the receptive field and input size leads to poor performance. This is particularly evident in the comparisons of cases within the tables presented.

2. **Filter Count**: Increasing the number of filters enhances the CNN's ability to extract features, thus improving performance. Case 5 demonstrates this capability effectively.

3. **Layer Configuration**: The study also evaluates the effect of layer count on accuracy. While a three-layer CNN architecture achieved the highest recognition accuracy of 99.89% with the Adam optimizer, the four-layer variant exhibited lower performance, likely due to overfitting. This suggests that more layers do not always equate to better accuracy.

4. **Optimizer Performance**: Various optimizers were tested, with the Adam optimizer yielding the best results due to its adaptive learning rates and rapid convergence capabilities. The paper suggests that optimizing training through careful selection of hyper-parameters and using techniques like dropout can mitigate overfitting.

Overall, the research underscores the importance of architectural choices and training strategies in developing effective CNNs for digit recognition tasks, with a clear preference for a three-layer model under optimal conditions.
