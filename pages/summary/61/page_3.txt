This paper presents a detailed examination of a speech recognition system using the Kaldi s5c recipe, focusing on various test sets including Hub5 2000, Switchboard, and CallHome. The authors utilize 40-dimensional speaker-independent log-Mel filterbank features, augmented with delta and double-delta coefficients, resulting in 240-dimensional vectors extracted every 20 ms. Additionally, 100-dimensional i-vectors are incorporated, derived from a Gaussian mixture model trained on speaker-independent PLP features.

The training data undergoes several augmentation techniques: speed and tempo perturbation, sequence noise injection, and SpecAugment settings. The model architecture consists of a bidirectional LSTM encoder with 6 layers and a unidirectional LSTM prediction network. The joint network merges the encoder and prediction embeddings, producing outputs projected to logits corresponding to characters and a blank token.

The authors evaluate the impact of different optimizers, batch sizes, and learning rate schedules on performance, revealing that the AdamW optimizer with OneCycleLR is optimal. An ablation study further investigates the contributions of individual components, indicating that disabling switchout can enhance performance when using external language models, contrary to previous findings.

The paper also compares additive and multiplicative integration in RNN-T models, showing the efficacy of combining outputs from both integration types. Recognition results across various test sets highlight the effectiveness of the proposed methods and configurations in improving speech recognition accuracy.
