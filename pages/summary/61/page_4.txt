The paper presents a detailed analysis of various modeling techniques for speech recognition using multiplicative integration in recurrent neural network transducers (RNN-Ts). The results indicate that multiplicative RNN-Ts perform comparably on the Hub5’00 and Hub5’01 datasets, but are slightly less effective on RT’03. Model combination notably enhances recognition performance across all test sets.

In comparison to existing systems on the Switchboard 300 hours corpus, the proposed modeling techniques demonstrate superior performance, particularly highlighting the effectiveness of multiplicative integration.

Subsequent experiments were conducted on conversational Spanish and Italian datasets. The Spanish dataset, comprising 780 hours of call center data, used a two-layer LSTM external language model. Various techniques were implemented, including DropConnect, speed/tempo adjustments, and CTC encoder pretraining, resulting in a significant reduction in word error rate (WER) from 34.8% to 20.0%.

The Italian dataset, consisting of 900 hours of conversational speech, was analyzed using both bidirectional and unidirectional RNN-Ts. Results showed that multiplicative integration outperformed additive integration, especially in unidirectional models. Additionally, density ratio language model fusion led to improved performance compared to shallow fusion.

The paper concludes by emphasizing the advantages of multiplicative integration and the importance of careful optimization, learning rate scheduling, and data augmentation. Future work will explore alternative architectures and training criteria to further enhance speech recognition capabilities.
