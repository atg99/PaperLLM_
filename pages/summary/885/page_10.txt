The paper presents an internal-external style transfer method enhanced by two novel contrastive losses. The model without external learning (EL) focuses solely on increasing style similarity between the stylized and style images, neglecting the naturalness of color distributions and texture patterns. In contrast, the model with EL utilizes human-aware style information derived from a large-scale style dataset, resulting in more realistic and harmonious stylized images that can be indistinguishable from real artworks.

The proposed contrastive losses improve the matching of target styles to content images by refining the stylization results through the consideration of stylization-to-stylization relationships. User preference tests indicate that the full model outperforms others. Additionally, ablation studies in video style transfer reveal that removing EL or contrastive learning leads to stability degradation, as evidenced by changes in color and LPIPS distance metrics. EL enhances stability by reducing distorted textures, while contrastive learning stabilizes adjacent stylized frames.

The paper acknowledges limitations, noting that the internal-external learning framework and contrastive losses cannot be applied to learning-free style transfer methods. Furthermore, style images significantly different from the training styles may not benefit from the external learning scheme during inference.

In conclusion, the proposed method effectively generates visually harmonious artistic images and enhances the stability and consistency of video style transfer. Future work aims to extend the method to other vision tasks, such as texture synthesis.
