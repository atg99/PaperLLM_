The paper presents a comparative analysis of various style transfer methods, focusing on the performance of the proposed method against established techniques. Table 2 summarizes the average LPIPS distances, where lower values indicate better performance. The proposed method achieved an LPIPS distance of 0.317, outperforming several alternatives like Gatys et al. and AdaIN.

The study includes user preference evaluations, where participants favored the stylized images produced by the proposed method over those from competing methods. Additionally, a deception score analysis was conducted to assess how closely the AI-generated artworks resemble human-created ones, revealing that the proposed method's results were most similar to human artworks.

LPIPS was used as a metric to evaluate the stability and consistency of the method in video style transfer, with the proposed approach yielding the best scores. Furthermore, ablation studies were performed to investigate the impact of external learning (EL) and contrastive learning (CL) on image style transfer. The absence of EL resulted in less coherent stylized images, highlighting its significance in maintaining quality.

Overall, the results indicate that the proposed method not only enhances user preference but also maintains higher stability and consistency in both image and video style transfer tasks.
