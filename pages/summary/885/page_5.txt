The paper presents a novel approach to artistic image generation through a combination of internal and external style learning, content structure preservation, identity loss, and contrastive learning. 

For internal style learning, the authors utilize a pre-trained VGG-19 network to extract style characteristics from a single artistic image. The style loss is defined based on the mean and standard deviation of feature maps captured at specific layers of the network. 

External style learning is achieved using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), where a generator creates stylized images and a discriminator evaluates their authenticity against real artworks. This adversarial training enhances the generator's capability to produce realistic images that embody learned human-aware styles.

To maintain the content structure of the original image in the stylized output, the authors employ a perceptual loss, while an identity loss is implemented to ensure that when the content and style images are identical, the output remains close to the input. 

Additionally, the paper introduces contrastive learning to strengthen the relationships between stylized images based on shared content or style. This method enhances the traditional style transfer techniques by focusing on the connections between stylizations, thereby improving the overall quality and coherence of the generated artistic images.
