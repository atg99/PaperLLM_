This section discusses the qualitative comparisons of image style transfer methods, specifically focusing on the performance of the proposed method in relation to several state-of-the-art baselines. The qualitative results are illustrated in Figure 3, which includes the original content and style images, followed by the stylized outputs generated by different methods.

The training process utilizes a learning rate of 0.0001 with a batch size of 16 over 160,000 iterations, using the MS-COCO dataset for content and WikiArt for style. Images are resized to maintain aspect ratio, and 256x256 patches are randomly cropped for input. The proposed method accommodates content and style images of any size during the reference stage.

Comparing the proposed method with six baselines—Gatys et al., AdaIN, WCT, Avatar-Net, LST, and SANet—reveals various shortcomings in each. Gatys et al. often falls into local minima, leading to suboptimal results. AdaIN can produce chaotic images with unintended colors and edge halation. WCT is noted for distorted patterns, resulting in less structured outputs. Avatar-Net struggles with sharp details, while LST yields limited texture diversity. SANet frequently applies repetitive texture patterns across styles. Overall, while advancements have been made, significant gaps remain in achieving high-quality artistic stylization.
