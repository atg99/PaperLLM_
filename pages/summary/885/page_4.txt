The paper presents a novel internal-external style transfer method designed to improve the quality of stylized images, addressing issues of color disharmony and repetitive patterns that are often found in AI-generated artworks. The proposed framework, illustrated in Figure 2, is built upon the existing SANet architecture and incorporates a pre-trained VGG-19 encoder for feature extraction, a style-attentional transformation module for matching style features to content features, and a decoder for generating stylized images.

Key components of the method include various loss functions: the style loss (Ls) and content loss (Lc) to capture style and content information, respectively, an adversarial loss (Ladv) to ensure the style is human-aware, and an identity loss (Lidentity) that helps maintain content structures and style characteristics in the final image. Additionally, two contrastive losses (Ls-contra and Lc-contra) are introduced to learn relationships between different stylizations.

The goal is to better bridge the gap between human-created and AI-generated artworks by learning both internal style characteristics from a single artwork and external style information that reflects human artistic preferences. The paper emphasizes the importance of these enhancements to achieve results that are more indistinguishable from real artwork.
