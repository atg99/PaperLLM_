The paper presents a comparison of various video style transfer methods, focusing on the effectiveness and quality of stylization results. Figure 4 illustrates several video frames and a style image, with subsequent rows displaying the outcomes of different style transfer techniques. The last column features heat maps indicating differences between frames, underscoring the stability and consistency of the synthesized video clips.

In Table 1, user study scores are provided for different methods, highlighting that the proposed approach significantly outperforms existing techniques in terms of preference and deception scores. The introduction of internal-external learning and contrastive learning is emphasized as a means to create more visually harmonious artistic images. These innovations lead to smoother stylization results by reducing distorted textures and ensuring consistency between adjacent frames.

The paper also discusses the importance of quantitative evaluations to complement qualitative assessments, employing user studies as a widely accepted metric for style transfer performance. A total of 150 stylized images were synthesized for evaluation, with selected content-style pairs presented to participants for preference scoring. This systematic approach reinforces the proposed method's superiority in achieving stable and consistent video stylization.
