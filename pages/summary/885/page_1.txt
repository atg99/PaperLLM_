The paper introduces a novel approach to artistic style transfer that seeks to address common issues found in existing methods, such as disharmonious colors and repetitive patterns. The authors propose an internal-external style transfer method that employs two contrastive losses. This approach utilizes internal statistics from a single style image to influence the colors and textures of the stylized output. Simultaneously, it incorporates external information from a large-scale style dataset to enhance the human-aware aspects of the style, leading to more coherent and visually appealing results.

The authors argue that traditional style transfer techniques often overlook the relationships between different stylizations, focusing primarily on content-to-stylization and style-to-stylization connections. To remedy this, the paper introduces two contrastive losses that encourage embeddings of multiple stylizations to be closer when they share the same content or style, while distancing them otherwise.

Through extensive experimentation, the proposed method demonstrates the ability to produce more harmonious artistic images and improve the stability and consistency of stylized video clips. The paper highlights the importance of leveraging both internal and external style information to bridge the gap between synthesized stylizations and real artworks.
