This paper presents a novel approach to artistic style transfer, addressing limitations in existing methods by incorporating both internal and external learning mechanisms. It emphasizes the importance of human-aware style information, which is often absent in traditional stylization techniques. The authors propose an internal-external learning scheme that utilizes internal statistics from individual artworks to capture color and texture, while also leveraging Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to learn broader style features from a large dataset. This dual approach enhances the coherence and harmony of the stylized images, making them more aligned with human-created artworks.

Additionally, the paper introduces two contrastive losses—content contrastive loss and style contrastive loss—that enhance the relationships between stylized images sharing the same content or style. This is a novel contribution, as it is the first application of contrastive learning in the context of style transfer, aiming to improve the consistency and quality of the stylization process.

The key contributions of the paper include:
1. A new internal-external style transfer method that bridges the gap between human and AI-created art.
2. The introduction of contrastive learning to style transfer, refining the relationships between stylized outputs.
3. Extensive experimental validation showcasing the effectiveness and advantages of the proposed method over existing state-of-the-art techniques.

The paper thus positions its approach as a significant advancement in the field of artistic style transfer, promising improved visual output and stability in applications such as video stylization.
