This paper discusses advancements in artistic style transfer techniques, highlighting the limitations of traditional methods that rely on low-level statistics and often neglect semantic information. The introduction of deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) by Gatys et al. marked a significant evolution, allowing for the use of the Gram matrix to characterize visual styles. Following this, various neural approaches have been developed to enhance efficiency, visual quality, and generalization in style transfer, although issues such as spurious artifacts remain prevalent.

The paper distinguishes between style transfer and image-to-image translation (I2I), noting that I2I operates within content-similar domains, whereas style transfer is more flexible with content and style images. It suggests that insights from I2I could improve stylization quality in style transfer by incorporating large-scale style image collections.

Furthermore, the paper explores the concept of internal-external learning, which has been effective in other image generation tasks but is underutilized in style transfer. The authors propose a novel internal-external style transfer method that leverages both internal and external learning to enhance stylization outcomes.

Finally, the paper introduces contrastive learning as a new approach for artistic style transfer. It outlines the components of contrastive learning and presents two innovative contrastive losses—content contrastive loss and style contrastive loss—to better capture relationships in stylization that previous methods have overlooked. This marks a pioneering effort to integrate contrastive learning into the realm of artistic style transfer.
