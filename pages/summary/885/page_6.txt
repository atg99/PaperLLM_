This paper presents a novel approach to stylization using two contrastive losses: style contrastive loss and content contrastive loss. These losses are designed to learn relationships between stylized images based on their style and content. The authors denote style images as si, content images as ci, and stylized images as sicj, where i and j index the respective images.

In the training process, a batch of style and content images is structured to facilitate contrastive learning. Each stylized image has corresponding positive examples that share either the same style or content, while negative examples are drawn from other images within the batch. This arrangement ensures that for each stylized image, there is a positive counterpart with matching style or content and multiple negative examples.

The style contrastive loss is defined to associate stylized images with the same style, selecting a positive example and multiple negative examples. It is mathematically expressed to optimize the similarity of style embeddings derived from a style projection network. Similarly, the content contrastive loss focuses on images sharing the same content, employing a content projection network for embedding extraction.

The final objective function of the model combines various losses, including adversarial loss and identity loss, weighted by hyper-parameters to ensure a balanced contribution from each component.

The experimental section outlines the methodology, which builds on the SANet architecture, enhancing it with the proposed contrastive learning framework. The implementation details include the structure of the projection networks and the settings for the hyper-parameters. The authors plan to provide qualitative and quantitative comparisons against baseline models and conduct ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components in their model.
