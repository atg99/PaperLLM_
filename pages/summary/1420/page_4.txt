The paper discusses a model for integrating Vision-Language and Concept-Language representations using attention-based representation modules. It describes how input question tokens are represented as queries and combined with knowledge graph (KG) embeddings to produce a final representation through Multi-Head Attention. The model enhances the question representation by incorporating common sense knowledge from the KG.

The Concept-Vision-Language embedding module aggregates outputs from three streams—question, answer, and image features—using a Compact Trilinear Interaction (CTI) to create a joint representation. This approach allows the model to detect high-level interactions while retaining lower-level details. The joint representation is computed efficiently, avoiding issues of dimensionality that arise from concatenating large matrices.

The final joint representation is then used by a classifier to predict answers. The model's performance is evaluated using the standard metric from the Visual Question Answering (VQA) challenge, highlighting its effectiveness in providing accurate answers based on the integrated representations.
