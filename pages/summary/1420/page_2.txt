Unlike prior models that rely on context-free word embeddings, ConceptBert leverages pre-trained language representation models, specifically BERT, while also incorporating knowledge graph (KG) embeddings. This integration allows for a more comprehensive understanding of the relationship between images, questions, and external knowledge. The model architecture consists of two main modules: the vision-language representation, which enhances both image and question embeddings, and the concept-language representation, which incorporates relevant KG information into the question embedding.

Key contributions of this work include the introduction of a novel methodology for infusing common sense knowledge into VQA models, the development of concept-aware representations that utilize knowledge graph embeddings, and the creation of multimodal Concept-Visual-Language embeddings. The model is designed to be fully trainable in an end-to-end manner, eliminating the need for additional knowledge annotations or search queries, thus reducing computational costs.

In summary, the paper presents a robust approach to VQA that effectively combines various modalities to predict meaningful answers by learning a joint representation of the question, image, and knowledge graph.
