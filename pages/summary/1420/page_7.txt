The evaluation results presented in the paper demonstrate that ConceptBert outperforms several state-of-the-art (SOTA) models, particularly in specific categories such as Cooking and Food, Plants and Animals, and Science and Technology, where it shows gains exceeding 3%. This success is attributed to the model's ability to leverage external knowledge from the ConceptNet knowledge graph, enabling it to provide contextually relevant answers that go beyond mere visual recognition.

In qualitative comparisons, ConceptBert's complete form, CVL, is shown to effectively utilize both visual and external knowledge, allowing it to derive correct answers based on semantic relationships and contextual facts rather than solely on visual cues. For instance, it can infer that an elephant is herbivorous and relate a black cat to Halloween, demonstrating a deeper understanding of the relationships between objects. Conversely, the VL model tends to focus narrowly on visual features, leading to less accurate answers.

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of integrating external knowledge and advanced embedding techniques in enhancing the capabilities of VQA systems, as evidenced by ConceptBert's performance in various question categories and its ability to maintain semantic consistency in responses.
