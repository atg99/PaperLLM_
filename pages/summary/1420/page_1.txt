The paper presents ConceptBert, a novel algorithm designed for Visual Question Answering (VQA) that incorporates external common sense knowledge through a Knowledge Graph (KG). Unlike traditional VQA approaches that rely solely on visual content and direct question analysis, ConceptBert enhances the answering process by integrating visual elements from images with structured knowledge. This integration allows the model to handle more complex questions requiring factual knowledge.

ConceptBert employs a multi-modal representation that jointly embeds Concept, Vision, and Language, leveraging the ConceptNet KG to encode common sense knowledge. The paper highlights the limitations of existing VQA methods, which often fail when external knowledge is necessary. By organizing facts into a structured database, the model benefits from the relational representation of objects and entities, enhancing its understanding of how to derive answers.

The authors note that while there has been some research into incorporating knowledge into VQA systems, many existing methods rely on pre-defined facts or search queries. In contrast, ConceptBert offers an end-to-end trainable solution that does not depend on such annotations or queries, marking a significant advancement in the VQA field. The methodology was evaluated on the Outside Knowledge-VQA (OK-VQA) and VQA datasets, with the code made available for further research.
