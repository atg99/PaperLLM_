VQA 2.0 consists of approximately 1.1 million questions paired with 204,721 images, primarily derived from the COCO dataset. Each image is associated with an average of 5.4 questions, and we focus on questions with at least 9 identical answers to ensure quality. The questions are categorized into Yes/No, Number, and Other, with a particular emphasis on the Other category, which often requires external knowledge for accurate answers.

OK-VQA, on the other hand, is tailored for questions that cannot be answered through direct analysis of images or entities. It contains 14,031 images and 14,055 questions across eleven categories, including vehicles, cooking, and science. Each question is linked to a unanimous answer deemed the ground truth.

The implementation of our model involves several key components. For image embedding, we utilize 36 region features derived from pre-trained Faster R-CNN, while question embedding employs BERT's BASE model to represent each word as a 768-D vector. We also explored various node embeddings for ConceptNet, finding that the embedding from Malaviya et al. performed best.

For the vision-language representation, we leverage pre-trained ViLBERT features, which are built on a dataset of image-caption pairs to learn interactions between visual and textual data. Our model includes multiple Transformer layers for both vision-language and concept-language representations. We tested the concept-vision-language representation with varying dimensions, identifying that a size of 1024 yielded optimal results despite increased computational costs.

Lastly, we employ a binary cross-entropy loss function for classification, utilizing a batch size of 1024 and training over a maximum of 20 epochs on multiple GPUs. The BertAdam optimizer is used to enhance training efficacy.
