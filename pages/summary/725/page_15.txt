The paper presents a Markov Decision Process (MDP) for the Fast-Slow Lane Selection problem, aiming to develop a policy for choosing between a faster left lane and a slower right lane. The study investigates how different risk appetites influence policy decisions, hypothesizing that varying levels of risk will yield distinct optimal policies.

The MDP is simple enough to allow brute force sampling-based policy improvement methods to identify optimal solutions. The methodology involves grid sampling of the policy space, evaluating each policy through 1000 random trials to compute the Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR). The optimal policy is determined by selecting the one that maximizes CVaR for each risk level.

Results indicate that for low risk levels, the optimal strategy favors the right lane due to lower reward variance, while higher risk levels shift preference towards the left lane for higher expected rewards. This confirms the hypothesis that risk appetite affects policy selection, demonstrating that the proposed CVaR objective can yield conditional policies tailored to individual risk preferences.

Additionally, the paper provides extrapolation results for unprotected left and merge environments, detailing percentages and standard errors from 100 trials with varying random seeds. Overall, this work illustrates the applicability of CVaR in policy learning within decision-making frameworks.
