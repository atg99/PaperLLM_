The paper presents testing performance results for a reinforcement learning policy in two environments: unprotected turns and merges. The analysis is organized into three main sections. 

In the first section, the left panel of Figure 5 illustrates that as the parameter beta decreases, the policy becomes more risk-averse, resulting in a lower probability of collisions, indicated by the red line representing collision-related rewards. The middle panel shows that a smaller beta leads to longer episode completion times due to the conservative behavior of the learned policy, which tends to wait for wider gaps. The right panel reveals that the critic's estimate of uncertainty increases with a higher beta, suggesting that the actor exhibits greater risk tolerance as beta increases.

The second section addresses extrapolation performance, where the policy's ability to handle out-of-distribution scenarios is tested. This involves increasing the velocity of non-ego agents and the density of spawned agents. Performance results are summarized in Tables 1 and 2, which report collision rates across 100 random trials for the WCPG algorithm and four baseline algorithms: DDPG, proximal policy optimization (PPO), C51/Rainbow, and D4PG. The training results for WCPG are highlighted, with comparisons made to ensure consistent parameters across the algorithms.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that the WCPG policy effectively balances risk and performance in different environments, suggesting its robustness in both standard and extrapolated scenarios.
