The paper discusses the challenges of driving simulations where the behaviors of other vehicles are not always observable, introducing a stochastic element with three possible actions: yielding, ignoring, or aggressively accelerating. This uncertainty necessitates the use of distributional reinforcement learning (RL).

In the driving simulation experiments, various parameters are detailed in Table 4, including environment scale, ego and agent velocities, spawn rates, and probabilities of yielding, ignoring, or being aggressive. The results regarding success and collision rates are also indicated.

The network parameters for the Weighted Continuous Policy Gradient (WCPG) actor and critic networks used in these simulations are specified in Table 5, outlining the architecture for both networks, including the dimensions of each layer and activation functions.

Additionally, a proof-of-concept experiment is presented, focusing on lane selection in a simplified highway scenario. This discrete setting involves a vehicle deciding whether to drive in the fast or slow lane, modeled as a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP) with stochastic rewards. The fast lane offers higher potential rewards with greater variability, while the slow lane provides lower, more stable rewards. The aim is to learn an optimal policy for lane changes, showcasing the fundamental concepts of the WCPG approach in a less complex environment.
