This paper presents a framework for the distributional Bellman operator, focusing on both the mean and variance of future returns in reinforcement learning. The mean projection follows the standard Bellman equation, similar to Q-learning, represented as Q(s,a) = r(s,a) + Σ_s' p(s'|s,a)Q(s',a'). The variance projection, as stated in Proposition 1, is defined in a more complex manner, incorporating the variance of returns through a parametric equation that combines rewards, state transitions, and existing value functions.

To handle the continuous state and action spaces, the authors employ function approximation using a neural network to estimate both the mean and variance of the critic's output. They utilize Temporal Difference (TD) learning, assuming Gaussian distributions and employing the Wasserstein metric for loss calculation. The paper highlights the advantages of using the p-th Wasserstein distance as a contraction operator in policy evaluations.

The framework also introduces a Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) actor, which computes the CVaR measure based on the critic's estimated mean and variance. This measure is defined for each state-action pair and represents the expected future CVaR when following a specific policy. The authors propose a new risk-averse objective function that incorporates the CVaR, allowing for a more nuanced approach to policy optimization.

The paper concludes with a theorem that relates the gradient of the new objective to the original Policy Gradient Theorem, enabling the derivation of a deterministic policy gradient that accounts for the risk level specified by the CVaR. The authors suggest a strategy for selecting the risk level by discretizing it into a range, enhancing the flexibility of their approach in risk-sensitive decision-making scenarios.
