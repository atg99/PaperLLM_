This paper presents a novel actor-critic framework aimed at developing risk-sensitive policies in reinforcement learning (RL) by maximizing Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR). The authors highlight significant improvements in collision and success rates in simulated driving scenarios, as illustrated in Table 3. The results indicate that lower risk tolerance values enhance robustness across various scenarios.

The paper discusses existing literature on safe RL and robust Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), categorizing them into two types: those modifying exploration processes to avoid unsafe actions, and those altering optimality criteria during training. The proposed method falls into the latter category, focusing on balancing pay-offs while minimizing the likelihood of catastrophic outcomes.

Various safe criteria have been previously proposed, including exponential utility functions and the minimax criterion. However, these approaches may lead to overly cautious policies. In contrast, the proposed WCPG method leverages distributional RL techniques to compute CVaR efficiently without extensive sampling, allowing for dynamic risk adjustment post-deployment.

The authors suggest potential future work, such as modeling uncertainty with heavy-tailed distributions and exploring automated risk tolerance selection to optimize maneuver completion while minimizing risk. Overall, the proposed framework demonstrates a significant advancement in risk-sensitive decision-making within RL, particularly in complex environments like autonomous driving.
