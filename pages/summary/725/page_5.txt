The paper discusses a novel approach to training policy networks for driving environments, specifically focusing on unprotected left turns and highway merges. Instead of creating separate policy networks for different risk settings, the authors propose a single network that is conditioned on a risk-sensitivity parameter. This allows the model to learn a continuous range of conditional policies, with actions varying from conservative to aggressive based on the parameter value.

The training process involves sampling the risk parameter uniformly at the start of each episode, fixing it for the duration of that episode, which allows for different loss functions to be applied based on the parameter. The network employs an off-policy training algorithm using an experience replay buffer, detailed in Algorithm 1 in the Appendix.

Experimental results were obtained from simulations using a discrete time kinematics bicycle model, where each vehicle aims to navigate from point A to B while avoiding collisions. The action space is limited to acceleration, with steering controlled by a non-linear feedback mechanism. Non-ego vehicles exhibit rule-based behaviors, randomly adopting one of three strategies: yielding, ignoring, or accelerating. The reward system includes significant penalties for collisions and rewards based on the efficiency of maneuver completion.

The proposed WCPG network builds on the DDPG framework with two key enhancements: the inclusion of a conditional value-at-risk input and an output that governs the distribution of future returns rather than a single scalar value. This approach aims to balance safety and efficiency in driving policy learning.
