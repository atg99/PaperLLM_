The paper presents a novel approach called worst cases policy gradients (WCPG) aimed at optimizing reinforcement learning policies by considering risk, specifically through the use of conditional Value-at-Risk. The architecture builds on the actor-critic model, but instead of predicting just the expected returns, the critic forecasts the full distribution of future returns. This allows for the development of a family of policies that can be tailored to different risk appetites, enabling varied actions from the same state based on the desired risk level.

The framework is tested in traffic simulation scenarios, demonstrating that WCPG effectively trains agents to safely interact with others while pursuing their objectives. The results indicate that WCPG learns risk-averse policies that exhibit greater robustness in unpredictable environments.

The foundational problem is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), encompassing state and action spaces, a reward function, and a transition model. The paper details the formulation of the policy and value functions, emphasizing the limitations of traditional reinforcement learning methods that focus solely on maximizing expected returns. Instead, WCPG aims to mitigate risks associated with high variance in return distributions, thereby fostering the development of more resilient policies against adverse outcomes. 

In summary, WCPG enhances policy gradient methods by integrating risk considerations into the learning process, resulting in policies that are better suited for environments characterized by uncertainty and variability.
