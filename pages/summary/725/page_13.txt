This paper discusses the application of a risk-sensitive training algorithm for reinforcement learning in self-driving environments, emphasizing the need for safety in decision-making. The gradient of the objective function is derived using the log-derivative trick, leading to expressions that facilitate the optimization of both the actor and critic networks in the context of risk management.

The proposed algorithm, termed WCPG (Weighted Conditional Policy Gradient), is outlined in a step-by-step manner, detailing its inputs, initialization, and iterative training process. The algorithm integrates exploration noise into action selection to enhance learning within the environment. A replay buffer is utilized to sample experiences for updating the actor and critic networks, with specific equations guiding the updates.

A key aspect of the approach is the distributional critic, which employs a Gaussian approximation for value distributions. This choice allows for efficient computation of Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) during policy updates, essential for managing risks associated with potential negative rewards, such as collisions. Despite the Gaussian's unimodality, the authors acknowledge that the actual reward distribution can be multi-modal, reflecting the complexities of real-world scenarios.

The experimental focus is on self-driving simulations, highlighting the critical nature of safety and risk-sensitive decision-making in these multi-agent environments. Overall, the paper presents a structured methodology for integrating risk considerations into reinforcement learning frameworks, aiming to improve safety in autonomous driving applications.
