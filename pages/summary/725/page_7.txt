The paper presents a comparative analysis of a novel approach, WCPG, against state-of-the-art reinforcement learning methods (DDPG, PPO, C51, and D4PG) in two driving environments: left turn and merge scenarios. The data in Tables 1 and 2 illustrate collision rates and success rates across various parameters, such as velocity and spawn conditions. 

In both environments, WCPG consistently achieves a 0% collision rate, demonstrating superior robustness compared to traditional methods, which experience significant performance degradation when faced with varying environmental parameters. For instance, while PPO shows severe overfitting and DDPG begins to collide under out-of-distribution parameters, WCPG maintains high success rates with minimal collisions.

The paper also explores uncertainty modeling, highlighting how the critic's estimation of the standard deviation of future returns fluctuates with the ego vehicle's position relative to oncoming traffic. As the vehicle approaches intersections and engages in maneuvers, uncertainty peaks, reflecting the unpredictable behavior of other vehicles.

Additionally, the research includes testing the transferability of the WCPG policy in the CARLA simulator, where the policy is applied to similar driving scenarios, indicating its potential for real-world applications despite slower simulation speeds.

Overall, the findings suggest that WCPG outperforms existing techniques in both safety and adaptability, marking a significant advancement in reinforcement learning for autonomous driving. Further research is suggested to enhance training directly within the CARLA environment.
