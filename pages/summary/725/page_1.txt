The paper "Worst Cases Policy Gradients" by Yichuan Charlie Tang, Jian Zhang, and Ruslan Salakhutdinov presents a novel actor-critic framework that addresses the need for risk sensitivity in deep reinforcement learning (RL), particularly for safety-critical applications. The authors emphasize the importance of accounting for uncertainty in future returns when making sequential decisions in complex environments, as traditional RL methods typically focus on maximizing expected returns without considering potential risks.

To tackle this issue, the proposed framework models the distribution of future returns for various states and actions, allowing the optimization of policies based on conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR). This approach enables the learned policy to adaptively select actions that vary along a risk continuum, ranging from conservative to riskier behaviors. The effectiveness of this method is demonstrated through driving simulations, where the risk-averse policies exhibit significantly better generalization across different simulation parameters compared to conventional RL approaches.

The authors argue that their risk-sensitive framework is crucial for leveraging deep RL in real-world applications such as autonomous driving, where understanding and mitigating risks is essential for safe operation. Overall, the paper contributes to advancing safe and robust decision-making in reinforcement learning by incorporating risk-sensitive mechanisms.
