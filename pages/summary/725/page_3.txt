This paper discusses a novel approach to reinforcement learning (RL) that integrates risk-sensitive methods, specifically focusing on Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR). Traditional RL methods aim to maximize expected future returns, as illustrated by the red line in the left panel of Figure 2. In contrast, the proposed approach seeks to optimize policies based on CVaR, which emphasizes performance in worst-case scenarios rather than average outcomes.

The paper begins by modeling the distribution of expected future returns for various state-action pairs. It defines the state-action value function Q as the expected return given a policy. The left panel of Figure 2 demonstrates the typical approach of shifting this expected value to maximize returns. However, for risk-averse learning, the goal shifts to maximizing CVaR, which assesses expected returns at the lower tail of the distribution. This is represented in the right panel, where optimizing CVaR aims to reduce the likelihood of negative outcomes.

Formally, CVaR is defined as the expected return conditional on the return falling below a specific percentile, denoted as pcntl. The paper highlights the challenge of directly computing CVaR over long time horizons due to computational costs. To address this, the authors extend the Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) algorithm, enabling the critic to learn the distribution of expected total discounted rewards. This allows for efficient computation of CVaR, which can be used to train the actor network through backpropagation.

The paper also notes the limitations of training a critic offline to derive a risk-sensitive policy online. Since the distribution of returns depends on the executed policy over time, selecting actions based solely on past performance metrics would be ineffective. The authors propose defining a distributional Bellman operator to model the returns, approximating the distribution of future returns with second-order statistics such as mean and variance.

By modeling the return distribution as a Gaussian, the paper facilitates closed-form calculations for CVaR, which is essential for updating policies across various state-action pairs and risk parameters efficiently. Overall, this approach provides a framework for integrating risk considerations into RL, enhancing the robustness of learned policies in uncertain environments.
