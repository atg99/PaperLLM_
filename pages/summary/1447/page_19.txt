The referenced papers discuss various advancements in speech recognition and associated technologies, focusing on bridging speech and text through innovative models and training techniques. 

Key developments include:

1. **Speechut** and **Speechlm**: These works introduce encoder-decoder models that leverage hidden units and unpaired textual data to enhance speech pre-training.

2. **Samu-xlsr**: This research presents a multimodal approach for cross-lingual speech representation, emphasizing semantic alignment at the utterance level.

3. **Mmspeech**: Proposes a multi-modal and multi-task encoder-decoder pre-training framework aimed at improving speech recognition capabilities.

4. **Joist** and **Jeit**: These studies focus on joint models that integrate speech and text for automatic speech recognition (ASR), enhancing performance through internal language model training.

5. **Modular hybrid autoregressive transducer**: This work explores modular architectures for improved speech recognition efficiency.

6. **Long-form speech recognition**: Several papers compare end-to-end models and address challenges related to input length for telephony speech recognition.

7. **Transformers and RNNs**: Key contributions highlight the application of transformer models and recurrent neural networks for sequence transduction and large-scale speech recognition.

8. **Real-time streaming models**: Innovations such as the streaming transformer transducer and Emformer focus on achieving low-latency performance in speech recognition tasks.

9. **Model specialization and adaptation**: Techniques for scalable model specialization and parameter-efficient adaptation to diverse speech types are also explored, addressing issues like accent and atypical speech patterns.

Overall, these contributions reflect significant progress in the integration of speech and text processing, leveraging deep learning techniques to enhance automatic speech recognition systems.
