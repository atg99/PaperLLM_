The paper discusses the limitations of attention-based encoders in automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems, particularly when processing long audio segments. Typically, these systems are trained on shorter audio clips, leading to a mismatch in the receptive fields during training and inference. The authors highlight that local self-attention mechanisms, which consider only a fixed amount of context, can create a situation where the receptive field grows disproportionately as more layers are added. This results in a significant performance degradation when models are applied to longer audio inputs than they were trained on, referred to as the "long-form performance degradation" problem.

To address this issue, the authors propose a modification to the attention mechanism by implementing chunk-wise attention. This approach divides the audio into smaller chunks, allowing attention to operate within each chunk without context leakage. Their experiments indicate that using an 8-second chunk provides an optimal balance between recognition quality and computational efficiency. 

The paper also notes that while other studies have explored similar modifications to attention for long-form audio, chunk-wise attention offers greater flexibility. Unlike block processing, which restricts encoder layers to the current chunk's context, chunk-wise attention permits the processing of contextual frames beyond the immediate chunk. The authors compare their method to the Whisper model, which segments audio into 30-second chunks, emphasizing that their approach maintains decoder access to the entire encoder output, leading to more robust performance in long-form ASR tasks with a simpler decoding process.
