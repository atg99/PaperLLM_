The referenced papers focus on advancements in speech recognition through various methodologies, particularly emphasizing unsupervised and semi-supervised learning techniques. Notable contributions include the development of wav2vec and vq-wav2vec, which utilize self-supervised learning for effective discrete speech representation. Studies demonstrate that unsupervised pre-training can enhance performance across multiple languages and improve low-resource acoustic models. 

The works highlight innovations in semi-supervised training approaches, such as self-training and local prior matching, which leverage untranscribed data to boost recognition accuracy. Additionally, multi-modal data augmentation and the integration of text inputs into acoustic models are explored as ways to further enhance model robustness and versatility.

Overall, the papers collectively underscore the importance of self-supervised learning and innovative training strategies in advancing end-to-end speech recognition systems.
