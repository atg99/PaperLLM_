The paper discusses the Multi-Objective Supervised Pre-training (MOST) framework for enhancing speech and text representation learning. It introduces a training methodology that incorporates unlabeled speech, unlabeled text, and paired speech-text data to improve the model's performance on automatic speech recognition (ASR) and audio-speech translation (AST) tasks.

The training employs a text-injection loss, which combines duration modeling and consistency regularization with a weighted BEST-RQ loss for the encoder. This approach has two main advantages: first, it aligns speech representations with text through paired data training, and second, it enhances the robustness of learned representations, especially for low-resource languages and domains.

Key architectural components include a speech-only encoder with a convolutional feature encoder and a conformer layer, a text-only encoder with an embedding layer and an upsampler, and a shared conformer encoder initialized from a pre-trained BEST-RQ speech encoder. The goal of the text-injection mechanism is to create joint embeddings of speech and text that can improve performance on speech-related tasks, even when text data lacks associated audio.

The architecture is trained using three types of data, each contributing to different loss functions, with unlabeled speech data primarily contributing to the BEST-RQ loss. Overall, the MOST framework aims to leverage diverse data types to enhance model performance and generalization capabilities in speech and text representation learning.
