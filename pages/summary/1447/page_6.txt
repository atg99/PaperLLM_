The paper introduces BEST-RQ, a pre-training method that utilizes a BERT-style training task for audio data by predicting masked speech features. The approach involves quantizing the original speech features corresponding to masked frames and predicting their discrete labels using a codebook of vectors. Cosine similarity serves as the distance measure for identifying the closest codebook vector. Unlike w2v-BERT, which requires an additional quantization module that complicates the model and tuning process, BEST-RQ eliminates this need, making it a more scalable option for pre-training.

The study enhances BEST-RQ by implementing a multi-softmax approach, using multiple codebooks to produce several probability predictions from the encoder's output. This strategy improves the stability and convergence of the model.

Additionally, the paper discusses the use of Noisy Student Training (NST) to create pseudo-labeled data, which augments supervised training. A teacher model is first trained on a supervised dataset with augmentations, and then it generates transcripts for unlabeled audio. A heuristic filtering method is applied to improve the quality of the pseudo-labeled data before mixing it with supervised data for training the student model.

The challenges of transcribing long audio segments in automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems are also acknowledged, indicating the need for effective solutions in real-world applications.
