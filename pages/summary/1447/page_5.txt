This paper presents a scalable self-supervised training framework for multilingual automatic speech recognition (ASR), building upon prior research in pre-training and semi-supervised learning. The authors analyze key components of their model, comparing its performance against existing methods. They highlight extensive literature on both pre-training and self-training for ASR, with a focus on large speech models in monolingual and multilingual contexts.

The research extends previous work on large speech models, particularly those exceeding 1 billion parameters, to create multilingual models. The authors enhance the self-supervised learning algorithm (BEST-RQ) and introduce multi-modal pre-training through text injection. An improvement to BEST-RQ is proposed, utilizing a multi-softmax loss, and they incorporate Multi-Objective Supervised Training to enhance speech representation quality using both transcribed and unlabeled data. The concept of chunk-wise attention is introduced as an alternative to traditional chunk-based decoding methods.

Key contributions include demonstrating that unsupervised speech models (USMs) pre-trained on 300 languages can effectively adapt to new languages with limited supervised data. A generic ASR model is built for 73 languages, fine-tuned on 90,000 hours of supervised data, achieving efficient inference on TPUs and reliable transcription of long audio segments on YouTube Caption ASR benchmarks. The authors conduct a systematic study on the impacts of pre-training, noisy student training, text injection, and model size on multilingual ASR performance.

The model architecture is based on the Conformer, which integrates convolutional layers with transformers, utilizing relative attention. The study examines two model sizes (600M and 2B parameters), with the larger model yielding primary results and the smaller model used for ablation studies. Pre-training is conducted using the BEST-RQ framework, characterized by a minimal number of hyperparameters for unsupervised training.
