The paper discusses the development of Universal Speech Models (USMs) utilizing a diverse array of datasets to enhance speech recognition capabilities. It highlights the potential of leveraging untranscribed speech and text data, which is abundant, alongside limited transcribed data through semi-supervised algorithms. This approach aims to create a robust universal automatic speech recognition (ASR) model that can effectively utilize large multilingual datasets.

The training pipeline employs three types of datasets: unpaired audio from sources like YouTube and public datasets, unpaired text from a multilingual corpus, and paired ASR data for supervised training. Specifically, the unpaired audio includes 12 million hours from YouTube and 429,000 hours from public datasets, while the unpaired text corpus comprises 28 billion sentences across 1140 languages. The paired data consists of approximately 100,000 hours of labeled multilingual data and 20,000 hours of public data.

The model architecture is based on a 2 billion parameter Conformer and involves three main training stages: unsupervised pre-training using the BEST-RQ method, multi-objective supervised pre-training incorporating various dataset types, and supervised ASR training utilizing connectionist temporal classification (CTC) and Listen, Attend, and Spell (LAS) methods. This results in two model types: pre-trained models that can be fine-tuned for specific tasks and generic ASR models that operate without further tuning.

The paper concludes that this innovative approach of combining extensive unpaired datasets with supervised learning techniques holds promise for advancing ASR technology across multiple languages and domains.
