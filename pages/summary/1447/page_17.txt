The references listed cover a range of advancements in automatic speech recognition (ASR) and self-supervised learning techniques. The paper by Li et al. presents ASR capabilities for around 2000 languages without the need for audio data, indicating a significant leap in multilingual speech processing. Baevski et al. introduce wav2vec 2.0, which emphasizes self-supervised learning for speech representation, showcasing the importance of leveraging unlabeled data in training models. 

Research by Xie et al. and Park et al. focuses on improving classification and ASR through self-training methods, demonstrating the efficacy of noisy student training techniques. The Conformer model proposed by Gulati et al. combines convolutional structures with transformers to enhance speech recognition performance.

Chiu et al. discuss random-projection quantizers for self-supervised learning in speech tasks, while Devlin et al. introduce BERT, which has influenced various language understanding applications, including speech. Chen et al. explore injecting text into self-supervised speech pretraining and the Maestro framework for speech-text representation alignment.

Graves et al. and Chan et al. contribute foundational work in labeling unsegmented data and large vocabulary speech recognition, respectively. The FLEURS project by Conneau et al. addresses few-shot learning in universal speech representations, which is crucial for resource-scarce languages.

The corpus of regional African American language and multilingual speech-to-text translation efforts are pivotal for inclusivity in language technology. Bapna et al. present MSLAM, which emphasizes joint pre-training for speech and text, while Chung et al. propose W2V-BERT, merging contrastive learning with masked language modeling.

Hsu and Glass's work on domain-invariant feature extraction enhances ASR robustness, while Chung et al.'s Speech2Vec framework advances word embedding learning from speech. The references collectively illustrate the ongoing evolution in ASR technologies, focusing on efficiency, multilingual capabilities, and the integration of self-supervised learning approaches.
