This paper discusses the creation and utilization of extensive datasets for speech and text data to enhance language identification and automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems. The YT-513-U dataset was developed by collecting unlabeled speech data from YouTube videos in 513 languages, focusing on lower-resource languages. This dataset includes over 102k hours of speech, with a significant portion coming from languages in South Asia, Southeast Asia, and West Africa.

In addition to YT-513-U, the paper describes the incorporation of public unsupervised and supervised datasets for training. The public unsupervised set includes 429k hours of unlabeled speech data spanning 51 languages, while the supervised set contains approximately 1.3k hours of speech and transcript data across 14 languages. These public datasets are only used for in-domain pre-training to benchmark the performance of generic USM-LAS/CTC models.

For text data, the authors utilized a web-crawled corpus containing over 28 billion sentences across 1140 languages, with a focus on up-sampling lower-resource languages. 

The paper also presents results from two public benchmarks, SpeechStew and FLEURS, and an internal benchmark on YouTube for ASR. The SpeechStew dataset combines several public speech corpora, while FLEURS is a multi-way parallel dataset with read speech in 102 languages. The authors report both Word Error Rate (WER) and Character Error Rate (CER) metrics to evaluate their ASR system against existing models, highlighting the importance of these benchmarks in assessing the performance of their developed systems.
