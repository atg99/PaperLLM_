The paper introduces two pre-trained models, USM and USM-M, with the latter indicating the use of MOST for model preparation. These models can be fine-tuned on various downstream tasks using different transducer units such as CTC, LAS, or RNN-T. The evaluation focuses on two main domains: Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and Automatic Speech Translation (AST). For ASR, the models were trained using YouTube data and tested on public benchmarks including SpeechStew, FLEURS, and CORAAL. In AST, performance was assessed on the CoV oST 2 dataset.

The training process is divided into three stages. The first stage involves training a conformer backbone on a large unlabeled speech dataset using the BEST-RQ objective. The second stage optimizes this model for multiple objectives, including supervised ASR losses and text reconstruction. The final stage fine-tunes the pre-trained encoder for specific ASR or AST tasks.

Key findings indicate that the USM models achieve state-of-the-art results for multilingual ASR and AST across various datasets, including impressive performance on YouTube captioning with a word error rate below 30% across 73 languages. This performance was achieved with 90k hours of supervised data, outperforming the Whisper model, which was trained on significantly more data. The results demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed training pipeline in developing both generic and domain-specific ASR systems.
