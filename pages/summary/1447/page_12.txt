The paper presents a comparison of Word Error Rates (WERs) across various speech recognition and translation tasks, focusing on the performance of the USM models against established baselines like Whisper. Specifically, it details experiments with long-form audio from YouTube, where the analysis is limited to the top-25 languages Whisper was trained on, excluding those with a WER exceeding 40% to mitigate distortion from hallucinations in the Whisper model. 

The results indicate that the USM models, including USM-LAS and USM-CTC, outperform Whisper in several settings, achieving lower WERs in tasks such as multilingual long-form ASR and speech translation (AST). The paper notes that specific adjustments, like using segmented decoding for Whisper and incorporating an adapter setup that minimally increases parameters while keeping the encoder frozen, contribute to improved performance.

Additionally, the study highlights the challenges faced by the Whisper-shortform model, particularly in the YouTube long-form dataset due to high deletion issues. The authors also report that for certain languages, Character Error Rates (CER) were used instead of WER to align with Whisper's reporting standards. Overall, the findings emphasize the robustness of the USM models in multilingual tasks, particularly in reducing hallucination effects compared to previous systems.
