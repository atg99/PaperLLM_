The paper presents a comparative analysis of the USM-CTC and USM-LAS models against the Whisper model in automatic speech recognition (ASR) tasks across various datasets, including YouTube en-US and out-of-domain sets like CORAAL and SpeechStew. Notably, USM-CTC outperforms Whisper by a significant margin, achieving superior performance on long-form tasks without degradation, while USM-LAS demonstrates a 30% relative improvement in word error rate (WER) on 18 languages, despite relying on less training data.

The multilingual performance of the models is also highlighted, with USM-LAS and USM-CTC surpassing Whisper by 66% relative WER on the FLEURS benchmark, utilizing fewer multilingual supervised hours. The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of in-domain fine-tuning, which contributes to a 30% relative improvement across 102 languages.

The study introduces the MOST training method, which aligns speech and text representations, allowing for effective adaptation to new domains with minimal parameter adjustments. The USM-M-adapter model shows competitive performance with only a 2% increase in parameters.

Additionally, the paper discusses the challenges of ASR for unseen languages, where paired transcriptions are unavailable. Using the USM-LAS-Adapter as a teacher model, the authors successfully generate pseudo-labels for these languages, demonstrating significant improvements in recognition results, particularly for languages like Shona and Hausa.

Overall, the findings underscore the robustness and adaptability of the USM models, showcasing their potential in enhancing ASR performance across diverse languages and datasets.
