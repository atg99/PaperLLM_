The paper investigates the performance of In-Context Learning (ICL) compared to Supervised Learning (SL) on datasets with noisy labels and label imbalances. The evaluation is based on various language models including BinaryGPT2-Large, GPT2-XL, and GPT-J, with results presented in tables showcasing mean performance and standard deviations across different classification types.

Key findings include:

1. **Effect of Noisy Labels**: The performance of different models varies with noisy labels, with ICL generally performing better than SL. ICL's ability to incorporate context helps mitigate the impact of noise and mislabeled data.

2. **Impact of Label Imbalance**: The study reveals that ICL shows resilience to label imbalances, with only minor performance drops as imbalance increases. In contrast, SL is significantly affected, with performance decreasing up to 15% under high imbalance conditions. Interestingly, a moderate level of imbalance can sometimes enhance performance by aiding the model in recognizing patterns among negative examples.

3. **Model Size Influence**: The findings indicate that as model size increases, ICL performance consistently surpasses SL, particularly in scenarios with imbalanced labels.

4. **Sensitivity Analysis**: Sensitivity measures demonstrate that ICL is less sensitive to changes in label imbalance compared to SL, showing a smaller fluctuation in performance across different imbalance ratios.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the advantages of ICL in handling noisy and imbalanced datasets, suggesting its potential for improved generalization in various classification tasks.
