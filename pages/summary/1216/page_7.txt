The paper presents a performance comparison between supervised learning (SL) and in-context learning (ICL) across various class imbalance ratios using multiple datasets and model sizes. The findings indicate that despite varying imbalance ratios, the attention scores assigned to different classes remain relatively consistent, suggesting that ICL maintains a balanced focus on classes even when label distributions are skewed.

In the related work section, the authors discuss the significance of large pre-trained language models (LLMs) in natural language processing. They outline two primary frameworks for utilizing LLMs: supervised fine-tuning, which adapts models for specific tasks, and ICL, which allows for few-shot learning without parameter updates. The review highlights various studies that explore the mechanisms of ICL, including its sensitivity to the selection of examples and the importance of correct input-label mappings.

The paper also addresses the challenges of learning with noisy labels, distinguishing between preprocessing methods that select clean data and robust learning techniques that aim to function effectively despite label noise. The authors emphasize that while imbalanced class distributions can affect performance, their experiments suggest that the impact is less pronounced for ICL compared to SL.

Overall, the research contributes to the understanding of how LLMs handle label imbalance and the effectiveness of ICL in maintaining performance across different scenarios.
