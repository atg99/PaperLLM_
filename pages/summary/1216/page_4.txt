The paper investigates the impact of the ratio of correct labels on the performance of in-context learning (ICL) across three language models: GPT2-Large, GPT2-XL, and GPT-J. The study aims to determine whether the ratio of correct labels influences ICL performance, how ICL compares to supervised learning (SL) in terms of sensitivity to noisy labels, and the effect of label imbalance.

Empirical findings reveal that the ratio of correct labels significantly affects ICL performance, with larger models showing greater sensitivity to label corruption. The results indicate that while all models perform better with gold labels, an optimal point may exist where a certain percentage of corrupted labels can enhance performance. In contrast, supervised learning demonstrates a marked decline in performance as corruption increases, with ICL exhibiting a more gradual decrease.

The analysis also distinguishes between binary and multi-class classification tasks. In binary tasks, ICL and SL perform similarly, while ICL starts to outperform SL in multi-class scenarios as corruption increases. This suggests that ICL leverages contextual information better in complex tasks, where SL may struggle due to its reliance on accurate input-label correspondence.

Overall, the findings suggest that ICL is less sensitive to noisy labels than SL, indicating a potential advantage for ICL in scenarios with imperfect data. The paper highlights the need for further exploration of how different learning paradigms interact with varying degrees of label integrity and task complexity.
