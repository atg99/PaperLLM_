This paper investigates the learning behavior of in-context learning (ICL) in the context of label perturbations, comparing it with traditional supervised learning (SL). The research is structured around three main contributions: understanding ICL through perturbed labels, explaining its behavior using attention scores, and providing a systematic comparison of ICL and SL under varying conditions of label perturbation.

The study focuses on a classification task where the model predicts output labels based on input text. It defines two learning paradigms: supervised learning, which updates model parameters using training losses, and in-context learning, which estimates predictions directly from input and demonstration examples without gradient descent.

The authors explore how both learning paradigms respond to two types of label perturbations: noisy labels and label imbalance. Noisy labels are created by corrupting original labels, while label imbalance simulates skewed data distributions. The findings reveal that ICL is less sensitive to label perturbations compared to SL and that ICL performance improves with larger model sizes, ultimately approaching the performance of SL.

The paper emphasizes the importance of high-quality labels for effective ICL and suggests that while label imbalance has limited impact, corrupted labels significantly affect learning outcomes, particularly in larger models. The implications of these findings contribute to a deeper understanding of ICL and its robustness in real-world applications.
