This paper explores the learning behavior of In-Context Learning (ICL) in the presence of label perturbations, demonstrating that ICL's performance is more influenced by the quality of input-label pairings than by the quantity of pairings for each class. The authors find that ICL is less sensitive to perturbed labels compared to traditional supervised learning (SL), particularly benefiting from larger language models. They argue that practitioners should consider using ICL instead of SL, especially when working with large models, as ICL may offer improved performance even in scenarios of label perturbation.

The study highlights the limitations of current experiments, which are confined to text classification tasks with models ranging from 774 million to 6.7 billion parameters, and acknowledges the computational challenges associated with extensive training. Future work is suggested to extend the findings to other tasks, such as text generation and computer vision, and to optimize the learning behavior of language models in varied contexts.

The paper concludes by emphasizing the significance of understanding ICL's behavior under label perturbations and encourages further analysis and application in diverse environments to enhance future implementations of ICL.
