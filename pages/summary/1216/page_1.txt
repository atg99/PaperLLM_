This paper investigates the learning behavior of in-context learning (ICL) in large language models (LLMs) and compares it with supervised learning (SL). ICL allows models to learn tasks from a few examples without updating their parameters, contrasting with the parameter updates typical in SL. The study focuses on understanding how ICL learns from prompts, particularly under conditions of label perturbations such as noisy labels and label imbalance.

Through extensive experiments, the authors discover that the quality of gold labels significantly affects ICL performance, especially in larger models, while imbalanced labels have minimal impact across all model sizes. Additionally, ICL demonstrates less sensitivity to label perturbations compared to SL, with ICL's performance approaching that of SL as model size increases. 

The research underscores the effectiveness of ICL in scenarios with unclean or imbalanced data, suggesting its potential advantages as a cost-effective learning approach. By examining model performance across six text classification datasets, the study provides insights into the strengths and weaknesses of ICL relative to SL and enhances understanding of ICL's learning dynamics through attention score analysis.
