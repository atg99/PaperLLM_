The paper investigates the performance of In-Context Learning (ICL) compared to Supervised Learning (SL) in the context of label imbalance and noise in datasets. It presents findings from experiments that assess model accuracy across various corrupted rates and imbalance ratios using different model sizes, including GPT2-Large, GPT2-XL, and GPT-J.

The results indicate that while ICL shows less sensitivity to label imbalance compared to SL, its performance does not always surpass that of SL, particularly with GPT2-Large and GPT2-XL. However, ICL demonstrates a clear advantage with larger models like GPT-J when handling class-imbalanced datasets. This suggests that ICL can maintain performance by leveraging context-specific information, mitigating the label bias typically encountered in SL.

The discussion emphasizes that SL tends to focus on frequent classes, leading to biased decision boundaries, whereas ICL can learn richer features that encompass both label-relevant and intrinsic properties of the input distributions. This ability may help ICL generalize better to rare classes, although further research is required to confirm this hypothesis.

Additionally, the paper explores the role of attention mechanisms in understanding ICL's robustness to label perturbations. By analyzing attention scores across different label conditions, it reveals that smaller models struggle to distinguish between correct and corrupted labels, while larger models like GPT-J effectively differentiate them, highlighting their superior interpretative capabilities.

Overall, the findings underscore the potential of ICL in scenarios with imbalanced and noisy data, suggesting it could be a preferable approach when working with large language models.
