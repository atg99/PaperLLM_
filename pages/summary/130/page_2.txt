The paper discusses methods for generating interpretable and explainable models, categorizing them into local and global approaches. Local methods focus on deriving explanations around specific test instances, while global methods aim to understand the overall behavior of a model. The authors also classify these methods based on criteria such as model agnosticism, intrinsic versus post-hoc, and perturbation versus saliency-based techniques.

Notably, post-hoc methods like LIME have gained popularity due to their ability to provide explanations for already trained models without needing to understand their internal workings. However, LIME and similar local post-hoc methods often struggle with consistency, stability, and local fidelity of explanations. The authors propose modifications to LIME to enhance these aspects.

LIME generates artificial data points through random perturbation around the instance of interest, fitting a linear model to explain it. Previous modifications to LIME have included using K-means clustering and hierarchical clustering to create more stable local models by reducing variability in feature weights across runs. However, these approaches may lead to overfitting, as they utilize training data that could misrepresent the behavior of the black box model when explaining test instances.

The paper emphasizes the need for improved stability and fidelity in local interpretability methods, particularly in how explanations are generated and the importance of using appropriate data for training local interpretable models.
