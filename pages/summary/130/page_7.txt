The Breast Cancer dataset consists of 699 observations with 11 features, while the Hepatitis dataset has 155 observations with 20 features, and the Liver dataset includes 583 observations with 11 features. A simple feed-forward neural network with a single hidden layer of 30 neurons was trained on these datasets, achieving accuracies of 0.95, 0.87, and 0.83, respectively, using a 70-30 training-testing split and binary cross-entropy loss.

Results are illustrated in Figure 2, where red bars indicate negative coefficients and green bars indicate positive coefficients from a linear regression model. Positive coefficients reflect a positive correlation between dependent and independent variables, while negative coefficients indicate a negative correlation. 

The paper highlights the absence of a suitable metric for comparing different interpretable models, emphasizing the importance of local fidelity and stability in their evaluation.
