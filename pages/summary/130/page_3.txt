In this paper, we introduce ALIME, an autoencoder-based approach designed to enhance the stability of local interpretable models while maintaining local fidelity. We address two primary questions: first, whether stability can be improved by using a sampling paradigm similar to LIME without relying on the training set; and second, whether it is possible to enhance stability without sacrificing local accuracy.

Our main contributions include the introduction of a novel weighting function that modifies LIME to tackle the challenges of stability and local fidelity, and the execution of comprehensive experiments on three distinct healthcare datasets to evaluate the performance of ALIME in comparison to LIME.

The paper begins with an overview of the LIME methodology, which employs interpretable models to explain individual predictions from complex black box models. LIME creates a new dataset through sampling and perturbation, using the predictions from the black box model as ground truth to train a local surrogate model. The proximity of the sampled instances to the point of interest is weighted to improve interpretability.

In summary, ALIME aims to provide a more robust framework for local interpretability in machine learning by leveraging autoencoders, enhancing both stability and local fidelity in model explanations.
