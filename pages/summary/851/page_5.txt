The paper discusses the architecture and methodology of inference attacks in machine learning models, particularly focusing on membership inference attacks. It highlights how attackers can design specific attacks for different layers of a model, thus allowing them to combine the inference tasks across layers to enhance the attack's effectiveness. The architecture of the attack model includes feature extraction components, which utilize fully connected networks (FCN) and convolutional neural networks (CNN), to process gradients, activation vectors, model outputs, and loss values separately before combining them to predict membership probabilities.

The paper differentiates between two primary training contexts: stand-alone centralized training and federated learning. In stand-alone training, an attacker can analyze a model before and after fine-tuning to assess information leakage about the training datasets. In the federated learning context, multiple participants train a global model using their local datasets, and an attacker can either be the centralized parameter server or one of the participants. The attacker's ability to infer information depends on their position within the federated learning structure, allowing them to observe multiple model versions over time.

Additionally, the paper distinguishes between passive and active inference attacks. Passive attacks involve the adversary observing the models without altering the training process, while active attacks may involve manipulating the learning process to extract more information. Overall, the paper emphasizes the complexity and potential risks associated with inference attacks in both centralized and federated learning environments.
