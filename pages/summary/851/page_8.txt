The paper investigates the vulnerability of machine learning models to membership inference attacks, focusing on the impact of model architectures and training data sizes. It presents datasets used for training and testing classification and inference models, detailing sizes for members and non-members across three datasets: CIFAR100, Texas100, and Purchase100.

In the stand-alone setting, where attackers have full access to a trained model, the study explores how different layers' outputs and gradients can leak membership information. Results indicate that the last layer's output contains the most sensitive information, as it captures complex relationships between features and classes. The study demonstrates that gradients also leak significant information, with later layers showing more pronounced leakage than earlier ones.

The paper further compares various models, such as CIFAR100-ResNet and CIFAR100-DenseNet, revealing that larger models with greater capacity tend to memorize more training data, leading to increased membership leakage. Additionally, the accuracy of membership inference attacks improves with larger training sizes for the attacker.

The research highlights the correlation between the norm of model gradients and membership inference accuracy, showing distinct patterns for member and non-member instances over training epochs. Overall, the findings emphasize the challenges of ensuring privacy in machine learning models, particularly in white-box settings where attackers have full visibility into model behavior.
