The paper presents an analysis of attack accuracy in a federated learning setting with four participants. It differentiates between global and local attackers, where the global attacker aggregates parameters centrally while the local attacker is one of the participants. The study reports average attack accuracy for both types of attackers, showcasing that global attackers generally achieve higher accuracy than local attackers due to their broader access to model parameters. 

Table X illustrates the attack accuracy against various datasets, highlighting that active attackers (who manipulate updates) outperform passive attackers (who merely observe updates). The results indicate that local attackers experience diminished accuracy with an increasing number of participants, as shown in Table XIII.

The paper also details the impact of different training epochs on the accuracy of passive global attackers, with Table XII revealing that accuracy improves significantly with more observed epochs. Furthermore, the Gradient Ascent Attacker is discussed, where the attacker enhances membership inference accuracy through adversarial manipulation of the learning process. Figures demonstrate how the gradient norms of target members and non-members behave differently during model training.

Overall, the findings underline the vulnerabilities in federated learning systems, especially concerning membership inference attacks, and emphasize the importance of understanding both the attack methodologies and their implications for data privacy.
