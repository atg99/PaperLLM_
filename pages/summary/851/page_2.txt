The paper examines the differences in knowledge and observation between black-box and white-box membership inference attacks, ultimately asserting that both types of attacks share the same objective: inferring whether a specific data point was part of a model's training set. The authors extend existing black-box attacks to the white-box setting, but empirical evaluations reveal that this approach does not yield better accuracy than black-box attacks. The findings indicate that early layers of a model generalize more quickly than later layers, which extract more complex features relevant to the training data.

The authors propose a novel white-box membership inference attack that leverages the privacy vulnerabilities associated with the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) algorithm. They argue that each training data point influences model parameters through SGD, creating a recognizable footprint in the gradients of the loss function. The attack model utilizes the gradient vector across all parameters for a target data point to compute membership probabilities, employing different training strategies based on the attacker's background knowledge.

The effectiveness of the white-box attack is demonstrated through evaluations on pre-trained models, such as DenseNet on the CIFAR100 dataset. While black-box attacks show a 54.5% inference accuracy, the white-box attack achieves 74.3%, highlighting significant information leakage even in well-generalized models.

The paper also explores membership inference attacks in a federated learning context, where both curious participants and parameter servers can successfully infer membership with high accuracy. For instance, a participant can achieve 72.2% accuracy, while a central server can achieve 79.2% accuracy by observing individual parameter updates. The authors introduce an active attack strategy that enhances the likelihood of inferring member data points by manipulating SGD, leading to even higher inference accuracies.

The paper categorizes various attack scenarios based on observation types, adversary knowledge, target training algorithms, and attack modes, providing a comprehensive overview of techniques employed in membership inference attacks.
