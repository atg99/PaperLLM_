The paper presents an analysis of attack accuracy using outputs and gradients from various layers of a pre-trained AlexNet model on the CIFAR100 dataset. The findings are summarized in several tables showcasing the attack performance in different scenarios.

Table III indicates that the attack accuracy varies slightly across the output layers, with the last layer achieving an accuracy of 74.6%, which is consistent when combining the last three layers. In Table IV, the attack accuracy improves marginally when using parameter gradients, with the last layer parameters yielding the highest accuracy of 75.1%. Table V further explores combinations of layer gradients and outputs, revealing that using gradients from all layers results in the highest attack test accuracy of 75.18%.

The paper also compares the generalization errors and gradient norm distributions between DenseNet and ResNet, showing that DenseNet exhibits a more distinguishable gradient norm distribution between members and non-members, leading to higher attack accuracy. The analysis highlights that larger differences in gradient norms between member and non-member instances correlate with increased attack accuracy, as illustrated in Figure 2a and 2b.

Additionally, the impact of prediction uncertainty on privacy leakage is explored, confirming that uncertainty plays a significant role in the effectiveness of membership inference attacks. The paper provides empirical evidence of varying attack accuracies based on the size of the attacker's training dataset, as shown in Table VI, where larger datasets improve accuracy.

Finally, the paper compares the proposed unsupervised attack method against the Shadow models approach in a white-box scenario, demonstrating superior performance of the unsupervised method across various architectures, as indicated in Table VII. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of layer-specific outputs and gradients in enhancing membership inference attack accuracy.
