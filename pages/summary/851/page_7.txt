The paper discusses a method for executing membership inference attacks using clustering algorithms, specifically spectral clustering, to classify data points into two clusters based on the output of an encoder model. The attack identifies the cluster with a larger gradient norm as representing non-members. 

The experiments were conducted using PyTorch on a system equipped with multiple Titan X GPUs. Three datasets were utilized: CIFAR100, a benchmark image recognition dataset with 60,000 RGB images divided into 100 classes; Purchase100, containing shopping records of online customers with binary indicators of product purchases; and Texas100, which consists of hospital discharge data records with patient characteristics and medical information.

For the target models, various architectures were employed. For CIFAR100, models such as AlexNet, ResNet, and DenseNet were trained using stochastic gradient descent with specific learning rates and regularization. For Purchase100 and Texas100, fully connected networks were used with different layer configurations, trained using the Adam optimizer.

The paper also highlights the use of pre-trained models for CIFAR100 to demonstrate the broader applicability of the attack beyond custom-trained models. In a federated learning context, the averaging aggregation method was applied, where each training party sends parameter updates to a central server for averaging.

The attack model architecture is outlined, with training conducted using the Adam optimizer and a batch size of 64. The evaluation metrics for the attack's performance include attack accuracy, true positives, and false positives, ensuring a balanced representation of member and non-member instances during training to mitigate bias.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the attack models across different datasets and training scenarios, emphasizing the robustness of the approach in various contexts.
