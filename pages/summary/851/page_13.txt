The paper discusses the effectiveness of membership inference attacks in deep learning, particularly focusing on the impact of active global gradient ascent attacks and isolating attackers in a federated learning scenario. 

Figure 7 illustrates how the gradient norms of target and non-target instances change during training under attack. The active gradient ascent attacker differentiates between target members and non-members, enhancing the accuracy of membership inference attacks compared to passive methods. The local attack shows lower accuracy due to parameter aggregation from multiple participants.

The isolating attacker improves attack accuracy by isolating a target participant's model from others, allowing it to retain more information about its training dataset. This method, when combined with gradient ascent attacks, significantly increases the attack's effectiveness.

The paper also reviews related work on membership inference attacks, highlighting foundational studies that established the vulnerability of machine learning models to such attacks. It cites research that employed shadow models and statistical properties of outputs to infer membership status without direct access to training data. Additionally, it notes advancements in collaborative learning attacks and the use of differential privacy as a defense mechanism against inference attacks.

Overall, the research emphasizes the ongoing challenges and developments in the field of membership inference attacks, particularly in the context of deep learning and federated learning environments.
