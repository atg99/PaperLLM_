The paper presents an analysis of attack accuracy across various datasets and target architectures, specifically evaluating the effectiveness of using layer outputs versus gradients in both black-box and white-box settings. Results show that for the CIFAR100 dataset, the AlexNet architecture achieves a test accuracy of 44%, with a black-box attack accuracy of 74.2% and white-box accuracies of 74.6% (outputs) and 75.1% (gradients). ResNet and DenseNet architectures demonstrate lower attack accuracies, with DenseNet showing a test accuracy of 82% and a white-box gradient attack accuracy of 74.3%. 

Further analysis on fine-tuned models reveals that the highest attack accuracy is maintained at 100% during training for both AlexNet and DenseNet, but test accuracies drop significantly, indicating potential vulnerabilities. The ability to distinguish between datasets is also examined, with varying degrees of success across different architectures. 

The paper includes visual representations of gradient norms and encoded values, illustrating the distinct distributions for member and non-member instances, which underpins the effectiveness of the unsupervised attack method proposed. The study highlights the challenges posed by federated learning scenarios, where averaging model updates can diminish the impact of individual contributions, complicating attacks from passive local attackers who lack access to participant model updates.
