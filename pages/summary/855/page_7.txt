The initial benchmarking algorithm is designed to evaluate the quality of local training data among participating parties without disclosing sensitive information. Each party pre-trains a model and generates artificial samples using a Differentially Private Generative Adversarial Network (DPGAN). These samples are shared with other parties, who use their models to predict labels for the samples. The algorithm serves two main purposes: it helps determine the reliability of each party based on their training data quality and estimates the data distribution overlap between parties.

Local credibility is initialized through a majority voting process, where each party's predicted labels are compared against the collective majority. This process involves calculating a credibility score based on the accuracy of predictions relative to the majority labels. If a party’s credibility falls below a defined threshold, it is reported as a low-contribution party and removed from the credible party set. The algorithm ensures that parties with significantly different data distributions do not share updates, thereby enhancing the collaborative learning process's effectiveness and fairness.

In summary, the initial benchmarking algorithm establishes a framework for assessing and normalizing the credibility of participating parties, ensuring that collaborative learning is built on a foundation of trust and quality data sharing.
