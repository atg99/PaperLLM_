The paper discusses a framework for Privacy-Preserving Collaborative Deep Learning (FPPDL), emphasizing the secure sharing of model gradients among multiple parties while maintaining privacy. The framework consists of two main stages: initial benchmarking and differential privacy implementation using Generative Adversarial Networks (DPGAN).

In the initial benchmarking stage, parties share gradients based on a calculated sharing level, which is determined by the number of artificial samples each party publishes relative to their local training data. This sharing level influences the amount of data a party is willing to share, with the aim of optimizing collaborative learning.

The DPGAN approach is introduced to mitigate privacy risks associated with sharing gradients. By adding noise to the gradients during the training of the GAN, the framework ensures that the privacy of local training data is preserved. The discriminator in the GAN accesses private data, while the generator operates under differential privacy guarantees, following the post-processing property of differential privacy.

To enhance the stability and scalability of DPGAN training, the paper employs multi-fold optimization strategies, including weight clustering and adaptive clipping. The DPGAN can generate an infinite number of samples while ensuring rigorous differential privacy, thereby allowing for effective analysis without compromising data confidentiality. The paper illustrates the effectiveness of DPGAN in generating high-quality artificial samples that closely resemble real data, demonstrating its utility in privacy-preserving collaborative learning settings.
