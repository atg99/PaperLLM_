The study investigates the relationship between party contributions and rewards, with the contributions measured by standalone model accuracies and sharing levels. The analysis reveals that parties with better generalization capabilities and higher sharing levels tend to contribute more to the collaborative model. The collaborative fairness is quantified using the correlation coefficient, where higher values indicate better fairness among the parties involved.

The experiments utilize two benchmark datasets: MNIST for handwritten digit recognition and SVHN for house number classification. The results are compared against three baseline frameworks: a standalone framework, a centralized framework, and a distributed framework using Distributed Selective SGD (DSSGD). The standalone framework maximizes privacy but limits utility, while the centralized framework violates privacy by accessing all data. The distributed framework, particularly DSSGD, offers a balance of accuracy and fairness by allowing independent training while sharing model updates selectively.

The study concludes that the FPPDL framework demonstrates superior performance in terms of collaborative fairness and accuracy when evaluated against the baselines, highlighting its effectiveness in federated learning scenarios.
