The paper investigates the fairness and performance of a federated learning framework, specifically the Federated Private Deep Learning (FPPDL), compared to a distributed framework using Distributed Stochastic Gradient Descent (DSSGD). It explores three realistic independent and identically distributed (IID) settings: 

1. **Same Sharing Level, Same Data Size**: Each party shares 10% of meaningful gradients and uses a small sample of local training data, maintaining consistency with prior work by Shokri et al.
2. **Different Sharing Levels, Same Data Size**: Sharing levels vary among parties, while local data size remains constant.
3. **Different Data Sizes, Same Sharing Level**: Parties have different amounts of training data, but the sharing level is fixed at 10%.

The experimental results demonstrate that FPPDL achieves higher fairness compared to the distributed framework across various settings and architectures, with fairness scores consistently above 0.5. In contrast, the distributed framework shows significantly lower fairness scores, indicating its inability to equitably account for the contributions of different parties. 

The paper also discusses system-level convergence, highlighting that FPPDL maintains comparable accuracy to non-private frameworks while ensuring both fairness and privacy. Although the convergence rate of FPPDL is slightly slower and more variable during early stages, it is attributed to the initial individual training phase. Overall, the findings support the effectiveness of FPPDL in enhancing collaborative fairness in federated learning environments.
