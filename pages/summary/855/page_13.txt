The paper presents findings on the performance of the Federated Private Deep Learning (FPPDL) framework in comparison to standalone models and centralized frameworks, particularly using the MNIST dataset with varying architectures such as Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN). The results indicate that FPPDL, across different settings and numbers of collaborating parties, consistently achieves better accuracy than standalone models. 

The experiments involved varying the number of parties (4, 15, 30, and 50) and assessed the convergence behavior over communication rounds. The FPPDL framework shows improvements in fairness and convergence rates by sharing model updates after each epoch of local training. Key hyperparameters such as local batch size, local training epochs, and learning rate were adjusted to optimize convergence. 

The findings also reveal that while FPPDL may result in slower convergence and fluctuations in accuracy for individual parties, it ultimately leads to superior local models compared to standalone approaches, enhancing fairness among participants. Notably, increasing local computation (by adjusting batch size and training epochs) can accelerate convergence without sacrificing accuracy or fairness.

The paper concludes that FPPDL provides competitive accuracy, nearing that of centralized frameworks, while ensuring privacy through collaborative learning. The results affirm that FPPDL outperforms standalone stochastic gradient descent (SGD) methods, illustrating its effectiveness in achieving high accuracy in a federated learning context.
