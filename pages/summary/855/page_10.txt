The paper discusses a secure method for additively homomorphic encryption utilizing a provably secure one-time pad (OTP) approach. This method employs modular addition instead of the traditional XOR operation, enhancing security against frequency analysis attacks. The security of the encryption relies on two key features: the variability of the keystream across messages and the operations being performed modulo a large integer M. The detailed encryption procedure is outlined in Algorithm 3, which includes setup, encryption, decryption, and aggregation of encrypted data.

In the setup phase, a trusted dealer generates keystreams ensuring that their sum modulo M equals zero. Each party receives a unique keystream for encrypting messages. The encryption process involves representing the message as an integer and computing the ciphertext through modular addition. Decryption is straightforward, allowing parties to aggregate and decrypt received gradients while maintaining the confidentiality of individual data.

To address the challenge of storing encrypted gradients accessible to all parties, the paper introduces a three-layer onion-style encryption scheme. The first layer uses symmetric key encryption for local model gradients, while the second and third layers employ hybrid encryption to protect the first layer's ciphertext and authenticate the receiver's identity.

Additionally, the paper describes a method for updating local credibility among parties during collaborative learning. Each party evaluates the contributions of others based on shared samples, allowing for a dynamic update of credibility reflecting actual contributions.

Finally, the paper emphasizes the importance of quantifying collaborative fairness within the system, indicating that fairness metrics should be assessed from a systemic perspective to ensure equitable participation in the collaborative learning process.
