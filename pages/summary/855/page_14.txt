The paper presents a framework called FPPDL, which is evaluated on the MNIST dataset using different settings and models, including Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN). The results indicate that FPPDL demonstrates effective convergence and high test accuracy across various configurations, achieving accuracy rates close to existing distributed frameworks such as DSSGD. Specifically, the accuracy reached 98.83% with DSSGD and 98.58% in a centralized framework.

Figures illustrate the performance of FPPDL in terms of test accuracy across multiple communication rounds and epochs, comparing results from standalone settings and the proposed framework in both two-party and multi-party scenarios. The analysis highlights that while FPPDL maintains reasonable fairness among parties, there is a slight trade-off in model utility.

The authors also explore how adjustments to the local credibility threshold affect fairness and accuracy, suggesting that the framework is adaptable to varying conditions without significantly compromising performance. Overall, the findings suggest that FPPDL is a promising approach for achieving fairness in distributed learning systems while ensuring competitive accuracy.
