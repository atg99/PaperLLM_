This paper presents FPPDL, a decentralized framework for privacy-preserving deep learning that emphasizes fairness among participants. The design allows local training, enabling each party to verify the claims of others and adjust their credibility based on the received gradients. The study addresses potential malicious behaviors, particularly from free-riders—participants with low contributions who might provide misleading information. The framework employs a reporting mechanism to isolate such attackers early in the collaboration process, ensuring the integrity of the remaining parties.

The paper also discusses strategies to enhance detection and isolation of malicious parties throughout the collaborative learning phases, including mutual evaluations and the use of majority voting on labels. To counteract external threats, it incorporates differential privacy and multi-layer encryption to protect exchanged information.

However, the authors acknowledge limitations in their design's resistance to all forms of malicious interference, particularly from parties that may adaptively alter their behavior to undermine model integrity. Proposed solutions to mitigate these risks include validating local predictions against hold-out sets and terminating the collaboration if accuracy thresholds are not met.

In conclusion, FPPDL offers a promising approach to decentralized learning with privacy and fairness, although the authors recognize the need for further research to address remaining challenges, especially in business applications where legal liabilities exist.
