The paper discusses various incentive schemes for federated learning (FL) participants, focusing on how to fairly distribute rewards based on their contributions. It highlights three main schemes: the Fair-value game, the Shapley game profit-sharing scheme, and a model improvement approach. The Fair-value game calculates rewards based on the sequence of participant departures, while the Shapley game aims to average contributions across all possible permutations of participant involvement, though it is computationally intensive. 

The paper emphasizes gradient-based federated learning, where reward systems based on output agreement are challenging due to the lack of a multi-task setting. It proposes that model improvement is the most relevant incentive design, detailing a scheme that compensates participants for their marginal contributions while correcting for potential overestimations. This scheme encourages early participation by offering higher rewards to those who contribute sooner.

Additionally, the paper introduces the FPPDL framework, which allocates different versions of the FL model to participants based on their contributions, rather than using monetary rewards. This alternative approach aims to enhance fairness among participants.

The preliminaries section covers essential technologies underpinning the proposed framework, including differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, and blockchain. Differential privacy is defined in terms of its privacy budget and guarantees against data recovery. Homomorphic encryption allows computations on encrypted data but can increase communication costs, prompting the development of efficient ciphertext compression techniques. Finally, the paper explains blockchain's role as a decentralized system that enhances transparency and accountability, with a preference for permissioned blockchains in stable participant environments. This integration is positioned as beneficial for federated learning, particularly in privacy-preserving contexts.
