The paper presents a privacy-preserving collaborative deep learning algorithm that enables multiple parties to share model updates without compromising their data privacy. The algorithm operates in several key steps:

1. **Gradient Exchange**: Parties exchange gradients based on their credibility and sharing levels. Each party aims to download a specific number of gradients from others while updating their local model parameters accordingly.

2. **Onion-Style Encryption**: The gradients are masked and subjected to a three-layer encryption process. This includes encrypting the masked gradients with a party's keystream, re-encrypting them with a fresh symmetric key, and further encrypting this key with the receiver's public key.

3. **Parameter Update**: The receiving party decrypts the gradients and updates its local model parameters using homomorphic properties to ensure that only the sums of encrypted gradients are revealed.

4. **Local Credibility Update**: Each party evaluates the credibility of others by sharing artificial samples for labeling, updating their credibility scores based on historical data.

5. **Normalization and Reporting**: If a party's credibility falls below a threshold, it may be reported as a low-contributor. If a majority of parties report a specific party, it may be removed from the credible party set.

6. **Data Augmentation**: To enhance privacy and training effectiveness, data augmentation techniques are applied, allowing parties to expand their training datasets significantly while maintaining privacy.

The algorithm emphasizes maintaining privacy during collaborative learning and ensuring that all parties can contribute meaningfully, with a focus on secure gradient sharing and credibility assessment. The use of homomorphic encryption and careful management of credibility ensures the integrity and privacy of the learning process.
