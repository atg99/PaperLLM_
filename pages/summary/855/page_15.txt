The results presented in the tables indicate the accuracy of various frameworks—Centralized, Standalone, Distributed, and FPPDL—across different architectures (MLP and CNN) and party configurations (P4, P15, P30, P50) after 100 communication rounds. For the MNIST dataset, Centralized frameworks achieved the highest accuracy, followed closely by Distributed methods. Standalone settings performed significantly lower, particularly in settings 1 and 2. FPPDL showed competitive results, particularly in Setting 2 for both architectures.

For the SVHN dataset, Centralized frameworks also led in accuracy, with Distributed methods again performing well but still trailing behind. Standalone methods exhibited lower performance, confirming the necessity for collaboration to enhance model accuracy.

The analysis of communication and computation costs highlighted that while the communication overhead is significant, the framework's design allows for efficient operation, especially in environments with limited parties. The complexity of the encryption scheme is managed to ensure practicality for real-world applications.

In conclusion, the study validates the effectiveness of the proposed frameworks in achieving high accuracy while maintaining privacy and fairness through innovative methods such as DPGAN and three-layer encryption. The findings underscore the importance of collaboration in improving model performance, particularly when leveraging local data and ensuring privacy.
