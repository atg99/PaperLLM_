This paper introduces the Federated Privacy-Preserving Deep Learning (FPPDL) framework, which enables participants in a federated learning system to collaboratively train a final model while ensuring fairness and privacy. FPPDL does not require trust among participants or a third party, as it utilizes blockchain technology to record all operations, including the uploading and downloading of differentially private artificial samples and encrypted model updates. The framework achieves collaborative fairness by evaluating the local credibility of each participant based on their contributions during both initial benchmarking and the collaborative learning process.

To enhance privacy without sacrificing model utility, the authors propose a three-layer onion-style encryption scheme. This approach is positioned as the first to adjust the performance of the federated learning model for each participant based on their contribution, thus achieving collaborative fairness. The experiments conducted demonstrate that FPPDL maintains high fairness and offers comparable accuracy to existing centralized and distributed deep learning frameworks while outperforming standalone models.

The paper operates under an honest-but-curious threat model, where participants may seek to infer sensitive information from others but are assumed to act honestly in their operations. This is justified by the incentives for parties to improve their local models through collaboration. The authors also discuss the effectiveness of their local credibility evaluation mechanism in mitigating risks from insider and outsider attacks.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews existing frameworks and literature related to deep learning, privacy preservation, and fairness in federated learning. Section 5 details the proposed FPPDL framework, while Section 6 evaluates its performance across various settings. Discussions on findings are presented in Section 7, and Section 8 concludes the paper, suggesting future research directions.

In summary, the paper addresses critical issues in federated learning, including privacy and fairness, and positions FPPDL as a novel solution that enhances collaborative deep learning while protecting sensitive information.
