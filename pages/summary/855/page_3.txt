The paper discusses various deep learning frameworks, specifically focusing on centralized, distributed, and decentralized approaches, as illustrated in different models. It evaluates the advantages and disadvantages of these frameworks, particularly highlighting issues related to privacy and computational costs.

Centralized frameworks, while allowing a global model, pose significant privacy risks as they require sharing local model updates with a central parameter server, which can infer sensitive information. This vulnerability is exacerbated in scenarios where malicious parties can disrupt the learning process. The paper contrasts this with decentralized frameworks, which aim to enhance privacy and fairness among participants by allowing them to share updates without a central mediator.

The authors also address existing privacy-preserving techniques such as CryptoNets and SecureML. Although these methods facilitate secure model training, they often incur high computational and communication overheads. The paper emphasizes the limitations of federated learning (FL), particularly when relying on a trusted server, which can compromise user privacy.

To overcome these challenges, the proposed Federated Privacy-Preserving Deep Learning (FPPDL) framework allows participants to integrate updates based on local credibility and sharing levels, thereby promoting collaborative fairness. The paper discusses various incentive schemes for ensuring fairness among federated learning participants, such as egalitarian and individual profit-sharing strategies.

In conclusion, the research highlights the need for frameworks that balance privacy, efficiency, and fairness in collaborative deep learning environments.
