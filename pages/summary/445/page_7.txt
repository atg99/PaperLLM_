The paper presents an evaluation of the UIU-Net architecture for infrared small object detection, focusing on the impact of different backbone networks and components. The study employs two datasets, the SIRST and a synthetic dataset, to assess performance through metrics such as Intersection over Union (IoU), normalized Intersection over Union (nIoU), and the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve.

In the comparative analysis of backbone networks, the UIU-Net with a multi-scale Residual U-block (RSU) outperformed the traditional ResNet. Specifically, the IoU increased from 0.6178 to 0.7825 on the SIRST dataset and from 0.4335 to 0.4773 on the synthetic dataset. The nIoU also showed significant improvements, indicating the effectiveness of the RSU in enhancing feature representation for small object detection.

The ablation study highlighted the contributions of the incremental modules in UIU-Net. The addition of RSU resulted in an IoU increase from 0.7330 to 0.7825 on the SIRST dataset and from 0.4100 to 0.4773 on the synthetic dataset. This shows the importance of integrating interactive-cross encoders with high-resolution and multi-scale features for optimal performance.

The paper further compares the UIU-Net's detection accuracy with nine other infrared detection methods, showcasing its competitive edge in terms of both IoU and nIoU metrics. Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of the UIU-Net architecture in infrared small object detection tasks and the necessity of utilizing effective backbone networks and modules for improved model performance.
