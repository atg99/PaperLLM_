This paper presents a comparative analysis of various methods for detecting small infrared objects in the SIRST dataset, focusing on both quantitative and visual performance metrics. A key finding is that data-driven networks significantly outperform model-driven methods in terms of Intersection over Union (IoU) and normalized IoU (nIoU) values. Among the methods evaluated, UIU-Net achieves the highest IoU and nIoU metrics, attributed to its ability to learn deep multi-scale and high-resolution features, enhancing detection accuracy and reducing missed detections.

The analysis highlights that while ACM and TBC-Net utilize classification backbones trained on the ImageNet dataset, this approach limits their performance on the SIRST dataset due to a mismatch in data distribution. The MDvsFA-cGAN model, while effective in balancing missed detection and false alarm rates, struggles with the small data size of the SIRST dataset, resulting in lower performance metrics compared to other data-driven approaches.

Visual comparisons further illustrate the limitations of model-driven methods, which often exhibit high missed detection rates and poor pixel value representation of detected objects. In contrast, data-driven methods demonstrate improved feature representation capabilities, leading to more accurate detections as depicted in the visual examples provided.

Overall, the findings indicate that leveraging data-driven methodologies is crucial for enhancing the detection of small infrared objects, particularly in datasets with limited data availability.
