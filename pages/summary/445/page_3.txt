The paper presents a novel architecture called U-Net in U-Net (UIU-Net) specifically designed for infrared small object detection. This architecture addresses the challenges of detecting small objects in images with limited data by leveraging two main modules: the resolution-maintenance deep supervision (RM-DS) module and the interactive-cross attention (IC-A) module. 

The RM-DS module utilizes ReSidual U-blocks (RSU) to enhance global context representations by generating deep multi-scale and high-resolution features, effectively addressing the trade-off between feature resolution and network depth. This module allows the network to learn from scratch while improving contextual representations at both global and local levels.

To enhance local context representations, the IC-A module captures long-range dependencies by interactively cross-coding low-level details and high-level semantic features, replacing the traditional skip layer typically found in U-Net architectures. The final output of UIU-Net is a fusion of multi-layer outputs from the RM-DS network.

The contributions of this work include modeling infrared small object detection as a semantic segmentation problem, proposing the UIU-Net architecture with RM-DS and IC-A modules, and demonstrating significant performance improvements over state-of-the-art methods on two infrared single-frame image datasets and an ATR ground/air video sequence dataset.

The paper is structured as follows: Section II reviews relevant object segmentation and attention mechanisms, Section III describes the UIU-Net architecture in detail, Section IV analyzes experimental results, and Section V discusses future directions.
