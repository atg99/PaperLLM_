The paper presents UIU-Net, a novel framework for infrared small object detection that addresses the limitations of existing learning-based methods reliant on deep classification networks. These traditional approaches often suffer from tiny object loss and reduced feature distinguishability as network depth increases. UIU-Net, which stands for "U-Net in U-Net," integrates a smaller U-Net within a larger U-Net architecture to facilitate multi-level and multi-scale feature representation learning.

The UIU-Net framework comprises two key modules: the resolution-maintenance deep supervision (RM-DS) module and the interactive-cross attention (IC-A) module. The RM-DS module incorporates Residual U-blocks to maintain resolution while learning global contextual information, enhancing feature representation. In contrast, the IC-A module focuses on encoding local context between low-level details and high-level semantic features.

The effectiveness of UIU-Net is validated through extensive experiments on two infrared single-frame image datasets, SIRST and Synthetic, demonstrating its superiority over several state-of-the-art detection methods. Additionally, UIU-Net exhibits strong generalization performance on video sequence datasets, such as the ATR ground/air video sequence dataset. The authors provide open access to the code for further research and application.

Overall, the proposed UIU-Net framework significantly improves the detection of small objects in infrared images, addressing the challenges posed by complex backgrounds and varying object contrast.
