The paper discusses various machine learning methods employed for modeling, focusing on Random Forest, Extreme Gradient Boosting, and Deep Learning for PM 2.5 prediction.

In the Random Forest (RF) section, the authors describe RF as a flexible and fast supervised ensemble learning method based on decision trees. They highlight the importance of hyperparameter tuning for optimal model performance, achieved through a grid search and 10-fold cross-validation based on the R2 metric. The optimized hyperparameters identified include 130 for n_estimators, SQRT for max_features, 2 for min_samples_split, and False for bootstrap.

The Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) section emphasizes its advantage over previous algorithms in controlling overfitting through regularization. Similar to RF, XGBoost requires hyperparameter tuning, which was also optimized using grid search and 10-fold cross-validation. The best hyperparameters found were 200 for n_estimators, 8 for max_depth, 0.7 for gamma, and 8 for min_child_weight.

In the Deep Learning section, the authors note the evolution of artificial neural networks (ANNs) into deep neural networks due to advancements in hardware and algorithms. Despite deep learning's success in various applications, challenges such as high missing values hindered the use of more complex models like CNN and LSTM in this study. Instead, the authors utilized a six-layer deep neural network with an Adam optimizer, incorporating L2 and L1 regularization to mitigate overfitting. The structure of this deep neural network is detailed in the paper.

Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of these machine learning techniques in environmental modeling, specifically for predicting PM 2.5 levels, while addressing the challenges and methodologies associated with each approach.
