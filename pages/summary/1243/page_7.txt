The paper presents a deep learning model designed to predict PM2.5 concentration using a six-layer neural network configuration. The model employs a ReLU activation function and incorporates regularization techniques to mitigate overfitting. The architecture consists of an input layer with 270 neurons, four hidden layers with decreasing neuron counts (120, 70, 50, and 20), and a single output layer. 

Feature importance assessment is a key component of the study, utilizing Spearman correlation analysis to understand the relationships among features. Three methods for estimating feature importance are applied: built-in functions from random forest and XGBoost regression, a permutation method evaluating model performance by permuting features, and recursive feature elimination using XGBoost, which identifies and removes less significant features iteratively.

The study also discusses the data quality issues encountered, noting a significant missing value rate for PM2.5 (approximately 54.11%) due to operational failures and maintenance issues. Other variables, such as AOD03 and AOD10, exhibited even higher missing value rates, highlighting challenges in data collection for accurate modeling. Descriptive statistics for the meteorological data and APM sites over the four-year study period are detailed in supplementary materials. Overall, the research emphasizes the importance of feature selection and data integrity in enhancing predictive performance for PM2.5 concentration.
