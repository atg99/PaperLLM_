The references listed provide a comprehensive overview of studies related to air pollution, particularly focusing on particulate matter (PM2.5) and its health impacts in urban areas, especially Tehran, Iran. Key themes include the use of satellite imagery for estimating air pollution levels, health impact assessments related to air quality, and predictive modeling techniques for forecasting PM2.5 concentrations. 

Studies such as Evans et al. (2013) estimate global mortality due to particulate pollution, while Rojas-Rueda et al. (2013) assess health impacts of increased public transport and cycling. Research by Taghvaee et al. (2018) focuses on lung cancer risk from PM2.5-bound polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in Tehran, highlighting the local health concerns associated with air quality. 

Various methodologies are employed in these studies, including machine learning approaches like Random Forest and hybrid models combining graph convolutional neural networks with long short-term memory for spatiotemporal forecasting (Qi et al., 2019; Wen et al., 2019). The importance of understanding seasonal trends and chemical speciation of fine particulate matter is emphasized in studies by Arhami et al. (2017, 2018). 

Additionally, the relationship between air pollution and respiratory health outcomes is explored through generalized additive models (Dehghan et al., 2018). The long-term trends of PM2.5 and O3 in Tehran are documented by Faridi et al. (2018), providing insights into the evolving air quality challenges in urban settings. 

Overall, these studies contribute to a better understanding of air pollution dynamics, health implications, and the effectiveness of different predictive modeling approaches, underscoring the critical need for effective air quality management strategies in urban environments.
