The paper presents a comparative analysis of feature importance in predicting PM2.5 concentrations using various machine learning techniques, including Random Forest (RF), Deep Learning (DNN), and XGBoost. A ranking of features based on their importance is provided, with PM2.5_lag1 consistently ranked highest across all modeling approaches, indicating its strong predictive capability. 

Key features such as visibility, wind speed, and meteorological parameters (temperature, relative humidity, air pressure) show varying degrees of correlation with PM2.5, highlighting both positive and negative relationships. The Spearman correlation heat map illustrates that historical PM2.5 values have the highest correlation with current PM2.5 levels, while other features exhibit dependencies that can simplify modeling by allowing for substitutions among correlated features.

The study finds that XGBoost outperforms the other techniques in terms of prediction accuracy and training time. Different methods for assessing feature importance, such as permutation and recursive feature removal, reveal logical rankings that can guide future research. The analysis emphasizes the potential for reducing model complexity by eliminating dependent features and leveraging seasonal trends in data, particularly through the inclusion of the "day of year" feature. Overall, the findings contribute valuable insights for improving PM2.5 prediction models.
