The paper examines the prediction of PM 2.5 concentrations in Tehran using machine learning methods, specifically random forest, XGBoost, and deep learning. Given the serious health impacts associated with high PM 2.5 levels, understanding and predicting these concentrations is crucial for public health. Researchers utilized 23 features, including satellite, meteorological, ground-measured PM 2.5, and geographical data to build their models.

The study found that the XGBoost model provided the best performance with an R² value of 0.81, MAE of 9.93 µg/m³, and RMSE of 13.58 µg/m³ after eliminating less important features. Overall, the performance of all three machine learning approaches was similar, with R² ranging from 0.63 to 0.67 when including Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) and improving to 0.77 to 0.81 when excluding it. Interestingly, satellite-derived AOD did not enhance the model's performance compared to the lag data of PM 2.5.

The authors highlight that the high annual PM 2.5 concentration in Tehran significantly exceeds WHO guidelines, primarily due to emissions from vehicles and industrial activities. The findings underscore the importance of feature selection in enhancing predictive performance for air quality management and public health interventions.
