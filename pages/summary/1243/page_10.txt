The paper discusses the prediction of PM 2.5 concentrations using various machine learning methods, specifically XGBoost, Random Forest (RF), and deep learning approaches. The scatter plots comparing predicted versus observed PM 2.5 values indicate a reasonable prediction capability, particularly when excluding the AOD03 variable. The analysis shows that XGBoost achieves the highest performance metrics, with an R2 value of 0.80, a mean absolute error (MAE) of 10.00 μg/m3, and a root mean square error (RMSE) of 13.62 μg/m3 when excluding AOD03. This suggests that AOD03 is not a significant predictor for PM 2.5 concentrations.

In contrast, deep learning, despite demonstrating acceptable performance, yielded a lower R2 value of 0.77 compared to RF and XGBoost. The study highlights that while all methods show similar performance, the sample size significantly impacts the results. The findings suggest that other features can effectively replace AODs in PM 2.5 prediction, thus reducing their importance. Overall, the XGBoost method is recommended due to its superior performance and efficiency, taking only 19 seconds for processing.
