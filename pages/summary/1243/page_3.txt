The paper discusses the environmental conditions and air pollution in Tehran, Iran, highlighting its geographical features, population growth, and sources of air pollution. Tehran's population was recorded at 8.69 million in 2016, with projections estimating it to reach approximately 9 million in 2019. The city, situated on the southern slope of the Alborz Mountain, experiences significant air pollution primarily from transportation, industry, and dust storms, which affects a high number of residents due to population density.

The study utilized ground-measured PM2.5 concentrations, satellite-derived Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) data, and meteorological information from 2015 to 2018. Data sources included local air pollution monitoring stations and the IRAN Meteorological Organization. The paper outlines the parameters collected, such as temperature, humidity, rainfall, and visibility, and emphasizes the importance of AOD data from the MODIS satellite for understanding air quality.

In total, 42 air pollution monitoring stations were established to record various air pollutants, including PM2.5, with data accessible through municipal platforms. The research aims to analyze the impact of these pollutants on the urban environment, using comprehensive data to support its findings.
