In this paper, feature permutation was employed to assess feature importance, complemented by recursive feature removal and model training utilizing XGBoost, with mean absolute error serving as a metric for feature elimination during modeling. 

Data preprocessing was essential due to the heterogeneous sources of data. Daily climatic data were sourced from the IMO portal, where interpolation was applied to address missing values. PM 2.5 data were obtained from the Tehran Municipality ICT website and the Air Pollution Monitoring System of the Iran Environment Department, including geographic coordinates for each monitoring station. Time formats were standardized to ensure compatibility across datasets. Missing values, often resulting from equipment failures or power outages, posed challenges, particularly for Tehran's air pollution monitoring sites.

MODIS AOD products at two spatial resolutions (10 km and 3 km) were downloaded from NASA's LAADS portal, and specific subdatasets were utilized for analysis. AOD values were sampled across 37 APM sites over four years, merging these with PM 2.5 data and climatic variables based on sampling dates. Additional variables like day of year, season, and lagged PM 2.5 and rainfall data were incorporated, along with Boolean indicators for the PM 2.5 monitoring organization and distances from a reference weather station. Descriptive statistics for the meteorological parameters, PM 2.5, and AOD values were computed to evaluate the datasets.

Normalization of data was conducted to enhance the performance of machine learning models, standardizing features to a range of -1 to 1. Standardization involved subtracting the mean and scaling the data based on variance. The dataset was split into training (70%) and testing (30%) sets. Due to a high percentage of missing values in AOD03, two training iterations were performed—once including AOD03 and once excluding it—ensuring that records were purged of missing values based on the features utilized for modeling.
