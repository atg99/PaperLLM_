The study highlights the challenges associated with using MODIS AOD data due to high missing values, particularly in AOD03, which had approximately 94% missing data. Although AOD10 showed a lower missing value rate of around 63.13%, its spatial resolution of 10 km was deemed insufficient for precise modeling. The analysis utilized data from 42 air pollution monitoring (APM) stations and incorporated machine learning techniques—Random Forest (RF), XGBoost, and deep learning—to predict PM 2.5 concentrations.

Model performance was validated using R2, MAE, and RMSE metrics. The RF model demonstrated the best performance, particularly when excluding AOD03, achieving R2 of 0.78, MAE of 10.8 µg/m³, and RMSE of 14.54 µg/m³. The XGBoost model provided comparable results, while deep learning yielded slightly lower R2 values (0.77) but maintained reasonable predictive capability. Overall, excluding AOD03 from the models improved prediction accuracy across all methods.
