This paper discusses the estimation of aerosol optical depth (AOD) over urban areas, specifically focusing on Tehran, Iran. It highlights improvements in the MODIS atmosphere algorithm, particularly for areas with complex topography like Iran, although it notes persistent issues with missing values and bias in AOD measurements. 

Tehran's population, estimated at around 9 million in 2019, contributes to significant air pollution exposure due to high density, despite it not being the most polluted city in Iran. The pollution primarily arises from transportation, industry, and dust storms. The city has 42 air pollution monitoring (APM) stations operated by the Department of Environment and the Municipality of Tehran, with the nearest weather station located approximately 10 km away.

The study employs ground-measured PM2.5 concentrations, satellite-derived AOD data at both 3 km and 10 km resolutions, and various meteorological data from 2015 to 2018. The AOD data was obtained from NASA's Atmosphere Archive. The climatic data includes temperature, humidity, rainfall, and wind speed, sourced from the Iran Meteorological Organization.

The methodology involves sampling, data preprocessing, aggregation, and modeling to predict PM2.5 concentrations at selected sites. The study aims to identify the most effective model for prediction, utilizing climatic, satellite, and auxiliary data, while also analyzing significant features within the context of the study area.
