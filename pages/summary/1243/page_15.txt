In this study, machine learning techniques, including Random Forest, XGBoost, and Deep Learning, were employed to predict PM2.5 concentrations in Tehran's urban area. The research utilized ground-measured PM2.5 data alongside meteorological and remote sensing data (AOD). The study highlighted the challenges posed by the high PM2.5 levels and missing AOD values in the area, as well as the sparse distribution of weather stations. 

The analysis revealed strong correlations among variables such as relative humidity and air pressure with temperature, suggesting that these can be used interchangeably for prediction. The Spearman correlation method and feature importance evaluations indicated that a selection of eight to twelve features could yield satisfactory prediction performance. XGBoost emerged as the most effective model, achieving an R² value of approximately 0.81 and low training time, outperforming the other methods.

The study concluded that the use of specific features, including historical PM2.5 values and meteorological data, is crucial for enhancing prediction accuracy. Despite the various machine learning approaches yielding similar performance, XGBoost's efficiency and simplicity make it a preferred choice for PM2.5 concentration prediction in this context. Overall, the findings provide valuable insights for future research and modeling efforts in air quality prediction.
