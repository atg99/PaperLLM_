The paper investigates the prediction of PM 2.5 concentrations in Tehran, emphasizing the significant impact of air pollution on public health, with a notable figure of 34,000 related deaths. It identifies motor vehicles as a primary source of air pollution, especially during winter. The study highlights advancements in predicting PM 2.5 concentrations using Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) derived from remote sensing technologies, alongside machine learning techniques and climatic variables.

Several methodologies have been employed for PM 2.5 prediction, including regression models and deep learning methods, with varying degrees of success. For instance, previous studies have utilized the Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) AOD products combined with meteorological data, achieving performance metrics such as R2 values of 0.88. However, the overall understanding of the features influencing PM 2.5 prediction remains limited, with few studies addressing feature importance.

The research focuses on Tehran's air pollution monitoring sites, noting a significant challenge due to missing data, particularly in AOD measurements. The study employs machine learning techniques, including Random Forest (RF), eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), and Deep Neural Networks (DNN), to predict PM 2.5 concentrations. A total of 23 features are considered, including various AOD measurements and meteorological data. The effectiveness of these predictions is evaluated using R2, root mean square error (RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE), allowing for an assessment of feature importance in PM 2.5 prediction.

The study area encompasses Tehran, characterized by a cold semi-arid climate, and spans from January 2015 to the end of 2018. The findings aim to enhance understanding of PM 2.5 concentration dynamics and improve forecasting methods, which are crucial for effective air quality management.
