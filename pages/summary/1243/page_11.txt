The paper discusses the assessment of feature importance in modeling using Random Forest (RF) and XGBoost algorithms. It highlights that some features may not contribute effectively to the model and can complicate it unnecessarily. The authors present feature importance rankings derived from RF and XGBoost, indicating that PM2.5_lag1 and visibility are among the most significant features. Notably, the rankings differ substantially between the two methods, with AOD10 being ranked much lower in XGBoost compared to RF. Previous studies have criticized RF's built-in feature importance ranking as biased and unreliable, prompting the authors to employ feature permutation as an alternative method.

For the feature permutation assessment, the authors utilized a Deep Neural Network (DNN) model, training it 23 times with varying combinations of features based on their importance rankings. They measured model performance using the R2 value, achieving optimal results with the top 15 features, yielding an R2 of 0.776. This approach underscores the importance of identifying and retaining relevant features for enhancing model accuracy.
