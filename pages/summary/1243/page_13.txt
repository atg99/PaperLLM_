The study employs the XGBoost machine learning method to analyze feature importance in predicting model performance, specifically focusing on Mean Absolute Error (MAE) metrics. The findings indicate that removing certain features, such as relative humidity (RH), significantly improved model performance, maintaining an MAE below 10 µg/m3. The removal of additional features, including longitude, temperature, and various meteorological variables, did not adversely affect the model's performance, with the best result recorded as R2 = 0.81.

A discussion of feature importance reveals that while different evaluation methods yielded slightly varied results, historical observations of PM2.5, wind speed, visibility, day of the year, altitude, and temperature consistently emerged as critical for modeling. The importance rankings of features, as presented in Table 7, suggest that latitude is vital for Random Forest (RF) and XGBoost models but ranks lower in deep learning approaches due to feature dependencies.

The study categorizes the features into three groups: those directly providing spatial information (latitude, longitude, altitude), those that indirectly reflect spatial characteristics of APM stations (AOD10, AOD03, PM2.5_lag1, PM2.5_lag2), and shared parameters across all stations (meteorological data, day of year, day of week, and sampling season). This classification underlines the complexity and interdependence of features in atmospheric modeling.
