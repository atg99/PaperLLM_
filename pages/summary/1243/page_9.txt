The study investigates the prediction of PM 2.5 concentrations using AOD derived satellite data and ground-measured climatic data, utilizing three machine learning methods: Random Forest (RF), XGBoost, and deep learning. The analysis involved 37 APM stations, where the performance of models was validated against observed PM 2.5 concentrations. 

The spatial resolution of the product (10 km) was identified as a limitation, as multiple APM stations shared the same AOD value. A significant challenge was the high missing value rate (94%) in AOD03, leading to various tests including the exclusion of AOD03 and both AOD values. The models were trained on datasets comprising non-missing records, with performance metrics including R2, MAE, and RMSE employed for evaluation.

For the RF model, optimal configurations yielded an R2 of 0.78, MAE of 10.8 μg/m3, and RMSE of 14.54 μg/m3, with scatter plots indicating reasonable prediction accuracy. XGBoost also demonstrated similar performance, while deep learning, despite a lower R2 of 0.77, maintained acceptable prediction performance. Overall, excluding AOD03 improved model performance, and the results suggest that the models effectively forecast PM 2.5 concentrations despite challenges posed by data quality and spatial resolution.
