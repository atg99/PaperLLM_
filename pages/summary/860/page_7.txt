The paper discusses various methods for optimizing the performance and efficiency of self-attention networks compared to convolutional networks, focusing on dimensionality reduction, transformation functions, footprint size, position encoding, and robustness.

The authors establish that using two linear layers is optimal for reducing memory and computational costs. They present a comparison of different model configurations, showing that various mapping functions yield differing accuracy and resource requirements. The results indicate that employing three distinct learnable transformation functions (ϕ, ψ, and β) generally provides superior performance, allowing for different bottleneck dimension reductions.

An assessment of footprint size reveals that larger sizes initially improve accuracy but eventually saturate, with the optimal default set at 7×7 for all models. The importance of position encoding is highlighted, with findings showing a significant drop in accuracy without it, particularly favoring relative position encoding over absolute.

The robustness of self-attention networks is tested through zero-shot generalization on rotated images. Results indicate that pairwise self-attention models exhibit greater resilience to image manipulations compared to convolutional networks, with pairwise SAN models showing less performance degradation.

Overall, the study underscores the advantages of self-attention mechanisms in terms of efficiency and robustness, suggesting that they can outperform traditional convolutional approaches under certain conditions.
