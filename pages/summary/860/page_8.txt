The paper investigates the effectiveness of image recognition models based entirely on self-attention mechanisms, specifically comparing pairwise and patchwise self-attention to traditional convolutional networks. The results highlight that self-attention models demonstrate superior robustness to image transformations such as rotations and adversarial attacks.

In the experiments, various models were tested for their accuracy under different conditions, including rotations and adversarial perturbations. The findings reveal that self-attention models, particularly the patchwise variant, outperform convolutional networks across various metrics. For instance, in tests involving rotations, self-attention models showed less vulnerability compared to convolutional approaches, with notable performance retention under transformations.

Additionally, self-attention models exhibited greater resilience to adversarial attacks. The paper presents data indicating that while ResNet50's accuracy drops significantly under attack conditions, self-attention models maintain higher accuracy levels, suggesting a robust performance even under adversarial circumstances.

The study concludes that self-attention networks can achieve comparable or superior discriminative power to convolutional networks, challenging the notion that deep learning's success in computer vision is solely dependent on convolutions. The research advocates for further exploration of self-attention mechanisms, especially given their potential advantages in robustness and generalization in image recognition tasks. Overall, the paper emphasizes the promise of self-attention models as a viable alternative to traditional convolutional architectures in computer vision.
