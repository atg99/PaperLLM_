The paper presents a comprehensive comparison of self-attention networks (SANs) and convolutional residual networks on ImageNet classification. Specifically, it evaluates various configurations of SANs, including pairwise and patchwise self-attention mechanisms, against standard ResNet architectures (ResNet26, ResNet38, ResNet50).

Table 3 showcases the performance metrics of these networks, indicating that SANs generally achieve superior accuracy with fewer parameters and lower floating-point operations per second (FLOPs) compared to their convolutional counterparts. For instance, the patchwise SAN10 outperforms ResNet38 while maintaining a significantly lower parameter count and FLOP budget. Similarly, patchwise SAN15 surpasses both ResNet38 and ResNet50 in top-1 accuracy, demonstrating the efficiency of SANs.

The paper also includes controlled experiments to evaluate various relation functions used in self-attention. Results in Table 4 indicate that pairwise self-attention using summation, subtraction, or Hadamard product achieves comparable accuracy, outperforming concatenation and dot product methods. For patchwise self-attention, concatenation yields slightly higher accuracy than other tested functions.

Furthermore, the authors discuss the challenges faced when attempting to reproduce results from related works, emphasizing the importance of open-source implementations for future comparisons. An ablation study on the attention mapping function reveals that using two linear layers in pairwise models enhances accuracy, while patchwise models show consistent performance across different configurations.

Overall, the findings suggest that vector self-attention is a valuable component in constructing self-attention networks for computer vision tasks, particularly highlighting the potential of patchwise self-attention for further exploration. The authors commit to releasing their implementation to support ongoing research in this domain.
