In our experiments, we found that pairwise self-attention networks either matched or exceeded the performance of traditional convolutional networks. More notably, patchwise self-attention models significantly outperformed convolutional baselines, demonstrating their potential as a robust alternative for image recognition tasks.

Furthermore, we examined the robustness of representations learned by self-attention networks. The results indicate that these networks exhibit advantages in robustness and generalization, which are crucial for real-world applications where data can be noisy or varied.

In conclusion, our exploration of self-attention in image recognition reveals that it can serve as a powerful alternative to convolutional networks, with variations such as pairwise and patchwise self-attention offering distinct advantages in performance and adaptability. This work opens up new avenues for designing image recognition models that leverage the strengths of self-attention mechanisms.
