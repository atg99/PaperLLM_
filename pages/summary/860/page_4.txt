The paper presents Self-Attention Networks (SAN) designed for image recognition, detailing their architecture and operations. The networks, labeled SAN10, SAN15, and SAN19, correspond to different numbers of self-attention blocks, roughly aligning with ResNet26, ResNet38, and ResNet50.

The architecture consists of multiple layers, starting from an input layer of size 224x224x3 and progressing through various transitions and self-attention blocks. Each transition layer reduces the spatial resolution while increasing the channel dimensionality, ultimately leading to a classification layer that employs global average pooling followed by a linear layer and softmax activation.

The self-attention block plays a crucial role in this architecture, where it utilizes two processing streams. The left stream calculates attention weights, while the right stream applies a linear transformation to the input features. The outputs from both streams are combined using a Hadamard product, normalized, and then passed through a final linear layer to restore the original dimensionality.

Overall, the SAN architectures leverage self-attention mechanisms to enhance feature aggregation and transformation, providing a robust framework for image recognition tasks.
