The paper discusses the dynamics of human-robot teams, focusing on the composition of agents—specifically, the differences between homogeneous and heterogeneous teams. Homogeneous teams consist of similar robots, which may simplify interaction interfaces but can increase the workload and decrease situational awareness for human operators. In contrast, heterogeneous teams comprise robots with varying capabilities, allowing for a broader range of tasks but requiring more complex control strategies. 

The literature suggests that the nature of interactions within these teams is significantly influenced by their composition. For example, managing a homogeneous robot team demands more interaction effort from the human operator compared to a heterogeneous team. The paper also highlights that human teams can exhibit heterogeneity based on roles, which can affect system dynamics. Different roles such as Supervisor, Operator, and Peer can be assigned to both robots and human teammates, impacting collaboration efficiency in various applications, including industrial and social robotics.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding team composition in optimizing human-robot interactions and operational effectiveness in diverse settings.
