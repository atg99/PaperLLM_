The paper elaborates on multi-agent HRI systems, which encompass scenarios involving multiple humans and/or robots. It highlights a specific example where a robot assists two humans in a tower construction task, illustrated through an interaction graph that denotes the flow of information between humans and the robot.

The literature on HRI is extensive, with various studies reviewing collaboration in HRI, taxonomies, command complexity in remote robot supervision, and computational frameworks. Notable works explore Human-Swarm Interaction and the role of robots in group dynamics. The authors aim to extend the current literature by incorporating systems with multiple robots interacting with either single or multiple humans, emphasizing both social and non-social contexts. They also include theoretical research that, while not involving physical agents, addresses critical aspects of HRI such as collaboration scheduling and optimization of tasks.

Overall, the research emphasizes the wide-ranging implications of HRI, involving co-located and remote interactions, and the development of algorithms to enhance these interactions. This paper seeks to broaden the understanding of multi-agent HRI systems, facilitating insights into real-world applications and theoretical advancements in the field.
