The paper discusses interaction models in human-robot systems, specifically focusing on one-to-one and one-to-many interactions. In a one-to-one model, a single robot interacts with one human at a time, which is typical in dyadic systems where coordination among multiple agents is not necessary. This model is often applied in scenarios where a human operator gives independent commands to multiple robots operating in separate environments. The operator can monitor the robots and provide assistance when needed.

The one-to-one model can also be used in human-swarm interactions, where a human directly controls leader robots, influencing the behavior of the rest of the swarm. It is crucial to distinguish whether an interaction targets one or multiple agents to identify the interaction model accurately.

Conversely, the one-to-many interaction model involves one robot interacting with several humans simultaneously or one human engaging with multiple robots at once. This model highlights the flexibility of communication channels in multi-agent systems, accommodating various configurations based on the agents involved. The paper illustrates these concepts through examples, emphasizing the importance of understanding different interaction models to enhance the effectiveness of human-robot collaborations.
