This paper surveys the field of Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), focusing specifically on multi-agent systems that involve multiple humans and/or robots. The authors identify three core aspects that distinguish multi-agent HRI systems from dyadic systems: Team structure, Interaction style among agents, and Computational characteristics. They highlight five key attributes that characterize these systems: Team size, Team composition, Interaction model, Communication modalities, and Robot control. 

The paper provides a comprehensive review of existing literature, categorizing various systems and discussing their applications. The authors emphasize the differences between multi-agent and dyadic systems, noting that multi-agent systems require more complex coordination, advanced interaction schemes, and a deeper understanding of human behavior to facilitate seamless interactions. 

They argue that as robots become more integrated into society, research should focus on these multi-agent systems to address the intricacies of coordination and communication among diverse agents. The paper concludes by identifying challenges and promising research directions, particularly in enhancing robot control policies and understanding human behavior in mixed teams, to better reflect real-world scenarios.
