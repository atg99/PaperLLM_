The cited papers collectively explore the dynamics of human-robot interaction (HRI), focusing on various aspects such as multi-robot assistance, social implications of robots, and the design of interfaces for effective communication. Dahiya et al. discuss scalable operator allocation in multi-robot systems using a restless bandit approach, while Dautenhahn highlights the roles of robots in society through the lens of autism therapy. Driewer et al. evaluate teleoperation interfaces for heterogeneous teams, and Druckman et al. compare the conflict mediation capabilities of robots, screens, and humans.

Erel et al. investigate the potential for ostracism in robot-robot-human interactions, while Faria et al. emphasize the importance of legibility in multi-party interactions with robots. The works of Felzmann et al. and Fernández-Llamas et al. address transparency in robot technologies and the educational impact of robotic teachers, respectively.

Fincannon et al. provide insights into best practices for human operation of robotic vehicles and team dynamics in mixed-initiative settings. The research covers various human factors such as situational awareness, workload, and communication modalities, assessing their impact on team efficiency and satisfaction. Notably, Gombolay et al. examine decision-making authority in human-robot teams and its influence on performance.

The challenges of collaborative HRI are underscored by Hastie et al., while Hong et al. focus on multimodal feedback for teleoperation in outdoor environments. Overall, these studies contribute to understanding the complexities of HRI in various contexts, highlighting the need for effective interfaces, transparency, and social cues in fostering successful interactions between humans and robots.
