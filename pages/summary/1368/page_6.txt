This paper examines the characteristics and comparisons of multi-agent human-robot interaction (HRI) systems, focusing on team structure, size, and composition. The team structure is a crucial aspect, influencing how agents interact and share information. The paper identifies two primary factors under team structure: team size and team composition, highlighting their implications on human perceptions and emotions towards robots.

In terms of team size, the paper categorizes HRI systems into three types: single-human single-robot, single-human multi-robot, and multi-human single-robot. Single-human single-robot systems are traditional and widely studied, while the paper emphasizes the significance of systems with multiple agents.

Single-human multi-robot systems involve one human working with several robots, often in applications requiring human oversight, such as large-scale assembly or search-and-rescue operations. These systems can also facilitate Human-Swarm Interaction, where robots coordinate autonomously while receiving human input.

Multi-human single-robot systems allow multiple humans to collaborate with a single robot, commonly seen in scenarios like exploration or UAV operations. Here, humans assume various roles to manage the robot effectively, especially when a single operator cannot oversee it alone.

The paper illustrates these concepts with recent literature examples, analyzing the strengths and limitations of each system type. Overall, the categorization of team size and structure is essential for understanding the dynamics of human-robot collaboration in diverse applications.
