7.2. Task-related Factors
Task-related factors also play a crucial role in the performance and effectiveness of multi-agent HRI systems. The complexity of tasks, the nature of interactions, and the specific roles of both robots and humans can significantly impact system outcomes. Key areas for future research in this domain include:

1. Task Complexity: As tasks increase in complexity, the coordination and communication among multiple agents become more challenging. Research is needed to develop frameworks that support effective task management and distribution among robots and humans, particularly in dynamic environments.

2. Interaction Patterns: Understanding how different interaction patterns between robots and humans influence task performance is essential. Investigating various communication strategies and their effects on collaboration can lead to more effective multi-agent systems.

3. Adaptability to Task Changes: Multi-agent HRI systems must be adaptable to changes in tasks or environmental conditions. Future research should focus on developing systems that can dynamically adjust their strategies and roles based on real-time feedback and changing circumstances.

4. Performance Metrics: Establishing standardized metrics for evaluating the performance of multi-agent HRI systems is vital for assessing their effectiveness. Research should aim to create metrics that consider both individual and collective performance, accounting for the complexities introduced by multiple agents.

In conclusion, advancing multi-agent HRI systems requires addressing both agent-related and task-related challenges. A multidisciplinary approach that combines insights from robotics, social sciences, and cognitive psychology can facilitate the development of systems that operate seamlessly and safely alongside humans. Further exploration of these areas will contribute to the realization of effective and intuitive human-robot collaboration in various applications.
