This collection of references highlights various studies and advancements in the field of human-robot interaction (HRI) and multi-robot systems. Key themes include the roles and operational protocols for robotic systems in urban environments, the prediction of trust in human control of robotic swarms, and the methodologies for effective human-robot collaboration.

Notable contributions include the exploration of socio-emotional behaviors in group interactions, measurement of human control over multiple robots, and the impact of robot group sizes on human psychological states. Several studies focus on enhancing human performance through mixed control strategies and direct communication methods in multi-human multi-robot interactions.

The literature also examines the design of interfaces for cooperative navigation among robots, the effectiveness of responsive gestures in improving interpersonal evaluations, and the role of adaptive synchronization in heterogeneous multi-agent systems. The research underscores the importance of situational awareness, immersion, and the integration of emotional cues in robotic systems to foster better collaboration and interaction with humans.

Overall, these works collectively contribute to a deeper understanding of the dynamics of human-robot collaboration, the implications of autonomy levels, and the design considerations necessary for effective and engaging robotic systems in various contexts.
