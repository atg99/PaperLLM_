The paper discusses interaction models in human-robot systems, focusing on one-to-many and many-to-many models. In the one-to-many model, multiple agents receive group commands from a single source, often through verbal communication or gestures, making it suitable for social settings like classrooms or public spaces. This model is also applicable in industrial scenarios where a group command node interprets commands for individual robots, enhancing efficiency in command dissemination.

The many-to-many model allows for multiple one-to-many interactions among agents, providing a flexible interaction framework. Although less common in literature, it is facilitated through proxy architectures that manage communication between humans and robots, ensuring seamless collaboration. Augmented Reality interfaces can enhance usability in such systems.

Communication modalities are critical in these interactions, encompassing verbal and non-verbal methods. The choice of modality depends on factors like environment and proximity, influencing the effectiveness of communication. Remote communication, particularly through screen-based interfaces, is essential in hazardous environments, allowing human operators to control robotic fleets effectively. Overall, the paper highlights the importance of interaction models and communication methods in optimizing human-robot collaboration.
