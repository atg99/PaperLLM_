The paper discusses the evolution of human-robot interaction (HRI) systems, emphasizing the increasing complexity of systems involving multiple humans and robots. Traditionally, systems required multiple humans to control a single robot, particularly in critical applications. However, advancements in robot autonomy have shifted this dynamic, allowing a single human to supervise multiple robots. This change presents challenges related to human cognitive limitations, workload, and situational awareness.

To address these challenges, the paper highlights ongoing research aimed at improving decision support systems, enhancing robot motion predictability, and developing intuitive interfaces for human-robot collaboration. The authors note that while there is significant research on heterogeneous robot systems, their application in social contexts remains limited.

The paper also explores the concept of interaction styles in multi-agent HRI systems. It categorizes interactions into three types: Human-Human, Robot-Robot, and one-to-many interactions, which differ from traditional dyadic interactions. The authors emphasize the importance of distinguishing between direct and indirect interactions, where direct interactions involve explicit communication between agents and indirect interactions occur when a third party observes these interactions.

The discussion extends to interaction models, which can vary in size and structure within multi-agent systems. These models allow for one-to-one or one-to-many communication, specifying the flow of information among agents. The paper concludes by underscoring the need for a deeper understanding of interaction dynamics to improve outcomes in multi-agent HRI systems.
