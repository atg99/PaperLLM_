This paper discusses the dynamics of human-robot interactions (HRI) within multi-agent systems, emphasizing how interactions among agents can influence behavior. It highlights the significance of indirect communication between agents, where human-human interactions may be affected by human-robot interactions and vice versa. The presence and embodiment of robots can shape human behavior and perceptions.

The authors identify various communication modalities, noting that the most common interface for human interaction with multiple robots is screen-based. However, advancements in Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR) technologies could shift these interactions to more immersive platforms, though further research is needed to assess their effectiveness.

The paper also addresses the computational characteristics of HRI systems, focusing on robot control methods. Two primary control types are discussed: optimization-based control, which aims to optimize performance parameters like task completion time and resource usage, and predefined/rule-based control. The authors introduce the concept of modeling these systems using frameworks from behavioral studies, such as psychology and economics, to inform robot actions.

Overall, the paper underscores the complexity of interactions in multi-agent HRI systems and the need for continued exploration in both communication modalities and computational control strategies to enhance the efficacy and naturalness of these interactions.
