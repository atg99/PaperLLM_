This paper surveys the multi-agent Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) literature, categorizing studies into three types: System Design, which proposes practical HRI systems; Observational, which examines system behaviors; and Algorithmic Design, focusing on computational models and algorithms for decision-making in HRI. The inclusion criteria for the survey emphasize real or synthetic interactions involving at least one human and one robot, while excluding studies that do not directly apply to human-robot interaction.

The authors identify five attributes to characterize multi-agent HRI systems, based on team structure, interaction style, and computational characteristics. The survey details these categories and provides illustrative examples from the literature. It also includes an interaction graph model to define multi-agent HRI systems, where interaction is characterized by the flow of information among agents.

The paper discusses the complexities introduced by multi-agent interactions compared to dyadic interactions, highlighting the increased uncertainty in outcomes and the need for predictability in human responses. It draws parallels between human-only group interactions and multi-agent HRI, suggesting that insights from social sciences can enhance understanding in this field. The survey concludes with a discussion of current challenges in multi-agent HRI research and potential future directions.
