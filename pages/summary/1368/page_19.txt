The paper discusses the complexities of Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) within multi-agent systems, emphasizing the importance of partner selection and interaction quality in human-robot teams. It highlights limited research on how humans choose robotic partners and vice versa, suggesting that enhancing these interactions is vital for seamless collaboration in social contexts.

The paper addresses conflict resolution as a critical aspect, noting the challenges in establishing effective interaction models among multiple agents and the coordination required for successful collaboration. It points out that human performance may decline when robots do not coordinate autonomously.

Several task-related factors are identified as challenges in implementing multi-agent HRI systems. Safety is paramount, necessitating frameworks that allow robots to operate safely while working collaboratively with humans, rather than merely avoiding them. Scalability is another concern, as the efficiency of decision-making techniques must be maintained as the number of agents increases. Existing studies primarily focus on theoretical approaches, with limited real-world implementations.

Transparency is highlighted as essential for fostering trust and understanding in HRI. It encompasses motion legibility, intention recognition, and decision explainability, with a noted lack of research in multi-agent contexts. The authors advocate for further exploration in transparency to enhance interactions among mixed teams of humans and robots.

In conclusion, the paper underscores the growing interest in multi-agent HRI systems, which present unique challenges beyond those faced in dyadic interactions. It serves as a survey of the current literature, aiming to facilitate comparison among diverse research efforts and identify key trends and challenges that must be addressed to achieve the vision of harmonious human-robot coexistence.
