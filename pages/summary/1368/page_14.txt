The paper discusses various interface designs and communication methods for controlling multiple robots, particularly in remote and proximate settings. It highlights the challenges faced by human operators, such as increased cognitive workload and maintaining situational awareness as the number of robots increases. Effective interaction interfaces are crucial for efficient human-robot interaction (HRI) in scenarios like search-and-rescue operations and teleoperation of underwater robots.

The paper outlines that traditional screen-based interfaces are prevalent, featuring components like camera feeds and environmental maps. However, immersive technologies like Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR) are emerging as beneficial for enhancing situational awareness and reducing operator workload. Additionally, multi-modal communication systems, which combine various input and feedback methods like haptics, speech, and gestures, are noted for improving interaction outcomes.

In proximate communication scenarios, where humans and robots share the same environment, a wider array of communication modalities is available. These include auditory, visual, and physical channels, allowing for more personal interactions. The importance of non-verbal cues in conveying intent and emotions is emphasized, particularly in social robotics, where such cues significantly influence the interaction dynamics. The paper underscores the necessity for intelligent interface designs and communication strategies to mitigate the challenges of HRI in both remote and proximal contexts.
