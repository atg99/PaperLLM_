The paper presents a study on multi-agent human-robot systems, highlighting various interaction graphs that illustrate the dynamics between human operators and robots. The key findings from different studies are summarized as follows:

1. **Short and Mataric (2017); Jung et al. (2020)**: Robot actions are independent of human teammates, who base their actions on the robots' behaviors and potential discussions with other human team members.

2. **Claure et al. (2020)**: Similar to the previous study, robots act independently, while humans decide their actions based on the robots' actions and the observations of other human teammates, without explicit communication.

3. **Tan et al. (2019)**: A human interacts with one of two robots, which communicates necessary information to the second robot while the human observes both robots.

4. **Swamy et al. (2020); Rosenfeld et al. (2017)**: Humans control each robot individually, making selections based on either the robots' states or interface suggestions.

5. **Dahiya et al. (2022)**: A central computational entity collects data from all robots and allocates human operators to assist specific robots, with direct assistance provided without interaction from the robots.

6. **Patel and Pinciroli (2020)**: Commands from multiple human operators are translated into individual robot actions. Operators can directly observe the robots and collaborate to resolve conflicts.

7. **Correia et al. (2018)**: In a competitive card game, two teams consisting of a human and a robot each decide their actions based on observations of each other's actions, with limited direct communication occurring only for emotional expression by the robots.

The interaction graphs illustrate both direct (solid arrows) and indirect (dashed arrows) interactions, with the potential for larger agent systems beyond the examples provided. The table serves as a foundational overview of existing models in human-robot interaction, though it is not comprehensive.
