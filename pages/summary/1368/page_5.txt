The paper presents a framework for categorizing multi-agent Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) systems based on three core aspects: team structure, interaction style, and computational characteristics. 

1. **Team Structure** focuses on the composition of the human-robot system, including the number and types of agents involved, as well as their homogeneity. Homogeneity refers to the similarities in roles, capabilities, and authorities among agents, which impacts how interactions are initiated and managed.

2. **Interaction Style** describes the modalities and models of communication between agents. This aspect addresses how interactions occur—whether through screens, speech, or other means—and the nature of these interactions, whether between two agents or among multiple ones.

3. **Computational Characteristics** examines the software elements that dictate agent behavior. This includes task planning algorithms and controllers that influence how robots act within the system and how these actions can affect human behavior.

The paper emphasizes that these three aspects are interrelated, and changes in one can influence others. The authors provide an example of a system with a human operator managing a team of mobile robots, illustrating how team size, agent homogeneity, interaction models, and communication modalities can be characterized. 

Additionally, the authors note that these core aspects are not exhaustive but are designed to create a flexible taxonomy applicable across various research areas in multi-agent HRI. The interaction of these aspects is further elaborated in the paper, providing a comprehensive understanding of multi-agent systems in HRI contexts.
