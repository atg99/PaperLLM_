The paper details the training and results of HoloGAN, a model designed for generating images with 3D representations. HoloGAN was trained on several datasets, including Basel Face, CelebA, Cats, Chairs, Cars, and LSUN bedroom, with varying resolutions depending on the dataset. Notably, the Chairs dataset provided multiple views of the same object, allowing the model to learn from a diverse set of perspectives.

The implementation of HoloGAN utilized adaptive instance normalization for the generator and a combination of instance and spectral normalization for the discriminator. The model was trained from scratch using the Adam optimizer and involved sampling from uniform distributions for poses and latent variables.

Results were presented in qualitative and quantitative formats. Qualitative evaluations demonstrated HoloGAN's ability to smoothly adjust poses while preserving object identities across various datasets, even in complex settings like LSUN. The model effectively disentangled pose from identity without prior knowledge of pose labels or 3D shapes.

In comparison to InfoGAN, HoloGAN showed superior control over azimuth and elevation in image generation tasks, highlighting its explicit control mechanism through rigid-body transformations. Quantitatively, the visual fidelity of generated images was assessed using the Kernel Inception Distance (KID), which indicated HoloGAN's performance relative to traditional 2D GAN models.

Overall, HoloGAN's design and training approach proved effective in generating high-quality images while successfully disentangling shape and appearance, as further supported by ablation studies included in the results.
