The paper compares the image generation capabilities of two models: VON and HoloGAN, particularly in generating car images under varying azimuth and elevation. VON struggles with generating images with complex backgrounds and maintaining car identity across different views, often resulting in a simple white background and distorted identities. In contrast, HoloGAN effectively generates images with intricate backgrounds, preserving the car's identity across a full 360-degree view.

HoloGAN's approach involves using explicit deep voxel representations, which are less memory-intensive than traditional voxel grids. It demonstrates the ability to disentangle shape and appearance by utilizing two latent codes that separately control 3D features and 2D features, allowing for independent manipulation of object shapes and appearances derived from unlabelled images.

The paper also discusses the importance of random 3D transformations during training, which significantly enhances HoloGAN's ability to learn a disentangled representation of pose and identity. Without these transformations, the model's ability to generate meaningful images when poses change is compromised, whereas HoloGAN maintains visual fidelity and adaptability.

Overall, the findings highlight HoloGAN's advantages in generating high-quality images with greater control over various attributes compared to VON, emphasizing its potential in the field of image generation.
