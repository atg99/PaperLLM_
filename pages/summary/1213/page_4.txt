The paper discusses HoloGAN, a model designed for generating 3D representations and images using a learned constant tensor and a style-based approach. HoloGAN employs adaptive instance normalization (AdaIN) to manipulate features from the image using a random noise vector as a style controller. This allows for the generation of images that match the mean and standard deviation of features at various levels, effectively disentangling pose and identity better than other models that directly use the noise vector.

HoloGAN differs from StyleGAN in two critical ways: it learns 3D features from a 4D tensor and incorporates rigid-body transformations during training to create a disentangled representation. This results in the separation of pose, shape, and appearance, enhancing the model's ability to generate images with complex backgrounds even at lower resolutions.

The model also includes a view-dependent mapping by applying random pose transformations to the learned features before projecting them into 2D images, ensuring the learned 3D representation can be rendered from multiple perspectives. A differentiable projection unit is utilized to account for occlusion and perspective projection without prior knowledge of camera intrinsics.

The loss functions implemented include an identity regularizer, which encourages the model to maintain object identity across varying poses, and multi-scale style discriminators that help control image attributes at different levels, preventing mode collapse and supporting extended training. The total loss combines the GAN loss with the identity and style losses, facilitating high-quality image generation.
