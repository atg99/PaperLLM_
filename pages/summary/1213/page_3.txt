The paper discusses HoloGAN, a novel generative model that learns 3D representations from 2D images without requiring labels. Unlike traditional methods that rely on explicit 3D data or assume simple backgrounds, HoloGAN effectively handles complex scenes with multiple objects and intricate backgrounds. It utilizes a generator network that incorporates 3D convolutions, adaptive instance normalization, and a projection unit to create realistic 2D images from learned 3D representations.

HoloGAN distinguishes itself from similar approaches like Pix2Scene by employing an explicit 3D representation rather than mapping to a surfel representation. This allows HoloGAN to render images from scratch, enabling it to work with complex natural images rather than only simple synthetic ones. 

The paper emphasizes the importance of disentangled representation learning, where different factors such as pose, shape, and appearance can be manipulated independently. HoloGAN achieves this by redesigning its generator architecture to facilitate pose control and shape/appearance editing. It incorporates a strong inductive bias about the 3D world, allowing for direct application of 3D rigid-body transformations to learned features, which aids in generating sharper images with fewer artifacts.

The method involves sampling random poses during training, which encourages the generator to learn a disentangled representation suitable for both transformations and image generation. The paper categorizes learned representations into intrinsic elements (shape, appearance) and extrinsic elements (pose, lighting), showcasing HoloGAN's ability to effectively differentiate and control these aspects through its design.
