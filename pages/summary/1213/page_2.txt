The paper introduces HoloGAN, an unsupervised generative model for learning 3D representations of objects directly from unlabelled natural images. Traditional representations in computer graphics, like voxels and meshes, are explicit in 3D but suffer from memory inefficiency and complexity in handling intricate objects. Implicit representations, while compact and semantically rich, struggle with explicit 3D transformations, leading to visual artifacts.

HoloGAN addresses these limitations by learning representations that are both explicit in 3D and semantically expressive. It innovatively combines 3D and 2D features in the generative process, first creating a 3D representation of an object, then transforming it to a target pose, projecting it to 2D features, and rendering the final images. Unlike prior models that rely on handcrafted differentiable renderers, HoloGAN learns the perspective projection and rendering processes from scratch, enabling it to effectively handle natural images without requiring prior geometry or pose information.

The model is trained end-to-end in an unsupervised manner, allowing for disentangled representation learning of pose, shape, and appearance without the need for labeled data. The key contributions of HoloGAN include a novel architecture that integrates strong inductive biases about the 3D world with deep generative models, an unconditional GAN that supports view manipulation while maintaining image fidelity, and a training approach that facilitates learning without supervision.

In the related work section, the paper contextualizes HoloGAN within the fields of GANs, structure-aware image synthesis, and disentangled representation learning. It highlights existing approaches in these domains, emphasizing the uniqueness of HoloGAN in achieving unsupervised disentangled representation learning and direct manipulation of 3D attributes.
