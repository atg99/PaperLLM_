HoloGAN introduces a new generative adversarial network designed for unsupervised learning of 3D representations from natural images. Traditional generative models often rely on 2D kernels, resulting in limitations such as blurry images and artifacts in tasks requiring strong 3D understanding, like novel-view synthesis. In contrast, HoloGAN learns a 3D representation and renders it realistically, allowing explicit control over the pose of generated objects through rigid-body transformations of the learned 3D features. 

The model effectively disentangles 3D pose and identity, further breaking them down into shape and appearance while maintaining or improving the visual quality of generated images compared to other models. HoloGAN is unique in that it can be trained end-to-end using only unlabelled 2D images, eliminating the need for pose labels, 3D shapes, or multiple views of the same objects. This capability positions HoloGAN as the first generative model to learn 3D representations from natural images unsupervised, addressing a significant gap in the field of computer vision and graphics. The architecture leverages a strong inductive bias about the 3D world, facilitating direct manipulation of view, shape, and appearance in generative models.
