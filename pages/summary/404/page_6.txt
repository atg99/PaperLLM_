The architecture of YOLO-v1 divides the input image into a grid of cells, with each cell predicting bounding boxes and class probabilities. The output parameters include bounding box coordinates, confidence scores, and class probabilities, leading to an output shape of s × s × (5 * B + C). The use of non-maximum suppression (NMS) helps manage overlapping bounding boxes by eliminating those with low intersection over union (IoU) values.

The loss function in YOLO incorporates several components to penalize incorrect predictions, particularly focusing on bounding box coordinates, dimensions, confidence scores, and class probabilities. The architecture's initial limitations, such as low recall and localization errors, prompted the development of subsequent YOLO versions, including YOLO-v2, which improved training efficiency through batch normalization, utilized anchor boxes for bounding box predictions, and incorporated better image resolution handling.

Overall, the evolution of the YOLO architecture reflects ongoing efforts to enhance object detection accuracy while maintaining high processing speeds, making it suitable for real-time applications.
