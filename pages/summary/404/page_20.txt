The paper discusses the COCO-2017 dataset, which includes over 80 object categories and a total of 121,408 images with 883,331 object annotations. The median image size is 640 by 480 pixels. It emphasizes that the accuracy and inference capabilities of detection models depend on their design and training strategies, particularly in industrial surface detection.

A comparison of various YOLO (You Only Look Once) variants is provided in Table 5, highlighting different frameworks, backbones, average precision (AP) scores, and comments on architectural features. The AP metric is explained as a combination of precision-recall metrics, using Intersection over Union (IoU) to assess the similarity between predicted and ground truth bounding boxes.

The paper identifies two primary factors contributing to the popularity of YOLO variants. The first factor is the architectural compatibility for one-stage detection and classification, which makes YOLO computationally efficient compared to other detectors. The second factor is the increased accessibility of the YOLO framework, particularly with YOLO-v5, which transitioned to the PyTorch framework, broadening its user base and application domains.

The text notes that YOLO-v5 has gained significant traction, evidenced by its 34.7k stars on GitHub, surpassing its predecessors. The ease of installation and reduced training time associated with YOLO-v5 have lowered experimentation costs, making it more appealing for researchers. Additionally, various deployment options are available, such as processing individual or batch images, video feeds, and converting weights for edge devices.
