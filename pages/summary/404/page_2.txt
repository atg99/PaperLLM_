This paper addresses the challenges associated with traditional quality inspection methods in manufacturing, which are often hindered by human bias, fatigue, costs, and downtime. It advocates for computer vision (CV)-based solutions to facilitate automated quality inspection, thus enhancing efficiency and addressing bottlenecks inherent in conventional methodologies. 

A critical aspect of implementing CV solutions is their adherence to specific deployment requirements that vary across manufacturing sectors. The focus extends beyond merely identifying defects to also include their localization, making object detection more favorable than image classification. Object detection architectures are categorized into single-stage and two-stage detectors. While two-stage detectors offer high accuracy, they require substantial computational resources, making them less suitable for real-time applications on constrained devices. In contrast, single-stage detectors streamline the process, allowing for simultaneous classification and regression, thereby reducing computational demands. Among these, the YOLO (You Only Look Once) family of architectures has gained prominence for its compatibility with industrial needs, including accuracy, lightweight design, and suitability for edge deployment, with YOLO-v8 being the latest variant introduced in 2022.

The paper presents a comprehensive review of YOLO variants, detailing their technical advancements and implications for industrial deployment, emphasizing key metrics such as accuracy, speed, and computational efficacy. This review aims to equip researchers and practitioners with insights into the strengths of each YOLO architecture, facilitating informed selection based on specific industrial requirements. The structure of the review encompasses an introduction to detection architectures, a chronological evolution of YOLO variants from YOLO-v1 to YOLO-v8, and a literature review on YOLO applications in industrial surface defect detection. The discussion concludes with a summary of findings, future research directions, and challenges in the field.

The paper also explores the architecture of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) used for classification, highlighting the importance of feature extraction through layers of convolution and pooling, which are essential for achieving high accuracy in object detection tasks. Popular architectures for feature extraction, such as AlexNet, VGGNet, GoogleNet, and ResNet, are mentioned, with AlexNet being noted for its foundational role in image classification.
