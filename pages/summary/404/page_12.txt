The paper discusses advancements in the YOLO (You Only Look Once) object detection models, specifically focusing on the YOLO-v6 and YOLO-v7 iterations. YOLO-v6 introduces knowledge distillation, where a larger teacher model trains a smaller student model using soft labels, enabling high performance without increased computational costs. Performance comparisons on the COCO dataset indicate that YOLO-v6 outperforms its predecessor, YOLO-v5, in terms of mean Average Precision (mAP) and frames per second (FPS).

Subsequently, YOLO-v7 was released with architectural reforms aimed at enhancing accuracy while maintaining high detection speeds. These reforms are categorized into architectural changes and a Trainable bag-of-freebies (BoF). A significant architectural change is the integration of the extended efficient layer aggregation network (E-ELAN) in the YOLO-v7 backbone, which optimizes network efficiency based on input/output channel ratios and gradient paths. Additionally, YOLO-v7 employs a compound model scaling approach, allowing simultaneous scaling of network width and depth to meet varying application requirements for accuracy and speed.

The paper also describes re-parameterization planning for a robust network and the introduction of an auxiliary head in the middle layers to aid training, although it is less efficient than the main head. Performance comparisons on the MS COCO dataset show that YOLO-v7 variants exceed the accuracy and speed of previous YOLO models, achieving between 5 and 160 FPS. However, it is noted that YOLO-v7 variants are not intended for CPU-based mobile deployment, with specific models designed for edge and high-end cloud GPUs.
