This paper discusses advancements in industrial fabric defect detection, highlighting various approaches to improve efficiency in textile manufacturing. Rui Jin et al. propose a YOLO-v5-based architecture utilizing a spatial attention mechanism to enhance the detection of smaller defects. Their method involves training a teacher network on a fabric dataset, followed by weight distillation to a smaller student network suitable for deployment on a Jetson TX2, achieving an AUC of 98.1% for the teacher and 95.2% for the student, with the latter offering faster inference at 16 ms.

Sifundvoleshile Dlamini et al. introduce a real-time detection framework that combines conventional image processing with the YOLO-v4 architecture, achieving an F1-score of 93.6% and a detection speed of 34 fps with a prediction speed of 21.4 ms, demonstrating the effectiveness of their approach.

Guijuan Lin et al. address challenges in fabric inspection, such as small defect sizes and slow detection speeds, by proposing a sliding-window self-attention mechanism integrated with YOLO-v5 and a Swin Transformer module for hierarchical feature extraction. Their solution achieves a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 76.5% at 58.8 FPS, making it suitable for real-time detection in embedded devices. Overall, these studies highlight the potential of advanced machine learning techniques for enhancing fabric defect detection in industrial settings.
