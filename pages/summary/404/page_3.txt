The paper discusses advancements in object detection architectures, highlighting key models such as AlexNet, VGGNet, GoogleNet, and ResNet. AlexNet, introduced in 2012, consists of five convolutional, three pooling, and three fully connected layers, primarily used for image classification. VGGNet enhanced performance by increasing architectural depth with variants like VGG-16 and VGG-19. GoogleNet introduced the cascading of multiple 'inception' modules, while ResNet introduced skip-connections to preserve information across layers.

Object detection is defined as the process of identifying whether objects of interest are present in images or video frames, providing class and location information. It is categorized into two methods: two-stage and one-stage. Two-stage methods, such as R-CNN variants, involve generating proposals and then predicting on those regions, achieving high accuracy but lower computational efficiency. In contrast, one-stage methods simplify the task into a regression problem, allowing for faster processing and higher frames per second (FPS), though they may sacrifice some accuracy.

The paper introduces the YOLO (You Only Look Once) algorithm, first presented in 2015 by Joseph Redmon, which redefined object detection as a single-pass regression problem. This approach enables simultaneous predictions of multiple bounding boxes and class probabilities, enhancing both speed and accuracy. Since its introduction, the YOLO family has evolved significantly, with the latest version being YOLO-v8. The original YOLO algorithm utilized a grid system to assign object detection responsibilities to specific grid cells, allowing for efficient handling of multiple object appearances.
