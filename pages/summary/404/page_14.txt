The paper discusses the introduction of YOLO-v8 by Ultralytics in January 2023, highlighting its advancements over previous YOLO versions, specifically YOLO-v5 and YOLO-v6. Initial benchmarks indicate that YOLO-v8 offers superior throughput at a similar parameter count, suggesting improvements in hardware efficiency and suitability for high-inference speed deployments on constrained edge devices.

The latter part of the paper focuses on the application of YOLO variants in industrial defect detection, particularly in textile manufacturing. It emphasizes the inefficiencies of manual inspection, such as high labor costs and reduced detection speed. Rui Jin et al. propose a YOLO-v5-based architecture enhanced with a spatial attention mechanism to better identify smaller defects. Their approach involves a teacher-student model where the teacher network, trained on a fabric dataset, outperforms the student network with an AUC of 98.1% compared to 95.2%. Despite the teacher network's higher performance, the student network achieves significantly faster inference times, demonstrating its practical deployment potential on a Jetson TX2 platform. This illustrates the balance between accuracy and speed that is central to the evolution of YOLO variants in industrial applications.
