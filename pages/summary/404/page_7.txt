The paper discusses advancements in the YOLO (You Only Look Once) architecture for real-time object detection, focusing on YOLO-v2, YOLO-v3, and YOLO-v4. YOLO-v2 introduced anchor boxes for bounding box predictions, enhancing mAP performance to 76.8 at 67 FPS, but struggled with smaller object detection, achieving a lower mAP of 19.7% when extended to YOLO-9000 for detecting over 9000 objects.

YOLO-v3 addressed the limitations of YOLO-v2 by incorporating a hybrid architecture that combined features from YOLO-v2 and ResNet, facilitating the preservation of fine-grained details through skip connections. This version included a total of 106 convolutional layers and enabled multi-scale detection, improving small object detection capabilities.

YOLO-v4, introduced by Alexey Bochkovsky et al., built upon prior advancements, experimenting with various backbones like CSPResNext-50 and EfficientNet-B3, ultimately selecting CSPDarknet-53 for its superior feature extraction. The authors employed the PANet for feature aggregation, enhancing the architecture's ability to link fine-grained features across layers. Overall, YOLO-v4 represents a culmination of techniques aimed at creating a lightweight yet powerful real-time object detector.
