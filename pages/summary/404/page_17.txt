The paper discusses advancements in defect detection using deep learning architectures, specifically YOLO models, in industrial applications. The first study by Dinming Yang et al. focuses on the detection of defects in steel pipes using X-ray images. They emphasize the importance of quality inspection in the context of increasing demand, particularly in China. Their implementation of YOLO-v5 achieved a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 98.7% at an Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold of 0.5, with a rapid detection time of 0.12 seconds per image, highlighting its potential for real-time monitoring.

In another study, Zhuxi MA et al. tackle the challenges of computation and hardware requirements for defect detection in aluminum strips. They utilize YOLO-v4, enhancing it with depth-wise separable convolutions and a parallel dual attention mechanism. Their approach yielded an mAP of 96.28% on real data from a cold-rolling workshop, while achieving an 83.38% reduction in model volume and tripling the inference speed compared to the original YOLO-v4. The improvements are attributed to a custom anchor strategy that aligns with the defect characteristics, such as scratch marks, indicating a tailored approach to the problem.
