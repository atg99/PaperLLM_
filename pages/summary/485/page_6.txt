This paper presents a study on the performance of Deep Belief Networks (DBN) in feature extraction for circuit fault diagnosis. Two cases are analyzed, with specific configurations for the DBN structure and parameter settings. In Case 1, the DBN is structured as 128-64-32-16-9, with pre-training and fine-tuning iterations set at 100 and 500 respectively, along with a learning rate of 0.11 and momentum of 0.9. The study employs kernel principal component analysis (KPCA) to visualize the feature distribution of original data versus DBN-extracted features. The results show minimal overlap between the original and extracted features, indicating effective separation of fault modes.

In Case 2, the DBN structure is adjusted to 128-64-32-16-13 while maintaining other parameters. Visualizations for both cases illustrate the distribution of original data and the enhanced separation achieved through DBN feature extraction. Additionally, a table is provided detailing various fault modes and corresponding values for a Sallen-Key bandpass filter circuit, highlighting the diagnostic capabilities of the proposed method. Overall, the findings underscore the efficacy of DBN in improving feature extraction for fault diagnosis in electronic circuits.
