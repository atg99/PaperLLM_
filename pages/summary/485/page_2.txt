This paper presents a method for detecting faults in analog circuits using a Deep Belief Network (DBN) in conjunction with a Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO) optimized Support Vector Machine (SVM). The proposed approach addresses several challenges in fault diagnosis, such as the need for effective feature extraction and the optimization of classification parameters. 

The DBN is highlighted for its capability to extract relevant features directly from the original signal without requiring function mapping or transformation, improving the distinguishability of signal features. The SVM model's performance relies heavily on the correct setting of its penalty factor and kernel parameters, which can be optimized using the GWO algorithm. The GWO mimics the social hierarchy and hunting strategies of gray wolves, providing a robust method for global optimization.

The paper outlines the process of fault detection, starting with the establishment of different fault modes in the analog circuit. The DBN is employed to extract features from the circuit's output signal, and the GWO-SVM is then utilized for diagnosing the identified fault modes. 

Additionally, the paper describes the structure of the Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM), a core component of the DBN. The RBM consists of bidirectional connections between units in different layers, facilitating the extraction of features from high-dimensional data. The energy function and probability distributions associated with the visible and hidden layers are also discussed, emphasizing the mathematical foundation underlying the feature extraction process.

In conclusion, the integration of DBN and GWO-optimized SVM presents a promising methodology for enhancing fault diagnosis in analog circuits, leveraging advanced deep learning techniques for effective feature extraction and classification.
