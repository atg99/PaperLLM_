This paper discusses the optimization of Support Vector Machine (SVM) classification through the Gray Wolf Optimization (GWO) algorithm. In scenarios where data is linearly inseparable, a kernel function is utilized to map input data into a higher-dimensional space, allowing for linear classification post-transformation. The study employs the radial basis function (RBF) as the kernel function and emphasizes the importance of optimizing two key parameters: the penalty factor (c) and the kernel parameter (g) for enhancing classification accuracy.

The GWO algorithm, inspired by the social hierarchy and hunting strategies of gray wolves, is introduced as an effective method for optimizing SVM parameters. The process involves a leadership structure among wolves, denoted as α (leader), β, δ, and ω (subordinates), with the α wolf representing the optimal solution. The optimization steps include initializing parameters, calculating fitness values, training the SVM model, and iteratively updating positions based on calculated distances between wolves and prey.

The algorithm iterates until a maximum number of iterations is reached, ensuring that the gray wolf population converges towards the optimal solution. The paper concludes that GWO provides a simple yet efficient approach for optimizing SVM parameters, outperforming other optimization techniques like particle swarm optimization and genetic algorithms.
