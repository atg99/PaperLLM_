The paper presents a comparative analysis of different classifiers for circuit fault diagnosis, focusing on the GWO-SVM (Grey Wolf Optimizer-Support Vector Machine), GA-SVM (Genetic Algorithm-SVM), and PSO-SVM (Particle Swarm Optimization-SVM). The results show that GWO-SVM achieves a classification accuracy of 100% in Case 1 and 99.68% in Case 2, demonstrating superior performance compared to GA-SVM and PSO-SVM. The GWO-SVM exhibits faster convergence, completing the optimization process in 18.48 seconds for Case 1 and 60.07 seconds for Case 2, significantly reducing fault diagnosis time by approximately 94% and 91%, respectively. 

The ROC curve analysis indicates that GWO-SVM's performance is superior, being closest to the upper left corner, which signifies better sensitivity and accuracy. The fitness curves for all classifiers demonstrate that GWO-SVM not only reaches optimal fitness with fewer iterations—just one iteration in Case 1 and ten in Case 2—but also stabilizes more quickly than the alternatives. Overall, the findings highlight that the proposed GWO-SVM method enhances convergence speed and predictive accuracy, leading to a more robust fault diagnosis system.
