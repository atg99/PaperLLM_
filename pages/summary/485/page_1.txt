This paper presents a novel method for diagnosing faults in analog circuits using a combination of deep belief networks (DBN) and gray wolf optimization (GWO) to enhance support vector machine (SVM) performance. The complexity of integrated electronic equipment necessitates effective fault detection, particularly in the analog components, which, despite constituting only 20% of device size, are responsible for over 80% of failures. 

The proposed methodology involves using DBN to extract deep features from the output signal of the analog circuit, followed by optimizing the SVM's penalty factor and kernel parameter through GWO. The effectiveness of this approach was tested on a Sallen-Key band-pass filter and a four-opamp biquad highpass filter. Results indicated a 100% diagnostic accuracy for the Sallen-Key filter and a 99.68% accuracy for the biquad filter, alongside significant reductions in diagnosis time—approximately 90% and 75%, respectively.

The research highlights the importance of timely fault diagnosis, especially for soft faults that can disrupt circuit functionality without obvious signs. The study addresses the limitations of traditional diagnostic methods and emphasizes the potential of deep learning and optimization techniques in improving the accuracy and efficiency of analog circuit fault detection. Overall, the findings contribute to advancements in diagnostic technology, crucial for the reliability of modern electronic systems.
