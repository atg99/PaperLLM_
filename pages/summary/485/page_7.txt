The paper discusses a diagnostic method for fault classification in circuits, leveraging a Deep Belief Network (DBN) for feature extraction followed by a Grey Wolf Optimization-Support Vector Machine (GWO-SVM) for classification. The study highlights that the DBN effectively separates overlapping fault modes in the original data, enhancing the clarity of features. The GWO algorithm optimizes the SVM parameters, specifically the penalty coefficient (c) and kernel function parameter (g), leading to improved classification accuracy and reduced time. 

In Case 1, the optimal parameters were c = 93.3724 and g = 3.7042, resulting in a classification accuracy of 100%. For Case 2, c was set at 100 and g at 26.9118, achieving a classification accuracy of 99.68%. The method demonstrates high efficiency in diagnosing circuit faults, achieving perfect accuracy for certain fault classifications. The paper also includes a table detailing various fault modes and their corresponding values in the context of a four-op-amp biquad highpass filter circuit.
