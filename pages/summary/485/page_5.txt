This study focuses on the fault detection of active filters, specifically the Sallen-Key band-pass filter and a four-op-amp biquad high-pass filter, which are crucial in various electronic systems. Due to the potential catastrophic consequences of filter faults, the research investigates single device faults within these circuits. 

For the Sallen-Key filter, the components identified as most influential on the center frequency are R2, R3, C1, and C2, leading to nine fault categories, including a no-fault mode. The four-op-amp biquad filter has its own set of 13 fault categories. 

The fault detection method employs a Deep Belief Network (DBN) for feature extraction from the analog circuit’s output data, followed by classification using a Grey Wolf Optimized (GWO) Support Vector Machine (SVM). The process involves setting the fault mode, collecting output data through time-domain transient analysis, and performing Monte Carlo analysis to generate fault instances. The data is then split into training and test groups, with feature extraction and optimization of SVM parameters occurring before the final fault diagnosis.

Data for this analysis is generated using PSPICE software, capturing the amplitude-frequency characteristics of the circuits under various fault conditions. The experimental setup yields a total of 2160 instances for Case 1 and 3120 for Case 2, utilizing a DBN with three hidden layers for enhanced feature extraction.
