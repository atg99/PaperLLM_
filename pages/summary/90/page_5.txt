This paper presents a training algorithm for an LSTM-based anomaly detection system using a dual formulation of One-Class Support Vector Machine (OC-SVM). The approach involves updating parameters iteratively to minimize an objective function. Initially, parameters are updated separately for the LSTM and OC-SVM components, with the first step involving the calculation of parameter 1 in relation to parameter 2, followed by a derivative-based update for parameter 2. The updates are constrained within a specific range to ensure validity.

The paper also describes a quadratic programming-based training algorithm, which directly optimizes the OC-SVM formulation but may face convergence issues due to sequential updates. To address this, a gradient-based training method is introduced, updating parameters simultaneously while requiring an approximation of the OC-SVM formulation, which is shown to converge to the original formulation.

An optimization problem is formulated to detect anomalies, where the non-differentiable function is approximated using a smooth differentiable function. The optimization is performed using first-order gradient methods, specifically stochastic gradient descent (SGD), to update the parameters until convergence is achieved. The overall process is designed to ensure efficient training and accurate anomaly detection through the integration of LSTM and OC-SVM methodologies.
