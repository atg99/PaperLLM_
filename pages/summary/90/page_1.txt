The paper investigates unsupervised anomaly detection using Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks, addressing the challenge of processing variable length data sequences. The authors propose a novel approach where data sequences are first transformed into fixed length representations via LSTM, which are then analyzed using One Class Support Vector Machines (OC-SVM) and Support Vector Data Description (SVDD) algorithms to identify anomalies. This is the first instance in the literature where the parameters of the LSTM architecture are jointly trained with OC-SVM or SVDD, utilizing gradient and quadratic programming based training methods. The authors modify the original objective criteria of OC-SVM and SVDD to facilitate gradient-based training and demonstrate convergence to the original criteria.

The paper further extends its framework to semi-supervised and fully supervised scenarios, enhancing the versatility of the proposed algorithms for various applications, particularly in time series data. The methodology allows for significant performance improvements over conventional methods, as evidenced by experimental results. The approach is also adaptable to Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) architectures, showcasing its general applicability. Overall, the study presents a robust solution to the variable length anomaly detection problem, emphasizing the effectiveness of LSTM networks when combined with established anomaly detection techniques.
