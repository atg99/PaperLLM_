The paper presents a novel approach to anomaly detection that leverages Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and jointly optimizes them with One-Class Support Vector Machines (OC-SVM) or Support Vector Data Description (SVDD). Unlike traditional methods that primarily focus on fixed-length sequences and prediction-based approaches, the proposed algorithms can handle variable-length sequences effectively. The authors emphasize the inadequacies of existing methods, particularly those based on OC-SVM and SVDD, which struggle with time series data due to their inability to capture temporal dependencies.

The authors introduce well-defined objective functions for anomaly detection within the LSTM framework, allowing for the optimization of LSTM parameters without the complex tuning typically required. This results in improved performance for time series data while eliminating the need for extensive preprocessing. They further extend their framework to Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), showcasing its versatility.

Key contributions of the paper include the introduction of unsupervised LSTM-based anomaly detection algorithms, the first joint training of LSTM and OC-SVM/SVDD parameters, and significant performance enhancements demonstrated through experiments on real and simulated datasets. The organization of the paper includes a detailed description of the variable-length anomaly detection problem, the proposed LSTM structure, and performance evaluations, culminating in a comprehensive conclusion.
