The paper discusses the optimization process for parameters in a semi-supervised learning framework, particularly focusing on updating weights and biases in a Support Vector Machine (SVM) context using gradient descent methods. It outlines the iterative update rules for parameters \( w \), \( \alpha \), and \( W(\cdot) \) using gradients derived from the objective function \( F \). The updates are defined in equations (33), (35), (37), and involve calculations of gradients with respect to each parameter.

The paper also details the optimization problem for SVM algorithms, highlighting the constraints and the role of slack variables. The modifications required for both quadratic programming and gradient-based training methods to accommodate the semi-supervised framework are explained, along with the adjustments made for supervised cases.

Proposition 1 establishes that as a parameter \( \xi \) increases, the approximation of the objective function converges to the actual SVM objective function. The proof elaborates on the monotonic behavior of the functions involved and provides a bound on the difference between the approximated and actual functions, demonstrating uniform convergence.

Overall, the paper presents a structured approach to parameter optimization in semi-supervised SVMs, supported by theoretical convergence results, ensuring the robustness of the proposed methods. The complete algorithm is provided in pseudocode form, facilitating implementation.
