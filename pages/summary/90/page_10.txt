In this paper, the authors evaluate various anomaly detection algorithms, specifically focusing on LSTM-based methods, including LSTM-GSVM, LSTM-QPSVM, LSTM-GSVDD, and LSTM-QPSVDD. They set parameters such as the output dimension of the LSTM architecture (m=2) and a regularization parameter (λ=0.5) for all algorithms. The performance metric used is the area under the ROC curve (AUC), which measures the true positive rate against the false positive rate.

The study demonstrates that LSTM-based methods outperform traditional OC-SVM and SVDD algorithms, with LSTM-GSVM and LSTM-GSVDD achieving the highest AUC scores in various scenarios. The authors note that the quadratic programming training method used in some algorithms may struggle to converge, while the gradient-based method generally ensures better performance due to its optimization capabilities.

Additionally, the paper explores performance on benchmark datasets, including an occupancy dataset and a financial dataset (HKE rate), confirming that both LSTM and GRU architectures yield superior AUC scores compared to conventional methods due to their ability to retain sequential information. The findings indicate that gradient-based training methods consistently outperform quadratic programming methods across different scenarios.

Ultimately, the results suggest that the proposed LSTM-based algorithms significantly enhance anomaly detection performance compared to traditional approaches, highlighting the advantages of using recurrent neural network architectures in this context.
