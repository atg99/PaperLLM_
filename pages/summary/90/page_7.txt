The paper presents a theorem establishing the uniqueness and convergence of solutions w* and z* for a given function F(w, z, θ). The proof demonstrates that the Hessian matrix of F with respect to w is positive definite, indicating that F is strictly convex in w, thus ensuring a unique global solution. A similar argument applies to z, confirming its unique global solution as well.

The paper then transitions to discussing anomaly detection using the Support Vector Data Description (SVDD) algorithm. The goal is to find a hypersphere that encapsulates normal data while excluding anomalies. The SVDD optimization problem is formulated with constraints, including parameters for LSTM and slack variables. An anomaly scoring function is defined to facilitate detection.

The authors outline a gradient-based training algorithm for the SVDD, initializing LSTM and OC-SVM parameters, and iterating until convergence is achieved based on a defined threshold. Additionally, a quadratic programming approach is introduced for optimizing SVDD parameters while keeping LSTM parameters fixed. The dual form of the optimization problem is derived, leading to a modified scoring function for anomaly detection.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive approach to establishing the theoretical foundation for the uniqueness of solutions and outlines practical methodologies for implementing anomaly detection using SVDD, including algorithmic steps for training and scoring.
