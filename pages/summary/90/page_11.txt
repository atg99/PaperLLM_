This paper investigates anomaly detection within an unsupervised framework by introducing LSTM-based algorithms. The performance of various algorithms is evaluated using ROC curves and AUC scores across different datasets, including a digit dataset and a financial dataset (Alcoa stock price). 

In the digit dataset, where digits "0" and "9" are treated as normal and anomalies respectively, and "1" and "7" in another experiment, the LSTM-based algorithms consistently outperform traditional SVM and SVDD methods. For instance, in the first experiment, the AUC scores for LSTM-QPSVM and LSTM-GSVM are notably higher than their SVM counterparts, with GRU-QPSVDD achieving the highest AUC score. 

Similarly, in the financial dataset, LSTM and GRU algorithms demonstrate significantly better performance due to their memory structures. The results indicate that while LSTM algorithms generally yield high AUC scores, GRU-QPSVDD achieves the best results. The paper concludes that the quadratic programming based training methods tend to perform better in some scenarios, but gradient-based methods often show superior learning capabilities.

Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of LSTM-based approaches for anomaly detection, underscoring the advantages of incorporating advanced neural network architectures into traditional machine learning frameworks.
