The paper presents a novel approach to anomaly detection using a combined framework of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and One-Class Support Vector Machines (OC-SVM) or Support Vector Data Description (SVDD). The authors propose an architecture that processes variable-length data sequences and jointly optimizes the parameters of both the LSTM structure and the scoring function derived from OC-SVM or SVDD. This optimization is achieved through gradient and quadratic programming methods, extending the algorithms to semi-supervised and fully supervised settings.

The effectiveness of the proposed algorithms is demonstrated through extensive experiments across various real and simulated datasets, showing substantial performance improvements over conventional methods. The results are summarized in Table I, which lists the Area Under the Curve (AUC) scores for different algorithms applied to datasets including occupancy, HKE rate, HTTP requests, and Alcoa stock prices, highlighting the superior performance of the proposed LSTM-based approaches compared to standard SVM and SVDD methods.

Overall, the paper contributes to the field of anomaly detection by providing a robust framework capable of handling time series data, thus addressing a significant challenge in the analysis of variable-length sequences.
