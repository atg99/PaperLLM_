In the semi-supervised framework for Support Vector Data Description (SVDD) based algorithms, an optimization problem is posed to minimize a cost function that includes terms for both labeled and unlabeled data. The formulation involves minimizing the function F defined by trade-off parameters C1, C2, and C3, which balance the influence of different components in the objective function. Constraints ensure that the distances from data points to a center ~c are within a specified radius R, and slack variables are introduced to handle non-conformities in the data.

The paper outlines modifications to previous quadratic programming and gradient-based training methods to accommodate this semi-supervised approach. It also establishes uniqueness and global optimality of the solutions for the center ~c and radius R through a positive definite Hessian matrix, demonstrating that the function F is strictly convex with respect to these parameters.

The algorithm, referred to as Algorithm 4, details a gradient-based training method for anomaly detection using SVDD, initialized with parameters and iteratively updated until convergence is achieved. The performance of these algorithms is evaluated on various datasets, including variable length data sequences, where the proposed methods outperform traditional OC-SVM and SVDD approaches.

Lastly, the paper includes experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of the introduced algorithms across different datasets, comparing LSTM-based and GRU-based models for anomaly detection, showcasing their capability to handle variable-length sequences efficiently.
