The proposed framework introduces a zigzag recurrent transition mechanism called spatiotemporal memory flow, enhancing the flow of information across the network layers. This mechanism allows memory states to be updated not only in the temporal direction but also across different spatial representations, addressing the challenge of limited interaction between layers in traditional ConvLSTM architectures.

In addressing the second challenge, the Spatiotemporal LSTM (ST-LSTM) is introduced, featuring two decoupled memory cells that separately manage long-term and short-term dynamics. This decoupling facilitates a more robust modeling of complex spatiotemporal variations, improving the overall performance of the network.

For the third challenge, the training procedure is refined to allow the predictive network to learn from non-Markovian dynamics, which are characterized by dependencies that extend beyond immediate previous observations. This adjustment aims to enhance the model's predictive capabilities for long-term forecasting.

Additionally, the action-conditioned PredRNN variant is proposed, enabling the model to simulate environmental changes in response to agent actions, thus broadening its applicability in decision-making scenarios.

In summary, the paper presents a novel approach to video prediction through the development of the PredRNN framework, which combines spatiotemporal memory flow, decoupled memory cells, and an improved training methodology to effectively model and predict complex dynamics in visual data.
