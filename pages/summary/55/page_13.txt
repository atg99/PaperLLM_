This paper evaluates various predictive models on real-world datasets, specifically focusing on traffic forecasting and robotic manipulation tasks. The authors utilize the Critical Success Index (CSI) to assess the performance of radar map predictions, with PredRNN consistently achieving superior results across multiple thresholds. 

For the Traffic4Cast dataset, the authors implement an autoencoder architecture with U-Net, integrating both traffic flow maps and spatiotemporal memory states. They find that using ST-LSTM enhances the predictive capabilities of both U-Net and CrevNet models, leading to improved performance metrics such as Mean Squared Error (MSE).

In the action-conditioned BAIR robot pushing dataset, the authors compare their action-conditioned PredRNN with existing models like SVG and SV2P. They note that incorporating action information significantly boosts performance, particularly in long-term predictions. The results indicate that the action-conditioned models outperform their action-free counterparts, underscoring the importance of observable dynamics in generating accurate future images.

Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of memory decoupling and reverse scheduled sampling in enhancing model performance, particularly in scenarios with complex and rapidly changing data. The paper concludes that integrating action information into predictive networks offers a robust approach to improve forecasting accuracy in both traffic and robotic applications.
