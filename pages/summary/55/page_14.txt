Our experiments on the BAIR, KTH, and radar datasets demonstrate that PredRNN-V2 significantly outperforms traditional action-conditioned ConvLSTM networks, particularly in scenarios with clear visual structures. The model excels at predicting future positions of dynamic elements, such as a robot arm in the BAIR dataset, while also enriching the details of generated images.

However, we acknowledge limitations in handling datasets with long-tail distributions, as seen in the radar echo dataset, where the model struggles to accurately capture dynamics of sparse high-intensity areas. This highlights the need for future research to integrate complex physical properties with deep learning approaches to better address such challenges.

In conclusion, our work introduces a novel framework for spatiotemporal predictive learning, leveraging the strengths of the Spatiotemporal LSTM architecture to enhance video prediction tasks, while also identifying areas for further improvement in model robustness against diverse data distributions.
