This paper discusses a novel training framework for sequence forecasting using a technique called reverse scheduled sampling (RSS) in conjunction with traditional scheduled sampling. The main objective is to bridge the training-inference gap and enhance the model's ability to learn long-term dynamics from historical data.

The model operates in two phases: encoding and forecasting. During the encoding phase, the model primarily relies on the true previous frame, X, to make one-step predictions. However, in the forecasting phase, it uses the previous prediction, bX, which lacks new information. This inconsistency can hinder effective optimization and the model's ability to learn non-Markovian properties.

To address this, the authors introduce RSS, which gradually shifts the input from the previous prediction to the true frame during training. The RSS strategy employs a curriculum learning approach, where the sampling probability of true frames increases over training iterations. Various functions, such as linear, exponential, and sigmoid, are proposed to control this increase.

The training scheme allows the encoder to start with a challenging task of predicting multiple future frames and progressively simplifies to one-step predictions, mirroring the test conditions. This approach encourages the model to capture long-term dynamics more effectively.

The paper also presents two strategies for combining RSS with original scheduled sampling, highlighting empirical findings that indicate better performance when the sampling probabilities of the encoder and forecaster are aligned in the early training stages.

Finally, the authors evaluate their approach using five spatiotemporal prediction datasets, underscoring the versatility of the RSS training strategy, which can be applied to various sequence-to-sequence models beyond PredRNN. The source code for the implementation is made available for further exploration.
