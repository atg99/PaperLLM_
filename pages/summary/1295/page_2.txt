The paper presents a masked autoencoder (MAE) approach for visual representation learning, demonstrating its effectiveness through qualitative results on ImageNet and COCO validation images. The MAE operates by masking a high percentage of random patches (up to 80%) from input images, allowing the model to focus on reconstructing the missing patches, which encourages a holistic understanding of the images rather than relying solely on low-level statistics.

The architecture features an asymmetric encoder-decoder design, where the encoder processes only the visible patches, and the lightweight decoder reconstructs the input from the latent representation. This design significantly reduces computational requirements, enabling the model to maintain high masking ratios while optimizing accuracy and reducing pre-training time and memory usage.

Experimental results indicate that the MAE achieves superior generalization performance on ImageNet-1K, with a vanilla ViT-Huge model reaching an accuracy of 87.8%, surpassing previous benchmarks using only ImageNet-1K data. Furthermore, the MAE demonstrates improved performance in transfer learning tasks, such as object detection and segmentation, compared to traditional supervised pre-training methods. Overall, the findings suggest that the MAE approach facilitates the development of high-capacity models that generalize well across various tasks, reminiscent of trends observed in self-supervised pre-training in natural language processing.
