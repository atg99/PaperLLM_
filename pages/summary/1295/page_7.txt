This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of self-supervised Vision Transformer (ViT) models, focusing on the MAE (Masked Autoencoder) method and its performance compared to existing approaches. 

In Table 3, the results show the accuracy of different ViT models (ViT-B, ViT-L, ViT-H) pre-trained on the ImageNet-1K dataset. The MAE method outperforms other self-supervised methods, achieving 86.9% accuracy with ViT-H at a 224 image size and 87.8% with an enlarged size of 448. This marks a significant improvement over previous state-of-the-art results, which reached 87.1% accuracy, thus demonstrating the effectiveness of MAE in scaling model sizes.

The paper highlights that while ViT-B models show close performance across various methods, larger models like ViT-L face challenges with overfitting, indicating that larger architectures require more careful tuning. The MAE method's ability to reconstruct normalized pixels rather than predicting tokens, as done by BEiT, contributes to its superior performance and efficiency, taking less time to train while yielding better results.

Figure 8 compares MAE pre-training with supervised pre-training, indicating that MAE generalizes better than traditional supervised approaches, especially for higher-capacity models. 

The authors also explore partial fine-tuning strategies, revealing that fine-tuning a small number of Transformer blocks significantly improves accuracy compared to linear probing. While MoCo v3 shows higher linear probing accuracy, MAE consistently surpasses it in partial fine-tuning scenarios, suggesting that MAE captures stronger non-linear features.

Overall, the findings position MAE as a robust self-supervised learning method that not only enhances performance on the ImageNet-1K benchmark but also demonstrates efficiency in training and generalization capabilities across various ViT architectures.
