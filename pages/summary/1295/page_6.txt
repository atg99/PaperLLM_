The paper discusses various aspects of Masked Autoencoders (MAE) focusing on mask sampling strategies and their impact on pretext task difficulty, reconstruction quality, and representation accuracy. Three masking strategies are analyzed: random sampling, block-wise sampling, and grid-wise sampling. Random sampling, which is the default, yields the best performance, achieving higher accuracy and sharper reconstructions compared to the other methods. Block-wise sampling, while effective at a 50% masking ratio, shows degraded performance at 75%, leading to blurrier outputs. Grid-wise sampling results in easier reconstruction tasks but sacrifices representation quality.

The paper also examines the influence of reconstruction targets, revealing that using pixel normalization enhances accuracy, while PCA-based targets degrade performance, indicating the importance of high-frequency components. A comparison with BEiT’s token prediction reveals that while tokenization improves fine-tuning accuracy slightly, it does not outperform normalized pixels and incurs additional complexity due to pre-training requirements.

Data augmentation’s role is further explored, demonstrating that MAE performs adequately with minimal augmentation, unlike contrastive learning methods, which heavily depend on it. The MAE's reliance on random masking generates diverse training samples, reducing the necessity for extensive data augmentation.

Lastly, the paper highlights the importance of training schedules, showing that longer training durations consistently improve accuracy, with no signs of saturation even at 1600 epochs. This contrasts with contrastive learning methods that reach saturation much earlier. Overall, the findings emphasize the advantages of the MAE framework in terms of simplicity, efficiency, and robustness in representation learning.
