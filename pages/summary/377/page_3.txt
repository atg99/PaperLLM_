The paper discusses the evolution of research on dependency probing in pre-trained transformer models (PTMs) like BERT and RoBERTa, highlighting the methods used to investigate the linguistic knowledge encoded within these models. Previous approaches, such as those by Clark et al. and Hewitt and Manning, faced challenges in isolating the inherent syntactic knowledge from the additional parameters introduced in their probing models. In contrast, Wu et al. proposed a parameter-free method called Perturbed Masking, which effectively identifies syntactic relationships directly from the representations produced by BERT and RoBERTa.

The section on inducing tree structures outlines the methodology for extracting dependency trees from PTMs, focusing on the Perturbed Masking technique. This method operates by masking tokens in a sentence and evaluating the impact of one token on another through Euclidean distance calculations between their contextualized representations. The resultant impact matrix enables the application of tree decoding algorithms to derive a syntactic structure, thus providing insights into the linguistic capabilities of the underlying models without the complications of additional parameters. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the advancements in understanding how PTMs encode syntactic information and the methodologies that facilitate the extraction of this knowledge in a structured manner.
