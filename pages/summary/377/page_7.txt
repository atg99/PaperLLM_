The paper discusses the analysis of different tree structures induced by various models, including dependency parser, RoBERTa, and fine-tuned RoBERTa (FT-RoBERTa). It highlights the differences in dependency patterns and the implications of fine-tuning on tree structures. The visualization in Figure 2 illustrates how the trees from RoBERTa exhibit more neighboring connections, while FT-RoBERTa demonstrates a more diverse dependency structure, particularly linking sentiment words with other words.

Moreover, the paper introduces the concept of Aspects-sentiment Distance (AsD), which measures the average distance between aspect words and sentiment words within sentences. The methodology for calculating AsD is detailed, using predefined sentiment word sets and the relative distances between words in the induced trees. 

Table 4 presents the AsD values across different datasets, revealing that FT-RoBERTa displays the shortest distance between aspects and sentiment words among the compared models. This indicates that fine-tuning leads to a more concise relationship between aspects and sentiments, contrasting with the patterns observed in the original pre-trained models. Overall, the findings underscore the structural changes resulting from the fine-tuning process and its impact on the semantic relationships within the trees.
