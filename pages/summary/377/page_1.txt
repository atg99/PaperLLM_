The paper "Does syntax matter? A strong baseline for Aspect-based Sentiment Analysis with RoBERTa" investigates the role of syntactic information in Aspect-based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA) by comparing traditional dependency parsing with the syntactic structures induced by pre-trained models (PTMs), specifically fine-tuned RoBERTa (FT-RoBERTa). The authors highlight that while previous ABSA approaches have benefited from explicit syntactic features, recent advancements in PTMs raise the question of whether these models can sufficiently capture the necessary syntactic information for effective sentiment analysis.

The study demonstrates that the syntactic trees generated from FT-RoBERTa outperform those produced by traditional parsers. The induced trees are found to be more focused on sentiment-related words, enhancing the model's ability to predict sentiment polarities for various aspects. Furthermore, the research shows that a model based solely on RoBERTa can achieve or closely approach state-of-the-art performance across multiple datasets in four different languages, indicating that the syntactic information required for ABSA can be implicitly captured by PTMs.

The paper emphasizes the potential of RoBERTa-based models in simplifying the ABSA task by reducing reliance on manually crafted syntactic features while still achieving high performance. The findings suggest that the integration of PTMs like RoBERTa into ABSA can serve as a strong baseline, offering insights into the importance of syntax in sentiment analysis tasks.
