This paper investigates the challenges in optimizing RoBERTa-based ALSC models, particularly regarding hyperparameter settings that may hinder performance compared to previous efforts. 

The study explores various tree structures for ALSC, including dependency trees derived from parsers and trees induced from PTMs using a Perturbed Masking method. The experiments conducted across six datasets in four languages demonstrate that fine-tuning PTMs for ALSC tasks encourages the models to learn sentiment-oriented tree structures, which enhances the performance of GloVe-based ALSC models. Notably, fine-tuning RoBERTa with a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) achieves state-of-the-art results for the ALSC task, leveraging its superior implicit syntactic understanding.

The findings suggest promising future directions for the development of tree-based models suitable for PTMs and the enhancement of tree-inducing methods.
