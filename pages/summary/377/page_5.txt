In the experiments, different trees were assessed, revealing that the FT-RoBERTa Induced Tree outperformed traditional dependency trees by an average of 1.1% in accuracy. Both BERT and RoBERTa Induced Trees showed similar performance patterns, closely aligning with the "Left-chain" and "Right-chain" methods. This behavior suggests that the masked language modeling pre-training influences the dependency structure, favoring neighboring word relationships.

The findings also indicate that while dependency trees generally yield better performance compared to PTMs induced trees, the fine-tuned RoBERTa trees can surpass both types of trees in specific scenarios. This highlights the potential of fine-tuning and the impact of the induced tree structures on model performance. Further analysis is warranted to explore these dynamics and their implications for future research in the field.
