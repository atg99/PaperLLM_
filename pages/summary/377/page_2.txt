The paper addresses two primary questions regarding the performance of trees induced from pre-trained transformers (PTMs) in aspect-based sentiment classification (ALSC). 

The first question investigates whether trees derived from PTMs yield better performance compared to those provided by dependency parsers when combined with different tree-based ALSC models. To explore this, the study selects models from three typical dependency tree-based methods and compares their performances using both parser-provided trees and PTM-induced trees.

The second question examines whether PTMs adapt the implicitly entailed tree structure to the ALSC task during fine-tuning. The authors employ trees induced from both off-the-shelf PTMs and fine-tuned PTMs (FT-PTMs) to enhance ALSC models. Results indicate that trees from FT-PTMs significantly improve model performance, with those using trees from fine-tuned RoBERTa surpassing those from dependency parsers.

The findings reveal that a basic RoBERTa model with an MLP layer achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) or near SOTA performance across six ALSC datasets in four languages. Interestingly, incorporating tree structures into RoBERTa-based ALSC models does not lead to notable improvements.

The contributions of the paper can be summarized as follows:
1. Extensive analysis of induced trees from PTMs and FT-PTMs demonstrates that models utilizing FT-PTM trees perform better, particularly when using trees from fine-tuned RoBERTa.
2. The induced trees from FT-PTMs tend to be sentiment-word-oriented, directly linking aspect terms to their sentiment adjectives.
3. The study achieves SOTA or near SOTA performances on multiple ALSC datasets, highlighting RoBERTa's ability to adapt effectively to ALSC tasks.

Additionally, the paper reviews related work in ALSC, noting early models that did not rely on dependency trees and those that integrated dependency structures, emphasizing advancements in neural networks and graph neural networks that have improved parsing quality and performance in ALSC tasks.
