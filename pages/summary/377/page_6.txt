The paper presents a comparative analysis of various tree-based models for the task of aspect-level sentiment classification (ALSC) across three English datasets: Rest14, Laptop14, and Twitter. The performance metrics used are accuracy (Acc.) and macro F1 score (F1), with results averaged over three runs to ensure reliability.

Key findings indicate that models utilizing the FT-RoBERTa Induced Tree consistently outperform others, achieving the highest average accuracy improvements of 1.56% compared to RoBERTa Induced Tree. Other notable models include FT-BERT Induced Tree and various configurations of ASGCN and RGAT, which also demonstrate competitive performance.

The analysis includes an investigation of the Proportion of Neighboring Connections among different tree structures, revealing that BERT and RoBERTa Induced Trees contain a significant number of neighboring connections (over 70%). This high proportion may negatively impact model performance when relying on topological structures, suggesting that models based on dependency trees (Dep.) perform better.

Overall, the results emphasize the effectiveness of fine-tuned transformer models in shaping tree structures for sentiment classification tasks, with a particular focus on the advantages of FT-RoBERTa. The paper highlights the importance of tree structure choice in enhancing model performance and informs future research directions in the field.
