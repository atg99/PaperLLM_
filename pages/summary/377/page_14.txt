The paper discusses the performance of various models, particularly focusing on the FT-RoBERTa Induced Tree and its stability in F1 scores compared to other models. It highlights that the quality of non-English language parsers affects the performance of models using PLM-induced trees, indicating sensitivity to the parser and corpus quality. The findings suggest that fine-tuning can significantly improve the performance of ALSC, particularly through trees induced from PLMs. 

Additionally, a comparison of the fine-tuned XLM-R model with other high-performing models shows that XLM-R combined with a multi-layer perceptron achieves state-of-the-art or near state-of-the-art results in non-English datasets. 

The paper also includes a predefined set of sentiment words used in the analysis, derived from Tian et al. (2020), which details 50 frequent sentiment words from Amazon-2 to calculate the Aspects-sentiment Distance across different tree structures in English datasets. This sentiment word set includes both positive and negative terms, contributing to the overall sentiment analysis framework.
