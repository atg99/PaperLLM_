The paper discusses advanced tree-based Aspect-Level Sentiment Classification (ALSC) models that utilize dependency trees to enhance sentiment analysis in text. Three specific models are highlighted: 

1. **Aspect-specific Graph Convolutional Networks (ASGCN)**: This model treats words as nodes and dependencies as edges in a graph, leveraging Graph Convolutional Networks to capture word relationships.

2. **Proximity-Weighted Convolution Network (PWCN)**: PWCN assigns proximity values based on the shortest path in the dependency tree between words and aspects, helping to identify relevant contextual words for aspects.

3. **Relational Graph Attention Network (RGAT)**: RGAT converts the dependency tree into an aspect-oriented structure, using the aspect as the root and focusing on syntactic relationships and tree-based distances for word relations.

The paper also outlines the experimental setup, including the use of six benchmark datasets, three of which are in English and cover sentiments in restaurant, laptop, and Twitter contexts. The authors detail their methodology for constructing tree structures from various sources, including dependency tree parsers and pre-trained BERT and RoBERTa models. Data statistics are provided, emphasizing the removal of samples with conflicting polarities or "NULL" aspects for clarity and accuracy in the analyses.
