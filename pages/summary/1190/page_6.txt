The paper presents an algorithm for label propagation in deep semi-supervised learning (SSL) called LPDSSL. The procedure begins with initializing model parameters and optimizing a loss function with labeled training examples. The algorithm then extracts feature descriptors for all examples and computes affinity values to create a symmetric affinity matrix, which is symmetrically normalized. A diffusion process is performed to solve for a matrix Z, which guides predictions for unlabeled examples based on their proximity to labeled examples.

The core idea is to encourage all examples to align their predictions with those obtained through transductive learning, minimizing a quadratic cost function. This approach allows the algorithm to efficiently drive predictions toward a solution as if all examples were labeled, without the need for extensive iterations through mini-batches.

The paper details experiments conducted on three image classification datasets: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Mini-ImageNet. Each dataset is utilized in an SSL context, with varying numbers of labeled images. The results demonstrate the performance of the proposed method, comparing it against state-of-the-art techniques and analyzing the impact of different components.

In terms of implementation, the authors use a PyTorch framework based on the Mean Teacher method, ensuring consistency in the reproduction of fully supervised and semi-supervised baselines. They describe the networks employed, including a 13-layer network for CIFAR datasets and ResNet-18 for Mini-ImageNet, along with specific training procedures and hyper-parameters adapted from prior work. The study concludes by reporting mean error rates and standard deviations across multiple dataset splits to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method.
