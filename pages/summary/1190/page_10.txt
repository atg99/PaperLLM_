The references provided discuss various approaches to visual representation learning and semi-supervised learning. Wang et al. explore transitive invariance in self-supervised visual representation learning, highlighting the importance of this property for improving model performance. Weston et al. present a method for deep learning through semi-supervised embedding, emphasizing the integration of labeled and unlabeled data to enhance learning outcomes. Wu et al. focus on unsupervised feature learning through non-parametric instance-level discrimination, showcasing a technique that does not rely on labeled data. Zhou et al. investigate the concept of learning with both local and global consistency, proposing a framework that balances these aspects for improved learning efficacy. Zhu et al. contribute to the field of semi-supervised learning by examining the use of graphs, presenting a PhD thesis that delves into the relationship between graph structures and learning processes. Additionally, they discuss the transition from Gaussian fields to Gaussian processes in semi-supervised learning, providing insights into the theoretical underpinnings of these methods. Collectively, these works advance the understanding of how various learning paradigms can be effectively applied to visual and semi-supervised learning contexts.
