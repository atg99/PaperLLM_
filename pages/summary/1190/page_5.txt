The paper presents a two-phase approach for training a neural network using both labeled and unlabeled data through a process of label propagation. In Phase 1, the network is initially trained for T epochs using only labeled examples, followed by a single epoch with both labeled and unlabeled examples. Descriptors are extracted from the training set, and a nearest neighbor graph is constructed to compute an affinity matrix. This matrix is then symmetrized and normalized to facilitate label propagation.

In Phase 2, the process iterates T' times, where labels are propagated from labeled to unlabeled examples using a linear system to assign pseudo-labels based on prediction certainty and class population. The network is retrained on the entire dataset, utilizing a weighted loss function that accounts for true and pseudo-labels. The paper also discusses the potential for combining this approach with unsupervised loss methods, suggesting that adding unsupervised loss terms can enhance the overall training process.

The methodology is illustrated with a toy example demonstrating the effectiveness of label propagation with varying numbers of labeled examples. The discussion highlights the inefficiencies of using traditional unsupervised loss terms due to the sparsity of the adjacency matrix, advocating for the proposed method's efficiency in leveraging neighborhood consistency for training the network.
