This paper presents a method for semi-supervised learning (SSL) that minimizes a quadratic cost function to optimize label propagation within a nearest neighbor graph framework. The cost function includes terms that promote smoothness in predictions and maintain accuracy for labeled examples. The approach begins with constructing a nearest neighbor graph and performing label propagation through an iterative process. 

Key components include the construction of a sparse affinity matrix, which captures relationships between data points efficiently, and the use of the conjugate gradient method to solve a linear system for estimating labels, improving computational efficiency for large datasets. 

The paper addresses two challenges: the uncertainty in pseudo-labels and class imbalance. It introduces weights for pseudo-labels based on prediction certainty, leveraging entropy as a measure of uncertainty. Additionally, it assigns class-specific weights inversely proportional to class population to mitigate imbalance issues. 

The learning process is iterative, starting with a supervised training phase on labeled examples, followed by the integration of pseudo-labels into the training loop. The proposed method aims to enhance learning by combining the strengths of labeled and pseudo-labeled data while ensuring effective label propagation and weight adjustment.
