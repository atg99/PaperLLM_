The paper evaluates the effectiveness of pseudo-labeling techniques on the CIFAR-10 dataset, particularly focusing on the use of weights for unlabeled images and class weights in the training process. The study compares different approaches, including diffusion predictions and network predictions, assessing their impact on classification performance.

The results, as illustrated in Table 1, indicate that incorporating weights ωi and class weights ζj positively influences the error rates, with the best performance achieved through a combination of both weights and diffusion predictions. The accuracy of pseudo-labels over epochs is visualized, highlighting the differences between diffusion and network predictions.

Figures further demonstrate the distribution of weights for unlabeled images at different training epochs, showcasing the effectiveness of the weights in distinguishing correct and incorrect pseudo-labels. An ablation study reveals that both ωi and ζj contribute positively to the model's performance, emphasizing the advantage of using diffusion predictions over other methods.

The paper concludes that their approach, which integrates pseudo-labeling with diffusion techniques, significantly enhances classification accuracy with minimal labeled data, outperforming fully supervised and other semi-supervised methods.
