This paper explores the use of efficient transductive label propagation to infer pseudo-labels for unlabeled data in the context of semi-supervised learning (SSL) for image classification. The authors argue that leveraging a powerful classifier trained on labeled data can yield high-quality pseudo-labels, allowing for effective learning from large-scale, unlabeled datasets. The approach, referred to as omni-supervised learning, posits that the performance on the labeled subset serves as a lower bound for overall efficacy.

The proposed method consists of two alternating steps: first, training the classifier using both labeled and pseudo-labeled data; second, constructing a nearest neighbor graph based on the embeddings from the previous step to propagate labels and infer certainty scores for unlabeled images. The paper demonstrates through experiments on standard datasets that this method outperforms existing semi-supervised approaches, particularly when fewer labeled samples are available.

The related work section discusses the landscape of SSL, categorizing existing methods into those that add unsupervised loss terms and those that utilize pseudo-labeling. The authors emphasize their contribution as belonging to the latter category and highlight the compatibility of their method with existing techniques. They also review various approaches to unsupervised loss, consistency loss, and pseudo-labeling, noting that their approach distinguishes itself by employing label propagation instead of relying solely on network predictions.

In summary, the paper presents a novel application of label propagation within a transductive learning framework, enhancing the classification performance by effectively utilizing both labeled and unlabeled data.
