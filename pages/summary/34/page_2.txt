This paper discusses the use of digital imaging and deep learning techniques in the analysis and detection of lung cancer through medical imaging modalities, particularly focusing on computed tomography (CT) scans. Medical images are stored in DICOM format, facilitating both quantitative and qualitative analysis. Various imaging techniques, including CT, X-ray angiography, and mammography, are employed for accurate disease diagnosis. The study highlights the challenges faced by medical experts in manually analyzing CT scans and emphasizes the benefits of low-dose CT scans in reducing lung cancer mortality rates.

The authors present a framework utilizing deep learning, specifically a Modified AlexNet (MAN) model, to detect lung cancer and pneumonia abnormalities from X-ray images, achieving an accuracy of 97.27% when compared to other models such as ResNet50 and VGG variants. Additionally, another deep convolutional neural network based on AlexNet is discussed, which automatically extracts features from CT images, attaining 99% training precision and 97% validation accuracy in classifying lung nodules.

The paper also examines various optimization algorithms such as RMSprop, Adam, and SGD in conjunction with convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for improved lung cancer detection. The structure of the paper is organized into sections covering related works, materials and methods, experimental results, discussions, and future directions, highlighting the evolution of CNN applications in medical imaging since the late 1980s. The research underscores the potential of deep learning in enhancing diagnostic accuracy and efficiency in lung cancer detection through advanced imaging techniques.
