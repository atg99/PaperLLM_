The paper presents a comparative analysis of the performance of different convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures—ResNet 50, Inception-V1, and AlexNet—using various optimizers, particularly focusing on the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) optimizer. 

For the ResNet 50 architecture, the SGD optimizer yielded the highest accuracy at 96.35%, surpassing the performance of both the RMSprop and Adam optimizers. The model's statistical metrics included a sensitivity of 95.56%, specificity of 97.25%, positive predictive value (PPV) of 97.53%, negative predictive value (NPV) of 95.07%, false omission rate (FOR) of 4.93%, and an F1-score of 96.54%.

Similarly, Inception-V1 achieved an accuracy of 95.06% with the SGD optimizer, outperforming the other optimizers as well. Its sensitivity was 93.55%, specificity was 96.79%, PPV was 97.07%, NPV was 92.95%, FOR was 7.05%, and the F1-score was 95.28%.

The analysis of AlexNet also showed varied results but indicated instances of misclassification, demonstrating the model's limitations. The results highlighted the effectiveness of the SGD optimizer across different architectures, particularly in enhancing overall accuracy and other performance metrics, while also identifying challenges in consistent classification outcomes. 

Overall, the findings underscore the potential of using SGD in improving CNN performance in image classification tasks, while also indicating areas for further investigation, especially in refining model predictions.
