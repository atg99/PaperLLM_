This study investigates the performance of various convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures—LeNet, AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet-50, and Inception-V1—in detecting lung cancer using the LUNA16 dataset. The research employs different optimizers, specifically Adam, RMSprop, and SGD, to analyze the effectiveness of these models. The CNNs are trained with a learning rate of 0.001, a batch size of 20, and for 200 epochs, using binary cross-entropy as the loss function.

The evaluation of model performance includes key statistical parameters such as accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), false omission rate (FOR), and F1 score. The results show that LeNet with the SGD optimizer achieved the highest accuracy at 95.92%, outperforming the other optimizers. The detailed validation performance for LeNet with SGD includes sensitivity at 94.76%, specificity at 97.25%, PPV at 97.51%, NPV at 94.22%, FOR at 5.78%, and an F1 score of 96.11%. The study presents a confusion matrix for the LeNet architecture, highlighting the model's classification performance.

Overall, the findings emphasize the effectiveness of CNN architectures and the influence of different optimizers in the accurate detection of lung cancer.
