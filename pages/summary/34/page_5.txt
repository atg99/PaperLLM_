This paper focuses on the automatic detection of lung cancer through the application of various convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures, building on previous work in the field. It highlights limitations in earlier studies, such as the need for deep knowledge to extract handcrafted features and challenges related to imbalanced datasets and model complexity. A literature survey is presented, summarizing the accuracy and weaknesses of different computational intelligence-based lung cancer detection methods.

Key contributions of this work include a performance-oriented analysis that combines deep learning algorithms with various optimizers for lung cancer classification. The study implements CNN architectures using Adam, SGD, and RMSprop optimizers on the LUNA16 dataset. Notably, the AlexNet architecture with the SGD optimizer yielded the highest accuracy, outperforming other existing techniques for lung cancer classification.
