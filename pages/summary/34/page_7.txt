The paper discusses various adaptive learning methods used in optimization algorithms, specifically focusing on techniques such as Root Mean Square Propagation (RMSprop), Adaptive Moment Estimation (Adam), and Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD).

RMSprop addresses issues with learning rates by calculating them adaptively through an exponentially weighted average of gradients. The key equations include the update for the learning rate and the model weights. The method adjusts the learning rate dynamically, enhancing convergence stability.

Adam combines RMSprop and momentum-based gradient descent to compute adaptive learning rates for each parameter. It maintains two moving averages—one for the gradients and another for the squared gradients—allowing for efficient computation with minimal memory use. The equations illustrate how parameters are updated based on these averages.

SGD, on the other hand, updates model parameters one at a time, resulting in faster performance. Although this method can lead to significant fluctuations in the cost function, it facilitates quick convergence to the global minimum and is capable of handling large datasets effectively.

Overall, these methods aim to optimize the learning process by adjusting learning rates and efficiently managing parameter updates, which is crucial for training complex models.
