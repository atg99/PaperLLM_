This study evaluates the performance of various state-of-the-art convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures for classifying lung cancer as benign or malignant. Five CNN models—LeNet, AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet-50, and Inception-V1—were analyzed using different optimizers including Adam, RMSprop, and SGD. The results indicated that AlexNet, when optimized with SGD, achieved the highest accuracy at 95.73%, surpassing other architectures and optimizers.

The authors acknowledge limitations in their study and propose future work to enhance the classification system. They suggest exploring additional CNN architectures such as Darknet, EfficientNet, VGG19, Xception, and various Inception models, along with the implementation of different optimizers and cross-validation techniques to mitigate randomness and improve accuracy.

The contributions of the authors are detailed, highlighting data collection, analysis, writing, and revisions. The research did not receive external funding, and there are no conflicts of interest reported. The simulation data supporting the findings are available upon request from the corresponding author.
