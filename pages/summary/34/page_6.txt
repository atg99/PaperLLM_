The research emphasizes the importance of early detection of lung cancer, which is critical due to the late appearance of symptoms in patients. The study applied various convolutional neural network architectures, including LeNet, AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet-50, and Inception-V1, using the LUNA16 dataset, which consists of 888 CT scans. The dataset was divided for training and validation using two methods: a random split of 80% for training and 20% for validation, and 5-fold cross-validation.

Key findings revealed that the AlexNet architecture, when combined with the SGD optimizer, achieved the highest accuracy in lung cancer classification compared to other models. The convolution operation was highlighted as crucial for image feature extraction, employing both large-sized and small-sized filters to capture different levels of detail from the images.

Overall, the study underlines the potential of CNNs and specific optimization techniques in improving the accuracy of lung cancer detection, which can significantly impact patient outcomes by enabling earlier diagnosis and treatment.
