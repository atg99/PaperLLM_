The paper discusses various convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures and their effectiveness in classifying lung nodules and detecting lung cancer using CT images. The LeNet-5 model achieved high accuracy rates, with 97.041% for classifying benign and malignant nodules. A hybrid CNN combining LeNet and AlexNet achieved 0.822 accuracy and an area under the curve of 0.877, emphasizing the importance of parameters like kernel size and learning rate. AlexNet, introduced by Krizhevsky et al. in 2012, marked a significant milestone in CNN performance. Subsequent studies utilized AlexNet for lung cancer detection, achieving up to 96% accuracy through image segmentation techniques.

Additionally, researchers proposed straight and hybrid 3D-CNN models, which demonstrated improved performance with accuracies of 91.8%. The CanNet model outperformed traditional methods with a 76% accuracy rate. The Taguchi-based CNN model showed reduced training time while maintaining effectiveness, evaluated on a large dataset of over 245,000 images. A study using AlexNet on a dataset from Iraqi hospitals achieved a notable 93.548% accuracy in classifying CT scans into normal, benign, and malignant categories. Lastly, the effects of VGG16 and VGG19 architectures were explored, highlighting their capability with RGB images in the context of lung cancer classification.
