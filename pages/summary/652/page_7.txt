In this section of the paper, the authors analyze the performance of various graph neural networks (GNNs) under non-targeted adversarial attacks, specifically using the metattack method. The results are presented across three datasets: Cora, Citeseer, and Pubmed. 

The findings reveal that traditional methods like Label Propagation (LP) perform poorly, indicating that structural information is less useful for node classification in disassortative graphs. In contrast, feature information is shown to be more critical, as models like kNN-GCN outperformed others such as GCN, GAT, and JK-Net. Despite attempts to combine structure and feature information, simply merging these components did not yield improvements, as seen with the (A+kNN)-GCN model.

SimP-GCN, however, demonstrates significant improvements in GCN performance, achieving state-of-the-art results across all datasets. The authors highlight the vulnerability of GNNs to adversarial attacks and emphasize the necessity for robust models in safety-critical applications. SimP-GCN's performance is notably enhanced under high perturbation rates, suggesting that when structure information becomes noisy due to adversarial manipulation, the model relies more on node feature similarity, which bolsters robustness.

The authors also explore the preservation of node feature similarity in SimP-GCN. They find that this model not only improves the overlapping percentage of feature and hidden graphs but also effectively retains more information from feature similarity, especially in disassortative graphs. However, it also shows a decrease in overlapping between hidden and original graphs in such contexts.

Overall, the analysis underscores the potential of SimP-GCN in enhancing robustness against adversarial attacks while effectively harnessing feature information in graph-based node classification tasks.
