The paper addresses the over-smoothing problem in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), where learned node embeddings become indistinguishable as the number of layers increases. The authors propose a new graph convolution model named SimP-GCN, which aims to preserve original node similarities more effectively. This approach tackles two main challenges: balancing information from graph structure and node features during aggregation, and capturing complex pairwise feature similarity relations.

To achieve the first challenge, the authors introduce an adaptive strategy that integrates graph structure and node features in a data-driven manner, allowing nodes to adjust the balance of information they use. For the second challenge, a self-supervised learning strategy is employed to predict pairwise feature similarity from hidden representations of node pairs, utilizing similarity prediction as a self-supervised task to encode these relations explicitly.

The contributions of the paper are threefold: it theoretically and empirically demonstrates that GCN can destroy original node feature similarity, proposes the SimP-GCN model that effectively preserves feature similarity while balancing graph structure and node features, and shows through extensive experiments that the framework outperforms existing baselines on various graph types. Furthermore, preserving feature similarity is found to enhance the robustness of GCN against adversarial attacks.

The paper also reviews related work in GNNs, categorizing them into spectral and spatial methods. While GCNs have shown effectiveness in various applications, their performance is hindered on disassortative graphs and when subjected to adversarial attacks. This highlights the necessity for a new GNN that maintains node similarity in the original feature space.

In summary, the proposed SimP-GCN model presents a novel solution to the limitations of existing GNNs, emphasizing the importance of preserving feature similarity and adapting the aggregation of information from graph structures and node features.
