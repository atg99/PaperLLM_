The paper presents a framework for recovering original signals through an objective function that minimizes the difference between the signal and its estimated value while incorporating a regularization term. It establishes that the Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) convolutional operator acts as a one-step gradient descent optimization of this objective function under specific conditions. The proof demonstrates that the GCN operator can lead to feature smoothing, which may destroy original feature similarities, particularly in disassortative graphs where homophily does not apply.

An empirical study is conducted on various graph datasets, contrasting assortative and disassortative graphs. The analysis involves constructing k-nearest-neighbor graphs from original features and learned representations, followed by calculating pairwise overlaps to assess feature similarity preservation. The findings reveal that assortative graphs maintain a higher degree of overlap between feature and structure information compared to disassortative graphs. However, GCN tends to amplify inconsistencies in feature similarity, indicating that it often prioritizes structural similarities over feature similarities.

To address these challenges, the paper proposes the SimP-GCN framework, which includes two main components: node similarity preserving aggregation and self-supervised learning. The aggregation component aims to balance the influence of graph structure and node features, while the self-supervised learning component focuses on preserving node similarities by evaluating both similar and dissimilar pairs. The feature graph construction process leverages cosine similarity to create a k-nearest-neighbor graph for enhancing the aggregation process within GCN.
