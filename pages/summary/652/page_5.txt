In this section, the authors introduce a contrastive self-supervised learning component to the SimP-GCN model to better capture feature dissimilarity among nodes. The existing kNN feature graph primarily focuses on preserving similarity among similar nodes but fails to adequately push dissimilar nodes apart in the learned embedding space. The proposed self-supervised learning technique involves designing a pretext task that predicts pairwise feature similarity, leveraging unlabeled data to enhance node representations.

The authors define a regression-based self-supervised loss function, which computes pairwise similarities for sampled node pairs. This contrasts with a classification approach, which could also predict similarity levels. The overall objective function combines the self-supervised loss with the traditional classification loss, controlled by a hyper-parameter.

The paper also discusses the complexity of the proposed model. While the naive computation of pairwise similarities has a time complexity of O(n²), it can be optimized using parallelization and approximation methods. In terms of model complexity, additional parameters introduced by the SimP-GCN are linear with respect to the feature dimension, indicating efficient scaling.

The effectiveness of SimP-GCN is evaluated through experiments focusing on its performance on assortative and disassortative graphs, its robustness against adversarial attacks, and the impact of different components on its performance. The authors emphasize the importance of node similarity, especially in the context of disassortative graphs and adversarial scenarios.
