The paper discusses a system designed to enhance legal understanding by correlating user-input legal cases with relevant legal concepts through the use of ontologies. The process begins with identifying enriched terms from the input text, allowing the system to connect imprecise language to appropriate legal concepts. These identified concepts are then utilized to retrieve pertinent documents and generate tailored learning paths.

The generation of learning paths involves two main steps: determining a sequence of concepts based on educational relationships and selecting appropriate learning resources. This approach is informed by adaptive learning models, aiming to create a structured educational experience. For example, when a user requests a learning path on "On-line Mediation," the system constructs a concept sequence that adheres to prerequisite relationships and subsequently curates learning resources from an internal repository.

To make the learning experience accessible, especially for those with minimal legal background, the system employs a storytelling approach that presents scenarios covering key concepts. Each scenario includes elements designed to engage prior knowledge, facilitate understanding, and reinforce learning.

The implementation of this system is realized as a web application developed in Java EE 7, which allows users to input legal cases in natural language and select the relevant legal ontology for classification. The results of the knowledge discovery process are displayed, showcasing the concepts linked to the extracted text and related topics. This system aims to bridge the gap between complex legal concepts and laypersons, fostering a better understanding of legal issues.
