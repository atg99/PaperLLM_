The paper discusses a system designed to enhance legal research and learning through a knowledge base that connects Wikipedia topics with legal resources. Users can select concepts related to their queries, and the system provides legal documents such as rules and judgments, complete with hyperlinks to original sources. Additionally, users can access a dynamically generated online course based on their selected topics.

Two experiments were conducted to evaluate the system's efficiency, effectiveness, and usability. The first experiment focused on the efficiency of the semantic search process, comparing the system's performance against a standard search engine using 50 queries that simulated real legal scenarios. The average query involved six concepts, and the system's performance was measured using precision and recall metrics.

The second experiment assessed the usability and perceived effectiveness of the system through user interactions. A diverse group of 35 participants engaged with the system in simulated legal situations, and their feedback was collected via questionnaires. The results of these experiments are detailed in subsequent sections, highlighting the system's search efficiency and user experience.
