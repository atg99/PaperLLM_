The paper discusses a semantic search system designed to improve the retrieval of relevant legal documents, focusing on the micro-averaging of recall and precision. The formulas for recall and precision at each step are outlined, emphasizing the dependence on the quality of the legal ontology and the enrichment mechanism used. Results indicate that the effectiveness of the system increases with the length of the input text query, as more context allows for better understanding and retrieval of meaningful concepts.

To evaluate the system's perceived effectiveness, an experiment was conducted with 35 users from diverse backgrounds. Participants engaged with the system through 15 scenarios related to motor liability, leases, and e-commerce, simulating disputes and utilizing the system for information on conflict resolution. Feedback was collected to test three hypotheses regarding the coherence and suitability of the resources provided by the system.

The users rated various aspects of the system's performance using a Likert scale, with an overall positive evaluation of about 75% for the prototype's effectiveness. The results showed a 72% positive response for the first hypothesis regarding case coherence, and 76% for the second and third hypotheses related to legal ontology and resource suitability. The lowest performance was noted for the first item assessing the coherence of legal concepts, attributed to potential user misunderstanding. Overall, the findings suggest that the semantic search system is effective in supporting users in legal dispute scenarios.
