The paper discusses the evaluation of a Smart Learning system designed for legal education, utilizing Knowledge Discovery and Cognitive Computing techniques. The system received an average System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 73.5, which is above the industry average of 68.0, indicating a positive reception among users. Feedback highlighted the system's intuitiveness and usefulness for both novice and expert users, although some users noted that the generated learning paths did not encompass all relevant topics, reflecting the current limitations of the system's content coverage.

The study assessed various dimensions of the system, including efficiency, effectiveness, and usability. Efficiency was quantitatively measured against a standard search engine, showing superior performance in recall and precision. Effectiveness, gauged through user ratings, yielded a score of 4.05 out of 5, with approximately 75% of users providing positive feedback regardless of demographic factors. The usability score further corroborated the system's favorable evaluation.

The encouraging results suggest that the system has significant potential to enhance user experiences and support legal training. The authors emphasize the importance of user feedback for future improvements, aiming to refine the system to better assist citizens and legal professionals. The work also illustrates the applicability of Cognitive Computing in advancing learning environments and proposes that the methodologies developed could be adapted for other educational domains. The research is supported by the eJRM project, co-funded by the Italian Ministry of Instruction and Research.
