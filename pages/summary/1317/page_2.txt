This paper discusses the development and implementation of the "electronic Justice Relationship Management" (eJRM) system, which aims to enhance knowledge discovery and learning in the legal field. Previous efforts in semantic methodologies and technologies have primarily targeted legal professionals and experts, often leaving learners and the general public without adequate resources. The eJRM project, funded by the Italian Ministry of Education and Research, seeks to bridge this gap by providing an integrated system that supports legal mediation and alternative dispute resolution for both experts and laypersons.

The eJRM system incorporates a storytelling approach to education, leveraging the recognized effectiveness of narrative in teaching legal concepts. This method fosters understanding through realistic scenarios and promotes engagement via role-playing, which is already a common practice in legal education, particularly in Anglo-American contexts.

The paper outlines the system's components, particularly focusing on the Knowledge Discovery and Learning component. This component manages an ontological knowledge base enriched with legal terms and common-sense knowledge sourced from platforms like Wikipedia. The ontology consists of approximately 13,000 interconnected concepts, facilitating a structured learning environment.

Key functionalities of the eJRM system include editing and versioning of the ontologies, enriching them with additional terms, and matching terms across different ontologies. These features are designed to be user-friendly, allowing non-experts to navigate and utilize the system effectively.

The paper also reports on the experimentation conducted with a diverse user base to assess the system's performance and effectiveness. The results indicate both strengths and areas for improvement, which will guide future refinements of the eJRM system as a valuable educational platform for aspiring legal professionals and informed citizens.

In conclusion, the eJRM system represents a significant advancement in the application of semantic technologies in legal education, aiming to make legal knowledge more accessible and comprehensible for a broader audience.
