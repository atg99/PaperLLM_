The paper presents HyGCN, a specialized accelerator designed to optimize the performance of graph convolutional networks (GCNs) through a hybrid architecture. GCNs, which have gained popularity for processing graph data, consist of two main execution phases: the Aggregation phase and the Combination phase. The Aggregation phase is characterized by its dynamic and irregular execution patterns due to the random and sparse nature of graph structures, while the Combination phase exhibits a more static and regular pattern akin to traditional neural networks.

To address the challenges posed by these hybrid execution patterns, the authors first analyze GCNs' behavior on Intel Xeon CPUs. They then propose a new programming model that harnesses fine-grained parallelism, complemented by a hardware design featuring two processing engines. These engines are tailored to mitigate irregularities in the Aggregation phase while capitalizing on the regularity of the Combination phase. Additionally, the design leverages intra-vertex parallelism and efficient data reuse.

The authors further enhance system performance through inter-engine pipelining for phase fusion and priority-based coordination of off-chip memory access, resulting in improved bandwidth utilization. Experimental results demonstrate that HyGCN achieves significant performance improvements compared to current state-of-the-art software frameworks on Intel Xeon CPUs and NVIDIA V100 GPUs, achieving an average speedup of 1509 times and a 2500 times reduction in energy consumption, as well as a 6.5 times speedup with a 10 times reduction in energy usage, respectively.

Overall, the paper highlights the need for novel architectural designs to overcome the unique challenges of GCNs, emphasizing the potential of HyGCN in enabling efficient graph data processing in various applications.
