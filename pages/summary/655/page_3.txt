The paper discusses several typical Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) models and their operations, emphasizing the importance of Aggregation and Combination phases. GCNs bridge spectral and spatial convolution approaches, with their inference model incorporating vertex features and neighborhood information. The models highlighted include GraphSage, which uses uniform neighbor sampling to optimize execution time, and GINConv, which maintains a strong discriminatory power akin to the Weisfeiler-Lehman test.

For graph classification, a Readout function is employed to derive a representation of the entire graph from individual vertex representations. DiffPool is introduced as a method to achieve hierarchical transformations of graphs, allowing for the reduction of vertices and feature vector lengths.

The paper quantitatively characterizes the execution patterns of GCNs using the PyTorch Geometric framework on Intel Xeon CPUs, revealing that both Aggregation and Combination phases significantly contribute to execution time, thus highlighting the need for acceleration in these areas. The motivation for designing a GCN accelerator is grounded in these observations, aiming to enhance performance by addressing the identified bottlenecks in processing GCNs.
