The paper discusses a method for optimizing graph computation through static graph partitioning aimed at enhancing data reuse and reducing redundant accesses in Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). The key concepts include:

1. **Interval-Shard Partitioning**: The graph is partitioned into intervals to facilitate efficient feature access and reduce redundancy.

2. **Window Sliding**: This process involves moving a sliding window over vertex intervals to identify effectual shards. The window slides downward until it encounters an edge, marking positions where effective computations can occur.

3. **Window Shrinking**: To minimize sparsity, the bottom of each recorded window moves upward until it meets an edge, resulting in a dynamic adjustment of window sizes and improving the efficiency of data access.

4. **Algorithms for Aggregation**: Two algorithms are presented: 
   - **Algorithm 2** performs interval-wise aggregation by iterating through intervals and aggregating features.
   - **Algorithm 3** improves upon this by dynamically determining neighbor intervals using the sliding and shrinking methods, which further eliminates redundant data access.

5. **Combination Engine**: The combination operation at each vertex is likened to a neural network and optimized using systolic arrays. These arrays are integrated into modules that can operate independently or cooperatively, enhancing computational efficiency.

The proposed optimizations significantly reduce the amount of data accessed by focusing only on necessary features, particularly in scenarios involving sparsity due to sampling operations in GCNs. This approach contrasts with traditional graph analytics, where vertex features are typically smaller and less complex.
