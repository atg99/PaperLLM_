The paper discusses the limitations of existing architectures in efficiently executing Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). It highlights that CPUs struggle with dynamic and irregular data access patterns during the Aggregation phase, leading to high energy consumption and inefficiencies in parameter reuse. GPUs, while optimized for neural networks, also face challenges with irregularity and data synchronization during the Combination phase.

To address these issues, the authors introduce a GCN accelerator named HyGCN, which employs a hybrid architecture designed for GCN workloads on Intel Xeon CPUs. The proposed solution includes a programming model that abstracts GCN operations into edge-centric aggregation for the Aggregation phase and matrix-vector multiplications (MVMs) for the Combination phase. HyGCN features two specialized processing engines: the Aggregation Engine and the Combination Engine. The Aggregation Engine employs techniques like interval-shard graph partitioning and window sliding-shrinking to enhance data reuse and reduce unnecessary memory accesses. The Combination Engine utilizes multi-granular systolic arrays for parallel MVM execution and parameter reuse.

Additionally, the architecture incorporates a fine-grained inter-engine pipeline and priority-based memory access coordination to optimize the execution of both phases. The authors validate their design through RTL implementation and microarchitectural simulation, demonstrating significant performance improvements over state-of-the-art frameworks, achieving an average speedup of 1509 times and energy reductions of 2500 times compared to existing solutions.

In summary, the paper contributes to the field by characterizing the execution patterns of GCNs, proposing an efficient accelerator architecture, and providing empirical evidence of its effectiveness through detailed evaluations.
