The paper discusses the execution characteristics of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), highlighting the differences in performance across various datasets due to the variable length of feature vectors. It identifies two main phases in GCN execution: Aggregation and Combination. The Aggregation phase is characterized by indirect and irregular memory access patterns, leading to high energy consumption and cache misses, while the Combination phase exhibits direct and regular access patterns, resulting in lower energy usage but significant synchronization overhead.

The authors emphasize the need for a specialized GCN accelerator that can address the inefficiencies observed in both general-purpose processors and existing accelerators. They outline design requirements aimed at improving performance and energy efficiency, such as optimizing for the irregularity of the Aggregation phase and enhancing the regularity and parallelism of the Combination phase. Furthermore, they note that current architectures fail to optimally fuse the execution of these two phases, which limits the potential for efficiency gains.

In conclusion, the paper advocates for the development of a GCN accelerator that can effectively leverage the unique execution characteristics of GCNs, ultimately aiming to enhance their performance and energy efficiency in various applications.
