The paper discusses the various challenges faced in real-world reinforcement learning (RL) applications, emphasizing the need for practical solutions. It highlights issues such as variable environments, safety constraints, and the necessity for interpretable models. The authors reference multiple studies that address these challenges, including deep reinforcement learning techniques for variable socket positions, programmatically interpretable RL, and safe exploration methods using Gaussian processes. They stress the importance of developing machine learning approaches that yield tangible benefits and propose methods like sequential actor-critics and experience replay for real-time RL. Additionally, the concept of action elimination in deep RL is introduced as a way to optimize learning by focusing on relevant actions. Overall, the paper advocates for advancements in RL that are robust, interpretable, and applicable to real-world scenarios.
