The paper discusses the various challenges associated with real-world reinforcement learning (RL), particularly in the context of humanoid tasks that necessitate safety constraints. It identifies safety as encompassing static, kinematic, and dynamic aspects of physical systems. To effectively evaluate algorithm performance, it recommends sampling domain parameters during evaluation episodes and emphasizes the importance of multi-objective rewards, focusing on both distance moved and energy used. Moreover, the paper underscores the necessity for agents to perform well across all perturbations, not just on average.

For explainability, the paper suggests qualitative evaluations by humans to assess the quality of explanations provided by the RL agents. It also highlights the need for real-time inference and introduces artificial delays in actuator responses to simulate real-world conditions.

The authors present a comprehensive set of evaluation metrics to address the challenges in real-world RL, including average return, warm-start performance, safety violations, worst-case performance, and multi-objective rewards. Each metric addresses specific challenges, providing a holistic view of the algorithm's effectiveness in practical applications.

The paper reviews related work, noting that while other researchers have identified challenges specific to robotics and algorithm performance variability, they do not encompass the full range of issues presented. The authors argue that existing literature often overlooks the complexity of real-world RL challenges.

In conclusion, the paper articulates nine distinct challenges that practitioners and researchers must consider when deploying RL in real-world scenarios. It aims to guide both practitioners in applying RL effectively and researchers in developing new algorithms that tackle these multifaceted challenges. The authors call for more comprehensive approaches that address all identified challenges rather than treating them in isolation.
