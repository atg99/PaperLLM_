Many real systems, such as physical machinery and user-interactive systems, lack complete visibility into all operational states, leading to challenges in decision-making. This partial observability is often modeled using partially observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs), where the agent's observations are distinct from the actual states, necessitating a probabilistic observation function.

To address partial observability, researchers have proposed two main strategies: incorporating history into observations and utilizing recurrent networks. For instance, the Deep Q-Network (DQN) approach stacks multiple frames of input to provide context, while recurrent networks allow agents to infer hidden states over time. Additionally, some approaches model the environment as non-stationary, employing meta-learning to adapt policies to changing dynamics.

Real-world systems are typically more stochastic and noisy than simulations, introducing further complexities. To cope with these challenges, various methods have emerged, including the Robust MDP framework, domain randomization, and system identification. Robust MDPs focus on optimizing policies against the worst-case scenario, enhancing robustness to environmental perturbations. Domain randomization involves training agents across diverse environmental variations, while system identification enables agents to adjust their strategies based on real-time assessments of their operating environment.

Evaluating the performance of robust policies is crucial, often done by testing them in perturbed environments and measuring their adaptability to changes. This evaluation can provide insights into the effectiveness of policies in noisy and dynamic settings.

Moreover, reinforcement learning typically emphasizes maximizing a singular reward function, yet many real systems have multi-objective considerations. Often, system owners may not have a precise understanding of the optimization goals, complicating the reward function design. This highlights the need for more nuanced approaches to define and balance multiple objectives in reinforcement learning applications.
