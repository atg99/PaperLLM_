2016), their application in real-world settings is hindered by several challenges. This paper identifies nine specific challenges that must be addressed to effectively implement reinforcement learning (RL) in real-world scenarios. These challenges include:

1. **Training off-line from fixed logs**: RL systems often learn from static datasets rather than dynamically interacting with the environment.
2. **Limited sample learning**: Real-world systems may not provide sufficient data for effective learning.
3. **High-dimensional state and action spaces**: The complexity of real-world systems can lead to large and intricate state and action representations.
4. **Safety constraints**: Real-world applications require that certain actions must not be taken to avoid harmful outcomes.
5. **Partially observable tasks**: Many tasks may involve incomplete information, making decision-making more complex.
6. **Complex reward functions**: Rewards may be multi-faceted or difficult to define, complicating the learning process.
7. **Need for explainable policies**: Stakeholders may require transparency in how RL systems make decisions.
8. **Real-time inference**: Decisions need to be made quickly, matching the control frequency of the system.
9. **Delays in system responses**: Delays in actuators, sensors, or rewards can affect the performance of RL systems.

The authors argue that while there has been some research addressing these challenges individually, there is a gap in developing comprehensive algorithms that tackle all of them simultaneously. They suggest that identifying and addressing these challenges can guide researchers in creating more applicable RL algorithms. Additionally, a modified control suite is presented as a testbed to illustrate these challenges in a practical context. The paper emphasizes the importance of aligning RL research with the realities of real-world systems, which are often stochastic, costly, and complex, contrasting sharply with the idealized conditions of simulated environments.
