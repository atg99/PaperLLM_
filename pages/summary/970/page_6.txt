The paper discusses the challenges associated with deploying reinforcement learning (RL) in real-world systems, particularly focusing on the formulation of reward functions that must balance multiple objectives. It emphasizes the need for a multi-dimensional reward function to capture various sub-goals, such as reducing time-to-target and energy consumption, and highlights the importance of understanding the trade-offs involved. 

A key argument is that policy performance should not be evaluated based solely on average cumulative rewards but should also consider the distribution of rewards across different task instances and user groups. Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) is proposed as a method to assess performance by focusing on specific percentiles of the reward distribution, which can help improve worst-case performance. The paper notes that approaches like Distributional DQN can be adapted to incorporate CVaR objectives.

The paper also reviews existing methods for inferring underlying reward functions from demonstrations, such as inverse reinforcement learning, and discusses the need for scalable solutions in deep reinforcement learning settings. It suggests a multi-objective analysis of returns to better evaluate policies based on individual components of the reward function, enabling stakeholders to make informed decisions about trade-offs.

Additionally, the paper addresses the importance of explainability in RL policies, particularly in human-operated systems. It argues that understanding the intent behind policies is crucial for stakeholder trust and error analysis. Various methods for enhancing explainability, including the use of domain-specific programming languages and model-based planning approaches, are discussed. Ultimately, the paper underscores that effective evaluation and explainability are vital for the successful application of RL in real-world scenarios.
