The paper discusses the challenges faced in real-world reinforcement learning (RL), particularly in environments with large state and action spaces, such as controlling cooling in data centers. Traditional RL algorithms struggle with these complexities, as highlighted by Dulac-Arnold et al. They propose various approaches to mitigate these issues, including action generation and nearest neighbor searches, as well as contextual bandit methods to eliminate irrelevant actions.

A significant concern in RL is ensuring safety during both operation and learning phases. Safety constraints can pertain to the system itself or its environment, necessitating careful consideration to prevent potential harm. The paper discusses the use of Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs) to frame safety as a constrained optimization problem, where policies must maximize cumulative rewards while adhering to predefined safety constraints. However, safety violations are often rare in system logs, complicating the implementation of these constraints.

The paper also contrasts CMDPs with budgeted MDPs, where the constraint levels are not fixed and can be adjusted based on the trade-offs between expected returns and safety. Recent advancements in RL safety include integrating safety layers into networks, which help agents learn tasks without safety violations, and developing policies that may initially violate constraints but ultimately comply.

Additionally, the paper highlights various approaches to ensure safety, such as using Lyapunov functions and probabilistic goal MDPs that balance risk and reward. To evaluate the safety of RL algorithms, the authors suggest counting individual safety violations for each constraint, providing a comprehensive overview of policy behavior concerning safety.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of addressing the challenges of large action spaces and safety in RL to enable effective deployment in real-world scenarios.
