The paper presents the Cross DQN model designed to optimize advertisement allocation in feed systems. Key findings include that Cross DQN (-aux) performs better than Cross DQN (-aux-mcau), confirming the effectiveness of extracting arrangement signals from various channel combinations. Cross DQN also surpasses Cross DQN (-aux) due to batch-level constraints that enhance revenue stability.

The authors conduct hyperparameter sensitivity analysis on three parameters: η, α, and β. Increasing η improves user experience but decreases ad revenue, as fewer ads are inserted. Adjusting α shows that auxiliary loss for batch-level constraint positively influences return and stability, while excessively high values may detract from learning goals. Similarly, increasing β generally leads to higher and more stable rewards, though it requires careful tuning to avoid decline beyond a threshold.

Online A/B testing on the Meituan platform indicates that Cross DQN significantly boosts revenue and user experience, with increases of 12.9%, 10.2%, and 9.1% in ad revenue, fees, and user experience, respectively. Offline experiments yield slightly higher increases, attributed to different data distributions.

The paper concludes with a focus on optimizing user experience for long-term growth and acknowledges the challenges of offline reinforcement learning, such as distribution shifts, which may impact ad allocation. Future research directions include enhancing user experience metrics and addressing these challenges.
