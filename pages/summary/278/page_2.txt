This paper addresses the challenges of traffic flow forecasting by proposing a novel framework called Spatial-Temporal Transformer Networks (STTNs). The authors highlight the limitations of existing methods that often overlook long-range temporal dependencies and fail to capture the dynamic nature of spatial dependencies influenced by varying factors such as traffic conditions, weather, and time of day.

The paper outlines the significance of accurately modeling both short-range and long-range dependencies to improve forecasting performance. It introduces a spatial-temporal block designed to dynamically model these dependencies, alongside a new variant of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) termed the spatial transformer, which captures time-varying directed spatial dependencies. Additionally, a temporal transformer is proposed to facilitate multi-step predictions by leveraging long-range temporal dependencies.

The authors emphasize that their approach differs from previous methods by dynamically modeling dependencies based on real-time traffic conditions rather than fixed relationships. They validate the effectiveness of STTNs through extensive experiments on real-world traffic datasets, PeMSD7(M) and PEMS-BAY, demonstrating superior performance in traffic flow forecasting, particularly for long-term predictions.

The paper is structured to first review existing methodologies for modeling spatial and temporal dependencies, followed by a detailed formulation of the spatial-temporal graph prediction problem and the proposed STTN approach. The results from the experiments are presented to showcase the advantages of STTNs over state-of-the-art methods, concluding with a discussion on future research directions.
