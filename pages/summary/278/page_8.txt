The paper presents a comparative analysis of traffic flow forecasting models, specifically focusing on the performance of the Spatial-Temporal Traffic Network (STTN) against various baseline models, including HA, ARIMA, LSVR, FNN, FC-LSTM, DCRNN, STGCN, and Graph WaveNet. The evaluation metrics include Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for two datasets: PEMS-BAY and PeMSD7(M) over different prediction horizons.

The results indicate that STTN outperforms the other models, particularly in longer prediction intervals, demonstrating its capability to capture dynamic spatial and long-range temporal dependencies effectively. For instance, STTN achieves the lowest MAE and RMSE across all time steps for both datasets, suggesting that it is more reliable in accurately forecasting traffic flow.

Visualizations of one-day traffic flow forecasts highlight STTN's superior performance in areas with significant variation, showing less prediction error over time compared to STGCN and DCRNN, where prediction errors tend to increase as time progresses. Additionally, the computational complexity analysis reveals that STTN is more efficient, reducing training time by 10-40% compared to Graph WaveNet and 40-60% compared to DCRNN, thus making it scalable for long-term predictions without high computational costs.

In conclusion, the findings substantiate STTN as a robust and efficient model for traffic flow forecasting, particularly for long-term predictions, while also being computationally advantageous.
