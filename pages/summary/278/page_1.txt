The paper presents Spatial-Temporal Transformer Networks (STTNs) as a solution to the challenges of accurate long-term traffic flow forecasting. Traditional forecasting methods struggle with non-linear and dynamic spatial-temporal dependencies. STTNs address these issues by integrating a new type of graph neural network called a spatial transformer, which utilizes a self-attention mechanism to dynamically model directed spatial dependencies, capturing real-time traffic conditions and their directions.

The model employs a multi-head attention mechanism to account for various patterns of spatial dependencies, including similarity, connectivity, and covariance. Additionally, a temporal transformer is introduced to handle long-range bidirectional temporal dependencies across multiple time steps. The STTNs enable efficient and scalable training, making them particularly effective for long-term forecasting.

Experimental results on real-world datasets, PeMS-Bay and PeMSD7(M), indicate that STTNs perform competitively compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, especially in the context of long-term traffic flow predictions. The findings highlight the importance of dynamically modeling spatial-temporal dependencies to enhance forecasting accuracy in intelligent transportation systems.
