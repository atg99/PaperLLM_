This paper presents a study on traffic flow forecasting using short-term and long-term prediction models, specifically comparing fixed and dynamic spatial dependencies. The results indicate that the model utilizing dynamical spatial dependencies, STTN-S(1,1), outperforms the baseline with fixed spatial dependencies in both short-term (5 minutes) and long-term (60 minutes) predictions, as evidenced by lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) values.

In the analysis of the PeMSD7(M) dataset, various models including convolutional approaches and the proposed temporal transformer were evaluated. The temporal transformer demonstrated superior performance in long-term predictions due to its ability to capture long-range temporal dependencies. The study also found that increasing the number of hidden layers improved forecasting accuracy, while multi-head attention did not significantly enhance performance, suggesting that the temporal dependencies in this context are less complex than spatial dependencies.

Furthermore, the paper discusses optimal model configurations, noting that cascading multiple spatial-temporal blocks effectively captures spatial-temporal dependencies, although performance stabilizes beyond two blocks. The findings underscore the importance of model architecture in traffic flow forecasting, emphasizing the need for dynamic modeling approaches to improve prediction accuracy.
