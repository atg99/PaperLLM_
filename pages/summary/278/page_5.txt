The paper discusses the decomposition of traffic signal data into stationary and dynamic components, influenced by road topology and real-time traffic conditions. It introduces two types of graph convolutional layers: a fixed graph convolutional layer for capturing stationary spatial dependencies based on road topology, and a dynamical graph convolutional layer to model time-evolving spatial dependencies. 

The fixed graph convolutional layer utilizes Chebyshev polynomial approximations to learn structure-aware node features, allowing for the aggregation of information from neighboring nodes based on their connectivity and distance. The normalized and scaled Laplacian matrices are employed to facilitate this process.

In contrast, the dynamical graph convolutional layer addresses the limitations of traditional GCN models by incorporating self-attention mechanisms to capture hidden spatial dependencies that evolve over time. This layer learns multiple linear mappings to project input features into high-dimensional latent subspaces, enabling the modeling of directed spatial dependencies influenced by various factors.

Additionally, the paper highlights the importance of spatial-temporal positional embeddings to incorporate both spatial and temporal information into node features. This is achieved through learnable embedding matrices initialized with graph adjacency and one-hot time encoding. The overall framework demonstrates a novel approach to effectively learning and integrating spatial features from traffic data.
