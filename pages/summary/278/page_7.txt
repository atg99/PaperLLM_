The architecture, a shared three-layer feed-forward neural network, effectively captures long-range bidirectional temporal dependencies through a temporal transformer. This design allows for stable training with the use of residual connections and facilitates scaling to longer sequences without significant computational overhead, unlike traditional RNN or convolution-based models.

In experiments conducted on two real-world traffic datasets, PeMSD7(M) and PEMS-BAY, STTN demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in traffic flow forecasting, particularly for long-term predictions. The datasets involved traffic data from sensor stations, with preprocessing that included normalization and adjacency matrix representation of road topology. The experimental setup involved a rigorous evaluation of the model against established baselines, utilizing metrics such as mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared error (RMSE).

Results indicated that STTN significantly outperformed several benchmark models, especially in long-term predictions, highlighting the model's ability to jointly consider dynamic spatial and temporal dependencies. The findings underscore the effectiveness of self-attention mechanisms in modeling complex traffic patterns, positioning STTN as a robust solution for traffic forecasting challenges.
