The paper discusses advancements in traffic flow forecasting, focusing on the modeling of spatial and temporal dependencies. Traditional statistical models, such as ARIMA and Bayesian networks, struggle with the nonlinearity of traffic flows, while neural networks, despite their ability to capture this nonlinearity, face challenges related to computation and memory consumption. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been applied to extract spatial features but often lose topology information due to grid conversion.

To address these limitations, graph neural networks (GNNs) and graph convolutional networks (GCNs) have emerged, enabling the modeling of spatial dependencies in traffic flows by utilizing the inherent traffic topology. However, many existing models, such as STGCN and DCRNN, do not adapt to dynamic changes in traffic conditions, as they rely on fixed spatial dependencies after training. Recent approaches have attempted to incorporate dynamic spatial dependencies, but they often rely on predefined graph structures, which may not capture hidden patterns effectively.

The paper introduces a new model, the Spatial-Temporal Transformer Network (STTN), which efficiently models dynamic directed spatial dependencies in high-dimensional latent subspaces, avoiding fixed graph structures. For temporal dependencies, the authors critique the limitations of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) due to gradient issues and the inefficiency of training on long sequences. They highlight the advantages of transformers, which utilize a self-attention mechanism to capture long-range dependencies adaptively, making them suitable for varying input sequence lengths.

The proposed model formulates the traffic forecasting task as a spatial-temporal prediction problem, represented as a graph. It comprises a spatial-temporal block and a prediction layer, aiming to learn dynamic spatial dependencies and long-range temporal dependencies effectively. The model is designed to predict future traffic conditions based on historical data and the traffic network's structure, addressing the limitations of existing methods by dynamically training spatial dependencies for each time step.
