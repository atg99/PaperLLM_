The paper discusses the use of a multi-head attention mechanism to learn various patterns of spatial dependencies through multiple pairs of subspaces. This approach enables the modeling of different hidden spatial dependencies across diverse latent subspaces. A shared three-layer feed-forward neural network with nonlinear activation is employed on each node to enhance predictions based on learned node features. The interactions among feature channels are explored to update the node features, incorporating a residual connection for stable training.

A gate mechanism is introduced for fusing spatial features derived from both fixed and dynamical graph convolutional layers. This mechanism weighs the outputs from these layers to produce a combined spatial feature output. The spatial transformer is framed as an iterative feature learning process that utilizes message passing and updates for each node in a dynamical graph neural network.

In addition, the paper presents a temporal transformer designed to efficiently capture long-range temporal dependencies, contrasting it with traditional RNNs. The temporal transformer employs a self-attention mechanism to dynamically compute dependencies across high-dimensional latent subspaces, facilitating the modeling of bi-directional temporal dependencies. The use of a scaled dot product function allows for simultaneous multi-step predictions from historical observations, thereby enhancing the modeling of context dependencies compared to the left-to-right architecture of RNNs. Overall, the proposed framework demonstrates improved capacity for handling complex spatial and temporal dependencies in graph data.
