This paper presents a novel end-to-end learning-based method for point cloud registration called DeepVCP (Virtual Corresponding Points). Traditional methods like ICP suffer from challenges such as local minima and sensitivity to noise, particularly with dynamic objects. The DeepVCP framework aims to overcome these challenges by utilizing semantic feature extraction from point clouds through PointNet++. This allows the system to focus on stable features while avoiding dynamic objects.

Key innovations include a point weighting layer that assigns significance to keypoints in the source point cloud, and a new method for generating corresponding points through a mini-PointNet structure. The approach incorporates both local similarity and global geometric constraints in its loss function, enhancing registration accuracy. The paper claims that DeepVCP is the first end-to-end learning-based registration framework yielding results comparable to state-of-the-art geometric methods.

The authors conducted rigorous tests and ablation studies using datasets such as KITTI and Apollo-SouthBay to validate their method. They highlight that prior works in point cloud registration have primarily focused on individual components rather than an integrated framework, and they argue that existing methods fail to account for global geometric constraints, which can lead to the inclusion of dynamic objects in keypoint selection.

In summary, the main contributions of this work include the development of a comprehensive learning-based framework for point cloud registration, the introduction of a novel corresponding point generation method, and empirical evidence demonstrating the framework's effectiveness through extensive testing.
