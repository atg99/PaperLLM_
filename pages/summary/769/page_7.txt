This section presents ablation studies aimed at evaluating the individual components of the proposed method using the Apollo-SouthBay dataset. 

The first analysis focuses on Deep Feature Embedding (DFE). Several configurations are tested: LLF-DFE, using only local coordinates and intensities; FEF-DFE, using only the FE feature; and FEF, where the DFE layer is removed entirely. The experiments reveal that the DFE layer significantly enhances performance, as indicated by the results in Table 3, which show that the proposed method outperforms alternatives.

Next, the effectiveness of Corresponding Points Generation (CPG) is assessed, emphasizing the challenge of convergence due to an imbalance in positive and negative samples when using the proposed loss function.

The study also investigates the loss function, which combines two losses with a balancing factor α. Results show that extreme values of α lead to higher errors, while values between 0.1 and 0.9 yield consistent accuracy, suggesting the generalization capabilities of deep neural networks. The optimal value of α determined for subsequent experiments is 0.6.

Visualizations are provided to illustrate the selection of keypoints by the point weighting layer and the similarity probability distributions from the CPG layer. The keypoints identified by the network effectively correspond to stable features in stationary objects, aligning with expectations. The CPG layer visualizations further demonstrate the matching probabilities among keypoints, reinforcing the method's effectiveness in point cloud registration tasks.
