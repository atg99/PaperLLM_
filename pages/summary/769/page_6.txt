The paper discusses the evaluation of angular and translational errors in relative transformations estimated from LiDAR scans, comparing various registration methods using the KITTI and Apollo-SouthBay datasets. The angular error is quantified using the chordal distance between the estimated rotation matrix and the ground truth, while translational error is computed as the Euclidean distance between the estimated and true translations.

In the KITTI dataset, the proposed method, DeepVCP, is compared against traditional geometry-based methods such as ICP, G-ICP, and AA-ICP. Results indicate that DeepVCP achieves comparable registration accuracy, particularly in terms of robustness and stability, despite slightly higher angular errors compared to G-ICP and ICP. The performance metrics show that both versions of the proposed method, Ours-Base and Ours-Duplication, demonstrate lower maximum errors, indicating their effectiveness in large point cloud registration tasks.

For the Apollo-SouthBay dataset, the proposed method shows improved performance, likely due to better ground truth poses. The Ours-Duplication version achieves the second-best mean translational accuracy and comparable angular accuracy against traditional methods. The results emphasize the robustness of the proposed learning-based approach, particularly in terms of low maximum translational errors.

Additionally, runtime analysis reveals that the proposed method operates efficiently, with an end-to-end inference time of approximately 2 seconds for frame pair registration, significantly faster than other learning-based methods like 3DFeat-Net. Overall, the findings highlight the potential of the proposed method in enhancing the performance of point cloud registration tasks.
