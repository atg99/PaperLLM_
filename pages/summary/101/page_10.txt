The paper presents an evaluation of two trading strategies for managing a Bitcoin portfolio using quantitative models, specifically focusing on a GRU model with recurrent dropout. The first strategy is a long-short approach, where buy signals lead to purchasing Bitcoin and sell signals result in short-selling at the start of the day, with transactions incurring a fee of 0.8%. This strategy is expected to face higher transaction costs due to daily settlements, potentially impacting portfolio value negatively. The second strategy, a buy-sell approach, involves going long on Bitcoin when a buy signal is triggered and selling all holdings when a sell signal occurs, waiting for the next buy signal to reinvest.

The findings indicate that the buy-sell strategy effectively predicted a decline in Bitcoin prices in June 2019, allowing the trader to maintain cash before reinvesting once prices rose. In contrast, the long-short strategy experienced slower portfolio growth due to simultaneous long and short positions. The paper notes that while short selling is not widely permitted on Bitcoin exchanges and carries higher volatility and regulatory risks, the long-short strategy may be more advantageous in a diversified cryptocurrency portfolio.

The conclusion highlights the prevalence of machine learning and time-series analysis in Bitcoin price prediction, pointing out that existing studies often rely on limited feature sets. This research contributes to the understanding of trading strategies in cryptocurrency markets, emphasizing the need for further exploration of complex market behaviors.
