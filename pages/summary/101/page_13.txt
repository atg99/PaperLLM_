This paper presents a comprehensive literature review focusing on various aspects of digital currencies, particularly Bitcoin. It highlights significant contributions from multiple authors discussing the economic implications, price dynamics, speculative trading, and the potential of Bitcoin as a diversification tool in investment portfolios. Key studies referenced include Barrdear and Kumhof's exploration of central bank-issued digital currencies, Bech and Garratt's analysis of central bank cryptocurrencies, and Bouri et al.'s investigation into Bitcoin's hedge and safe haven properties.

The paper also addresses the methodologies employed in predicting Bitcoin prices, showcasing the application of machine learning techniques as discussed by Chen et al. and Chong et al. Furthermore, it delves into the behavioral aspects of Bitcoin markets, including speculative bubbles as examined by Cheah and Fry, and the psychological influences impacting investor decisions. 

Overall, the paper synthesizes insights from diverse sources, emphasizing the evolving nature of digital currencies and their implications for traditional financial systems. It underscores the need for continued research in this dynamic field, particularly concerning the integration of digital currencies into mainstream finance and their regulatory challenges.
