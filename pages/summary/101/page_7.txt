The study investigates the prediction of Bitcoin prices using machine learning models, specifically focusing on the impact of multicollinearity among predictor variables. Features with variance inflation factor (VIF) values exceeding 10 were excluded from the analysis, leading to the selection of 15 relevant features. The chosen features include Bitcoin daily lag returns, transaction volume, and various market indicators, while features like miner revenue and interest rates were discarded due to high multicollinearity.

The paper emphasizes the suitability of machine learning models for time series prediction due to their reported performance in previous literature. It highlights the non-stationary nature of Bitcoin prices, characterized by seasonality and trends, making neural networks particularly effective for this task. The study employs a range of shallow and deep learning models, including recurrent neural networks (RNN) with long short-term memory (LSTM) and gated recurrent units (GRU), utilizing the Keras package for implementation.

The authors discuss the bias-variance trade-off crucial for model accuracy, aiming to achieve low bias and variance simultaneously. They prefer root mean square error (RMSE) over mean absolute error (MAE) for evaluating model performance due to its sensitivity to large errors. Hyperparameter tuning was performed to optimize model performance, focusing on parameters such as learning rate, number of iterations, activation functions, and look-back periods. The hyperbolic tangent (TanH) activation function was selected for its capability to sustain gradients longer than ReLU, thus enhancing prediction accuracy.

The study initially set the look-back period at 30 days, aligning with typical trading cycles, and also evaluated models with varying look-back periods of 15, 45, and 60 days. Despite attempts to adjust the learning rate for better training efficiency, the default value from the Keras package was ultimately employed, with the Adam optimization method used for training all models.
