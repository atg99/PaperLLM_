The paper discusses the implementation of dropout techniques in Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models to mitigate overfitting during training. Specifically, a recurrent dropout rate of 0.1 was utilized, along with additional dropout in the GRU architecture. This approach was aimed at optimizing the models to retain enough training data without memorizing it, thereby reducing overfitting.

The study compared various neural network architectures, including a simple neural network with two dense layers, an LSTM layer, and multiple GRU configurations. It was found that the LSTM architecture outperformed the simple neural network due to its better memory retention capabilities. The GRU model with recurrent dropout achieved an RMSE of 0.014 for the training set and 0.017 for the test set, indicating superior performance over LSTM, attributed to its computational efficiency and effectiveness with smaller datasets.

The research confirmed that recurrent dropout significantly reduced RMSE, with the GRU model demonstrating the least difference between training and test RMSE, suggesting it was the best performing model for predicting Bitcoin prices. The Diebold-Mariano statistical test indicated that the differences in prediction accuracy among the models were statistically significant.

Additionally, the study explored the impact of different lookback periods on model performance, concluding that a 30-day lookback period yielded optimal RMSE results. The predicted Bitcoin prices using the GRU model with recurrent dropout closely followed actual prices, effectively capturing trends during significant market movements in 2019. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of recurrent dropout in enhancing the predictive accuracy of GRU models for time series forecasting.
