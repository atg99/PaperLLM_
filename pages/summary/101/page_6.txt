The paper discusses the methodology used to predict Bitcoin prices using various features and data sets. The data is divided into a training set (January 2010 to June 2018), a validation set (July to December 2018), and a test set (January to June 2019). A lookback period of 15, 30, 45, and 60 days is utilized to forecast the future one-day price of Bitcoin.

Feature selection is highlighted as a crucial step in the data mining process, aimed at identifying useful features that enhance the performance of machine learning models. An analysis of 20 features reveals that endogenous features show a stronger correlation with Bitcoin prices compared to exogenous features, among which Google trends, interest rates, and Ripple price are noted as the most correlated.

The paper also addresses the issue of multicollinearity in statistical learning, where highly correlated features can distort prediction outcomes. This analysis is essential for ensuring that the predictive model is robust and reliable.
