This paper examines the challenges and methodologies associated with predicting Bitcoin prices, highlighting the significance of robust predictive models for investors and government agencies. The authors note that cryptocurrency trading presents unique time series forecasting issues due to high volatility, differentiating it from traditional financial markets. Previous research indicates that factors such as market beta, trading volume, and volatility are essential predictors of cryptocurrency prices.

Traditional forecasting methods, including ARIMA and seasonal ARIMA, struggle to capture the long-term dependencies inherent in highly volatile cryptocurrency markets. In contrast, machine learning techniques, particularly neural networks, offer improved prediction accuracy by addressing non-linear relationships within the data. Although machine learning applications in cryptocurrency price prediction are limited, studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of various methods, including random forests and Bayesian neural networks.

The paper specifically focuses on the use of gated recurrent unit (GRU) architecture, which is proposed as a faster alternative to traditional RNN and LSTM models for predicting Bitcoin prices. The authors aim to evaluate the performance of GRU against traditional neural networks and LSTM, as well as to incorporate a range of exogenous and endogenous variables that have not been previously explored in Bitcoin price prediction research. This comprehensive approach aims to clarify Bitcoin's status as a financial asset and enhance the accuracy of price predictions.
