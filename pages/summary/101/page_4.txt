The paper discusses the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), introduced by Cho et al. in 2014, as a solution to the vanishing gradient problem commonly faced by standard Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). The GRU structure is similar to Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks but simplifies the architecture by combining the forget and input gates into a single update gate and merging the cell state with the hidden state. The GRU unit consists of an update gate, reset gate, and current memory content, which work together to store and manage information over time. The update gate determines how much past information to retain, while the reset gate controls the amount of previous information to forget. The final output of the GRU unit is computed based on these gates, allowing for effective information flow and learning over long sequences.

The paper also highlights the efficiency of GRUs compared to LSTMs, noting that GRUs require fewer tensor operations and are quicker to train. The authors apply these neural network models—simple neural network, LSTM, and GRU—to predict Bitcoin prices, optimizing hyperparameters and selecting the model with the lowest root mean squared error (RMSE) for portfolio strategy execution.

In summary, the research emphasizes the advantages of GRUs in handling long-term dependencies in time series data and their effectiveness for financial predictions, particularly in the context of algorithmic trading strategies.
