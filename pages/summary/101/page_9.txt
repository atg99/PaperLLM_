The paper discusses the implementation of various neural network architectures for predicting Bitcoin prices, specifically focusing on the use of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models. The authors utilized the Adam optimization method and applied dropout techniques to mitigate overfitting. The models were trained with specific configurations, including dropout and recurrent dropout rates of 0.1 for the GRU architecture.

Results indicate that LSTM outperformed a simple neural network due to its enhanced memory retention capabilities. However, the GRU model with recurrent dropout achieved a lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) compared to LSTM, suggesting that GRUs may offer computational advantages with fewer gates and operations. The study also highlights the volatility of Bitcoin and the implications of trading strategies, contrasting the buy-sell approach with long-short strategies, especially in the context of transaction fees and market dynamics.

The findings contribute to the understanding of machine learning applications in cryptocurrency price prediction, noting that while traditional models have been studied, the unique characteristics of Bitcoin trading necessitate further exploration of strategies that leverage machine learning insights effectively.
