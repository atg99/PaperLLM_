The paper discusses the challenges associated with using outdated global batch normalization (BN) statistics in pruned neural networks, which can lead to low accuracy and biased candidate selection. To address this issue, the authors propose recalculating the BN statistics adaptively by performing a few iterations of inference on a portion of the training set. This process involves freezing network parameters and updating the moving average statistics through forward propagation only, resulting in adaptive BN statistics denoted as ^T and ^σ².

The authors provide empirical evidence showing that adaptive BN leads to improved evaluation accuracy, as illustrated in their experiments with MobileNet V1 on ImageNet classification. They compare the correlation of fine-tuning accuracy with both vanilla and adaptive BN evaluations, finding that adaptive BN yields a stronger correlation to the true accuracy.

Furthermore, the paper highlights a comparison of the distances between the adaptive BN statistics and the true statistics derived from validation data. The results indicate that the adaptive approach yields values much closer to the true BN statistics than the global BN method, which is attributed to the outdated nature of the global statistics that do not align with the pruned network connections. The authors note that while fine-tuning can mitigate the problem by recalculating BN statistics during training, BN statistics are not trainable parameters and are only computed during inference. This distinction emphasizes the importance of using adaptive BN for accurate performance evaluation in pruned networks.
