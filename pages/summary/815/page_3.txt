The paper introduces the EagleEye pruning algorithm, which addresses existing limitations in pruning methods by providing a faster and more accurate evaluation process. The authors highlight the deficiencies of the vanilla evaluation step commonly utilized in current algorithms, which often lead to suboptimal pruning outcomes. They introduce a correlation analysis to quantitatively demonstrate these issues.

EagleEye incorporates adaptive batch normalization to enhance the evaluation process, allowing for effective estimation of converged accuracy for pruned models with minimal inference iterations. This algorithm is versatile enough to improve the performance of existing pruning methods. 

Experimental results indicate that despite its simplicity, EagleEye outperforms more complex approaches, achieving state-of-the-art pruning performance. In tests on ResNet-50, EagleEye achieved accuracy improvements of 1.3% to 3.8% compared to other algorithms. It also excelled in pruning the MobileNet V1 model, attaining a top-1 classification accuracy of 70.9% while reducing operations by 50%.

The paper also discusses the evolution of pruning techniques, noting that early methods relied on manual heuristics, which were time-consuming and often ineffective in selecting the optimal pruned networks. Recent strategies introduced greedy methods, but these can lead to local optima. EagleEye circumvents this issue by simultaneously searching for pruning ratios across all layers in a single step. The paper references other works that have induced sparsity during the training phase for pruning, but emphasizes the unique approach of EagleEye in addressing the limitations of prior methodologies.
