EagleEye 11 presents a correlation analysis using various statistical coefficients to assess the relationship between evaluated and fine-tuned accuracy in the context of pruning neural networks. Table 1 details the Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall correlation coefficients under different FLOPs constraints, revealing that the adaptive-BN-based evaluation method demonstrates a strong correlation, particularly with higher pruning rates, thus indicating its robustness in predicting performance.

The paper emphasizes the generality of the adaptive-BN-based evaluation method, showcasing its ability to enhance existing pruning techniques. One application highlighted is its integration with the AMC method, which utilizes Reinforcement Learning (RL) to determine layer-wise pruning ratios. By substituting the traditional vanilla evaluation with the adaptive-BN-based approach, the accuracy of the MobileNetV1 model on ImageNet improved from 70.5% to 70.7%. This indicates that the adaptive evaluation method aids the RL-agent in discovering more effective pruning strategies.

Moreover, the adaptive-BN method can replace short-term fine-tuning strategies for quicker pruning selection and can be combined with other methods, such as evolutionary algorithms, to enhance the basic Monte Carlo sampling strategy. Overall, the findings underscore the efficacy and adaptability of the proposed evaluation method in improving neural network pruning outcomes.
