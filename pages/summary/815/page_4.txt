The paper discusses advancements in model pruning techniques, particularly focusing on the challenges posed by hyper-parameters in automated pruning strategies like reinforcement learning and generative adversarial learning. These complexities can hinder reproducibility and may require extensive trial-and-error to optimize auxiliary models effectively. The authors note the potential of batch normalization (BN) adjustments, which have been utilized in domain adaptation but not specifically for model pruning, highlighting a gap in the current methodologies.

The methodology section outlines a typical pipeline for neural network training and pruning, illustrated in Figure 2. The process begins with training a full-size model, followed by identifying pruning candidates and implementing a pruning strategy aimed at reducing redundancy. This is mathematically formulated to minimize a loss function while respecting constraints related to the desired model size and performance. The authors emphasize structured filter pruning, where various pruning ratios are applied across layers to optimize the model's accuracy post-pruning. The goal is to identify the optimal pruning strategy that achieves the highest accuracy for the compacted model.
