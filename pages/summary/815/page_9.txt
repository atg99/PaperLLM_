The EagleEye pruning algorithm consists of three main components: pruning strategy generation, filter pruning, and adaptive Batch Normalization (BN)-based evaluation. 

1. **Pruning Strategy Generation**: This module generates layer-wise pruning rate vectors for a model, adhering to constraints such as inference latency and global reductions in operations or parameters. It utilizes a Monte Carlo sampling method to randomly select pruning ratios for each layer from a specified range. Although other methods like evolutionary algorithms or reinforcement learning could be employed, the simplicity of random sampling yields effective pruning candidates quickly, maintaining state-of-the-art accuracy due to the subsequent adjustment of BN statistics.

2. **Filter Pruning**: In this step, the full-size trained model is pruned according to the strategies developed earlier. Filters are ranked based on their L1-norm, and the least important filters are permanently removed. This process prepares the sampled pruning candidates for the next evaluation stage.

3. **Adaptive-BN-Based Evaluation**: This module adapts the BN statistics and evaluates the pruned candidates. It involves freezing all learnable parameters in the pruned network and calculating adaptive BN statistics using a small sample of the training data. In experiments with ImageNet, a subset of the training set was used for efficient computation.

Overall, the EagleEye algorithm is designed to efficiently prune neural networks while preserving accuracy, leveraging a straightforward strategy generation process and adaptive evaluation to minimize computational costs.
