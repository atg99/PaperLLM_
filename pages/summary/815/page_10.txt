In this paper, Li et al. present a method for evaluating candidate networks using a process that incorporates a sub-validation set to identify the most accurate networks. The efficiency of this method is highlighted by the quick evaluation time of approximately 10 seconds on an Nvidia 2080 Ti GPU. The authors conduct a correlation analysis to assess the effectiveness of their evaluation process, particularly comparing adaptive batch normalization (adaptive-BN) against a vanilla approach.

The quantitative analysis reveals that the adaptive-BN method demonstrates significantly better correlation with fine-tuning accuracy across various pruning ratios. This is evidenced by the correlation coefficients, which show that adaptive-BN outperforms the vanilla method by an average increase of 0.67 in the Pearson correlation coefficient, 0.79 in the Spearman correlation coefficient, and 0.46 in the Kendall's tau coefficient. The findings indicate that the adaptive-BN method provides a more reliable prediction of the pruned networks' fine-tuning accuracy, which is crucial for selecting the best pruning candidates. Overall, the results underscore the advantages of using adaptive-BN over traditional evaluation techniques in network pruning scenarios.
