The paper discusses various methods for determining optimal pruning strategies in neural networks, including greedy algorithms, reinforcement learning, and evolutionary algorithms, all of which rely on evaluation results of the pruning strategies. The motivation for this research stems from observations that sub-networks selected based on evaluation accuracy do not necessarily perform well during inference, often leading to low accuracy. An initial experiment involved randomly generating pruning rates for MobileNet V1, which resulted in poor accuracy outcomes before fine-tuning. The stark contrast in accuracy distribution between pre- and post-fine-tuning highlighted two critical questions: why the removal of seemingly unimportant filters leads to significant accuracy degradation and the correlation between low-range accuracy and final converged accuracy. These inquiries prompted a deeper investigation into the traditional evaluation process referred to as vanilla evaluation. The findings underscore the limitations of current evaluation methods and suggest that further refinement is necessary to improve pruning strategies.
