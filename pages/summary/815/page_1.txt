The paper introduces EagleEye, a novel method for efficiently evaluating sub-networks during the pruning of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs). Pruning aims to reduce computational redundancy while maintaining model accuracy. Traditional pruning algorithms struggle with accurately predicting the performance of pruned sub-nets, often resulting in complex and inefficient processes.

EagleEye addresses this issue by implementing a simple evaluation component based on adaptive batch normalization, which reveals a strong correlation between various pruned DNN structures and their eventual accuracy. This correlation enables the quick identification of pruned candidates that are likely to yield high accuracy without the need for extensive fine-tuning.

The experimental results demonstrate that EagleEye outperforms existing pruning algorithms, achieving up to a 3.8% improvement in accuracy when pruning MobileNet V1 and ResNet-50. In more challenging scenarios, such as pruning MobileNet V1's compact model, EagleEye achieves a top-1 ImageNet classification accuracy of 70.9% while reducing operations by 50%. The source code and models are made available to the open-source community, enhancing accessibility for further research and application. 

In summary, EagleEye offers a compelling solution to the pruning challenge, facilitating efficient and effective model compression while maintaining high accuracy.
