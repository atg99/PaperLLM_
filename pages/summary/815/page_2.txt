The paper by Li et al. discusses the challenges and methodologies associated with deep neural network (DNN) pruning, framing it as a search problem where the goal is to identify the most accurate sub-net from a vast space of pruning candidates while minimizing search efforts. The authors outline a generalized pipeline for pruning tasks, which includes an evaluation process designed to assess the potential of different sub-nets to ensure the selection of the best pruning strategy.

The evaluation process is critical, as it allows for quick decision-making without the need for exhaustive training of all sub-nets, which can be impractical due to time constraints. However, the authors identify shortcomings in existing evaluation methods, which tend to be either inaccurate—leading to sub-nets that do not achieve high accuracy upon convergence—or overly complicated, involving resource-intensive techniques such as reinforcement learning or auxiliary training. 

To address the inaccuracy, the paper introduces a correlation-based analysis for sub-net selection and highlights the inadequacy of using sub-optimal statistical values for Batch Normalization (BN) layers. The authors propose an adaptive BN technique that enhances correlation in the evaluation process, thereby improving accuracy. The complexity issue is also addressed, emphasizing the need for simpler and more efficient evaluation methods to facilitate reproducibility and reduce computational demands.
