The paper discusses the effectiveness of adaptive Batch Normalization (BN) in the context of pruning evaluation for MobileNetV1. It highlights the visualization of distances in BN statistics, comparing both global and adaptive BN with true values, indicating that adaptive BN provides a more efficient alternative to fine-tuning during the inference step. 

Section 3.3 focuses on correlation measurement, emphasizing the importance of a strong positive correlation between pruning candidates and their converged accuracy. The authors compare adaptive-BN-based evaluation with vanilla evaluation using Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall correlation coefficients to assess the relationship between the accuracy vectors of pruning candidates and the fine-tuned accuracy. The results consistently show that adaptive-BN-based evaluation yields a stronger correlation than the vanilla method across various pruning rates.

Finally, the paper introduces the EagleEye pruning algorithm, which builds on the accurate evaluation process discussed earlier, outlining the overall workflow for effective pruning. The findings underline the advantages of adaptive BN in enhancing the pruning evaluation process, ultimately supporting the development of more efficient pruning strategies.
