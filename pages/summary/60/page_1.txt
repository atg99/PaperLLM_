In this paper, the authors establish a formal hierarchy for recurrent neural network (RNN) architectures, focusing on their expressive capacity. The hierarchy is constructed based on two key formal properties: space complexity, which indicates the memory capabilities of the RNN, and rational recurrence, which assesses if the recurrent update can be represented by a weighted finite-state machine. The authors place various RNN variants within this hierarchy, notably proving that Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks are not rational, thereby distinguishing them from the Quasi-Recurrent Neural Network (QRNN). The paper also discusses how stacking multiple layers or employing different pooling functions can enhance the expressive capacity of these models.

Building on the theory surrounding "saturated" RNNs, the authors suggest that the capacity of unsaturated RNNs might follow a similar hierarchical structure, although a formal extension of these findings is reserved for future research. Supporting this hypothesis, experimental results from training unsaturated networks on formal languages are presented. The paper highlights the gap between theoretical insights and practical applications in the context of RNNs, aiming to bridge this divide through its findings.
