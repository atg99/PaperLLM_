This paper investigates the expressive power of rational and non-rational recurrent neural networks (RNNs) by examining state expressiveness and language expressiveness. It builds on the theory of saturated RNNs, proposing a unified hierarchy that distinguishes the functions expressible by the states of different RNN architectures based on two key properties: space complexity and rational recurrence.

The authors highlight significant differences between Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Quasi-Recurrent Neural Networks (QRNNs), establishing that LSTMs can encode functions that rational recurrences cannot. In contrast, both the saturated Elman RNN and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) are shown to be rational recurrences with constant space complexity, while QRNNs exhibit unbounded space complexity. Furthermore, unrestricted weighted finite automata (WFA) demonstrate richer expressive power than any saturated RNN considered, including LSTMs.

The paper also addresses language expressiveness, noting that RNNs are often paired with decoders that map hidden states to predictions. While rational RNNs may achieve similar performance to non-rational RNNs on language tasks, the authors find that a sufficiently strong decoder can mitigate some differences in state expressiveness. For instance, an LSTM can recognize a specific language with one decoding layer, whereas a QRNN requires two layers. However, there exist languages that LSTMs can recognize without a decoder, which QRNNs cannot recognize regardless of decoder strength.

Empirical experiments on formal languages validate the theoretical predictions regarding the learning capabilities of unsaturated RNNs, positioning the proposed hierarchy as a valuable framework for understanding the relative capabilities of different RNN architectures. The paper outlines its structure, detailing the formal analysis of RNNs, the development of the hierarchy, the study of RNNs as language recognizers, and empirical evaluations in subsequent sections. 

Overall, this work contributes to the understanding of RNN expressiveness by leveraging automata theory and formal models to analyze the computational capabilities of various architectures.
