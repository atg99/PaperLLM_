The paper discusses the architecture and expressiveness of various types of recurrent neural networks (RNNs), specifically focusing on the QRNNs (Quasi-Recurrent Neural Networks) as proposed by Bradbury et al. in 2016. QRNNs combine the features of Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to achieve computational efficiency. The QRNN utilizes convolutions over time with specified window lengths to compute its state vectors, replacing the traditional state vector usage in LSTMs with a computation over the last few input tokens, thus resembling CNNs.

The authors introduce a hierarchy of single-layer RNNs based on their state expressiveness, categorizing them into rationally recurrent (RR), RR-hard, and RR-complete classes. An encoder is classified as RR if its state expressiveness is a subset of the set of rational series, while RR-hard encoders can express all rational series. RR-complete encoders have expressiveness equivalent to rational series. The paper proves that the s-LSTM does not fall into the RR category, highlighting its limitations in counter awareness through specific examples and constructions.

Further analysis reveals that encoders with restricted counting abilities, such as QRNNs, and other finite state encoders (like CNNs and s-RNNs) are classified as RR. However, none of these RNNs are RR-hard. The paper concludes by extending the discussion to self-attention mechanisms, indicating the broader implications of their findings on the understanding of RNN expressiveness and computational capabilities.
