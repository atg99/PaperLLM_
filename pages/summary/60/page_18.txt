The paper argues that generalization accuracy for string lengths greater than 64 is a more reliable measure of a network's ability to learn a language compared to in-distribution test accuracy at length 64. This is because a finite-state model can perform well at this fixed length due to the likelihood of encountering familiar configurations in the test set. 

The language L5 is defined in terms of string configurations, where c(x) represents the difference between the counts of two symbols, a and b, in a string. This configuration behaves like a random walk, with each step being positive or negative with equal probability. Consequently, c(x) follows a binomial distribution, with a mean of 0 and a variance of n/4. For strings of length 64, approximately 95% will have their configuration values within a range of ±8. To challenge the model with new configurations, it is essential to increase the string length n beyond 64.
