The paper discusses the computability of a function f through the use of a weighted finite automaton (WFA) and establishes key results regarding the rank of a matrix associated with f. 

Initially, the authors reorder components and expand expressions to derive a simplified form, leading to Corollary 1, which states that for every k in natural numbers, the series c(k) can be expressed as a linear combination of series r, s, and t. 

The proof of Theorem 8 asserts that f is computable by a WFA if the matrix Hf has finite rank. The authors demonstrate this by defining specific subsets P and S and creating three finite rank matrices A, B, and C that sum to Hf. The matrix C is shown to have rank 1, while matrices A and B are analyzed to show their ranks do not exceed 3. 

The rank of Hf is concluded to be at most 7, and the authors confirm that this bound is tight, establishing that rank(Hf) is indeed 7. This allows for the construction of a WFA for f using the spectral method outlined by Balle et al. 

Lastly, Theorem 9 states that no s-QRNN with a linear threshold decoder can recognize the language anbn, indicating limitations of such neural networks in recognizing specific patterns.
