The paper discusses the capabilities of saturated stack recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in computing non-rational series, specifically focusing on a function denoted as f2. It begins by establishing a proof involving a controller that consistently pushes a value, leading to a specific encoding for time t. The paper introduces a geometric counting function and highlights that the Hankel matrix associated with f2 has infinite rank over certain sub-blocks.

A critical lemma asserts that the saturated stack RNN can compute the function f2, with a detailed proof provided. The paper also includes an erratum that corrects previous experimental results originally reported. It clarifies that the theoretical analysis was based on the ifo-QRNN, while the initial experiments used the fo-QRNN. The updated experiments demonstrate that, while the trends in performance remain similar, the ifo-QRNN achieved 100% accuracy on a specific language (L5), a milestone not reached by the original QRNN even at a training length of 64.

The results are illustrated in updated figures, showing accuracy comparisons across different models for L5 and the language anbn*. The findings indicate that while the performance of the ifo-QRNN improved, the 1-layer QRNN consistently underperformed due to its limitations in recognizing specific language patterns. Overall, the paper emphasizes the distinctions between the models and the implications of these results for understanding the computational power of saturated stack RNNs.
