This section discusses Weighted Finite Automata (WFAs) and Counter Machines (CMs), highlighting their definitions, functions, and relationships.

WFAs are non-deterministic finite automata characterized by weighted states, transitions, and final states. The weights are encoded by three functions: initial state weights, transition weights, and final state weights. The path score of a WFA is calculated based on these weights, and the encoding of a string by a WFA is the sum of scores from all paths that produce the string. The Hankel matrix, derived from a function mapping strings to rational reals, is introduced, with a theorem stating that a WFA can compute a function if and only if the corresponding Hankel matrix has finite rank. Rational series are defined as functions that can be computed by a collection of WFAs.

Counter Machines are deterministic finite-state machines enhanced with integer counters. They update these counters during the processing of input strings and rely on them to influence behavior. A general k-counter CM consists of a finite alphabet, a set of states, a counter update function, and a state transition function. Its configuration at any time includes the current state and the values of the counters. The encoding of a string is determined by the counter values after processing.

Restricted CMs impose limitations on how the update and transition functions can depend on input and state, preventing them from being influenced by the counters' values. This restriction draws parallels with rational recurrent neural networks. The section concludes with a mention of analyzing encoders in terms of state space complexity, measured in bits.
