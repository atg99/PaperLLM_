The paper discusses the limitations of various recurrent neural network (RNN) architectures, specifically noting that saturated and unsaturated versions of RNNs, including GRU, QRNN, and LSTM, are not rationally recursive (RR-hard). It presents a theorem demonstrating that these networks cannot compute a function mapping binary strings to their numeric values, as the growth of the function's value exceeds the bounds of the network's capacity. In contrast, memory networks can utilize more space, allowing them to potentially compute such functions.

The paper also explores the expressiveness of different encoder-decoder configurations, particularly focusing on the languages that can be recognized with these setups. It introduces a linear decoder and illustrates how it enhances the computational power of the encoder, enabling the recognition of certain languages that are otherwise non-computable by the encoder alone.

A case study on the language anbn is presented, highlighting that while simple linear decoders can assist in recognizing certain patterns, more complex languages require deeper analysis. The findings suggest that while some languages are not directly computable through simple encoders, the addition of decoders can facilitate their recognition, thereby expanding the capabilities of RNNs in language processing tasks.
