The experimental setup involves training models on strings of lengths up to 64, with the goal of classifying whether prefixes are valid strings within the defined language. Models were evaluated on independent datasets of various lengths, using a substantial training dataset of 100,000 strings and validation and test datasets of 10,000 strings each. The training process was capped at 100 epochs with early stopping based on validation loss, utilizing the AllenNLP framework for hyperparameter settings.

In the string sampling process for language L5, tokens were uniformly sampled from two characters, with a specific method for generating strings of the form anbn. The experimental cost for the reported results was approximately 20 GPU hours on a Quadro RTX 8000.

The paper further explores the architecture of saturated self-attention, detailing the computation of queries, keys, and values from input embeddings. It presents a theorem asserting that saturated masked self-attention is not rank-restricted (RR). The proof involves constructing a self-attention layer to compute a function based on the occurrences of specific characters in strings. The analysis shows that the attention mechanism can represent functions in a way that demonstrates it is not RR, concluding that the saturated self-attention architecture can indeed compute complex relationships within the input data.
