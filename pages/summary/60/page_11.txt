In this paper, the authors extend the results from Theorem 3 to establish that any (Q)-restricted counter machine (CM) is rationally recurrent, as stated in Theorem 7. The proof involves constructing a weighted finite automaton (WFA) that computes an arbitrary counter in a (Q)-restricted CM. This construction comprises two independent copies of the CM's transition graph: one designated as the add graph and the other as the multiply graph. The add graph is initialized with a weight of 1 at the starting state, while all other states start with a weight of 0. Conversely, the multiply graph assigns an accepting weight of 1 to its states.

Transitions in the add graph receive a weight of 1, while those in the multiply graph receive a weight of 0 for representing a 0 operation and 1 otherwise. Non-multiplicative updates in the original CM introduce transitions in the WFA, where each counter update generates a path that ends in the multiply graph, with path scores reflecting the value of the counter.

The paper further discusses WFAs and their limitations in directly encoding certain languages. Theorem 8 demonstrates that the language anbn=fanbn|n∈N is in D1(WFA), by constructing a function with a finite rank Hankel matrix that can be thresholded to recognize anbn. The authors detail the creation of series used to form base vectors for rank 3 matrices, ultimately proving that the Hankel matrix can be expressed as a combination of these series.

Overall, the paper presents significant advances in the understanding of counter machines and their relationship with weighted finite automata, providing new insights into their computational properties.
