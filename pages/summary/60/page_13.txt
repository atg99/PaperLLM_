The paper discusses the limitations of the Anifos-QRNN model in recognizing specific languages, particularly the language anbn. It establishes that the QRNN can be viewed as a k-restricted counter machine (CM) with particular update operations. The proof demonstrates that when this machine is coupled with a linear decoder D1, it fails to recognize the language anbn due to the constraints posed by the window size k. The analysis reveals that counter updates are identical across different inputs, leading to contradictions when attempting to satisfy the conditions required for recognizing anbn.

In contrast, the paper introduces a two-layer decoder D2 that allows the s-QRNN to recognize anbn by maintaining two counters that track specific token counts. This approach overcomes the limitations of the simpler decoder by enabling checks for conditions necessary to recognize the language.

Furthermore, the paper presents a theorem asserting that no s-QRNN with any decoder can recognize the language anbn followed by arbitrary combinations of a's and b's. This is attributed to the s-QRNN's inability to "freeze" counter values, which prevents it from maintaining the necessary state against future inputs.

The proofs leverage the structure of the input sequences and the properties of the counters, demonstrating that operations performed on the counters remain consistent even when the order of token processing changes. Overall, the findings highlight both the potential and the constraints of the s-QRNN model in recognizing certain formal languages.
