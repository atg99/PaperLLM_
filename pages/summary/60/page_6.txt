The paper discusses the computational capabilities of counter machines (CMs) and their relation to rational functions, specifically focusing on the limitations and characteristics of restricted CMs and finite-space encoders. 

In the first section, the authors present an example of a one-counter machine (1-CM) that processes inputs of the form a^i b^j, where i and j are non-negative integers. They describe how the machine can utilize a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) model to emulate the transitions of the 1-CM, concluding that the specific LSTM and general CMs are not rationally recursive (RR).

The paper then establishes that any σ-restricted CM is RR by demonstrating that the functions computable by such CMs can also be computed by weighted finite automata (WFAs). The authors present theorems that connect the update operations of CMs to functions that can be expressed in terms of WFAs, highlighting that σ-restricted CMs function as a special case of unigram WFAs.

Further, they explore the concept of finite-space encoders, showing that any function computable by a space-1 encoder is a rational series, implying that encoders like CNNs, s-RNNs, and s-GRUs are also RR. This aligns with previous findings that certain architectures, while initially thought to be limited, are capable of expressing complex functions.

Finally, the authors argue that despite the simplicity associated with rational recurrence in RNN architectures, WFAs possess significant computational power, capable of simulating complex behaviors that RNNs cannot replicate. The paper concludes with the assertion that none of the examined RNNs can simulate arbitrary WFAs, emphasizing the unique capabilities of WFAs in the context of computational complexity.
