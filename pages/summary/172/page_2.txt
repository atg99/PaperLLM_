This paper provides an extensive overview of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), detailing various algorithms and their applications. It categorizes GAN variants into representative types like InfoGAN, CycleGAN, and WGAN, as well as different objective functions and learning strategies. The discussion highlights the evolution of generative algorithms, distinguishing between explicit and implicit density models.

Explicit density models, such as maximum likelihood estimation and Markov chain methods, are characterized by their direct estimation of data distributions but face limitations, including computational expense and difficulty in capturing complex data distributions. In contrast, implicit density models, including GANs, generate data instances without fitting an explicit distribution, allowing for more efficient practical applications.

The paper emphasizes the advantages of GANs over traditional generative algorithms, particularly their parallelization capabilities and the adversarial learning process, where a generator creates realistic examples to fool a discriminator, leading to mutual improvement of both components. This adversarial approach positions GANs as a powerful tool in generative modeling, fostering advancements in various fields such as image synthesis, super-resolution, and natural language processing.
