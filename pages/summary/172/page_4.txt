This paper discusses various aspects of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), particularly focusing on the optimization challenges and different game formulations. It highlights the contradiction in the non-saturating game, where the generator aims to minimize the divergence between generated and real distributions while simultaneously trying to maximize it, leading to unstable training gradients. The paper emphasizes the asymmetry of KL divergence and points out that the penalties for generator errors are significantly different, resulting in a preference for generating safe, repetitive samples, which can lead to mode collapse.

The paper also introduces the concept of the maximum likelihood game as an alternative to the traditional minimax approach in GANs. It outlines the equivalence between minimizing the cost functions derived from the discriminator's responses and the original GAN formulation. Three key observations are made regarding the behavior of different game formulations: the original and maximum likelihood games suffer from gradient vanishing when samples are likely to be fake, while the non-saturating game avoids this issue. Furthermore, it notes that the maximum likelihood game is affected by high variance in gradient computation, suggesting that variance reduction methods could enhance performance. The non-saturating game appears to perform better in practice due to lower sample variance.

The paper concludes by mentioning the existence of various GAN variants, such as InfoGAN, which innovatively decomposes the input noise vector into distinct components, aiming to capture significant features effectively.
