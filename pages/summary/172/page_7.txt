The paper discusses key differences between the original Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Wasserstein GANs (WGANs). The WGAN objective function lacks the logarithmic component present in original GANs and utilizes the discriminator (D) to approximate Wasserstein distance, shifting from a binary classification to a regression task. The output of D in WGAN is unconstrained, unlike the original GAN which restricts outputs to a range between zero and one. Additionally, WGAN imposes a K-Lipschitz constraint on D, implemented through weight clipping. This framework enhances learning stability and provides meaningful learning curves, yet approximating the K-Lipschitz constraint poses challenges.

To address these issues, WGAN-GP introduces a gradient penalty to maintain the K-Lipschitz constraint, modifying the objective function accordingly. The paper also mentions related approaches, including DRAGAN and WGAN-div, which propose alternative metrics and objectives for GAN training.

Further, the Loss Sensitive GAN (LS-GAN) is introduced, which also incorporates a Lipschitz constraint and assumes that the generated samples should incur a larger loss than real ones. The LS-GAN objective function is structured to reflect this assumption through specific loss functions.

Despite theoretical frameworks suggesting unique solutions, the training of GANs remains difficult and unstable due to various factors, including saddle points in the loss function. The paper highlights various strategies proposed in the literature to stabilize GAN training, such as two time-scale update rules and addressing empirical symptoms of training instability.

In summary, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of advancements in GAN frameworks, focusing on the evolution of objective functions, the introduction of constraints, and ongoing challenges in training methodologies.
