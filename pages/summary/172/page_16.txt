The paper discusses advancements in image synthesis, manipulation, and related applications utilizing Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). It begins by highlighting specific methods for face super-resolution and moves on to image synthesis and manipulation techniques. Notable contributions include the Disentangled Representation Learning GAN (DR-GAN) for pose-invariant face recognition and the Two-Pathway GAN (TP-GAN) for photorealistic frontal view synthesis. The Pose Guided Person Generation Network (PG2) and other models like deformable GANs are also mentioned for generating images in arbitrary poses or for customizable makeup transfer.

The paper further emphasizes improvements in face generation quality over the years, detailing the evolution from basic GANs to more sophisticated architectures like deep convolutional networks and multiscale training methods that enable high-fidelity megapixel face images. In contrast, generating images from diverse datasets like ImageNet poses more challenges, although progress is being made.

Additionally, it explores the interaction between humans and image generation processes, addressing the difficulties in realistic image manipulation. Interactive GANs (IGAN) and introspective adversarial networks facilitate user-controlled editing, while GauGAN can transform sketches into photorealistic landscapes.

Texture synthesis is covered through methods like Markovian GANs (MGAN) and Spatial GAN (SGAN), which enable real-time texture generation. The section on object detection discusses how GANs can help create datasets with instances of deformations and occlusions, enhancing the robustness of object detectors. Finally, the paper mentions applications in video generation, referencing early work and subsequent models that predict future frames and synthesize video content.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of the state of GANs in various image and video processing applications, showcasing their versatility and ongoing advancements.
