The paper "A Fourier Perspective on Model Robustness in Computer Vision" by Dong Yin and collaborators addresses the challenge of achieving robustness in computer vision models against distributional shifts. Despite advancements in deep learning, models often perform poorly when faced with common image corruptions, such as random noise and contrast changes, which highlights a gap in their robustness compared to human vision.

The authors explore the effectiveness of data augmentation techniques, such as Gaussian data augmentation and adversarial training, in improving robustness. They identify a critical trade-off: while these methods enhance resilience to high-frequency corruptions, they tend to weaken performance against low-frequency corruptions. This insight underscores the need for a diverse set of augmentations to mitigate such trade-offs.

The study highlights the success of AutoAugment, a data augmentation strategy optimized for clean accuracy, which achieves state-of-the-art robustness on the CIFAR-10-C benchmark. The findings emphasize the importance of understanding the frequency domain characteristics of data augmentations in order to develop more robust computer vision models.
