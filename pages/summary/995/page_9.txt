The paper investigates the effects of adversarial perturbations on image classification models, particularly focusing on their Fourier spectrum. The authors analyze the differences between naturally trained and adversarially trained models. The Fourier spectrum of adversarial perturbations shows that naturally trained models have a uniform distribution across frequency components, while adversarially trained models exhibit a bias towards lower frequencies. The study also explores the generation of content-preserving black box adversarial examples by adding Fourier basis vectors with large norms, demonstrating significant degradation in model accuracy while maintaining image semantics.

In the conclusions, the authors discuss the trade-offs in robustness observed in image models, particularly emphasizing the relationship between frequency of corruption and model performance under data augmentation. They note that while high-frequency corruptions can be better managed through Gaussian data augmentation and adversarial training, this often leads to reduced robustness against low-frequency corruptions. The effectiveness of AutoAugment in enhancing robustness without overfitting to specific corruptions is highlighted, yet concerns remain regarding the evolving nature of robustness benchmarks and the necessity for continued exploration into new methodologies beyond data augmentation. The authors suggest the development of complementary approaches, such as improved architectures and loss functions, to address the robustness challenge comprehensively.
