In this study, the authors investigate the robustness of different models against additive noise with a fixed L2 norm but varying frequency bandwidths. They analyze two scenarios: one where the noise is centered at low frequencies and another at high frequencies. Results indicate that models trained naturally exhibit lower error rates for low frequency noise, while those trained with Gaussian data augmentation and adversarial methods perform better under high frequency noise.

The experiments involve applying low and high pass filters to the noise and assessing the models' performance on CIFAR-10 images. The findings support the hypothesis that models with noise augmentation are biased towards low frequency information. The authors also explore the impact of applying low and high pass filters at the model's input stage, observing that low pass filtering enhances performance against additive noise but reduces performance on other corruptions like fog and contrast.

To quantify the relationship between frequency and model robustness, the authors measure the energy ratio in high and low frequency domains across various corruptions. Their analysis reveals that models biased towards low frequencies perform better against high frequency corruptions. Conversely, models with a high pass filter bias exhibit poorer performance overall, particularly on high frequency corruptions.

The study also questions whether low frequency data augmentation indeed improves robustness against low frequency corruptions. The authors conduct experiments with additive noise that mimics the characteristics of fog corruption, highlighting the complex interplay between data augmentation techniques and robustness. Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of frequency characteristics in training models for enhanced robustness against various types of noise and corruptions.
