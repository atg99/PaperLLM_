This paper investigates the performance of ImageNet models under extreme filtering in the frequency domain, specifically focusing on the effects of both low and high-frequency filtering on model generalization. The authors conduct experiments that reveal models can achieve over 30% accuracy with low-frequency filtering, and an even more impressive 50% accuracy when subjected to severe high-frequency filtering, despite the images appearing as indistinct color blobs. This suggests that models utilize high-frequency features, which are not easily discernible to humans, indicating their potential utility in generalization.

The study also explores the trade-offs in model robustness in relation to different types of data augmentations, particularly Gaussian data augmentation and adversarial training. These methods appear to bias models towards low-frequency information, enhancing their robustness against various noise and blurring corruptions but degrading performance against others like fog and contrast.

Using a Fourier perspective, the authors analyze the frequency characteristics of natural images and common corruptions, noting that natural images have higher low-frequency concentrations, while Gaussian noise is uniformly distributed across frequencies. The findings imply that understanding the frequency characteristics of data augmentations and corruptions can provide insights into model robustness and classification performance. The paper further discusses connections to previous research that highlights the significance of imperceptibly perturbed images in aiding model generalization.
