The paper investigates the sensitivity of different machine learning models to additive noise in the context of image classification on datasets like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. It compares three types of models: a naturally trained model, an adversarially trained model, and a model trained with Gaussian data augmentation. The study focuses on how these models respond to noise aligned with various Fourier basis vectors.

Key findings reveal that the naturally trained model exhibits high sensitivity to additive noise across most frequency ranges, except for the lowest frequencies. In contrast, both adversarial training and Gaussian data augmentation enhance robustness against high frequency noise but lead to decreased sensitivity to low frequency noise. This trade-off suggests that the augmentation methods encourage models to be less reliant on high frequency details, potentially affecting their performance on corruptions like fog and contrast, which primarily impact low frequency components.

Further analysis confirms these observations, indicating that Gaussian data augmentation and adversarial training improve performance against high frequency perturbations while compromising robustness to low frequency disturbances. The results are consistent across both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets, highlighting the nuanced impact of training strategies on model robustness in varying frequency domains.
