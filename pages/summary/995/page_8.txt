The paper presents a comparison of various models trained on the CIFAR-10-C and ImageNet-C benchmarks, focusing on their performance against different types of corruptions including noise, blur, and weather-related distortions. The models evaluated include a naturally trained model, one using Gaussian data augmentation, an adversarially trained model, and one employing AutoAugment.

Results indicate that the AutoAugment model consistently achieves the highest average test accuracy and the best mean corruption error (mCE) across most corruptions, particularly in blurring and weather conditions. The study also notes that when evaluating on corruptions applied in memory for ImageNet-C, AutoAugment continues to outperform other models.

Furthermore, the paper explores the nature of adversarial perturbations, challenging the common belief that they are predominantly high frequency. Using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) to create adversarial examples, the authors analyze the frequency characteristics of the perturbations. Findings reveal that while naturally trained models exhibit higher concentrations of perturbations in the high frequency domain, adversarially trained models show a distribution more akin to that of natural images. This suggests that removing high frequency content does not guarantee robustness against adversarial attacks. The analysis highlights a potential connection between adversarial training and feature visualization techniques, indicating that adversarial training may steer perturbations toward lower frequencies, thus producing semantically meaningful gradients.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of AutoAugment in handling image corruptions and provides insights into the frequency characteristics of adversarial perturbations, contributing to a deeper understanding of model robustness in the presence of adversarial attacks.
