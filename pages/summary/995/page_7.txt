The paper investigates the relationship between test accuracy and the fraction of high-frequency energy in various corruption types in the CIFAR-10-C dataset. It presents findings that different data augmentation techniques have varying impacts on model robustness against high and low-frequency corruptions. Specifically, Gaussian data augmentation, adversarial training, and low-pass filtering enhance robustness to high-frequency corruptions while weakening it against low-frequency ones. Conversely, high-pass filtering significantly decreases accuracy for high-frequency corruptions.

The authors note that training with fog noise, despite being similar to fog corruption in the Fourier domain, negatively affects performance on actual fog corruption, suggesting a complex interaction between low and high-frequency information. This asymmetry arises from the natural distribution of images, which are predominantly low-frequency, making it easier for models to disregard high-frequency details.

To achieve broader robustness, the authors propose utilizing varied data augmentation strategies. They highlight the effectiveness of AutoAugment, which employs a learned mixture of transformations and demonstrates superior performance on CIFAR-10-C. AutoAugment achieves an average corruption test accuracy of 86%, significantly outperforming models trained with Gaussian data augmentation and adversarial training, which achieve average corruption errors of 98 and 108 respectively. The results indicate that AutoAugment enhances robustness across nearly all corruption types compared to naturally trained models.
