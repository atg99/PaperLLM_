The cited works focus on various aspects of deep learning, particularly in relation to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and their vulnerabilities, feature visualization, and model robustness.

Jo and Bengio (2017) investigate the propensity of CNNs to capture surface-level statistical regularities rather than deeper features. Liu et al. (2019) introduce feature distillation as a method for enhancing JPEG compression in deep neural networks to mitigate the impact of adversarial examples. Madry et al. (2018) emphasize the need for developing deep learning models that can withstand adversarial attacks, proposing strategies for improving model robustness.

Olah et al. (2017) present techniques for visualizing features learned by neural networks, aiding in understanding model behavior. Recht et al. (2019) question the generalization capabilities of ImageNet classifiers, suggesting concerns about their performance on unseen data. Tsipras et al. (2019) argue that robustness and accuracy in deep learning may be inherently conflicting objectives, highlighting a trade-off in model performance.

Finally, Tsuzuku and Sato (2019) explore the sensitivity of CNNs to the directions of Fourier basis functions, while Zagoruyko and Komodakis (2016) introduce wide residual networks, which enhance learning capacity through increased width in network architecture. Collectively, these studies contribute to the ongoing discourse on the efficacy, robustness, and interpretability of deep learning models in various applications.
