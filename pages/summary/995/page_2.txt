This paper investigates the effects of Gaussian data augmentation and adversarial training on model robustness to various types of image corruptions, specifically within the CIFAR-10-C and ImageNet-C benchmarks. The authors note that while these augmentation strategies enhance performance against noise and blurring, they adversely affect performance on fog and contrast corruptions. This raises the question of what distinguishes the corruptions that benefit from augmentation versus those that do not.

The authors hypothesize that the frequency characteristics of different corruptions contribute to these performance discrepancies. Through extensive experiments in the Fourier domain, they find that Gaussian data augmentation and adversarial training tend to bias models towards low-frequency information. This results in improved robustness against high-frequency corruptions but leads to performance degradation on low-frequency corruptions.

To address these trade-offs, the authors propose the use of more diverse data augmentation techniques. They highlight the effectiveness of the AutoAugment policy, which achieves state-of-the-art results on the CIFAR-10-C benchmark and has also been successfully applied to ImageNet-C.

Additionally, the paper discusses implications for security, noting that certain Fourier domain perturbations can significantly increase model error rates while maintaining image semantics, qualifying as effective black-box attacks.

Finally, the authors extend their frequency analysis to understand the nature of adversarial perturbations, revealing that naturally trained models produce more high-frequency adversarial perturbations, whereas adversarial training shifts these perturbations towards the low-frequency domain.

The paper establishes a connection between corruption types and frequency information, suggesting that a deeper understanding of these relationships can inform the design of more robust machine learning models.
