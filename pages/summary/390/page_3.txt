The paper discusses the classification of brain tumors (BT), specifically focusing on their types and the implications for diagnosis and treatment. It categorizes tumors into three grades: Grade I tumors, which are slow-growing and account for about 80% of cases; Grade II atypical tumors, which grow faster and comprise approximately 17% of cases; and Grade III malignant tumors, also known as anaplastic tumors, representing about 1.7% of cases. 

Additionally, the paper highlights pituitary tumors, or pituitary adenomas, which are abnormal growths in the pituitary gland, crucial for hormone regulation. These tumors can be benign or malignant and may cause hormonal imbalances, vision problems, headaches, and behavioral changes. The treatment options include radiation therapy, medication, and surgical removal, emphasizing the importance of early detection through MRI scans to reduce treatment complexities.

The study evaluates the EfficientNet family of models for their effectiveness in detecting and classifying BT from MRI scans, utilizing a dataset of approximately 18,500 images. The trained models are designed to accurately identify tumor types based on the input MRI scans, aiming to improve diagnostic accuracy and patient outcomes.
