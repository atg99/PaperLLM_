The paper discusses the complex functions and significance of the human brain, which weighs about 1.4 kilograms and serves as the center for cognition, emotions, and consciousness. It regulates essential bodily functions while also enabling thinking, learning, memory, and emotional responses. The brain's adaptability and plasticity highlight its capacity for learning and recovery, making it a focal point for scientific research aimed at understanding human behavior and consciousness.

The study specifically addresses brain tumors (BT), which are abnormal growths of brain cells that can be benign or malignant. These tumors can disrupt normal brain functions, affecting cognitive abilities and sensory perception. Symptoms vary based on the tumor's size, location, and growth rate, with common signs including headaches, seizures, and neurological impairments. Early detection and treatment are crucial, as untreated BTs can be life-threatening. Treatment options include radiation therapy, surgery, and chemotherapy, tailored to the tumor type.

The focus is on classifying three types of BTs: glioma tumors (GT), meningioma tumors (MT), and pituitary tumors (PT). Gliomas are primary brain tumors arising from glial cells and can occur in various brain and spinal cord regions. Early detection is key for effective treatment, with significant mortality rates associated with BTs globally. In India, around 28,000 cases are reported annually, leading to approximately 24,000 deaths.

Meningiomas originate from the protective meninges surrounding the brain and are usually benign, though they can occasionally be malignant. They are primarily found on the brain's outer surface and have an incidence rate of about 97 cases per 100,000 individuals. The paper further categorizes MTs into three grades, emphasizing the need for ongoing research to improve treatment outcomes and quality of life for affected individuals.
