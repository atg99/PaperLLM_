The paper presents a robust deep learning model for the detection and classification of brain tumors using the EfficientNet architecture. The study aims to enhance the accuracy of identifying three types of brain tumors from MRI scans by evaluating various EfficientNet models, specifically from EfficientNet-B0 to EfficientNet-B7. A dataset comprising around 18,500 MRI images was utilized for training, validation, and testing, employing Softmax and ReLU activation functions along with the Adam optimizer at specific learning rates.

The results indicate a notable increase in classification accuracy correlated with model complexity, achieving accuracies of 96.07% for EfficientNet-B0, 97.86% for B1 and B3, 98.21% for B2, 98.93% for B4, 99.64% for B5 and B7, and 98.57% for B6. These findings highlight the potential of the EfficientNet models in improving brain tumor diagnosis, suggesting that deep learning techniques can significantly assist medical professionals in making accurate and timely decisions. The research contributes to the growing field of medical image analysis and emphasizes the importance of model complexity in enhancing diagnostic performance.
