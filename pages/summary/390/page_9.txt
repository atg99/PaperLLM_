The paper discusses the implementation of the ADAM optimizer and the EfficientNet models in deep learning applications. The ADAM optimizer is highlighted for its efficiency and robustness, particularly with large datasets. It operates by computing the first and second moment estimates of gradients, utilizing exponential decay rates for moving averages. The equations provided detail the calculations for these moments, bias correction, and the final parameter updates.

The EfficientNet architecture is introduced, ranging from EfficientNet-B0 to EfficientNet-B7, designed for efficient image classification. This architecture employs a compound scaling method that optimizes depth, width, and resolution simultaneously, resulting in models that are both computationally efficient and highly accurate. The paper emphasizes the versatility of EfficientNet in various computer vision tasks, including image classification, object detection, and segmentation, making it a significant tool in deep learning. Each variant of EfficientNet builds upon the previous one, incorporating more layers and channels to tackle increasingly complex challenges.
