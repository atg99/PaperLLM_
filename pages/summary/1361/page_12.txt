The paper presents a taxonomy for human-robot interaction (HRI) using two iconic robots, R2-D2 and C-3PO, as case studies. It categorizes robots based on various dimensions, including their morphology, autonomy, field of application, task specification, communication channels, and human roles. 

R2-D2 is characterized as a small astromech droid, primarily used for navigation and maintenance tasks in space. Its autonomy includes information acquisition and decision-making capabilities. R2-D2's morphology is described as technical, and it operates in fields such as space expeditions and military applications, performing tasks like physical load reduction and manipulation.

C-3PO, in contrast, is a human-sized protocol droid focused on language translation and diplomacy. It serves as an interpreter between robots and humans, showcasing a more anthropomorphic design. C-3PO's role emphasizes collaboration and communication, functioning in various fields, including service and education.

The taxonomy highlights the importance of robot design and functionality in shaping human interactions, illustrating how different robots can serve distinct purposes while engaging with humans in various capacities. The analysis supports the argument that understanding robot characteristics is essential for optimizing HRI across diverse applications.
