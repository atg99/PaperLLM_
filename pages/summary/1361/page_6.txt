The paper presents a new taxonomy for human-robot interaction (HRI), structured into three main clusters: interaction context, robot characteristics, and team classification. This taxonomy aims to enhance the classification of HRI across various domains, addressing the limitations of existing frameworks that lack generalizability. 

The interaction context classification provides a comprehensive overview of the application fields, extending beyond the traditional categories of industrial and service robots to include military, police, space exploration, and social robotics. This classification considers the specific requirements and tasks associated with each domain, such as the need for robots in space to adapt to extreme environments.

The taxonomy also outlines robot characteristics, focusing on task, morphology, and autonomy. It categorizes robots based on their roles in interactions and the nature of communication channels, highlighting factors like proximity and the degree of robot autonomy in decision-making and action implementation.

The hierarchical structure allows for top-down analyses of existing interactions and informs the design of HRI from a bottom-up perspective. Although not all categories are mutually exclusive, the taxonomy facilitates comparisons across studies, revealing structural and functional differences that can explain varying outcomes in HRI research.

Overall, the proposed taxonomy serves as a structural basis for classifying and analyzing different aspects of HRI, with graphical support to aid in the specification of HRI scenarios.
