This paper discusses the classification of human-robot interaction (HRI) scenarios, emphasizing the importance of team composition, communication channels, and the proximity of human and robot interactions. It identifies three categories for team composition: equal numbers of humans and robots, more humans than robots, and more robots than humans. The paper advocates for specifying the exact number of humans and robots to enhance comparability across different HRI scenarios.

Communication is further categorized into input and output channels, highlighting how humans convey information to robots and how robots respond. Input methods include electronic, mechanical, acoustical, and optical channels, while output can be perceived through tactile, acoustical, and visual channels.

The paper also categorizes physical proximity into five types: following, touching, approaching, passing, avoiding, and none. Each category describes the nature of the spatial relationship between the human and robot. Additionally, it distinguishes between synchronous and asynchronous temporal proximity, indicating whether interactions occur simultaneously or at different times.

To facilitate the application of the HRI taxonomy, the authors introduce a graphical canvas representation inspired by the Business Model Canvas. This canvas organizes the classification variables and provides a structured overview of HRI scenarios. It encourages users to select at least one category per variable and offers an interactive PDF for practical use. Overall, the taxonomy aims to provide a comprehensive framework for analyzing and understanding various HRI contexts.
