The paper discusses the classification of human-robot interaction (HRI) based on three key variables: robot task specification, robot morphology, and degree of robot autonomy. The authors propose a simplified categorization of autonomy on a 10-point scale, dividing it into low, medium, and high levels for practical applicability. These variables serve as a foundation for design guidelines aimed at enhancing beneficial HRI.

Additionally, the paper presents a team classification framework for HRI scenarios, which is characterized by four variables: human role, team composition, communication channel, and proximity. The human role is expanded from previous models, defining five distinct roles: 

1. **Supervisor**: Monitors and instructs the robot.
2. **Operator**: Controls the robot directly, typically in high-stakes environments like bomb disposal or surgical procedures, where human and robot work synergistically towards a common goal.
3. **Collaborator**: Works alongside the robot without managerial responsibility, sharing the same goals, as seen in industrial settings.
4. **Cooperator**: Engages with the robot on separate but complementary tasks, such as in assembly lines.
5. **Bystander**: Shares space with the robot without direct interaction, focusing on avoiding collisions.

This classification provides insights into the structure of interaction and teamwork in HRI, highlighting the importance of understanding the roles and relationships between humans and robots in various contexts.
