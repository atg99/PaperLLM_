Social interaction involves the robot engaging in social behaviors that simulate human-like interactions, which can enhance emotional well-being, especially in therapeutic contexts.

Entertainment focuses on the robot’s role in providing amusement or enjoyment, as seen with robotic toys or companions designed primarily for leisure.

The degree of robot autonomy varies from fully autonomous systems that operate independently to semi-autonomous and teleoperated robots that require human intervention. This classification significantly influences the interaction dynamics between humans and robots.

Robot morphology refers to the physical characteristics and design of the robot, which can affect user perception and acceptance. Different shapes, sizes, and functionalities can lead to varied responses from users, impacting the overall effectiveness of the robot in its intended application.

In conclusion, the taxonomy presented provides a comprehensive framework for understanding the diverse roles and impacts of robots across different fields, including therapy, education, and entertainment. By considering factors such as exposure type, task specification, autonomy, and morphology, researchers can better assess and enhance human-robot interactions.
