The paper discusses various aspects of human-robot interaction (HRI) by categorizing robots based on several key dimensions. These dimensions include the degree of robot autonomy, robot morphology, fields of application, task specifications, proximity, communication channels, and team composition.

The degree of robot autonomy is defined through stages of information acquisition, analysis, decision-making, and action implementation. Robot morphology is classified into three types: anthropomorphic, zoomorphic, and technical, influencing how robots are perceived and interacted with. The fields of application range from industry and service to therapy and entertainment.

Task specifications for robots include information exchange, precision tasks, physical load reduction, transport, manipulation, cognitive stimulation, emotional stimulation, and physical stimulation. Proximity factors encompass various interaction modes, such as synchronous or asynchronous communication and physical interactions like following, touching, and avoiding.

Communication channels are classified into input (e.g., electronic, mechanical, acoustic, optic) and output forms (e.g., tactile, acoustic, visual). The paper highlights studies by Kuz et al. and Riek et al., which employed different robotic systems to illustrate these concepts. Kuz et al. utilized a virtual simulation environment with a six-axis gantry robot, while Riek et al. focused on a humanoid robot torso, BERT I, which performs interactive gestures.

Finally, the human role in these interactions can vary, encompassing positions such as supervisor, operator, collaborator, cooperator, and bystander. The paper employs a taxonomy framework to analyze and represent the chosen specifications for each variable in HRI scenarios.
