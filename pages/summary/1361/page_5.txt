The paper discusses various frameworks and models that have been developed to characterize and investigate Human-Robot Interaction (HRI). It notes that while existing models provide valuable insights into different aspects of HRI, they often have limitations in their applicability across various scenarios. For instance, some frameworks focus specifically on robot autonomy or are tailored to particular domains, such as autism therapy, limiting their generalizability.

Key contributions from different authors are highlighted, such as Beer et al.'s detailed framework on robot autonomy, Dautenhahn's role definitions for robots in therapeutic contexts, and Kahn et al.'s psychological benchmarks for human-like robots. Yet, many models remain conceptual with insufficient guidance for practical implementation or fail to adequately address the actual interaction dynamics between humans and robots.

The authors argue for the need for a comprehensive framework that integrates the human, robot, interaction, and context, is applicable to a wide range of HRI scenarios, and offers predefined categories for structured comparisons. This proposed framework aims to build on the strengths of existing models while addressing their shortcomings, thereby facilitating a more holistic understanding of HRI.
