The paper discusses the application of a taxonomy for Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) to compare different studies on robot communication and movement. It highlights that in the examined scenarios, the human participant acted as a bystander and not a collaborator with the robot, which communicated through gestures that participants needed to interpret. The taxonomy reveals both similarities and differences between two studies, specifically in their field of application, task specification, and robot morphology. These differences suggest that the contrasting results between the studies can be attributed to fundamentally different HRI scenarios, indicating that comparisons may be misleading.

The authors argue that the taxonomy serves as a valuable tool for structured comparisons, allowing for a clearer understanding of HRI characteristics and the implications of their findings. It can help identify dimensions on which studies differ, thus facilitating meta-analyses and generalizability of insights across different contexts. The paper concludes that the taxonomy not only aids in research but also provides a framework for designing experiments that can enhance teamwork between humans and robots.
