The analysis of both HRI scenarios using the taxonomy highlights key differences in human-robot interaction. While both studies involve robots with high autonomy, the nature of the tasks, the human roles, and the contexts differ significantly. The findings suggest that the morphology of robots and the expectations they evoke influence human likability and engagement. R2-D2's design fosters a caregiver effect, making it more approachable, while C-3PO's humanoid characteristics project authority and expertise. This distinction in design aligns with the differing human roles in their respective interactions.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes that anthropomorphic design and the context of interaction play crucial roles in shaping human perceptions of robots. The taxonomy serves as a useful framework for understanding these dynamics, suggesting that people's likability of robots is affected by their morphology, the perceived human role, and the nature of the interaction.
