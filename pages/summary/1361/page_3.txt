The paper presents a comparative overview of various experimental studies on Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) focusing on the effects of anthropomorphism. The studies highlight how anthropomorphic characteristics influence human responses to robots across different domains and contexts.

1. **Darling et al.** found that anthropomorphism increases empathetic concern and reluctance to destroy robots, suggesting that personification impacts emotional responses in destructive scenarios.
   
2. **Kuz et al.** demonstrated that anthropomorphic trajectories and speed profiles facilitate easier identification of robot actions, indicating that design features can enhance user interaction in industrial settings.

3. **Nijssen et al.** revealed that robots resembling humans are sacrificed more often than both human-like robots and humans in moral dilemmas, underscoring the ethical implications of robot design in military contexts.

4. **Riek et al.** reported that action identification is quicker with machine-like trajectories than with human-like movements, suggesting a difference in cognitive processing based on robot design.

5. **Onnasch and Roesler** observed a negative impact of anthropomorphic framing on donation behavior compared to functional framing, although this effect diminished when the robot's functional value was emphasized, indicating the importance of context in shaping perceptions.

Overall, the studies collectively illustrate the complex relationship between anthropomorphism in robots and human emotional and cognitive responses, emphasizing the significance of design and contextual information in HRI.
