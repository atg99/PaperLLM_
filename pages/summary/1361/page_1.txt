The paper presents a new taxonomy aimed at structuring and analyzing human-robot interaction (HRI) to address the challenges posed by the variety of robots and interaction scenarios. As robotic systems increasingly integrate into both personal and professional spheres, the diversity in their designs and applications complicates the comparability and generalizability of HRI research findings. The authors argue that a structured approach is necessary to facilitate efficient research, identify gaps, and guide future studies.

The proposed taxonomy encompasses four key dimensions: the human, the robot, the interaction, and the context of HRI. This framework is designed to be applicable across various HRI scenarios and provides predefined categories that facilitate structured comparisons. A graphical representation of the taxonomy aids in its application to specific cases, allowing researchers to analyze contrasting results from different studies effectively.

The authors demonstrate the taxonomy's utility by applying it to existing HRI studies, highlighting its potential for reviews and meta-analyses. They conclude that this taxonomy not only organizes current knowledge but also serves as a framework for future HRI research, guiding systematic investigation into the distinctive variables that influence human-robot collaboration.
