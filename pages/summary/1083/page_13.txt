The paper discusses the learning problem from a maximum likelihood estimation perspective, specifically in the context of noisy data. It defines models that treat initial states as trainable parameters, allowing for the optimization of both the model parameters and the initial state. The likelihood function is derived based on the independence assumption of the data points, leading to an L2 loss minimization framework. 

The authors propose a generalization where both the model parameters and the initial state are optimized simultaneously. This approach aligns with methods in inverse problems and systems biology, known as the initial value approach. However, two main challenges are identified: the risk of convergence to local minima and potential instability in the numerical solutions of the ordinary differential equations (ODEs). 

To address the stability issue, the paper suggests using Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNN) in conjunction with a leapfrog integrator. However, it raises concerns about the potential difficulties in optimizing the initial value, even if the neural network optimization performs well. The discussion highlights the importance of symplecticness and the convexity of the initial-state optimization problem, suggesting that these factors may influence the success of the optimization process.
