The paper presents a method for predicting the trajectories of a heavy billiard ball with perfect rebounds using a neural network architecture known as a Stochastic Recurrent Neural Network (SRNN) augmented with a rebound module. The approach involves leapfrog updates to compute tentative positions and momenta, taking into account potential rebounds when modeling the dynamics of the billiard ball.

To efficiently incorporate rebound events, the authors utilize visual cues from a background image representing the walls of the billiard table, rather than directly training the network on numerous rebound events. The rebound direction and magnitude are modeled as multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) that process specific neighborhoods of the background image. A regularization term is applied to ensure the rebound module is only active when necessary.

The training process employs backpropagation through the leapfrog scheme and the associated models, using 5000 trajectories for training and 32 for testing. Results indicate that the proposed method outperforms several baseline models, including those without the rebound module or with a non-learning rebound mechanism, despite a limitation regarding the assumption of a single rebound event per time step. The findings are illustrated through comparisons of predicted versus actual trajectories.
