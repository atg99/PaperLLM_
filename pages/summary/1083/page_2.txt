This paper presents a novel approach to learning Hamiltonian dynamics using a framework called SRNN (Symplectic Recurrent Neural Network). The authors address the challenge posed by rebound events in Hamiltonian systems, where the motion of a point mass is altered drastically by obstacles. Traditional methods struggle to simulate these dynamics on fixed time grids due to the infinite forces involved during rebound events. To overcome this, the authors enhance the leapfrog integrator in their SRNN with a trainable operator that models these rebound events, linking them with visual cues.

The paper reviews related work in the field, including methods that replicate human perception of physical interactions and those that learn differential equations governing physical systems. Previous approaches include using neural networks to model the Lagrangian function and learning Hamiltonian dynamics through graph networks and ODE integrators. The authors highlight that while some methods perform well on simple systems, they fall short in more complex scenarios.

The contributions of this paper include the ability of SRNN to learn Hamiltonian dynamics directly from position and momentum time series, demonstrating effectiveness in noisy and complex systems, such as spring-chain and three-body systems. Additionally, SRNN is designed to handle perfect rebound scenarios, illustrating its capability in managing stiff Hamiltonian dynamics.

In the framework section, the authors define Hamiltonian systems and outline Hamilton's equations, which govern the evolution of these systems through ordinary differential equations. They emphasize their focus on conservative Hamiltonians and suggest that extending their work to non-conservative systems should involve adding dissipation or intervention operators rather than altering the Hamiltonian's time dependency.
