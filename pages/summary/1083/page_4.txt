This paper presents a framework for modeling dynamical systems through a Symplectic Recurrent Neural Network (SRNN), which utilizes symplectic integrators for both training and testing. The authors discuss the flexibility in design choices for constructing algorithms, including the use of O-NET or H-NET for time derivative modeling, various explicit integrators for training, and the option to employ single-step or multi-step training trajectories. 

The SRNN is particularly highlighted for its robustness in learning complex Hamiltonian dynamics, demonstrated through experiments on a spring-chain system comprising 20 masses connected by springs. The training data consisted of 1000 trajectories, each beginning from random initial states and extending over 10 time-steps. The authors also detail how single-step training can be compared to recurrent training by restructuring the training data.

Key advantages of the SRNN include its resilience to issues of vanishing and exploding gradients due to the volume-preserving nature of the leapfrog integrator and its reversibility, which allows for the recomputation of states during the backward pass. The paper concludes by noting the potential for further exploration of these computational benefits in future research.
