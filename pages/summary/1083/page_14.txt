The paper investigates the convexity of optimization with respect to initial values in the context of autonomous ordinary differential equations (ODEs), focusing on the flow map generated by models such as H-NET and O-NET. The authors define the flow map as a function that maps initial states to their evolved states after a specified time. They impose an L2 loss function on the flow map's output compared to observed data and pose the question of whether this loss function is convex.

To analyze convexity, the authors derive the gradient and Hessian of the loss function. They conclude that if the flow map corresponds to either the exact flow of a Hamiltonian system or a symplectic integrator, then the Jacobian matrix is symplectic, which leads to a positive definite Hessian under certain conditions. Specifically, when the difference between the flow map output and observed data is small, and the model closely approximates the true system, the Hessian indicates strong convexity of the optimization problem.

The paper suggests that strong convexity is likely when the model is symplectic and trained effectively, with low noise in the observed data. This provides a heuristic understanding of the optimization landscape for initial values in the realm of learned ODE models.
