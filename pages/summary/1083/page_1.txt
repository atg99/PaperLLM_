In this paper, the authors introduce Symplectic Recurrent Neural Networks (SRNNs) as a novel approach for learning the dynamics of physical systems through observed trajectories. SRNNs model the Hamiltonian function using a neural network and employ symplectic integration, multiple-step training, and initial state optimization to overcome the numerical challenges inherent in Hamiltonian systems. The authors demonstrate that SRNNs effectively handle complex and noisy Hamiltonian systems, outperforming traditional Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs) particularly in the presence of observation noise.

The paper begins by discussing the limitations of existing methods, notably the difficulty of numerical integration in Hamiltonian dynamics due to issues such as discretization errors and measurement noise. It highlights how symplectic integrators, such as the leapfrog method, are better suited for preserving the structure of Hamiltonian systems but still struggle with phenomena like mechanical rebounds.

In the proposed SRNN framework, the authors integrate the partial derivatives of the Hamiltonian using the leapfrog integrator and back-propagate the loss through multiple time steps. They also optimize both the initial state and the Hamiltonian network simultaneously, suggesting that this optimization is likely convex due to the symplectic nature of the system. The results from experiments, including the three-body problem, indicate that the SRNN can compensate for discretization errors and may even outperform traditional numerical solutions using the true Hamiltonian.

The latter part of the paper examines the phenomenon of perfect rebound as a case study for addressing severe stiffness in dynamical systems. Overall, the findings suggest that SRNNs present a promising direction for applying machine learning techniques to the numerical resolution of differential equations in physical systems.
