This conference paper presented at ICLR 2020 focuses on enhancing the prediction of dynamic systems under noisy observations through three main extensions to existing neural network models. The authors analyze various methods, including single-step and recurrent training frameworks, and their effectiveness in reducing prediction errors measured by L2 distance.

A key contribution of the paper is the introduction of Initial State Optimization (ISO), which aims to address biases introduced by noise in initial state observations. By estimating and optimizing the initial states (denoted as ^p0 and ^q0) rather than using the noisy observed states, the authors demonstrate that their approach can guide models towards more accurate predictions. The optimization process employs the L-BFGS-B algorithm after initial training epochs, allowing the model to refine its predictions over time.

The results, illustrated in figures, highlight that the SRNN model equipped with ISO outperforms all other methods, showcasing significant qualitative improvements. The paper also discusses the limitations of vanilla RNN and LSTM models in benefiting from ISO, suggesting that effective initial state optimization relies on having a reasonable model of the system.

In conclusion, the authors propose that the combination of leapfrog integrators, recurrent training, and ISO leads to substantial advancements in learning and predicting complex dynamic systems in the presence of noise.
