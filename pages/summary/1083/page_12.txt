The paper presents experimental setups for three distinct simulations: the spring-chain experiment, the three-body experiment, and the heavy billiard experiment, all aimed at validating the performance of neural network models in trajectory prediction.

In the spring-chain experiment, the authors simulate ground truth trajectories using a leapfrog integrator with a small time-step of 0.001, coarsened to 0.1 for training and testing. The models include an O-NET, a one-hidden-layer MLP with 40 input and output units and 2048 hidden units, and an H-NET composed of two one-hidden-layer MLPs for the kinetic and potential energy. The models are trained in PyTorch over 1000 epochs using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001 and a ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler.

In the three-body experiment, trajectories are generated using SciPy’s adaptive solver RK45 and coarse-grained to a time-step of 1. Training data includes 100 sample trajectories, which are transformed into shorter trajectories for training. The O-NET is a three-hidden-layer MLP with 12 input and output units, while the H-NET consists of two three-hidden-layer MLPs. The models are trained similarly to the spring-chain experiment but with a lower initial learning rate of 0.0003.

The heavy billiard experiment involves a simulation with a 128x128 pixel image, where the billiard moves in a constrained space. The O-NET and H-NET architectures are similar to those in the spring-chain experiment, but with fewer input and hidden units. The training utilizes 5000 trajectories, and the models are trained over 1500 epochs with an initial learning rate of 0.005, employing an ExponentialLR scheduler.

Overall, the paper details the architecture and training protocols for each experiment, emphasizing the use of specific neural network configurations and optimization techniques to enhance performance in trajectory prediction tasks.
