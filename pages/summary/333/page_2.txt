The paper discusses the limitations and potential applications of GPT-3 in healthcare. While GPT-3 demonstrates strong performance in free-form conversations, it struggles with dynamic interactions and lacks true understanding, making it unsuitable for high-stakes healthcare scenarios. The authors highlight feasible applications, such as automating routine tasks and enhancing patient experiences via online support, but emphasize the necessity of strict implementation guidelines to mitigate risks.

In more complex applications, like triaging non-critical patients in emergency departments, careful consideration of equity and accessibility is crucial. The paper warns against potential allocational and representational harms, particularly for patients who may not effectively interact with a virtual agent. It underscores the importance of maintaining human oversight in triage processes and ensuring language accessibility.

Transparency in the use of AI technology is essential to prevent misinterpretation of the system's capabilities. The authors advocate for involving diverse stakeholders in the development and evaluation phases to address potential harms and ensure that the technology meets user needs. Overall, while GPT-3 holds promise for improving patient care, the authors call for cautious optimism and thorough consideration of implementation challenges to avoid exacerbating healthcare disparities.
