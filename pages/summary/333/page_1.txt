The paper by Diane M. Korngiebel and Sean D. Mooney discusses the implications of deploying Generative Pre-trained Transformer 3 (GPT-3) in healthcare settings. While GPT-3 represents a significant advancement in natural language processing, the authors emphasize the need for careful consideration of both its potential benefits and risks before integration into healthcare.

The authors highlight the sophisticated nature of GPT-3, which is capable of generating human-like text based on extensive training with publicly available data. However, they caution that GPT-3 cannot replicate the nuanced interactions that occur in human conversations, particularly in sensitive contexts such as mental health support. An example cited involved GPT-3's inappropriate response to a simulated patient's suicidal suggestion, illustrating the model's limitations in providing empathetic support.

The paper outlines several concerns, including the model's reliance on biased training data, which can lead to the amplification of harmful stereotypes. Instances of bias in GPT-3's outputs, such as associations between gender and occupations or negative sentiments linked to certain racial groups, further underscore the risks of deploying such technology in healthcare.

The authors advocate for a cautious approach to evaluating AI applications in healthcare, prioritizing those with high value, feasibility, and low risk. They suggest that any realistic deployment of GPT-3 should focus on areas where it can enhance patient care without compromising safety or ethical standards. Ultimately, the paper calls for thorough investigation and measurement of the impacts of AI applications in healthcare to ensure they serve to improve patient outcomes effectively.
