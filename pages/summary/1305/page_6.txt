The paper discusses a method for mixed-example data augmentation that involves adding random zero-centered noise to mixing coefficients for pixel values. This approach is compared to strictly linear methods like mixup and BC. The authors implement this method on datasets such as CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, using a pre-activation ResNet-18 architecture. They emphasize the importance of performing other data augmentations, such as random cropping and flipping, before applying mixed-example techniques, as this significantly impacts accuracy.

In the experiments, the authors detail implementation specifics, highlighting that they used a desktop setup with Nvidia Geforce GTX 1080 Ti GPUs. For CIFAR-10, they maintained a uniform distribution for mixing coefficients and adjusted weight decay based on the dataset, achieving notable accuracy improvements. The authors also address learning rate strategies, noting the instability during transitions and the importance of running multiple model copies to mitigate variability in training results.

The results for CIFAR-10, a dataset with 60,000 images across 10 categories, confirm that linearity is not strictly necessary for the effectiveness of mixed-example data augmentation, suggesting that the proposed method can yield competitive performance without adhering to linear constraints. The paper provides evidence supporting the efficacy of their approach, contributing to the ongoing discussion on data augmentation techniques in deep learning.
