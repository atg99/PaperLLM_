The paper discusses generalized methods for data augmentation, particularly focusing on functions that combine two training examples into a new one. This approach expands on previous augmentation methods, such as mixup, which uses linear combinations of inputs. While mixup has been effective, the authors assert that non-linear methods can also yield significant benefits.

Several key methods are highlighted:

1. **Mixup**: This method combines two examples using a weighted average, where the mixing coefficient follows a Beta distribution. It encourages linearity between examples, which has been beneficial across various tasks.

2. **Between Class (BC+)**: This method builds upon mixup by incorporating improvements that account for the non-zero mean of images and ensuring perceptually linear combinations by using standard deviations for mixing. While it is technically non-linear, it still relies on element-wise averaging.

3. **Vertical Concat**: This method introduces a non-linear approach by vertically concatenating portions of two images based on a randomly sampled coefficient. This technique allows for more complex patterns to be learned by the model.

The authors argue that while linear methods like mixup provide a strong inductive bias, non-linear methods can also be effective and may enhance the performance of neural networks in various tasks. They emphasize that these methods are not mutually exclusive and can be combined with traditional augmentation techniques for improved results.
