This paper presents experimental results on mixed-example data augmentation techniques applied to the CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, aiming to improve model performance beyond the baseline ResNet-18. The study evaluates various methods and reports error rates for each technique, highlighting that many new approaches outperform traditional state-of-the-art methods such as mixup and BC+.

Key findings include:

1. **Performance Improvement**: Most mixed-example techniques improved upon the baseline, with methods like "Horiz. Concat" and "Vert. Concat" demonstrating significant enhancements. The best-performing methods, "VH-Mixup" and "VH-BC+", achieved new state-of-the-art results.

2. **Linearity Not Required**: The results suggest that linearity is not a necessary condition for effective mixed-example augmentation. For instance, "Rand. 2x2", which lacks element-wise averaging, performed comparably to linear methods.

3. **Negative Examples**: Certain methods, specifically "Rand. Elems" and "Rand. Pixels", underperformed relative to the baseline. These techniques introduced high-frequency signals that may hinder the model's ability to learn local details, thus complicating the learning process.

4. **Consistency Across Datasets**: The trends observed in CIFAR-10 were largely mirrored in CIFAR-100, though the ranking of methods varied slightly. The results indicate the robustness of the mixed-example approaches across different tasks in computer vision.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of non-linear mixed-example data augmentation techniques to enhance model performance while challenging the conventional belief that linearity is essential for such methods.
