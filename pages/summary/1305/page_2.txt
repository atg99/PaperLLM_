This paper investigates the effectiveness of mixed-example data augmentation methods in training deep neural networks, particularly in image classification tasks. Despite the lack of realism in the augmented data for human perception, such methods have been shown to enhance model performance significantly across various tasks. The authors discuss existing hypotheses regarding the success of these techniques, including the idea that they introduce beneficial linearity in the data and impose constraints on internal feature distributions.

The central question posed in the paper is whether linearity is crucial to the success of mixed-example data augmentation. The authors explore this by proposing a range of alternative methods for generating example combinations, demonstrating that most of these methods lead to improvements compared to models trained without mixed-example augmentation. While the paper does not present new theoretical insights into the mechanisms behind the effectiveness of these methods, it empirically evaluates existing hypotheses and suggests they may be incomplete.

The structure of the paper includes a review of related work in data augmentation and regularization, followed by a detailed exploration of various mixed-example data augmentation methods. The authors conduct experiments on datasets such as CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Caltech-256, concluding with a discussion on their findings.

The discussion also touches on the broader context of data augmentation and regularization strategies in neural networks, emphasizing their roles in improving generalization and reducing the train-test performance gap. The authors note the interplay between the two, illustrating that effective data augmentation can reduce the need for regularization techniques like weight decay.
