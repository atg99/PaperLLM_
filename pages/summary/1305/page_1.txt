The paper "Improved Mixed-Example Data Augmentation" by Cecilia Summers and Michael J. Dinneen investigates the effectiveness of data augmentation techniques in training neural networks, particularly focusing on mixed-example data augmentation. Traditional data augmentation typically involves label-preserving transformations of existing training data to combat overfitting. However, recent findings suggest that non-label-preserving augmentations, specifically through linear combinations of training examples, can also yield significant improvements.

This work proposes a more generalized approach to mixed-example data augmentation that expands beyond linear transformations to include various non-linear functions. The authors explore a broader range of augmentation techniques, revealing many practical methods that surpass previous state-of-the-art performance. This generalization not only enhances the potential for improved model performance but also indicates that existing explanations for the effectiveness of such augmentation methods are incomplete. The study suggests that a comprehensive theory must account for a wider array of phenomena related to mixed-example data augmentation.

The authors emphasize the importance of generating synthetic data to address the challenge of acquiring large datasets, particularly in data-scarce fields like robotics and healthcare. They provide examples of the methods explored in their research, demonstrating that the potential functions for data augmentation are much more diverse than previously recognized. The code for their proposed methods is accessible via a provided GitHub link.
