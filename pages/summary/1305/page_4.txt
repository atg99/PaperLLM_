The paper discusses various methods for concatenating images to enhance classification performance. The primary goal is to not only classify the top and bottom portions of an image but also to determine the fractional area they occupy.

1. **Horizontal Concat**: This method concatenates the left fraction of one image with the right fraction of another. The output image is constructed such that the classification label is a weighted sum of the labels from the two input images based on their respective fractions.

2. **Mixed Concat**: This approach combines vertical and horizontal concatenation. It involves sampling two fractions from a Beta distribution to create a grid that determines how the input images contribute to different areas of the output image. The resulting label reflects the relative areas of the two images.

3. **Random 2x2**: This method introduces randomness into the Mixed Concat by dividing the image into a 2x2 grid with varying sizes and randomly assigning each grid square to one of the input images. This prevents the network from relying on fixed assignments, enhancing adaptability to changing image content. A constraint is applied to ensure grid intersections occur within a specific range, improving performance.

4. **VH-Mixup**: This method aims to combine the strengths of linear and non-linear methods by applying both vertical and horizontal concatenation before utilizing mixup techniques. This allows for greater flexibility in how the images are combined, potentially improving classification performance.

The paper emphasizes the importance of these methods in enhancing image classification tasks by effectively utilizing input image content and their spatial relationships.
