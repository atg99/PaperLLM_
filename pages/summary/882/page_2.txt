This paper discusses advancements in image style transfer, specifically addressing the limitations of existing methods that require a reference style image. While traditional approaches excel in creating visually appealing artworks by applying known styles to content images, they fall short in scenarios where users wish to emulate styles without direct access to reference images. The authors propose a novel method that utilizes a text-image embedding model, CLIP, to facilitate style transfer based on user-defined text conditions. This approach allows for the creation of unique styles that users can imagine, thereby expanding the creative possibilities of style transfer.

The proposed method leverages a lightweight CNN to capture texture information relevant to the text conditions, enhancing the realism and colorfulness of the results. Instead of traditional pixel optimization, the authors introduce a patch-wise CLIP loss that guides the network to treat each image patch as a brushstroke, allowing for localized style application. Additionally, they implement augmentation techniques to foster diversity and vividness in the style transfer process, while a threshold regularization mechanism helps mitigate over-stylization.

The paper also reviews existing literature on style transfer, noting significant contributions like the work of Gatys et al. on pixel optimization and various techniques that have emerged since then, such as Adaptive Instance Normalization and the whitening and coloring transform. Ultimately, the experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in achieving a broader range of style transfers driven by textual descriptions, surpassing the capabilities of prior methods reliant on style images.
