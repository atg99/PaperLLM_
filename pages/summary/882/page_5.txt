The paper presents a method for style transfer that utilizes text conditions to synthesize realistic textures while preserving image content. Figures 1 and 3 illustrate how this method can effectively match image styles to specified text conditions, enabling control over both the texture type and detailed attributes, such as color and object type. For instance, users can specify textures like "white wool" or "green crystal" and choose corresponding objects for enhanced stylistic effects.

The method diverges from traditional style transfer frameworks, which typically rely on style images. Instead, it leverages the CLIP model trained on diverse natural and artistic images, allowing for the transfer of styles from famous artworks based on text input. A comparison with state-of-the-art artistic style transfer methods, including AdaAttn, SANet, CST, and AdaIN, reveals that the proposed approach produces results comparable to these baseline methods, despite relying solely on text conditions.

The analysis highlights the limitations of earlier models, which primarily focused on major color changes and struggled to express target texture styles effectively. In contrast, more recent methods retain complex texture information while preserving the structure of content images. Overall, the findings underscore the potential of text-guided style transfer in achieving rich and varied stylistic outputs.
