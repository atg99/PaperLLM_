This section presents a comparison of the proposed method against baseline style transfer methods and text-guided manipulation models. The results indicate that the proposed method can produce stylized images using only text conditions, without needing a style reference image. The output images exhibit complex textures that align with the semantics of the text, maintaining the original content's structure.

In contrast, baseline methods, predominantly trained on artistic styles, struggle to effectively transfer textures from non-artistic styles. The proposed method successfully extracts and applies semantic textures from the input text to the content image.

In a comparison with text-guided manipulation models, particularly those utilizing the CLIP model and pre-trained StyleGAN, the proposed method demonstrates an ability to generate realistic textures across the entire image. In contrast, baseline models, such as StyleGAN-NADA, often modify only parts of the image or fail to adequately reflect the text conditions. The proposed approach emphasizes texture application, allowing for a more cohesive artistic style, while baseline models tend to alter identity based on the text prompt.

Additionally, the paper explores the potential of integrating CLIP loss with other manipulation techniques to enhance style transfer capabilities. These investigations aim to leverage the strengths of CLIP loss in producing better style outcomes. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of the proposed method in achieving nuanced and contextually relevant image transformations.
