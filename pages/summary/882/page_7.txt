The paper presents an ablation study and comparison of various style transfer methods using the CLIP-loss optimization on pre-trained networks. Figure 6 illustrates the results of the ablation study, demonstrating the impact of different loss functions and techniques on the quality of style transfer outputs. The study reveals that using all proposed loss functions yields the best results, capturing global semantics and producing clean images without artifacts. Removing specific components like the directional CLIP loss or threshold rejection leads to irregular color mapping and over-stylization, respectively. The use of augmentation is critical for achieving realistic textures, and substituting the proposed perspective augmentation with standard affine transformations introduces unwanted artifacts.

In Figure 7, the comparison between different manipulation approaches highlights the superiority of the proposed method over baseline techniques, such as pixel optimization and VQGAN+CLIP, which fail to maintain content structure while applying textures. The paper also discusses the training framework for fast style transfer, which aims to enable the use of various content images by training on multiple texture patches.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of each component in the proposed method and suggest avenues for further enhancements in style transfer applications. User study results and additional comparisons are provided in the supplementary materials for further validation.
