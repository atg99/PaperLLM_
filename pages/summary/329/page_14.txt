The paper details the development and evaluation of VL-GPT, a model designed for vision and language tasks, and its instruction-tuned variant, VL-GPT-I. The training process involved a smaller dataset with a batch size of 512 and utilized four GPUs, aiming to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by employing a reduced learning rate and frozen parameters, with the addition of LoRA modules in the transformer model. 

For evaluation, the model's capabilities were assessed against various benchmarks, with results obtained through specific task-oriented prompt templates. These templates facilitate the model in completing multimodal sequences in an auto-regressive manner. The paper emphasizes the importance of these prompt designs in leveraging the knowledge from pre-training while adhering to evaluation styles. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of VL-GPT and its instruction-tuned version in understanding and generating multimodal outputs.
