The paper introduces VL-GPT, a model designed for vision and language (VL) understanding and generation tasks. It employs a unified autoregressive pre-training approach on large-scale multimodal corpora, predicting the next token in sequences that include both continuous visual embeddings and discrete text tokens. VL-GPT shows strong performance in zero-shot and few-shot scenarios across various benchmarks, such as image captioning, visual question answering, and text-to-image generation. Additionally, it exhibits effective multimodal in-context learning capabilities when provided with multimodal prompts and has potential as a general multimodal assistant through instruction tuning.

The paper also reviews related work in multimodal pre-training, contrasting approaches from the pre-LLM and LLM eras. Earlier research focused on model architectures for cross-modal data fusion, while recent studies have successfully developed multimodal language models that can handle both image and text inputs. However, existing models often face challenges in achieving unified modeling for images and language, as they typically require special queries for image generation and struggle with auto-regressive training for visual embeddings due to inconsistencies in embedding space.

The authors highlight that while some models, like Emu, enable auto-regressive training for both visual and text embeddings, they necessitate costly fine-tuning processes. In contrast, VL-GPT utilizes a novel image tokenizer-detokenizer framework that leverages a pre-trained image diffusion model, enhancing training efficiency and maintaining semantic and appearance details in visual embeddings. This framework ultimately leads to improved performance across VL tasks.
