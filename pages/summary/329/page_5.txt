The paper discusses a novel approach to multimodal modeling using a large vision-language model (VL-GPT). It begins by outlining the creation of an interleaved multimodal sequence that combines discrete text tokens with continuous visual embeddings. This sequence is fed into the VL model for unified auto-regressive modeling, allowing for flexible transformation of output embeddings into either text or visual formats depending on the target.

The training process consists of three phases: developing a tokenizer-detokenizer framework, conducting unified multimodal pre-training, and performing instruction tuning. The pre-training utilizes a large corpus of multimodal data, following a standard auto-regressive modeling objective to maximize the likelihood of the sequences. The model applies cross-entropy loss for text tokens and mean squared error loss for visual embeddings.

To enhance the model's ability to follow human instructions, instruction tuning is performed using publicly available datasets. The data is restructured into a conversational format, which is then used for further tuning.

The paper presents experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed tokenizer-detokenizer framework, highlighting its performance in image reconstruction tasks compared to existing models. The datasets used for training include a variety of image-text pairs and interleaved sequences from different sources, ensuring a comprehensive training approach.

Overall, the VL-GPT model shows promise in generating multimodal content and effectively following instructions, indicating its potential for applications in various domains.
