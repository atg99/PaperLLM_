This paper presents a method for quantizing neural networks, focusing on the spatial quantization of convolutional filters to leverage information redundancy. The authors implement a sequential quantization approach, starting from the lowest layers and guided by a non-compressed teacher network. 

The quantization process involves learning a codebook from the input activations of each layer, which are obtained by passing a batch of images through the quantized lower layers. The authors note that using the activations from the non-compressed network can lead to increased reconstruction and classification errors. 

To refine the codebook, the authors employ a distillation technique, optimizing the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the output probabilities of the teacher and student networks. The gradients for each subvector assigned to a codeword are averaged for updates. This method outperforms traditional finetuning on image targets and does not require labeled data.

A final global finetuning step is performed on the codebooks of all layers to minimize residual drifts, updating the BatchNorm layers' running statistics. This step utilizes the standard ImageNet training set and demonstrates that finetuning through distillation yields better results than using true labels, as the teacher network provides a richer supervisory signal. The authors detail their training parameters and present empirical results supporting their approach.
