This paper discusses dedicated architectures for efficient neural networks, highlighting models such as SqueezeNet, NASNet, ShuffleNet, MobileNets, and EfficientNets. These architectures are optimized for memory efficiency and utilize depth-wise and point-wise convolutions, making them less susceptible to structured quantization compared to traditional ResNets. For instance, MobileNet achieves a model size of 13.4MB with a top-1 accuracy of 71.9%, while ResNet-50 has a larger size of 97.5MB and an accuracy of 76.2%. 

The paper emphasizes that larger models can leverage large-scale weakly or semi-supervised learning for improved performance. It also notes the effectiveness of combining various compression techniques, as demonstrated by Deep Compression, and highlights the importance of hardware-specific optimizations for quantization to enhance energy efficiency and reduce latency. Furthermore, it points out that the memory overhead of storing activations is minimal compared to weights, primarily because real-time inference typically involves a batch size of one and only the current layer's activations need to be stored.

The authors introduce their approach to network compression, which focuses on minimizing the reconstruction error of layer outputs rather than the weights themselves. They outline a method for quantizing fully connected and convolutional layers, starting with the Product Quantization (PQ) technique. PQ involves splitting weight columns into subvectors and mapping them to a learned codebook, effectively quantizing the weights. However, the paper argues that preserving the output of a layer is more crucial than preserving its weights. Therefore, the authors propose an alternative approach that directly minimizes the reconstruction error on the output activations when applying the layer to in-domain inputs, thus ensuring better fidelity in the model's performance.
