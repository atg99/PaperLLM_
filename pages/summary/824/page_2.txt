This paper presents a novel method for quantizing binary classifiers, specifically aimed at improving performance for in-domain inputs. The authors contrast their approach with standard quantization methods, which tend to perform poorly for in-domain data due to their objective functions that optimize across the entire input space. By introducing a new objective function, the authors demonstrate that their quantization method enables a classifier to better classify in-domain inputs, as illustrated in their example of distinguishing between dogs and cats.

The authors apply their method to the semi-supervised ResNet-50 model, achieving a significant reduction in memory footprint to 5 MB while maintaining a top-1 accuracy of 76.1% on ImageNet, resulting in a 20x compression relative to the original model. Additionally, the method is shown to generalize effectively to other tasks, such as image detection, where they successfully compress a Mask R-CNN model to around 6 MB with a 26x compression factor while retaining competitive performance.

The paper also discusses related work in the field of network compression, including low-precision training techniques that have evolved since early studies, various quantization methods, and network pruning strategies. It emphasizes the importance of optimizing the right objective function in quantization to avoid performance degradation in deep networks, highlighting the limitations of existing methods like vector and product quantization. Overall, the work contributes to the ongoing research in efficient neural network design, particularly in the context of resource-constrained environments.
