This paper addresses the challenge of reducing the memory footprint of convolutional network architectures, particularly focusing on preserving the quality of network output reconstructions rather than just the weights. The authors propose a vector quantization method that minimizes reconstruction error on in-domain inputs using unlabelled data during quantization. Their approach allows for efficient inference on CPUs through byte-aligned codebooks for compressed weights.

The authors validate their method by quantizing a ResNet-50 model to a memory size of 5MB, achieving a compression factor of 20 while maintaining a top-1 accuracy of 76.1% on ImageNet classification. They also demonstrate a 26x compression factor for a Mask R-CNN model.

The introduction highlights the increasing necessity of compressing convolutional networks for deployment on embedded devices, driven by advancements in network architectures like ResNets and DenseNets. The authors leverage structured quantization algorithms, specifically Product Quantization (PQ), to exploit spatial redundancy in convolution filters. Unlike traditional quantization methods, their approach emphasizes the accuracy of activations through a novel weighted k-means technique.

The compression is performed sequentially to manage dependencies between layers, utilizing a distillation training process with unlabelled data to guide the compression and fine-tune the codewords. This method contrasts with other approaches that do not compress classifier weights or use different quantization techniques. The paper positions its contributions as advancing the field of neural network compression, particularly for high-performing architectures.
