The paper discusses the challenges of splitting deep neural networks for distributed inference, particularly on resource-constrained edge devices. It highlights that horizontal partitioning of networks leads to significant communication costs due to the need for access to all channels from the previous layer. This issue is exacerbated in low-frequency environments typical of IoT devices, necessitating a new approach to model compression that considers memory and communication constraints.

The proposed solution is structured in two stages. First, it utilizes network science principles to partition the teacher's knowledge into disjoint segments that minimize communication costs among independent student networks. Second, it introduces a NoNN architecture tailored for each student, aiming to reduce memory usage and computation requirements while training these student models on the distinct partitions of the teacher's knowledge.

The problem of knowledge partitioning is formally defined, focusing on the final convolution layer of the teacher network. The approach involves identifying and removing non-contributory filters to enhance validation accuracy. The partition sizes must be approximately equal to meet memory and computation budgets, leading to a formulated optimization problem that seeks to balance partition effectiveness with resource constraints.

In summary, the paper presents a novel method for partitioning deep learning models to enable efficient distributed inference on IoT devices, addressing significant communication and computational challenges.
