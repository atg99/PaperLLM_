The paper "Memory- and Communication-Aware Model Compression for Distributed Deep Learning Inference on IoT" by Kartikeya Bhardwaj et al. addresses the challenges of deploying deep learning models on resource-constrained Internet-of-Things (IoT) devices. It highlights that traditional model compression techniques do not account for communication costs, which become significant when compressed models must be distributed across multiple devices due to memory limitations.

To tackle this, the authors propose a novel distributed learning paradigm called Network of Neural Networks (NoNN). This approach compresses a large pre-trained "teacher" model into several disjoint, highly-compressed "student" modules while maintaining accuracy. A key innovation is their network science-based knowledge partitioning algorithm, which effectively divides the teacher model into manageable parts for training individual student modules.

Experimental results on five image classification datasets demonstrate that NoNN can achieve higher accuracy than several baselines, with accuracy comparable to the teacher model, all while minimizing communication among student modules. In a case study with the CIFAR-10 dataset, the authors show remarkable improvements in memory footprint (up to 24 times), performance (up to 12 times), and energy efficiency (up to 14 times) compared to the original teacher model. Furthermore, NoNN leads to a reduction in total latency by up to 33 times compared to state-of-the-art model compression methods for distributed inference on multiple edge devices.

Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in the efficient deployment of deep learning models in IoT environments, addressing both memory and communication constraints effectively.
