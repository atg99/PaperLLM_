The paper presents results comparing various models for the CIFAR-100 dataset, focusing on the performance of the NoNN model against traditional teacher-student frameworks such as Knowledge Distillation (KD) and Adaptive Transfer Knowledge Distillation (ATKD). The teacher model, WRN28-10, demonstrates an accuracy of 79.28%, while the NoNN-2S model, with only 1.5 million parameters, achieves an accuracy of 75.74%, which is higher than both KD and ATKD models with similar parameter counts.

The results indicate that the NoNN model can effectively create disjoint student models that fit within memory constraints, allowing for further distribution of computations using techniques like MoDNN. This is significant as it promotes model efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.

Additionally, the NoNN model outperforms SplitNet models of similar sizes, achieving 79.07% accuracy for a 7.42M parameter model and 77.42% for a 4.13M parameter model, while the NoNN-2S model achieves higher accuracy with fewer parameters. The paper also explores the impact of varying the number of students, demonstrating that NoNN models maintain close accuracy to the teacher model while significantly reducing memory and FLOPS requirements, with reductions ranging from 2.5× to 24× in parameters and 2× to 15× in FLOPS compared to the teacher model.
