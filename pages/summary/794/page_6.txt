The paper discusses knowledge distillation (KD)-based techniques for distributed inference in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), highlighting their application in network science for model compression. The authors introduce key concepts from network theory, such as node degree and community structure, which are relevant to their approach.

In the context of network science, node degree refers to the number of connections a node has, with certain nodes acting as hubs due to their high connectivity. Community structure is characterized by groups of nodes that are more densely connected to each other than to the rest of the network, allowing for natural groupings without predefined parameters.

The motivation for this research stems from the challenges of horizontally splitting a CNN when it cannot fit into a single memory-constrained device. The authors conduct an experiment using a WideResNet model trained on the CIFAR-10 dataset, demonstrating that such a split can lead to significant communication overhead. When deploying the model across two powerful machines, the inference time increased dramatically from 86ms to 1006ms due to the need for all devices to access outputs from each other. This highlights the inefficiencies that arise when attempting to execute deep networks in a distributed manner, particularly in resource-constrained environments like IoT devices.
