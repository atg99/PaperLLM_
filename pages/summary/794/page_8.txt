The paper discusses a method for knowledge partitioning in neural networks, particularly focusing on filter activation patterns from a pretrained teacher model. The approach involves several key steps: 

1. **Activation Patterns Analysis**: The teacher model's activation patterns for the validation set are analyzed to build a network of filter activity. This network allows for community detection, which helps in partitioning the teacher's knowledge into simpler functions.

2. **Student Training**: Individual student models are trained using the NoNN framework to replicate each partition of the teacher’s knowledge. The method emphasizes the importance of maintaining a balance between the sizes of student models and their performance, adhering to fixed memory and computational constraints.

3. **Knowledge Distribution**: The study presents a network science-based solution for partitioning knowledge from the teacher model's convolutional layer. It constructs a filter activation network where the average activity of each filter is used as a measure of its importance for specific classes of images. Filters with higher average activity are deemed more significant for classification tasks.

4. **Constraints and Objectives**: The paper outlines constraints to ensure that student models remain within memory and computational limits while minimizing the impact on the teacher's validation accuracy through the removal of less critical filters.

Overall, the research highlights a structured approach to efficiently distribute knowledge from a teacher model to student models, optimizing their performance based on filter activity patterns.
