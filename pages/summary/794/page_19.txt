The paper investigates the performance and energy consumption of a network of neural networks (NoNN) using Raspberry Pi (RPi) devices and a more powerful Odroid board. It highlights how the workload distribution affects latency and energy efficiency while processing a total of eight students. 

In the setup, each RPi handles one student, while the Odroid can manage multiple students due to its superior processing capabilities. The analysis in Figure 7(a) reveals that a single Odroid can execute four students in the same time it takes an RPi to handle one. However, as the number of students assigned to the Odroid increases, its latency becomes a bottleneck, leading to increased total latency. The communication time between the server and devices accounts for an average of 30.65 ms, while the server's processing time is negligible at 0.1 ms.

Figure 7(b) illustrates the energy consumption patterns, indicating a trade-off between performance, energy use, and the number of devices. When the Odroid processes just one student, it demonstrates better energy efficiency compared to multiple RPis. However, as the workload on the Odroid increases, total energy consumption rises, even though fewer devices are used. The Odroid ultimately consumes 25% more energy when handling all eight students compared to using eight separate RPis.

The evaluation concludes with three key insights: (i) the NoNN approach performs well in both homogeneous and heterogeneous setups, (ii) distributing tasks across multiple devices can lower total energy consumption compared to using a single, powerful device, and (iii) maximizing energy savings involves strategically deploying NoNN across various devices to balance energy dissipation and the number of devices utilized.

Additionally, the paper discusses the robustness of the NoNN system by evaluating scenarios where devices might fail, considering various failure combinations among active devices to ensure reliability in operation.
