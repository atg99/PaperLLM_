The paper discusses the performance of a novel framework called Network of Neural Networks (NoNN) in the context of transfer learning and model compression. The results are presented in Table 5, which compares various models based on their parameters, FLOPS (floating point operations per second), and accuracy across different datasets, specifically Indoor Scene Classification and Caltech-UCSD Birds (CUB). 

The teacher model used is ResNet-152, which achieves an accuracy of 79.44%. The NoNN architecture, consisting of two separate ResNet-18 models that do not communicate until the final fully connected layer, demonstrates competitive accuracy while significantly reducing FLOPS and parameters compared to the teacher model. For instance, NoNN-2S achieves an accuracy of 79.81% and showcases a 2× reduction in FLOPS and 2.5× reduction in parameters.

In contrast, larger student models like WRN-40-2, while achieving similar accuracy, present challenges in terms of distribution across devices due to their size and increased inter-device communication requirements. The paper emphasizes the advantages of NoNN in maintaining high accuracy with minimal communication.

Preliminary results on the ImageNet dataset further validate NoNN's effectiveness, showing it achieves comparable top-1 and top-5 accuracy to baseline models while utilizing lower parameter counts. The study concludes that NoNN not only achieves higher accuracy than several baseline approaches but also facilitates efficient communication among student models, highlighting its potential for effective transfer learning and model deployment in resource-constrained environments.
