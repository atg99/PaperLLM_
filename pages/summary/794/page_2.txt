The paper discusses the challenges of deploying deep learning models on memory-constrained Internet of Things (IoT) devices, specifically those with only 500KB of available memory. Traditional deep learning models, such as AlexNet and ResNet, are unsuitable due to their large size, necessitating the development of highly efficient compressed models for fast inference.

To address this, various model compression techniques are explored, including pruning, quantization, and Knowledge Distillation (KD). Pruning removes redundant weights without sacrificing accuracy, while quantization reduces the bit representation of weights and activations. KD involves training a smaller student model to mimic a larger teacher model, allowing for significant model compression.

The paper highlights the limitation of existing approaches for extremely constrained environments, where even compressed models may exceed memory budgets and require distribution across multiple devices. This distribution incurs heavy communication overhead, complicating deployment. The authors propose a novel approach, NoNN, which generates disjoint student models that fit within individual IoT devices, thus minimizing communication until the final layer. 

The focus is primarily on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for image classification, with an acknowledgment that future work could explore distributed inference for other architectures, such as Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) for speech and natural language processing applications.
