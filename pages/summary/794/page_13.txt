The paper presents results comparing various neural network models on the CIFAR-10 dataset, highlighting the performance of a new architecture called NoNN. The study particularly emphasizes the advantages of NoNN in terms of parameter efficiency and communication costs when distributed across devices. 

Table 2 summarizes the performance of different models, with the teacher model being a Wide Residual Network (WRN) with 8.9 million parameters achieving an accuracy of 95.49%. In contrast, the NoNN-2S-XL, which has 0.46 million parameters, achieves an accuracy of 94.53%, showcasing the effectiveness of the NoNN approach. The NoNN architecture allows for disjoint student models that do not communicate until the final layer, thereby reducing communication overhead that is prevalent in traditional models that communicate at every layer.

The paper argues that the NoNN model can maintain high accuracy while fitting within strict memory constraints, as individual student models can operate efficiently on devices with limited memory. It also highlights that the increase in accuracy for NoNN-2S-XL is significantly influenced by community-based knowledge transfer from the teacher model, rather than just an increase in computational resources.

Additionally, the scalability of the NoNN architecture is discussed, indicating its applicability to various datasets and model sizes. The subsequent section of the paper presents results for the CIFAR-100 dataset, further evaluating the NoNN architecture against other models like SplitNet, thereby confirming its robustness and versatility in different configurations.
