The paper discusses the deployment of NoNN models on hardware-constrained devices, specifically Raspberry Pi and Odroid-XU4S, to evaluate their performance in terms of latency and energy consumption. The NoNN models demonstrate significant improvements, achieving accuracy with significantly lower memory and fewer floating-point operations compared to the teacher model. 

The study includes a detailed hardware setup where both homogeneous and heterogeneous scenarios are explored. In the homogeneous case, identical devices are used, while the heterogeneous case involves devices with different resources and computing power. The evaluation focuses on three models: the teacher model WRN40-4, NoNN-2S, and NoNN-8S, all trained on the CIFAR-10 dataset.

The hardware setup consists of eight Raspberry Pi-3 Model-B devices and eight Odroid-XU4S boards, with the Raspberry Pi devices running at a reduced frequency to avoid instability. The Odroid-XU4S utilizes a big.LITTLE architecture for optimized performance. The performance metrics, including voltage, current, and power, were measured using specific tools for each device.

The deployment of the models involved using the TVM deep learning compiler to convert PyTorch models into a format suitable for the target devices. The experiments reveal that when all devices are identical, the NoNN models maintain performance while significantly reducing latency compared to the teacher model, which is executed on a single edge device to avoid excessive latency. The results indicate the effectiveness of NoNN models in resource-constrained environments, paving the way for energy-efficient deep learning applications.
