The paper presents a comparison of the performance, accuracy, and energy efficiency of a neural network framework called NoNN (Network of Neural Networks) on two different hardware platforms: Raspberry Pi and Odroid-XU4S. The results are summarized in tables that detail the accuracy, latency, and energy consumption for two configurations of NoNN, referred to as NoNN-2S and NoNN-8S, which utilize two and eight student models, respectively.

Key findings indicate that NoNN-2S significantly improves performance and energy efficiency, achieving 12.22 times better performance and 14.36 times better energy per node compared to the teacher model on Raspberry Pi. Similarly, NoNN-8S shows improvements of 9.37 times in latency and 13.77 times in energy per node. For Odroid boards, NoNN-2S demonstrates 8.96 times better performance and 14.08 times better energy efficiency, while NoNN-8S shows improvements of 6.22 times in latency and 12.99 times in energy.

The theoretical FLOPS for NoNN is reduced by approximately 15 times compared to the teacher model, yet it maintains accuracy within a range of 0.5% to 1.17%. The average latency breakdown reveals that the computation time increases minimally when moving from 2S to 8S, but communication latency increases substantially, particularly for Raspberry Pi.

The paper further compares the latency of NoNN against a traditional model compression method, ATKD, which has achieved similar accuracy results. The NoNN framework not only offers significant gains in performance and energy efficiency but also shows that the communication overhead is relatively low compared to the latency incurred when using larger, split teacher models. Overall, NoNN demonstrates a promising approach for distributed inference in neural networks while maintaining competitive accuracy and efficiency metrics.
