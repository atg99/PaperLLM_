The paper presents a comparison of the NoNN-8S and Split-ATKD models using Raspberry Pi devices for distributed inference in PyTorch. The results, summarized in Table 8, indicate that both models achieve similar accuracy rates of approximately 95%. However, they differ significantly in terms of parameters, FLOPS, and latency.

In the Split-ATKD experiment, two configurations were tested: one with four Raspberry Pi devices (split-ATKD-4S) and one with eight devices (split-ATKD-8S). The split-ATKD-4S configuration has each device storing around 550K parameters and executing 163M FLOPS, while split-ATKD-8S has each device with 275K parameters and 82M FLOPS. Despite the lower computational load of the split-ATKD models, the communication overhead at each layer results in total latencies of 23 seconds for split-ATKD-4S and 28.5 seconds for split-ATKD-8S. In contrast, NoNN-8S demonstrates a significantly lower latency of only 0.85 seconds, which is 27 times and 33 times faster than the split-ATKD configurations, respectively.

The findings emphasize the effectiveness of the NoNN model for distributed deep learning inference on IoT devices, highlighting its greater efficiency despite the comparable accuracy to the Split-ATKD models. Additionally, an experiment with heterogeneous devices showed NoNN's adaptability, further supporting its application across various resource-constrained environments. The paper concludes that the optimizations provided by NoNN are crucial for enhancing performance in distributed deep learning scenarios.
