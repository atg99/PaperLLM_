The paper discusses the concept of a Filter Activation Network (FAN) that connects filters in a neural network based on their activation properties. It introduces two main strategies for connecting filters: Activation Hubs (AH) and Co-Activation (CA). The AH strategy promotes connections between important filters and less important ones, ensuring that highly important filters from the same class do not cluster together, thereby distributing knowledge across different filters. The CA strategy connects filters that activate for similar classes, potentially leading to unbalanced student models if classes are too similar. 

The authors focus on the AH strategy to maintain uniformity in student model sizes. They mathematically represent the AH network using an adjacency matrix and propose a method for partitioning the network into communities that maximize a modularity function. This involves grouping tightly connected nodes while ensuring that the resulting partitions are of similar sizes for training student networks. The study concludes that removing a community of disconnected nodes has minimal impact on the teacher model's accuracy, allowing for efficient community detection and partitioning.
