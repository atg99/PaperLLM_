The paper by K. Bhardwaj et al. presents a framework for knowledge transfer in neural networks, specifically focusing on the NoNN (No Knowledge Network) architecture. The study evaluates the performance of teacher, baseline student, and NoNN models across various datasets, including CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, as well as transfer learning scenarios.

In the experiments, the authors utilize a Wide Residual Network (WRN) as the teacher model for CIFAR-10 and a smaller WRN as the baseline student model. The findings indicate that the NoNN-2S model outperforms the baseline student model in terms of accuracy while adhering to a computational budget. The NoNN framework allows for communication among students solely at the fully connected layer, which helps in reducing the number of parameters and FLOPS required.

The results demonstrate that, despite each NoNN student utilizing fewer FLOPS, the model achieves competitive accuracy levels with a total parameter count that remains comparable to the baseline model. The implementation is carried out using PyTorch on NVIDIA GPUs. Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of the NoNN architecture in transferring knowledge while maintaining efficiency in resource usage.
