This paper introduces VV-NET, a novel algorithm designed for point cloud segmentation by transforming unstructured point clouds into regular voxel grids. The approach employs a kernel-based interpolated variational autoencoder (VAE) to encode local geometry within each voxel, addressing the limitations of traditional voxel representations that only capture Boolean occupancy information. By incorporating radial basis functions (RBF), the method computes a continuous representation within voxels, effectively managing sparse point distributions and enhancing robustness against noisy datasets.

The authors also introduce group equivariant convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to 3D data processing, defining convolution operations on a symmetry group acting on Z3 and its isomorphic sets. This innovation improves expressive capacity without increasing the number of parameters, leading to enhanced segmentation results. The performance of VV-NET is validated against standard benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art segmentation algorithms on ShapeNet and S3DIS datasets.

The paper emphasizes the need for effective learning methods that can retain and exploit spatial distributions in 3D data processing. By transforming point clouds into voxel grids and utilizing smooth RBF signals in conjunction with VAE, the authors present a compact representation of point distributions. Additionally, the use of group equivariant convolutions helps ensure that segmentation results remain invariant to transformations like rotation and scaling, addressing a critical challenge in the field.
