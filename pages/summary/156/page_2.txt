This paper introduces VV-Net, a novel Voxel Variational Autoencoder network that utilizes group convolutions to enhance point cloud segmentation. The method effectively segments objects into parts and identifies individual semantic objects within 3D scenes. Evaluations on standard datasets like ShapeNet and S3DIS demonstrate that VV-Net outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques, achieving improvements of 2.7% and 16.12% in mean Intersection over Union (IoU), respectively. Notably, VV-Net maintains robust performance even when faced with incorrectly labeled ground truth data.

Key contributions of the work include the development of an information-rich voxel-based representation that captures point distribution within each voxel using a variational autoencoder with Radial Basis Function (RBF) input. This approach combines the benefits of structured data with detailed distribution capture, enhancing learning capabilities. Additionally, the introduction of group convolutions tailored for 3D data encodes symmetry and increases network expressiveness without adding parameters.

The paper also reviews related work in 3D data processing, highlighting the evolution of deep learning applications for point cloud data, including PointNet and various hierarchical and mesh-based segmentation approaches. It addresses challenges in neighborhood mining and voxel processing, emphasizing the limitations of existing methods in capturing topology and spatial distribution. The authors argue that their method's focus on symmetry rather than explicit neighborhood encoding provides a more effective solution for point cloud segmentation.
