In this paper, Dai and Wipf explore the relationship between the density \( p_{t}^{\star}(x) \) and the probability measure \( \nu_{gt}(x) \) in the context of probability distributions defined on different dimensional spaces. Unlike the case where the dimensions are equal, \( p_{t}^{\star}(x) \) is defined as a density across all of \( \mathbb{R}^d \), while \( \nu_{gt} \) is confined to a lower-dimensional manifold \( \mathcal{F} \). 

To compare these two, the authors evaluate the probability mass assigned to a measurable subset \( A \) of \( \mathbb{R}^d \). For \( p_{t}^{\star}(x) \), this is done by integrating the density over \( A \), while for \( \nu_{gt} \), the measure is determined by the intersection of \( A \) with the manifold \( \mathcal{F} \).

The authors derive an expression for \( p_{t}^{\star}(x) \) through integration, leading to a representation that incorporates \( \nu_{gt} \). They apply Fubini's theorem to interchange the order of integration and utilize the bounded convergence theorem to justify interchanging limits and integrals.

As \( t \) approaches infinity, the term \( N(x|x_{0}; \mathcal{I}_{t}) \) converges to a Dirac delta function, which indicates that the integration over \( A \) relies on whether \( x_{0} \) is within \( A \). This leads to a separation of the manifold \( \mathcal{F} \) into three distinct parts, allowing for a detailed analysis of the contributions from each section to the overall limit.

The results demonstrate a clear framework for relating the density and the measure, highlighting the impact of the manifold's structure on the probability distributions involved.
