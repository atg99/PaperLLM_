The paper "Diagnosing and Enhancing VAE Models" by Bin Dai and David Wipf explores the limitations of variational autoencoders (VAEs), particularly regarding the Gaussian encoder/decoder assumptions that are thought to hinder the generation of realistic samples. The authors analyze the VAE objective to clarify when these assumptions are detrimental and when they are not. Based on their findings, they propose a straightforward enhancement to the VAE that does not require additional hyperparameters or sensitive tuning. This enhancement leads to improved sample quality and stable Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores, significantly narrowing the gap between VAEs and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), while preserving the beneficial characteristics of the original VAE architecture. The work is briefly summarized in a shorter version accepted at the ICLR 2019 conference, with the implementation code made publicly available. The authors emphasize the importance of understanding the probabilistic generative model's structure, particularly in cases where observable variables exist within a low-dimensional manifold embedded in a higher-dimensional space.
