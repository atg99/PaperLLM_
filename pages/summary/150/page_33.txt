The paper discusses the diagnosis and enhancement of Variational Autoencoder (VAE) models, focusing on the relationship between the true posterior distribution and the approximate posterior in high-dimensional spaces. It establishes that the first set of dimensions can perfectly match the true posterior, while the remaining dimensions follow a standardized Gaussian distribution, contributing no information to the generation of data. Consequently, these dimensions' true posterior aligns with the prior distribution, which is also a standardized Gaussian, and their independence implies that the covariance's off-diagonal elements are zero.

The authors present a theorem supported by a proof in Appendix F, which constructs a bijection between a manifold and a higher-dimensional space, transforming the ground-truth measure into a normal Gaussian distribution. The proof details a sequence of transformations using a diffeomorphism, ensuring that both the original and transformed distributions remain non-zero everywhere. This work ultimately aims to improve the convergence of the log-probability of the data under the model, enhancing the performance of VAE models in capturing complex data distributions.
