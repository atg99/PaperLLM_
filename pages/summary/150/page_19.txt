The paper discusses the essential criteria for generating realistic samples using Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and similar encoder-decoder architectures. Two key criteria are identified: achieving a small reconstruction error when data passes through the networks, and ensuring that the aggregate posterior distribution closely resembles a known distribution, such as a standard Gaussian. While the first criterion can be met by deterministic autoencoders, the second is crucial for generating realistic outputs from the learned decoder.

The authors highlight various approaches to address these criteria, including those by Tomczak and Welling, as well as Zhao et al., which involve using parameterized prior distributions or adversarial training to align the posterior with the prior. However, these methods have not demonstrated improvements in Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores, with previous studies focusing on limited metrics.

The paper contrasts these approaches with the WAE model, which aims to enhance FID scores by minimizing an objective function that combines reconstruction penalties with a Wassenstein distance measure. The proposed methodology in this paper consists of two VAE stages that correspond directly to the aforementioned criteria. The first stage focuses on minimizing variances in both the encoder and decoder to achieve accurate reconstruction, while the analysis indicates that the resulting posterior may not necessarily align closely with the desired distribution.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of balancing reconstruction accuracy and the alignment of the posterior distribution in improving the performance of VAE models.
