The paper discusses the behavior and optimization of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) through the lens of Theorem 5, which builds upon the foundations established in Theorem 4. It asserts that for any input x drawn from the distribution gt, the VAE encoder and decoder can perfectly reconstruct x at optimal solutions, even in the presence of stochastic noise represented by Sz(x; θ). 

The analysis highlights that when the latent variable z has no redundant capacity (indicated by r), the squared eigenvalues of Sz(x; θ) diminish proportionally to the small parameter ε, suggesting that the reconstruction error becomes negligible near optimal solutions. The VAE data term integrand behaves predictably, yielding a theoretical lower bound that cannot be significantly reduced further.

When additional latent dimensions are included (where r < 0), the paper argues that this does not enhance the VAE data term and may adversely affect the KL regularization term, particularly as ε approaches zero. The pressure to minimize the number of low-noise latent dimensions is emphasized, as maintaining too few can lead to significant reconstruction errors, pushing the cost function towards infinity. 

Ultimately, the findings suggest that in the vicinity of optimal solutions, VAEs inherently strive to achieve perfect reconstructions while utilizing the minimal number of effective latent dimensions, thus balancing data fit and model complexity.
