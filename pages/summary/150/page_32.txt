The paper by Dai and Wipf discusses the convergence of certain probability distributions as a parameter t approaches infinity. Specifically, it evaluates the limit of two distributions, q0 and p0, which depend on latent variables z0 and x. The authors derive an expression showing that as t approaches infinity, the ratio of these distributions converges to one, indicating that their Kullback-Leibler divergence approaches zero.

In the case where the number of latent dimensions (denoted as ϑ) exceeds r, the authors propose a method to utilize the first r latent dimensions to construct a projection between the variables z and x, leaving the remaining dimensions unused. They define the encoder and decoder functions accordingly, ensuring that as t approaches infinity, the decoder produces the same distribution as previously defined.

The paper highlights that the additional latent dimensions do not contribute to the generative process and establishes a framework for defining the encoder with respect to the latent variables. Finally, it discusses the variance of the approximate posterior, emphasizing the properties of the null space of the defined functions.

Overall, the study provides insights into the behavior of probability distributions in the context of latent variable models and offers a structured approach for generalization in scenarios with varying latent dimensions.
