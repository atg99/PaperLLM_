In this paper, Dai and Wipf explore the properties of the logarithm of the conditional probability distribution p(z|x), which is expressed as a quadratic form in z. They demonstrate that the log probability can be decomposed into terms involving the normal distributions of z and x, leading to an expression that indicates p(x) must be Gaussian for the KL divergence between p(z|x) and q(z|x) to be zero. However, this contradicts the assumption that p_gt(x) is not Gaussian, suggesting that the KL divergence is always greater than zero, which implies that the loss function L cannot achieve the theoretical optimal solution represented by the integral of p_gt(x) log p_gt(x).

The authors denote the gap between the loss function L and the optimal solution as ε. They argue that there exists a threshold t₀ such that for any t > t₀, the gap between their proposed solution and the theoretical optimal solution becomes smaller than ε. By selecting an appropriate t, they show that a better solution can indeed be achieved, indicating that their approach can improve upon the existing solutions.

In a second case where the dimensionality r of z is less than d, the KL divergence does not need to be zero. They analyze the objective function, demonstrating that it can be bounded and leads to a conclusion that the loss function remains less than a specific logarithmic term, thus providing insights into the behavior of the KL divergence and the implications for the loss function in relation to the dimensionality of the involved variables.
