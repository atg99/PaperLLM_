In their work, Dai and Wipf discuss the challenges associated with identifying disentangled representations in latent variable models, particularly concerning factors such as gender and age. They illustrate this issue using a 2D binary vector representation, where two candidate encodings exhibit zero total correlation between their latent factors. Despite this, only one encoding allows for independent manipulation of gender (z1) and age (z2), representing a disentangled structure. The second encoding, however, leads to simultaneous changes in both factors when varying z1, indicating an entangled representation.

The authors emphasize that without supervision or explicit labels for the underlying factors, it is fundamentally impossible for generative models, such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), to distinguish between these encodings. They acknowledge that some studies have reported partial identification of disentangled representations, but they attribute these findings to specific experimental conditions that may not generalize. Their empirical tests have not produced consistent disentangled representations, leading them to reference the comprehensive work of Locatello et al. (2018), which provides a broader examination of this topic.
