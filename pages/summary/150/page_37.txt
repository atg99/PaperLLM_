The paper discusses the diagnosis and enhancement of Variational Autoencoder (VAE) models, focusing on the mathematical foundations of their performance. It presents a detailed analysis of the behavior of probability densities associated with ground-truth and approximate posteriors.

Key findings include the exploration of the limits of certain integrals as a parameter t approaches infinity, particularly in relation to boundary conditions on a set A. The results indicate that under specific assumptions, the integral converges to zero, highlighting the nuanced relationship between the densities involved. This addresses the fact that while the approximate posterior can assign zero mass to certain regions, it can still align closely with the ground-truth distribution when considering infinitesimally expanded subsets.

In the proof of Theorem 4, the paper defines the true posterior and the approximate posterior, illustrating that the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between them is strictly positive under the condition that they differ in distribution. The authors argue this by contradiction, emphasizing that if the KL divergence were zero, it would imply that both distributions are identical, which is generally not the case.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of considering boundary effects and the behavior of posteriors in the context of VAEs, providing insights into the conditions under which these models can be effectively diagnosed and enhanced.
