In this section, Dai and Wipf emphasize the importance of training the two-stage Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for achieving high-quality results. They begin with quantitative evaluations of their proposed two-stage VAE compared to various GAN and VAE baselines, as well as experiments that validate their theoretical findings. 

The quantitative evaluation follows the large-scale testing protocol established by Lucic et al. (2018), which highlights that GANs traditionally outperform VAEs in terms of Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores. To ensure a fair comparison, the authors employed a standardized architecture across all models. Their approach involved a first-stage VAE with a common encoder and decoder architecture and a low-dimensional second-stage VAE using small networks that added minimal parameters.

The proposed two-stage VAE was compared against three baseline VAE models, differing in the decoder output layer, and included adaptations for a Gaussian decoder with fixed and learned parameters, a cross-entropy layer, and a Gaussian decoder combined with normalizing flows. Additionally, they tested the Wasserstein autoencoder (WAE) that retains a VAE-like structure. The WAE-MMD variant was chosen for comparison due to its stability compared to adversarial training methods.

The evaluation utilized four distinct datasets: MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CelebA, and included results from various competitive GAN models to contextualize performance. Overall, the results from this empirical evaluation are expected to demonstrate the enhanced capabilities of the proposed two-stage VAE in generating high-quality samples.
