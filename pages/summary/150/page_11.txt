The paper investigates the performance of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) compared to Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) using Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores across multiple datasets. The authors conducted 10 trials for each dataset without tuning VAE hyperparameters, applying a generic setting across all models. Their findings indicate that the 2-Stage VAE performs competitively with the best GAN model, while other VAE approaches did not match the performance.

The poor performance of the WAE-MMD on simpler datasets like MNIST and Fashion MNIST is attributed to its sensitivity to a fixed parameter value, which may be higher than necessary for these types. The paper also discusses a broader evaluation of GAN models using optimized hyperparameters and acknowledges that despite the advantages of GANs, the 2-Stage VAE's FID scores are comparable.

To further validate sample quality assessments, the authors mention the Kernel Inception Distance (KID) as a potentially superior metric to FID, though they could only compare their VAE to other VAE/AE models due to limitations in evaluating all GAN baselines. The results using KID reinforce the improvements seen with FID, thereby supporting the effectiveness of their VAE approach. Consistency in implementation across different frameworks is emphasized as crucial for accurate result calibration. Overall, the study presents a VAE pipeline that can compete with GANs in generating high-quality samples.
