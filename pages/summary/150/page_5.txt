The paper discusses the challenges and strategies associated with diagnosing and enhancing Variational Autoencoder (VAE) models, particularly focusing on the implications of Gaussian assumptions in VAE training. It presents a scenario where the global optimum of the objective function can be achieved without the constraints imposed by Gaussian assumptions, emphasizing the importance of accurately capturing the underlying data manifold.

The authors introduce a manifold density, denoted as ~pgt(x), which represents the probability density associated with the true data distribution on the manifold. They establish Theorem 3, which asserts that when the dimensionality of the data (d) exceeds the dimensionality of the manifold (r), it is possible to find a sequence of VAE parameters that minimizes the objective function while allowing the model to approximate the true distribution closely.

Theorem 3 indicates that the VAE can converge to a solution where the KL divergence between the approximate and true posterior approaches zero and the negative log-likelihood approaches negative infinity. This suggests that the Gaussian assumptions do not inherently prevent the model from achieving a globally optimal solution and can still yield a density estimate that closely resembles the ground-truth measure.

However, the authors caution that while both Theorem 2 and Theorem 3 detail conditions for achieving minimal objectives, the uniqueness of the solution differs significantly. In the case where r equals d, the ground-truth measure must be recovered almost everywhere to achieve the lower bound. In contrast, when r is less than d, different parameter settings could lead to the same objective value without adequately approximating the true distribution.

This highlights a fundamental challenge in VAE modeling: the lack of direct access to the true low-dimensional manifold forces the model to rely on a more complex density, which can result in a non-unique solution. The paper emphasizes that this issue is not solely due to the Gaussian assumptions but is an intrinsic difficulty of the VAE framework itself.
