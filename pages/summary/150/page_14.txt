The paper by Dai and Wipf presents a validation of their proposed theorems regarding Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and their performance in image reconstruction. Figure 1 illustrates the convergence of the log of the square pixel error during training, indicating effective learning. The comparison between VAEs with fixed and learnable parameters reveals that the latter consistently yields lower reconstruction errors and better Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores across multiple datasets, suggesting improved image quality.

The authors further validate Theorem 5 by examining the effects of noise in the latent space, demonstrating that perturbations in directions with large eigenvalues have minimal impact on the decoder output, while smaller eigenvalues lead to significant changes, confirming the model's ability to navigate the manifold effectively.

Despite achieving high-quality reconstructions, the paper notes a challenge in achieving a standard Gaussian distribution for the aggregated posterior, which complicates the ancestral sampling process. The proposed 2-Stage VAE addresses this issue, as evidenced by the singular value spectrum comparisons and the metrics indicating a closer alignment to a standard Gaussian distribution in the second stage. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of high-quality reconstructions for effective generative modeling and the advantages of using learnable parameters within VAEs.
