In the paper by Dai and Wipf, the authors explore the capabilities of a -simple Variational Autoencoder (VAE) in achieving optimality criteria in scenarios where the manifold dimension is either equal to or less than the ambient space dimension. They define key components of the VAE framework, including tunable scalars and parameterized differentiable functions, which can be complex like deep neural networks.

The analysis begins with the case where the manifold dimension (r) equals the ambient space dimension (d). In this context, they assert that if the ground-truth probability density exists in the Euclidean space, the optimal conditions can be met where the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the approximate and actual posterior is zero, and the marginalized data distribution matches the ground-truth density. The authors present Theorem 2, which states that for r=d, a sequence of model parameters exists such that both the KL divergence and the marginalized distribution converge to the ground-truth density almost everywhere. This finding challenges the notion that richer distributions are necessary for optimal reconstruction, provided that the latent dimension is sufficiently large.

In the second scenario where the manifold dimension is less than the ambient space dimension (r<d), the authors note that additional complexities arise. They imply that when both the encoder and decoder distributions are unconstrained, different implications for VAE performance can be explored. The paper promises further unpacking of these subtleties in subsequent sections, indicating that the results from the first case can still inform and enhance VAE performance in the more complex scenario.
