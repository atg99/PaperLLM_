The paper discusses the implications of total correlation (TC) in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and its role in achieving disentangled representations. It defines TC as a measure of independence among latent variables, asserting that TC equals zero when the aggregate posterior distribution is composed of independent factors of variation. The authors argue that minimizing TC can promote desirable disentangled representations, as supported by previous work.

However, the paper highlights a critical limitation: minimizing TC does not guarantee that the latent factors correspond to semantically meaningful concepts. Without supervision or constraints, achieving meaningful disentanglement is statistically unidentifiable. The authors illustrate this with an example where data can be generated through an arbitrary transformation of latent factors, resulting in zero total correlation but retaining entangled representations relative to the original ground-truth factors.

The discussion emphasizes that even simplistic scenarios can exhibit this lack of identifiability when searching for disentangled representations. Overall, the paper provides a nuanced view of the relationship between total correlation and the interpretability of latent representations in VAEs, suggesting that further considerations are needed to ensure meaningful disentanglement.
