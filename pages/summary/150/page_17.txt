The paper discusses the performance of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) in terms of their latent dimensions and how these affect the models' ability to reconstruct and generate data. It presents empirical results using two datasets: Fashion and CelebA, measured by Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores for both reconstruction and generation tasks. 

Key findings indicate that different configurations of VAEs, including cross-entropy, fixed gamma, learnable gamma, Wasserstein Autoencoders (WAE), and a proposed two-stage VAE model, show varying FID scores as latent dimensions change. The results suggest that optimizing the latent dimension is crucial for improving both reconstruction and generation quality in VAE models. The comparison highlights the effectiveness of the proposed two-stage VAE in achieving better performance in FID scores across different latent dimensions.
