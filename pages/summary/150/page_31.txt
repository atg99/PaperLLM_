The paper discusses the convergence of the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between approximate and true posteriors in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). The authors focus on finding a sequence of encoders such that the KL divergence between the approximate posterior \( q \) and the true posterior \( p \) approaches zero as a parameter \( t \) tends to infinity.

Key definitions include the encoder networks and their differentiability, which is established through the composition of differentiable functions. The true posterior \( p_t(z|x) \) and approximate posterior \( q_t(z|x) \) are expressed in terms of normal distributions. 

A transformation is applied to the latent variable \( z \) to obtain \( z_0 \), and the KL divergence remains invariant under this transformation. The authors demonstrate that as \( t \) increases, the transformed posteriors \( q_0 \) and \( p_0 \) converge to a constant, specifically 1, indicating that the KL divergence converges to zero.

The expression for the limiting ratio of the posteriors is derived, emphasizing the importance of the terms related to the distributions and their independence from \( z_0 \). The findings suggest that careful construction of encoder networks can facilitate the desired convergence in VAEs, enhancing their performance.
