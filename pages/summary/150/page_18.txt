In the study by Dai and Wipf, the authors present results from their two-stage Variational Autoencoder (VAE) model, showcasing randomly generated samples from the MNIST and CelebA datasets. They acknowledge a common belief that there is a trade-off between stable training and the high-quality images produced by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). While they do not claim that their model surpasses the best GAN architectures in terms of image realism, they argue that their two-stage VAE significantly narrows this gap, making VAEs viable for a wider range of applications.

The discussion highlights the connections between their work and existing VAE improvements. Unlike previous enhancements that primarily aimed at increasing log-likelihood scores for test data, their focus is on enhancing visual quality. They note that while various methods have replaced Gaussian encoders with more complex distributions to achieve better log-likelihoods, these improvements do not necessarily translate to better visual outputs. Furthermore, they suggest that addressing the uniqueness issue within VAEs could also benefit from a second stage, indicating potential for future enhancements in VAE architectures.
