The paper discusses the performance and characteristics of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) in modeling complex datasets, specifically MNIST and CelebA. It presents experimental results that validate theoretical predictions regarding the latent space and the impact of random perturbations along different directions. 

The authors analyze the eigenvalues associated with the latent variables, noting that larger eigenvalues indicate less sensitivity to perturbations. For instance, in the initial rows of their experiments, significant visual differences are observed in the generated images, showcasing how certain dimensions capture more crucial attributes. In contrast, as the eigenvalues decrease, the generated images exhibit subtle differences, ultimately leading to complete inactivity at an eigenvalue of one, where all outputs become identical.

The paper also includes histograms of the eigenvalues for both datasets, revealing that while both converge towards zero or one, the CelebA dataset has more eigenvalues near zero due to its higher complexity and likely larger ground-truth manifold dimension compared to MNIST. This observation underscores the VAE's capability to effectively discern the manifold dimension and select an appropriate number of latent dimensions in complex environments.
