The paper discusses a two-stage approach to diagnose and enhance Variational Autoencoder (VAE) models, particularly when the decoder does not closely approximate the ground-truth distribution. The proposed remedy involves the following steps:

1. Train a simple VAE with a minimal number of active latent dimensions to estimate the unknown r-dimensional ground-truth manifold embedded in a higher-dimensional space. Latent samples are generated from the learned distribution.

2. Train a second simple VAE with independent parameters to learn the distribution of the latent samples generated in the first stage. This distribution is treated as a new ground-truth distribution.

3. Use the second-stage VAE to generate samples that approximate the original ground-truth distribution through an extended ancestral process.

The efficacy of this two-stage method lies in ensuring that the latent space encompasses the necessary dimensions to represent the manifold effectively. If the first stage is successful, the samples will cover the full ambient space, and the second-stage VAE can then be trained to align closely with the new ground-truth distribution.

The paper emphasizes that concatenating the two stages and training them jointly does not yield better performance. Joint training tends to prioritize the reconstruction term of the first stage, hindering the second stage from addressing the mismatch between the learned distribution and the assumed Gaussian distribution. Empirical tests confirm that separate training of the two stages is more effective in optimizing the VAE's performance.
