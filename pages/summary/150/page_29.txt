The paper discusses the training and optimization of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) across various datasets, including MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CelebA. The authors outline specific training configurations for each dataset, emphasizing the number of epochs and learning rate adjustments based on dataset complexity and size.

For MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, the authors utilize a batch size of 100, employing the ADAM optimizer with a starting learning rate of 0.0001, which is halved after specified epochs. The first VAE is trained for 400 epochs, while the second is trained for 800 epochs. CIFAR-10 requires more extensive training, with the VAEs trained for 1000 and 2000 epochs, respectively, also adjusting the learning rate at different intervals. In contrast, CelebA, having a larger number of examples, uses shorter training periods of 120 and 300 epochs for its VAEs, with learning rate halving occurring more frequently.

To ensure a fair comparison with various GAN models, the authors adopt the InfoGAN network structure for the first-stage VAE and a more complex architecture for the second-stage VAE, which includes three hidden layers. Despite the addition of parameters in the second stage, these do not enhance the performance of other VAE baselines when considered collectively.

In the appendix, the authors provide a proof of Theorem 2, beginning with the scenario where the latent dimension equals the manifold dimension and extending to cases where the latent dimension exceeds it. The proof involves constructing a bijection that transforms the ground-truth distribution into a Gaussian distribution, detailing a sequence of decoders that converge the distribution of the model to that of the ground truth. The functions and equations used to define this transformation are included, establishing a mathematical foundation for the model's efficacy.
