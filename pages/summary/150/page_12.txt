In the paper by Dai and Wipf, the authors present a comparative analysis of various generative models, particularly focusing on the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores across several datasets including MNIST, Fashion, CIFAR-10, and CelebA. The results demonstrate the performance of different Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). 

The table outlines the optimal FID scores obtained from a comprehensive hyper-parameter search for GAN-based models, indicating that models like MM GAN and LSGAN performed well across the datasets. The authors note that the reported values exclude outlier cases, such as severe mode collapse, which could skew the results.

For VAEs, the authors employed a single default setting across all datasets, without tuning, leading to different FID scores compared to GANs. They mention that no mode collapse cases were omitted in the VAE results. The authors also highlight the potential for improved FID scores through specialized architectures or random seed optimization.

A specific note is made regarding the WAE-MMD model's performance, particularly its high FID scores for the MNIST and Fashion datasets, which they attribute to the model's robustness to latent-space dimensionality. Additionally, they address a discrepancy in the pre-processing of CelebA data compared to previous works, which could affect the results.

Overall, the paper sets the stage for future comparisons of the authors' proposed 2-Stage VAE against state-of-the-art VAE models, emphasizing the need for evaluations that utilize dataset-specific architectures and training protocols.
