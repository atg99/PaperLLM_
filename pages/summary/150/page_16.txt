The paper by Dai and Wipf discusses the challenges associated with the Wasserstein Autoencoder with Maximum Mean Discrepancy (WAE-MMD) in generating high-quality samples, particularly when the dimensionality of the latent space exceeds the intrinsic dimensionality of the data manifold. The study highlights that when the latent dimension is greater than the true dimension of the data, WAE-MMD struggles to match the distribution of generated samples to the training data, which contributes to its poor performance on datasets like MNIST and Fashion MNIST.

To investigate these issues, the authors employed a neutral testing framework and retrained various models while varying the latent dimension. The experiments were conducted on Fashion MNIST and CelebA datasets, with results indicating that both the 2-Stage VAE and WAE-MMD produced improved reconstruction FID scores as the latent dimension increased. However, while the VAE models displayed stability across a broader range of latent dimensions, the performance of WAE-MMD was more sensitive to these changes.

The authors note that while hyperparameter tuning could potentially enhance WAE-MMD performance, the VAE models maintained consistent results without any tuning, suggesting inherent robustness. They also caution that excessively high latent dimensions can complicate training, as the model must learn to navigate unnecessary dimensions. Nevertheless, the 2-Stage VAE remained effective even when the latent dimension was not strictly minimized.

Finally, the paper qualitatively evaluates samples generated by the 2-Stage VAE, employing a residual network structure that is parameter-efficient compared to other architectures like InfoGAN. The results emphasize the VAE's ability to generate high-quality samples while maintaining a manageable model complexity, although the authors acknowledge potential pitfalls in model capacity management that can lead to overfitting or degenerate solutions.
