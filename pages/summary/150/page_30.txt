In this paper, Dai and Wipf explore the relationship between various mathematical functions and distributions related to a two-dimensional Gaussian model. They introduce the functions F and G, where F is defined as an invertible function mapping a variable x from a certain space to a distribution, and G is a differentiable and invertible function that transforms a real vector z into a space between 0 and 1. The authors derive expressions for the probability density function p_t(x) using these transformations, showing how it can be expressed as an integral over the transformed variables.

They demonstrate that as the parameter t approaches infinity, the function N converges to a Dirac delta function, leading to a simplification of the probability density function to p_gt(x), which indicates that the transformed density aligns with the ground truth distribution. This result emphasizes the robustness of their mathematical framework in accurately modeling the underlying distributions when certain conditions are met. The findings are significant for understanding the behavior of probabilistic models in high-dimensional spaces.
