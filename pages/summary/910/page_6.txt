The paper discusses a novel method called HDML for deep metric learning, which trains a metric and generator network simultaneously without relying on auxiliary sampling processes typical of hard negative mining methods. This approach ensures that the augmentor and generator are only utilized during training, thereby not adding any computational load during the embedding computation phase.

In the experimental section, the authors evaluate HDML through image clustering and retrieval tasks, conducting an ablation study to assess the effectiveness of each component. The performance metrics used are normalized mutual information (NMI) and F1-score for clustering, while Recall@Ks is employed for retrieval tasks. The experiments are conducted under a zero-shot setting, ensuring that the training and test sets contain image classes with no overlap.

Three datasets are utilized: CUB-200-2011, Cars196, and Stanford Online Products, each split into training and testing sets based on specific species or product models. The authors implement the experiments using TensorFlow, employing GoogLeNet as a feature extractor and a generator with two fully connected layers. The images are preprocessed through resizing and data augmentation techniques.

The results indicate that the proposed HDML framework outperforms baseline models in both clustering and retrieval tasks. The ablation study highlights the contributions of various components, showcasing the learning curves and performance metrics across different model configurations. Overall, the findings suggest that the HDML method effectively enhances deep metric learning performance while maintaining efficiency in training and evaluation processes.
