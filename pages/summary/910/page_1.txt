The paper introduces a novel framework for hardness-aware deep metric learning (HDML), addressing limitations of traditional hard negative mining strategies. While existing methods focus on a limited subset of training data, potentially neglecting the broader geometry of the embedding space, the proposed HDML framework leverages linear interpolation on embeddings to adaptively manipulate the hardness levels of samples. This approach generates label-preserving synthetic samples, allowing for a more comprehensive use of the training data.

The key innovation lies in the ability to control the difficulty of the generated samples, ensuring that the metric learning model is consistently challenged with appropriately difficult examples. This method enhances the exploitation of all available information, leading to improved performance on standard datasets such as CUB-200-2011, Cars196, and Stanford Online Products. The authors argue that their approach not only maintains the integrity of labels but also optimizes the training process, thus contributing to more effective deep metric learning.
