The paper addresses a critical issue in deep metric learning related to sampling strategies, specifically the challenge of obtaining informative samples for training. Traditional methods often rely on hard negative mining, which focuses on selecting samples that produce significant gradients. However, this approach can lead to overfitting on frequently sampled data points while neglecting others, resulting in an incomplete representation of the embedding space.

To overcome this limitation, the authors propose a hardness-aware deep metric learning (HDML) framework. This framework uniformly samples all data points in the training set while also synthesizing additional hardness-aware samples that complement the originals. The synthetic samples are adjusted based on the model's training status, ensuring that a better-trained model encounters more challenging examples. An adaptive linear interpolation method is employed to manipulate the difficulty levels of the embeddings. The resulting augmented embeddings are mapped back to the feature space through a generator that preserves labels and hardness levels.

The effectiveness of the HDML framework is validated through an ablation study and extensive experiments on well-known datasets, including CUB-200-2011, Cars196, and Stanford Online Products. The results demonstrate that HDML significantly enhances the performance of existing deep metric learning models in image clustering and retrieval tasks.

The paper also reviews related work in metric learning, highlighting conventional methods such as Mahalanobis distance and various loss functions like contrastive and triplet losses. It discusses the role of hard negative mining in improving training efficiency and performance across different machine learning tasks. Finally, the authors emphasize that their proposed HDML framework uniquely generates synthetic samples with controlled hardness levels, thereby improving training outcomes without the instability associated with adversarial methods.
