The paper discusses a framework for hardness-aware deep metric learning, utilizing an auto-encoder architecture to implement mappings between feature and embedding spaces. The encoder takes a feature vector from an image, maps it to an embedding, and then applies hardness-aware augmentation to modify the embedding. The generator maps both the original and augmented embeddings to synthetic features.

The generator's objective is to ensure that synthetic features retain the original labels while also reflecting the augmented hardness. This is formalized through a loss function that combines reconstruction loss and softmax loss, where the reconstruction loss ensures proximity between original and synthetic features, and the softmax loss maintains label integrity. 

The proposed framework consists of three main components: a metric network for embeddings, a hardness-aware augmentor, and a generator network that preserves hardness and labels. The embeddings are modified through linear interpolation based on the model's training status, and a generator creates synthetic tuples that are then used to train the metric.

The training objective adapts to the use of synthetic samples by balancing the contributions of original and synthetic losses, adjusting weights based on the generator's training state. The framework can enhance conventional deep metric learning methods and is demonstrated with two specific loss functions: triplet loss and N-pair loss. The triplet loss utilizes the distance of positive pairs to generate negatives, while the N-pair loss generates multiple negatives per anchor, leveraging the hardness-aware synthesis for improved metric learning efficacy.
