The paper presents a framework called Hardness-aware Deep Metric Learning (HDML), which integrates a CNN-based metric model with a generator for synthetic sample creation. The architecture includes a loss computation module that supervises the metric model, an augmentor that performs linear manipulations, and a generator composed of fully connected layers, resembling an autoencoder structure.

A key aspect of the framework is the adaptive hardness-aware negative augmentation method. This approach dynamically adjusts the hardness of synthetic samples based on the average metric loss during training. Initially, the embedding space lacks a well-defined semantic structure, making hard samples potentially misleading. As training progresses, the model becomes more adept at handling harder samples, necessitating the generation of increasingly challenging synthetic tuples to maintain learning efficiency.

The paper further discusses the challenge of ensuring that augmented samples preserve both hardness and label consistency. To address this, the authors propose a hardness-and-label-preserving feature synthesis method that maps augmented embeddings back to the feature space while maintaining proximity to the original data manifold. This ensures that the synthetic samples generated are not only difficult but also relevant to the correct classification.

The proposed methods aim to enhance the training process by balancing the complexity of synthetic samples with their utility in learning, thereby improving the overall performance of the deep metric learning framework.
