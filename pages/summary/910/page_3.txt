This paper presents a novel approach to metric learning by introducing a hardness-aware augmentation method. The primary goal is to enhance the training of distance metrics in feature space by generating harder training tuples, which can lead to more effective learning outcomes.

The problem is formulated by defining a data space with points labeled from a set of classes. The objective is to learn a distance metric that accurately reflects the semantic distance between data points in the feature space. This is achieved through deep learning methods that utilize neural networks to extract features and map them to an embedding space, where the distance metric is typically the Euclidean distance.

The authors highlight the limitations of current training methods, which often select tuples that do not contribute meaningful gradients, resulting in a biased model. To overcome this, they propose an adaptive hardness-aware augmentation technique that modifies distances between negative pairs in the embedding space. By controlling the hard level of tuples based on the training status, the method aims to create harder examples for the model to learn from.

A specific strategy is employed where the authors linearly interpolate between negative pairs to form augmented harder negative samples. The manipulation of distances allows for a more effective training process, as it ensures that the generated tuples provide informative gradients that better characterize the global geometry of the embedding space.

Overall, this approach aims to improve the efficiency of metric learning by focusing on the hardness of training tuples, potentially leading to better performance in various applications.
