The paper presents a comparative analysis of various methods for clustering and retrieval tasks, focusing on the impact of different pulling factors on performance. It highlights that the absence of softmax loss leads to significant performance degradation due to the potential inconsistency of synthetic samples. Despite this, the proposed method without reconstruction loss outperforms the baseline, suggesting that the softmax layer effectively differentiates between realistic and false synthetics.

Figures 6 and 7 illustrate how different pulling factors affect performance, indicating that an optimal value of alpha (α) enhances results initially, peaking at α = 90, before declining. This suggests that tuple synthesis must balance hardness to avoid inconsistent tuples and maintain the embedding structure.

Quantitative results across several datasets (CUB-200-2011, Cars196, and Stanford Online Products) show the proposed method surpassing various baseline methods, including contrastive, triplet, and N-pair losses. The results reflect improvements in metrics like Normalized Mutual Information (NMI), F1 score, and recall at different ranks, underscoring the effectiveness of adaptive hard synthesis in enhancing retrieval performance.
