This paper presents a novel approach for automating this process through the use of sequence-to-sequence models, specifically exploring recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and Transformer architectures.

The authors highlight the increasing burden of medical documentation on physicians, which has been linked to burnout and decreased job satisfaction. The paper posits that automating the generation of medical reports from conversations could alleviate some of this burden, as the content is already discussed during patient encounters. The research leverages a dataset of 800,000 orthopedic encounters to compare the performance of various model architectures against an oracle extractive baseline.

Key enhancements to the models include the introduction of a pointer-generator network, which allows for the selective copying of conversation elements into reports, and a hierarchical RNN encoder that accelerates training for long input sequences. The findings indicate that Transformer models outperform RNNs in both accuracy and training efficiency, showcasing the potential of sequence-to-sequence modeling for generating medical reports effectively when sufficient data is available.

In summary, the paper underscores the value of employing advanced neural network architectures to automate medical documentation, thereby addressing the dual issues of physician workload and patient care quality.
