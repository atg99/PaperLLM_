This paper presents a novel approach for generating medical reports from automatic speech recognition (ASR) transcripts of doctor-patient conversations. The authors propose a translation and summarization framework that leverages recent advancements in neural transduction technology, enabling an end-to-end model for report generation. This method circumvents the need for annotated corpora typically required for intermediate processing, making it scalable across various medical specialties.

The dataset utilized consists of speaker-diarized transcripts from ambulatory orthopedic surgery encounters, organized into four sections: history of present illness (HPI), physical examination (PE), assessment and plan (AP), and diagnostic imaging results (RES). Each section captures distinct aspects of the medical encounter, with the HPI detailing the reason for visit and relevant history, PE recording examination findings, RES summarizing diagnostic impressions, and AP outlining treatment plans.

Experimental results are derived from approximately 800,000 encounters involving 280 doctors, with a focus on evaluating the generated reports against a partition of 4,000 encounters from 80 doctors. The study finds that the source and target sequences in this orthopedic report creation task are significantly longer than those in established summarization datasets like CNN and Daily Mail and XSum.

In modeling, the authors employ neural sequence-to-sequence models, treating the task akin to machine translation. Each report section is produced using a distinct model, adapting techniques from neural machine translation while noting that the source and target sequences share the same language. The paper highlights both the advantages and limitations of the end-to-end approach, particularly in terms of the ability to inject prior knowledge and the potential impact on performance.
