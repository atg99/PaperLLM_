This paper compares RNN and Transformer-based architectures for generating medical reports from patient-doctor conversations. It highlights the effectiveness of sequence-to-sequence models, particularly the Transformer, in extracting relevant excerpts and summarizing them into coherent and factually accurate reports. The study finds that as training length increases, the issue of repetition in model outputs decreases, with a target goal of 0% repetition rate. The analysis reveals common problems such as repeated and hallucinated sentences, emphasizing the need for human oversight in verifying machine-generated reports. Future research will explore end-to-end approaches versus a pipeline method for clinical information extraction and natural language generation.
