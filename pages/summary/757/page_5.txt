The paper presents an evaluation of various neural network architectures for text summarization, specifically focusing on ROUGE-L and fact extractor F1 scores. The models assessed include RNN, Hierarchical RNN, Hierarchical RNN with a pointer-generator mechanism, and Transformer models, both with and without the pointer-generator. The results indicate that the Transformer models, particularly when combined with the pointer-generator, outperform other architectures in terms of relative error rate reductions compared to an oracle baseline.

The Transformer model employs relative position representations within the attention mechanism, which proved to be more effective than sinusoidal position information. Additionally, the implementation of a pointing mechanism allows the model to copy tokens from the source effectively. The authors suggest using a dedicated attention head for pointing, as it can focus on relevant tokens without interfering with the functions of other heads.

Training was conducted on NVIDIA V100 GPUs using data-parallel training, with a batch size of up to 7,000 tokens per GPU. The models utilized different optimization techniques, with RNN models using Nesterov’s Accelerated Gradient and Transformer models using Adam. The training duration was limited to one week, with a maximum of 400,000 steps, and performance was improved by averaging parameters from multiple checkpoints.

The results of the experiments highlight the efficiency of Transformer architectures in producing high-quality summaries, as evidenced by their superior ROUGE-L scores and overall performance metrics. The paper emphasizes the importance of efficient training strategies and the effectiveness of the pointer mechanism in enhancing summarization tasks.
