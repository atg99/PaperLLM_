The paper discusses a sequence-to-sequence model employing recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and transformers, focusing on mechanisms for improving attention and representation in conversational models. 

Figure 1 illustrates the RNN model with a hierarchical encoder and a pointer-generator copy mechanism. The model encodes segments of the source conversation using two bidirectional LSTM layers, with a third layer processing the final segment embeddings. Attention distributions are calculated from both token-level and segment-level outputs, and the final distribution is formed by interpolating the attention distribution with the vocabulary distribution, using a predicted coefficient. The authors introduce a coverage loss to enhance training, which penalizes the model for attending to already heavily covered positions, promoting a more uniform attention across the input sequence and reducing repetition.

Figure 2 presents the transformer model, which utilizes self-attention in both the encoder and decoder to create context-dependent representations. Each encoder and decoder consists of six layers of self-attention, with the decoder layers attending to the encoder's output. The architecture includes position-wise feed-forward networks and applies layer normalization before self-attention and feed-forward sub-layers, improving training stability and convergence speed. The transformer model's parallel computation capability enhances training efficiency compared to RNNs. The paper emphasizes the importance of incorporating position-dependent information in the input to account for the order of inputs effectively.
