This paper discusses the evaluation of automatic speech recognition (ASR) outputs in clinical settings, focusing on fluency and factual correctness. The authors utilize a proprietary machine-learning-based clinical fact extractor to assess the accuracy of medical facts such as conditions and medications. The evaluation methodology includes comparing model outputs to a reference output derived from the longest common subsequence of the input conversation.

The results show that Transformer models outperform recurrent neural network (RNN) models in both fluency and factual accuracy, as indicated by relative error rate reduction (RERR) metrics derived from ROUGE-L and the fact extractor's F1 scores. Despite RNN models being constrained by maximum training time, hierarchical RNNs performed better than standard RNNs. The pointer-generator mechanism generally enhances performance, particularly in the report sections focused on relevance and accuracy, although it can negatively impact certain areas.

The study also identifies issues with repetition in generated reports, categorizing them into repeated sentences and n-grams. While some repetitions are acceptable, the authors aim to quantify excessive repetition to improve the quality of generated outputs. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of Transformer models in clinical ASR tasks and the importance of addressing repetition to enhance report clarity and accuracy.
