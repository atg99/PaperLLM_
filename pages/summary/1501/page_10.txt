The paper discusses the integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance their performance and interpretability in natural language processing tasks. It highlights the importance of using KG-enhanced pre-training methods to manage time-insensitive knowledge in specific domains, while KG-enhanced inference methods are suggested for open-domain knowledge that requires frequent updates.

The paper categorizes the use of KGs in LLMs into two main areas: probing and analysis. LLM probing aims to uncover the knowledge embedded in LLMs, addressing challenges such as the hallucination problem where models generate inaccurate statements. The LAMA framework is introduced as an initial approach to probe LLM knowledge through cloze questions derived from KGs. However, it notes the limitations of predefined prompts, leading to the development of methods like LPAQA and Autoprompt, which improve prompt generation for more accurate knowledge assessment.

In terms of analysis, the paper discusses how KGs can ground the reasoning processes of LLMs, providing insights into how these models generate results. Techniques such as KagNet and QA-GNN are mentioned for analyzing inference processes, while causal-inspired analysis is used to explore the patterns LLMs rely on during result generation. The findings suggest that LLMs may struggle with factual knowledge retention, particularly for less common information.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of KGs to improve both the interpretability and reliability of LLMs, making them more applicable in critical domains like medical diagnosis and legal judgment.
