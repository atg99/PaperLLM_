The paper discusses various methods for integrating large language models (LLMs) with knowledge graph embeddings (KGE) and knowledge graph completion (KGC) tasks. It highlights the effectiveness of LLMs in generating rich representations that combine textual and graph structural information. Key contributions include the development of joint embedding techniques that treat entities and relations as tokens in LLMs, enabling the prediction of masked entities within a text context.

The paper categorizes LLM-augmented KGC methods into two styles: LLM as Encoders (PaE) and LLM as Generators (PaG). In the PaE approach, LLMs encode both textual information and knowledge graph facts to predict the plausibility of triples. Techniques such as KG-BERT use LLMs to process triples as text sequences, while others like MTL-KGC enhance performance through multi-task learning.

The study also emphasizes the importance of both language semantics and graph structure in KGC, proposing methods that jointly learn semantic and structural embeddings. The integration of masked language modeling further allows for effective predictions of missing entities, enhancing the overall capability of KGC systems.

Overall, the paper illustrates the significant advancements made by incorporating LLMs into knowledge graph tasks, showcasing their potential to improve the accuracy and efficiency of knowledge representation and reasoning.
