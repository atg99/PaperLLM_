The paper discusses various methodologies for Knowledge Graph Completion (KGC) utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs). It outlines two main approaches: LLMs as encoders (PaE) and LLMs as generators (PaG). In the encoder framework, multitask learning is employed for entity and super-class predictions based on textual descriptions, with models like OpenWorld KGC that utilize Entity Description Prediction (EDP) and Incomplete Triple Prediction (ITP) to manage incomplete data. Separated Encoding techniques partition triples into distinct parts for independent processing, enhancing the scoring of potential triples.

In the generator framework, LLMs are used for sequence generation, where models like GenKGC and KGT5 focus on generating tail entities from given triples. GenKGC introduces relation-guided demonstrations to improve learning efficiency, while KGT5 uses a T5 model fine-tuned for KGC tasks.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of LLMs in enhancing KGC through various encoding and generative techniques, highlighting advancements in both methodologies and their applications in handling incomplete knowledge graphs.
