The paper presents a factorized hierarchical search space for convolutional neural networks (CNNs), structured into predefined blocks that facilitate the exploration of various architectural configurations. Each block is characterized by a specific input resolution and filter size, containing multiple identical layers where only the first layer may have a different stride. The architecture of each layer within a block is defined by several parameters, including convolutional operations, kernel sizes, squeeze-and-excitation ratios, skip operations, filter sizes, and the number of layers.

The search space is designed to optimize the balance between layer diversity and the total size of the search space. By partitioning the network into blocks, the total search space size is significantly smaller than a flat per-layer approach, making it more efficient to explore. For instance, with a typical configuration yielding a search space size of approximately 10^13, the method demonstrates a marked reduction compared to traditional methods.

To navigate this search space, the authors employ a reinforcement learning approach to identify Pareto optimal solutions. The CNN models are represented as sequences of tokens, with a focus on maximizing a reward function based on the model's performance. The search framework consists of a recurrent neural network controller, a model trainer, and a mobile inference engine to assess latency, following a sample-eval-update training loop.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of structured search spaces in effectively optimizing CNN architectures while balancing computational constraints and model performance.
