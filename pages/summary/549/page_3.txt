The paper discusses a method for optimizing neural network architecture with a focus on balancing accuracy and latency, particularly for mobile platforms. The authors define a model's accuracy as ACC(m) and its inference latency as LAT(m), with a target latency T. Traditional optimization approaches typically maximize accuracy under a hard latency constraint, which limits the exploration of multiple Pareto-optimal solutions. A Pareto-optimal model offers the best accuracy without increasing latency or the lowest latency without sacrificing accuracy.

To address this, the authors propose a customized weighted product method to find multiple Pareto-optimal solutions during architecture search. The optimization goal is defined to maximize the product of accuracy and a function of latency, adjusted by weight factors α and β. The values for α and β are application-specific, and empirical observations suggest that a latency increase typically correlates with a predictable accuracy gain. The authors use specific values (α=β=−0.07) in their experiments to maintain consistency in evaluating models.

The paper introduces a novel factorized hierarchical search space that allows for greater diversity in layer architecture compared to previous methods, which often rely on stacking identical cells. By factorizing a convolutional neural network (CNN) model into unique blocks and searching operations and connections for each block separately, the authors aim to achieve better accuracy-latency trade-offs. This approach accounts for the varying impact of different layers on overall latency, especially in the early stages of the network where data processing demands are higher.

In summary, the paper presents a framework for optimizing mobile neural architecture that emphasizes the importance of a well-defined search space and a flexible approach to balancing accuracy and latency through customized optimization techniques.
