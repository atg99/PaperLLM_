This paper presents a performance comparison of various model scaling techniques, focusing on MnasNet and MobileNetV2, while also investigating the impact of Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) on model performance. MnasNet is evaluated against MobileNetV2 using different depth multipliers and input sizes to analyze their inference latency and Top-1 accuracy.

Key findings include that the MnasNet A1 model achieves a Top-1 accuracy of 75.2% with a latency of 78ms, outperforming MobileNetV2 by 1.8 times in speed and providing a 0.5% higher accuracy. The study highlights that MnasNet models, even without SE, still surpass both MobileNetV2 and NASNet, indicating the effectiveness of the automated architecture search approach. 

The paper also discusses model scaling performance, where adjusting the depth multiplier significantly affects latency and accuracy. MnasNet consistently shows better accuracy than MobileNetV2 across various depth multipliers and input sizes, suggesting its robustness in real-world applications. Furthermore, the paper emphasizes that MnasNet can be tailored for specific latency targets, allowing for flexible architecture design based on application requirements. 

In conclusion, MnasNet demonstrates superior performance and efficiency, setting a new standard in mobile model design while accommodating varying latency and accuracy requirements.
