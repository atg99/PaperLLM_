The paper "MnasNet: Platform-Aware Neural Architecture Search for Mobile" addresses the challenges of designing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for mobile devices, which need to be compact, efficient, and accurate. The authors introduce an automated mobile neural architecture search (MNAS) that incorporates real-world model latency into its optimization process, as opposed to relying on less accurate proxies like FLOPS. This approach allows for the identification of models that effectively balance accuracy and latency.

To manage the complexity of the search space while maintaining flexibility, the authors propose a novel factorized hierarchical search space that promotes layer diversity within the network. Experimental results demonstrate that MnasNet consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art mobile CNNs across various vision tasks. Specifically, on the ImageNet classification task, MnasNet achieves a top-1 accuracy of 75.2% with a latency of 78ms on a Pixel phone, outperforming MobileNetV2 in both speed and accuracy, and also surpassing NASNet in terms of efficiency. Additionally, MnasNet shows improved mean Average Precision (mAP) in COCO object detection compared to MobileNets.

Overall, the study highlights the importance of considering real-world performance metrics in the design of mobile CNNs and presents MnasNet as a significant advancement in this area.
