The paper presents an automated approach for neural architecture search aimed at designing resource-efficient mobile convolutional neural networks (CNNs) using reinforcement learning. Key contributions include the introduction of a multi-objective reward system and a novel factorized hierarchical search space. The results demonstrate that incorporating latency constraints into the search process significantly enhances model performance, allowing for faster models with competitive accuracy.

The analysis distinguishes between single-objective and multi-objective rewards in the context of different search spaces. Initial experiments with the NASNet cell-based search space, when augmented with latency constraints, yielded faster models. Further application of both the multi-objective reward and the new factorized search space produced models with improved accuracy and reduced latency, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed search space.

The MnasNet architecture, exemplified by the MnasNet-A1 model, showcases diverse layer architectures, utilizing both 3x3 and 5x5 convolutions, which contrasts with previous mobile models that predominantly used 3x3 convolutions. A comparison of MnasNet with its variants reveals that layer diversity plays a crucial role in achieving better accuracy-latency trade-offs in resource-constrained environments.

In conclusion, the paper asserts that the proposed search approach can automatically identify superior mobile models compared to existing methodologies, achieving state-of-the-art results on ImageNet classification and COCO object detection while adhering to mobile inference latency constraints. The findings emphasize the significance of layer diversity in future mobile CNN model development.
