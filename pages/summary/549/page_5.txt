This paper presents a comparison of various convolutional neural network (CNN) models for image classification on the ImageNet dataset, focusing on their architecture, parameters, accuracy, and inference latency. The models are categorized into manually designed and automated architectures. The results indicate that MnasNet models, particularly MnasNet-A1, A2, and A3, demonstrate competitive performance with varying latency.

The authors employ a reinforcement learning approach to optimize model architecture, utilizing a recurrent neural network (RNN) controller to sample and evaluate models based on accuracy and inference latency. The architecture search is conducted directly on the ImageNet dataset with a reduced training duration of five epochs, allowing for effective scaling to larger tasks.

Experimental results highlight that while searching for CNN architectures is resource-intensive, the proposed method successfully identifies top-performing models that balance accuracy and efficiency. The paper emphasizes the importance of real-world latency measurements in model selection, with MnasNet-A3 achieving a top-1 accuracy of 76.7% at a latency of 103ms.

Overall, the research contributes to the field by demonstrating an effective architecture search strategy for CNNs, with implications for mobile and edge computing applications where latency is critical.
