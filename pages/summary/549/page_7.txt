This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various object detection models, specifically focusing on the performance of MnasNet in comparison to other established networks like YOLO and SSD. The results are summarized in two tables: Table 3 details the COCO object detection performance, while Table 4 highlights model scaling and search outcomes.

Table 3 indicates that MnasNet models, when used as feature extractors for SSDLite, achieve significant improvements over previous MobileNet versions, demonstrating a comparable mean average precision (mAP) to SSD300 while utilizing substantially fewer parameters and multiply-add operations. For example, the MnasNet model achieves an mAP of 23.0 with only 4.9 million parameters, while SSD300 has 36.1 million parameters and an mAP of 23.2.

Table 4 explores the trade-offs between model scaling and search, showcasing the latency and accuracy of various models scaled down to a depth multiplier of 0.35. MnasNet models outperform MobileNetV2 in both accuracy and inference latency.

The paper further investigates the implications of latency constraints in model architecture design through an ablation study. It distinguishes between hard and soft latency constraints in the multi-objective search method, demonstrating how varying parameters can influence model selection based on latency and accuracy. The results indicate that by adjusting the constraints, the search can yield models that cater to specific latency requirements while maintaining competitive performance.

In conclusion, the findings underscore MnasNet's efficiency and effectiveness in mobile object detection tasks, emphasizing the importance of architecture search and latency management in developing high-performing models.
