The paper presents a comparison between the traditional Mask R-CNN and the proposed Boundary-preserving Mask R-CNN (BMask R-CNN), particularly focusing on their performance on the COCO val2017 dataset. The results, summarized in Table 1, demonstrate that BMask R-CNN consistently outperforms Mask R-CNN across various configurations and backbone architectures, achieving higher Average Precision (AP) metrics. 

The study includes ablation experiments to analyze the contributions of different components within BMask R-CNN. It highlights the significance of boundary supervision for mask prediction, revealing that incorporating boundary targets enhances AP scores compared to using only mask supervision or no supervision at all. Specifically, boundary supervision improves mask localization performance significantly.

Furthermore, the paper discusses RoI (Region of Interest) feature extraction, emphasizing the need for precise spatial information for predicting boundaries. The authors explore different strategies for RoI feature extraction, focusing on the source of features from various levels of the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) and the resolution of features. The findings indicate that directly extracting boundary features from lower levels of the FPN, specifically at a higher resolution, yields better performance for boundary prediction. The paper concludes that BMask R-CNN provides a robust improvement over the standard Mask R-CNN, particularly in tasks requiring accurate boundary predictions.
