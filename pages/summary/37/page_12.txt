This paper presents experimental results on the COCO val2017 dataset, focusing on the effects of different fusion blocks and loss functions on model performance. The results in Table 6 indicate that utilizing both mask-to-boundary (M2B) and boundary-to-mask (B2M) fusion improves average precision (AP) to 34.7, compared to configurations using only one fusion method or none. In Table 7, various loss functions are evaluated, with Dice-BCE achieving the highest AP of 34.7, indicating its effectiveness in enhancing model accuracy.

The training methodology involved using images resized randomly between 800 and 1024 pixels on their shorter edge, with inference on original-sized images (1024 x 2048). The model was trained using SGD across four GPUs for 48,000 iterations, with a learning rate that decreased after 36,000 iterations. Results on the Cityscapes dataset showed that BMask R-CNN achieved an AP of 29.4 on the test set, marking a significant improvement of 2.2 AP over the baseline Mask R-CNN without additional data.

The discussion highlights the trade-off between coarse boundary annotations and precise boundary predictions. While COCO offers extensive instance-level annotations, the coarse nature of mask and boundary annotations can hinder performance. However, BMask R-CNN demonstrates the ability to produce smoother and more precise boundaries with reduced mask overlap. Additionally, the paper introduces a Sobel mask head for boundary prediction, providing an alternative approach to enhance boundary detection from masks.
