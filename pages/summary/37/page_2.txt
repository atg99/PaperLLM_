In the paper, Cheng et al. introduce the Boundary-preserving Mask R-CNN (BMask R-CNN), an enhancement to the existing Mask R-CNN framework used for instance segmentation. While Mask R-CNN has set a benchmark in instance segmentation by performing pixel-level classification, it struggles with accurately classifying pixels near object boundaries, often resulting in coarse and indistinct segmentation outcomes. The authors argue that precise boundaries can significantly improve localization and the clarity of object masks.

To overcome the limitations of Mask R-CNN, BMask R-CNN integrates instance boundary information into the mask prediction process. This approach allows the network to output masks that are better aligned with ground truths by treating boundary information as a dual representation of instance masks. The authors propose replacing the original mask head of Mask R-CNN with a new boundary-preserving mask head, which comprises two sub-networks designed for jointly learning object masks and their boundaries. Additionally, feature fusion blocks are incorporated to enhance the relationship between boundary and mask feature learning.

The proposed method aims to leverage boundary features to improve object localization, although the authors acknowledge the challenges posed by noisy boundary ground truths derived from sparse annotated polygons within datasets like COCO. Overall, the BMask R-CNN demonstrates improved performance in instance segmentation by addressing the shortcomings of its predecessor regarding boundary precision.
