The paper presents a comprehensive comparison of various self-supervision-based methods for domain adaptation (DA), focusing on the architecture and self-supervision tasks employed. It categorizes methods based on the number of encoders and decoders, the type of domain (source or target), and the loss functions used. Key findings include the importance of high-level structural tasks in self-supervision for effective domain adaptation, as low-level visual differences can impede performance.

Several techniques are highlighted, such as those by Sun et al., which combine multiple self-supervision tasks, and Kim et al., which emphasize cross-domain self-supervised learning. The paper also discusses the limitations of solely finding invariant representations, citing examples where this approach fails, particularly in real-world applications.

The discussion extends to pseudo-labeling methods, where labels are assigned to target domain images for alignment, and the implications of noisy pseudo-labels are addressed. Ensemble methods are also explored, showcasing their utility in improving adaptation results through techniques like stochastic data augmentation.

Additional innovative approaches, such as curriculum learning and manifold embedded distribution, are noted for their unique contributions to minimizing domain gaps in tasks like semantic segmentation. Overall, the paper underscores the evolving landscape of self-supervised learning and domain adaptation, highlighting the need for robust methodologies that can address both covariate and label shifts in diverse application scenarios.
