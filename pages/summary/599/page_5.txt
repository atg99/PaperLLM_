The paper provides a comprehensive overview of various released and freely available datasets for domain adaptation, categorized by task and type of data (synthetic or real). Key tasks include digit recognition, object classification, pose estimation, 3D point cloud segmentation, object detection, and semantic segmentation.

For digit recognition, datasets like MNIST, USPS, and SVHN are highlighted, with a focus on their variations in image resolution and sample sizes. The object classification section discusses datasets such as Office-31 and DomainNet, the latter being the largest with 569,010 images across multiple domains.

Pose estimation datasets such as UnityEyes and MPIIGaze are noted for their applications in gaze estimation, while NYU provides hand pose data. In the realm of 3D point cloud segmentation, KITTI-LiDAR and GTA-LiDAR datasets are presented, emphasizing their use in autonomous driving.

Object detection datasets include Cityscapes and its foggy variant, Foggy Cityscapes, along with synthetic datasets like SIM10k and KITTI-Obj, which are designed for real-world scenarios. Semantic segmentation is covered with datasets like Cityscapes, BDDS, GTA, and SYNTHIA, the latter offering a large collection of synthetic urban images.

The paper also introduces a theoretical perspective on domain adaptation and aims to summarize various categories within the field, setting the stage for further exploration into single-source domain adaptation (DUDA). This structured approach underscores the significance of these datasets in advancing domain adaptation research.
