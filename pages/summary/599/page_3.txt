This paper presents a comprehensive survey on unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA), focusing on single-source, single-target scenarios without target labels. It highlights the evolution from early non-deep methods that aimed to align feature distributions to contemporary deep learning approaches that emphasize end-to-end learning of domain-invariant features. The authors categorize single-source deep UDA methods into four types based on the domain shift loss and generative/discriminative settings.

The survey distinguishes itself from prior works by providing an in-depth analysis of the latest deep UDA methods, including their advantages and disadvantages, and by systematically comparing results across popular benchmark datasets. It also addresses overlooked aspects such as label shift and theoretical considerations.

The organization of the paper includes definitions of domain adaptation strategies, a summary of datasets for evaluation, and a theoretical framework. The authors categorize various UDA approaches, including discrepancy-based, adversarial discriminative, adversarial generative, and self-supervision-based methods. The final sections discuss qualitative and quantitative comparisons, as well as potential future research directions.

The paper aims to provide insights into the state of deep domain adaptation, contributing to a clearer understanding of the field and suggesting new avenues for exploration.
