The findings indicate that self-supervision-based methods generally outperform discrepancy-based methods in terms of performance, while being less effective than adversarial methods. The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of self-supervised techniques in adapting CNN encoders to extract domain-invariant features across different tasks and datasets.

In the quantitative comparison, object detection performance was evaluated using Cityscapes and Foggy Cityscapes datasets, with mean Average Precision (mAP) as the key metric. Various methods were assessed, showcasing their performance across eight object categories. The results highlight the challenges posed by domain gaps, particularly in appearance and scene layout discrepancies between datasets.

The analysis of other tasks, such as semantic segmentation and image classification, further illustrates the varying effectiveness of different adaptation methods across diverse datasets, including synthetic and real-world scenarios. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of current methodologies in domain adaptation and their relative performances, underlining the importance of selecting appropriate techniques based on specific tasks and dataset characteristics.
