The paper discusses various adversarial discriminative models and their effectiveness in aligning feature representations across source and target domains. Table IV provides a comparative overview of these models, focusing on the adversarial level, encoder weight, discriminator input, type, and loss function used. Most models employ global alignment strategies, utilizing either shared or unshared features from the source and target domains, with many implementing GAN loss or cross-entropy as their loss functions.

The work highlights the limitations of existing methods that primarily measure domain discrepancy at a high level, potentially neglecting intra-class relationships which can lead to misalignment. To address this, several models have been proposed: the Contrastive Adaptation Network minimizes intra-class discrepancies while maximizing inter-class discrepancies; the Transferrable Prototypical Networks align class prototypes across domains; and joint distribution alignment methods reduce distribution shifts in both feature and label spaces.

The paper also contrasts the traditional weight-sharing approach in adversarial models, which aims to learn domain-invariant features, with newer approaches that allow for a more flexible relationship between source and target domains. Rozantsev et al. proposed a model that optimizes both weight regularization and unsupervised regularization to address this issue.

The foundational concept of adversarial discriminative models is derived from domain adversarial training, which seeks to learn representations that are invariant to domain changes while remaining task-relevant. This approach, particularly exemplified by the Domain-Adversarial Neural Networks, optimizes the representation mappings to minimize the domain discriminator loss, emphasizing the need for careful consideration of the loss functions used in training these models.
