The paper discusses various frameworks for single-source deep unsupervised domain adaptation (DUDA), categorizing them into four main types: discrepancy-based methods, adversarial discriminative methods, adversarial generative methods, and self-supervision-based methods. Each framework leverages a combination of task loss, adversarial loss, and feature extraction with weight sharing to adapt models from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled or sparsely labeled target domain.

The paper emphasizes that DUDA is part of the sample-efficient learning category, which includes zero-shot learning, few-shot learning, and self-supervised learning. It highlights the differences between these methods, noting that while unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) requires unlabeled target samples, zero-shot learning does not need any target data at all. Zero-shot learning involves generalizing from known classes to unknown classes, contrasting with domain adaptation, which focuses on the same classes across different domains. Few-shot learning, similar to zero-shot learning, includes a limited number of annotated samples for unknown classes.

Self-supervised learning (SSL) is also discussed as a method that constructs auxiliary tasks based on the input data itself, without relying on human-provided labels. SSL aims to develop meaningful representations through these tasks but does not specifically address domain shifts.

The paper identifies domain shift as a primary challenge in single-source UDA, describing it as the discrepancy between source and target distributions that leads to poor predictions on the target domain. It categorizes domain shifts into three types: covariate shift, label shift, and concept drift. Through examples, the paper demonstrates the challenges of direct model transfer from source to target domains, highlighting significant accuracy drops in image classification and pixel-wise segmentation tasks.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of the landscape of domain adaptation techniques, their distinctions, and the challenges faced in practical applications.
