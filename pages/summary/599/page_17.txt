This paper reviews recent advancements in deep unsupervised domain adaptation (DUDA), focusing on homogeneous settings involving single-source and single-target domains with strong supervision and closed-set configurations. The authors categorize various methods, summarize representative approaches, and compare them using experimental results. A significant argument presented is the importance of learning "common sense," which enables models to generalize better across different domains by understanding the underlying relationships between visual tasks. For example, recognizing that a computer screen is typically found on a desk enhances detection performance across varying conditions.

The paper concludes that DUDA remains a dynamic and promising area for further research, proposing several future directions and addressing associated challenges. The references cited provide a comprehensive background for readers seeking to delve deeper into the methodologies and findings discussed. Overall, the work serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners in the field of domain adaptation.
