In this paper, the authors explore ensemble classifiers, which are machine learning classifiers that combine multiple scoring functions to improve accuracy. They present a framework for categorizing these classifiers and discuss several ensemble techniques within this framework. The focus then shifts to the application of ensemble learning in malware analysis, providing a survey of techniques used in this field.

The paper highlights the prevalence and success of ensemble methods in machine learning competitions, noting their tendency to create complex systems that prioritize accuracy over practicality. The authors argue that while many ensemble techniques have been documented in malware research, there has been a lack of comparative studies using common datasets and metrics.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 surveys examples of ensemble learning in malware research and outlines a framework for classifying ensemble classifiers. Section 3 presents experimental results from applying various ensemble techniques to a large malware dataset, along with details about the dataset, scoring metrics, and software used. Finally, Section 4 concludes with reflections on the findings and suggestions for future research. The overall goal is to bring clarity to the evolving field of ensemble learning, particularly in the context of malware analysis and machine learning as a whole.
