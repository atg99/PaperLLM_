In this paper, the authors explore the structure of ensemble learning, categorizing combination architectures into three main types: bagging, boosting, and stacking. They present experimental results using a challenging malware dataset, demonstrating that ensemble methods can significantly improve performance over standard techniques. This empirical evidence supports the expectation that more complex stacking architectures can yield superior results, particularly in competitive machine learning contexts. However, the authors note that increasing complexity in stacking can lead to impractical training and scoring costs, especially with extensive feature sets.

The authors suggest future research should focus on quantifying the tradeoff between accuracy and model complexity, as marginal gains in accuracy may not justify the additional training costs in practical applications. They also propose investigating additional features to enable a more comprehensive analysis of bagging versus boosting, and to consider more advanced stacking techniques. Overall, the findings underline the importance of careful evaluation of ensemble methods in real-world scenarios.
