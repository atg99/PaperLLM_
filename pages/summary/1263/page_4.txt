The section discusses two ensemble learning methods: boosting and stacking. Boosting is defined as a process of combining weak classifiers that differ only by their parameters to create a stronger classifier. This method can be seen as a form of bagging focused on classifiers rather than data or features. The scoring functions used in boosting are reparameterized versions of the same technique.

Stacking is presented as a more general ensemble method that combines diverse classifiers using a meta-classifier. Unlike boosting, stacking allows for significant differences among the scoring functions, making it a generalization of both bagging and boosting. Stacking methods have shown superior performance in competitive machine learning scenarios, though practical considerations regarding efficiency must also be taken into account.

The section foreshadows a discussion on specific ensemble classifier examples, including maximum, averaging, and voting methods. Each of these techniques fits within the broader framework of ensemble learning, which aims to enhance classification performance through the combination of multiple models.
