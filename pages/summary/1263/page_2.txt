The section presents an overview of related work on ensemble methods, highlighting foundational contributions while noting their limitations in modern contexts. The paper [18] introduces various combinations of classifiers, including maximum, sum, and majority vote, but is considered somewhat outdated given the evolution of ensemble techniques. The book [20] expands on this topic with greater detail, while the more recent book [62] offers a contemporary perspective yet maintains theoretical elements from earlier works. Additionally, a blog [35] summarizes key topics from these books in a more accessible format.

The authors propose a practical framework for categorizing and discussing ensemble techniques, which serves as a reference for their experimental results on different ensemble methods. In Table 1, they summarize a selection of research papers applying ensemble classifiers to security issues, particularly malware detection, showcasing various strategies such as bagging, boosting, and stacking.

In the following section, the authors outline a high-level framework for constructing ensemble classifiers. They focus on combinations of scoring functions, allowing for both real-valued and binary classifiers. Importantly, they emphasize that their framework applies broadly to ensemble methods, not strictly to traditional ensemble learners, and that the ensemble functions used are intended for classification tasks.
