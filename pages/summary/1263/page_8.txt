In this paper, we focus on malware classification using opcode sequences extracted from malware samples. We utilize the reversing tool Radare2 to extract the first 1000 mnemonic opcodes from each sample, discarding any samples that yield fewer than 1000 opcodes or are corrupted. This process results in a dataset of 9725 samples across 21 malware families, with a notable imbalance in sample sizes per family.

The key metrics used to evaluate our experiments include accuracy, balanced accuracy, precision, recall, and the F1 score. Accuracy measures the ratio of correct classifications, while balanced accuracy averages accuracy across families. Precision assesses the proportion of true positives among classified positives, and recall measures the fraction of actual positives identified. The F1 score combines precision and recall into a single metric.

For our experiments, we employ a range of software packages commonly used in machine learning, including hmmlearn, XGBoost, Keras, TensorFlow, and scikit-learn, along with Numpy for linear algebra. We categorize our experiments into three broad groups, starting with standard machine learning techniques that serve as a baseline and highlight the challenges associated with malware classification. The use of opcode sequences as features allows us to explore various methodologies for effective classification in this domain.
