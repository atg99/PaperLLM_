This section lists various references related to quantum computation, quantum information, and related theoretical frameworks. Key contributions include the revisitation of the infinite size density matrix renormalization group by McCulloch, efficient classical simulations of slightly entangled quantum computations by Vidal, and foundational texts on quantum computation by Nielsen and Chuang. 

Additionally, the work of Verresen, Moessner, and Pollman explores one-dimensional symmetry-protected topological phases, while Bertlmann and Krammer discuss Bloch vectors for qudits. Hinton's lecture notes provide an introduction to machine learning concepts relevant to the field. Further contributions by Schuch, Pérez-García, and Cirac focus on the classification of quantum phases using matrix product states and projected entangled pair states, alongside Chen, Gu, and Wen's classification of gapped symmetric phases in one-dimensional spin systems. Notably, Aharonov and Ben-Or address theoretical aspects of quantum computing in their symposium paper, and Samman et al. review quantum information applications involving Rydberg atoms. 

These references collectively underline the interplay between quantum theory, computational efficiency, and the classification of quantum states and phases.
