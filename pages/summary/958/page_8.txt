This paper discusses the application of a Quantum Convolutional Neural Network (QCNN) to analyze the Haldane phase in a one-dimensional chain of spin-1 particles. The Hamiltonian for the system is defined, highlighting its protection by a Z2 x Z2 symmetry. The ground state transitions from a symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phase to a paramagnetic state as the parameter ! increases relative to J.

To connect the Haldane phase to the QCNN, the authors propose a quasi-local isometric map that aligns the distinct symmetry representations of the two models. This involves introducing a spin singlet state and mapping it to pairs of spin-1/2 particles. The QCNN circuit is designed to operate on this modified representation.

The paper presents numerical results for a system of 54 spin-1 particles, identifying the critical phase transition point at !/J ≈ 1.035. The QCNN effectively detects this transition by measuring a non-local observable derived from the final operator of the circuit, which recognizes the SPT phase.

The authors also explore the structure of the operator measured by the QCNN, noting that it can be expressed as a sum of exponentially many products of string operators. This leads to recursive relations that allow for the computation of multiscale string order parameters, enhancing the QCNN's capability to classify the phase even with long correlation lengths. The results underscore the QCNN's efficacy in detecting and characterizing phase transitions in quantum systems.
