The paper by Abdullah A. Yub Khan, Asif Ali Laghari, and Shafique Ahmed Awan discusses the significance of machine learning (ML) and deep learning in the fields of computer vision (CV) and image processing. It begins by defining ML as a subfield of artificial intelligence (AI) that enables machines to learn from datasets, which are categorized into qualitative and quantitative forms. The authors outline three primary ML techniques: supervised learning (SL), unsupervised learning (USL), and semi-supervised learning (SSL), explaining their respective roles, algorithms, and applications.

The historical context of CV is provided, tracing its origins to Larry Roberts’ work in the 1960s. The dual goals of CV are emphasized: to mimic human visual tasks and to apply computational models in medical imaging for disease detection. The paper highlights various applications of CV, including face recognition for attendance monitoring and the use of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAV) for surveillance.

In the realm of medical imaging (MI), the authors note the challenges posed by image quality and the costly nature of the process, while also discussing the potential of technology to enhance MI practices. The paper identifies limitations in ML applications, particularly in agriculture, real-time image recognition, and the scarcity of image datasets.

The structure of the paper is outlined, with sections dedicated to ML techniques, neural network models, tools and applications, datasets, challenges and limitations, and future directions for research in ML for CV and image processing. The authors aim to address open issues in the effective application of ML in these domains, ultimately providing a comprehensive survey of the current state and future potential of ML technologies.
