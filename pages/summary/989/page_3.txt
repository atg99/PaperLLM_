This review paper discusses the application of machine learning (ML) techniques in image processing (IP) and computer vision (CV). It begins by defining IP as the process of manipulating images using digital computers to enhance their quality and extract essential information. The paper distinguishes between analog and digital image processing, highlighting the importance of digital image manipulation through preprocessing, enhancement, and information extraction.

The phases of IP are outlined, starting with image acquisition, which involves converting images to grayscale. Image enhancement techniques are employed to reveal hidden details, while image restoration addresses degradation using mathematical models. The paper also discusses color image processing, multi-resolution processing, and the critical role of morphological processing in segmenting images into objects for further analysis.

ML is emphasized as a crucial component of IP, with unsupervised learning being used for feature extraction and supervised learning for object labeling. Clustering techniques, such as Fuzzy C and K-means, are mentioned for color-based and texture segmentation. The paper cites various applications of unsupervised ML in optical remote sensing and medical imaging, where it aids in disease diagnosis.

Supervised learning, on the other hand, involves training models with labeled datasets. Techniques like Support Vector Machines (SVM) are highlighted for their efficacy in recognizing handwritten text, classifying medical images, and facilitating visual perception in robotics.

Finally, the paper introduces neural networks (NN) and deep learning (DL) as advanced ML models inspired by biological neurons, noting that DL is a subfield of ML focused on neural network architectures. The review concludes by identifying open research issues in the field, paving the way for future investigations in machine learning applications within image processing and computer vision.
