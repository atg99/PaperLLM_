The paper discusses the importance of targeted inductive biases in data augmentation for different tasks, emphasizing that various tasks necessitate distinct inductive biases. It presents specific augmentation strategies tailored for image and text classification tasks.

For image classification, the authors utilize RandAugment, a simplified version of AutoAugment. Unlike AutoAugment, which employs a search method to optimize augmentation strategies, RandAugment uniformly samples from a set of image processing transformations without requiring labeled data.

In the context of text classification, the paper highlights the use of back-translation as an effective augmentation method. This technique involves translating a text example into another language and then back to the original language, resulting in diverse paraphrases that maintain the original semantics. The authors note that they employ random sampling with a tunable temperature for generating paraphrases, enhancing diversity.

Additionally, the paper introduces a word-replacing technique using TF-IDF scores for text classification. This method focuses on retaining informative keywords while replacing less significant words, which is particularly beneficial for tasks like topic classification.

The authors also explore additional training techniques, including confidence-based masking, where examples that the model is uncertain about are masked out during training. This approach improves training efficiency by focusing on high-confidence examples.

Overall, the paper outlines various data augmentation strategies and techniques that enhance model performance across different tasks while addressing common challenges encountered in training.
