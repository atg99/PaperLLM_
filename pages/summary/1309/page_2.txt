This paper introduces Unsupervised Data Augmentation (UDA), a method that enhances data augmentation in consistency training for semi-supervised learning. UDA is evaluated across various language and vision tasks, demonstrating significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art models. Notably, on the IMDb text classification task, UDA with only 20 labeled examples surpasses a model trained with 1250 times more labeled data. In semi-supervised learning benchmarks like CIFAR-10 and SVHN, UDA achieves error rates of 5.43 and 2.72 with just 250 labeled examples, outperforming all current methods. Additionally, UDA shows effectiveness even with large supervised datasets, improving ImageNet top-1 accuracy from 58.84 to 68.78 using 10% of the labeled set and from 78.43 to 79.05 with the full labeled set plus 1.3 million unlabeled examples.

The paper outlines several key contributions: 

1. UDA leverages state-of-the-art data augmentations from supervised learning as a noise source within a consistency enforcing semi-supervised framework.
2. UDA can match or exceed the performance of purely supervised learning models that utilize significantly more labeled data.
3. UDA works effectively in conjunction with transfer learning, particularly when fine-tuning models like BERT, and is beneficial in high-data scenarios such as ImageNet.
4. A theoretical analysis is provided to explain how UDA enhances classification performance and the role of advanced augmentations.

The discussion includes a formal formulation of the classification task, the role of labeled and unlabeled data distributions, and insights into the implementation of UDA, which extends traditional supervised data augmentation techniques to semi-supervised learning contexts.
