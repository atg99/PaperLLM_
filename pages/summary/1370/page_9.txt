This paper provides a comprehensive survey on the control of multi-agent systems (MASs), which are increasingly utilized in complex tasks across various domains such as space applications, smart grids, and machine learning. The authors, Fei Chen and Wei Ren, trace the evolution of MASs over the last two decades while acknowledging their historical roots.

The survey begins with an introduction to fundamental concepts and definitions associated with MASs. It then delves into the dynamic models of agents, distinguishing between linear and nonlinear frameworks. The paper categorizes various cooperative tasks that MASs can perform, including consensus, coordinated tracking, formation control, distributed average tracking, distributed estimation, containment control, surrounding control, and distributed optimization.

Further, the authors address critical research challenges in the field, such as the effects of time delays, noise, disturbances, quantization, and the maintenance of connectivity among agents. This review serves as a valuable resource for understanding the advancements and ongoing challenges in the control of multi-agent systems.
