The introduction discusses the significance of multi-agent systems (MASs) in various research fields, highlighting their multidisciplinary nature. Notable recognitions such as the George S. Axelby outstanding paper award from IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control and the establishment of conferences and journals focused on MASs underscore their importance in systems and control. Key contributions by researchers like Craig Reynolds and Tamas Vicsek are mentioned, illustrating foundational principles and models that explore group behavior and interaction rules among agents.

The text contrasts MAS studies with traditional natural sciences, emphasizing that while reductionism has been effective in those fields, it falls short in explaining the emergent global behaviors in MASs. Instead, understanding local interactions among agents is crucial for leveraging their collective behavior. The introduction concludes by asserting that the complexity of interactions in MASs necessitates a deeper examination to harness their potential effectively.
