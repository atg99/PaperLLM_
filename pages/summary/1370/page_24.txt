Notable works include the study by Pósfai et al. on the effects of correlations on network controllability, highlighting the importance of network structure in control theory. Sun and Motter's research discusses the phenomena of controllability transition and nonlocality, which is crucial for understanding dynamic systems.

Pu et al. provide a robustness analysis of network controllability, emphasizing the resilience of networks under varying conditions. Chapman et al. explore the controllability and observability of network-of-networks through Cartesian products, expanding the theoretical framework for complex systems.

Chen et al. focus on minimal edge addition strategies to enhance network controllability, while Su et al. and Tanner et al. investigate flocking behaviors in multi-agent systems, contributing to the understanding of coordinated control strategies. Porfiri et al. propose a two-level consensus approach for tracking and formation control, underscoring the effectiveness of hierarchical methods.

Balch and Arkin's work on behavior-based formation control for multi-robot teams, alongside Lewis and Tan's research on high precision formation control using virtual structures, further illustrates the practical applications of these control theories. Lastly, Van den Broek et al. and Dong and Farrell examine formation control in mobile robots, emphasizing the significance of virtual structures and cooperative strategies in achieving desired formations.

These references collectively provide a comprehensive overview of advancements in network controllability and multi-agent coordination, offering valuable insights for both theoretical exploration and practical implementation in control systems.
