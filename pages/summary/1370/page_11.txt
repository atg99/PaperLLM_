The introduction discusses the concept of multi-agent systems (MASs), which consist of groups of agents that utilize local interaction rules to achieve collective goals. Examples such as schools of fish and flocks of birds illustrate the natural occurrence of MASs, which have gained interest across various scientific disciplines including physics, biology, and computer science. MASs offer advantages in solving complex engineering problems that may be unmanageable for individual agents, such as using a network of cameras to monitor large areas effectively. They are also characterized by robustness, as the failure of one or a few agents does not significantly impact the overall system, and scalability, as they maintain low computation and communication costs regardless of the system's size. Although the study of MASs has historical roots, it became a distinct research field in the early 21st century, highlighted by significant contributions such as the 2005 paper on coordinating mobile autonomous agents using the nearest neighbor rule.
