The references provided encompass various studies on multi-agent systems, focusing on coordinated control, consensus algorithms, and the impact of network structure on controllability. 

Key contributions include Ren and Atkins' work on distributed multi-vehicle coordination through local information exchange, and Ren's exploration of consensus algorithms for double-integrator dynamics. Hong, Chen, and Bushnell examine distributed observer designs for leader-following control, while Park et al. investigate leader-following consensus in systems with Markovian switching and delays. Meng, Dimarogonas, and Johansson address coordinated tracking of heterogeneous Lagrange systems, and Back and Kim propose a disturbance observer-based controller for uncertain multi-agent systems.

Khoo et al. present a robust finite-time consensus algorithm for multi-robot systems, and Egerstedt et al. analyze the relationship between network structure and controllability in consensus networks. Godsil discusses compact graphs and equitable partitions, and Martini et al. explore controllability decompositions using quotient graphs. The structural controllability of multi-agent systems is examined by Zamani and Lin, while Ji et al. investigate the effects of time-delay and switching topologies on system controllability.

These studies collectively advance the understanding of coordination and control in multi-agent systems, highlighting methodologies and challenges in achieving desired behaviors in complex networks.
