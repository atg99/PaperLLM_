This paper provides a comprehensive overview of multi-agent systems (MASs), beginning with foundational concepts and definitions. It defines key terms such as "agent" and "autonomy," and explains what constitutes a multi-agent system. The discussion includes an analysis of network topologies that facilitate information exchange, and contrasts centralized, decentralized, and distributed systems.

The paper then delves into various agent models, distinguishing between linear and nonlinear approaches. It further explores cooperative tasks that agents can perform, including consensus, leader-following coordination, flocking, formation control, coverage control, distributed average tracking, and distributed estimation.

Overall, this work serves as a foundational text for understanding the dynamics and functionalities of MASs, highlighting both theoretical models and practical applications.
