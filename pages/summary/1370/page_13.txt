The paper reviews the extensive literature on multi-agent systems (MASs), highlighting numerous survey articles, special issues, and books. It notes that significant overviews have been published in various IEEE journals, covering topics such as consensus in multi-vehicle cooperative control and motion coordination with distributed information. The paper emphasizes the dynamic development of MAS theory in the 21st century, with research expanding from consensus and formation control to distributed estimation and optimization. It also mentions the presence of MAS control courses at both undergraduate and graduate levels, reflecting the practical applications of MAS in fields like sensor networks, space systems, and distributed machine learning. A key characteristic of these systems is their decentralized nature, where agents operate autonomously without a central decision-maker. The paper indicates that the research scope of MASs continues to grow.
