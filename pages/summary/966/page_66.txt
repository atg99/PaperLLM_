The paper presents Algorithm 16, which implements the LSTD-Q(λ) algorithm using linear function approximation to evaluate a given stochastic policy π. The algorithm processes a list of transitions D, initializing matrices A, b, and z to zero. It iterates through the transitions, updating the matrices based on the features of the state-action pairs and the rewards received. The update rules incorporate both the features and the estimated action-value function, resulting in a final value of the policy evaluation denoted by θ.

The paper also discusses the concept of greedy improvements in policy iteration, where the current policy is evaluated using a critic based on extensive data. It explains that in finite action spaces, a greedy policy can be computed on an as-needed basis, which allows for the application of this algorithm in large or infinite state spaces. This leads to the LSPI (least-squares policy iteration) approach as proposed by Lagoudakis and Parr.

Additionally, the paper references finite-sample performance bounds for LSPI and its generalizations, as established by Antos et al. They extend these results to continuous action spaces, emphasizing the importance of restricting the policy class to avoid overfitting. The methods discussed allow for policy switching without ensuring continuity, which may have implications for the stability and performance of the learning process.
