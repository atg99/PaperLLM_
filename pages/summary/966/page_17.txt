To address these challenges, value iteration and policy iteration methods provide systematic approaches to estimate the value function. The convergence properties of these methods are supported by Banach's fixed-point theorem, ensuring that both value iteration and action-value function iteration converge to their respective optimal values at a geometric rate. 

In value iteration, the process involves updating the value function according to the Bellman equation, while in the case of action-value functions, the update is expressed through Q-values. Once these functions approximate their optimal counterparts closely, a greedy policy derived from them is expected to be near-optimal.

Policy iteration, on the other hand, alternates between evaluating the current policy and improving it based on the action-value function derived from that policy. Although policy iteration can yield a policy that is at least as good as that obtained from value iteration after the same number of iterations, it is computationally more intensive due to the policy evaluation step.

Value prediction problems, which include estimating the value function in Markov reward processes, have broad applications ranging from assessing the reliability of power grids to predicting flight delays. While Monte Carlo methods can estimate these values through averaging over multiple trials, they suffer from high variance and may not be feasible in all scenarios due to the costs associated with environmental interactions.
