The paper discusses the challenges of distinguishing between transient states and those that are merely difficult to reach in the context of reinforcement learning algorithms. It introduces the UCRL2 algorithm, which adheres to the principle of optimism in the face of uncertainty by constructing confidence intervals around estimates of transition probabilities and immediate rewards. This results in a set of plausible Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), denoted as Ct. The UCRL2 algorithm computes a policy that maximizes average reward within this class without updating the policy at every time step, instead waiting for sufficient improvement in the statistics of at least one state-action pair.

A key component of UCRL2 is the procedure OptSolve, which employs undiscounted value iteration over a specialized MDP. In this MDP, actions are represented as pairs of an action and a plausible next-state distribution, which reflects the statistics collected thus far. The immediate reward is determined by the highest plausible reward based on local statistics.

Additionally, the paper explores PAC-MDP algorithms, which focus on minimizing the number of instances where the learner's expected return falls below the optimal return by a specified margin. These algorithms, which include R-max, MBIE, Delayed Q-learning, and others, are characterized by their ability to bound this measure with high probability as a polynomial function of the MDP's natural parameters, while performing polynomially many computational steps at each time step. Among these, MorMax is noted for providing the best bounds on the number of suboptimal steps, T. The paper also references previous work that has established the PAC-MDP status of certain algorithms and provided lower bounds for their performance.
