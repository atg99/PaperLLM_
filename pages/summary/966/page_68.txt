This section discusses policy gradient methods, which utilize stochastic gradient ascent on a performance surface defined by a parameterized policy class of stochastic stationary policies. In scenarios with a finite action space, Gibbs policies are commonly employed, where the action-selection probability is expressed using a feature-extraction function. For continuous action spaces, Gaussian policies are often used, characterized by parametric mean and covariance functions. It is crucial to maintain positive definiteness of the covariance matrix, which is frequently simplified to a scaled identity matrix.

The objective is to determine the parameter that maximizes the expected return of the policy, which can be evaluated based on an initial state distribution. This approach aligns with maximizing long-run average rewards, provided the Markov chain generated by the policy is ergodic.

To estimate the gradient of the expected return, the score function is defined as the derivative of the log-probability of selecting an action given a state. In the context of Gibbs policies, the score function accounts for the difference between the feature representation of the action and its expected value under the policy. The section concludes with a definition of a gradient term that incorporates the score function and the expected action distribution, highlighting the complexities in finding an optimal policy within a restricted class.
