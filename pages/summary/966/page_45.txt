The paper discusses advancements in nonparametric techniques for control learning, highlighting their limitations in managing the trade-off between approximation and estimation errors. While earlier methods, such as those by Keller et al. and Parr et al., did not address this issue, subsequent research has integrated supervised learning methods, including regression trees and kernelized algorithms, to regularize estimates and control loss. Notably, Kolter and Ng's LASSO-inspired algorithm employs l1-regularization for feature selection within the context of Least-Squares Temporal Difference learning (LSTD). However, despite these innovations, the statistical properties of these approaches remain underexplored.

Farahmand et al. have introduced a regularization-based approach that offers statistical guarantees. Nonetheless, the computational expense associated with some nonparametric techniques poses a challenge. In scenarios where rapid data generation is feasible, simpler incremental methods or linear function approximations may be preferred over complex nonparametric methods. Conversely, in cases with limited data or complex problems requiring meticulous function tuning, the focus shifts to solution quality rather than computational efficiency.

The paper transitions into control learning, categorizing various learning problems based on whether the learner can influence observations, leading to discussions on interactive learning and classical dynamic programming methods. This structured exploration aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of control learning challenges and methodologies.
