The section discusses least-squares methods in reinforcement learning, drawing parallels to the LMS algorithm in adaptive filtering. It highlights that both approaches are sensitive to step-size selection, initial parameter distance, and the eigenvalue structure of the matrix governing updates. Various strategies have been proposed to mitigate these issues, such as adaptive step sizes, normalizing updates, and reusing samples, although each has its limitations.

The key focus is on Least-squares temporal difference (LSTD) learning, which aims to satisfy the equation E['tϕt+1(θ)] = 0 in the limit of an infinite number of examples. With a finite sample, the equation can be approximated, leading to a linear relationship in θ. If the matrix ^An is non-singular, the solution can be computed as θn = ^An⁻¹^bn, where ^bn is derived from the rewards and features. This method can yield a more accurate approximation of the equilibrium solution compared to TD(0) and other incremental first-order methods, especially when the dimensionality of features is manageable and computational resources allow for matrix inversion. The approach of directly solving the linear equation is credited to Bradtke and Barto.
