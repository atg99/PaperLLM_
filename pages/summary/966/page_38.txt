The paper discusses the LSTD(λ) solution derived from a specific equation, emphasizing that it is defined as the parameter value that results in zero cumulative updates. The eligibility traces play a crucial role in this process and are expressed in a linear form. The recursive variant, RLSTD(λ), has been explored by various researchers. 

A significant concern with LSTD(λ) is the potential lack of a solution for Equation (30). However, in on-policy scenarios with sufficient sample sizes, a solution typically exists. When a solution is absent, a common strategy is to add a small positive diagonal matrix to facilitate inversion, although this does not guarantee success. A more robust approach is to focus on minimizing the projected Bellman error, which is always well-defined when the matrix is invertible.

The paper states that under standard assumptions regarding the sample, LSTD(λ) and its recursive forms converge almost surely to the solution of the projected fixed-point equation, provided the solution exists. This convergence has been validated for both on-policy and off-policy cases, with earlier research confirming these findings.

Furthermore, (R)LSTD(λ) circumvents challenges associated with tuning incremental algorithms, such as sensitivity to step-sizes and eigenvalue structures. Experimental evidence indicates that parameters derived from (R)LSTD(λ) converge more rapidly than those from TD(λ), though their computational characteristics differ significantly. 

Lastly, the paper introduces LSPE, or least-squares policy evaluation, as an alternative to LSTD and LSTD(λ), which aims to replicate multi-step value iteration while assuming linear function approximation.
