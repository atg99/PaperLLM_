The paper introduces the Bellman optimality operator, denoted as T, which maps from RX to RX and is defined by the equation (T V)(x) = sup_{a ∈ A}(r(x; a) + Σ_{y ∈ X}P(x; a; y)V(y)). This operator is nonlinear due to the supremum operation involved. The relationship T V = V indicates a fixed-point equation where T is a contraction mapping when 0 < γ < 1, ensuring the existence of a unique solution.

To reduce complexity, expressions such as (T V)(x) will be simplified to T V(x), with the understanding that the operator T takes precedence over point evaluation. Additionally, the paper discusses action-value functions associated with policies and Markov Reward Processes (MRPs), defining two operators: T for the optimal action-value function and T for the policy action-value function. Both operators are maximum-norm contractions, with the action-value function of policy π satisfying T Q = Q, ensuring uniqueness.

The section concludes by laying the groundwork for dynamic programming algorithms, specifically value iteration, which produces a sequence of value functions expressed as Vk+1 = T Vk for k ≥ 0. This establishes the foundation for solving Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) using the value- and policy-iteration algorithms.
