This paper discusses reinforcement learning, a paradigm focused on controlling systems to maximize a numerical performance measure that reflects long-term objectives. Unlike supervised learning, reinforcement learning relies on partial feedback regarding predictions, which can influence future states of the system, highlighting the importance of time. The aim is to create efficient learning algorithms while understanding their advantages and limitations. The paper emphasizes the practical applications of reinforcement learning across various fields, including artificial intelligence, operations research, and control engineering. It specifically explores algorithms based on dynamic programming, providing a detailed catalog of learning problems and various methods such as closed-loop interactive learning, direct methods, and actor-critic methods. Additionally, it offers guidance for further exploration through suggested readings, applications, and software resources, along with theoretical foundations related to discounted Markovian decision processes.
