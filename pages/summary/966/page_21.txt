The paper discusses off-policy learning, a technique in reinforcement learning where the behavior policy used to generate data differs from the target policy being evaluated. This method enables simultaneous learning about multiple policies and long-term prediction problems. The learning process is defined using triplets (Xt, Rt+1, Yt+1), where Yt+1 is derived from the transition kernel P(X, ·). In episodic contexts, when a terminal state is reached, the process restarts from an initial distribution P0, creating episodes characterized by continual sampling with restarts.

The paper also explores the convergence rate of the tabular TD(0) method, which follows a standard linear stochastic approximation approach, with a convergence rate of O(1/√t). This rate is influenced by the choice of step-size sequences, the transition kernel P0, and the value of the return.

Additionally, the every-visit Monte-Carlo method is introduced as an alternative for estimating state values by calculating sample means. In episodic problems, where trajectories are finite, the method involves generating episodes through continual sampling from the distribution P0. The return from a specific time t until the end of the episode is defined, allowing for the updating of value estimates through a specified formula. This method provides a systematic way to refine value estimates based on observed returns, enhancing the understanding of state values in the context of reinforcement learning.
