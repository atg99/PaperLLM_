The listed references highlight significant contributions to the field of reinforcement learning and related areas. Tanner and White (2009) introduce RL-Glue, a versatile software framework for conducting reinforcement learning experiments independent of programming language. Taylor and Parr (2009) present kernelized value function approximation techniques that enhance learning efficiency. Tesauro (1994) showcases TD-Gammon, a self-learning backgammon program that achieves master-level performance, illustrating the potential of reinforcement learning in complex games.

Thrun (1992) discusses efficient exploration strategies in reinforcement learning, while Toussaint et al. (2008) focus on optimizing hierarchical POMDP controllers through likelihood maximization. Tsitsiklis' body of work (1994, 1996, 1997, 1999a, 1999b, 2001) covers various aspects of reinforcement learning, including asynchronous stochastic approximation, exploration in multi-armed bandit problems, and the application of temporal difference learning with function approximation. Lastly, Tsybakov (2009) provides foundational insights into nonparametric estimation, which is relevant for statistical learning methods in reinforcement learning contexts. 

Overall, these works collectively advance the understanding and application of reinforcement learning methodologies, providing a robust framework for future research and experimentation.
