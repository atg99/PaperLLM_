The paper discusses the inventory management problem framed within the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), highlighting the significance of transition functions and their relationship to transition kernels. It notes that while not all actions are relevant in every state, meaningless actions can be remapped to more appropriate ones, although this may complicate the dynamics. An alternative solution is to establish a mapping that defines admissible actions for each state.

The paper also differentiates between various types of states within MDPs, particularly focusing on terminal or absorbing states, where once entered, the process remains in that state indefinitely, incurring no rewards. This leads to the concept of episodic MDPs, where the focus is on the time period until reaching a terminal state, often considering undiscounted rewards.

An illustrative example is provided involving a gambling scenario, where a gambler stakes a fraction of her wealth with uncertain outcomes, modeled as an episodic MDP. The gambler's objective is to maximize the likelihood of achieving a predetermined wealth target, with the state space defined by her current wealth. This example emphasizes the practical application of MDPs in decision-making under uncertainty.
