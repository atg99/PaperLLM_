The paper discusses an error decomposition related to the modeling of m-step rewards and transitions in reinforcement learning. It presents a formula that illustrates how the Bellman error can be minimized by ensuring that the features accurately capture both immediate rewards and the structure of the value function. The parameter ϕ plays a crucial role, as its value influences whether the focus is on short-term or long-term dynamics. As ϕ approaches 1, capturing the value function becomes more critical, while a value closer to 0 emphasizes immediate rewards.

The paper also addresses the challenges of gradient temporal difference (TD) learning, particularly in off-policy scenarios, which can lead to divergence. It introduces alternative algorithms developed by Sutton et al. that maintain efficiency similar to TD(ϕ) while addressing instability issues. The analysis is simplified by considering the case where ϕ equals 0 and linear function approximation is used. The objective function for minimizing the error is defined, highlighting that all solutions to the corresponding equation are also minimizers of this objective function, thus providing a pathway to achieve convergence in the context of TD learning.
