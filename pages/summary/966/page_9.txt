The paper discusses a decision-making framework modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). It introduces a random state denoted as At2A, representing the action chosen by the decision maker at time t. Once an action is selected, the system transitions to a new random state Xt+1 and reward Rt+1, governed by a probabilistic function P. The expected reward is expressed as E[Rt+1|Xt;At] = r(Xt;At). The decision maker's objective is to maximize the expected total discounted reward over time.

The decision-making process is influenced by a behavior rule, which dictates how actions are selected based on the observed history of states and actions. The return from a behavior is defined as the total discounted sum of rewards, where a discount factor 0 < < 1 gives more weight to immediate rewards compared to future ones. An MDP is classified as a discounted reward MDP when this return is used, whereas it is termed undiscounted when = 1.

An example provided illustrates inventory control with uncertain demand, where the decision maker must determine the quantity of inventory to order daily. The costs associated with purchasing and holding inventory are highlighted, emphasizing the need to maximize the present value of expected future income. The paper outlines the complexities involved in optimizing decision-making in uncertain environments, showcasing the application of MDPs in real-world scenarios.
