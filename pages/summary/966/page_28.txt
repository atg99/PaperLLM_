The paper discusses the challenges of state representation in high-dimensional spaces, particularly when using multiple cameras. It highlights that while the representation can reach millions, the intrinsic dimensionality may remain low, around 12. The author critiques a simplistic approach that suggests minimizing the number of cameras to reduce dimensionality, arguing that more information can be beneficial. Instead, the focus should be on developing advanced algorithms and function approximation methods capable of handling high-dimensional yet low-complexity problems.

Potential methods include strip-like tilings with hash functions, low-discrepancy grids, and random projections. Nonlinear function approximation methods, such as neural networks and radial basis function networks, are noted for their promise, along with nonparametric techniques that allow for a flexible representation that adapts as needed.

The paper details a nonparametric method exemplified by k-nearest neighbor regression, where predictions are made based on the proximity of data points without a fixed dimensional representation. It introduces nonparametric kernel smoothing as an extension of this method, using a data-based kernel to predict values. Additionally, it mentions other approaches that involve finding appropriate functions in infinite-dimensional function spaces, such as Reproducing Kernel Hilbert spaces, leading to techniques like spline smoothers and Gaussian process regression. The paper concludes with the idea of recursively splitting the input space to predict values in finer regions.
