The paper discusses the implementation of the fitted Q-iteration algorithm, specifically focusing on one iteration of the algorithm as outlined in Algorithm 14. This function, called FittedQ, operates on a list of transitions, denoted as D, and regression parameters, denoted as ϕ. The algorithm iteratively constructs a target value T for each transition, utilizing the predict method to estimate the maximum value of future rewards based on current regression parameters.

Key steps in the FittedQ function include initializing an empty list S, iterating through the transitions to compute target values, appending input-output pairs to S, and finally updating the regression parameters through the regress method. The algorithm is characterized as a non-expansion, which in conjunction with a contraction, ensures the convergence of the fitted value iteration process.

The paper references previous works that highlight the necessity of using specific regressors to avoid divergence in fitted Q-iteration. It notes recommendations for kernel averaging and tree-based regressors, which ensure convergence through local averaging. Additionally, empirical results indicate that neural networks can be effective when incrementally updating samples based on a policy that is greedy concerning the most recent estimates.

The importance of modifying the sample set to include states frequently visited by effective policies is emphasized, particularly when a good initial policy is not available. This aspect is theoretically supported by prior research in state aggregation, underscoring the algorithm's reliance on diverse and representative sampling for optimal performance.
