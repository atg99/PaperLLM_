The paper discusses the iterative Least-Squares Temporal Difference (iLSTD) method, highlighting its per-iteration complexity of O(sd) and its competitive accuracy relative to LSTD when samples are sparse. The storage requirement for iLSTD after processing n samples is O(min(ns² + d; d²)). When the sparsity condition ns² < d² holds, iLSTD may be a viable alternative to LSTD and incremental TD-methods.

The choice of function space is crucial for measuring the quality of an approximate value function, which is typically assessed using mean-squared error (MSE) based on a specific state distribution. While MSE serves as a useful metric, the paper acknowledges that this may not be adequate for scenarios where value estimation is a component of more complex algorithms. Nevertheless, it suggests that the conclusions drawn regarding function space choices are likely applicable to other quality measures as well.

Learning is framed as selecting a function from a finite space represented by d parameters. The approximation error is defined as the infimum of the distance between the approximated function and the target function. Increasing the function space is generally encouraged to reduce approximation error; however, this approach has its drawbacks due to the inherent limitations of learning from incomplete information.

In the context of linear function approximation, the paper simplifies the discussion by assuming LSTD is used to derive parameters with a zero discount factor. Under these conditions, LSTD computes the minimizer of the empirical loss, highlighting the importance of function space selection in achieving effective value approximation.
