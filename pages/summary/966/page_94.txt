The cited works primarily focus on advancements in reinforcement learning, particularly on algorithms and theoretical frameworks that enhance learning efficiency and convergence. Singh et al. (2000) present convergence results for single-step on-policy algorithms, while Singh and Sutton (1996) explore the concept of replacing eligibility traces in reinforcement learning. Singh and Yee (1994) discuss the limitations of approximate optimal-value functions, establishing an upper bound on potential losses.

Sutton's contributions, spanning from his PhD thesis on temporal credit assignment (1984) to various methodologies like temporal differences (1988) and policy gradient methods (1999), lay foundational principles in reinforcement learning. The 1998 book co-authored with Barto serves as a comprehensive introduction to the field.

Strehl et al. (2006) introduce the PAC model-free reinforcement learning approach, while their subsequent works analyze model-based interval estimation (2005) and online linear regression applications (2008). Strens (2000) presents a Bayesian framework, enriching the theoretical landscape of reinforcement learning.

Collectively, these studies contribute to the understanding of reinforcement learning dynamics, addressing both practical algorithms and theoretical constraints, ultimately aiding in the development of more robust learning systems.
