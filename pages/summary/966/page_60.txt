The paper discusses two approaches to address the challenges of aggregation in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the smoothness of value functions at the boundaries of regions in state-action spaces. The first approach, proposed by Singh et al. in 1995, introduces a softened version of Q-learning. This method utilizes a linear averaging form for the action-value function, defined as Qθ(x;a) = Σdi=1 si(x;a)θi, where si(x;a) is non-negative and the sum of si equals one. The update rule in this algorithm is modified to update only one component of the parameter vector θ at a time, selected via a multinomial distribution based on the values of si.

The second approach, presented by Szepesvári and Smart in 2004, is known as interpolation-based Q-learning (IBQ-learning). This method enhances the previous algorithm by simultaneously updating all components of the parameter vector, thereby reducing variance in updates. IBQ-learning generalizes Q-learning by treating each component θi as an estimate of the value for a representative state-action pair (xi;ai). The algorithm employs similarity functions to determine how closely related the current state-action pair is to the representative pairs, affecting the update rules accordingly.

The update rule for IBQ-learning combines the immediate reward with the maximum estimated future rewards, adjusted by the similarity of the current state-action pair to the representative pairs. The paper outlines conditions for convergence of the algorithm, including requirements on the function class Qθ, the choice of local step sizes, and sufficient exploration of the state-action space. Error bounds on the action-value function are also provided, emphasizing the importance of the interpolation property in ensuring convergence.
