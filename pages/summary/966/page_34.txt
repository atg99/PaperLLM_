The paper discusses the formulation and implementation of two algorithms in the context of gradient temporal difference learning, specifically GTD2 and TDC. It introduces shorthand notations for key variables and reformulates the objective function J(θ) in a concise manner. The gradient of J(θ) is derived, leading to the establishment of updates for two sets of weights, θt and wt. 

In GTD2, the update for θt is based on the negative stochastic gradient of J, under the assumption that wt approximates w(θt). The update equations for both θt and wt are presented, where wt follows a Least-Mean Square (LMS) rule. The paper cites previous work that confirms the convergence of θt to the minimizer of J(θ) under standard conditions, highlighting that this convergence is independent of the distribution of the input variables. 

The algorithm also notes that the computational cost of GTD2 is only twice that of the simpler TD(0) algorithm. Additionally, the pseudocode for GTD2 is provided. Following this, the paper introduces the TDC algorithm, reformulating the gradient in a way that incorporates corrections, thereby enhancing learning efficiency. Overall, these algorithms aim to improve the convergence and stability of gradient-based learning methods in reinforcement learning contexts.
