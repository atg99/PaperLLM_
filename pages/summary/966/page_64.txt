In this section, the focus is on implementing a critic within the context of reinforcement learning, specifically for estimating the value of the current target policy utilized by the actor. The critic's role is framed as a value prediction problem, where methods from previous sections are applicable. The algorithms discussed are SARSA(λ) and LSTD-Q(λ), which adapt the underlying techniques to estimate action values directly.

SARSA, similar to Q-learning, manages action-value pairs for finite state and action spaces. It is characterized by its update rule, which incorporates the current state, action, reward, next state, and next action to compute the temporal difference (TD) error. The algorithm’s convergence is assured by the foundational convergence results of TD(0). The multi-step extension of SARSA leads to the SARSA(λ) algorithm, which can be adapted for function approximation, although it may face convergence issues in off-policy situations. Extensions like GTD2 and TDC can mitigate these limitations.

LSTD-Q(λ) emerges from generalizing LSTD(λ) for action-value functions, allowing it to address the value prediction problem more effectively. This algorithm incorporates the transition dynamics and rewards into its updates, indicating its adaptability for action-value estimation.

Overall, both SARSA(λ) and LSTD-Q(λ) are significant contributions to the reinforcement learning landscape, providing robust methods for value estimation in various contexts.
