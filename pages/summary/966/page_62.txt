Antos et al. (2007) and Munos and Szepesvári (2008) establish finite-sample performance bounds for a broad range of regression methods that utilize empirical risk minimization within a specified space of candidate action-value functions. These bounds are influenced by the worst-case Bellman error of the function space, denoted as e1(F), which quantifies the closeness of the function space F to the true Bellman operator. The derived bounds resemble those found in supervised learning but replace the approximation error with e1(F). Notably, in cases where fitted-value iteration fails to converge, e1(F) equals one, indicating that the rigidity of the function approximation method may lead to divergence.

In the context of actor-critic methods, which implement generalized policy iteration (GPI), the process alternates between policy evaluation and improvement. Due to sample limitations or function approximation constraints, exact policy evaluation may be unfeasible. Consequently, GPI allows for policy updates based on partial evaluations. The actor works to enhance the current policy while the critic assesses it to guide the actor's improvements. It is important to differentiate between the behavior policy, which generates samples, and the target policy, which is evaluated and refined. This distinction is critical for the critic to learn about actions that are not favored by the current target policy. Typically, the target policy is stochastic to ensure exploration of the state space. However, estimating values for low-probability actions can be unreliable due to insufficient information, and while random action selection might seem informative, it can neglect significant areas of the state space. Thus, practical implementations must strike a balance between exploration and targeted action selection to optimize learning outcomes.
