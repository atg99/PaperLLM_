The paper discusses an episodic Markov reward process characterized by deterministic transitions and a specific reward structure. The reward is zero except for a transition from state 3 to state 4, which is determined by a Bernoulli random variable with a parameter of 0.5. State 4 is a terminal state, and upon reaching it, the process resets to either state 1 or state 2 with probabilities of 0.9 and 0.1, respectively.

The paper contrasts the efficiency of the Monte-Carlo method and TD(0) in terms of convergence speed. While the Monte-Carlo method benefits from having a true target value of 1, thus converging faster, TD(0) requires the value of an intermediate state to approximate its true value, which can slow down its convergence. This discrepancy becomes more pronounced in scenarios with longer chains of states, where rewards are sparse.

The text introduces the TD(λ) family of methods, which unifies the strengths of Monte-Carlo and TD(0). By varying the parameter λ between 0 and 1, one can interpolate between the two methods: λ=0 corresponds to TD(0), while λ=1 aligns with the Monte-Carlo approach. This flexibility allows for a mixture of updates that can leverage the advantages of both methodologies.
