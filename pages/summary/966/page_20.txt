The paper discusses the convergence of the value function estimate ^Vt in the context of reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly focusing on the choice of step-sizes. It establishes that ^Vt converges almost surely to the true value function under certain conditions. A simple step-size sequence is proposed, where the step-size is defined as βt = c t^(-γ) with c > 0 and 1/2 < γ < 1. While γ = 1 yields the smallest step-sizes, a value closer to 1/2 is recommended for better transient performance due to larger step-sizes leading to more significant updates.

The paper introduces the iterate-averaging method by Polyak and Juditsky, which is theoretically optimal for asymptotic convergence but rarely applied in practice. Instead, practitioners often prefer constant step-sizes despite their violation of the Robbins-Monro (RM) conditions, justified by the non-stationary nature of the environments and the small sample regime in which these algorithms are often executed. The paper also highlights ongoing research into automatic step-size tuning methods, although no consensus exists on the most effective approach.

Additionally, the algorithm can accommodate a sequence of observations from an ergodic Markov chain, maintaining convergence to the underlying value function without dependency on the state distribution. This flexibility allows for the potential control of state distributions in simulations and the ability to learn about a target policy while following a different behavior policy, enhancing the algorithm's applicability in various contexts.
