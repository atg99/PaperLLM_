The paper discusses the convergence of the policy iteration procedure in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and establishes key results regarding optimal policies. It demonstrates that if a contraction mapping T has a fixed point, then the existence of a unique fixed point V implies that a sequence of policies generated through policy iteration will eventually converge to an optimal policy. This is confirmed by showing that the last policy in the iteration satisfies the condition TV = TπV = V, indicating optimality.

Corollary 4 asserts that in finite MDPs, the policy iteration process will terminate after a finite number of steps, yielding an optimal policy. It further states that a stationary policy is optimal if its value function is a fixed point of T.

Corollary 5 extends these results by stating that if V is the unique fixed point of T, then any policy that is greedy with respect to V is optimal. It also notes that if there exists an optimal stationary policy π*, then V = V* and π* is greedy with respect to V*.

The proofs rely on previous theorems, emphasizing that the strictly improving nature of the policy sequence ensures termination and the conditions for optimality. The references indicate a foundation of reinforcement learning applications and related research.
