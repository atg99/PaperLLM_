The paper discusses two types of learning problems in the context of reinforcement learning: non-interactive and interactive learning. Non-interactive learning focuses on finding an effective policy based on a fixed sample of data, often derived from prior experimentation. This scenario aligns with batch learning, where the learner faces off-policy challenges due to uncontrolled observations. The learner's objective is generally to develop a good policy efficiently, even when limited by the fixed nature of the sample.

In contrast, interactive learning allows the learner to influence the sample distribution while engaging with a real system in a closed-loop manner. The primary goals in this setting include optimizing online performance, which can be measured by the total rewards accrued during the learning process or by tracking the frequency of suboptimal decisions made by the learner. Unlike non-interactive learning, interactive learning provides the learner with the flexibility to actively control the sampling process, enhancing the likelihood of discovering an effective policy.

Furthermore, if a simulator is available, learning algorithms can be adapted for planning problems, where traditional performance metrics may no longer apply. The paper clarifies the terminology used in the discussion of learning types, noting that "active learning" is already defined within the machine learning community, complicating the classification of these learning scenarios.
