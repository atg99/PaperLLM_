The UCRL2 algorithm is an approach for reinforcement learning focusing on finite state spaces. It initializes a policy and updates the model based on new information gathered during execution. The algorithm maintains arrays for tracking state-action counts and rewards, and it executes actions in a simulated environment while incrementally updating its estimates.

A notable feature of UCRL2 is its performance bounds, which scale logarithmically with the size of the state space. This is significant as it provides a favorable comparison to other algorithms, such as Delayed Q-learning, which do not exhibit this scaling. However, UCRL2 and similar algorithms face limitations in handling larger state spaces effectively.

Kakade et al. and Strehl and Littman address these limitations by proposing restricted classes of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and meta-algorithms to tackle exploration challenges. Nonetheless, these methods present practical difficulties, including the challenge of classifying specific problems within these restricted classes and the reliance on black-box MDP solvers, which complicates implementation due to the inherent complexity of solving large MDPs.
