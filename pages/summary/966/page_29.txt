The paper discusses the distinction between parametric and nonparametric methods in the context of machine learning, particularly focusing on tree-based methods. It highlights that the boundary between these two categories is not clear-cut. For instance, a linear predictor can become nonparametric if the number of basis functions is allowed to vary, suggesting that many practical applications lean towards nonparametric techniques due to their flexibility. However, this flexibility often results in higher computational complexity, necessitating efficient implementations, such as k-D trees for nearest neighbor methods. 

The paper also addresses the challenge of tuning nonparametric methods to avoid overfitting or underfitting, exemplified by the k-nearest neighbor method, where choosing an appropriate k is crucial. While the discussion predominantly centers on parametric function approximation, it notes that many algorithms can extend to nonparametric techniques.

Additionally, the paper acknowledges that in real-world scenarios, direct measurement of the state is often impractical. However, effective methods can still operate using feature-based representations of states, such as camera images, and state estimators can be constructed from historical observations.
