The referenced works encompass significant contributions to the field of reinforcement learning and approximate dynamic programming. Farahmand et al. (2008, 2009) introduce regularized fitted Q-iteration and regularized policy iteration, emphasizing techniques that enhance planning efficiency. Frank et al. (2008) explore reinforcement learning in contexts with rare events, addressing challenges in learning under such conditions. 

George and Powell (2006) discuss adaptive step sizes for recursive estimation, which have implications for improving the convergence of algorithms in dynamic programming. Geramifard et al. (2007) present iLSTD, focusing on eligibility traces and convergence analysis, contributing to the understanding of learning efficiency. 

Ghahramani (2007) compiles proceedings from the ICML, showcasing a variety of advancements in machine learning. Ghavamzadeh and Engel (2007) propose Bayesian actor-critic algorithms, which integrate Bayesian methods into reinforcement learning frameworks. 

Gittins (1989) provides foundational work on multi-armed bandit problems, critical for decision-making under uncertainty. Glynn (1990) introduces likelihood ratio gradient estimation, enhancing stochastic system analysis. Gordon (1995) addresses stable function approximation in dynamic programming, a key aspect for ensuring reliable learning outcomes. 

Lastly, Gosavi (2003, 2004) focuses on simulation-based optimization and reinforcement learning for long-run average costs, expanding the application of these techniques in operational research contexts. Collectively, these works reflect the evolving landscape of reinforcement learning methodologies and their applications.
