The paper discusses the convergence of empirical loss functions to true loss functions in the context of estimating value functions and control algorithms. It highlights that if the empirical loss, Ln(µ), is uniformly close to the true loss, L(µ), across parameters, then the losses at optimal parameters are expected to be similarly close. The challenges of estimating and approximating these losses increase with higher dimensionality of the parameters, emphasizing a trade-off between estimation errors and approximation errors.

The paper references various studies, such as those by Antos et al. and Munos and Szepesvári, which establish finite-sample performance bounds for algorithms like fitted value iteration and actor-critic methods, even in the presence of noise from rewards and state transitions. Furthermore, it addresses the critical role of selecting the appropriate function space for these estimations and notes the growing interest in automating this selection process. Methods discussed include constructing a concise set of features or basis functions, with examples such as tuning Gaussian RBF parameters through gradient or cross-entropy methods.
