The paper discusses the convergence of value functions in the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and presents two key theorems related to optimal policies and policy improvement.

The first theorem establishes that if a fixed point V exists for the operator T and there is a policy π that is greedy with respect to V, then V is also the fixed point of T, confirming that π is an optimal policy. The proof utilizes properties of inequalities and the nature of contractions in functional spaces, demonstrating that for any stationary policy π, the value function derived from π will not exceed the value function derived from T. This leads to the conclusion that the value function associated with any policy is less than or equal to V, thus establishing that V is optimal.

The second theorem, known as the policy improvement theorem, states that starting from an arbitrary stationary policy π₀, if a new policy π is greedy with respect to the value function of π₀, then π will improve upon π₀. The proof involves showing that the value function corresponding to the new policy is greater than or equal to that of the old policy, and if a strict improvement occurs for any state, it confirms that π outperforms π₀ at that state. The theorem also notes that if the value function does not improve, then the original policy is optimal.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of greedy policies and their relationship to optimality in dynamic programming, particularly in finite action spaces, while also addressing conditions under which these results hold in infinite action spaces.
