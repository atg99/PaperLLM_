In this paper, the focus is on addressing challenges in reinforcement learning, particularly in closed-loop systems where resetting the state is not feasible. The Monte-Carlo technique, commonly used for estimation, introduces bias under these conditions. To tackle this issue, the paper introduces Temporal Difference (TD) learning, a pivotal concept in reinforcement learning, which utilizes bootstrapping—using predictions as targets during learning.

The section begins by defining the basic TD algorithm and explaining the mechanics of bootstrapping. A comparison is drawn between TD learning and traditional Monte-Carlo methods, highlighting their respective advantages. The TD(λ) algorithm is also introduced, which integrates both approaches.

Specifically, the paper discusses the tabular case of TD(0) within finite Markov Reward Processes (MRPs). It details the process of estimating the value function V given a realization of the MRP. The update mechanism of TD(0) is presented, where the value associated with the most recently visited state is adjusted based on the immediate reward and the estimated value of the next state. This adjustment is influenced by a user-defined step-size sequence, facilitating the bootstrapping process that characterizes TD learning. 

Overall, the paper lays the groundwork for understanding TD learning in finite state spaces and sets the stage for extending these concepts to larger state spaces in subsequent sections.
