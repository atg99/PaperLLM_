The paper discusses the relationship between policies in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and their long-run average rewards, denoted as ρ(π). Each policy leads to a recurrent Markov chain with a unique stationary distribution π, and the optimal long-run average reward is defined as ρ*. The regret of a learning algorithm A is defined as RA_T = Tρ* - RA_T, where RA_T is the cumulative reward obtained by following algorithm A up to time T. Minimizing regret is equivalent to maximizing total reward, and a consistent algorithm A achieves a sublinear growth rate of regret (RA_T = o(T)), ensuring its long-term average reward aligns with ρ*.

The UCRL2 algorithm is presented as achieving logarithmic regret, with its performance characterized by the diameter D of the MDP and the gap g between the optimal and second-best policy. Under certain conditions, if the confidence parameter is set to ε = 1/(3T), the expected regret can be bounded as O(D²|X|²|A| log(T)/g). However, this bound may not be useful if the gap g is small or if the MDP has infinite diameter due to transient states. An alternative bound independent of g is also presented, which has the form O(D|X|√|A|T log T), but remains vacuous for MDPs with infinite diameters.

The paper further notes that the only known algorithm providing regret bounds in the presence of transient states requires prior knowledge of some MDP parameters, as discussed by Bartlett and Tewari.
