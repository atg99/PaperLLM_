The paper discusses the determination of the value function for a policy in a Markov Decision Process (MDP). It introduces the Bellman equations for deterministic policies, which express the value function V under a policy π as a recursive relationship involving the immediate reward function r and the expected values of future states. The Bellman operator Tπ is defined, and it is shown that this operator is a contraction mapping when the discount factor is between 0 and 1, ensuring a unique solution for the fixed-point equation TπV = V.

For finite state spaces, the value function can be viewed as a vector in a Euclidean space, leading to a compact representation of the Bellman equation. The paper also addresses the case of Markov Reward Processes (MRPs) with a similar form of the Bellman operator.

Furthermore, the optimal value function V* is characterized by its own fixed-point equation, which involves taking the supremum over possible actions a, thus integrating the immediate rewards and future value expectations. This highlights the relationship between the value function, policies, and the structure of MDPs in determining optimal strategies.
