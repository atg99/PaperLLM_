The cited works encompass significant contributions to the fields of reinforcement learning and multi-agent systems. Li et al. (2009) focus on learning exercise policies for American options, highlighting the intersection of financial applications and reinforcement learning techniques. Lin (1992) discusses self-improving reactive agents that utilize reinforcement learning, planning, and teaching to enhance performance. Littman (1994) introduces Markov games as a framework for multi-agent reinforcement learning, establishing a foundation for future research in cooperative and competitive environments.

Further advancements are presented by Littman et al. (2001), who explore predictive representations of state, and Maei et al. (2010a, 2010b), who work on convergent temporal-difference learning and off-policy control with function approximation, emphasizing the importance of function approximation in reinforcement learning. Maei and Sutton (2010) introduce GQ(λ), a general gradient algorithm for temporal-difference prediction learning with eligibility traces, contributing to the theoretical understanding of learning algorithms.

Mahadevan (2009) discusses new frontiers in learning representation and control within Markov decision processes, while McAllester and Myllymaki (2008) provide insights from the 24th Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence. Melo et al. (2008) analyze reinforcement learning with function approximation, and Menache et al. (2005) investigate basis function adaptation in temporal difference learning.

Mnih et al. (2008) present empirical Bernstein stopping, contributing to the understanding of learning efficiency, and Munos and Szepesvari (2008) offer finite-time bounds for fitted value iteration, advancing the theoretical boundaries of reinforcement learning methods. Collectively, these works represent a rich tapestry of research that has shaped the development of reinforcement learning and its applications across various domains.
