The paper discusses the Actor-Critic architecture in reinforcement learning, highlighting its flexibility in handling different action spaces. For small action spaces, the critic can utilize an approximate action-value function, while the actor may implement exploration strategies like epsilon-greedy or Boltzmann. In scenarios with large or continuous action spaces, function approximation becomes essential for the actor.

It is noted that Generalized Policy Iteration (GPI) may produce policies that are inferior to prior ones, leading to oscillation or divergence in policy quality during incomplete evaluations. While initial policy improvements are common, later stages often result in fluctuating performance. To address this, practitioners typically store a sequence of policies and assess their performance post-learning, selecting the best empirically.

The effectiveness of actor-critic methods can be enhanced by refining function approximation techniques, with performance bounds established by Antos et al. in 2007. The paper outlines the structure of the upcoming sections, which will cover value estimation methods utilized by the critic and various policy improvement methods employed by the actor, including greedy methods and gradient ascent approaches.
