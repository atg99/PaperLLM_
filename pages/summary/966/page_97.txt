This collection of references spans various contributions to the fields of reinforcement learning, machine learning, and adaptive signal processing. 

Van Roy (2006) discusses performance loss bounds in approximate value iteration, particularly emphasizing state aggregation. Wahba (2003) provides insights into reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, which are foundational in various learning algorithms. Wang et al. (2008) introduce stable dual dynamic programming, presenting a novel approach in the reinforcement learning domain.

Watkins (1989) and Watkins and Dayan (1992) present foundational work on Q-learning, a key algorithm in reinforcement learning. Widrow and Stearns (1985) delve into adaptive signal processing, laying the groundwork for many machine learning applications. Williams (1987) explores gradient-estimating algorithms for reinforcement learning within neural networks.

The proceedings edited by Wrobel et al. (2010) compile significant research from the 27th International Conference on Machine Learning, showcasing advancements in the field. Xu et al. (2002; 2007) contribute to efficient reinforcement learning techniques using recursive least-squares methods and kernel-based least squares policy iteration, respectively.

Yu and Bertsekas (2007) focus on Q-learning algorithms tailored for optimal stopping problems, while Yu and Bertsekas (2008) present new error bounds relevant to projected linear equations. Yu et al. (2009) examine Markov decision processes with arbitrary reward structures, highlighting the flexibility in modeling decision-making scenarios. Lastly, Zhang and Dietterich (1995) investigate a reinforcement learning approach to job-shop scheduling, demonstrating practical applications of the theory. 

Overall, these works collectively advance the understanding and methodologies in reinforcement learning and related fields, contributing to both theoretical foundations and practical implementations.
