In the context of interactive learning, particularly within closed-loop systems, the necessity for exploration becomes paramount. This section explores the significance of exploration in both online and active learning, using bandits, specifically Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with a single state, as a foundational example. 

In this scenario, maximizing returns while learning is akin to classic bandit problems. A crucial insight is that a learner who consistently selects the action with the highest estimated payoff risks missing the optimal action, leading to potential losses. Therefore, it is essential to balance exploration (suboptimal actions) with exploitation (greedy actions).

Two primary strategies for achieving this balance are highlighted: the epsilon-greedy strategy, where a small probability allows for random action selection, and the Boltzmann exploration strategy, which considers the relative values of actions based on their sample means. The latter draws actions from a multinomial distribution influenced by a temperature parameter that dictates the level of greediness.

Both strategies can be adapted for more complex MDPs, contingent upon having action-value estimates. While epsilon-greedy can be tuned over time to remain competitive with more advanced algorithms, there is no universal method for optimizing its performance. The same limitation applies to Boltzmann exploration.

An alternative approach suggested is the optimism in the face of uncertainty (OFU) principle, which encourages the selection of actions that maximize potential gains despite uncertainty. This principle forms a foundation for further exploration of algorithms in online learning scenarios.
