This paper discusses the computational complexity of lightweight, incremental methods compared to least-squares methods in the context of processing observations. Lightweight methods exhibit a complexity of O(nd), allowing multiple passes on the sample, while least-squares methods are limited to a single pass. The concept of "experience replay," introduced by Lin in 1992, enhances the accuracy of temporal-difference (TD) algorithms by reusing observations. When the number of features (d) is large, lightweight methods can perform comparably or even better than least-squares methods under the same computation time, particularly when least-squares methods become infeasible due to high dimensionality.

The paper emphasizes the complexity of comparing these methods when considering factors like observation frequency, storage space, and access time. It narrows the focus to a scenario where new observations come at negligible cost, simplifying the comparison to the computation speed of the methods. Given a fixed computation time (T), the least-squares methods can process a sample size limited by nT/d², while lightweight methods can handle a larger sample size of n0 = nd.

The expected precision of parameters derived from these methods is analyzed, revealing that lightweight methods may yield better accuracy when certain conditions are met (specifically when the ratio C2/C1 is less than d^(-1/2)). Generally, it is anticipated that least-squares methods converge faster when d is small, whereas lightweight methods excel with larger d under a fixed computation budget. This analysis is relevant beyond reinforcement learning, applicable to any scenario comparing incremental lightweight procedures with least-squares methods.

Lastly, the paper mentions the introduction of an incremental version of least-squares TD, called iLSTD, by Geramifard et al. in 2007, which updates one dimension of the parameter vector at each time step, thereby enhancing efficiency, particularly for sparse feature scenarios.
