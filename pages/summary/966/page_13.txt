The paper discusses the concept of optimal behavior in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and introduces deterministic and stochastic stationary policies. A deterministic stationary policy is defined as a mapping from states to actions, while a stochastic stationary policy maps states to distributions over actions. Following a stationary policy results in a time-homogeneous Markov chain for the state process.

The relationship between stationary policies and Markov reward processes (MRPs) is established, where an MRP is characterized by a state space and a transition probability measure. The transition kernel of the MRP induced by a stationary policy is defined, and it is noted that an MRP is finite if its state space is finite.

The paper further defines value functions associated with stationary policies. For a fixed stationary policy, the value function provides the expected cumulative reward starting from a given state, taking into account the rewards received over time. This definition assumes that the initial state is chosen randomly from a distribution that assigns positive probability to all states, ensuring that the conditional expectation is well-defined.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of stationary policies in the context of MDPs and MRPs, providing a foundation for understanding how these concepts relate to value functions and optimal decision-making.
