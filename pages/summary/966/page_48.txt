The paper discusses the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) principle, specifically focusing on the UCB1 algorithm introduced by Auer et al. in 2002. The UCB1 algorithm assigns an upper confidence bound to each action based on its observed rewards and the number of times it has been selected. The formula for the UCB is defined as Ut(a) = rt(a) + Rs * sqrt(2 log t / nt(a)), where rt(a) is the sample mean of the observed rewards for action a, and nt(a) is the number of times action a has been selected.

The paper highlights that UCB values are higher for actions with less available information and that these values can increase even if the action is not tried. The authors present pseudocode for UCB1, which includes routines for action selection and updating statistics. Additionally, they mention an enhancement to the algorithm that incorporates variance estimation, proposed by Audibert et al. in 2009, which often outperforms UCB1.

The context of the discussion is the frequentist agnostic setting, where the only assumption is that rewards are independent and lie within the [0, 1] interval. The paper contrasts this with a Bayesian approach, where reward distributions have a known parametric form and parameters follow a prior distribution. In this setting, the goal is to maximize expected cumulative discounted rewards, represented as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a state that reflects the posterior distribution of the parameters.

Gittins' 1989 work is cited, demonstrating that the optimal policy in this Bayesian MDP can often be expressed in a simple index form, allowing for efficient calculation in specific scenarios, such as with Bernoulli distributions. However, the paper notes that while the Bayesian policy is optimal on average across various environments, it does not guarantee strong performance in individual settings. The simplicity of the Bayesian approach is highlighted as a key advantage.
