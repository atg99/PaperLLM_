The paper discusses the existence and uniqueness of fixed points in the context of a contraction mapping T. Initially, it establishes that if v is a fixed point of T, then taking limits on both sides leads to the conclusion that v equals Tv, affirming the existence of a fixed point. Furthermore, it explores the uniqueness of this fixed point by assuming there are two fixed points, v and v0. By using the properties of norms and the contraction condition, it demonstrates that the distance between the two fixed points must be zero, which means v equals v0.

In the application to Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), the paper defines the function V* as the supremum of the value functions across stationary policies. It emphasizes that while considering all policies might yield a higher value function, for the MDPs in question, the optimal value functions are equivalent. The space of bounded functions B(X) is introduced, and it is shown to be a complete normed vector space under the norm k·k1. The completeness is demonstrated through the property that Cauchy sequences in this space converge to a limit within the space, ensuring the stability and robustness of the function space used in the analysis.
