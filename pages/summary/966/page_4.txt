This paper discusses the challenges associated with solving Markovian Decision Processes (MDPs) and the role of Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms in addressing these challenges. Traditional dynamic programming methods, while effective for simple MDPs with limited states and actions, become impractical for larger problems. The paper identifies two key concepts that enable RL algorithms to make dynamic programming feasible for large-scale applications.

First, RL algorithms utilize sampling to efficiently represent the dynamics of control problems, which is beneficial for scenarios when the dynamics are unknown or when exact reasoning is computationally intensive. Second, powerful function approximation methods allow for the compact representation of value functions, which is crucial for managing high-dimensional state and action spaces. These two ideas work synergistically, as samples can be concentrated in specific areas of the state space, enhancing the effectiveness of function approximation techniques.

The paper aims to provide insight into the evolving field of RL, acknowledging previous works that have laid the foundation for current understanding, including surveys and books by notable authors such as Kaelbling, Bertsekas, Sutton, and Barto. It highlights the rapid development of the field and the necessity for updated resources, referencing online updates and newer publications that expand upon RL methods and their applications in dynamic programming and optimal control. The paper emphasizes that RL, often referred to as neuro-dynamic programming, frequently employs artificial neural networks in its implementation.
