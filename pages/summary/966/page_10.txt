The paper discusses an inventory management problem modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The inventory size at the end of day t is denoted as Xt, with a maximum size M. The manager decides the number of items to order, At, each evening, where possible actions range from 0 to M. The next day's inventory size, Xt+1, is determined by the current inventory, the ordered items, and the demand, Dt+1, which follows an independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) sequence of random variables.

The revenue generated on the following day incorporates costs related to ordering and holding inventory, represented in a specific formula. The MDP framework captures the dynamics of inventory control through the function f, which relates the current state, actions, and demand to the next state and revenue. 

The paper emphasizes that inventory control is one of many operations research problems that can be modeled using MDPs. Other applications include optimizing logistics, scheduling, production, and various engineering control systems. Additionally, MDPs are relevant in information theory and finance, including areas like portfolio management and option pricing. The necessity of the condition p > h ensures that there is a financial incentive to order new items, highlighting the critical balance between costs and revenues in inventory management.
