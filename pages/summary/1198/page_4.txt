Hierarchical clustering, on the other hand, builds a tree-like structure of the data based on the distances between samples. This method can be agglomerative, where clusters are progressively merged, or divisive, where the data is recursively split into smaller clusters. The result is a dendrogram that visually represents the nested grouping of data. This approach allows for a more flexible interpretation of the data's structure, as the user can choose the level of granularity at which to analyze the clusters.

Both k-means and hierarchical clustering highlight the fundamental goal of clustering: to uncover the inherent structure within data without prior labels. The advancements in computational techniques and statistical theory have greatly enhanced the efficiency and effectiveness of these clustering methods, making them invaluable tools in data analysis today.
