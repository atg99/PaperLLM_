In this section, D. S. Watson discusses the ethical challenges and opportunities presented by generative modeling techniques in data privacy. While there are significant concerns about accountability for bad actors in machine learning, focusing solely on these risks may overlook potential benefits. Generative models can synthesize realistic data while preserving privacy, potentially simplifying access to sensitive datasets for research, as shown in the case of a study requiring extensive measures to access tax records. 

However, the paper notes that synthetic data is not immune to privacy threats, such as membership inference attacks, raising concerns about the balance between privacy and utility. The challenges of hyperparameter optimality, data quality, and model opacity in unsupervised learning mirror those in supervised learning, highlighting the need for expanded ethical frameworks that include unsupervised tasks. 

Mayo’s concept of severity in hypothesis testing is introduced to emphasize the importance of rigorous testing for unsupervised models, which often lack established standards. Tools from classical statistical inference, such as sensitivity analysis and resampling methods, can enhance the evaluation of model robustness and generalization. 

The paper also discusses the evaluation of generative models, particularly the challenges associated with Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which do not inherently provide density estimates. It suggests that supervised learning techniques can be employed to score the performance of GANs, thereby contributing to a more comprehensive assessment of their utility and reliability.
