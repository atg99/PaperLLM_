The paper discusses the contrasting approaches of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and random forests in generative modeling, likening them to the artistic techniques of Seurat and Chuck Close. While GANs employ a top-down approach requiring abstraction and imagination, akin to dreaming or game play, random forests utilize a bottom-up method that emphasizes particular details, similar to an author developing individual narrative scenes. The text highlights that generative modeling is a creative endeavor, transcending traditional prediction and optimization tasks, and reflects human cognitive development through imaginative recombination of experiences. It acknowledges that while data synthesis may appear simpler than supervised learning due to the absence of a single correct output, the complexity of generative models arises from the multitude of potential errors they can produce. The visual analogies serve to illustrate these concepts, emphasizing their broader applicability beyond image data.
