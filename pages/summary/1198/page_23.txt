The paper discusses various philosophical and theoretical aspects related to unsupervised learning and its implications in artificial intelligence. It references a wide array of literature, including foundational works on clustering and causal learning, as well as contemporary discussions on explainable AI and the challenges posed by black-box models. Key themes include the exploration of clusterability, the significance of causal abstraction, and the evaluation of clustering quality through established axioms. The authors also delve into the philosophical implications of foundational models in AI, addressing both the opportunities they present and the inherent risks. The survey covers theoretical frameworks such as probabilistic circuits and decision forests, emphasizing the importance of understanding deep learning mechanisms and adversarial examples. Overall, the paper seeks to bridge theoretical insights with practical applications in the realm of machine learning and artificial intelligence, advocating for a more nuanced understanding of unsupervised learning methodologies.
