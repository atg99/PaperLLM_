The paper explores the philosophical underpinnings of unsupervised learning, particularly focusing on abstraction methods within machine learning (ML). It connects these techniques to philosophical concepts introduced by classical empiricists like Locke and Hume, as well as to contemporary discussions in information theory. Abstraction algorithms, defined as methods for creating simplified representations of data, are central to this exploration. These methods include latent variable transformations and data-driven discretization processes that lead to dimensionality reduction.

The paper emphasizes that effective abstraction can enhance the efficiency of data representation, as illustrated through examples of image data with varying noise levels. It discusses how high-level patterns can be utilized for compression, revealing the tradeoff between compression efficiency and accuracy—wherein some contexts prioritize patterns over details, while others demand precision. 

Additionally, it mentions coarsening as a straightforward approach to abstraction, where data is described in larger units. The discussion is contextualized within the broader challenges of balancing accuracy and complexity in supervised learning, referencing pertinent literature on the curve-fitting problem in the philosophy of statistics.
