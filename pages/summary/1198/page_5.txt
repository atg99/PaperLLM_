The paper discusses the distinctions between hierarchical clustering and k-means clustering, emphasizing their methodologies and applications. Hierarchical clustering constructs a symmetric n×n dissimilarity matrix, which captures the relationships between data samples, potentially using non-Euclidean distances. Unlike k-means, which requires a predefined number of clusters (k), hierarchical clustering does not specify k upfront and instead generates a full solution path for k ranging from 2 to n. This method reflects a nested structure among clusters, allowing for a more nuanced representation of the data, such as identifying subcategories within broader categories. The results of hierarchical clustering are often visualized in a dendrogram, which illustrates the hierarchical organization of clusters. The optimal tree height can be determined through visual inspection or scoring heuristics, enhancing the interpretability of the data structure. Overall, hierarchical clustering offers a flexible approach to understanding complex datasets.
