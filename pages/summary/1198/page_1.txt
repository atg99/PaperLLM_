In the paper "On the Philosophy of Unsupervised Learning," David S. Watson explores the philosophical implications of unsupervised learning algorithms, which are prevalent in various applications but have received limited philosophical attention compared to supervised and reinforcement learning. The author examines three key unsupervised learning problems: clustering, abstraction, and generative modeling. He argues that these methods pose significant epistemological and ontological questions, offering tools for identifying natural kinds and differentiating essence from contingency. 

Watson emphasizes the importance of recognizing unsupervised learning as a legitimate area of philosophical inquiry, potentially serving as the foundational branch of artificial intelligence. He warns, however, about the epistemic and ethical risks associated with an uncritical reliance on these methods. To address these challenges, he advocates for a pragmatic, error-statistical approach that leverages the benefits of unsupervised learning while acknowledging its limitations. This work aims to fill a gap in contemporary philosophical discourse regarding unsupervised learning and to provide a clearer understanding of its unique contributions to the field of machine learning.
