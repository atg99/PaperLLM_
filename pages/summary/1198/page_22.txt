In this paper, D. S. Watson addresses metaphysical questions related to natural kinds and the limitations of human imagination in the context of unsupervised learning. He argues that this approach can clarify debates by replacing ontological speculation with computable and testable methodological hypotheses. However, he warns against overestimating the objectivity of unsupervised learning results, as these algorithms depend on human-selected data and hyperparameters, which introduces biases. The internal workings of these algorithms are often opaque, raising epistemic and ethical concerns regarding their use.

Watson advocates for a pragmatic approach that recognizes the inherent limitations of unsupervised learning while maintaining high standards for statistical rigor and algorithmic transparency. He notes that these methods are often subjected to less scrutiny than supervised and reinforcement learning models, suggesting that a reevaluation is necessary.
