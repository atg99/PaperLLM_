The paper discusses the NAS-Bench-101 dataset, which serves as a benchmark for neural architecture search (NAS) algorithms. It simulates the stochasticity of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) training by randomly selecting architectures and tracking their validation accuracy during the search process. The search is constrained by a fixed training time, and after each rollout, the mean test accuracy of the best-found architecture is evaluated to compute immediate test regret. This regret is used to score the search runs, and multiple independent rollouts are necessary to assess the robustness of different search algorithms.

The analysis of the NAS-Bench-101 dataset reveals significant insights into the influence of neural network operations and cell topology on the performance of convolutional neural networks. The empirical cumulative distribution function (ECDF) indicates that most architectures achieve 100% training accuracy, with validation and test accuracies above 90% for the majority of models. The best architecture reaches a mean test accuracy of 94.32%, comparable to well-known architectures like ResNet and Inception, suggesting that the dataset's architectures are competitive.

A strong correlation (r=0.999) between validation and test accuracy indicates that robust optimizers do not overfit on validation error, and the noise in accuracy measurements decreases with longer training epochs. The relationship between the number of parameters, training time, and validation accuracy shows a positive correlation, but the best-performing models are not necessarily the most computationally intensive. This underscores the importance of architectural choices in achieving high accuracy with lower computational costs.

The paper further explores how various architectural decisions impact performance. For instance, substituting a 3x3 convolution with either a 1x1 convolution or 3x3 max-pooling generally results in reduced validation accuracy and training time. Despite max-pooling being parameter-free, it has a higher average training cost than 1x1 convolutions. This suggests that while certain operations are associated with better performance, other factors are also critical in determining the success of an architecture. Overall, NAS-Bench-101 provides valuable insights into the design and evaluation of neural network architectures.
