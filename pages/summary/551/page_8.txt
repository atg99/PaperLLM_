The paper introduces NAS-Bench-101, a new tabular benchmark designed for neural architecture search (NAS) that allows for efficient evaluation while maintaining the integrity of the original NAS optimization problem. The benchmark facilitates rapid comparisons of various algorithms without the need for extensive computational resources typically required in the field. 

Key findings include the correlation between approximate hyperparameter-optimized accuracy and exact accuracy across different architectures, with a Spearman rank correlation of 0.9155. This suggests that the approximate metrics used in NAS-Bench-101 are effective for ranking architectures. Additionally, the absolute accuracy of models evaluated through NAS-Bench-101 does not reach state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-10 due to limitations in search space, hyperparameter choices, and the absence of advanced training techniques.

The authors emphasize that NAS-Bench-101 aims to enhance accessibility and reproducibility in NAS research. By open-sourcing the dataset and accompanying code, they hope to establish a foundational benchmark that can be continuously refined as the NAS field evolves.
