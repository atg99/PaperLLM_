The paper discusses the performance comparison of various search algorithms within the context of NAS-Bench-101, a benchmark for neural architecture search (NAS). The analysis includes a comparison of algorithms such as HB*, RS, SMAC, TPE, RE, BOHB*, and RL, showcasing their mean performance over 500 independent runs against estimated wall-clock time. The robustness of these algorithms is also examined with respect to the random number generator seed.

To further understand the dynamics of these algorithms, a subset called NAS-Bench-Mini is introduced, which contains neural network architectures with six or fewer vertices, contrasting with the full NAS-Bench-101 that includes architectures with up to seven vertices. The study compares regularized evolution (RE) and non-regularized evolution (NRE) against a random search baseline, revealing consistent rankings across both datasets.

The paper also discusses the relationship between NAS and hyperparameter optimization (HPO). It highlights that NAS-Bench-101 was designed with a fixed set of hyperparameters to simplify the architecture search process. The authors justify this design choice by explaining that HPO and NAS are intertwined; NAS aims to find optimal architectures while HPO seeks to optimize training parameters for those architectures. They propose an approximation method for finding optimal architectures under fixed hyperparameters, which is validated through experiments on a different NAS-HPO-Bench dataset.

In summary, the findings suggest that results from NAS-Bench-Mini can generalize to the larger NAS-Bench-101 dataset, indicating that conclusions drawn about algorithm performance in smaller search spaces may apply to larger ones as well.
