The paper discusses the findings from NAS-Bench-101, focusing on the relationship between network architecture and performance metrics, particularly validation accuracy and training time. 

Figure 4 illustrates the impact of substituting one operation for another, revealing that using pooling operations with 1x1 convolutions is not always optimal. Figure 5 analyzes the effects of depth and width on validation accuracy and training time. It finds that a depth of 3 yields optimal average validation accuracy, while increasing width enhances accuracy up to a maximum of 5. However, training time generally increases with depth and width, except for width 1 networks, which are the most computationally expensive due to their feed-forward structure.

The concept of locality is introduced, showing that architectures that are similar tend to exhibit comparable performance. This property, defined by edit-distance, is quantified using random-walk autocorrelation (RWA), which indicates high correlations for smaller distances, suggesting that nearby architectures share performance characteristics. Figure 6 illustrates these correlations and highlights that locality exists around a global accuracy maximum, with a peak width of approximately 6.

In examining the rarity of proximity to global maxima, the best architecture has 4 distinct graph isomorphisms, leading to multiple peaks in the search space. Overall, about 35.4% of the search space is within a distance of 6 from these top graphs, indicating that locality-based search strategies could be effective in navigating the architecture space.
