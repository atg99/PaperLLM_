The paper discusses the NAS-Bench-101 framework, focusing on an alternative encoding for neural architecture search (NAS). It highlights challenges such as the presence of invalid models, which lack connection paths or exceed edge limits. After filtering, approximately 423,000 unique graphs were identified.

The translation from graphs to neural networks is detailed, emphasizing the need to combine tensors when multiple edges converge on the same vertex. A fixed rule was adopted where tensors directed to the output vertex are concatenated, while those to other vertices are summed. This approach accommodates both ResNet and Inception-like architectures.

Training is a crucial aspect of the architecture search benchmark. The authors established a unified training pipeline across all models to ensure comparability. A single set of hyperparameters, optimized through a grid search, was used to maintain robustness across various architectures. Models were trained on the CIFAR-10 dataset, employing standard data augmentation, with training conducted using RMSProp and a cosine decay learning rate schedule.

Performance metrics for each architecture were collected after training, including training, validation, and testing accuracies, training time, and the number of trainable parameters. It is noted that training and validation metrics should guide model searches, whereas testing accuracy is reserved for offline evaluation.

Finally, the paper outlines best practices for benchmarking NAS algorithms within the NAS-Bench-101 framework. It emphasizes the importance of querying the dataset effectively to identify architectures that achieve high testing accuracy, while addressing the limitations of using long training runs for intermediate performance evaluation due to potential noise in accuracy measurements.
