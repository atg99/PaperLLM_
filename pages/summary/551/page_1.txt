The paper presents NAS-Bench-101, a pioneering public architecture dataset designed to facilitate reproducible research in neural architecture search (NAS). The authors highlight the challenges posed by the high computational demands of NAS, which hinder accessibility for many researchers. To address these issues, they constructed a compact yet expressive search space, identifying 423,000 unique convolutional architectures and evaluating them on the CIFAR-10 dataset, resulting in over 5 million trained models.

The dataset allows researchers to quickly assess the performance of various models without the need for extensive computational resources. The authors emphasize that NAS-Bench-101 enables comprehensive analysis of NAS search spaces, as it exhaustively evaluates architectures, making it easier to compare different optimization algorithms. They benchmark a variety of architecture optimization methods, showcasing the dataset's utility in advancing NAS research.

The key contributions of the paper include the introduction of NAS-Bench-101 as a large-scale, open-source resource, a detailed evaluation of a wide range of architectures, and the promotion of reproducibility within the NAS community. The data and code for NAS-Bench-101 are made publicly available to support further research and development in this field.
