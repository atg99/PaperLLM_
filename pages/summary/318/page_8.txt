The section discusses the significance of sequence length in the BERT-Attack method, which utilizes a contextualized masked language model for generating adversarial samples. It highlights that longer premises are more effective for contextual replacement, as shorter sequences may lead to less reasonable perturbations. The authors propose combining word-level synonym replacement strategies with BERT-Attack to enhance its applicability.

The transferability of generated adversarial samples is evaluated, showing that samples aimed at different target models maintain effectiveness in the NLI task but are less transferable in text classification. Fine-tuning the target models with these samples improves robustness, as indicated by a closer accuracy to models trained on clean datasets, making subsequent attacks more challenging.

The effects of sub-word level attacks using the BPE method are examined, demonstrating that including sub-word level strategies results in higher attack success rates and lower perturbation percentages.

Furthermore, the paper addresses word importance ranking, which is crucial for identifying significant words in neural network models. The absence of this ranking leads to reduced effectiveness in the attacking algorithm.

Lastly, a runtime comparison reveals that BERT-Attack with sub-word tokenization is more efficient than other methods, underscoring its practicality in generating adversarial samples. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of sequence length, transferability, sub-word strategies, and word importance in enhancing the robustness of neural models against adversarial attacks.
