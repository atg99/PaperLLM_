The paper introduces BERT-Attack, a method for generating adversarial samples using the BERT masked language model. It highlights the effectiveness of BERT-Attack in producing semantically consistent adversarial examples while maintaining a high success rate and minimal perturbation. The method notably outperforms previous approaches in runtime efficiency, as it does not rely on language models or sentence encoders to evaluate output sequences, resulting in faster query times.

Examples provided in the paper illustrate that adversarial samples can lead to incorrect predictions by target models without misleading human judges. However, the authors acknowledge that some generated samples may be antonyms or semantically irrelevant, indicating a potential area for improvement in enhancing language models for better semantic relevance.

The study is supported by various funding sources and expresses gratitude to reviewers and colleagues for their contributions. Future work may focus on refining BERT-Attack to minimize semantic loss in generated adversarial examples.
