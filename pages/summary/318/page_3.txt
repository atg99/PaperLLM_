The paper introduces BERT-Attack, a method designed to exploit the original BERT model to create adversarial samples that can deceive a fine-tuned BERT model. The approach involves two main steps: identifying vulnerable words that significantly influence the target model's output and replacing these words with semantically similar and grammatically correct alternatives until a successful attack is achieved.

In the first step, the method identifies the most vulnerable words in the input sentence based on their importance scores, which are calculated by comparing the logit output of the target model with and without the word in question. The words are ranked, and only a small percentage of the most important ones are selected for perturbation to minimize changes.

The second step involves iteratively replacing the identified vulnerable words with new words generated by a masked language model. This method differs from previous strategies by ensuring that the replacements maintain fluency and semantic integrity, as BERT's masked language model is context-aware and generates more coherent alternatives compared to traditional synonym-based approaches. The efficiency of BERT-Attack is highlighted by its ability to generate perturbations with a single forward pass through the model, significantly reducing the need for extensive evaluations typically required by other methods.

Overall, BERT-Attack presents a novel and efficient strategy for creating adversarial examples, leveraging the strengths of BERT's architecture to enhance the effectiveness of adversarial attacks on fine-tuned models.
