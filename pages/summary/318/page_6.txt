The paper presents a novel attack method, BERT-Attack, designed to evaluate the robustness of various fine-tuned BERT models against adversarial examples. It demonstrates that the BERT-Attack method effectively deceives these models in both text classification and natural language inference tasks, achieving an average post-attack accuracy below 10%. This indicates a high success rate in perturbing the input samples while maintaining a low perturbation percentage, typically under 10%, which is a significant improvement over previous methods.

The results, summarized in Table 1, show that BERT-Attack outperforms existing state-of-the-art baselines such as TextFooler and Genetic Algorithms (GA) in terms of efficiency and imperceptibility. It successfully applies to diverse tasks, including news and review classification, highlighting its robustness across different domains. The paper notes that the review classification task is particularly vulnerable, as minor changes in keywords can mislead the model, exposing its reliance on a few words for decision-making rather than a holistic understanding.

Additionally, the authors conducted human evaluations to assess the fluency, grammatical correctness, and semantic preservation of the generated adversarial samples, ensuring that the attacks do not produce nonsensical outputs. Overall, the findings emphasize the effectiveness of BERT-Attack in revealing the vulnerabilities of BERT-based models and suggest areas for improving model robustness against such adversarial attacks.
