The paper introduces BERT-Attack, a novel method for generating adversarial samples against BERT and other deep learning models in natural language processing tasks. The authors highlight the challenges of crafting adversarial examples for discrete data such as text, which require maintaining semantic consistency and grammatical fluency. Existing methods often rely on heuristic replacement strategies that do not guarantee these qualities.

BERT-Attack leverages pre-trained masked language models, specifically BERT, to generate high-quality adversarial samples. By using BERT against its fine-tuned models, the method successfully misleads the target models into making incorrect predictions. The authors demonstrate that BERT-Attack outperforms state-of-the-art attack strategies in terms of both success rate and the percentage of perturbation, while also ensuring that the generated samples remain fluent and semantically coherent. Additionally, the computation cost is low, allowing for large-scale generation of adversarial examples.

The paper emphasizes the importance of exploring adversarial attack methods to enhance the reliability and robustness of neural networks, particularly in the context of natural language processing where adversarial attacks are less understood compared to computer vision. The authors provide their code for public access, facilitating further research in this area.
