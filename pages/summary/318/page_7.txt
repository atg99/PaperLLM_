This paper investigates the effectiveness of adversarial samples on model performance, specifically using the BERT-Attack method against various models. The study employs the IMDB and MNLI datasets, where human judges evaluate original and adversarial texts on a scale from 1 to 5 for semantic and grammatical quality. Results indicate that while adversarial samples maintain similar semantic and grammar scores compared to original samples, they result in lower accuracy, particularly in the MNLI task due to the structure of sentence pairs.

The paper further explores the applicability of BERT-Attack beyond BERT models, demonstrating its success against LSTM-based models, with the ESIM model showing greater robustness on MNLI due to its separate encoding of sentences.

Additionally, the importance of the candidate pool size in the BERT-Attack algorithm is examined. A larger candidate pool correlates with higher attack rates, although it may reduce semantic similarity. The paper proposes using a flexible threshold to eliminate less plausible candidates, which results in fewer queries without significantly affecting accuracy, as shown in Table 4.

Overall, the findings underscore the potential of adversarial attacks on various models and highlight the importance of optimizing candidate selection in enhancing the effectiveness of such attacks.
