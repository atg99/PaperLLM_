This paper presents a method for generating adversarial samples using a two-step BERT-Attack process. The approach begins by filtering out antonyms using synonym dictionaries, as the BERT masked language model does not differentiate between synonyms and antonyms. For each candidate word, a perturbed sequence is constructed, and if the model predicts incorrectly, the process concludes with the final adversarial sample. If not, the best perturbation is selected for the next word.

When dealing with words tokenized into sub-words by BERT, the method employs perplexity to identify suitable substitutes. This involves generating combinations of sub-words, ranking them by perplexity, and selecting the top candidates for perturbation. The process iteratively replaces original words with the most likely perturbations to derive the final adversarial sample efficiently, requiring only a single iteration of the masked language model.

The effectiveness of the method is evaluated across various NLP tasks, including text classification and natural language inference. Datasets utilized include Yelp Review, IMDB, AG's News, FAKE, SNLI, and MNLI. The evaluation metrics focus on the success rate (inverse of after-attack accuracy), the percentage of perturbation, and the number of queries made to the target model, emphasizing the balance between semantic preservation and attack success.

Overall, the paper outlines a systematic approach to creating adversarial samples that can effectively challenge NLP models while maintaining semantic integrity.
