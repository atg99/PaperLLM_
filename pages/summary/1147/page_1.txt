The paper "Interpreting CNNs via Decision Trees" by Quanshi Zhang et al. presents a novel method for explaining the predictions of pre-trained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) at a semantic level. The authors propose the use of decision trees to decompose high-level feature representations from CNNs into elementary concepts related to object parts. This approach not only identifies which object parts activate specific filters but also quantifies their contributions to the overall prediction score.

The motivation behind this research stems from the ongoing challenge of model interpretability in neural networks, despite their strong performance in various tasks. Existing methods often focus on pixel-level analysis, which may not provide deep insights into the decision-making process of CNNs. The proposed decision tree method organizes potential decision modes of the CNN in a hierarchical manner, allowing for explanations at different levels of granularity.

The study emphasizes two key perspectives: first, transforming chaotic filter features into semantically meaningful concepts, and second, quantitatively analyzing the contributions of various filters to the predictions made by the CNN. The effectiveness of this method is demonstrated through experimental results, highlighting its advantages over traditional interpretative approaches such as grad-CAM and CNN visualization techniques.

In summary, the paper provides a significant advancement in understanding CNN predictions by offering both semantic clarity and quantitative analysis, thereby enhancing interpretability in neural network applications.
