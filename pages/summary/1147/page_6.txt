The paper discusses a method for refining decision trees to enhance the interpretability of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The approach involves merging nodes in a tree structure to gradually transition from an initial tree to a final tree through a series of merging operations. The objective is to maximize the discrimination power while maintaining sparsity in the decision modes, which is crucial for generating explanations for CNN predictions.

The learning process is defined mathematically, with the likelihood of an instance being classified as positive calculated based on the tree's predictions. The merging of nodes is executed through a greedy strategy, selecting pairs that maximize the change in log-likelihood. This enables a more compact representation of decision-making processes within the CNN.

For inference, the decision tree provides a parse tree that offers a quantitative rationale for predictions at various levels of granularity. The contribution of different filters and object parts to the CNN's prediction is evaluated, allowing for a detailed understanding of how specific features influence outcomes.

The experimental section details the implementation of four disentangled CNNs based on established architectures, using benchmark datasets that include ground-truth annotations for part localization. The study emphasizes the importance of assigning filters to specific object parts to achieve meaningful interpretations of CNN predictions. Overall, the method aims to improve the transparency of CNNs by providing clearer, quantitative explanations for their decisions.
