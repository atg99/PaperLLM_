The paper discusses the analysis of object parts in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for improving prediction accuracy. It presents a method to evaluate the contribution of different object parts to CNN predictions by assigning specific filters to particular parts of an object. This is based on datasets such as ILSVRC 2013 DET Animal-Part and Pascal VOC Part, where filters are linked to the nearest major landmark parts of animals.

Figures illustrate the decision modes and object-part contributions visually. The contribution of each part is quantified and compared with ground-truth contributions to assess prediction accuracy. Two key metrics are employed: one measures the error in estimated contributions, while another evaluates the fitness of contribution distributions against ground-truth data.

Additionally, the paper examines the information loss incurred when using a decision tree to represent the CNN. It assesses the classification accuracy of the decision tree outputs compared to the original CNN predictions, providing a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method's effectiveness in analyzing CNN decision-making processes.
