The paper discusses the development of a method to enhance the interpretability of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) by focusing on the rationale behind their predictions. It differentiates between ordinary CNN feature maps and disentangled feature maps, which are designed to represent specific object parts without the need for additional part annotations. 

The authors propose a "gradient-based" decision tree approach to explain CNN predictions, contrasting it with existing "distillation-based" methods. This approach aims to clarify which object parts activate specific filters in the CNN and how these contributions affect the final prediction score. 

To achieve semantic-level explanations, the study emphasizes the importance of ensuring that middle-layer features are semantically meaningful and extracting their contributions to the prediction score. The method introduces a filter loss that encourages each filter in the top convolutional layer to exclusively activate based on a specific object part, thereby simplifying the feature processing into a more interpretable linear form.

The paper also quantitatively analyzes how fully-connected layers utilize features from the top convolutional layer to generate predictions, employing a piecewise linear representation to express the relationship between input features and classification scores. This quantitative rationale allows for a clearer understanding of the contributions of different features to the CNN's predictions, offering a pathway toward more interpretable deep learning models.
