The referenced works explore the theme of interpretability in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and deep learning. Zhang et al. (2018) discuss methods for making CNNs interpretable, emphasizing the importance of understanding the features learned by these models. They propose techniques that enhance the transparency of neural networks, allowing users to comprehend how decisions are made based on input data.

Additionally, Zhang et al. introduce unsupervised learning approaches that aim to explain the behavior of neural networks, which is crucial for trust and reliability in AI applications. The concept of network transplanting is also presented, allowing for the transfer of learned features between networks, thus facilitating interpretability.

Zhou et al. contribute to the discourse by highlighting the emergence of object detectors within deep scene CNNs, demonstrating how interpretability can be achieved through the analysis of network structures. They also propose a basis decomposition method for visual explanations, which aids in understanding the decision-making process of CNNs.

Overall, these studies collectively underscore the necessity for interpretability in deep learning models, offering various methodologies and insights that advance the field.
