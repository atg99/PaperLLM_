The paper discusses the development of a convolutional neural network (CNN) that utilizes disentangled representations to improve the interpretability of CNN predictions. The authors focus on creating a decision tree that semantically and quantitatively explains how different object parts contribute to the CNN's predictions. This approach addresses the challenge of linking middle-layer features of the CNN with semantic concepts and final predictions.

The research emphasizes the need for disentangled filters in the high convolutional layers, where each filter corresponds to a specific object part. This disentanglement is crucial for generating meaningful interpretations of CNN activations without relying on annotated data. The paper outlines two primary tasks: first, associating disentangled filters with explicit semantic meanings, and second, explaining how these features contribute to prediction scores.

The contribution of each object part to the prediction is quantified, allowing for a clearer understanding of the rationale behind CNN classifications. The decision tree constructed represents various decision modes, with nodes closer to the root denoting common rationales shared by multiple images and leaf nodes representing specific modes for individual images. This hierarchical structure aids in explaining the CNN's behavior in a systematic manner, thereby enhancing trust and understanding in critical applications, such as medical recommendations.
