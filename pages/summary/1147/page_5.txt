The paper discusses the learning of a decision tree for interpreting CNN classifications. It outlines how the decision tree is structured to represent decision modes that are common among positive training images, with each node encoding a distinct rationale shared by a subset of these images. The process involves defining and calculating vectors that approximate the activation strengths of filters, which correspond to different object parts. 

Key concepts include the representation of decision modes through learned weights and biases, and the hierarchical organization of these modes from coarse to fine, allowing for detailed interpretations of predictions. The algorithm for learning the decision tree starts by initializing decision modes for each positive image and progresses through iterative merging of nodes to refine the tree structure. The final tree captures the shared rationales among the classes, aiding in understanding the classification decisions made by the CNN. 

Overall, the work emphasizes the interpretability of CNNs by creating a structured framework that links filter activations to decision-making processes in image classification.
