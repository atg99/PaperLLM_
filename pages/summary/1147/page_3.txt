The paper discusses how Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) utilize parts or filters to make predictions and how a decision tree can be constructed to provide explanations for these predictions. The decision tree acts as a parse tree, with nodes representing different levels of abstraction that quantify the contributions of various object parts or filters to the overall prediction score.

The authors emphasize the importance of generic decision modes in higher-level nodes of the decision tree, as they focus on significant object parts while ignoring less relevant ones. This approach aids in creating compact rationales for CNN predictions.

The main contribution of the paper is the proposal of a method to learn a decision tree that explains CNN predictions using semantic visual concepts, achieved without strong supervision. This generic method has shown effectiveness across various benchmark CNNs.

In the related work section, the paper reviews existing literature on interpretability in CNNs, including methods for visualizing filters, diagnosing network representations, and extracting meaningful representations. It also highlights the emerging trend of using decision trees to distill knowledge from neural networks, noting that previous studies did not focus on providing human-interpretable semantic explanations for predictions.

Overall, the paper aims to enhance the interpretability of CNN predictions by linking them to semantic visual concepts through a structured decision tree framework.
