This paper presents a method to explain CNN predictions at a semantic level using decision trees. The approach involves coupling a CNN with a decision tree that encodes decision modes as quantitative rationales for each prediction. The study evaluates the effectiveness of this method across different benchmark datasets, showing that while fine-grained decision modes yield lower prediction errors, they do not necessarily improve classification accuracy. This is attributed to the method's focus on common decision modes for object categories, excluding random or negative images.

The paper includes several tables summarizing key metrics, such as the average number of nodes in decision trees for various layers, errors of object-part contributions, and classification accuracy alongside prediction errors. The findings suggest that decision trees can provide approximate explanations of CNN predictions, although they cannot accurately reconstruct the CNN's internal representations due to the lack of precise object-part annotations and the exclusion of insignificant filters for sparse representation.

The authors acknowledge the limitations of their method and highlight the significance of their approach in enhancing the interpretability of CNNs without requiring detailed annotations. The research is supported by various funding sources, indicating a collaborative effort to advance the understanding of CNN decision-making processes.
