The paper discusses the incremental evolution from DN-ResNet to DS-DN-ResNet, focusing on enhancing denoising performance through an edge-aware loss function. The authors argue that incorporating a constraint related to high-frequency information, particularly edges, is crucial for improving perceptual quality in denoising tasks. They explore two methods for constructing the edge-aware loss function: using the gradient magnitude from a Sobel filter and creating a binary edge mask through thresholding. Experimental results demonstrate that this approach significantly improves the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) and overall perceptual quality.

Additionally, the paper presents a table evaluating the Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of various DN-ResNet configurations across different noise levels using the PASCAL VOC 2010 dataset. The results indicate that different model architectures (with varying numbers of layers) and noise conditions yield varying denoising performances, with the conventional Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function applied to all models. The findings emphasize the importance of model design and loss function selection in achieving optimal denoising outcomes.
