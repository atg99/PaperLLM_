This paper introduces DN-ResNet, an efficient deep residual network designed for image denoising and restoration. The authors evaluate DN-ResNet's performance across various tasks, including Gaussian, Poisson, and Poisson-Gaussian denoising, demonstrating its effectiveness through visual examples. Additionally, the network is tested for compressed image restoration, where it shows notable improvements in the quality of decoded images, achieving gains of 1-2 dB for JPEG, 0.5-0.7 dB for JPEG 2000, and 0.3-0.4 dB for BPG. The authors provide images restored at a compression ratio of 0.15 bits per pixel, highlighting the perceptual quality enhancement achieved by DN-ResNet.

Moreover, DN-ResNet is adaptable for image super-resolution by treating low-resolution images as noisy inputs and high-resolution images as clean targets. The results indicate that DN-ResNet outperforms existing networks like MemNet and DRRN in terms of PSNR and SSIM while maintaining a smaller network size, suggesting its potential as a unified framework for various image enhancement tasks.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes the high accuracy and efficiency of DN-ResNet for image denoising, supported by effective cascade training and an edge-aware loss function. The introduction of the depthwise separable ResBlock further contributes to the network's performance.
