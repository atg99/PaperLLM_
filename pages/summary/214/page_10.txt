The experiments demonstrate that increasing the number of layers in DN-ResNet consistently improves the Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) across different noise scenarios, supporting the notion that deeper networks yield better performance. However, the one-shot training approach results in a slight degradation in PSNR compared to cascade training, highlighting the benefits of a more structured training process. 

The study compares various configurations of DN-ResNet and its variants, such as DS-DN-ResNet, noting the trade-offs in terms of parameters and computational complexity. The findings suggest that while deeper models have higher computational demands, they also provide superior denoising performance, particularly in challenging scenarios involving multiple noise types. 

Overall, the paper underscores the effectiveness of DN-ResNet for both blind and non-blind image denoising, with potential applications in improving image quality in various practical settings.
