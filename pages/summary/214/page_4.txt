DN-ResNet is designed for denoising compressed images, addressing the artifacts introduced during the compression process. The authors emphasize the challenge of integrating deep learning methods with traditional image compression algorithms like JPEG. They propose treating compressed image restoration as a denoising problem, where a trained deep convolutional neural network, DN-ResNet, refines the quality of decoded images.

The DN-ResNet architecture utilizes a simplified version of the ResBlock, omitting batch normalization and ReLU layers post-addition to maintain performance. This network is constructed by concatenating these ResBlocks. To mitigate difficulties in training deeper networks, the authors adopt a cascade training approach, where training is conducted in stages. Initially, a simple 3-layer CNN is trained, followed by gradual addition of ResBlocks based on performance improvements. This staged process allows for effective training as the network progresses to deeper architectures with more layers.

The ultimate goal is to learn a model that accurately predicts clean images from their noisy counterparts, enhancing the restoration quality of images compressed at low bit rates.
