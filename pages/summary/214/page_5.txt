The paper introduces DN-ResNet, an efficient deep residual network designed for image denoising. It utilizes ResBlocks, each consisting of 32 filters, which allows for a deeper network while maintaining a smaller overall size. New layers are added before the final 5x5 layer, inheriting weights from prior stages while initializing new ResBlocks with a Gaussian distribution. This approach limits the number of weights that need to be randomly initialized, facilitating easier convergence.

The authors advocate for a fixed learning rate of 0.0001 across all layers without decay, which has proven effective in their experiments. To address the reduction in feature map size caused by new convolutional layers, zero padding of 2 pixels is applied to each new 3x3 layer, ensuring uniform output size across all stages in the cascade training. The DN-ResNet architecture, specifically DN-ResNet-13, which incorporates five ResBlocks, achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across various noise types. The paper highlights the design and training methodology of DN-ResNet, emphasizing its efficiency and performance in image denoising tasks.
