The paper presents DN-ResNet, an efficient deep residual network designed for image denoising. Key findings are summarized in Table 2, which evaluates the performance of 13-layer DN-ResNets using various loss functions and denoising approaches, including blind and non-blind methods. The results indicate that the network trained with an edge-aware loss function, particularly using a binary edge mask (edge-b), achieves the best Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) across different noise levels, demonstrating its effectiveness in preserving image details while reducing noise.

The experiments utilized the PASCAL VOC 2010 dataset, with a training and testing split to evaluate the DN-ResNet. Various noise levels of Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN) and Poisson noise were generated, and the network was trained on a combination of noisy and clean image patches. Additionally, the study included testing on other datasets, such as Set14 and BSD, to compare the proposed method against existing denoising techniques.

For compressed image restoration, the authors leveraged datasets from the challenge on learned image compression (CLIC), employing common image compression algorithms to derive decoded images for training. The network was trained on the Y, Cb, and Cr channels, focusing on non-blind denoising by creating separate models for different noise levels. The quantitative evaluation emphasizes the PSNR and SSIM metrics for fair comparison with other methodologies. Overall, the DN-ResNet demonstrates robust performance in image denoising tasks, particularly when utilizing edge-aware training techniques.
