The paper discusses the numerical solution of nonlinear first-order non-homogeneous delay differential equations (DDEs) using discretization techniques. Specifically, it focuses on the application of the backward Euler method to equations derived from earlier formulations. 

The discretization process begins by defining the sampling time step as ΔT and indexing time samples as n. The backward Euler discretization is applied to transform the continuous equations into a discrete format, leading to a system of equations that can be solved iteratively. The key equations are derived, culminating in a nonlinear first-order difference equation.

The transformation allows for the time axis to become dimensionless, converting all time-dependent signals into sequences indexed by n. This results in a difference equation that expresses the relationship between the state signal at the next time step and the current state, as well as the influences of input signals and other factors.

To facilitate the computation, a matrix Ws is defined, which represents the influence of the state signal over time. The final equation relates the state signal at the current time step to its previous value, incorporating the effects of input signals and a constant term, thereby providing a structured approach for numerical integration of the DDEs.

This approach is significant as it allows for the systematic evaluation of the system's behavior over time while accommodating the complexities introduced by delays in the input signals.
