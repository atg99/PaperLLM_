This section focuses on deriving the equations necessary for training a Vanilla Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network using Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT). The process begins by computing two backward-moving gradient sequences, denoted as ⃗ χ[n] and ⃗ψ[n], which represent the total partial derivatives of the objective function E with respect to the observable value signal ⃗ v[n] and the state signal ⃗ s[n], respectively. This approach is based on modular design principles, allowing for a simplified computation of the backward pass by expressing intra-cell derivatives in terms of ⃗ χ[n], thus reducing the complexity of intermediate variables.

The gradients ⃗ χ[n] and ⃗ψ[n] propagate in reverse order, meaning their values depend on those at the subsequent index n+1. Once these gradients are established, they facilitate the calculation of total partial derivatives of E with respect to model parameters, achieved through intermediate gradient sequences. These sequences summarize contributions from each time step, requiring a summation across all steps to obtain the total derivatives.

During inference, only the input signal ⃗ x[n] and the value signal ⃗ v[n] are accessible, while other intermediate signals remain internal. The cell computes the value signal for all steps, and this can be transformed into an output signal ⃗ y[n] through linear transformations and functions like softmax. Input data can also be pre-processed to enhance model performance, such as converting one-hot vectors into compact representations or using non-causal filters to improve contextual awareness.

The section concludes with the derivation of derivatives for the warping functions, which are essential for the training equations. The definition of ⃗ χ[n] as the total partial derivative of E with respect to ⃗ v[n] aids in simplifying and modularizing the training equations for the Vanilla LSTM cell.
