The paper discusses the advantages of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks over standard Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) in the context of Gradient Descent training. It emphasizes that the gating mechanisms in LSTM enhance the numerical stability of gradient calculations, allowing for effective training over long sequences. Specifically, it highlights the importance of well-behaved intermediate and border gradient sequences to sustain information flow during training.

Equations presented demonstrate how the gradients, denoted as ψ[n] and χ[n], depend on their subsequent values, indicating a recursive relationship that influences the overall sensitivity of the LSTM system. The analysis involves applying the chain rule to explore how changes in one gradient affect others, focusing on derivatives with respect to various parameters. The results illustrate a complex interplay of gradients and their corresponding dependencies, which contribute to the robustness of LSTM networks in mitigating gradient decay. 

In summary, the paper provides a detailed mathematical foundation for understanding the mechanisms that make LSTMs more suitable for Gradient Descent, highlighting the role of gate functions in maintaining gradient stability across long sequences.
