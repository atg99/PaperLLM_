The paper presents Proposition 1, which establishes the conditions under which a standard Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) can effectively optimize its performance and generalization capabilities. It starts by assuming the existence of a parameter set Θ that allows the objective function L to be close to an optimum for a sequence of N samples. The ground truth output sequence can be divided into independent segment-level subsequences, which leads to the conclusion that a single RNN cell, unrolled for a variable number of steps, Km, is sufficient for both optimizing L and inferring outputs for unseen inputs.

The proof elaborates that the objective function measures the RNN's performance across training, validation, and testing phases, indicating that when L is near optimum, the RNN's output closely approximates the ground truth output. The segmentation of the output sequence mirrors the segmentation of the ground truth, allowing for the establishment of independent segment-level components of the objective function. As a consequence, the observable outputs of the RNN system must track the ground truth outputs at both the entire sequence level and the segmented level.

The paper further discusses the invertibility of certain functions, which ensures that the relationships between the RNN's outputs, readout signals, and state signals can be maintained across different segments. It concludes that the mutual independence of state signal subsequences across segments imposes specific restrictions on their initial conditions, indicating that the state of one segment cannot depend on the states or inputs of another segment. This independence is crucial for ensuring the effectiveness of the RNN across multiple segments of data.
