The paper discusses the characteristics of infinite impulse response (IIR) sequences and their comparison to finite impulse response (FIR) sequences. It begins by demonstrating the unending nature of IIR sequences governed by specific equations, using an impulse input to illustrate the response pattern over time. The sequence is shown to be defined for all positive indices, even with a single impulse.

The advantages of FIR systems are highlighted, primarily their intrinsic stability and feasibility for computation with finite resources. FIR systems require a limited number of steps and memory locations for processing and storing outputs, making them more manageable for design purposes.

Furthermore, the paper introduces the concept of partitioning a sequence of output values into non-overlapping segments for analysis. It defines a vector-valued rectangular window function to facilitate this segmentation, illustrating how to represent the segments using the unit step function. The window function is mathematically formalized, paving the way for further exploration of signal processing techniques in the context of FIR and IIR systems.
