The paper discusses the computation of fractional control signals in a Vanilla Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, specifically focusing on the signals ⃗ gcs[n], ⃗ gcu[n], and ⃗ gcr[n]. These signals are determined using data from previous and current steps, with ⃗ gcs[n] and ⃗ gcu[n] relying on signals ⃗ s[n−1], ⃗ v[n−1], and ⃗ x[n], while ⃗ gcr[n] incorporates ⃗ s[n], ⃗ v[n−1], and ⃗ x[n]. The use of linear combinations of these signals facilitates the application of Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) and creates a comprehensive representation for each gate.

The parameters of the model, including weights and biases, are trained, allowing the gate functions to adapt to the flow of training data over time. This adaptability enables the gates to manage the effects of artificial boundaries in input sequences, enhancing the robustness of the LSTM model and improving the quality of output sequences.

The paper concludes with an overview of the LSTM mechanism, detailing how the cell processes input signals and maintains internal state signals. It emphasizes the dual objectives of data handling and control within the LSTM architecture, where candidate data signals are modulated by control signals to determine the amount of data that propagates through the network. The LSTM cell features three distinct stages for managing candidate data and control: update, state, and readout.
