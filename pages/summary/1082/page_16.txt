The section discusses the training challenges associated with Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on the truncated unrolled RNN system. Proposition 1 indicates that the specified equations define this system, which is inherently stable when truncated. However, RNNs face significant training difficulties, notably "vanishing gradients" and "exploding gradients," especially when the target subsequence dependencies extend over many samples. This necessitates a wide unrolling window to effectively capture long-range dependencies.

Training of truncated unrolled RNNs typically employs Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT), an adaptation of the standard Back Propagation method suitable for sequential data. By unrolling the RNN, it transforms into a directed acyclic graph, allowing for the application of BPTT, which uses the chain rule to compute gradients for weight optimization. The objective function, E, is optimized using available data pairs, and BPTT provides a systematic way to derive the necessary gradients.

The text emphasizes the interdependencies of the state signals in RNNs, where the state at one time step influences the next. This is crucial for correctly calculating the gradients, as overlooking these dependencies can lead to incomplete gradient calculations. Thus, the training process must account for how the state signals evolve over time, ensuring that the overall objective function reflects contributions from all relevant time steps.
