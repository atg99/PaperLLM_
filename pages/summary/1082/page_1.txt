The paper titled "Fundamentals of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Network" by Alex Sherstinsky, published in the Elsevier journal "Physica D: Nonlinear Phenomena," aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of RNNs and LSTMs. It addresses gaps in existing literature, particularly the lack of detailed training formulas and the rationale behind the "unrolling" technique for RNNs, which are often presented without justification.

The tutorial begins by deriving the canonical RNN formulation from differential equations, thus grounding the concepts in formal theory. It then articulates a precise statement that clarifies the unrolling technique. The paper highlights the challenges associated with training standard RNNs and proposes the transition to a "Vanilla LSTM" model through logical reasoning, presenting all relevant equations and detailed descriptions of the LSTM components.

Sherstinsky's unconventional notation and presentation style aim to enhance understanding of the LSTM system, offering insights that could lead to further enrichments of the model. The intended audience includes Machine Learning practitioners who seek an alternative pedagogical approach and a deeper grasp of RNNs and LSTMs, particularly those interested in implementing an augmented LSTM model for research and experimentation.

The introduction contextualizes the significance of LSTMs since their inception in 1997, noting their impressive performance across various applications, including language modeling and machine translation. The paper underscores the importance of a thorough understanding of LSTM functionality for effective application in both academic and industrial settings, addressing the common challenges practitioners face when navigating the existing literature. Overall, the work serves as a valuable resource for those looking to gain a solid foundation in RNN and LSTM fundamentals.
