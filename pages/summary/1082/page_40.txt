In this paper, the authors explore the foundations of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks through a principled approach. They begin by demonstrating how the canonical formulation of RNNs can be derived from delay differential equations, which are prevalent in various scientific fields. The authors derive the standard RNN formulation by selecting appropriate parameters and considering stability.

The paper discusses the unrolling of RNNs in the context of approximating Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) systems with Finite Impulse Response (FIR) models, providing sufficient conditions for effective sequence learning. The training of RNNs is addressed using Back Propagation Through Time, highlighting issues such as vanishing and exploding gradients that complicate training with Gradient Descent.

To overcome the limitations of standard RNNs, the authors transition to LSTM networks, detailing the construction of the Vanilla LSTM and presenting a comprehensive overview of its components and processes, including both the forward and backward passes. A significant contribution of this work is the pedagogical approach that analyzes RNNs and LSTMs from a Signal Processing perspective, clarifying complex concepts through meaningful notation.

Additionally, the authors propose two novel extensions to the Vanilla LSTM: convolutional non-causal input context windows and an external input gate. They augment the LSTM equations to incorporate these features, along with a recurrent projection layer introduced by another research team. Future work recommendations include implementing the Augmented LSTM in high-performance computing environments and benchmarking its effectiveness across various applications, particularly in language representation tasks like question answering and customer support automation. The authors emphasize the importance of evaluating the performance impacts of the new extensions compared to existing models, such as the Vanilla LSTM and Transformer networks.
