The paper discusses the stability and gradient behavior of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), particularly in the context of long-range interactions. It highlights that the overall Jacobian matrix's magnitude, as described in Equation 91, is influenced by the product of individual Jacobian matrices. While RNNs are designed for stability, challenges arise with long sequences where both the unrolled window size and the distance between samples are large. If all eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix satisfy stability conditions, the Jacobian's magnitude diminishes as the distance between samples increases. However, if any eigenvalue violates stability, the Jacobian can grow exponentially, leading to either saturation of the readout signal or potential overflow.

The paper emphasizes that training standard RNNs using Gradient Descent can be problematic due to vanishing or exploding gradients, making it difficult to update system parameters reliably. To address these issues, the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architecture is introduced, designed to prevent vanishing gradients by incorporating nonlinear, data-dependent controls. The LSTM modifies the traditional RNN structure to enhance robustness and versatility, allowing for more effective training and parameter updates. This is achieved by separating the state update into contributions from the previous state and the readout signal, which mitigates the gradient issues encountered in standard RNNs. Overall, the LSTM architecture represents a significant advancement in overcoming the limitations of traditional RNNs.
