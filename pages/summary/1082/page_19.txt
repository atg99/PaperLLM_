The paper discusses a mechanism for controlling the contributions of state and update signals in a recurrent neural network (RNN) using gate signals. The state signal from the previous step is represented by ⃗Fs(⃗ s[n−1]), while the update information, combining the previous readout signal and the current input signal, is represented by ⃗Fu(⃗ r[n−1], ⃗ x[n]). The state signal at each step is a blend of these two sources, with the proportions adjustable through gate signals: ⃗ gcs[n] for controlling the retention of the state signal and ⃗ gcu[n] for regulating the update signal injected into the state signal.

The gate signals are non-negative fractions, allowing fine-grained control over the contributions at each step. This enables the model to retain varying amounts of information from previous states and adaptively incorporate new inputs. The paper also notes the concept of temporal adjacency in discrete systems, suggesting that the terms "previous" and "current" are used for convenience. It highlights that RNNs can be unrolled in both directions, with bi-directional processing improving performance in various applications.

Additionally, the paper mentions the potential for non-causal processing, where future input data can inform current decisions, particularly beneficial in analyzing contexts in audio, speech, and text data. The significance of element-wise control in LSTM networks has been recognized by others, emphasizing the importance of these gate mechanisms in managing the flow of information within the network.
