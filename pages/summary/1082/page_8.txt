The paper discusses the stability conditions for a recurrent neural network (RNN) as defined by a set of equations. It establishes that for the system to be stable, every eigenvalue of the matrix ˆW must lie within the complex unit circle. To simplify the analysis, the matrix A can be chosen as a diagonal matrix with large negative entries, which ensures stability. The paper derives that the stability of the RNN is primarily determined by the matrix ˆW, approximated as ˆW ≈ Wr ≈ -A^-1B, where B is also diagonal.

The key condition for stability is that the eigenvalues µi, derived from the product of the eigenvalues of B and the inverse of the diagonal entries of A, must satisfy 0 < µi < 1. If this condition is not met, the system may become unstable, leading to oscillations or saturation in the outputs. The paper also notes that setting certain parameters to zero can yield the standard RNN definition while preserving the stability analysis.

Furthermore, the RNN is compared to the backward Euler numerical integration method for ordinary delay differential equations (DDEs). The modeling approach suggests that the original phenomena being represented are fundamentally analog, and the RNN approximates these phenomena in a discrete domain. An example is provided where the RNN is used to reproduce a transcript of a spoken lecture from an audio source, highlighting the limitations of the model in capturing nuances such as intonation and emotion.

Finally, the paper indicates that the analysis will be expanded to justify the evolution of the RNN into more complex architectures, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, addressing the need for improved handling of stability and representation of temporal data.
