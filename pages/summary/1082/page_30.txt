The paper discusses the total partial derivatives of the objective function E in the context of a Vanilla LSTM cell, focusing on both intermediate and border variables. The key derivatives defined include ρ, γ, α, and ψ, where ψ is particularly significant as it represents the total partial derivative with respect to the state signal s[n]. This makes ψ the most crucial error gradient in the Vanilla LSTM network, influencing parameter updates.

The backward pass equations are derived using these derivatives, applying the chain rule to derive relationships across various intermediate variables and their contributions to the overall error gradient. Notably, equations are presented for calculating gradients related to the output, cell values, and state signals, which are essential for updating model parameters during training.

The paper also highlights the importance of managing "accumulation derivatives," which can become excessively large during training. To mitigate potential numerical issues, these derivatives are clipped to a range between -1 and 1. This ensures stability in the training process while maintaining the effectiveness of the control and data warping functions used in the LSTM architecture.
