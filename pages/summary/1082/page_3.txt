In this section, the derivation of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) is approached through the lens of differential equations. The state signal vector, denoted as ⃗s(t), is articulated as a function of time, governed by a nonlinear first-order non-homogeneous ordinary differential equation. The evolution of this state is described by the equation d⃗s(t)/dt = ⃗f(t) + ⃗ϕ, where ⃗f(t) is a time-dependent vector function and ⃗ϕ is a constant vector.

A specific formulation of ⃗f(t) is introduced as ⃗f(t) = ⃗h(⃗s(t), ⃗x(t)), linking the state vector to an input signal vector ⃗x(t). This results in a system that frequently appears across various scientific disciplines. The discussion extends to the representation of continuous signals in a discrete form, with an example involving a video signal on a 2D screen.

The section also explores a particular case of ⃗f(t), termed the "Additive Model," which sums three vector-valued functions of time, ⃗a(t), ⃗b(t), and ⃗c(t). This model is significant in brain dynamics and neural network research, as it captures the dynamics of neuronal activity and has been adapted to include various biological factors. The Additive Model has been applied to diverse fields, including vision, decision-making, and memory, and is noted for its relationship to the Hopfield model, which has implications in both biological and engineering contexts.

The section further elaborates on a saturating version of the Additive Model, detailing the definitions of its constituent terms, with ⃗r(t) as a warped version of the state signal vector. The activation function G(z) is highlighted as a critical component, often chosen for its nonlinear properties.
