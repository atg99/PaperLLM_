The paper aims to provide a comprehensive introduction to Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, addressing a gap in the existing literature. It emphasizes the importance of a unified reference that conveys the underlying theories of these systems, making it beneficial for students and practitioners in the Machine Learning (ML) community. The authors stress the need for clarity in notation and concepts to foster a deeper understanding of the "how" and "why" behind RNNs and LSTMs.

The discussion begins with RNNs, as they provide foundational insights into LSTMs. The authors derive canonical RNN equations from differential equations to highlight the relationship between RNNs and their continuous-time counterparts. This approach is justified by the historical significance of differential equations in modeling neural networks and their practical applications in ML tasks. The paper outlines the advantages of differential equations in representing real-life scenarios more naturally than difference equations.

The authors set specific goals for the paper, ensuring that it is intuitive, complete, general, illustrative, modular, and utilizes vector notation. They aim to present the Vanilla LSTM system comprehensively, including detailed explanations of inference and training equations, clear diagrams, and a structure that allows for easy integration into various architectures.

The document is organized into sections that progressively build upon each other, starting with the background of RNNs, followed by the unrolling of RNNs, training mechanisms, and the construction of the Vanilla LSTM cell. The paper concludes with a summary of the topics covered and suggestions for future research. Overall, the paper seeks to demystify RNNs and LSTMs, making the concepts accessible and understandable for researchers and practitioners in the field.
