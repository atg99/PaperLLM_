The Vanilla LSTM network processes the state signal of the cell at the current step as a weighted combination of the previous cell state and both historical and novel update information. Similar to standard RNNs, the Vanilla LSTM can learn short-range dependencies within the subsequences during training. If the model parameters lead to the condition where the norm of the gate signal for cell state is less than one, the error gradient will diminish over time, potentially vanishing, despite the update candidate and reset gate signals being active. 

To define the gate signals, it is essential that all functions, including those for the gates, are differentiable for training via Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). The logistic function, which is continuous, differentiable, and monotonically increasing, is utilized for this purpose. It maps any real number into the range of zero to one and can be expressed in various forms, including a re-parameterized version of the hyperbolic tangent. When applied to vector inputs, the function operates on each element individually, ensuring that the computations for the gate signals are consistent and efficient.
