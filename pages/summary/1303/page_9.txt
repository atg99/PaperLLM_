The paper discusses the challenges and opportunities of data augmentation (DA) in natural language processing (NLP), particularly in low-resource languages and specialized domains. It highlights that techniques effective in high-resource scenarios may not translate well to low-resource languages, especially those with unique linguistic characteristics. The authors suggest exploring more vision-inspired DA techniques from computer vision (CV), such as grayscale transformations and dependency tree morphing, which could provide novel approaches to text manipulation.

The paper also addresses the role of self-supervised learning in DA, noting that recent models like BART and ELECTRA have successfully integrated DA into their pretraining processes. However, a key challenge in NLP is the difficulty of implementing online DA due to reliance on external resources, which contrasts with the stochastic methods commonly used in CV. The authors call for the development of lightweight modules for online DA in NLP and emphasize the need for a unified framework to facilitate research.

Furthermore, the paper points out the lack of standardized benchmarks and datasets for comparing DA methods in NLP, which hampers reproducibility and accessibility. To enhance the field, the authors recommend establishing common practices, including sharing code and datasets, transparent reporting of results, and thorough hyperparameter analysis.

In conclusion, the paper serves as a structured survey of DA in NLP, outlining current methodologies, challenges, and future research directions. The authors aim to guide researchers in selecting DA techniques and to inspire further exploration in this area. A GitHub repository is provided for additional resources.
