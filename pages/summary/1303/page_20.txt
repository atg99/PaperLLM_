The provided text summarizes various works related to data augmentation (DA) methods for grammatical error correction (GEC) and neural machine translation (NMT). 

In the realm of GEC, Lichtarge et al. (2019) generated synthetic noisy examples from Wikipedia sentences through backtranslation across multiple languages. White and Rozovskaya (2020) conducted a comparative study of DA approaches for GEC systems, while Foster and Andersen (2009) introduced GenERRate, an error generation tool that creates ungrammatical text based on an error analysis file. Kimn (2020) applied syntactic rules to create augmented sentence pairs addressing common Japanese grammatical errors. Felice (2016) surveyed existing error generation methods and explored new probabilistic approaches. Xu et al. (2019) examined five types of noise—concatenation, misspelling, substitution, deletion, and transposition—demonstrating effective performance in the BEA 2019 Shared Task. Zhang et al. (2019b) investigated backtranslation and feature discrimination, while Mizumoto et al. (2011) extracted training data from a language learning platform's revision logs for Japanese GEC.

For NMT, Vaibhav et al. (2019) presented a model that adds social media noise to text through synthetic noise induction. Hassan et al. (2017) proposed a DA method that projects words from high-resource to low-resource languages using word embeddings. Cheng et al. (2020) introduced AdvAug, an adversarial augmentation method for NMT. Graça et al. (2019) focused on improving sampling-based approaches and synthetic data generation via backtranslation. Bulte and Tezcan (2019) leveraged Translation Memory information, while Moussallem et al. (2019) augmented an NMT model with knowledge graphs for improved semantic feature extraction. Peng et al. (2020) developed a dictionary-based DA method for cross-domain NMT, and Li et al. (2020a) improved NMT robustness on ASR transcripts through sentence boundary segmentation. Nishimura et al. (2018) introduced methods for multi-source NMT, and Sugiyama and Yoshinaga (2019) examined backtranslation for context-aware NMT. Li and Specia (2019) explored noise incorporation from external data, while Chinea-Ríos et al. (2017) created synthetic data using sentence embeddings. Alves et al. (2020) proposed pipeline-based speech translation methods involving error introduction, and Kang (2019) focused on low-resource settings with noisy dictionaries. Chen et al. (2020a) worked on lexically constraint-aware NMT, and Li et al. (2020b) generated diverse synthetic parallel data for low-resource NMT. Finally, Duan et al. (2020) proposed syntax-aware DA methods utilizing dependency parsing for word selection.

Overall, these works contribute to enhancing GEC and NMT systems through innovative DA strategies, focusing on noise induction, error generation, and leveraging linguistic data.
