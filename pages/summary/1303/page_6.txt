This paper discusses various data augmentation (DA) techniques and their applications across different natural language processing (NLP) tasks. It begins with a focus on methods for generating augmented examples for novel classes, referencing Schwartz et al. (2018) and Kumar et al. (2019b), who explore the use of autoencoders and few-shot learning in task-oriented dialogue. It highlights the significance of adversarial examples (A VEs) generated through innocuous transformations, as demonstrated by Jia et al. (2019) and others, to enhance model robustness.

The paper then delves into specific NLP tasks, emphasizing non-classification tasks. In summarization, Fabbri et al. (2020) explore backtranslation as a DA method for few-shot abstractive summarization, while other studies propose various iterative and dataset-specific approaches. For question answering (QA), multiple DA techniques including paraphrasing and distant supervision are discussed, with contributions from Longpre et al. (2019) and Yang et al. (2019) that improve QA model performance.

Sequence tagging tasks are also examined, with Ding et al. (2020) introducing a two-step DA process and other researchers modifying existing techniques for named entity recognition (NER). Lastly, the paper touches on parsing tasks, specifically through methods like DATA RECOMBINATION, which integrates task-specific priors into semantic parsing.

Overall, the paper underscores the versatility and effectiveness of DA methods in enhancing various NLP models across multiple tasks, demonstrating their importance in advancing the field.
