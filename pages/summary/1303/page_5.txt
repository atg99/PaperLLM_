The paper presents a comprehensive comparison of data augmentation (DA) methods, categorizing them based on various aspects such as their requirements for external knowledge, pretrained models, preprocessing needs, modification levels, and task-agnostic applicability. The table summarizes different DA techniques, indicating whether they utilize tokenization, constituency parsing, or dependency parsing, and their effectiveness across tasks.

In addressing bias, the authors highlight strategies like gender bias mitigation through augmented datasets and causal interventions. Specific methods include Zhao et al.'s gender swapping and Lu et al.'s Counterfactual DA, which aims to sever associations between gendered terms.

The paper also discusses solutions for class imbalance, noting the effectiveness of techniques like SMOTE, which generates synthetic examples for minority classes, and its adaptation for multi-label classification through MLSMOTE. Additionally, it explores the role of DA in enhancing few-shot learning by generating more examples for new classes.

Overall, the paper underscores the significance of data augmentation in improving model performance and addressing challenges such as bias and class imbalance.
