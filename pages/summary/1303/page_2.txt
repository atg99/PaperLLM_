The paper discusses data augmentation (DA) in the context of natural language processing (NLP), highlighting its significance and various methodologies. It begins by contrasting the Google Trends scores for "data augmentation" with a control term, "minibatch," indicating a growing interest in DA across multiple NLP tasks such as summarization, question answering, and dialogue generation.

DA is defined as a technique to enhance training data diversity without extra data collection, typically through modified or synthetic data. The primary goal is to act as a regularizer, reducing overfitting in machine learning models. While DA is well-established in computer vision, its application in NLP presents unique challenges due to the discrete nature of text data.

The paper outlines the trade-offs involved in DA techniques. Simpler rule-based methods are often easier to implement but yield limited performance improvements. In contrast, model-based approaches enhance data variation and performance but are more complex and resource-intensive. Effective DA must balance similarity to the original data to avoid overfitting and divergence that could hinder performance.

The paper also critiques the ad hoc nature of current DA practices, emphasizing a lack of theoretical understanding of why DA is effective. While some studies suggest that DA serves as a regularization technique, more research is needed to clarify its underlying principles. The authors reference foundational work that connects DA to regularization and variance reduction, underscoring the need for a deeper exploration of DA methodologies in future research.

Finally, the paper reviews various representative DA techniques applicable across NLP tasks, setting the stage for further discussion in subsequent sections.
