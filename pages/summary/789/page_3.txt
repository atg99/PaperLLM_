The paper presents a study that aims to predict overall survival (OS) for patients with high-grade gliomas using advanced neuroimaging techniques. The authors utilize multiple diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) metrics and resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) data to create multi-channel images, including frequency information and functional connectivity anisotropy, referred to as fTensor-fMRI. The study builds on a preliminary conference presentation and expands the methodology to incorporate additional modalities, enhancing feature learning through multi-modal data.

The research involves a training dataset of 68 patients and a validation dataset of 25 patients, all screened for high-grade gliomas using specific inclusion and exclusion criteria. OS is defined as the time from surgery to death, with a threshold of 650 days used to categorize patients into short and long OS groups. The imaging process was conducted under ethical guidelines, and informed consent was obtained from all participants.

The authors explore various aspects of their framework, including the impact of different convolutional kernels and the comparison between supervised and unsupervised feature extraction. The proposed method employs a 3D convolutional neural network (CNN) for feature learning, followed by a support vector machine (SVM) for classification of OS outcomes. The results indicate the efficacy of their multi-modal approach in predicting survival in high-grade glioma patients.
