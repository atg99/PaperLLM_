The paper discusses a multi-channel convolutional neural network (mCNN) architecture designed for predicting patient survival based on brain tumor imaging data. The network processes 32x32x32 patches from tumor regions, utilizing four convolutional layer groups and three fully-connected layers to classify patients into long or short survival groups. 

To enhance feature extraction, the mCNN computes multiple metric maps from diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) and resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI), treating each metric map as a separate input channel. The architecture integrates outputs from these channels using a fusion layer, which distinguishes it from traditional single-channel CNNs that struggle to effectively model multi-channel data due to varying statistical properties across channels.

The training process involves CNNs for T1 MRI and mCNNs for DTI and fMRI, generating high-level features that correlate with patient survival. Additional demographic and tumor-related features, such as age, gender, tumor location, and size, are also included in the analysis. The study finds no significant differences in tumor volume or performance status between the survival groups, suggesting these factors may not significantly impact overall survival predictions. The mCNN's capability to fuse multi-channel data is highlighted as a key advantage in generating effective survival predictions.
