The paper discusses a proposed method for predicting overall survival (OS) in tumor patients using presurgical neuroimaging data. The authors emphasize that while their model is robust, it lacks transparency regarding which imaging features contribute to improved accuracy. They validated their method through experiments on datasets of 68 and 25 patients, achieving promising results. The authors note similar successful studies in deep learning, suggesting their method could aid in developing new OS prediction models.

However, they acknowledge limitations, including the use of limited clinical data and the absence of genetic indicators, which are increasingly important for OS prediction according to the revised WHO grading system. Future work aims to incorporate genetic information and treatment-related features, addressing the need for a more comprehensive analysis of OS prediction. The authors also recognize the small sample size and the simplistic approach to feature concatenation, proposing enhancements such as multi-instance learning and separate models for different WHO grades to improve predictions. They plan to expand their research by including a broader range of features to refine their predictive capabilities.
