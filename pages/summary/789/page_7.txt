The paper presents a novel deep learning-based method for predicting overall survival (OS) in patients using multimodal imaging data obtained presurgically. The authors highlight the advantages of deep learning, including automatic and high-level feature learning, and its capacity to handle both single-channel and multi-channel imaging data. They emphasize that their approach aims to assess the feasibility of deep learning in OS prediction rather than critique traditional models.

The study avoids including conventional features like the Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) score, resection percentage, and genomic data, citing reasons such as the subjective nature of KPS, the irrelevance of treatment-related factors given the study's focus, and the unavailability of genomic data for most subjects. The authors argue that their primary goal is to predict OS based on presurgical imaging data, allowing for a streamlined comparison of their proposed method.

The framework effectively utilizes a small dataset by extracting numerous patches from each subject for feature learning, enabling sufficient training samples for neural networks. After training, a Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifies patients based on the learned deep features, which outperform those derived from traditional methods due to their higher accuracy.

The study also underscores the importance of fusing multimodal imaging data, demonstrating that this approach enhances OS prediction. The results indicate that the combination of features from different modalities significantly improves classification performance compared to single-modality analysis. The quantitative results support the effectiveness of the proposed framework, leading to improved predictive accuracy for OS among the studied patients.
