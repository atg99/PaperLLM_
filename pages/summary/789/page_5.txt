The study explores the use of fTensor-fMRI in predicting overall survival (OS) in glioma patients by integrating various imaging modalities, including T1 MRI, diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), and frequency-fMRI (freq-fMRI). The authors compute six additional metric maps from fTensor-fMRI, resulting in a total of eleven metric maps focused on both white and grey matter. These maps are normalized and resized to facilitate analysis through a 3D convolutional neural network (CNN).

The methodology employs a supervised feature extraction approach using the CNN architecture implemented in Caffe. Features from the multi-modal brain images are classified based on patient survival. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Sparse Representation (SR) are utilized for feature reduction and selection, respectively. The study reports that integrating features learned from CNNs with limited demographic and tumor-related features yields a classification accuracy of 90.46%, significantly outperforming the 63.23% accuracy achieved with demographic features alone.

Cross-validation experiments, including 10-fold and 3-fold methods on 68 patient subjects, demonstrate improved sensitivity and specificity of the proposed model compared to using demographic features alone. The Kaplan-Meier plot indicates a clear separation of survival curves between groups with long and short OS, confirming the effectiveness of the method in distinguishing patient outcomes. Overall, the findings suggest that the combination of deep learning with multi-modal imaging can enhance survival prediction in glioma patients.
