The study aims to develop a novel method for preoperatively predicting overall survival (OS) outcomes in patients with high-grade gliomas, based on presurgical imaging data. All patients underwent maximal safe tumor resection performed by an experienced neurosurgeon, followed by concurrent high conformal radiation therapy and chemotherapy with Temozolomide, adhering to established clinical guidelines.

Data preprocessing involved spatial co-registration of T1-weighted MRI, diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), and resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) to ensure accurate analysis. Specific preprocessing steps included extracting tumor areas from T1 MRI, processing DTI data using PANDA to calculate diffusivity metrics, and applying DPARSF for rs-fMRI data, which focused on grey matter and functional features in white matter through the functional connectivity tensor.

The analysis revealed no significant differences in preoperative tumor volumes, extent of resection, or postoperative treatment between the OS groups. The study emphasizes that while treatment is crucial, predicting OS from presurgical data can enhance preoperative planning. Key patient demographics included a mean age of 51.4 years, with a predominance of males and various tumor types, notably glioblastomas. The findings provide a foundation for further research into integrating imaging data for survival predictions in glioma patients.
