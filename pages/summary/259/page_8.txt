The paper compares the performance of Capsule Networks (CapsNet) and AlexNet across four datasets—MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10—over 50 epochs without deformation. The results indicate that CapsNet outperforms AlexNet in classification accuracy on all datasets under normal conditions. For instance, CapsNet achieved 99.50% accuracy on MNIST compared to AlexNet's 98.47%. However, when tested on deformed data, CapsNet exhibited a more significant drop in accuracy than AlexNet, raising questions about its ability to generalize spatial relationships. The accuracy drop percentages highlight this discrepancy: for MNIST, CapsNet dropped 56.75% while AlexNet dropped 43.26%.

The authors suggest that AlexNet's performance may indicate overfitting, particularly on simpler datasets like MNIST, while CapsNet might be learning more generalizable representations. However, they caution that the results may not universally apply to all CNNs, as AlexNet might not be a representative model.

Additionally, the paper notes that Capsule Networks need to enhance their performance on more complex datasets to match the efficacy of CNNs. Initial tests on hyperparameter optimization revealed that variations in standard parameters (batch size, learning rate, etc.) did not significantly impact the final performance of CapsNet, as it was already converging effectively. This points to the need for further exploration in refining CapsNet's architecture and hyperparameters to improve its generalization capabilities.
