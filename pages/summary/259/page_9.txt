The paper examines the impact of the number of routing iterations in Capsule Networks (CapsNets), specifically focusing on its implications for performance and runtime. The authors investigate routing iterations set to 1 through 5 across four datasets, noting that while the original recommendation of 3 iterations yields good accuracy, 2 iterations often perform comparably or even better. This observation raises questions about the effectiveness of the dynamic routing algorithm, suggesting it may be overly abrupt in decision-making regarding the flow of information between PrimaryCapsules and DigitCapsules.

Additionally, the paper explores the reconstructions generated by CapsNets to gain insights into their performance. By visualizing reconstructions after various training epochs, the authors find that while the MNIST dataset shows significant improvement in reconstruction, the classification accuracy plateaus sooner, indicating a disconnect between reconstruction quality and classification performance. Fashion-MNIST also exhibits improvement, though it struggles with fine details. The SVHN dataset shows limited reconstruction capability, primarily failing to capture color, while CIFAR10 reconstructions remain largely unrecognizable, except for certain categories like horses.

The authors suggest that analyzing the reconstructions can help in understanding errors, particularly for datasets like MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, where the quality of reconstruction can be used to decode embeddings and identify sources of error.
