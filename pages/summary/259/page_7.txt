The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of CapsNets and AlexNet models, highlighting several key aspects of their training and performance across different datasets. 

First, basic support for training an AlexNet model was introduced, aligning its conditions with those of CapsNets. The visualization of error and accuracy logging was transitioned from Visdom to TensorBoard, which enhanced the ability to visualize internal model embeddings through its dimensionality reduction suite.

To improve training efficiency, the authors utilized CUDA for GPU acceleration, running multiple trials with various hyperparameters across different datasets. Each author had access to multiple GPUs, with a total of at least 30 CapsNet runs conducted, each taking approximately four hours, resulting in a minimum of 120 GPU-hours used.

In terms of performance, both CapsNets and AlexNet were tested on four datasets. CapsNets achieved notable results: 99.5% accuracy on MNIST, 89.8% on Fashion-MNIST, and 91.06% on SVHN, while struggling with CIFAR10 at 68.53%. CapsNets outperformed AlexNet across all datasets, with the margin being more pronounced on the more complex datasets. However, neither model achieved state-of-the-art results, and it should be noted that the hyperparameters used were not optimized for all datasets.

A significant aspect of the study was testing the robustness of CapsNets against affine deformations, which they claim can recognize entities regardless of configuration. Interestingly, AlexNet outperformed CapsNet on deformed MNIST data, despite having lower accuracy on the standard test data. However, CapsNets showed better performance on deformed versions of Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN, suggesting their potential in handling more complex samples with greater intraclass variation.

Overall, the findings indicate that CapsNets have promising capabilities in certain contexts, particularly against more complex datasets, despite their limitations in specific scenarios.
