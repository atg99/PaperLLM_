The paper discusses the visualizations of misclassifications made by a neural network, particularly focusing on the reconstruction process of images based on a masked embedding vector. The decoder uses a 160-length embedding vector, where only the section corresponding to the most confident class is unmasked for image reconstruction. When the network is confident, reconstructions of incorrect classes show minimal components, but for ambiguous cases, such as digits in MNIST or clothing categories in Fashion-MNIST, the reconstructions reveal meaningful errors that align with human perception.

Furthermore, the paper explores perturbations in the embedding space to understand the learned representations for each class. By altering the non-zero components of the embedding vector, various characteristics such as stroke thickness, height, and specific features related to digits and clothing items are observed across different datasets like MNIST and Fashion-MNIST. The results indicate that while some properties are general, others are class-specific, highlighting the complexity of the learned embeddings.

Additionally, PCA visualization of dynamic routing is employed to track the evolution of digit output embeddings across multiple iterations. The visualizations show a significant shift in the distribution of embedding vectors as the dynamic routing process progresses, indicating the network's adaptability and the effectiveness of the routing mechanism in refining the classifications. This analysis underscores the importance of both the architecture and the learning process in achieving accurate image reconstructions and classifications.
