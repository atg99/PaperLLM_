This paper investigates the performance of capsule networks (CapsNets) on more challenging datasets compared to the well-established MNIST dataset. The authors aim to assess whether CapsNets can effectively handle various complexities such as noise, color variations, affine transformations, and intra-class variations that are present in more difficult datasets.

The study focuses on two main objectives. First, it evaluates the performance of CapsNets on datasets that are incrementally more complex than MNIST, including Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10. Each of these datasets presents unique challenges, such as the presence of color and multiple digits in SVHN, and diverse real-world objects in CIFAR-10. This exploration aims to provide insights into the limitations of CapsNets when faced with different data types.

Second, the authors seek to understand the sources of error in the model by visualizing the representations that CapsNets capture from the data. The paper contributes to the existing literature by extending CapsNet applications to new datasets and offering visual insights into their performance.

The datasets used include:
1. **MNIST**: A standard set of 28x28 black and white images of handwritten digits.
2. **Fashion-MNIST**: Similar to MNIST but consists of images of clothing items.
3. **SVHN**: Contains cropped color images of house number digits from Google Street View, with more complexity than MNIST.
4. **CIFAR-10**: A dataset of 32x32 color images of real-world objects across ten classes.

Additionally, the authors aim to test CapsNets' robustness by introducing synthetic deformations such as rotation, shear, translation, and scaling to the original datasets. This approach will help determine the extent to which CapsNets are invariant to deformations, thereby evaluating their classification capabilities under varied conditions.
