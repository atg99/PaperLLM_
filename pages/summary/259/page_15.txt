The paper investigates the performance and expressiveness of Capsule Networks (CapsNets) across various datasets, highlighting their strengths and limitations. The findings reveal that while CapsNets outperform AlexNet on slightly more complex datasets than MNIST, they struggle to handle deformations effectively. Notably, increasing routing iterations did not enhance performance, suggesting that CapsNets may not fully utilize routing to capture spatial relationships.

CapsNets successfully reconstruct samples from MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, indicating that their embeddings reflect meaningful properties. However, they fail to achieve similar results with SVHN and CIFAR10, where embeddings primarily represent intensity and color. This inconsistency in embedding quality correlates with the disorder of the visualizations, which further reinforces that only MNIST exhibits a cleanly separable label space. Consequently, the current CapsNet design appears inadequate for broader classification tasks.

The authors propose three avenues for future improvement: enhancing the routing algorithm for better data structure learning, deepening the network architecture to better capture complex features, and exploring CapsNets in more intricate tasks, such as segmentation, where the relationships between embeddings could indicate object proximity.
