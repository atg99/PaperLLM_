The paper discusses the challenges of training generative adversarial networks (GANs) with limited data, which often leads to discriminator overfitting and training divergence. To address this issue, the authors propose an adaptive discriminator augmentation mechanism that stabilizes training without altering loss functions or network architectures. This approach can be applied both when training GANs from scratch and when fine-tuning existing models on different datasets.

The authors demonstrate that their method allows for effective training using only a few thousand images, achieving results comparable to those of StyleGAN2 with significantly fewer training images. This advancement could expand the application of GANs in various domains, including medicine, where acquiring large datasets is challenging.

The paper also critiques the commonly used CIFAR-10 dataset, highlighting that it represents a limited data scenario. The authors improve the record Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score for CIFAR-10 from 5.59 to 2.42, underscoring the effectiveness of their proposed method.

Overall, the paper contributes to the field by providing a solution to the overfitting problem in GANs trained on small datasets, enabling broader usability and improved performance in generative modeling.
