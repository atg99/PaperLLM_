The paper discusses the effects of various data augmentation techniques on the performance of generative models, specifically in the context of StyleGAN2. It highlights the detrimental impact of certain augmentations on model performance, leading to the selection of pixel blitting, geometric, and color transformations for further experimentation. The authors note that while stronger augmentations can mitigate overfitting, they also tend to slow down convergence rates.

To address the challenge of manual tuning of augmentation strength, the paper introduces Adaptive Discriminator Augmentation (ADA). This method dynamically adjusts the augmentation probability based on the degree of overfitting, which is measured using two heuristics derived from discriminator outputs. The first heuristic compares training and validation set outputs, while the second heuristic assesses positive outputs from the training set.

The paper demonstrates that ADA effectively prevents overfitting and yields better results than a fixed augmentation probability determined through grid search. The chosen heuristic for subsequent tests is the second one, with a target value set at 0.6. The results indicate a successful adaptation of augmentation strength over time, ultimately enhancing the quality of generated images.
