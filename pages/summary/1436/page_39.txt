The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR), highlighting key techniques and models that have contributed to the field. It references various studies that explore different aspects of ASR, such as attention mechanisms, self-supervised learning, and the implementation of recurrent neural networks. Notable works include the introduction of monotonic infinite lookback attention for simultaneous machine translation, mixture model attention to support flexible streaming, and the development of competitive direct acoustics-to-word models for conversational speech.

The paper emphasizes the significance of self-supervised learning frameworks, particularly vq-wav2vec and wav2vec 2.0, which facilitate the learning of discrete speech representations. Additionally, it addresses the importance of layer normalization and neural machine translation techniques that jointly learn to align and translate, which have implications for large vocabulary speech recognition. The use of semi-supervised approaches and the exploration of neural transducers are also discussed as methods to improve ASR performance.

Overall, the advancements in these areas demonstrate a trend towards more efficient, accurate, and flexible ASR systems, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in automatic speech recognition technology.
