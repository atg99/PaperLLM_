The references listed encompass a range of research contributions to the field of automatic speech recognition (ASR) and related areas, highlighting advancements in model architectures, training methods, and techniques for improving recognition accuracy. Key studies include:

1. Song et al. introduced a non-autoregressive transformer ASR model enhanced by a CTC (Connectionist Temporal Classification) decoder, aiming to improve efficiency in speech recognition tasks.
2. Sperber et al. explored self-attentional acoustic models, emphasizing the role of attention mechanisms in enhancing ASR performance.
3. Sriram et al. presented "Cold Fusion," a method for training sequence-to-sequence models alongside language models, which aims to leverage the strengths of both for better recognition outcomes.
4. Srivastava et al. discussed the dropout technique as a means to prevent overfitting in neural networks, a critical consideration for training robust ASR systems.
5. Sterpu et al. investigated audio-visual fusion techniques to enhance the robustness of ASR against challenging conditions.
6. Sun et al. focused on improving multilingual transformer transducer models by reducing language confusions, which is crucial for effective ASR in multilingual contexts.
7. Tang et al. developed end-to-end neural segmental models for speech recognition, contributing to advancements in model design for ASR tasks.
8. Tao and Busso proposed an end-to-end audiovisual ASR system utilizing multitask learning to enhance performance by integrating visual information.

These studies collectively contribute to a deeper understanding of ASR technologies, exploring novel architectures and methodologies aimed at improving the accuracy and reliability of speech recognition systems.
