The paper discusses advancements in end-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition (ASR) technologies, highlighting their effective use of text-only data and their ability to address large context biasing challenges. It outlines several training methodologies, including teacher-student (T/S) training, which involves creating smaller models that mimic larger ones, and minimum word error rate (MWER) training, which aligns training criteria with ASR evaluation metrics.

The emerging trend in E2E modeling includes the development of non-autoregressive models for faster decoding, unified models that adapt to various runtime needs, and two-pass models that combine the benefits of streaming and non-streaming approaches. Notably, E2E models have demonstrated superior accuracy compared to traditional hybrid models, leading to a shift in focus toward integrating various speech processing tasks into a single E2E framework.

The paper also addresses the challenges faced by E2E models, particularly in speech translation, where the scarcity of paired speech and text data presents a significant hurdle. Furthermore, it emphasizes the need for E2E models to leverage large amounts of text-only data, which hybrid models utilize effectively through separate acoustic and language models.

In conclusion, while E2E modeling has gained prominence in ASR, further research is needed to fully realize its potential and address existing limitations, particularly in data utilization and handling word reordering in translation tasks.
