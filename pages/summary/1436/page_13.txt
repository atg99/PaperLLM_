The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end automatic speech recognition, focusing on the challenges and solutions related to attention mechanisms in Transformer models. It highlights the full attention case, where the model considers the entire sequence, leading to increased memory and runtime costs as the utterance history grows. To mitigate this, the context operation was introduced, allowing the model to work with a limited history while still accessing a long context through context expansion.

Moreover, the paper addresses the need for including future frames to enhance modeling accuracy. However, traditional context expansion strategies can introduce significant latency due to the requirement for many future frames, especially with multiple Transformer layers. Alternative approaches, such as segmenting the input speech into fixed-size chunks, have been proposed to allow frames within the same chunk to interact while limiting the reception field, thus achieving high accuracy with reduced latency.

The discussion also includes the Gaussian soft-mask method, which modifies the softmax calculation in self-attention to enforce locality through either hard or soft masks. While these masks improve the focus on relevant time steps, they do not significantly reduce computational costs or latency, as they still consider the entire sequence. Overall, the paper emphasizes ongoing innovations aimed at balancing accuracy and efficiency in automatic speech recognition systems.
