The paper provides a comprehensive overview of end-to-end (E2E) models in automatic speech recognition (ASR) and discusses advancements that enable these models to outperform traditional hybrid models in both academic and industrial applications. Key approaches such as noise-invariant feature learning and data augmentation are highlighted for enhancing model robustness. The evaluation of technologies on public datasets, particularly Librispeech, is emphasized, showcasing significant reductions in word error rate (WER) over recent years. 

The paper identifies two main trends: the transition from Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) to Attention-based Encoder-Decoder (AED) and Recurrent Neural Network Transducer (RNN-T) models, and the shift in encoder architecture from Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks to Transformers. The importance of the encoder module is underscored, with advancements in masking strategies to optimize transformer encoders for low latency and computational efficiency in industry applications.

Multilingual E2E models are discussed as a means to cater to diverse user needs, though a performance gap exists between models that utilize language identification and those that do not. Configurable multilingual models are proposed as a solution to bridge this gap. The paper concludes that adaptation, particularly domain adaptation, is crucial for enhancing E2E model accuracy and enabling their widespread adoption in industry, suggesting a need for further research in this area. Factors influencing model performance, such as size and latency, are acknowledged but not detailed in the analysis.
