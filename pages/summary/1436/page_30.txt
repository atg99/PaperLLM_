This paper discusses advancements in speech recognition systems, particularly focusing on handling overlapping speech from multiple speakers. It highlights the network architecture used for separation and recognition, where a mixture encoder processes overlapping audio, and results are generated from two distinct branches. The training loss is derived from permutations of speaker outputs, which poses several limitations.

The first limitation is that the model's output branches must match the number of speakers, restricting its ability to manage scenarios with more speakers. Second, the training process is computationally intensive, with a cost of O(S^3) due to the Hungarian algorithm, making it impractical for systems with many speakers. Lastly, there is a risk of duplicated decoding hypotheses across branches because outputs are independent.

To overcome these challenges, Kanda et al. introduced a serialized output training (SOT) method. This approach employs a single attention-based encoder-decoder (AED) model to predict a combined label sequence for all speakers, simplifying the training loss calculation. The model also incorporates a symbol to indicate speaker changes and organizes reference sequences by their start times.

Furthermore, the paper notes a shift in industry demands towards streaming automatic speech recognition (ASR) models. While previous approaches utilized non-streaming architectures, new methods like the Streaming Unmixing and Recognition Transducer (SURT) have been proposed. SURT employs a mask-based unmixing module to separate speech for recognition. However, it still faces computational challenges with label permutations, which led to the development of Heuristic Error Assignment Training (HEAT) to address these issues.
