The paper discusses advancements in adapting end-to-end (E2E) models for automatic speech recognition (ASR) across different domains. It highlights the use of language model (LM) fusion techniques, which, while effective, can increase computational costs and may not be suitable for on-device applications. A notable trend is the adaptation of E2E models using synthesized speech from text-to-speech (TTS) technologies, particularly beneficial for models like RNN-T that function similarly to LMs. Research indicates that TTS-based adaptation can outperform LM fusion methods.

However, TTS adaptation has limitations, including differences between synthesized and real speech, which can reduce recognition accuracy. This challenge has been addressed through the implementation of a mapping network that transforms TTS audio features to align with real speech features during training, which is removed during testing. Other drawbacks include reduced speaker variation in TTS data and the high costs associated with creating multi-speaker TTS models.

To overcome these issues, the paper introduces a spliced data method, which adapts E2E models by randomly extracting and concatenating audio segments from source training data to create new utterances. This method demonstrates superior performance compared to TTS adaptation. Both adaptation methods require audio synthesis from text, followed by updates to the E2E models, emphasizing the ongoing need for efficient approaches in ASR model training and adaptation.
