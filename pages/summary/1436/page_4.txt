This paper discusses the Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) approach used in end-to-end Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR). It illustrates how CTC manages the relationship between input speech sequences and output label sequences by inserting blank labels between characters. This allows for the construction of CTC paths that match the length of the input speech. The paper presents an example with the word "team," showing different CTC paths generated through the mechanism.

The CTC architecture includes an encoder network that transforms acoustic features into high-level representations. The CTC loss function is defined as the negative logarithm of the probability of the correct labels given the speech input. The probability of a label sequence is derived from all possible CTC paths, with a conditional independence assumption that allows for the decomposition of probabilities into frame posteriors.

CTC is recognized as a pioneering end-to-end technology in ASR, though the conditional independence assumption has faced criticism. The paper suggests that integrating attention mechanisms can alleviate this criticism by enabling implicit language modeling across speech frames, thus refining the encoder while preserving the CTC objective.
