The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR) with a focus on the RNN-Transducer (RNN-T) model, which has gained popularity in the industry due to its natural streaming capabilities. Unlike Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC), RNN-T eliminates the conditional independence assumption by considering the entire label sequence up to the current time step. The RNN-T architecture consists of three main components: an encoder network that generates a high-level feature representation, a prediction network that produces a representation based on the previous output label, and a joint network that combines these two representations to generate the final output. The model uses a softmax function to calculate the probability of each output token, and its loss function is based on the negative log probability of the given output sequence. This framework highlights the effectiveness of RNN-T in capturing sequential dependencies in speech recognition tasks.
