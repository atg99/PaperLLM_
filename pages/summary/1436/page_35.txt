The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition (ASR), highlighting key technologies and their impact on word error rates (WER) in both non-streaming and streaming contexts. 

In non-streaming E2E ASR, various models have been developed over the years, showcasing improvements in WER through techniques like connectionist temporal classification (CTC) and attention-based encoders. Notable advancements include the use of SpecAugment, which significantly reduced WER, and the integration of self-supervised learning (SSL) methods such as wav2vec 2.0. The best-performing model in this category achieved a WER of 1.4 for test-clean data.

In streaming E2E ASR, the focus has been on optimizing real-time performance while maintaining accuracy. Innovations such as triggered attention and various self-attention mechanisms have contributed to lowering WER, with the most effective models achieving a WER of 2.5 for test-clean data. 

Overall, the paper underscores the progress made in E2E ASR through the introduction of advanced neural network architectures, training techniques, and data augmentation strategies, leading to enhanced performance metrics in both non-streaming and streaming scenarios.
