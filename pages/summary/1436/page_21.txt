The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR), particularly focusing on the factorized neural transducer model. This model enhances the prediction capabilities of the RNN-T by separating the predictions for normal tokens and blank tokens, allowing the prediction network to function similarly to a standalone language model (LM). The adaptation methods for this model utilize text from new domains to refine its predictions without compromising performance on the original domain.

Key studies mentioned include Meng et al., who applied ILMT to align the internal LM with standalone neural LMs, and Chen et al., who identified the limitations of the RNN-T prediction network. They proposed the factorized neural transducer, which can be adapted using established neural LM techniques to improve recognition accuracy for new domain speech.

The paper also highlights the importance of maintaining performance on the source domain during adaptation. Lifelong learning is suggested as a strategy to integrate new tasks while preserving previously acquired knowledge. When source-domain data is available, it can be combined with new-domain data for adaptation. In scenarios where this is not possible, regularization methods are recommended to ensure the adapted model remains close to the original model.
