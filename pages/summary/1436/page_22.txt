This paper discusses the concept of customization in automatic speech recognition (ASR), distinguishing it from speaker adaptation and domain adaptation. Customization leverages user-specific contexts—like contacts and locations—to improve ASR accuracy by biasing the model outputs towards relevant terms, such as contact names.

The paper highlights that while hybrid systems can employ on-the-fly rescoring to adjust language model weights easily, end-to-end (E2E) systems face more significant challenges. A proposed solution involves adding a context bias encoder to the E2E model, which integrates context lists into the recognition process. This approach has evolved into the contextual Listen, Attend, and Spell (CLAS) ASR model, enhancing standard ASR with a contextual encoder and attention mechanisms that consider both speech input and contextual phrases.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the difficulties encountered when the contextual phrase list is extensive, suggesting that shallow fusion with a contextual biasing language model may be more effective. It also discusses innovations such as class tags for context-aware training and the combination of various methods to optimize performance in recognizing rare words or phrases not seen during training. Overall, the paper outlines significant advancements and ongoing challenges in the realm of ASR customization through the integration of contextual information.
