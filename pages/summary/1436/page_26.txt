The paper discusses advancements in automatic speech recognition (ASR) models that unify streaming and non-streaming capabilities while optimizing computational efficiency. It highlights various approaches to achieving this dual functionality. For instance, Yu et al. introduced a dual end-to-end model with shared weights for both modes, employing dual-mode modules within the Conformer architecture. This method utilized in-place teacher-student learning, enhancing accuracy and reducing latency for streaming ASR.

Another approach by Audhkhasi et al. involved decomposing the Transformer's softmax attention into causal and anti-causal components, utilizing only causal attention for streaming and both for non-streaming scenarios. Additionally, Wu et al. proposed a dynamic sparsity network for RNN-T, allowing for runtime adjustments of sparsity configurations to adapt to varying computational needs across devices.

Furthermore, a dynamic encoder concept was illustrated, where layer dropout during training enabled models to maintain low computational costs while decoding full utterances. Advanced configurations allowed for varying computational costs within a single utterance, with fast and slow encoders being selected based on input speech frames. This flexibility is crucial for applications requiring different latency levels, such as voice search and command control versus dictation and video transcription.
