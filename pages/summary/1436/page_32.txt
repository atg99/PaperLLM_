The paper discusses advancements in end-to-end (E2E) modeling within the speech recognition community, highlighting ESPnet as a prominent toolkit. It emphasizes the significance of model unit selection in E2E modeling, noting that while most units are derived from text, phoneme units are also being explored. The paper suggests that bridging text and phoneme units, such as with pronunciation-guided units, may enhance performance.

E2E models typically require substantial amounts of training data, and their performance diminishes with smaller datasets. To mitigate this issue, various strategies are proposed, including regularization techniques like dropout and data augmentation methods such as SpecAugment and speed perturbation. Additionally, approaches like adversarial learning and meta-learning are mentioned.

A widely adopted solution involves pre-training E2E models using multilingual data or self-supervised learning (SSL) before fine-tuning with low-resource labeled data. This method allows the model to leverage cross-linguistic information, making transfer learning more effective. SSL, in particular, is highlighted as a promising approach for addressing challenges in low-resource languages, as it does not require labeled data for pre-training. The paper concludes that SSL is emerging as a significant trend in automatic speech recognition for resource-limited languages.
