The references listed pertain to various advancements and evaluations in the field of automatic speech recognition (ASR) and related technologies. Key contributions include the exploration of end-to-end models without lexicons, as demonstrated by Sainath et al. in 2018, and the application of deep neural networks for multi-channel signal processing in ASR, highlighted by Sainath et al. in 2017. The development of efficient streaming models and improvements in rare-word modeling were also addressed by Sainath et al. in 2021.

Other notable works include the introduction of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architectures for large-scale acoustic modeling by Sak et al. in 2014, and the implementation of self-attention networks for connectionist temporal classification by Salazar et al. in 2019. Saon et al. contributed to RNN transducer technology and synchronous decoding methods for improved speech recognition in 2020 and 2021.

Research on unsupervised speaker adaptation using attention-based mechanisms was presented by Sarı et al. in 2020, while the wav2vec model for unsupervised pre-training in speech recognition was introduced by Schneider et al. in 2019. Additional studies explored language tracking in mixed-language speech recognition and the use of acoustically grounded word embeddings to enhance ASR performance. Overall, these contributions reflect a growing trend towards leveraging deep learning and novel architectures to improve speech recognition systems.
