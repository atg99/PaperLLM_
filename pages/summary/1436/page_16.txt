The paper discusses the development of end-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition (ASR) models capable of recognizing speech in multiple languages. While a universal multilingual model can leverage data from various languages, it may lead to confusion during recognition due to the overlap between languages. Introducing language identity (LID) significantly enhances performance by guiding the model to transcribe the target language accurately. However, this approach requires prior knowledge of the language being spoken, which limits its practicality.

To address this limitation, the paper highlights the need for multilingual systems that can recognize speech without knowing the language in advance. One proposed solution is the configurable multilingual model (CMM) that combines data from all languages into a single model, which can be adjusted to recognize any language combination as needed. By activating specific language modules based on user selection, CMM reduces confusion and performs better than traditional multilingual models lacking LID.

The paper also notes challenges in designing multilingual E2E models, particularly when scaling to cover numerous languages, as data imbalance can lead to poor performance on low-resource languages. To mitigate this, data sampling techniques are employed to balance training data. Additionally, expanding model capacity is essential for effectively recognizing a large number of languages, with some models reaching billions of parameters. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of addressing these challenges to create effective multilingual ASR systems for diverse user needs.
