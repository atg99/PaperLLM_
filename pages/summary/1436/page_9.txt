Recent advances in end-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition (ASR) have focused on improving latency and alignment during speech processing. Techniques such as constrained alignment have been introduced to restrict training alignments within a specified delay threshold, enhancing the alignment between input speech sequences and output labels, particularly in streaming RNN-T models. This approach not only minimizes latency but also optimizes GPU memory usage and accelerates training by limiting the number of alignment paths.

FastEmit has emerged as another method to reduce latency without relying on ground truth alignments, promoting the emission of vocabulary tokens while discouraging blank transitions. However, this aggressive latency reduction may compromise recognition accuracy. An alternative, proposed by Kim et al., utilizes self-alignment to achieve a more balanced latency reduction, resulting in improved accuracy and latency tradeoff compared to constrained alignment and FastEmit.

In the realm of E2E ASR models, the encoder plays a crucial role by transforming speech input into high-level feature representations. The Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network has been a prevalent choice for encoders in early E2E ASR implementations, with configurations including multi-layer unidirectional and bidirectional LSTM architectures. These configurations facilitate effective speech representation and processing, laying the groundwork for subsequent advancements in the field.
