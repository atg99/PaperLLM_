This collection of references highlights significant advancements in speech recognition and machine learning, particularly focusing on neural network architectures and methodologies. 

Key contributions include Graves et al.'s exploration of Connectionist Temporal Classification for unsegmented sequence labeling, which laid foundational work for handling sequential data with recurrent neural networks. Graves and Jaitly further advanced the field with their end-to-end speech recognition framework utilizing recurrent neural networks. The introduction of the Conformer model by Gulati et al. represents a convergence of convolutional and transformer architectures, enhancing performance in speech recognition tasks.

Guo et al. proposed a spelling correction model tailored for end-to-end speech recognition, reflecting the need for accuracy in transcriptions. Their work on efficient minimum word error rate training of RNN-Transducer systems indicates a focus on optimizing training processes for better outcomes. The ESPnet toolkit's advancements, highlighted by Guo et al., show the integration of recent developments in speech processing technologies.

Deep Speech by Hannun et al. exemplifies the scalability of end-to-end models in speech recognition, while He et al. focused on applying sequence-to-sequence models for keyword spotting, emphasizing the trend towards lightweight models for mobile applications. Heigold et al. addressed multilingual capabilities in acoustic modeling, showcasing the adaptability of deep neural networks across diverse languages. 

Overall, these references illustrate a trajectory of innovation in speech recognition, emphasizing the significance of neural architectures and efficiency in model training and deployment.
