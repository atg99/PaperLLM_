The paper discusses two primary models for end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR): Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) and Attention-based Encoder-Decoder (AED). CTC has gained traction by integrating Transformer architectures, which leverage powerful attention mechanisms, alongside self-supervised learning techniques that enhance semantic representation. 

In contrast, the AED model comprises an encoder, an attention module, and a decoder, calculating the probability of output labels based on the input features. While AED is effective for tasks like machine translation, its application in ASR presents alignment challenges due to the monotonic nature of speech signals and output sequences. To improve this alignment, AED models are often optimized in conjunction with CTC within a multi-task learning framework, which has become a standard approach in the field. 

Moreover, combining outputs from both the AED and CTC models during decoding has been shown to enhance performance. The paper highlights the necessity for ASR systems to operate with low latency, making the advancements in attention mechanisms particularly relevant for real-time applications.
