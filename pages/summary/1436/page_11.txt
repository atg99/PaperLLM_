Recent advances in end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR) have focused on enhancing model architectures to improve accuracy and efficiency. The paper discusses the transition from traditional recurrent neural network transducers (RNN-T) to Transformer-based models, specifically the Transformer Transducer. This model utilizes multi-head self-attention (MHSA) to enhance capacity by processing multiple parallel self-attention mechanisms, resulting in improved accuracy due to the robust modeling capabilities of Transformers.

However, while Transformers excel at capturing global context, they struggle with local patterns. To address this limitation, the Conformer architecture was introduced, which integrates convolutional neural networks (CNN) with Transformers. The Conformer model features a structure that includes two half-step feedforward networks (FFNs) positioned around the MHSA and convolution modules. Despite its increased complexity, the Conformer has demonstrated superior performance over the standard Transformer in various ASR tasks.

The paper also notes that the application of full self-attention in streaming ASR poses challenges, prompting the development of a masking strategy to facilitate its use in real-time speech recognition scenarios. This evolution in ASR architectures signifies a significant step toward more effective and efficient speech processing models.
