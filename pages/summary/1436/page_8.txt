The paper discusses the alignment paths in the RNN-Transducer (RNN-T) model for automatic speech recognition (ASR). It defines the mapping from an alignment path to a label sequence, illustrated with an example where a speech sequence is aligned to a label sequence that includes a start token. The valid alignment paths traverse a grid, and each path's length is determined by the combined duration of the speech and the labels.

The authors highlight the memory requirements for calculating posteriors in the alignment grid, noting that RNN-T requires a three-dimensional tensor, which is more memory-intensive than other end-to-end models like Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) and Attention-based Encoder-Decoder (AED). They reference methods to enhance training efficiency, such as vectorizing forward and backward probabilities and merging functions to lower memory costs, thereby allowing for larger minibatches.

The paper emphasizes the importance of ASR latency for user experience, indicating that high latency can lead to perceptions of unresponsiveness. Recent studies on user-perceived latency are acknowledged, along with improvements in end-of-sentence (EOS) token generation and detection to mitigate latency issues. Despite these advancements, the authors note that the vanilla RNN-T model still faces challenges in latency, particularly in delaying label predictions.
