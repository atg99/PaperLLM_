NAR models aim to improve efficiency by predicting all target tokens simultaneously rather than sequentially. This approach can significantly reduce decoding time, making it suitable for real-time applications. 

Recent studies have demonstrated that NAR models can achieve competitive performance compared to traditional autoregressive models, particularly in scenarios with large datasets and complex language structures. However, challenges remain, such as maintaining the quality of output and effectively handling the dependencies between tokens.

Furthermore, contextual biasing techniques are being refined to enhance ASR performance. The integration of external language models (LM) using methods like density ratio and HAT has shown improved biasing results. These techniques help manage large bias lists, often exceeding 1000 phrases, by employing contextual spelling correction, which optimizes the selection of relevant phrases based on the ASR output. 

Overall, advancements in NAR models and contextual biasing strategies are pushing the boundaries of ASR technology, addressing both efficiency and accuracy challenges in speech recognition tasks.
