Recent advancements in end-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition (ASR) highlight the challenges of adapting E2E models to new domains. These models often excel in memorizing training data but struggle significantly when applied to unfamiliar domains due to a lack of sufficient paired speech-text data. Unlike hybrid models, which benefit from a larger language model (LM) trained on extensive text data, E2E models require substantial new-domain speech transcription, which is difficult to obtain. As a result, current adaptation strategies primarily utilize new-domain text data.

One prevalent method for adapting E2E models involves fusing them with an external LM trained on new-domain text. Various LM fusion techniques exist, including shallow fusion, deep fusion, and cold fusion. Shallow fusion, the most straightforward and commonly used method, interpolates the external LM with the E2E model during inference but lacks a clear probabilistic framework.

To enhance integration, methods such as maximum a posteriori decoding for connectionist temporal classification (CTC) and a density ratio approach based on Bayes’ rule for recurrent neural network transducer (RNN-T) have been introduced. The latter modifies E2E model outputs by applying the ratio of the new-domain LM to the source-domain LM.

Another development is the hybrid autoregressive transducer (HAT) model, which improves RNN-T by normalizing label distributions, allowing for a mathematically sound integration with external LMs using the density ratio method. Additionally, an internal LM estimation-based fusion (ILME) approach has been proposed to enhance LM integration by estimating internal LM scores during inference, leading to further developments such as internal LM training (ILMT) that optimizes internal LM components.

The challenge of tuning LM weights across multiple development sets is recognized as computationally intensive. To address this, minimum word error rate (MWER) training with LM fusion has been proposed, allowing for simultaneous LM fusion during training and simplifying the inference process. Overall, these advancements aim to improve the adaptability and performance of E2E ASR systems in new domains.
