The paper discusses the use of LSTM and Transformer architectures in automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems, highlighting their functionalities and differences in handling input sequences. 

For LSTM-based encoders, the output at the last layer serves as the encoder output. Unidirectional LSTM is preferred in streaming ASR systems, while bidirectional LSTM (BLSTM) encoders enhance accuracy by utilizing complete utterance information. However, BLSTM models are non-streaming. To bridge the accuracy gap between LSTM and BLSTM, techniques like latency controlled BLSTM (LC-BLSTM) and contextual LSTM (cLSTM) are proposed. LC-BLSTM operates in an overlapped multi-frame chunk mode while cLSTM processes frames sequentially, using future frame outputs as inputs for current frames.

The paper also emphasizes the advantages of Transformer models over LSTM, particularly in capturing long-term dependencies due to their attention mechanism. Transformers are now preferred in end-to-end (E2E) models, consisting of stacked blocks with multi-head self-attention layers and feed-forward networks. The architecture includes residual connections and layer normalization for enhanced performance. The self-attention mechanism computes attention distributions using a dot-product similarity function, allowing for a comprehensive understanding of input speech sequences.

Overall, the study illustrates the evolution from LSTM to Transformer models in ASR, with a focus on improving accuracy and efficiency in processing speech data.
