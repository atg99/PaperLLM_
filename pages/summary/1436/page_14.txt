In this section, the paper discusses alternative training criteria for end-to-end (E2E) models beyond the standard training loss of minimizing the negative log probability of the output given the input. One prominent method is teacher-student (T/S) learning, which minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the output distributions of teacher and student networks, a concept popularized by Hinton as knowledge distillation.

The T/S learning approach involves a token-level loss function that compares the posteriors of the teacher and student networks. This method is particularly useful for training smaller student models from larger teacher models and for adapting streaming E2E models from non-streaming counterparts. The paper highlights the challenges of delayed decision-making in streaming models and discusses techniques to align output spike patterns between different model types.

Additionally, T/S learning can facilitate the adaptation of E2E models to different environments by leveraging paired acoustic data from source and target domains. The paper notes a shift in recent studies towards simultaneous training of teacher and student models, contrasting with the traditional two-step process.

Overall, the section emphasizes the versatility of T/S learning in various E2E modeling scenarios, including model compression and environmental adaptation.
