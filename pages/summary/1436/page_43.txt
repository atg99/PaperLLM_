The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR), highlighting various innovative approaches and techniques. Key contributions include methods for improving streaming ASR through model distillation on unsupervised data, as presented by Doutre et al. Combining end-to-end models with adversarial training for low-resource scenarios is explored by Drexler and Glass. The integration of speech recognition with speaker diarization through sequence transduction techniques is illustrated by El Shafey et al.

Fan et al. introduce a single-step non-autoregressive transformer model, CASS-NAT, that leverages CTC alignment. Additionally, an online attention-based model for ASR is proposed, showcasing real-time capabilities. Quantization techniques are examined by Fasoli et al., focusing on reducing the memory footprint of LSTM-based models.

Knowledge distillation from BERT for sequence-to-sequence ASR is explored by Futami et al., while Gaur et al. propose a mixture of informed experts for multilingual ASR. The paper also addresses acoustic-to-phrase models and the use of a stateless prediction network within RNN-Transducers.

Visual context-aware ASR is discussed by Ghorbani et al., emphasizing the integration of pre-trained text-video representations. The multilingual training of deep neural networks is presented by Ghoshal et al. Lastly, Gong et al. introduce a self-attention channel combinator frontend aimed at enhancing multichannel far-field ASR, illustrating the ongoing evolution of techniques in the field.
