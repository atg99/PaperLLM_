Recent advancements in end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR) highlight the introduction of techniques like Mask CTC, which addresses specific issues in token prediction by masking low-confidence tokens. The model optimizes the masked tokens based on the observed tokens and the input speech sequence, thus focusing on substitution errors while also extending capabilities to handle deletion and insertion errors through partial target sequence predictions.

The encoder remains a critical component in these E2E models, with a variety of designs tailored to meet specific requirements such as streaming capabilities, latency considerations, and computational efficiency. Overall, the trend towards unified models signifies a shift in ASR development, aiming for versatility and reduced training costs across different applications.
