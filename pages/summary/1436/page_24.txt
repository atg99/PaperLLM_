The paper discusses the use of non-autoregressive (NAR) modeling in context spelling correction, highlighting its ability to generate all target tokens simultaneously. NAR methods leverage the feature sequences from acoustic encoders, which are thought to contain both acoustic and some semantic language information. However, the assumption of independence among tokens leads to generally poorer performance compared to autoregressive (AR) models, which rely on the sequential prediction of tokens. The main advantage of NAR models lies in their faster decoding speed, as they predict all tokens in parallel, unlike AR models that face complexities due to token dependencies. The paper references Bai et al. for a typical NAR token generation approach, where the sequence length is either predicted or set as constant. Despite the efficiency of NAR models, the strong independence assumption poses challenges, particularly in accurately predicting the token sequence length, which is addressed through techniques like Mask CTC.
