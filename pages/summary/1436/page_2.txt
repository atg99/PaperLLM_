1 Introduction

The accuracy of automatic speech recognition (ASR) has significantly improved since the adoption of deep neural network (DNN) based hybrid modeling. This innovation replaced traditional Gaussian mixture models for acoustic likelihood evaluation while maintaining components like the acoustic model, language model, and lexicon model. A recent advancement in the speech community is the shift from hybrid modeling to end-to-end (E2E) modeling, which translates input speech sequences directly into output token sequences using a single network. This approach revolutionizes ASR by eliminating the traditional modeling components used for decades.

E2E models offer several advantages over traditional hybrid models. Firstly, they utilize a single objective function aligned with the ASR objective to optimize the entire network, unlike hybrid models that optimize individual components separately, which may not achieve a global optimum. Consequently, E2E models have demonstrated superior performance in both academic and industrial settings. Secondly, E2E models simplify the ASR pipeline by directly outputting characters or words, whereas hybrid models require extensive expert knowledge and complex design. Lastly, because E2E models operate on a single network, they are more compact and can be deployed on devices with high accuracy.

Despite achieving state-of-the-art results in ASR accuracy benchmarks, hybrid models continue to dominate commercial ASR systems. This is due to several practical factors, such as streaming, latency, and adaptation capability, which influence the deployment of models. Traditional hybrid models, optimized for production over many years, excel in these areas. Without addressing these challenges effectively, E2E models face hurdles in widespread commercialization. This paper aims to provide an overview of popular E2E models, focusing on technologies that tackle these industry challenges.
