The paper discusses recent advancements in end-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition (ASR), particularly focusing on multilingual modeling and code-switching (CS) challenges. It highlights that a simple E2E structure may not effectively handle the nuances of different languages, leading to the incorporation of language-specific adaptation layers to enhance model performance. The paper categorizes CS into intra-sentential, where switches occur within an utterance, and inter-sentential, where switches happen between utterances. Intra-sentential CS poses a greater challenge and has garnered more research attention.

To address these challenges, various strategies have been proposed. For inter-sentential CS, synthetic utterance-level CS text has been generated to improve ASR accuracy. For intra-sentential CS, methods such as using a language identification (LID) model to adjust the posterior of a connectionist temporal classification (CTC) model have been explored. The paper notes that a significant hurdle for CS ASR is the scarcity of CS data, which is problematic for data-hungry E2E models. To mitigate this issue, techniques to leverage monolingual data effectively have been investigated, including constrained output embedding methods and the use of bi-encoders with separate encoders for each language. These approaches aim to facilitate smoother transitions between languages in CS speech recognition.
