Variable context training has emerged as a method to adapt models for different latency requirements in real-time applications. By configuring the Transformer encoder with varying right context lengths during training, models can be optimized for specific latency thresholds based on task needs. Additionally, the use of task IDs allows the Transducer model to dynamically adjust its encoder segments and latency at runtime.

The paper also highlights the benefits of implementing two-pass models to enhance ASR performance. Although single end-to-end models demonstrate strong capabilities, a second-pass model can further refine output. Spelling correction methods have been developed using text-to-speech data to create separate translation models aimed at correcting errors from the first-pass model. Wang et al. introduced a two-pass RNN-T model where the first-pass transcribes speech into syllables, and the second-pass translates those syllables into character sequences, benefiting from a larger text data pool for improved language modeling.

Moreover, a two-pass model utilizing an attention-based encoder-decoder (AED) was proposed to enhance processing by attending to the encoder output of a streaming RNN-T model, thus integrating both speech and text inputs for more accurate recognition outcomes.
