Recent advances in end-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition (ASR) have leveraged self-supervised learning (SSL) techniques, such as wav2vec 2.0 and HuBERT, to enhance performance even with limited supervised training data. E2E ASR models typically process individual utterances independently, but context from previous utterances can significantly aid recognition. Some studies have explored concatenating previous audio and hypotheses to improve current utterance recognition.

Efficient decoding strategies are crucial for deploying E2E models in production, with recent approaches simplifying the prediction network to reduce costs. Techniques such as model compression and quantization are essential for small-footprint deployments. Confidence measures and word timing are also important, particularly in applications like video transcription, where users may need to verify potentially misrecognized words.

Most E2E models utilize Mel filter bank features derived from speech, but recent research has examined the potential of using raw speech waveforms directly. Combining E2E and hybrid models has shown promise in enhancing performance through methods like rescoring and two-pass modeling. Additionally, integrating multi-modal inputs, especially visual signals, has demonstrated benefits in E2E ASR.

Robustness remains a critical challenge, as E2E models may struggle with training and testing data mismatches. While some approaches, such as transfer learning, have been explored to address these issues, there is still significant room for growth in enhancing the robustness of E2E ASR systems.
