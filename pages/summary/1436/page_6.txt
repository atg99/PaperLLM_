The paper discusses advancements in streaming automatic speech recognition (ASR) models, particularly focusing on attention-based encoder-decoder (AED) models and RNN Transducer (RNN-T) models. Traditional AED models require the entire utterance for decoding, which is impractical for continuous speech input. Various methods have been proposed for streaming AED, primarily differing in how input speech is segmented into chunks for applying attention. 

Monotonic attention is a prominent approach where attention is focused on specific time steps corresponding to trigger points. This method has evolved through various implementations, such as monotonic chunkwise attention (MoChA), which utilizes fixed-size chunks, and adaptive-size chunk methods. Recent innovations include monotonic infinite lookback (MILK) attention, which considers the entire sequence leading to the trigger point, and triggered attention that uses CTC segments for chunk determination.

Despite these advancements, many streaming AED models struggle with low latency, a critical requirement for commercial applications. Recent approaches, such as those proposed by Inaguma et al. and Wang et al., aim to address this issue by incorporating external alignments and predictive networks for word boundaries, respectively.

A comparative analysis highlights the advantages of RNN Transducer over MoChA, particularly in latency and stability, suggesting a trend in the industry favoring RNN-T as the preferred model for streaming ASR. In contrast, AED models may still find relevance in non-streaming contexts. Overall, the paper underscores the ongoing developments in streaming AED models while recognizing RNN-T's dominance in practical applications.
