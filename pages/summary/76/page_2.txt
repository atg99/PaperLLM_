The paper discusses the limitations of physical-model-based methods for predicting urban air quality, which often rely on empirical assumptions and require expert adjustments for different urban environments. In contrast, the authors propose a data-driven approach that leverages advancements in sensor technology and big data, particularly through the use of neural networks, to predict air quality with potentially greater accuracy.

Previous studies have utilized various neural network models to forecast concentrations of pollutants like NO2 and PM10, demonstrating superior performance compared to traditional statistical models. However, many of these models have not effectively addressed the requirements of time-series predictions. To overcome this limitation, the authors introduce a novel model called Long Short-Term Memory - Fully Connected Neural Network (LSTM-FC), designed to predict PM2.5 levels over a 48-hour period. This model integrates historical air quality data, meteorological data, and temporal information to capture both long-term and short-term features.

The LSTM component processes local historical data to generate preliminary predictions, while a fully connected neural network combines these predictions with data from nearby monitoring stations to enhance accuracy. The paper includes experimental results that validate the effectiveness of the proposed method. Subsequent sections detail the architecture and performance of the LSTM-FC neural network, contributing to the field of air quality prediction.
