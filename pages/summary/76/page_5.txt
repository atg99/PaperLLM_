The paper presents a neural network model, specifically an LSTM-FC (Long Short-Term Memory with Fully Connected layers), designed for predicting air quality based on a comprehensive dataset from the Urban Air project by Microsoft Research. The dataset includes 278,023 hourly air quality records, 116,867 meteorology records, and 390,702 weather forecast records from Beijing, covering the period from May 1, 2014, to April 30, 2015. Each air quality instance tracks six pollutants while meteorological records contain seven variables such as temperature and humidity. 

To handle missing data, linear interpolation was applied, and the model's effectiveness was assessed using 12-folder cross-validation. The mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean square error (RMSE) were employed as metrics for evaluation. The optimal parameters for the LSTM-FC were determined through trial and error, with the number of surrounding stations set to five and the input sequence length to three.

Results indicate that the model's predictions of PM2.5 concentrations align closely with actual values, demonstrating the model's capability to forecast air quality effectively. The findings underscore the challenges posed by the variability in air quality data, as indicated by the standard deviation of PM2.5 concentrations in Beijing. Overall, the study contributes to the understanding of air quality prediction through advanced neural network techniques.
