This paper discusses advancements in predicting PM2.5 concentrations using a modified long short-term memory (LSTM) model, specifically the LSTM-FC model. Unlike the approach by Li et al. (2017), which utilized historical PM2.5 data from various stations combined with auxiliary data through a neural network, the LSTM-FC model integrates all local features from a single station, including PM2.5 data and auxiliary data, into one LSTM. This approach enhances the model's ability to leverage geographic information from auxiliary data.

The paper further elaborates on the LSTM-based temporal simulator, which effectively captures long-range dependencies in time series data. The LSTM structure consists of a memory cell and three gates: input, forget, and output gates. These gates regulate how new information influences the model's memory and output, allowing for effective tracking of PM2.5 concentration trends. The formulation of the LSTM's operations is detailed, highlighting the importance of the input and output gates in controlling the model's response to changes in PM2.5 levels.

Additionally, a three-layer fully-connected neural network is introduced as a spatial combinator to merge predictions from various temporal simulators. This design takes advantage of neural networks' nonlinear expression capabilities, making them suitable for integrating complex data inputs. The sigmoid function is employed as the activation function for the neural network, facilitating the prediction process.
