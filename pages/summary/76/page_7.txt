The paper presents a study on predicting PM2.5 concentrations using a Long Short-Term Memory Fully Connected (LSTM-FC) neural network model. Employing 12-fold cross-validation across 36 monitors, the authors conducted 432 experiments, revealing that prediction errors vary geographically and seasonally. Results indicate that monitors in downtown Beijing and its western regions perform better due to higher monitoring station density and more green areas, leading to improved air quality. In contrast, monitors on the city's edges experience poorer performance, particularly during winter months when heating exacerbates smog pollution.

The study further analyzes the influence of various meteorological factors on prediction accuracy, finding wind speed to be the most significant contributor, while pressure has the least impact. A comparative analysis among ANN, LSTM, and LSTM-FC models demonstrates that LSTM-FC consistently outperforms the other methods across various time intervals, as evidenced by lower mean absolute errors (MAE) and root mean square errors (RMSE).

In conclusion, the LSTM-FC model effectively captures long-range temporal dependencies and integrates spatial information for PM2.5 prediction over a 48-hour horizon, outperforming traditional models. Future work aims to develop data-driven models for predicting sudden air pollution changes.
