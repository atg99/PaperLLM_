The paper presents a novel predictive model for PM 2.5 concentration, termed the long short-term memory - fully connected (LSTM-FC) neural network. This model aims to forecast urban air quality over the next 48 hours by utilizing historical air quality data, meteorological data, weather forecasts, and the day of the week. The LSTM-FC model comprises two main components: a long short-term memory (LSTM) temporal simulator for modeling local variations in PM 2.5 levels, and a fully connected neural network that captures spatial dependencies between a central monitoring station and its neighboring stations.

The authors emphasize the significance of accurate PM 2.5 prediction due to the severe air pollution challenges faced in China, particularly over the past decade. They highlight that the PM 2.5 concentrations are interrelated both temporally and spatially, which allows for enhanced prediction capabilities. The study evaluates the model on a dataset from 36 air quality monitoring stations in Beijing, spanning from May 2014 to April 2015, and compares its performance against traditional artificial neural network (ANN) and LSTM models. The results indicate that the LSTM-FC model outperforms these other models, demonstrating its effectiveness in predicting PM 2.5 contamination.

Overall, this research contributes to the understanding and forecasting of air quality, which is crucial for public health and environmental management.
