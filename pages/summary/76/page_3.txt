This paper presents a novel LSTM-FC model designed for forecasting PM 2.5 concentrations over a 48-hour period. The model aims to predict the real-valued concentration for the next 1 hour and the maximum and minimum concentrations for the subsequent time intervals of 7-12, 13-24, and 25-48 hours. The forecasting process utilizes historical data from the past hours, establishing it as a multi-step-ahead forecasting model.

The LSTM-FC model consists of two key components: a temporal simulator based on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and a spatial combinatory neural network. The temporal simulator processes input feature vectors that include district-level meteorological data, air quality data from the target and neighboring stations, and the day of the week. These inputs are crucial since meteorological factors significantly influence air quality. The LSTM component focuses solely on temporal information, simulating PM 2.5 concentration variations.

The predictions generated by the LSTM are then integrated into the spatial combinatory component, which combines results from the central and neighboring stations to produce the final prediction for the central station. The model's innovation lies in its dual approach: it effectively separates the handling of temporal and spatial information, thus enhancing prediction accuracy and interpretability. Additionally, it incorporates data from neighboring stations based on geographical proximity, which helps to prevent overfitting and reduces computational complexity.

Overall, the LSTM-FC model demonstrates a sophisticated method for air quality forecasting by leveraging both historical data and geographical relationships among monitoring stations.
