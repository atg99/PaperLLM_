The section discusses the challenges associated with training Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), specifically focusing on the truncated unrolled RNN system as established in Proposition 1. It highlights that while the truncated system, defined by specific equations, is inherently stable, practical training issues arise due to "vanishing gradients" and "exploding gradients." These problems become more pronounced when the dependencies in the target subsequence span many samples, necessitating a wider unrolled model to effectively capture long-range dependencies.

The training of truncated unrolled RNNs is typically performed using Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT), which adapts the traditional Back Propagation method for sequence data. BPTT involves unrolling the RNN for a finite number of steps, transforming it into a directed acyclic graph, which allows for the application of gradient descent techniques to optimize the parameters of the system.

The objective function for training, which is based on the readout signal, is expressed as a sum of contributions from individual time steps. The section emphasizes the importance of considering the influence of the state signal across time steps when calculating gradients. This dependency indicates that the state at one time step affects subsequent states, necessitating a careful application of the chain rule to capture the complete gradient information. Overall, the discussion underscores the complexity of training RNNs and the need for meticulous gradient computation to address the inherent challenges.
