The paper discusses the advantages of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks over standard Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) in the context of gradient descent training. It highlights that the gate mechanisms in LSTMs contribute to better numerical behavior of the gradient elements, ensuring that intermediate and border gradient sequences can effectively propagate information over extended ranges. This is critical for the success of gradient descent, as it relies on well-behaved gradients.

Equations presented detail the relationships between various gradient sequences and their dependencies. The analysis focuses on how changes in these gradients influence the LSTM's sensitivity to factors that may lead to gradient decay. The paper employs the chain rule and change of indices to explore these relationships, ultimately leading to a formulation that captures the dependencies of gradients in the LSTM framework.

Key equations illustrate the propagation of gradients through the network and their relationships with various components, emphasizing the structured approach to ensuring that gradients can be sustained across time steps. The discussion culminates in a summary equation that encapsulates the interactions between the gradients and the LSTM parameters, suggesting a robust mechanism for maintaining gradient flow during training.
