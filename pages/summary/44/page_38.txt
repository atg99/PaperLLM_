The section outlines the backward pass equations for training the Augmented Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) cell using Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). It starts by establishing the total partial derivatives necessary for the training process, including the derivative of the objective function with respect to the qualifier signal. This inclusion is crucial due to the projection layer incorporated into the cell's architecture.

The backward pass equations are derived by applying the chain rule to the Augmented LSTM cell, resulting in a series of equations that capture the gradient flow through various components of the cell. Key equations include the calculation of intermediate variables such as χ, β, ρ, γcr, αcr, ψ, and others, which reflect the relationships among the cell's outputs, states, and internal parameters.

The equations systematically express how each component's gradient is influenced by the errors propagated from the output layer back through the various layers of the Augmented LSTM. The equations also incorporate terms that link the gradients of the activated outputs with respect to their pre-activation values, ensuring that the training process effectively updates the model parameters.

Overall, the section provides a comprehensive framework for understanding the backward pass in the Augmented LSTM training process, highlighting the importance of each variable and its role in optimizing the network's performance.
