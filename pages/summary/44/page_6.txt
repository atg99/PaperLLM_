The paper discusses the numerical evaluation of nonlinear first-order non-homogeneous delay differential equations (DDEs) through discretization in time. Specifically, it applies the backward Euler discretization method to Equation 14, which involves defining the sampling time step (ΔT) and indexing the time samples (n). The equations are reformulated to express the relationships between the state, input signals, and their respective timed delays.

Key steps include approximating the derivative of the state signal, leading to the formulation of a difference equation. By setting the delay (τ0) equal to the time step (ΔT), the equations are simplified, allowing for the storage and updating of signal values at each time step. This results in a transformation of the continuous-time equations into discrete sequences, which are expressed in terms of an index (n).

The discretized form of the original equation is presented as a nonlinear first-order difference equation. The paper further defines a matrix W, which facilitates the manipulation of the equations, enabling the representation of the state and input signals in a recursive format. Ultimately, this approach provides a framework for solving the initial DDEs numerically, accommodating various input and state signal combinations while maintaining stability and accuracy in the discretization process.
