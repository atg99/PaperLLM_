The paper discusses several key remarks regarding the implementation and limitations of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their unrolling techniques. 

Remark 1 highlights that the initial propositions and proofs do not encompass advanced RNN architectures, such as Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Attention Networks, which may limit their applicability to complex models like those involving multiple Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks.

Remark 2 emphasizes that while segment-level state signal subsequences are independent, input signal subsequences from different segments are not required to be independent. It notes that dependencies in input signals can be managed through appropriately trained hidden layer weights, thereby preserving the independence of state signal subsequences.

Remark 3 points out that there is no universally optimal method for converting Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) systems to Finite Impulse Response (FIR) systems. The approximation of IIR systems can only be evaluated concerning specific fidelity and performance measures. The effectiveness of approximation techniques is contingent on their adaptability to the data distribution and the quality metrics relevant to the application. The unrolling technique, while straightforward for approximating RNNs, introduces artificial discontinuities due to step truncation. However, if the underlying data consists of independent segments, an unrolled RNN can be effectively trained to meet the desired output criteria. 

Remark 4 discusses the challenges of adhering to the independence assumption between output subsequences in practical applications. Often, subsequences exhibit inter-dependence, and the initial state of one may influence another. The paper suggests that empirical experimentation and statistical analysis can help identify suitable initial conditions that align with the input sequence generation process, allowing for effective subdivision into independent subsequences.

Overall, the remarks underscore the complexities and practical considerations of applying RNNs, particularly concerning independence assumptions, architecture limitations, and the implications of unrolling techniques.
