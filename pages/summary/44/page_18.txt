The paper discusses the stability and gradient behavior of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on the Jacobian matrix's magnitude derived from long-range interactions. It highlights that while RNNs are designed to be stable, issues arise with vanishing and exploding gradients during training, especially over long time windows. The stability condition requires all eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix to be between zero and one. If this condition is met, the gradients diminish over time; however, if any eigenvalue exceeds this threshold, the gradients can grow exponentially, leading to saturation or overflow in the state signal.

To address these challenges, the paper introduces the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architecture, which was developed to mitigate the vanishing gradients problem. LSTMs incorporate nonlinear, data-dependent controls that help maintain gradient flow during training. The design modifications separate the state signal update into two components: one dependent on the previous state and another influenced by the readout and input signals. This separation enhances the robustness and versatility of the system, allowing for more effective training and parameter updates.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the critical role of LSTMs in overcoming the inherent limitations of standard RNNs, enabling them to learn and infer effectively over extended sequences.
