The paper discusses a nonlinear ordinary delay differential equation (DDE) system, represented by Equation 9, which incorporates discrete delays in its formulation. This system is relevant in various scientific fields, including biology, chemistry, and engineering, where time delays are common in feedback processes involving sensors and actuators. The equation's time rate of change is influenced by three primary components: the state signal, the readout signal, and an external input, along with a constant bias term.

The use of the hyperbolic tangent as the warping function is justified due to its monotonic and negative-symmetric properties, which allow for regulation of its slope and ensure boundedness of the readout signals. This function's quasi-linear region is particularly beneficial for system parameter design and behavior interpretation in the small signal regime. Additionally, the memory aspects of the system, represented by the time delay terms, enhance the model's ability to capture causal and contextual information by integrating past values of the state, readout, and input signals.

The paper highlights the significance of time delays in neural networks, noting their intrinsic role in determining system dynamics. Historical research into recurrent networks has leveraged DDEs to model neuron interactions, contributing to advancements in continuous-time and discrete-time network training methods, particularly through Back Propagation and its extension, Back Propagation Through Time. This approach is essential for training the systems analyzed in the paper, facilitating the performance of various tasks by recurrent networks.
