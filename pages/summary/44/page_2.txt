The paper addresses a gap in the understanding of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks by providing a comprehensive introductory text for students and practitioners. It emphasizes a clear and consistent notation to demystify these systems and aims to foster a deeper understanding of both the "how" and "why" behind RNN and LSTM architectures.

The focus begins with RNNs, as LSTMs are a specific type of RNN. The paper derives the canonical RNN equations from differential equations, highlighting the importance of these equations in both theoretical understanding and practical applications of neural networks. The authors argue that grounding RNNs in differential equations provides insights into their discrete counterparts, which are critical for modeling real-world scenarios.

The document outlines several key requirements for discussing the Vanilla LSTM system and its extensions, including intuitiveness, completeness, generality, illustrative clarity, modularity, and the use of vector notation. By addressing these aspects, the tutorial aims to serve as a thorough introduction that captures all essential details, making it accessible for those new to the topic.

The structure of the paper is organized into several sections: Section II provides foundational background on RNNs; Section III discusses RNN unrolling; Section IV details the training mechanism through Back Propagation Through Time; Section V constructs the Vanilla LSTM cell; Section VI elaborates on the Vanilla LSTM; Section VII introduces the Augmented LSTM system; and Section VIII concludes with a summary and future project suggestions. 

Overall, the paper seeks to present a cohesive and educational resource for understanding RNNs and LSTMs, fostering both theoretical knowledge and practical application within the machine learning community.
