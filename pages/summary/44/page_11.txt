The paper discusses the sampling process of an RNN sequence using a full N-samples-long window, represented by the equation w[n] = u[n] - u[n-N]. This process is broken down into components, where the sequence is expressed in terms of the sum of wm[n] across M segments. Each segment wm[n] is defined as a rectangular window of size Km, indicating that extracting a segment from the overall output sequence v[n] involves multiplying v[n] by wm[n]. 

This multiplication yields a subsequence vm[n] that retains non-zero values only within a specific index range determined by j(m), aligning with previously established equations. Additionally, an invertible map Q is introduced to transform the readout signals ⟨r[n]⟩ into observable output signals ⟨y[n]⟩. An objective function L is also defined to measure the deviation of the observable output from the desired ground truth output values based on the input data. 

Finally, all parameters of the standard RNN system are consolidated under a single symbol Θ for simplification. This framework provides a structured approach to understanding the RNN's functioning and the relationships between its components.
