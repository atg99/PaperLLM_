In this section, the paper derives the Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) framework from differential equations. It begins with a general nonlinear first-order non-homogeneous ordinary differential equation that describes the evolution of a d-dimensional state signal vector, ⃗s(t), over time. This evolution is expressed as the sum of a vector-valued function of time, ⃗f(t), and a constant vector, ⃗ϕ.

A specific form of ⃗f(t) is presented as a function of both the state vector and an input signal vector, ⃗x(t). The relationship is encapsulated in a system of equations that emerges in various scientific fields. The discussion further extends to the representation of input signals displayed on a screen, transitioning from continuous to discrete forms, ultimately leading to the construction of the input vector, ⃗x(t).

The paper also introduces the "Additive Model," a specific case of ⃗f(t) that combines several time-dependent vector functions, which influences the rate of change of neuronal activities. This model has been foundational in neural network research and has been adapted in numerous ways, including considerations of delays and saturation effects. Its applications span across various domains such as vision, decision making, and memory.

The section also details a saturating version of the Additive Model, defining its constituent terms and illustrating how the readout signal vector, ⃗r(t), is derived from the state signal vector through a nonlinear activation function. This framework underlines the connection between biological processes and computational models, reinforcing the significance of the Additive Model in both neuroscience and engineering contexts.
