This section focuses on deriving the necessary equations for training a Vanilla LSTM network cell through Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT) over K steps. The process begins by computing two backward-moving gradient sequences: ⃗χ[n], which represents the total partial derivative of the objective function E with respect to the observable value signal ⃗v[n], and ⃗ψ[n], which represents the total partial derivative of E concerning the state signal ⃗s[n]. The authors emphasize the importance of modular design by anchoring the chain rule at the cell's border, which simplifies the computation of total partial derivatives.

The backward-moving recursion of these gradient sequences indicates that their values at index n depend on those at index n+1, relying on initial conditions. Once ⃗χ[n] and ⃗ψ[n] are established, they facilitate the calculation of total partial derivatives for various accumulation nodes, ultimately contributing to the model parameters' updates. The contributions from different steps (0 to K-1) are summed to derive the total partial derivatives of E with respect to these parameters.

During inference, only the input signal ⃗x[n] and the value signal ⃗v[n] are externally accessible, while other intermediate signals remain internal. The cell's value signal can be transformed to generate the output signal ⃗y[n], which may involve linear transformations followed by decision functions such as softmax. Additionally, preprocessing techniques can enhance the input data representation, such as converting one-hot vectors into more compact forms or utilizing non-causal input filters to improve context awareness.

The section proceeds with the derivation of the warping functions' derivatives, establishing a foundation for expressing ⃗χ[n] as the total partial derivative of E with respect to ⃗v[n]. This approach not only aids in modular implementation but also simplifies the training equations for the Vanilla LSTM cell.
