The paper discusses the derivation and modification of a standard recurrent neural network (RNN) system, particularly focusing on the stability and gradient issues that arise in traditional RNNs. It highlights that the matrix W_s in the RNN is a diagonal matrix with positive fractions, allowing for the simplification of the gating mechanism. By setting W_s equal to the identity matrix, the contribution from the previous state remains fractional, which helps stabilize the system.

The paper introduces a control readout gate, g_cr[n], to modulate the readout signal, v[n], thus controlling the amount of readout signal that influences the cell's observable value. This modification prevents the direct connection between state signals from causing gradient issues, as the control readout gate determines how much of the readout signal is utilized. The update term is further refined by incorporating a control input gate, g_cx[n], which allows for a flexible combination of the readout signal and the external input.

The dynamic range of the cell's value signal is managed through a saturating warping nonlinearity, G_d(z), ensuring that the contributions from the input signal do not destabilize the system. The final update equation incorporates these modifications, emphasizing the roles of the control gates in maintaining stability and flexibility within the RNN architecture. Overall, the paper outlines a systematic approach to enhancing the traditional RNN framework to address common issues like vanishing and exploding gradients.
