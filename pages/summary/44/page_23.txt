The paper discusses the computation of fractional control signals in a Vanilla Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, specifically focusing on three key signals: gcs[n], gcu[n], and gcr[n]. These signals are derived from the data signals available at each step, with gcs[n] and gcu[n] relying on the previous state signal s[n-1] and the current input signal x[n], while gcr[n] also incorporates the current state signal s[n].

The equations for these signals are presented as linear combinations of the available data signals, allowing for straightforward application of the backpropagation through time (BPTT) algorithm. The model parameters associated with these equations are trained, enabling the gate functions gcs[n], gcu[n], and gcr[n] to adapt to the variations in the training data, thus enhancing the LSTM's ability to process information effectively.

The gates play a crucial role in mitigating issues arising from artificial boundaries in input sequences, which can be caused by implicit truncation during the unrolling of the LSTM. This capability helps the model to maintain robustness against imperfections in external data, resulting in high-quality output sequences.

The paper concludes the derivation of the Vanilla LSTM mechanism, outlining its internal functioning over K steps. It emphasizes the dual objectives of data processing and control within the LSTM cell, where candidate data signals are modulated by control signals that determine the extent of propagation to subsequent nodes. Overall, the LSTM architecture is designed to efficiently manage the flow of information, making it a powerful tool in sequence modeling tasks.
