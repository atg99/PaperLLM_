The paper discusses the application of the chain rule to derive total partial derivatives of an objective function in the context of recurrent neural networks (RNNs). It specifically focuses on the relationship between the readout signal and the state signal, providing equations that describe how these derivatives progress in reverse order with respect to an index n. The total partial derivatives, represented in Equations 82 and 84, indicate that they form sequences that correspond to the forward direction of computations in RNNs.

The discussion emphasizes the necessity of initializing the error gradient sequence, as shown in Equation 85, to facilitate the training process. The paper explains that the model parameters are shared across all steps of the unrolled RNN, which leads to the formulation of the total derivative of the objective function with respect to these parameters, encapsulated in Equation 90. This derivative incorporates contributions from all time steps, making it essential for optimization via Gradient Descent.

Additionally, the paper highlights that the error gradient signal flows backward through the network, which allows the gradient at a specific state signal to influence earlier states. This is critical for capturing long-range dependencies in the data. The retention of gradient information from earlier steps can significantly impact the training process, as outlined by Proposition 1, which suggests that the gradients can adjust model parameters based on distant samples. 

Finally, the paper expands the recursion in Equation 84 to relate gradients from different indices, demonstrating how gradients at later steps can affect earlier ones, thereby reinforcing the importance of long-range interactions in RNN training.
