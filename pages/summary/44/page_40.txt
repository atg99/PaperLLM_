The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, emphasizing their foundational principles and practical applications. It begins by deriving the canonical formulation of RNNs from delay differential equations, relevant across various scientific fields. The authors demonstrate the connection between RNN unrolling and the approximation of Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) systems, establishing conditions for effective sequence learning.

The training process of standard RNNs using Back Propagation Through Time is discussed, highlighting the challenges of vanishing and exploding gradients encountered in Gradient Descent optimization. To address these issues, the authors transition to LSTM networks, detailing their construction and providing a thorough overview of both the forward and backward passes in the context of LSTM cells.

A key contribution of this work is its pedagogical approach, which clarifies the RNN and LSTM systems from a Signal Processing perspective and offers a formal derivation of the unrolling process. The authors introduce two novel extensions to the Vanilla LSTM: convolutional non-causal input context windows and an external input gate, enhancing the LSTM's capabilities.

Future work is suggested, including the implementation of the Augmented LSTM in high-performance computing settings and evaluating its effectiveness in applications like language representation for question answering and customer support automation. The authors advocate for comparative studies against baseline models, such as Vanilla LSTM and Transformer networks, to assess the impact of the new features.
