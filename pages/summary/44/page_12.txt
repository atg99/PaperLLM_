This paper proposes Proposition 1, which asserts that within a standard RNN framework, there exists a parameterization Θ that allows the objective function L to be optimized for a sequence of N samples. It is assumed that the ground truth output sequence can be divided into independent segment-level subsequences. Under these conditions, a single RNN cell, unrolled for a definable number of steps (Km), is sufficient for optimizing L and inferring outputs from unseen data.

The proof begins by establishing that L measures the system's performance across training, validation, and testing phases, and that when L is near optimum, the RNN's output closely approximates the ground truth output within a specified tolerance. The output sequence can be segmented similarly to the ground truth, resulting in segment-level outputs that are non-zero only within certain indices. 

The mutual independence of the segment-level ensembles allows L to be expressed as a combination of independent components, implying that the RNN's observable outputs must also track the ground truth at both the overall sequence and segment levels. The paper further establishes that transformations of these outputs are also functions of independent random variables, leading to the conclusion that the RNN's outputs and states across segments are independent. This independence necessitates that the initial conditions of state signal subsequences for different segments must not be influenced by one another. 

In summary, the proposition emphasizes the capability of a single RNN cell to effectively model and infer outputs from independent segments of data when appropriately parameterized and unrolled.
