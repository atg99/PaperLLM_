The paper discusses the total partial derivatives of the objective function E in the context of the Vanilla LSTM cell, specifically focusing on three intermediate variables and one border variable. It defines these derivatives as follows: ρ[n], γ[n], α[n], and ψ[n], with ψ[n] being particularly significant as it represents the total partial derivative with respect to the state signal s[n]. This derivative is crucial for parameter updates in the Vanilla LSTM network, making it a key component of the error gradient sequence.

The paper derives backward pass equations using the chain rule, leading to various expressions that involve the derivatives of E with respect to different variables. Notably, the equations show how the derivatives interact with each other and contribute to the overall error gradient. The contributions from the next time step, n+1, are captured in the terms fχ[n+1] and fψ[n+1].

Additionally, the paper highlights the concept of accumulation derivatives, which are proportional to the total partial derivatives of E with respect to model parameters. It notes that during training, these accumulation derivatives can become excessively large, prompting the need for clipping them to a range between -1 and 1 to mitigate numerical issues. This clipping ensures stability in training while maintaining the effectiveness of the control and data warping functions.
