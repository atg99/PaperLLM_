The paper discusses a mechanism for enhancing recurrent neural networks (RNNs) by incorporating control gates that modulate the contributions of previous state signals and current input signals. Specifically, the state signal at step n, represented as s[n], is influenced by two components: the retained state from the previous step, denoted by Fs(s[n-1]), and the update information from the previous readout signal and the current input, indicated by Fu(r[n-1], x[n]). The blending of these components occurs in equal proportions, but this can be adjusted through gate signals gcs[n] and gcu[n]. 

These gate signals allow fine-tuned control over how much of the previous state and current update are incorporated into the new state signal. The values of these gate signals are constrained between 0 and 1, enabling a fractional contribution from each component. The paper emphasizes the importance of these control mechanisms in optimizing the processing of information in RNNs, particularly in scenarios where the entire dataset is available beforehand, allowing for non-causal processing. This approach can enhance performance in various applications, including audio, speech, and text analysis, by leveraging future context to inform current decisions.
