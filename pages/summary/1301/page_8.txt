The paper presents a novel data augmentation technique called GridMask, which enhances object detection and semantic segmentation performance across various models. In experiments on the COCO2017 dataset, the Faster-RCNN-50-FPN model demonstrated improvements in mean Average Precision (mAP) with the application of GridMask, achieving a maximum increase of 1.8%. The best results were observed with a probability parameter (p) of 0.7, yielding a mAP of 39.2%. Similarly, Faster-RCNN-X101-FPN showed a 1.4% improvement with GridMask.

The authors also evaluated GridMask's effectiveness in semantic segmentation on the Cityscapes dataset using the PSPNet model. The results indicated that GridMask outperformed baseline models, with mIoU improvements of 0.8% for PSPNet50 and 0.4% for PSPNet101. Notably, the baseline models already employed strong augmentation strategies, highlighting GridMask's robustness as an additional augmentation method.

Furthermore, the paper explores the versatility of GridMask beyond data augmentation by integrating it with other regularization techniques such as Mixup, achieving state-of-the-art results on the ImageNet dataset.

In conclusion, GridMask offers a structured approach to information dropping, resulting in consistent performance enhancements across different tasks and models. The authors advocate for further exploration of structured information dropping methods, which may have broader implications for future advancements in data augmentation and reinforcement learning.
