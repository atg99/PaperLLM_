The paper presents a detailed analysis of model accuracy improvements on the CIFAR10 dataset using various augmentation techniques, particularly focusing on the proposed GridMask method. The results, summarized in Table 3, show that GridMask consistently enhances the performance of multiple models, achieving the highest accuracy of 96.54% for ResNet18, an increase of 1.26% over the baseline. Similar improvements are noted for WideResNet-28-10 and ShakeShake-26-32, with accuracies of 97.24% and 97.20%, respectively.

The study emphasizes the effectiveness of GridMask in directing model attention to larger, salient regions within images, which enhances generalization capabilities. This is illustrated with an example where GridMask successfully recognizes both important objects in an image, unlike the baseline and AutoAugment methods which focus on only one.

In the ablation study, the impact of hyperparameters on GridMask is explored. The optimal settings for the parameter r were found to be 0.6 for ImageNet and 0.4 for CIFAR10, indicating a need for more information retention on complex datasets to prevent underfitting. Conversely, the paper notes that less information retention is beneficial for simpler datasets to mitigate overfitting.

Overall, the findings indicate that GridMask is a significant advancement in data augmentation techniques, outperforming existing methods and contributing to state-of-the-art results in model accuracy across various architectures.
