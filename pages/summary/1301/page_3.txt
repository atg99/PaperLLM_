The paper introduces GridMask, a novel data augmentation technique that enhances image processing by randomly removing specific pixel regions from input images. Unlike traditional methods that either remove continuous areas or random pixels, GridMask employs a binary mask that creates disconnected pixel sets, effectively reserving certain areas while deleting others. 

The mask is defined using parameters (r, d, δx, δy), where r determines the keep ratio of the image, d defines the length of a unit, and δx and δy specify the distances from the first intact unit to the image boundaries. The keep ratio, k, is crucial for balancing between overfitting and underfitting, and it is mathematically related to r. The length of the dropped squares, l, is proportional to d and r, allowing for variability in the augmentation process while maintaining a consistent keep ratio during training.

GridMask has been shown to outperform existing unsupervised augmentation methods, including those proposed by AutoAugment, establishing it as a new baseline for data augmentation strategies. The paper emphasizes the efficiency and generalizability of GridMask, supported by extensive experimental validation.
