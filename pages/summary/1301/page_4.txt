The paper discusses a new data augmentation method that demonstrates improved performance in various computer vision tasks, particularly in image classification using the ImageNet dataset. The authors present statistical evidence showing that their method has a significantly lower probability of failure compared to existing methods such as Cutout and HaS, especially as the size of the removal squares increases. 

The authors introduce a systematic approach to selecting mask parameters, δx and δy, which allows for comprehensive coverage of possible scenarios during augmentation. They emphasize that achieving a balance between information deletion and retention is critical to minimizing failure cases, defined as instances where 99 percent of an object is either removed or preserved.

Extensive experiments reveal that their proposed augmentation method, which incorporates GridMask, consistently improves the accuracy of various models, including ResNet50, ResNet101, and ResNet152, when evaluated on ImageNet. The authors report increases in accuracy of 1.4%, 1.1%, and 1.4% for these models, respectively, demonstrating the effectiveness of their approach without the need for color augmentation. The paper concludes that their method outperforms both Cutout and HaS, establishing it as a superior choice for data augmentation in image classification tasks.
