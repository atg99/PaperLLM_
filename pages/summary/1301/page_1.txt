In this paper, the authors introduce a novel data augmentation technique called "GridMask." This method focuses on information removal to enhance performance across various computer vision tasks, demonstrating superior results compared to existing techniques like AutoAugment, which relies on computationally intensive reinforcement learning for policy determination.

The introduction highlights the importance of data augmentation in training deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) due to their substantial parameter counts, which necessitate large datasets to avoid overfitting. Current augmentation strategies fall into three categories: spatial transformations, color distortions, and information dropping. The authors emphasize the latter, which includes methods like random erasing and cutout, asserting that these techniques help CNNs learn from less sensitive information and enhance model robustness.

The authors argue that successful information dropping requires a careful balance between deleting and preserving regions of an image. Excessive deletion can lead to loss of crucial context, while too much preservation may result in trivial images that do not enhance robustness. They posit that existing methods often struggle to achieve this balance effectively.

Through extensive experiments on datasets such as ImageNet, COCO2017, and Cityscapes, the authors demonstrate that GridMask consistently improves model performance, as evidenced by improved accuracy and mAP scores across various architectures. Their findings underscore the effectiveness and generality of the GridMask method, establishing it as a significant advancement in data augmentation techniques for computer vision applications.
