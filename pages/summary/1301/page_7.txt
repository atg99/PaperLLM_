This paper investigates the effectiveness of GridMask, a data augmentation technique, on various datasets including ImageNet, CIFAR10, and COCO. The experiments demonstrate that different configurations of the parameter d can significantly influence network performance, enhancing robustness through diversity.

The study introduces variations of GridMask, including reversed GridMask, which retains what is typically dropped. Results show that reversed GridMask outperforms other augmentation methods, confirming the advantages of structured information dropping. Conversely, a random GridMask variant, which randomly drops information, leads to decreased accuracy, indicating that structured approaches are more effective.

In object detection tasks using the COCO dataset, the authors utilize Faster-RCNN with GridMask, achieving improved accuracy compared to baseline augmentation methods. The paper notes that while baseline models face overfitting issues with extended training, models employing GridMask demonstrate resilience against overfitting, resulting in superior performance.

Overall, the findings support the conclusion that GridMask and its variations are beneficial for training deep learning models, enhancing robustness and accuracy across different datasets.
