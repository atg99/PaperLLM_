The paper introduces a new information removal method called GridMask, which addresses the limitations of previous strategies like Cutout and HaS in image data augmentation. Traditional methods often delete either a continuous region or select random squares, leading to an imbalance in the coverage of objects in images. GridMask improves upon these approaches by utilizing structured dropping of uniformly distributed square regions. This method allows for better control over the density and size of removed areas, resulting in a statistically improved balance between the two conditions.

Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of GridMask across various tasks. For instance, in image classification on the ImageNet dataset, GridMask enhances the accuracy of ResNet50 from 76.5% to 77.9%, outperforming Cutout and HaS as well as AutoAugment. Additionally, it shows significant improvements in object detection and semantic segmentation tasks on COCO2017 and Cityscapes datasets, respectively.

The paper also reviews related work in regularization techniques for neural networks, highlighting the importance of methods like dropout, Mixup, and CutMix, as well as data augmentation strategies that enhance model generalization without altering network structures. The authors assert that GridMask is a simple yet effective policy that can be integrated into existing CNN models, showcasing its high effectiveness and versatility across diverse datasets and tasks. The code for GridMask is made available for further research and application.
