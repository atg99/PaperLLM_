The paper presents a comparison of action recognition methods on the NTU RGB+D 60 and 120 datasets, highlighting the performance of the proposed P4Transformer. In Table 2, various methods are evaluated based on their accuracy in recognizing actions from skeleton and depth data. P4Transformer achieves the highest accuracy across all evaluation settings, surpassing other state-of-the-art techniques such as 3DV-PointNet++ by a margin of 4% in the cross-setup evaluation on NTU RGB+D 120. 

The NTU RGB+D 60 dataset contains 56,000 videos with 60 action categories, while the NTU RGB+D 120 dataset is an extension with 114,000 videos and 120 categories. Both datasets utilize Kinect v2 for video capture, and evaluations are conducted in cross-subject and cross-view settings. 

The paper also emphasizes the computational efficiency of P4Transformer, as shown in Table 3, where it significantly reduces the running time per video compared to 3DV-PointNet++. Specifically, P4Transformer takes only 865 ms per video, a reduction of 1903 ms compared to 3DV-PointNet++. 

The authors visualize the attention weights of the transformer, indicating its ability to focus on relevant regions in the video frames, thus supporting the effectiveness of P4Transformer in capturing the spatio-temporal structures of point cloud videos. Overall, the results demonstrate the superiority and efficiency of the proposed method in the realm of 3D action recognition.
