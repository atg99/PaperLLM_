The paper discusses the implementation of a P4Transformer for 3D action recognition and 4D semantic segmentation, utilizing a multi-head attention mechanism to enhance the learning capabilities of the transformer architecture. The multi-head attention allows for processing multiple self-attention operations simultaneously, which are then concatenated to form the final output. The architecture is designed to capture both appearance and motion information from point cloud videos through a series of point 4D convolution layers followed by stacked transformer layers.

For 3D action recognition, the P4Transformer processes point cloud videos by first encoding local spatio-temporal features, followed by the application of transformer layers to extract global features for action predictions. In contrast, the 4D semantic segmentation task focuses on point-level classification, employing multiple convolution layers to reduce the number of points before processing with the transformer. Feature propagation layers are integrated to interpolate point features, with a specific method for weight calculation based on inverse distance.

Experimental results are presented for 3D action recognition on the MSR-Action3D dataset, demonstrating the effectiveness of the P4Transformer compared to other existing methods. The model shows competitive accuracy across various input frame configurations, emphasizing its capability in video-level classification. The paper highlights the limitations of skeleton-based and depth-based methods, positioning the P4Transformer as a robust alternative for complex action recognition tasks.
