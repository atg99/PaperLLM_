The paper presents the Point 4D Transformer (P4Transformer) network designed to enhance 3D action recognition from raw point cloud videos by capturing spatio-temporal correlations. Key components of the P4Transformer include a point 4D convolution and a transformer. The point 4D convolution processes the input to extract spatio-temporal local structures, allowing for effective subsampling of frames and points. The transformer then utilizes self-attention mechanisms on these compact representations to analyze appearance and motion information throughout the video.

The study investigates the impact of various parameters on model performance, including the number of transformer layers and heads. It finds that while increasing the number of layers generally improves accuracy, excessively deep networks can lead to issues like vanishing gradients. Similarly, the number of attention heads can enhance performance up to a point; too many heads reduce the feature dimension per head, which can detract from the model's effectiveness.

Moreover, the research compares frame-level and video-level self-attention, highlighting that video-level self-attention significantly outperforms frame-level attention due to its ability to account for point flow across the entire video, thus improving temporal modeling. The length of video clips is also examined, revealing that longer clips can help models capture essential frames for action reasoning, reducing noise and confusion.

In conclusion, the P4Transformer effectively models point cloud videos, as demonstrated by extensive experiments, and is supported by the Agency for Science, Technology and Research under its AME Programmatic Funding Scheme.
