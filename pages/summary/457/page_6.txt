In this section of the paper, Smith et al. detail their implementations of two segmentation methods: the Frangi Vesselness enhancement filter and a U-Net convolutional neural network (CNN). 

For the Frangi Vesselness method, the authors selected the filter due to its effectiveness in analyzing structures similar to blood vessels, which aligns with the root structures they aimed to segment. The implementation was done using Python and the scikit-image library. Vesselness is quantified for each pixel, and segmentation is achieved by thresholding the filter's output. Noise reduction is performed through connected component analysis, and optimal parameters are determined using the Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES), with the objective of maximizing the F1 score of the segmentations.

The U-Net implementation, also developed in Python but with PyTorch, utilized a modified architecture to accommodate RGB input. Group normalization was applied after ReLU activations to mitigate issues arising from small batch sizes, and dropout was omitted to enhance model performance. The network processes image tiles of size 572x572, outputting segmentations for a central 388x388 region. Randomly selected tiles containing roots were used during training, with a maximum of 40 tiles extracted to address class imbalance. The training employed mini-batch stochastic gradient descent with a batch size of 4 to optimize GPU memory usage. Image tiles were normalized to improve convergence, while data augmentation techniques were employed to enhance the dataset artificially.

Overall, the authors emphasize the significance of both methods in accurately segmenting root structures, highlighting their careful consideration of model architecture, training strategies, and preprocessing steps to improve performance.
