In the study by Smith et al., a combination of Dice loss and cross-entropy was employed, with the latter weighted at 0.3, based on preliminary trials indicating enhanced performance over using either loss function alone. The optimization process utilized Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with Nesterov momentum, setting momentum at 0.99 as per the original U-Net implementation. An initial learning rate of 0.01 was determined through trial and error, with weight decay set at 1×10−5. A learning rate schedule was implemented, reducing the learning rate by a factor of 0.3 every 30 epochs. The authors avoided adaptive optimization methods like Adam due to concerns about generalization.

The results demonstrated successful segmentation of roots in both training and validation datasets, as well as a held-out test set. The U-Net outperformed the Frangi filter system across all metrics, including F1 score, recall, and precision. Notably, the training F1 score improved continuously, while the validation F1 score plateaued around epoch 40, indicating potential overfitting to training data. Performance metrics for the best U-Net model indicated high accuracy, with an F1 score of 0.769 on the training set and 0.692 on the validation set. For the held-out test set, U-Net achieved an accuracy of 0.997 and an F1 score of 0.701, while the Frangi system had an accuracy of 0.996 and an F1 score of 0.462.
