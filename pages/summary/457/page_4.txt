In this section of the paper by Smith et al., the authors detail their methodology for selecting and annotating images for analysis of root intensity in rhizotron studies. They began by excluding photos with excessive equipment that could interfere with analysis, narrowing down from 3300 original images to a more manageable set. Specific panels (3, 6, 9, 12, 15, and 18) were excluded due to equipment presence, and panel 1 was discarded for not being fully covered with soil. The authors aimed to ensure a representative sample by considering depth distribution from the 2016 rhizotron images when selecting images for annotation.

For annotation, 50 images were chosen based on the annotator's availability, and these were cropped to focus on a specific area, removing parts that included the metal frame. A trained agronomist conducted a detailed per-pixel annotation, labeling root pixels, which varied significantly per image. The authors explained the data splitting process for training a neural network, where images are divided into training, validation, and test sets. They randomly selected 10 images for the test set, ensuring a range of panel heights was included, but acknowledging that some heights were omitted.
