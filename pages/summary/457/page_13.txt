The paper discusses the application of transfer learning and the feasibility of a system for analyzing root images from various plant species using a fine-tuned U-Net architecture. Previous studies have shown that pre-training U-Net on diverse datasets can significantly reduce training time and mitigate overfitting, suggesting that using images different from the target root images may enhance performance. However, another study indicated that training from scratch could yield comparable results to transfer learning, emphasizing efficiency over model performance in some cases.

Challenges in the current implementation include gaps in CNN outputs when roots are obscured by soil, which could potentially be addressed by methods like those proposed in other studies to improve root length estimates and provide detailed root architecture information. While the Frangi filter provides a simpler alternative for image processing, its implementation was found to be slow, necessitating modifications for optimization. The U-Net model, while requiring GPU for training, can be adapted for CPU inference, and methods exist to compress its parameters significantly without substantial accuracy loss, potentially increasing accessibility for researchers with limited hardware.
