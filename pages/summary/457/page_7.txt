In this section, the authors discuss their methodology for image data augmentation and loss function implementation in training convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for image classification. They utilized color jittering with specific parameters for brightness, contrast, saturation, and hue to enhance the dataset's diversity. Elastic grid deformation was also employed, parameterized by a Gaussian distribution's standard deviation and an intensity control factor. Unlike previous studies, they introduced an interpolation coefficient to dynamically adjust these parameters, ensuring realistic image deformations.

The authors also addressed the challenge of class imbalance in their training dataset, where only a small percentage of pixels represented the target class. To mitigate this issue, they employed Dice loss, which emphasizes the minority class's errors, thereby improving the model's ability to learn from imbalanced data. The Dice coefficient, used to measure segmentation quality, was converted into a loss function to be minimized during training. Overall, the augmentation techniques and loss function were designed to enhance the model's performance on unseen data while addressing the challenges posed by class imbalance.
