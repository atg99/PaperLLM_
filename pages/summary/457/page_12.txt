The current study primarily focused on segmentation methods rather than directly converting segmentation data to root length measurements. The employed technique involved skeletonization and pixel counting, which poses a limitation as root length estimates may vary based on root orientation. The study acknowledges the need for improved annotation quality, highlighting that even high-quality segmentation can exhibit significant errors due to annotation issues. Previous research indicated substantial variability in human segmentation ratings, suggesting a similar error level in root annotations that could be mitigated through enhanced annotation methods. 

One proposed solution for improving annotation quality is to aggregate annotations from different experts using a majority vote algorithm, though care must be taken to avoid overestimating performance. Additionally, the study suggests exploring methods to minimize the impact of errors near annotation borders, as these often stem from ambiguous regions. 

The authors excluded non-root images and stickers from their training data to address class imbalance but recognize that excluding such elements could hinder the model's performance in real-world scenarios. Future research aims to assess the segmentation system's performance on images of different crop species and experimental setups, leveraging data augmentation and transfer learning to enhance outcomes. Notably, a strong correlation was found between root length indices and segmented root lengths, indicating the potential validity of the segmentation approach.
