In this section of the paper, the authors detail their methodology for developing and evaluating a model designed to segment root pixels in images. Initially, they excluded two images from a set of 40, resulting in 38 images, which were then divided into training and validation datasets based on root pixel counts. This division ensured that both datasets contained a representative range of root intensities.

To assess the model's performance, the authors chose the F1 score as their primary metric, valuing precision and recall equally. Precision refers to the accuracy of predicted root pixels, while recall measures the model's ability to identify actual root pixels. A high F1 score indicates effective segmentation, which was calculated using the training and validation sets. The final model was then tested on a separate test set to evaluate its performance on unseen data, with accuracy also reported as the ratio of correctly predicted pixels to total pixels.

Additionally, the authors employed a method to estimate root length from segmentation outputs using the scikit-image skeletonize function. This approach allowed them to compare their model's performance with the traditional line-intersect method, measuring the correlation between root lengths derived from both techniques. They calculated the coefficient of determination (r2) for the root lengths across 867 images, excluding 25 images that were part of the training dataset. Visual annotations were used to train the U-Net convolutional neural network, which aimed to improve the accuracy of root segmentation.
