The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in root detection and measurement techniques, particularly focusing on the limitations of the line-intersect method traditionally used in rhizotron studies. This method, while effective in counting roots, does not provide detailed information about root architecture, including traits such as branching and growth metrics. Attempts to automate root detection, such as the "RootFly" software, still require manual supervision and are time-consuming, which limits their practicality in extensive studies.

To improve upon existing methods, the paper highlights the potential of fully automatic root segmentation systems that could save time and provide detailed insights into root growth and structure. However, the low contrast between roots and soil has posed significant challenges in automation, with previous methods often only detecting young or unpigmented roots.

The authors propose using common RGB photographs for root segmentation, which would avoid the need for specialized equipment. They review previous work that employed feature extraction and traditional machine learning for root segmentation but note the difficulties associated with feature engineering, which requires extensive domain knowledge.

In contrast, deep learning, particularly using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), is presented as a more effective approach. CNNs automatically learn feature hierarchies from raw data, allowing for greater adaptability to various datasets without extensive manual programming. This decoupling of domain knowledge from technical expertise in machine learning facilitates collaboration between root researchers and deep learning practitioners.

The paper concludes that CNNs have demonstrated success in various recognition tasks and can effectively be applied to root segmentation, paving the way for more efficient and detailed root analysis in agricultural research.
