This paper discusses advancements in Visual Question Answering (VQA) systems, particularly focusing on the challenges posed by language priors and the introduction of datasets designed to test generalization capabilities. The authors highlight the limitations of existing VQA systems, which often rely heavily on language cues rather than visual reasoning, leading to significant performance drops when tested on the VQA-CP dataset that features a different distribution of question-answer pairs compared to the training set.

The paper categorizes approaches to improving VQA by incorporating human explanations into the systems. It presents two main trends: the first involves creating interpretable models that separate vision from language processing, exemplified by GVQA, while the second aligns the system's internal attention with human attention maps, as seen in works like HINT. The authors propose a novel approach that not only enhances sensitivity to important image regions identified by humans but also reduces the system's reliance on these regions for incorrect answers.

In the preliminaries section, the authors introduce their base model, the Bottom-Up Top-Down (UpDn) VQA system, which utilizes a dual attention mechanism—first detecting objects and attributes before applying attention to higher-level concepts. The UpDn model processes visual features and question vectors to predict answers through a multi-label regression framework, employing binary cross-entropy loss for training.

Lastly, the paper outlines the challenge of identifying influential objects that humans prioritize in their reasoning. The authors propose a method to construct a set of influential objects for each question-answer pair, acknowledging the complexity of isolating these objects due to the interconnectedness of visual and textual explanations. Despite potential noise in the proposed sets, the authors believe they can enhance the VQA system's performance by focusing on these critical regions.
