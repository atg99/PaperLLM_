The paper discusses a modified version of GradCAM to analyze the sensitivity of a model's predictions to specific object features. The modifications include removing ReLU units and not weighting gradients by feature vectors, as negative gradients can provide valuable insights against predictions. The authors propose a method to identify the most influential object for a given answer and strengthen its sensitivity through a sensitivity violation term. This term measures how much the sensitivity of one object surpasses another, ensuring that the most sensitive object from a proposal set is at least as sensitive as any object outside it.

Additionally, the paper addresses the challenge of incorrect dominant answers by criticizing these decisions based on the most influential object. The authors introduce a self-critical objective that minimizes the weighted sensitivities of incorrect answers with respect to the selected influential object. They also utilize a bucket of predictions that are more confident than the correct answer and weight the sensitivity gaps by the cosine distance between the GloVe embeddings of the answers. This approach allows for a more nuanced understanding of model behavior and aims to improve the accuracy of Visual Question Answering systems by focusing on influential objects and their relationships to the correct answers.
