The paper discusses a visual question answering (VQA) system that aims to improve the accuracy of predictions by analyzing the sensitivity of detected objects to the correct answer. The system utilizes a base UpDn VQA framework to identify objects within an image, such as a fork and knife near a cake, and then assesses the influence of these objects on the predicted answers. 

The approach is organized into three main methods for constructing a proposal object set: 

1. **Construction from Visual Explanations**: This method utilizes a dataset of image-question pairs to score objects based on human attention maps, selecting the most relevant objects based on their attention energy.

2. **Construction from Textual Explanations**: This involves extracting nouns from annotated textual explanations using part-of-speech tagging and selecting objects that have a high cosine similarity with these nouns.

3. **Construction from Questions and Answers**: This simpler method extracts nouns directly from the question-answer pairs to identify relevant objects without relying on external datasets.

The paper emphasizes a self-critical mechanism that aims to mitigate the dominance of common incorrect answers by reducing their sensitivity to the most influential objects. This is achieved through a two-step process: recognizing and strengthening the most influential objects, followed by criticizing the incorrect answers that are ranked higher than the correct one.

Overall, the methodology aims to enhance the VQA system's performance by focusing on the most relevant objects in the context of the question, thereby leading to more accurate predictions.
