In this paper, we introduce a self-critical approach aimed at enhancing visual question answering (VQA) systems by directly addressing the sensitivity of incorrect predictions to important visual regions. We demonstrate that existing VQA systems, such as UpDn, often mispredict common answers despite having valid reasoning based on visual cues. Our method penalizes the model for focusing on key regions when it predicts the wrong answer, thereby encouraging it to learn from its mistakes.

We explore three methodologies to identify significant visual regions influencing the model's predictions. The first approach utilizes human-annotated important regions, which, while accurate, requires substantial effort to generate. The second method leverages textual explanations from the VQA-X dataset to ground important objects in images. The third approach identifies relevant objects based solely on the question or answer without needing additional annotations.

Our evaluation on the VQA-CP dataset shows that our self-critical training method achieves a state-of-the-art performance of 47.7%. This includes 49.5% accuracy when using textual explanations from VQA-X, 49.1% with visual explanations from VQA-HAT, and 48.5% when relying on mentioned objects in questions and answers. 

This work highlights the importance of grounding VQA systems in visual content and demonstrates how self-critical learning can improve their performance by refining their focus on relevant regions in images.
