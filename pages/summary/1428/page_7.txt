The paper presents a comparative analysis of various Visual Question Answering (VQA) systems, focusing on the effectiveness of a self-critical reasoning (SCR) approach. The results reveal that the SCR method significantly enhances performance on the VQA-CP and VQA v2 datasets. In particular, the SCR approach outperforms state-of-the-art systems, achieving an overall score of 49.5% on VQA-CP when using human textual explanations, which is a 1.5% improvement over the HINT model.

The analysis shows that the SCR method effectively shifts the decision boundary towards a more accurate position, resulting in increased robustness and accuracy in test data. The performance improvements are most notable in the 'Yes/No' and 'Other' question categories, while the 'Num' category shows less improvement. The paper notes that the QA proposal object sets used for supervision contain a significant overlap with human explanations, indicating that while the QA-based approach is effective, it is not exhaustive.

Overall, the findings highlight the advantages of incorporating self-critical reasoning and human explanations in enhancing VQA system performance, contributing to advancements in the field.
