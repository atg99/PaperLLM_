In this section, the authors detail the implementation and training of their self-critical approach to Visual Question Answering (VQA) leveraging VQA-X explanations. They start by pre-training their UpDn VQA system on the VQA-CP dataset using a binary cross-entropy loss, optimizing with the Adam algorithm for up to 20 epochs with a fixed learning rate of 0.01 and a batch size of 384. The model is initialized with 1280 hidden units. Following pre-training, they fine-tune the system on the intersection of VQA-X and VQA-CP datasets to enhance object recognition, employing a different loss function with a reduced learning rate of 0.00001 for up to 15 epochs. The best influence strengthening loss weight is determined during this stage. The final fine-tuning uses a joint loss function for another 15 epochs with the same learning rate.

The implementation utilizes the UpDn system architecture, which employs a Faster R-CNN head with a ResNet-101 backbone for object detection, pre-trained on the Visual Genome dataset. The detection module identifies 1600 object categories and 400 attributes, applying non-maximum suppression for final outputs. Visual features are extracted for the top 36 objects per image. Questions undergo text pre-processing and are embedded using a GRU network and Glove vectors.

The experimental results section first presents findings from a synthetic dataset to demonstrate basic functionalities of the approach. It also covers performance on the VQA-CP dataset, where the training and testing distributions differ significantly, and includes results from the VQA v2 validation set. The authors compare their self-critical VQA performance against state-of-the-art systems and perform ablation studies to assess the impact of their influence strengthening and answer criticism strategies. They conclude the section with qualitative examples to showcase the effectiveness of their approach in addressing incorrect answers. 

In synthetic data experiments, the authors simulate a mixture of two Gaussian distributions, adjusting weights to ensure different training and testing distributions. They investigate the self-critical approach's effectiveness by varying the probability of drawing from each distribution and report positive outcomes, indicating that their method aids performance across different settings.
