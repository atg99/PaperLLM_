The paper presents an ablation study on the impact of different loss weights in a Visual Question Answering (VQA) system, specifically focusing on the UpDn model enhanced with a self-critical reasoning (SCR) approach. The study evaluates the performance of the VQA system on the VQA-CP v2 test dataset, comparing results across various configurations of influence-strengthening and self-critical loss weights.

Results show that the UpDn model achieves a baseline score of 39.7%. When incorporating the SCR method, significant improvements are observed, particularly with specific weight settings. For instance, using an influence-strengthening weight of 20 and a self-critical loss weight of 2000, the system attains a new state-of-the-art score of 49.5%. The findings suggest that the system's performance is robust across a range of loss weights, consistently enhancing VQA outcomes.

Additionally, the paper explores the size of the proposal influential object set, indicating that varying set sizes yields stable performance results, with the highest score of 49.2% achieved with a set size of 6. The analysis also emphasizes that the SCR method effectively addresses false sensitivity in predictions, contributing positively to the overall model performance. The results reinforce the approach's competitive edge against previous methods when training and testing data distributions align.
