The paper discusses improvements in Visual Question Answering (VQA) systems through a self-critical approach that reduces false sensitivity to influential objects in images. The authors analyze the sensitivity of answers to the most relevant objects and propose a method that enhances the identification of these objects, ultimately improving VQA performance.

Key findings include the reduction of the false sensitivity rate (FSR) from 35.5% to 20.4% and 19.6% after implementing self-critical training with different types of human explanations. The approach strengthens the influence of the correct answer's key object while decreasing the influence on incorrect answers, thereby mitigating false sensitivity.

The authors conclude that their self-critical method enables VQA systems to generalize better to test data with differing distributions of question-answer pairs and outperforms existing state-of-the-art systems on the VQA-CP dataset without relying on additional human explanations. Future work aims to integrate visual and textual explanations to further enhance VQA training, acknowledging the challenges posed by different types of noise in the proposal object sets.
