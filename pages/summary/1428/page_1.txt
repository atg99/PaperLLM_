This paper addresses the challenges faced by Visual Question Answering (VQA) systems, particularly their tendency to rely on superficial statistical correlations in training data, leading to poor generalization on test data with different question-answer distributions. The authors propose a self-critical training objective aimed at aligning visual explanations of correct answers with the most influential image regions, as determined by either human annotations or significant words in questions and answers. They evaluate their method on the VQA generalization task, specifically using the VQA-CP dataset, achieving state-of-the-art results of 49.5% with textual explanations and 48.5% with automatically annotated regions. 

The introduction highlights the limitations of existing VQA systems, which perform well on similar training and test distributions but struggle with varying distributions, often defaulting to common answers rather than reasoning based on visual content. The paper critiques previous approaches that encourage focusing on important image regions without discouraging the model from focusing on these regions when incorrect answers are produced. The authors emphasize the need for a more robust training methodology to improve the reliability of VQA systems in diverse scenarios.
