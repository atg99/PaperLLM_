The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of model performance across different rounds of the ANLI dataset, focusing on the impact of various training configurations on error rates. The results are summarized in Table 3, which outlines the performance of BERT, RoBERTa, and XLNet models on multiple datasets, including SNLI and MNLI. 

Key findings indicate that model error rates generally decrease as the rounds progress, although there was a slight increase in error rates in Round 3 due to the inclusion of more diverse contexts. Notably, while the overall error rate declined from approximately 20% in Round 1 to below 10% by Round 3, the study emphasizes that even state-of-the-art models remain vulnerable to untrained annotators, highlighting the ongoing challenges in model robustness.

The paper also details the average number of attempts required to identify a model error, which increased in later rounds, suggesting that the tasks became more challenging over time. Furthermore, it discusses the performance of base models, which consistently underperformed on the respective test sets, validating the design choice to expose model vulnerabilities that are broadly applicable rather than specific to individual architectures. 

Overall, the findings underscore the progress made in model training while acknowledging the persistent vulnerabilities that need to be addressed in future iterations.
