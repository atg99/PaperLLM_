This paper presents a novel approach to natural language understanding (NLU) by employing a human-and-model-in-the-loop training strategy to develop a new benchmark, ANLI, aimed at challenging state-of-the-art models. The methodology involves iterative rounds where annotators act as adversaries, identifying vulnerabilities in models that lead to misclassification, while ensuring that these examples are classifiable by humans. The study highlights the effectiveness of non-expert annotators in a gamified environment, demonstrating their creativity in discovering model weaknesses.

Over three rounds of data collection, the models improved their robustness, and the complexity of the test sets increased. Training on the newly gathered data resulted in state-of-the-art performance on existing NLI benchmarks. The ANLI benchmark is designed to address limitations of previous datasets and encourages further research by providing annotator explanations for misclassified examples and inference labels for the development set.

The authors acknowledge the potential cost and time trade-offs between dynamic adversarial data collection and static approaches. They argue that adversarially collected examples can still be valuable, even if not verified or successful in fooling models. The method is model-agnostic and can be adapted for various classification tasks, with the potential for future research to explore its application in generative contexts.

The paper concludes by emphasizing that the ANLI benchmark serves as a significant challenge for measuring progress in NLU and can be refreshed with new rounds of data collection as needed.
