The paper discusses the process of developing a natural language inference (NLI) system through iterative rounds of data collection and model training. It begins by presenting examples of hypotheses derived from various contexts, analyzing the reasoning behind the predictions made by the model, and the challenges faced in interpreting certain phrases or idioms.

The methodology involves using Mechanical Turk workers to generate hypotheses based on provided contexts and target labels, which are categorized as entailment, contradiction, or neutral. Annotators are given structured guidance to ensure clarity in their tasks, and the model predictions are used as feedback to refine the hypothesis generation process.

In the first round, a BERT-Large model trained on existing datasets was employed to sample short passages from Wikipedia, while the second round utilized a RoBERTa model, which was trained on a broader dataset including previous round data and additional NLI-related sources. The paper emphasizes the importance of constructing a balanced test set and the iterative nature of refining the model through human verification and feedback.

Overall, the research illustrates a systematic approach to enhancing NLI capabilities, highlighting the significance of accurate annotation and model training in achieving reliable predictions.
