The paper presents a novel dataset called Adversarial NLI (ANLI) developed through a human-and-model-in-the-loop training process termed HAMLET. This approach consists of four iterative steps: writing examples, obtaining model feedback, verifying examples, and retraining the model. The aim is to generate a challenging benchmark for natural language inference (NLI) that exposes the shortcomings of existing models. The authors collect three rounds of increasingly complex data, with each round providing annotator explanations to enhance understanding.

The contributions include the introduction of the ANLI dataset, which results in state-of-the-art performance across various NLI benchmarks when used for training. The dataset serves as a dynamic challenge to encourage ongoing model improvement and learning. Additionally, the authors provide a comprehensive analysis of the data, categorizing it by inference type to reveal model weaknesses and assess performance under stress tests.

In summary, the ANLI dataset, accessible online, represents a significant advancement in NLI research, fostering the development of more robust models through iterative human feedback and verification.
