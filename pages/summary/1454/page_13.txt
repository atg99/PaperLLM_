This paper investigates the performance of natural language inference (NLI) models on challenging datasets designed to expose biases learned during training. The authors focus on two specific test sets: SNLI-Hard, which targets hypothesis-only models, and NLI stress tests, which incorporate complex linguistic features such as antonyms and negations. The models were fine-tuned on the respective development sets to address potential domain mismatches, and results were compared with those from previous studies.

A linguistic analysis reveals that the ANLI dataset contains a higher incidence of difficult linguistic phenomena compared to popular datasets like FEVER and SNLI. Both ANLI and MultiNLI show similar proportions of long hypotheses and negations, but MultiNLI has more pronouns and quantifiers. Notably, ANLI includes more numerals and time-related terms, likely due to its construction from Wikipedia articles.

The paper also discusses dataset properties, including label distribution and inter-annotator agreement metrics, which indicate a strong level of agreement among annotators. Additionally, the authors present examples of the data collected and the user interface used for annotation, illustrating the methodology behind the dataset construction. Overall, the findings suggest that the dataset construction process evolves in complexity across rounds, particularly in the A3 context.
