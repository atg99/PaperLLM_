The findings in this analysis of a development set consisting of 500 examples reveal variations in reasoning types across three rounds of data collection. In particular, numerical and quantitative reasoning was predominant in the first two rounds, with a notable decline in round three. Round one exhibited the highest reliance on numerical reasoning at 38%, which decreased to 32% in round two and further dropped to 17% in round three. 

The analysis categorized reasoning types into several categories, including Reference & Names, Standard Inferences, Lexical Inference, and Tricky Inferences, with each displaying varying prevalence across rounds. For instance, Standard Inferences and Lexical Inferences increased from round one to round three, suggesting that models trained on more complex data exhibit improved performance. The quality of annotations remained high, with only 3-4% flagged for ambiguity.

The paper also contextualizes these findings within existing literature on dataset biases in machine learning, highlighting how biases can lead to models performing well without genuine reasoning capabilities. Previous studies indicated that various tasks, including visual question answering and natural language inference, are susceptible to biases that can be exploited, resulting in superficial model performance. The work discusses strategies like adversarial filtering to address these biases, emphasizing the importance of dynamic datasets that adapt to the evolving challenges faced by machine learning models. Overall, the study contributes to the understanding of reasoning processes in AI and the implications of dataset biases on model performance.
