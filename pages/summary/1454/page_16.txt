The paper presents an analysis of various datasets, specifically focusing on the Adversarial Natural Language Inference (ANLI) dataset alongside others such as SNLI and MNLI. It details the linguistic phenomena present in the development set sentences, highlighting the percentage occurrence of specific tags in contexts and hypotheses. The findings indicate that certain linguistic features, such as the use of conjunctions like "and" and "or," as well as numerical expressions, show a higher incidence in adversarially created hypotheses compared to those from traditional datasets.

Table 8 illustrates the percentages of sentences tagged with various linguistic phenomena, with bolded values indicating a significantly higher incidence in ANLI. The paper also provides a breakdown of label distributions across different rounds for the ANLI dataset, showing the number of examples in training, development, and testing splits.

The inter-annotator agreement is assessed using Fleiss’ kappa, with results indicating a strong level of agreement among annotators for ANLI as well as comparisons with SNLI and MNLI. The agreement percentages for the validators with the author label are also presented, showing a high level of consistency across the datasets.

Overall, the analysis underscores the unique characteristics of adversarial hypotheses in ANLI and evaluates the reliability of annotations across multiple datasets.
