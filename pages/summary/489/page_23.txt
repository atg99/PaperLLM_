The paper discusses the impact of the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of the weight matrix on the performance of Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods used to compute gradients in Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs). It emphasizes the importance of initializing the weight matrix with a small variance to prevent the system from entering the spin-glass phase at the start of learning. 

As learning progresses, the dynamics of the system are expected to transition towards a ferromagnetic phase, where the behavior can be categorized into two scenarios based on the eigenvalues of the weight matrix. In the first scenario, only one dominant eigenvalue is learned, leading to a system that behaves similarly to a ferromagnet. In the second scenario, multiple eigenvalues are significant, prompting questions about whether the system will condense along a single mode or combine several modes.

The analysis of the second scenario requires understanding the quenched averaging over matrices involved in the learning process. The results of this averaging depend on the kurtosis of the distribution used for the matrices. Three distinct behaviors are identified based on kurtosis:

1. For kurtosis equal to zero (e.g., Gaussian distribution), only the strongest mode is stable, and the system condenses along this mode.
2. For negative kurtosis (e.g., uniform or Bernoulli distributions), weaker modes may be metastable, but the system still condenses toward the strongest mode.
3. For positive kurtosis (e.g., sparse Bernoulli or Laplace distributions), the strongest mode becomes unstable against weaker ones, allowing the system to condense across multiple modes, reflecting a dual compositional phase.

The paper suggests that the statistical properties of the SVD of the weight matrix influence the recall phase of the RBM. Depending on the learning trajectory, the system may evoke a single macroscopic state or an equilibrium state comprised of multiple modes. The learning trajectory is illustrated with examples from both artificial and MNIST data, setting the stage for a discussion on pattern formation mechanisms in RBMs.
