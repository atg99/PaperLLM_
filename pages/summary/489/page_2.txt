This paper focuses on the Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM), a generative model initially designed for unsupervised tasks, with the potential for supervised learning. RBMs learn a latent representation of data to generate statistically similar new data, differentiating them from the training set. They gained prominence in the Machine Learning community due to their ability to learn complex datasets, such as MNIST, using the contrastive divergence algorithm. RBMs serve as universal approximators of discrete distributions and can be stacked into multilayer models, specifically deep Boltzmann machines.

Recent interest in RBMs arises from their applicability to both continuous and discrete variables, as well as the interpretability of hidden nodes. Despite their advantages, the learning process of RBMs remains poorly understood, primarily due to approximated gradient computations and the sensitivity of learning dynamics to changes in hyperparameters. The paper highlights the relevance of statistical physics in understanding the inference and learning processes in machine learning models, noting that the RBM can be viewed as a disordered Ising model on a bipartite graph. However, its phase diagram is complicated by a non-trivial coupling matrix influenced by the learning process, posing challenges for traditional analytical methods.
