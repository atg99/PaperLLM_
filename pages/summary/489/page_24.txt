The paper discusses the learning dynamics of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), particularly focusing on Gaussian-Gaussian RBMs. It highlights the relationship between the eigenvalues of the weight matrix and the likelihood function as new modes emerge during the learning process. Specifically, it presents an artificial dataset with four distinct Gaussian clusters and illustrates how the eigenmodes of the weight matrix evolve. The results indicate that the eigenvectors align with the principal directions of the dataset, confirming that the model captures the underlying structure effectively.

The authors propose a shift from traditional Monte-Carlo methods for gradient computation, such as contrastive divergence, to a mean-field (MF) approach using self-consistent equations to compute the learning dynamics. This method allows for a numerical integration of the MF theory, leading to insights about the learning curve of the weight matrix over time.

Additionally, the paper references prior work that connects the learning dynamics of a simple Bernoulli-Bernoulli RBM to spatial diffusion equations, setting the stage for further numerical investigations on the MNIST dataset. The findings suggest that early learned features can be interpreted through the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the weight matrix, while later stages exhibit significant changes, reflecting the dynamic nature of the learning process. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of eigenvalue behavior and likelihood progression in understanding RBM learning dynamics.
