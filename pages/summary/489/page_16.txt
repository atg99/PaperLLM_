The Bernoulli-Bernoulli Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) is a model commonly referenced in discussions about RBMs, characterized by both visible and hidden nodes taking binary values. The prior distributions for these nodes are defined as uniform over {0,1}, without free parameters. This model is particularly suited for discrete datasets, in contrast to the Gaussian-Bernoulli model, which is used for continuous data. 

The activation functions for the nodes are sigmoid functions, determining the probabilities of activation based on weighted inputs and biases. A limitation of the Bernoulli prior is its binary nature, which restricts the influence of features to either fully activated or not, lacking the ability to adjust the intensity of activation based on the hidden node's response. 

To address this limitation, the idea of duplicating hidden nodes is proposed. By increasing the number of replicas of a hidden node, it becomes possible to modulate the average number of activated nodes based on the strength of the input signal. This leads to a scenario where a stronger input results in more activations, similar to the behavior of the Rectified Linear Unit (RELU) activation function.

The relationship between this duplication strategy and RELU is highlighted through mathematical approximation, showing that as the number of replicas increases, the activation function approaches RELU characteristics. However, training with numerous sigmoids can be inefficient, prompting the use of a truncated Gaussian distribution as an approximation to achieve a similar effect while optimizing the training process.
