The paper discusses the dynamics of gradient updates for weights in a model, specifically when projected onto the singular value decomposition (SVD) basis. It establishes that the gradient update of the weight \( w \) can be decomposed into a component over the mode \( w_\alpha \) and a rotation of the matrices \( u_\alpha \) and \( v_\alpha \). The evolution of the singular values \( w_\alpha \) is derived, indicating that the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) will adjust \( w_\alpha \) to align with the variance in the direction defined by \( u_\alpha \). 

Key equations showcase that when the empirical variance from the data is less than the prior variance of the visible variables, the corresponding mode is effectively filtered out. The dynamics of the matrices \( u_\alpha \) and \( v_\alpha \) are also provided, demonstrating how infinitesimal rotations occur and lead to a steady state when a direction \( u_\alpha \) diagonalizes the empirical covariance matrix of the dataset.

The Gaussian-Gaussian restricted Boltzmann machine (RBM) is highlighted for its ability to learn the principal components of the dataset, adjusting the weight matrix until the modes \( w_\alpha \) align with the variances dictated by the data. The paper suggests that the Gaussian-Gaussian RBM performs a form of SVD on the dataset, retaining only those modes that exceed a certain threshold. Additionally, it notes that parameter updates using the \( k \)-CD approximation converge to the same solution as maximizing the model's likelihood, reinforcing the efficacy of the approach. 

Overall, the findings illustrate a systematic method for learning principal components while highlighting the relationship between empirical variance and model parameters in the context of RBMs.
