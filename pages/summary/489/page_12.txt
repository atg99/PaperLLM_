This section discusses the analysis of finite size regimes within the context of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) and introduces the Gaussian-Softmax framework. The authors note that exact analysis can be achieved when the weight matrix spectrum is doubly degenerated. They explore a model where visible nodes follow a Gaussian distribution and hidden variables are binary, constrained by a softmax activation function.

The conditional probability of activating a hidden node is expressed as a softmax function, revealing its alignment with Gaussian mixture models (GMMs). The latent variable indicates whether a sample belongs to a specific center, represented by vectors. The marginal probability over visible nodes is derived by summing over the hidden nodes, leading to a formulation that identifies the weights of the Gaussian mixture modes.

The authors highlight that an additional parameter can be integrated into the weight matrix, simplifying the update process. They reformulate the gradient update into an Expectation Maximization (EM) framework, illustrating the steps for expectation and maximization to derive new parameter values. The discussion emphasizes the connection between RBM gradients and GMMs, which is crucial for understanding the optimization process within this framework.
