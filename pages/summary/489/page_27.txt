The paper discusses the pattern formation mechanism in the MNIST dataset, a widely used collection of 60000 black and white handwritten digit images, each sized 28x28 pixels. The study focuses on the learning processes of a Bernoulli-Bernoulli Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) and compares it with the previously analyzed Gaussian-Gaussian RBM. 

Initially, the learning behavior is understood through a linear stability analysis, recalling how learning was initiated by Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) in the Gaussian-Gaussian RBM. The dynamics of the Bernoulli-Bernoulli RBM are analyzed by expanding the log-likelihood gradient in the context of small weights and zero local biases. The resulting equations depict the learning dynamics, where the growth of modes is influenced by the projected SVD modes of the weights.

This investigation highlights the transition from SVD to Independent Component Analysis (ICA) in understanding how patterns emerge during the learning phase, suggesting a deeper exploration of feature extraction techniques in neural networks.
