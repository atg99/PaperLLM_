The paper discusses the concept of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), initially defined with binary variables for both visible and hidden nodes, closely related to the sigmoid activation function of the perceptron. It introduces a more generalized definition that accommodates arbitrary prior distribution functions for both visible and hidden variables, enabling the analysis of discrete or continuous variables. This generalization highlights the connections between RBMs and other machine learning models.

The RBM distribution is defined through a Hamiltonian framework, with the average over the RBM measure expressed mathematically. The authors emphasize the importance of the prior distribution choice, linking it to activation functions for the hidden layer, which can be derived using Bayes theorem.

The paper also outlines the practical design of RBMs as learnable generative models. The typical approach involves feeding the RBM a dataset and tuning parameters to ensure the learned model captures the correlations present in the data. This is achieved through stochastic gradient ascent on the likelihood function, which is framed as a maximization problem. The learning process is characterized by Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), focusing on minimizing the negative log likelihood.
