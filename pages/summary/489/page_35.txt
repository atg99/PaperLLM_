This paper presents a mean-field estimate of response functions related to the gradient of the log-likelihood in Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs). The response term for the data is expressed as ⟨sατβ⟩data, which incorporates the spin-glass parameter qβ[s] as a function of m in the context of pinned visible nodes. The complexity of the negative term arises from fixed points derived from specified equations for given model parameters. The response terms of the RBM can be computed using mean-field free energy, allowing for the calculation of skew-symmetric rotation generators for visible and hidden singular vectors.

The learning process of the RBM can be numerically integrated, demonstrating a typical learning trajectory. The results indicate that eigenvalues emerge sequentially, with each new eigenvalue causing a jump in likelihood and an increase in the number of fixed points. Ultimately, the mean-field fixed points align with the centers of data clusters, paralleling behaviors observed in conventional persistent contrastive divergence learning.

Additionally, the paper discusses alternative mean-field approaches such as message-passing techniques like belief propagation (BP) for inferring magnetization in RBMs. While BP is effective in cases of weak coupling and can iteratively update messages for convergence, its applicability in practical learning tasks, especially within ferromagnetic phases, remains uncertain. Previous studies have indicated that BP may exhibit poor convergence properties in such phases when the underlying factor graph is not tree-like.
