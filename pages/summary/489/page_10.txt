The paper discusses the dynamics of a system represented by modes and their interactions, particularly focusing on the evolution of quantities related to these modes. The analysis begins with an equation that describes the behavior of a quantity \( x_\alpha \) under certain conditions, leading to a solution that exhibits sigmoid characteristics for small perturbations \( \delta_\alpha \). 

The dynamics of two specific modes \( u_1 \) and \( u_2 \), which are linear combinations of dominant modes, are examined. The angle \( \theta \) between these modes is introduced, and the evolution of \( \theta \) is expressed through a differential equation that incorporates the correlation of the modes. This results in a system of equations that describe the dynamics of \( x_1 \), \( x_2 \), and \( \theta \), revealing pendulum-like motion for the angle \( \theta \) under fixed conditions of \( x_1 \) and \( x_2 \).

Further, the paper explores the Gaussian-Gaussian case as a solvable model of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBM), noting its limitations due to the reduction of the probability distribution \( p(s) \) to a multivariate Gaussian. It also introduces a non-linear RBM based on the spherical model, highlighting its solvability and potential applications in understanding the phase diagram of the system. The results contribute to the broader understanding of dynamical systems in the context of data alignment and mode interactions.
