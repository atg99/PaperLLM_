The paper discusses the training of a Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) with three hidden nodes on a 1D homogeneous Ising model dataset with periodic boundary conditions. The analysis reveals that the potentials generated at each visible site exhibit peak shapes that reflect correlation patterns around central nodes, but fail to capture the translational symmetry of the system. As training progresses, the positions of these peaks diffuse while repelling each other, facilitating the encoding of correlation patterns consistent with translational symmetry.

The study also examines the gradient of the log-likelihood function for a system with one hidden node and derives an expression that, when expanded, indicates a form of spatial diffusion in the 1D Ising chain. This leads to the identification of a peaked function that diminishes rapidly with distance, consistent with experimental observations. For two hidden nodes, an effective coupling results in repulsive interactions between feature vectors, further emphasizing the local correlations learned by the RBM.

Overall, the findings underscore how the features learned by the RBM evolve to represent local correlations, while their diffusion across the system during training helps restore translational symmetry without overlap, a behavior that will also be explored in the context of the MNIST dataset in the following section.
