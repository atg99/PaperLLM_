The paper discusses the learning dynamics of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) in the context of synthetic datasets and specific models, including the Gaussian-Spherical RBM and a 1D Ising chain. 

In the Gaussian-Spherical RBM, an analytical expression for the response function is derived, showing that while linear correlations between modes disappear, the singular values interact, causing lower modes to exert pressure on higher modes. This interaction results in the accumulation of modes at the top of the spectrum, as illustrated in the learning curves and scatter plots of the training and generated data.

Moreover, the paper explores pattern formation in a 1D Ising chain with a focus on a model featuring one hidden node. The training dataset utilizes a uniform coupling constant and periodic boundary conditions, allowing for an exact solution of the learning dynamics due to translational symmetry. The correlation function derived from the model depends solely on the relative distances between variables. Notably, the weights exhibit a peak for a specific visible node and decay with distance, highlighting the impact of the center's position on breaking translation symmetry.

Overall, the study provides insights into the interactions of modes within RBMs and the emergence of features in specific models, emphasizing the importance of understanding mode dynamics in the learning process.
