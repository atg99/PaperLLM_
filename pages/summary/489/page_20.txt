The paper investigates the behavior of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) during the learning process, particularly focusing on their performance with the MNIST dataset. It suggests that the learned RBM exhibits characteristics akin to a "compositional phase." The authors emphasize the importance of theoretically characterizing the learning curve to understand how this phase is attained. Additionally, they reference a recent study that highlights the diminishing proportion of diluted weights in a RBM with a single hidden node, positing that this observation may indicate an automatic adjustment to a ferromagnetic phase when the number of hidden features is minimal, enabling the RBM to learn overarching patterns from the dataset.

The paper further discusses the challenges of analyzing the phase diagram of RBMs, particularly due to the correlation of weights as learning progresses. It critiques traditional statistical mechanics approaches that assume independent and identically distributed weights, noting that such assumptions fail as correlations develop during learning. To address this, the authors propose a mean-field approach using a low-rank decomposition of the weight matrix, which includes a random noise component. They suggest that the eigenvalues of the weight matrix reflect intrinsic properties of the learned dataset, while the noise can be treated as quenched disorder. The paper also introduces overlap parameters to decouple the interactions between visible and hidden nodes, facilitating the computation of the system's free energy and its implications for the phase diagram.
