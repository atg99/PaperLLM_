The paper explores the learning dynamics of a Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) applied to the MNIST dataset. Initially, the first ten modes of the dataset and the RBM are shown to be similar, indicating that the RBM's learning trajectory aligns with the Gaussian-Gaussian case. This suggests that the RBM amplifies the principal modes of the dataset at the beginning of the learning process. The authors note that the features learned by the RBM correspond to modes of the dataset, revealing that the model starts by capturing global features.

As training progresses, MC samples generated by the RBM exhibit high overlap with learned features, suggesting that the model is effectively learning prototypes. The paper delineates various learning stages by examining the features, samples produced, and the overlap of discretized features. By the end of training, the RBM shows localized features akin to those produced by Independent Component Analysis (ICA), raising questions about the influence of the dataset on this learning aspect.

Further training leads the RBM to learn additional modes, although it remains uncertain whether this represents a genuine new phase or simply overfitting. The authors also analyze the spectrum of the weight matrix at different learning stages, observing a transition from a Marchenko-Pastur law, indicating a complex relationship between the learning dynamics and the underlying data structure.
