This collection of references highlights significant contributions to the field of restricted Boltzmann machines (RBMs) and related statistical mechanics concepts. The papers span various approaches and methodologies, including training techniques, mean-field inference, and unsupervised learning frameworks.

Key works include the application of the Thouless-Anderson-Palmer free energy in training RBMs, as presented by Gabrié et al. and further explored by Tramel et al. in a deterministic framework for unsupervised learning. The mean-field inference in Gaussian RBMs is elaborated by Takahashi and Yasuda, while Huang and Toyoizumi provide insights into advanced mean-field theories.

The historical context is enriched by foundational works such as the solvable model of a spin glass by Thouless, Anderson, and Palmer, which lays groundwork for understanding complex systems. The convergence conditions for the TAP equation discussed by Plefka and high-temperature expansion methods explored by Georges and Yedidia further inform the theoretical landscape.

Recent advancements include applications of RBMs in compressed sensing and multi-output learning with incomplete data, indicating the versatility of these models in practical scenarios. The use of message passing algorithms and comparisons of different inference methods, as seen in the works of Ricci-Tersenghi and Nguyen & Berg, underscore ongoing research into optimizing and understanding RBMs.

Overall, these references collectively illustrate the evolving understanding of RBMs from theoretical constructs to practical applications, emphasizing their relevance in statistical mechanics and machine learning.
