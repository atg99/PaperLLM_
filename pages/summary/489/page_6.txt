The paper discusses the varying phases of a model based on parameter values, highlighting the distinctions between paramagnetic, spin glass, and recall phases. In the paramagnetic phase, sampling configurations from the distribution p(s, τ) is straightforward, while the spin glass phase presents significant challenges due to the presence of spurious free energy minima. The recall phase, though promising, still poses difficulties in transitioning between states due to large energy barriers.

To address the complexities of learning the model, particularly the computational challenges associated with the negative term in the likelihood function, the paper introduces contrastive divergence (CD) as an efficient approximation method. The dataset is divided into minibatches, and gradient ascent is performed sequentially on these smaller subsets. CD employs multiple Monte Carlo chains, initiated from each sample in a minibatch, to explore nearby configurations. This technique aims to create an attraction basin around the data points, facilitating the learning of the model's parameters.

However, CD does not directly minimize the likelihood or the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the data distribution and the Boltzmann distribution. Instead, it minimizes the KL divergence between the data distribution and the distribution after k Monte Carlo steps. The procedure is described as roughly equivalent to minimizing a KL divergence difference, although it has limitations, such as not fully exploring the phase space of the learned Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) due to the restriction to k steps.
