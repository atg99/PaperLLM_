The references listed include significant contributions to the understanding of neural networks, statistical mechanics, and learning models. Amit, Gutfreund, and Sompolinsky (1985, 1987) explore the capacity of spin-glass models for storing patterns in neural networks. Rosenblatt (1958) introduces the perceptron as a model for information organization in the brain. Gardner (1988) discusses the interaction space in neural networks and optimal storage properties with Derrida. 

Mézard, Parisi, and Virasoro (1987) provide a foundational text on spin glass theory and the replica method. Carreira-Perpinan and Hinton (2005) delve into contrastive divergence learning, which is further examined by Tieleman (2008) in the context of training restricted Boltzmann machines (RBMs). Fischer and Igel (2014) offer an introduction to training RBMs, while Karakida et al. (2014, 2016) analyze feature extraction and the dynamics of contrastive divergence in RBMs with Gaussian visible units.

Decelle and colleagues (2017, 2018, 2020) investigate the thermodynamics and spectral dynamics of RBMs, including Gaussian-spherical models. Additionally, Berlin and Kac (1952) and Stanley (1968) contribute to the understanding of spherical models in ferromagnetism, linking these concepts to broader statistical physics. 

These works collectively enhance the theoretical framework and practical understanding of neural network models, learning dynamics, and their applications in various fields.
