The paper discusses the limitations of estimating probability distributions for data configurations that are distant from the training dataset in the context of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs). To address this issue, a modification known as persistent contrastive divergence (pCD) is introduced. This algorithm utilizes parallel Markov Chain (MC) chains initialized from random conditions, updating their states over time rather than being strictly tied to the dataset. This approach reduces the need for as many chains as there are samples in the mini-batch, although maintaining a comparable statistical error between positive and negative terms is still necessary.

The paper also provides an overview of different RBM configurations, specifically focusing on the Gaussian-Gaussian RBM, where both visible and hidden nodes follow Gaussian distributions. The learning dynamics in this model can be explicitly characterized, leading to a multivariate Gaussian distribution over the visible nodes after integrating out the hidden variables. The marginal distribution over visible nodes is derived, showcasing the relationships between the variances and the precision matrix of the model. The conditions for the existence of this measure are established, highlighting the necessity for the precision matrix to be strictly positive definite. 

Further theoretical and numerical aspects of the learning process in RBMs are to be explored in subsequent sections.
