This review paper by Aurélien Decelle and Cyril Furtlehner explores the Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) through the lens of statistical physics. The RBM, a foundational model in machine learning, has significantly influenced the advancement of deep learning. The authors draw parallels between RBMs and Spin Glass models, highlighting the connections with other statistical physics models. They present recent findings related to mean-field theory, which aids in understanding the RBM’s functionality through phase diagrams associated with various statistical ensembles. Notably, they identify a compositional phase where a limited number of features combine to create complex patterns.

The paper also discusses recent advancements in developing mean-field based learning algorithms and how these can replicate aspects of the learning process using ensemble dynamics equations and linear stability analysis. The introduction emphasizes the rapid evolution of machine learning over the past decade, its applications in image recognition, and its broader implications in fields like biomedical imaging and phase transition detection. The authors categorize machine learning tasks into supervised and unsupervised learning, explaining their methodologies and objectives.

Overall, the review highlights the significance of RBMs in the machine learning landscape and their theoretical underpinnings in statistical physics, paving the way for future research and applications.
