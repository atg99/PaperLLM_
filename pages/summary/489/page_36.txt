The paper presents results from training Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) on synthetic datasets with varying cluster configurations and dimensions. 

In the top panel, an RBM with 1000 visible and 500 hidden units was trained on a dataset of 10,000 samples containing 20 clusters in a 15-dimensional sub-manifold. The learning curve illustrates the evolution of eigenmodes and the log likelihood function over epochs, showing that initially there is one fixed point. As more modes are learned, the number of fixed points increases, indicating the model's ability to capture the underlying structure of the data.

The bottom panel explores an RBM with 100 visible and 50 hidden units, trained on a dataset with 11 clusters in a 5-dimensional sub-manifold. The scatter plot displays the training data alongside the positions of fixed points projected onto the first two singular value decomposition (SVD) directions. A second projection along the third and fourth axes is also provided, revealing that 16 fixed points were identified after learning five modes, which exceeds the number of hidden clusters.

Overall, the findings suggest that RBMs can effectively learn complex data structures and that the number of fixed points can reflect the richness of the learned representations.
