The paper discusses the representation of hidden nodes in Bernoulli-Bernoulli Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) using a Truncated-Gaussian prior. The focus is on the phase diagram and learning dynamics of these models in a mean-field regime. 

Section 4 delves into the phase diagram of the Bernoulli-Bernoulli RBM, utilizing {±1} notation for convenience. It references two significant works that analyze RBMs in the thermodynamic limit, each based on different assumptions about the statistical ensemble of the weight matrix. The first approach considers a weight matrix with independent and identically distributed (iid) elements, while the second includes a structured part alongside random noise. Both methods employ replica computations to derive macroscopic behavior, particularly in systems with quenched disorder.

The mean-field approach to the RBM, termed the random-RBM, is characterized by constructing a weight matrix from binary patterns. The degree of sparsity is quantified, and the term random-RBM is attributed to Tubiana et al. The paper also references the work of Agliari et al., who explored similar models with different theoretical frameworks, particularly focusing on the phase diagram.

The discussion connects the RBM to the Hopfield model, noting how it operates under low storage conditions, characterized by extensive overlaps with patterns. This analogy is critical for understanding the behavior and dynamics of the RBM in the context of pattern retrieval and learning.
