The paper discusses the mathematical formulation of a model involving visible and hidden nodes, represented by indices i and a, respectively. The model uses hyperbolic tangent functions to compute magnetization for both types of nodes, leading to the derivation of a free energy expression. This expression comprises entropy terms related to the visible and hidden nodes, and it highlights the connection between the nMF approximation and higher-order approximations such as the TAP equations.

The authors demonstrate how to derive first and second-order approximations for cases involving ±1 variables, centering the terms around their mean values to simplify computations. They explore the Hamiltonian associated with the system and compute magnetization in the infinite temperature limit by expanding the free energy in a high-temperature series.

The relationship between magnetization and Lagrange multipliers is established through constraints that equate magnetization to expectation values. The paper emphasizes that the first and second derivatives of the free energy yield important insights into the system’s behavior, allowing for the computation of relevant quantities like average energy and fluctuations in magnetization. Overall, the work provides a framework for understanding the dynamics of interacting systems through statistical mechanics.
