The paper discusses the derivative of the density function with respect to the weights in a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) and the emergence of negative terms in the equations. It focuses on the equation that relates the expected value of the density and the Gaussian mode, highlighting how this can be interpreted within the Expectation-Maximization (EM) framework. It notes that the difference in results between the Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) formalism and GMM is due to the varying parameterization methods.

A key phenomenon described is the phase transition during the learning process, particularly as the variances of the Gaussians undergo an annealing process. Starting with a high variance forces the learning towards the dataset's center of mass. As the variance is reduced, the stability of this solution can be analyzed through linear perturbations, leading to a critical threshold where the learning begins to capture more complex features of the dataset.

The stability analysis indicates that when the variance exceeds the largest eigenvalue of the covariance matrix, the solution remains stable. Conversely, when the variance falls below this threshold, the system learns additional structure beyond the center of mass. This behavior parallels findings in other models, suggesting a hierarchical cascade of phase transitions in structured datasets.

The paper also examines the impact of using an exact gradient approach instead of EM, revealing how the learning rate influences the stability of the update equations. The results show that the dynamics of learning can be significantly affected by the choice of method and parameters, underscoring the importance of understanding these interactions in model training.
