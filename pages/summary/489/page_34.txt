The paper discusses the performance of different learning methods for Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) applied to the MNIST dataset. It compares samples generated using Persistent Contrastive Divergence (PCD), P-nMF (a non-negative matrix factorization approximation), and P-TAP (a mean-field approximation). The results, illustrated in Figure 12, show that samples and learned features from all three methods are qualitatively similar, indicating that the mean-field/TAP approximations perform well in this context. 

The authors highlight that the advantages of the mean-field approximation extend beyond speed, as it provides additional insights such as fixed points representing local maxima of free energy. This is demonstrated in applications like compressed sensing and image reconstruction, where the mean-field equations help infer equilibrium values and states of missing information. 

Furthermore, the paper emphasizes the difference between mean-field equations, which represent an ensemble average over parameters and noise, and TAP equations, which are solved for individual instances. This approach allows for a more comprehensive understanding of the learning process in RBMs.
