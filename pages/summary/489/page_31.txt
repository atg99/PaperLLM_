The paper discusses the behavior of singular values in the context of training random matrices, specifically in relation to the Marchenko-Pastur law. It presents three figures illustrating the evolution of singular values during training. Initially, the singular values follow the Marchenko-Pastur distribution, but as training progresses, they exceed the established threshold. After extensive training, the distribution shifts to a fat-tailed characteristic with most eigenvalues above the threshold and a concentration of near-zero values, deviating from standard random matrix ensembles.

The paper also reviews the high-temperature (Plefka) expansion method, which simplifies the computation of mean-field magnetization and approximates the system's free energy in weak coupling regimes. This method has been successfully applied to train Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) using mean-field equations. The Ising model's naive mean-field approximation is introduced, with self-consistent equations for magnetizations and a corresponding free energy function derived from these magnetizations. The equations are adaptable to the RBM context by defining visible and hidden variables appropriately.
