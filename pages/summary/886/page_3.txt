Radzikowski et al. (2021) present a method for accent modification in speech using a convolutional neural network (CNN)-based autoencoder. The process begins with converting sound samples into spectrograms, which are visual representations of the audio signal. The autoencoder is trained using spectrograms of native speakers to learn how to modify the accents of non-native speakers. During training, back-propagation enables the autoencoder to adjust its parameters to effectively convert non-native accented speech to a native accent.

In the inference phase, the input spectrogram is processed by the trained autoencoder, which outputs a modified spectrogram reflecting the learned accent changes. Additionally, the authors explore a style transfer approach for accent modification, indicating a broader methodological framework for their research. The paper includes visual diagrams to illustrate the overall process and the structure of the CNN-based autoencoder, emphasizing the training and inference stages in the accent modification pipeline.
