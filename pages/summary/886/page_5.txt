In their 2021 paper, Radzikowski et al. present a detailed architecture for a CNN-RNN model utilized in a style transfer approach for accent modification in speech recognition. The model consists of multiple layers, including convolutional layers, max pooling, bidirectional RNNs, batch normalization, and softmax activation, totaling over three million parameters. The architecture is designed to extract style and content representations from audio samples through a loss network.

Two types of losses are defined: content loss, which measures the difference between the content representations of the original and modified samples, and style loss, calculated using Gram matrices to capture the style characteristics of the audio. The overall loss function combines these two components, allowing backpropagation to train the style-modifying autoencoder while keeping the loss network's weights fixed.

The authors also explore speech recognition using spectrograms, employing a CNN-RNN architecture trained on the LibriSpeech dataset. Additionally, they investigate cloud-based automatic speech recognition (ASR) services as an alternative method for speech recognition. The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of the model in accent modification and its potential applications in enhancing speech recognition systems.
