The paper addresses the challenge of local earthquake prediction, highlighting a gap in current methodologies that primarily focus on global predictions. The authors propose a novel approach that divides the target area into sub-areas measuring approximately 10 km by 10 km and predicts the occurrence of earthquakes in these smaller regions over a time frame of 10 to 50 days. They utilize a machine learning model that incorporates past earthquake data to generate predictions.

To enhance their model, the authors employ Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to effectively learn spatial correlations, combined with Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to maintain a long-term memory of past events. The architecture allows for the handling of both spatial and temporal dependencies, which is crucial for accurate prediction. They specifically focus on predicting earthquakes with magnitudes greater than a defined threshold, addressing the issue of class imbalance in their dataset.

The dataset used for this study includes records of 247,204 earthquakes from Japan, spanning 26 years. The authors acknowledge the imbalance in the dataset, particularly the scarcity of higher magnitude earthquakes, and propose methods to adjust their classification model accordingly. They also present a naive baseline model, which uses the historical mean occurrence of earthquakes in a given sub-area, and highlight the potential of Region-Time-Length (RTL) features for mid-term predictions.

Overall, the paper contributes to the field by offering a localized prediction model that leverages advanced neural network techniques to improve earthquake forecasting accuracy at a smaller geographical scale.
