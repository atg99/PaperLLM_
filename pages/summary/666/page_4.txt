The paper discusses the performance of a machine learning model that utilizes a combination of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for predicting earthquakes. The study highlights the impact of class imbalance on model performance, employing an oversampling technique to adjust weights for minor class objects during training. Results show that optimal weights significantly enhance model quality, with weights greater than one yielding improved outcomes, while excessively large weights for the minor class result in performance drops.

The paper presents various methods for earthquake prediction, including baseline models and different configurations of Gradient Boosting and CNN architectures. The proposed CNN+LSTM model, particularly when trained with residuals, outperforms other methods in terms of ROC AUC and PR AUC metrics, indicating better predictive performance. For instance, the CNN+LSTM, resid. model achieves a ROC AUC of 0.975 and a PR AUC of 0.0890, compared to lower values for traditional approaches.

Quality metrics are emphasized, particularly the importance of ROC AUC and PR AUC for imbalanced classification problems. The findings demonstrate that modern neural network architectures effectively predict earthquakes, leveraging time and spatial dependencies without relying on manually crafted features. The results offer a promising approach for midterm earthquake prediction, showing a clear trade-off between false alarms and missed detections based on varying probability thresholds. The study concludes that the proposed neural network model provides a robust solution for anticipating earthquakes, particularly in the context of historical data from Japan.
