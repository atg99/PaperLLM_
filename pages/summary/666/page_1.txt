The paper investigates the use of modern neural network architectures, specifically Recurrent Convolutional Neural Networks, for midterm earthquake prediction. The proposed model predicts the likelihood of an earthquake with a magnitude exceeding a certain threshold occurring within a specified area over a time frame of 10 to 60 days. It integrates a recurrent component (LSTM) to capture temporal dependencies and a convolutional component to address spatial dependencies.

The model was tested on historical earthquake data from Japan, achieving impressive performance metrics: a ROC AUC of 0.975 and a PR AUC of 0.0890, resulting in 1,180 correct predictions, while missing 2,090 earthquakes and generating 192,000 false alarms. In comparison, a baseline feature-based model showed a similar ROC AUC of 0.992, with slightly better correct predictions but significantly worse PR AUC and fewer false alarms.

The introduction highlights the challenges of earthquake prediction, discussing the limitations of physical modeling due to uncertainties and nonlinear behaviors in seismicity. Machine learning offers an alternative by utilizing past data to predict future probabilities. The paper outlines various machine learning strategies, including the use of seismic indicators and representation learning through neural networks.

The authors emphasize the evolution of machine learning techniques in earthquake prediction, referencing previous works that employed different neural network architectures, including CNNs and RNNs, to enhance prediction accuracy by considering both spatial and temporal correlations among seismic events.
