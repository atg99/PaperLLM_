The paper discusses an optimization approach for the least squares 2-bits binary quantization of a function, presenting a minimization problem with specific constraints. The optimal solution is shown to be foldable, meaning that the functions used in the quantization can be expressed in terms of the sign of the input values. The mathematical formulation leads to an expression for the error as a function of the quantization parameters, from which optimal values for the parameters can be derived by setting the gradient to zero.

In a separate section, the authors detail their methodology for training a ResNet model on the ImageNet dataset. They adopt stable practices from existing literature, using standard training and validation splits without hyperparameter search. The architecture is similar to XNor-Net, incorporating specific non-linearities and normalization techniques. The quantization process is addressed with various configurations for dynamic range, and the authors report top-1 accuracies for different quantization methods.

The training procedure includes a polynomial learning-rate annealing schedule, a fixed number of epochs, and various data augmentation techniques. The quantized networks are initialized randomly, and the authors emphasize the absence of weight decay in their training process. Overall, the paper provides insights into both the theoretical aspects of binary quantization and practical training strategies for deep neural networks.
