The paper discusses the k-bits scaled binary quantization of a variable x, represented as a weighted sum of scalar values and functions, with the aim of optimizing this quantization through a least squares approach. The quantization is defined in such a way that any permutation of the pairs of scalars and functions results in the same quantization, leading to a specific ordering constraint on the scalars.

The inner product of quantized activations and weights is formulated, allowing for efficient computation using bitwise operations. The optimization problem for finding the least squares k-bits scaled binary quantization is presented, which is non-convex and requires specific solutions for k=1 and k=2. An approximate solution using a greedy algorithm is also discussed.

A notable contribution is the introduction of foldable quantization, which allows for rapid computation under certain conditions. The least squares 2-bits binary quantization algorithm is detailed, showcasing optimal conditions for the scalars v1 and v2 based on the expected values of x. The process for efficiently solving for these values is outlined, emphasizing its effectiveness during training.

The paper contrasts general k-bits quantization methods with the proposed k-bits scaled binary quantization, highlighting the trade-offs in representation power and computational efficiency. Overall, the work presents a structured approach to optimizing quantization for practical implementation, particularly in contexts requiring efficient computation.
