In this paper, we investigate the accuracy of binary quantization for training deep neural networks (DNNs) with quantized weights and activations. We propose methods to enhance quantization accuracy through scaling and increased bit usage, introducing rank-1 binary quantization as a general scaling technique. By employing singular value analysis, we advocate for scaled binary quantization as a computationally efficient strategy and define a general k-bits scaled binary quantization.

We present provably least squares solutions for 1-bit, 2-bits, and ternary quantizations, alongside a greedy k-bits quantization algorithm. Our results demonstrate significant accuracy improvements in both post-training and during-training quantization, particularly when least squares quantization is applied. Comparisons with state-of-the-art binary neural networks (BNNs) on the ImageNet dataset reveal enhanced classification accuracies.

In exploring optimal rank-1 binary quantization, we focus on minimizing the Frobenius norm between a matrix and its rank-1 approximation, showing that the solution involves the sign of the original matrix and its best rank-1 approximation. We also discuss the behavior of singular values in random matrices, indicating that the optimal rank-1 binary quantization captures a significant portion of the total energy of the matrix, making it a robust approximation.

Lastly, we address least squares 1-bit binary quantization, establishing foundational principles for quantization decisions based on the properties of real-valued inputs. Overall, our findings underscore the potential of refined quantization techniques to enhance DNN performance while maintaining computational efficiency.
