This paper discusses scaling strategies for quantized activation tensors in deep neural networks (DNNs) using low-rank matrix analysis. It concludes that multiplying the quantized tensor by a single scalar is computationally efficient and yields accuracy comparable to more complex methods. The authors introduce rank-1 binary quantization, approximating a matrix X as a product of a rank-1 matrix and a binary sign matrix. The quantization error is measured using the Frobenius norm. The optimal rank-1 binary quantization is achieved when the energy of the quantized tensor is concentrated in its first singular component.

The paper also reviews a previously proposed method that quantizes activations using the sign function and scales them by their channel-wise averages. The authors derive that the optimal rank-1 binary quantization can be represented in terms of the sign of X and the truncated singular value decomposition (SVD) of the absolute values of X. They note that under the assumption of normally distributed entries, the first singular value captures a significant portion of the energy.

In the section on scaled binary quantization, the authors explore improving accuracy by using more bits. They present a 1-bit scaled binary quantization approach and derive an optimization problem to find the least squares solution. The optimal quantization is characterized by a scalar and a sign function based on the input vector's statistics. The authors conclude that further accuracy can be achieved through k-bits quantization, suggesting potential enhancements to the basic 1-bit approach.
