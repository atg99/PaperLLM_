This paper discusses the challenges and solutions associated with quantizing weights and activations in deep neural networks (DNNs) to improve inference efficiency. The authors focus on binary quantization, where values are represented as -1 and 1, and aim to reduce the accuracy gap resulting from quantization errors. They propose a unified framework for analyzing various scaling strategies and introduce a novel two-bit quantization method that minimizes least squares error.

The study highlights that while hardware and compiler optimizations can be lossless, model optimization, which modifies the model structure, often involves a trade-off between computational cost and accuracy. The authors categorize model optimization techniques into architecture optimization, pruning and compression, and low-precision arithmetic. They note that low-precision arithmetic, including binary quantization, can lead to significant memory savings but typically reduces model accuracy unless addressed through training adaptations.

The proposed quantization algorithms are efficient for hardware implementation using bitwise operations. The authors provide theoretical proofs demonstrating the optimality of their methods and conduct empirical error analysis. Experiments on the ImageNet dataset reveal that their least squares quantization algorithms significantly reduce the accuracy gap compared to traditional methods. Overall, this work contributes to the ongoing efforts to enhance DNN efficiency while mitigating the impact on accuracy.
