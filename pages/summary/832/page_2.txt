The paper discusses advancements in quantization methods that utilize a single bit to represent scalar weights in deep neural networks (DNNs). Initial approaches, such as those in [11], quantized weights to binary values (-1, 1), achieving high compression but resulting in significant accuracy loss. Subsequent works, including [35] and [57], explored ternary quantization (-1, 0, 1) to mitigate accuracy degradation while sacrificing some compression efficiency.

Further improvements in computational efficiency can be realized by quantizing intermediate activation tensors, enabling the use of high-performance operators on quantized inputs. The authors highlight that when both weights and activations are quantized using binary quantization, termed Binary Neural Networks (BNNs), efficient implementations can be achieved through bitwise operations like XNor and bit-counting. However, this dual quantization can exacerbate accuracy loss.

To counteract this loss, the authors suggest increasing model capacity through architectural modifications, such as augmenting the number of filters. They present empirical findings that illustrate the trade-off between computational cost and classification error across various quantization schemes, indicating that 2-bit quantization strikes a balance between efficiency and accuracy.

The paper also outlines several strategies to enhance BNN accuracy, including learned clipping, double skip-connections, parametric ReLU, multi-stage knowledge distillation, and tailored binary optimization. These strategies can complement the proposed 2-bit quantization methods.

The main contributions of the work include:
1. A unified framework for analyzing scaling strategies in BNNs, demonstrating that scaled binary quantization serves as a viable approximation.
2. The derivation of 2-bit and ternary scaled binary quantization algorithms aimed at minimizing least squares error, along with greedy variants.
3. Experimental validation on the ImageNet dataset showing that the proposed algorithms effectively reduce quantization error and enhance classification accuracy.

In summary, the research emphasizes the relevance of quantization in DNNs, proposing innovative methods to improve accuracy while maintaining computational efficiency.
