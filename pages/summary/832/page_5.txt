The paper discusses the implementation of quantization techniques for deep neural networks, specifically focusing on binary and ternary quantization methods. It presents a framework for computing optimal quantization thresholds and introduces a greedy algorithm termed Greedy Foldable (GF) for k-bits scaled binary quantization. The GF algorithm iteratively computes residuals and optimally finds sign and threshold values for each step, leveraging least squares 1-bit binary quantization principles. 

The paper highlights the challenges posed by non-differentiable loss functions due to the use of sign functions in quantizers. To address this, it employs the Straight Through Estimator (STE) during training, allowing the use of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) to update full precision weights while relying on quantized weights during the forward pass. The training process utilizes online statistics to compute quantizers and maintains a running average of scalars for improved inference efficiency.

Experiments are conducted on the ImageNet dataset using the ResNet-18 architecture, evaluating quantization errors and classification accuracy across various quantization schemes, including least squares 1-bit, 2-bits, ternary, and the proposed GF method. Comparisons are made with state-of-the-art algorithms such as BWN-Net, XNor-Net, and others, using quantization error as a measure of performance, specifically analyzing the angle between original and quantized values as an accuracy metric. 

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of advanced quantization techniques aimed at optimizing neural network performance while reducing computational requirements.
