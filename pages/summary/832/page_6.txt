The paper discusses the performance of various quantization methods applied to a ResNet-18 model trained on the ImageNet dataset, focusing on post-training and during-training quantization techniques. 

In Table 1, validation accuracy is presented for different quantization methods, specifically comparing the Greedy Foldable and Least Squares approaches. The results show that the Least Squares quantization, even at 2 bits, outperforms the Greedy Foldable method at 4 bits, particularly in later layers of the network where the activation distributions are more skewed. The accuracy of the Least Squares method exhibits less variance across different input images and layers.

Table 2 outlines the validation accuracy of various networks, including those utilizing Least Squares quantization. Notably, the proposed Least Squares method achieves higher accuracies than other state-of-the-art methods, even when using more bits. The results demonstrate a significant improvement in accuracy for the 2-bits Least Squares quantization compared to the 2-bits Greedy Foldable approach, highlighting the effectiveness of optimal quantization strategies.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes that quantization during training leads to improved accuracy, as the model adapts to quantized weights and activations. The proposed methods show superior performance in classification tasks, validating their potential in reducing quantization errors while maintaining accuracy in deep neural networks.
