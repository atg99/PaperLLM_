The paper "Trained Ternary Quantization" by Chenzhuo Zhu et al. discusses a novel approach for quantizing neural network weights into three levels: -1, 0, and 1. This method aims to reduce the model size and improve computational efficiency without significant loss in accuracy. The authors propose a training process that integrates quantization into the learning phase, allowing the model to adapt to the quantization constraints. This results in a more effective representation of weights and better performance on various tasks.

In contrast, the work by Barret Zoph et al. titled "Learning Transferable Architectures for Scalable Image Recognition" presents a method for automating the design of neural network architectures. The authors utilize a reinforcement learning approach to discover architectures that are transferable across different image recognition tasks. Their experiments demonstrate that the discovered architectures outperform manually designed ones, showcasing the potential of automated architecture search in enhancing scalability and performance in computer vision applications. 

Both papers contribute significantly to advancements in neural network optimization and architecture design, emphasizing efficiency and effectiveness in deep learning models.
