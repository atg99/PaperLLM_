In the paper, the authors discuss advancements in image style transfer and model compression techniques, highlighting the limitations of existing methods and proposing a novel approach. 

The section on style transfer outlines the evolution from traditional techniques, which relied on low-level statistics, to the introduction of neural style transfer by Gatys et al. This method leverages deep features from the VGG-19 model to match second-order statistics between the generated and style images. While various improvements have been made in terms of visual quality, speed, and user control, a common challenge remains: the inability to handle ultra-resolution images due to memory constraints. Some methods have addressed high-resolution stylization but lack generalization to unseen styles. The authors aim to develop a universal style transfer model capable of processing ultra-resolution images.

In the model compression section, the authors categorize existing methods into five groups, including low-rank decomposition, pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, and architecture redesign. They note that most research focuses on high-level vision tasks, leaving low-level tasks like style transfer less explored. The authors propose a deeply-supervised knowledge distillation method to create a smaller model from the pre-trained VGG-19, achieving significant reductions in parameters and computation while allowing for universal style transfer on high-resolution images.

The proposed method, named Collaborative Distillation, enhances the encoder-decoder framework commonly used in style transfer. The authors identify the need for a more expressive encoder and suggest that the decoder's training should be closely tied to the encoder's representations. They discuss two state-of-the-art approaches: WCT and AdaIN, illustrating their different collaborative relationships with the encoder. The authors emphasize the exclusivity of decoder-encoder pairs, proposing that if a smaller encoder can be made compatible with an existing decoder, it would fulfill the model compression goal. Thus, their method aims to facilitate this compatibility, enabling the use of a more compact encoder without sacrificing performance.
