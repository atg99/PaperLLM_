The paper presents a comparison between original and compressed models for neural style transfer, specifically focusing on the VGG-19 architecture. The original model has 17.1 million parameters and a storage size of 66 MB, while the proposed compressed model significantly reduces these figures to 1.1 million parameters and 5.2 MB of storage. The compressed model also demonstrates improved efficiency with 451.6 GFLOPs and reduced inference times on both GPU and CPU, allowing for a maximum input resolution of 6000x6000 pixels compared to the original’s 3000x3000 pixels.

Stylization quality was assessed through a user study, revealing that while the compressed model is slightly less preferred than the original, it still outperforms another model compressed by a different method. The paper discusses the limitations of existing knowledge distillation methods in the context of style transfer, noting that small gains in classification accuracy do not translate to perceptual improvements. The authors emphasize that their approach, termed Collaborative Distillation, effectively reduces model size while maintaining quality, leveraging the collaborative relationship between the encoder and decoder in the style transfer process.

The study concludes that their method shows promise not only for neural style transfer but also for other low-level vision tasks, suggesting avenues for future research. The work is supported by grants from the Natural Key R&D Program of China and the US National Science Foundation.
