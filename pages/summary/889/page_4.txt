The paper presents a Collaborative Distillation framework designed for universal neural style transfer, utilizing a two-stage process involving a collaborator network (decoder) and a smaller encoder (SEncoder). In the first stage, the framework employs both pixel reconstruction loss and perceptual loss for image reconstruction tasks, using a VGG-19 architecture to extract feature maps. The losses are defined as Lr for reconstruction and Lst for style transfer, balancing content and style losses through respective weights.

In the second stage, the original encoder is replaced with a smaller encoder that maintains equivalent functionality but with fewer filters. This replacement aims to streamline the model while preserving performance. To address the feature size mismatch between the smaller encoder and the decoder, the authors propose a linear embedding scheme. This scheme enables the transformation of features from the smaller encoder to match the required dimensions of the decoder by utilizing a transform matrix.

The approach builds on existing methodologies, such as the Gram matrix used for style representation, and emphasizes the importance of feature size compatibility in the stylization process. The paper outlines the theoretical underpinnings and practical implementations of the proposed framework, aiming to enhance the efficiency of neural style transfer applications.
