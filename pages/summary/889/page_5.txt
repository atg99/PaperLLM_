The paper discusses a method for compressing the VGG-19 model while maintaining its style description capability in the context of neural style transfer. The authors introduce a small encoder, F′, which replaces a redundant original encoder output, F. A transformation matrix Q is learned through a linear layer to ensure linearity, allowing for the formulation of a linear embedding loss, Lembed, to measure the difference between F and the transformed output Q·F′.

The proposed approach extends beyond the final output layer, applying linear embedding to four middle layers of the small encoder to enhance gradient flow during training, particularly addressing issues like gradient vanishing in deep networks. This method also aims to preserve the style description power of these intermediate layers, which is critical for effective style transfer.

The total loss for training the small encoder is expressed as Ltotal, incorporating both the embedding loss and a collaborative loss term, balanced by a factor β. The paper further details experimental results that validate the effectiveness of the compressed VGG-19 model compared to the original model within universal style transfer frameworks, such as WCT and AdaIN.

The authors evaluate their method against a typical filter pruning compression algorithm, demonstrating that their compressed encoder achieves visually superior stylized images with fewer parameters. The results indicate that the compressed model produces clearer and more semantically relevant stylizations, avoiding excessive emphasis on nuanced textures that can make images appear messy, a common issue with over-parameterized models. Overall, the paper presents a compelling case for the proposed collaborative distillation approach in enhancing the performance of compressed neural networks for style transfer tasks.
