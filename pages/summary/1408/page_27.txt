The paper by OKSUZ et al. reviews various approaches to addressing imbalance problems in object detection. It highlights that current object detectors do not adequately consider semi-hardness or adaptive hardness levels during training. Alternative strategies, such as those proposed by Cui et al., involve using human input to label false positives as hard examples, which are then incorporated into training. Other researchers, like Song et al., focus on mining hard negatives for both anchors and positives, emphasizing the need to consider relationships between positives and negatives.

Huang et al. propose that larger intra-class distances can complicate hard example mining, leading to the introduction of a position-dependent deep metric unit to account for intra-class variations. Generative methods, including Deep Adversarial Metric Learning, are noted for their ability to produce synthetic hard negatives and positives. Hardness Aware Metric Learning aims to manipulate features post-backbone to control example hardness through interpolation, coupled with an autoencoder for label preservation.

The comparative summary indicates that metric learning can create embeddings that better reflect data similarities than manually designed metrics. Self-paced learning, which adapts hardness levels during training, is recognized as a valuable concept for mitigating imbalance issues.

The paper also discusses multi-task learning, which involves balancing potentially conflicting objectives across tasks. Strategies for assigning weights to tasks, such as those by Li et al. and Guo et al., are explored, highlighting the dynamic adjustment of weights based on performance indicators. Zhao et al. emphasize the importance of synchronizing the learning pace of tasks, suggesting a normalization approach to balance training.

In terms of open issues, the authors call for a unified method to tackle all imbalance problems, considering their interdependencies. This comprehensive perspective is essential for advancing the field of object detection.
