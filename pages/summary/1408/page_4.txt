The paper by OKSUZ et al. reviews imbalance problems in object detection, focusing on the training pipeline and various types of imbalances that can occur. The training process consists of three main phases: feature extraction, detection, and bounding box (BB) matching, labeling, and sampling. Each phase may experience specific forms of imbalance, categorized into scale imbalance, objective imbalance, class imbalance, and spatial imbalance.

Key components of the object detection pipeline are defined, including the feature extraction network, classification network, regression network, detection network, and region proposal network (RPN). The paper explains concepts such as bounding boxes, anchors, regions of interest (RoI), ground truth, detection, and Intersection Over Union (IoU), which are fundamental to understanding object detection.

Imbalances are characterized by the representation of classes within datasets, where under-represented classes have fewer samples compared to over-represented classes. The authors emphasize the importance of addressing these imbalances to improve the performance of object detection systems. The paper aims to provide a comprehensive overview of these issues and guide future research in the field.
