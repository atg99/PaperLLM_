The paper by OKSUZ et al. discusses the challenges of objective imbalance in object detection, which arises during the training phase when minimizing the multi-task loss function that encompasses both classification and regression tasks. The authors highlight that differences in gradient norms, loss function ranges, and task difficulties can lead to one task dominating the training process, as illustrated in their analysis of the MS COCO dataset.

To address this imbalance, the authors propose several solutions. The most common method is Task Weighting, where hyper-parameters are adjusted to balance the loss terms based on validation sets. However, this approach complicates the optimization when multiple tasks are involved due to increased search space dimensions.

The paper also examines specific loss functions, such as AP Loss and GIoU Loss, which operate on different ranges, suggesting that normalizing these ranges can enhance training balance. A notable method discussed is Classification-Aware Regression Loss (CARL), which correlates classification and regression tasks by scaling regression loss based on classification confidence scores, thus improving localization of high-quality examples.

Additionally, the authors introduce Guided Loss, which dynamically weights the classification component based on the overall loss magnitude to prevent imbalance. They note that while linear task weighting can yield performance improvements, a comprehensive analysis of objective imbalance remains lacking in current literature.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for further exploration of the correlation between classification and regression tasks and the effectiveness of proposed methods like CARL and Guided Loss in addressing objective imbalance in different object detection architectures.
