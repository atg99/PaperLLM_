The paper by OKSUZ et al. reviews various methods addressing imbalance problems in object detection, focusing on feature pyramids, image pyramids, and their combinations. 

In methods based on feature pyramids, features from different levels are used independently, which can lead to unreliable predictions, particularly from lower layers. Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) were introduced to mitigate this issue by integrating features from multiple scales through a top-down pathway, enhancing performance for small and medium-sized objects. However, FPN suffers from a feature imbalance problem due to its straightforward feature combination approach.

Image pyramid methods, although less popular in deep learning due to high computational demands, have been revitalized by recent studies. Singh and Davis analyzed scale imbalance issues and proposed a method called SNIP that trains multiple detectors at different scales while addressing GPU memory limitations through an image cropping strategy. This approach allows for multi-scale training without losing data.

Combining image and feature pyramids offers a balance between efficiency and performance. For instance, the Efficient Featurized Image Pyramids method utilizes a lightweight network for various scales and integrates these features with backbone network features using attention modules. Another approach, the Enriched Feature Guided Refinement Network, uses downsampled images to enhance multi-scale contextual features.

Additionally, Noh et al. introduced a super-resolution method to improve small object detection by generating high-resolution feature maps. Their architecture employs a generator-discriminator framework to enhance representations of small regions of interest (RoIs) in detection tasks.

Overall, the review highlights the advancements and ongoing challenges in addressing feature and scale imbalances in object detection, underscoring the importance of integrating multiple approaches for improved performance.
