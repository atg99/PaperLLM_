The paper by OKSUZ et al. reviews imbalance problems in object detection, focusing on various sampling methods to address these issues. It categorizes common hard and soft sampling techniques, presenting a toy example with six bounding boxes to illustrate selection methods based on predicted ground truth probabilities. The discussion includes hard sampling methods like OHEM and IoU-based sampling, which prioritize negative examples with maximum loss or specific IoU thresholds, respectively. Soft sampling methods, such as Focal Loss and Gradient Harmonizing Mechanism, emphasize examples with higher loss values or IoU scores.

The authors also introduce generative methods that directly create artificial samples to mitigate imbalance. Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are highlighted for their ability to produce challenging examples during training, as seen in the Adversarial-Fast-RCNN model which generates occluded and deformed feature maps. Additionally, the paper mentions techniques for augmenting datasets through composite image generation, where cropped objects are blended into images, aiming to maintain visual coherence.

The paper presents a comprehensive overview of approaches to tackle imbalance in object detection, including sampling and generative methods, emphasizing the need for effective strategies to improve model performance in challenging scenarios.
