The paper by OKSUZ et al. reviews various approaches to addressing imbalance problems in object detection, drawing parallels to techniques used in image classification. The authors highlight several strategies that can enhance training efficiency and model performance. One approach focuses on training with uncertain examples based on the classifier's prediction history. Additionally, methods such as generating hard examples through image cropping and employing curricular learning are discussed.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of addressing data redundancy, with studies indicating that a significant portion of datasets like ImageNet and CIFAR-10 is redundant. This redundancy can be exploited to improve training efficiency. Active learning methods are mentioned, where an oracle identifies the most informative training examples.

Weak supervision techniques are explored, where unlabelled examples are incorporated into the dataset, even if they come with noisy labels. Generative models, particularly Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), are noted for their effectiveness in augmenting datasets for under-represented classes.

The authors argue that the object detection community can benefit from the imbalance studies in image classification, particularly in addressing foreground-background class imbalance. While loss functions like focal loss and class-balanced loss exist, their application in object detection remains limited. The paper also notes the scarcity of generative approaches in object detection compared to image classification.

In discussing metric learning, the authors explain that these methods aim to create embeddings where similar examples are closer together than dissimilar ones. The training process often involves pairs or triplets of data, which can introduce imbalance in positive and negative examples. The size of the dataset complicates the mining of hard examples, necessitating strategic sampling methods.

The review concludes by highlighting various sampling strategies aimed at selecting useful training examples, including methods that focus on semi-hard examples to avoid the pitfalls of local minima during training. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the current state of imbalance problem solutions in object detection and their potential extensions from image classification methodologies.
