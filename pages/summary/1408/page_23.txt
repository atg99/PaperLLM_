The paper by Oksuz et al. reviews imbalance problems in object detection, focusing on various aspects that affect model performance. It highlights the relative spatial distribution of regions of interest (RoIs) generated by the Region Proposal Network (RPN) in Faster R-CNN, noting a bias towards the top-left corner of ground truth boxes. This concentration suggests a potential imbalance that could influence detection performance.

The authors discuss various loss functions used in object detection, such as Focal Loss and Balanced L1 Loss, emphasizing the need for appropriate analysis methods to understand how these functions penalize examples. They illustrate that more complex loss functions, like AP Loss and GIoU Loss, complicate this analysis due to their dependency on multiple parameters and overall set performance rather than individual examples.

The paper identifies several open issues related to imbalance in object detection, including the design of better anchor sets to improve recall, the relative spatial distribution of bounding boxes, and the imbalance caused by overlapping bounding boxes during sampling. This imbalance can lead to overfitting on highly sampled regions and under-sampling of others, which may adversely affect learning.

Furthermore, the authors point out the need to investigate the effects of orientation imbalance in object instances, as it may lead to detectors overfitting to specific orientations, impacting overall detection accuracy. The paper concludes by emphasizing the necessity for further research to address these imbalance issues in object detection systems.
