OKSUZ et al. review the challenges and advancements in object detection, particularly focusing on imbalance problems that affect model performance. They emphasize the importance of addressing scale imbalance within detection pipelines, highlighting that methods such as feature pyramids can significantly enhance performance. For instance, the integration of feature pyramid networks (FPN) into Faster R-CNN resulted in a notable improvement of 3.7% on the MS COCO test-dev dataset, showcasing both increased accuracy and efficiency, as these models are faster than their predecessors.

The authors discuss the advancements achieved through neural architecture search (NAS) by optimizing the combination of backbone features, which led to a 10.2% relative improvement in detection accuracy, although at the cost of increased parameters and inference time. They also note the trade-off in inference time-performance when using multi-scale images and lighter networks.

The paper identifies open issues in the field, particularly the characteristics of different layers in feature hierarchies. The authors argue for a deeper understanding of how abstractness, coarseness, and cardinality impact model performance. They suggest that these aspects should be quantified for better comparison across methods. Additionally, they point out the limitations of using image pyramids in deep learning due to memory constraints, suggesting that solutions to this problem remain an area for further research.

The discussion extends to spatial imbalance, which pertains to the uneven contributions of bounding box attributes such as size, shape, and location to regression loss during training. The paper highlights the imbalance in regression loss contributions from different examples, particularly noting the dominance of certain losses (like L2) over others (like L1) in training. It outlines the evolution of regression loss functions, including the introduction of Smooth L1 Loss, which was designed specifically for deep object detectors and has gained widespread adoption.

Overall, the review underscores the significance of addressing both scale and spatial imbalances in object detection to enhance model performance and generalization.
