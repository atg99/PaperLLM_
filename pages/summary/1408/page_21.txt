The paper by Oksuz et al. reviews imbalance problems in object detection, particularly focusing on the Intersection over Union (IoU) distribution and its impact on model performance. It presents empirical results showing that the distribution of positive anchors is skewed towards lower IoUs in detectors like RetinaNet, which leads to issues during regression. The authors highlight that while regression improves some IoUs, it can also introduce false positives, particularly at lower IoU thresholds.

To address these imbalances, the paper discusses several solutions, notably the Cascade R-CNN method, which utilizes a cascaded pipeline of detectors trained on different IoU thresholds (0.5, 0.6, and 0.7). This approach mitigates the skewness of the IoU distribution by allowing the model to train on a more uniform set of samples. The paper also mentions the Hierarchical Shot Detector, which balances the distribution by running classifiers post-regression.

Further methods discussed include IoU-uniform R-CNN, which generates bounding boxes with a balanced IoU distribution for the regression stage, and the pRoI Generator that systematically creates bounding boxes to improve performance. The findings indicate that the IoU distribution not only affects regression but also classification performance, with a right-skewed distribution showing significant improvements when using Online Hard Example Mining (OHEM).

Additionally, the paper addresses object location imbalance, noting that current detectors may not account for the non-uniform distribution of objects within images. It suggests that learning the attributes of anchors concurrently can enhance recall and reduce the number of anchors required.

Overall, this review emphasizes the importance of addressing IoU and object location imbalances to improve the efficacy of object detection models.
