The paper by OKSUZ et al. discusses the imbalance problems prevalent in object detection, with a particular focus on the limitations of current methods for addressing these issues. The authors highlight that commonly used approaches involve linearly combining loss functions for different tasks, such as classification and regression. However, this method may fail to address the interdependence of these tasks, which is evident as both losses are affected when the bounding box moves across an image. The authors suggest that further exploration into the interdependence of these tasks could enhance object detection performance, particularly through methods like classification-aware regression loss.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of analyzing the learning paces of different tasks, as observed in the training of Region Proposal Networks (RPN) on the Pascal VOC dataset, where classification loss decreases faster than regression loss. This discrepancy suggests that balancing the learning rates of various tasks could be a productive area for future research.

Additionally, the authors extend their discussion to imbalance problems in related domains such as image classification. They summarize various strategies for addressing class imbalance, including resampling techniques like oversampling and undersampling, which aim to balance training datasets. They note that while oversampling can lead to overfitting, deep learning models can often benefit from it without significant drawbacks. Advanced methods like SMOTE and ADASYN are mentioned for generating new training samples, and transfer learning strategies are also discussed as effective means to mitigate class imbalance.

Overall, the authors advocate for a deeper understanding and innovative adaptations of techniques from related fields to improve object detection methods, particularly in addressing the inherent imbalances in data representation and task learning dynamics.
