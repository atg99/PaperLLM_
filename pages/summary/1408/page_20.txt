In the paper "Imbalance Problems in Object Detection: A Review" by OKSUZ et al., various methods for addressing imbalance issues in object detection are discussed, particularly focusing on loss functions and their optimization. The authors highlight several approaches, including KL Loss, which improves localization by treating box coordinates as independent and modeling them with a Gaussian distribution. This method incorporates a prediction branch for standard deviation and utilizes KL divergence to backpropagate losses.

The paper also explores IoU-based loss functions, starting with IoU Loss, which directly formulates loss based on the Intersection over Union (IoU) metric. The Bounded IoU loss modifies IoU into a smooth L1 function, allowing for better handling of bounding box parameters. The Generalized Intersection over Union (GIoU) is introduced as a more robust alternative to IoU, addressing its limitations by providing non-zero gradients even when boxes do not overlap, thus improving optimization.

Additionally, the authors discuss Distance IoU (DIoU) Loss and Complete IoU (CIoU), which incorporate penalty terms to enhance convergence speed and accuracy by considering the distances between box centers and aspect ratios. The paper emphasizes the significance of selecting appropriate loss functions to mitigate imbalance issues, particularly when the IoU distribution of bounding boxes is skewed, as illustrated through examples.

Overall, the review presents a comprehensive examination of various loss functions designed to improve object detection performance by addressing the challenges posed by imbalance in bounding box distributions.
