The paper by OKSUZ et al. reviews the challenges of class imbalance in object detection, focusing on both foreground-background and foreground-foreground imbalances. The authors present a comparison of various generative methods aimed at addressing class imbalance, highlighting techniques such as Adversarial-Fast-RCNN, Task Aware Data Synthesis, PSIS, and pRoI Generator. Each method generates synthetic examples to enrich the training dataset.

The paper also provides statistical insights into common datasets, illustrating the distribution of classes and objects within images. Notably, it discusses the impact of sampling strategies on performance, revealing that positive examples with higher Intersection over Union (IoU) values can be more beneficial for training, challenging the notion that hard examples are inherently superior.

The authors identify critical open issues in the realm of class imbalance, particularly the need for improved sampling methods that consider the usefulness of examples, especially in foreground-foreground class imbalance scenarios. They emphasize the necessity for further investigation into optimal weighting of examples and the effectiveness of soft sampling methods. The paper concludes by underscoring the importance of refining sampling criteria to enhance the robustness of object detection systems.
