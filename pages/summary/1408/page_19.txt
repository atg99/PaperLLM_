The paper by OKSUZ et al. reviews various loss functions utilized in bounding box (BB) regression tasks within the context of object detection, highlighting their strengths and weaknesses in addressing imbalance issues. The loss functions discussed include:

1. **L2 Loss**: Historically used in deep object detectors, it is stable for small errors but heavily penalizes outliers.
2. **L1 Loss**: Less stable for small errors compared to L2 Loss.
3. **Smooth L1 Loss**: A baseline function that is more robust to outliers than L1 Loss, defined using a piecewise function to minimize the impact of outliers while maintaining stability.
4. **Balanced L1 Loss**: Enhances the gradient contribution of inliers over outliers, improving performance particularly for larger Intersection over Union (IoU) values.
5. **Kullback-Leibler Loss**: Addresses ambiguities in ground truth boxes by predicting a probability distribution for bounding box coordinates.
6. **IoU Loss**: Utilizes an indirect calculation of IoU as the loss function.
7. **Bounded IoU Loss**: Fixes all parameters of an input box in the IoU definition except for the one being optimized during backpropagation.
8. **GIoU Loss**: Extends IoU by incorporating the smallest enclosing rectangle for the inputs, using both IoU and extended IoU as loss functions.
9. **DIoU and CIoU Loss**: These further extend IoU by adding penalties related to aspect ratio differences and center distances between bounding boxes.

The paper also illustrates the imbalance in regression loss through a visual example, demonstrating how different prediction boxes contribute to L1 and L2 errors. The authors emphasize that while Smooth L1 Loss reduces the negative impact of outliers, Balanced L1 Loss particularly enhances the learning process by focusing on inliers, leading to improved performance metrics, such as Average Precision at higher IoU thresholds.
