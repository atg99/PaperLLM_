The paper by Oksoz et al. reviews imbalance problems in object detection, highlighting that existing literature primarily addresses foreground class imbalance and neglects other critical aspects. The authors categorize various imbalance issues and provide a comprehensive analysis of proposed solutions, alongside open challenges. 

The structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 offers a background on object detection and terminology, while Section 3 introduces the taxonomy of imbalance problems. Sections 4 to 7 delve into specific imbalance issues, presenting definitions, solutions, and open questions. Section 8 extends the discussion to related domains, and Section 9 outlines overarching challenges across all imbalance categories. The paper concludes in Section 10.

The authors differentiate between top-down and bottom-up approaches in object detection. Top-down methods, which dominate current research, generate holistic object hypotheses early in the process and are further divided into two-stage and one-stage methods. Two-stage methods reduce negative examples through a proposal mechanism, while one-stage methods predict detection results directly from anchors without a proposal elimination phase. Bottom-up methods, on the other hand, focus on predicting key points and grouping them to form object instances.

The paper includes a table of frequently used notations to clarify terms utilized throughout the discussion. This structured approach allows readers to navigate the complexities of imbalance problems in object detection effectively.
