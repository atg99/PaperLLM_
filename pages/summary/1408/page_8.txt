The paper by OKSUZ et al. reviews various strategies to address imbalance problems in object detection. The authors categorize these strategies into three main types: hard sampling methods, soft sampling methods, and sampling-free methods.

1. **Hard Sampling Methods**: These methods focus on selecting a subset of training examples that are deemed more informative. For instance, Kong et al. proposed an end-to-end learning method that utilizes objectness priors to balance positive and negative examples during training. Zhang et al. introduced negative anchor filtering to refine anchor confidence scores, while Nie et al. implemented a cascaded-detection scheme using objectness modules to filter out easy anchors.

2. **Soft Sampling Methods**: Unlike hard sampling, soft sampling methods adjust the contribution of each training example based on its importance without discarding any samples. For example, YOLO applies a straightforward weighting scheme where background examples contribute less to the loss. Focal Loss dynamically adjusts weights to emphasize harder examples, and Gradient Harmonizing Mechanism (GHM) penalizes loss based on the gradient norms of samples. Additionally, PrIme Sample Attention (PISA) assigns weights based on the intersection over union (IoU) and classification scores.

3. **Sampling-Free Methods**: These recent approaches eliminate the need for manual sampling strategies, reducing hyperparameter tuning complexity. Chen et al. introduced an objectness branch to predict objectness scores, enhancing performance without sampling. Another method proposed appropriate hyperparameter settings to improve detection performance using standard loss functions. Lastly, AP Loss formulates the loss as a ranking task based on average precision, thus addressing imbalance directly through performance modeling.

Overall, the review highlights the evolution of techniques in handling class imbalance in object detection, emphasizing the importance of balancing positive and negative samples to improve model performance.
