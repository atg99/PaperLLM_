The paper by OKSUZ et al. discusses the challenges of class imbalance in object detection, particularly focusing on foreground-foreground class imbalance and its implications for dataset and batch levels. It highlights various methods to address these issues, including object swapping techniques like Progressive and Selective Instance-Switching (PSIS), which enhance detection quality by swapping low-performing class objects between images. Additionally, the use of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in Task Aware Data Synthesis is presented, where a synthesizer generates hard examples, assisted by a discriminator and a target network.

The authors also introduce the Positive RoI (pRoI) Generator, which creates positive Regions of Interest (RoIs) based on Intersection over Union (IoU) and bounding box distributions, thus facilitating the analysis of hard sampling methods. 

The paper further categorizes foreground-foreground class imbalance into dataset-level and batch-level issues. Dataset-level imbalance arises from natural frequency variations among object classes, potentially leading to overfitting towards over-represented classes. Solutions discussed include generative methods and the approach by Ouyang et al., which fine-tunes long-tail distributions by clustering classes based on visual similarity and using a hierarchical classifier structure.

Batch-level imbalance, characterized by uneven class distribution in training batches, can bias model learning. The Online Foreground Balanced (OFB) sampling method aims to mitigate this by ensuring uniform class representation in batches, although its effectiveness is noted as limited.

A comparative summary reveals that early methods relied heavily on hard sampling, exemplified by OHEM, which significantly improved model performance. In contrast, recent techniques, including Focal Loss and AP Loss, focus on using all examples and achieving relative improvements over baseline models. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of addressing class imbalance to enhance the performance of object detection systems.
