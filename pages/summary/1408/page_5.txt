The paper by OKSUZ et al. presents a comprehensive review of imbalance problems in object detection, categorizing various types of imbalances and their corresponding solutions. The authors define imbalance as a distributional bias in the training pipeline of object detection, identifying eight distinct imbalance problems grouped into four main categories: class imbalance, scale imbalance, spatial imbalance, and objective imbalance.

Class imbalance is explored in detail, highlighting challenges such as foreground-background and foreground-foreground class imbalances. The paper discusses various methods to address these issues, including hard sampling techniques like random sampling and hard example mining, as well as soft sampling methods such as focal loss and gradient harmonizing mechanisms.

Scale imbalance is addressed through techniques that utilize pyramidal features, scale-dependent pooling, and multi-scale networks, focusing on the different scales at which objects appear in images. Spatial imbalance covers issues related to the distribution of objects within the image space, while objective imbalance pertains to the discrepancies in loss functions used during training.

The authors provide a detailed taxonomy that includes proposed solutions for each imbalance problem, illustrated in figures that map these challenges to the object detection pipeline. The review emphasizes the need for a systematic approach to tackle these imbalances effectively, contributing valuable insights to the field of object detection.
