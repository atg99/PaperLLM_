The paper by OKSUZ et al. discusses various types of imbalance problems in object detection, emphasizing their prevalence and impact on detection performance. The authors categorize imbalances into four main types: class imbalance, scale imbalance, spatial imbalance, and objective imbalance.

Class imbalance arises when certain classes are over-represented in the dataset, manifesting as foreground-background imbalance, where background instances significantly exceed positive instances, or foreground-foreground imbalance, where a few classes dominate. This imbalance is typically addressed during the sampling stage of the detection pipeline.

Scale imbalance occurs when object instances vary in size, leading to unequal representation across different scales. This affects both the detection performance and feature extraction, suggesting that a single scale of processing is insufficient for effective detection of objects of different sizes.

Spatial imbalance relates to spatial properties of bounding boxes and encompasses three sub-types: imbalance in regression loss, IoU distribution imbalance, and object location imbalance. Each of these sub-types presents unique challenges, particularly in loss function design and the distribution of object instances within images.

Objective imbalance arises when multiple objectives (loss functions) are involved in the training process. The incompatibility among these objectives necessitates a balanced approach to minimize loss across all tasks.

The paper highlights the varying levels of research attention these imbalances have received, noting that while foreground-background class imbalance has been extensively studied, other types, particularly objective imbalance and object location imbalance, have only recently begun to garner interest. The authors argue for a structured perspective on these imbalances and their solutions, recognizing that some issues stem from data distribution, while others are influenced by methodological choices. 

In conclusion, the paper provides a comprehensive review of the imbalances in object detection, underscoring the need for ongoing research to address these challenges effectively.
