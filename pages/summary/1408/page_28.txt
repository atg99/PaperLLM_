In the paper by Okşuz et al., the authors discuss the inter-dependency of various imbalance problems in object detection. They illustrate this using a toy example, demonstrating how shifts in the input bounding box affect class, scale, objective, and bounding box (BB) imbalances. 

Initially, a bounding box with high Intersection over Union (IoU) is altered in two steps, leading to different outcomes. In the first shift, while the number of positive bounding boxes remains the same, their IoUs decrease, resulting in more hard examples and altering the overall loss contributions. The scales of the Regions of Interest (RoIs) stay unchanged, indicating that scale imbalance is unaffected. However, the objective imbalance is impacted as the shifted bounding box incurs higher loss, thus affecting its contribution to the total loss.

In the second shift, the bounding box's label changes, reducing the number of positive examples and increasing negatives. This shift affects the class imbalance as the example transitions from a hard positive to a hard negative. The scale imbalance is also affected since fewer positives share the same scale. The objective imbalance is further complicated as the negative bounding box becomes a hard negative, altering the loss contributions across classification and regression tasks.

The authors identify three open issues regarding measuring and identifying imbalance: defining a balanced distribution for critical properties, establishing the desired distribution that may differ from the balanced one, and quantifying the degree of imbalance in distributions. They highlight the need for a consensus on labeling bounding boxes as positive or negative, emphasizing that consistent labeling is crucial for accurately identifying hard examples and mitigating imbalance issues. Current practices using a fixed IoU threshold for labeling are criticized for generating noisy examples, which can adversely affect model performance.

Overall, the paper underscores the complexity of imbalance problems in object detection and the necessity for thorough investigation and precise methodologies to address these challenges effectively.
