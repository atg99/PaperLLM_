In the paper by OKSUZ et al., the authors review various imbalance problems in object detection, focusing particularly on the challenges posed by class imbalances. They highlight the importance of sampling methods that assign weights to examples rather than discarding samples, which has been shown to enhance performance. The discussion includes contrasting methodologies, such as the hard example mining approach and specific sampling techniques based on Intersection over Union (IoU), which prioritize positive samples with higher IoUs. 

The authors point out that while foreground-background class imbalance has received attention, foreground-foreground class imbalance remains underexplored. They note that class performance does not necessarily correlate with the number of examples, indicating a need for further analysis to identify effective sampling mechanisms. The paper suggests that lessons from image classification, such as transfer learning and tailored loss functions, could be applied to address these imbalances in object detection.

Additionally, the authors discuss how batch-level class imbalances can occur, leading to discrepancies between the distribution of classes in a batch compared to the overall dataset. They emphasize that even when batches appear balanced, sampling methods may still introduce biases that affect detection performance.

Lastly, the authors critique existing ranking-based loss functions that treat all classes uniformly, arguing that optimal confidence scores vary by class and that a class-specific approach could improve outcomes. The paper concludes with a discussion on scale imbalance, setting the stage for further exploration of object and box-level scale issues.
