This paper introduces a DenseNet-based approach to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of camera model identification, particularly in scenarios where image metadata is missing or images have undergone post-processing.

The authors implemented a pipeline that begins with the extraction of image patches sized 256x256 from a labeled dataset, utilizing Empirical Mode Decomposition for data augmentation. They employed the DenseNet-201 architecture to train a neural network, concatenating output features from three different patch sizes (64x64, 128x128, and 256x256) before passing them to a secondary network for final predictions. This method yielded an impressive accuracy of 98.37% on the Forensic Camera-Model Identification Dataset from the IEEE Signal Processing Cup 2018, marking a new state-of-the-art performance for single-model identification.

Additionally, the model demonstrated exceptional adaptability, achieving over 99% accuracy across 19 camera models when tested on the Dresden Database for Camera Model Identification. The authors further validated the robustness of their approach by applying it to detect post-processing types in images, achieving an accuracy of 96.66%. 

Overall, the paper highlights the significance of DenseNet in addressing challenges in image forensics, particularly in camera model identification and post-processing detection, emphasizing the need for innovative techniques in the face of increasing digital image manipulation.
