The paper discusses the architecture and training methodology of a model that utilizes a Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block for image classification tasks, specifically focusing on differentiating between various camera models. The SE block enhances feature representation by applying a self-gating mechanism that adjusts channel activations based on their importance. The modified features are then processed through a Dropout layer, followed by Global Average Pooling, resulting in a feature vector that is fed into a Dense Layer with Softmax activation to classify into ten camera models.

The training procedure is divided into three phases. In Phase-I, the DenseNet-201 model is initialized with ImageNet weights, and training is conducted using Dataset-I. A total of 20 patches of size 256x256 are extracted from each image, with 85% allocated for training and the rest for validation. Stochastic Gradient Descent is employed as the optimizer. Features are extracted from various patch sizes and averaged to form a combined feature vector of size 3x1920, which is then used in the classification network to predict camera models.

Phase-II involves transfer learning with Dataset-II, where the previously trained model from Phase-I is fine-tuned using high-quality patches. The classification block is directly trained on the feature output to classify into 27 classes.

In Phase-III, the model is trained on all Phase-I images, excluding EMD data, and is designed to detect image manipulations across four classes, while still leveraging the architecture and weights from Phase-I.

Overall, the paper outlines a systematic approach to leveraging deep learning techniques for image classification and manipulation detection, highlighting the effectiveness of transfer learning and feature extraction through a modified DenseNet architecture.
