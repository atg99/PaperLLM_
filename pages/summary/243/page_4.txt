This paper discusses the methodology for training a convolutional neural network (CNN) to identify source camera models using image patches. The authors emphasize the importance of using appropriately sized patches (256x256) to ensure that the network can learn spatial features effectively. They highlight that saturated patches, which lack statistical information about the camera model, should be excluded from training. The quality of each patch is determined using a specific formula that accounts for the mean and standard deviation of the RGB components, allowing for the selection of the highest quality patches.

Additionally, the paper introduces Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) as a technique for augmenting the training data. EMD decomposes an image into Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs) and a residue, where the residue is retained for training purposes. This approach serves as a denoising method, helping to remove high-frequency noise while preserving essential features related to the camera model. The authors used Python's PyEMD library for this process.

Finally, the paper mentions the use of a Dense Convolutional Network architecture for the CNN model, which will be trained using the prepared patches and augmented data. Overall, the methods outlined aim to enhance the accuracy of source camera model identification by focusing on high-quality image patches and effective data augmentation techniques.
