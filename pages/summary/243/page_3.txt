The dataset used in the study consists of images from various camera models, specifically Dataset-I and Dataset-II. Dataset-I includes images from 11 camera models with a total of 8459 images, where 2640 images are of size 512x512, with 1320 unaltered and the rest manipulated. Dataset-II includes a varying number of images from 27 different camera models.

Data augmentation techniques were employed to enhance the training dataset, including JPEG compression, resizing, gamma correction, and random rotations. This process helps the model learn robust features and generalize better across different image conditions.

The proposed model utilizes Dense Convolutional Networks, specifically the DenseNet-201 architecture, to extract features from image patches. The model first extracts patches of size 256x256 and subsequently generates feature vectors from smaller patches (128x128 and 64x64). These vectors are concatenated and fed into a secondary network with a Squeeze-and-Excitation block followed by a classification block. During testing, feature vectors are generated similarly, and predictions are averaged for improved accuracy.

Overall, the methodology emphasizes the importance of data diversity and the extraction of detailed features to effectively distinguish between images captured by different camera models.
