In recent years, neural networks have significantly improved accuracy in image classification tasks. Previous works, such as those by researchers who trained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on datasets like the Dresden database, have demonstrated this progress. However, these methods often fall short of state-of-the-art accuracy, particularly in the context of camera model detection from post-processed images. While some studies have explored specific aspects of image manipulation and classification, a comprehensive approach that integrates these factors remains scarce.

This paper highlights the potential of deep neural networks (DNNs) to enhance classification rates, especially when traditional feature extraction methods struggle with altered image characteristics due to post-processing. The authors advocate for the use of DNNs due to their ability to automatically learn relevant features from images without explicit feature engineering.

The study employs the 201-layer DenseNet architecture for its ability to retain subtle camera model features throughout the network's layers. DenseNet's design allows for direct access to features from preceding layers, mitigating the risk of feature loss during propagation. Moreover, the introduction of a Squeeze-and-Excite block enhances the network's representational capabilities, improving classification accuracy.

To combat overfitting, the authors implement various data augmentation techniques, including gamma correction and random cropping, to diversify the training dataset. The network is also trained for image manipulation detection, aiming to establish its utility in broader image forensics applications.

The paper outlines the methodology, including the dataset used for training, which comprises images from ten different camera models, and additional data sourced from Flickr. This comprehensive dataset supports the training and evaluation of the proposed network, which is detailed in subsequent sections of the paper.
