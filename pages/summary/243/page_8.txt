This paper presents a DenseNet-oriented pipeline for identifying the source camera model of an image, achieving a state-of-the-art accuracy of 98.37% on the IEEE Forensic Camera-Model Identification Dataset. The authors utilized DenseNet-201 along with a Squeeze and Excitation (SE) network architecture, incorporating various data augmentation techniques, notably Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD). The use of EMD significantly improved accuracy, with the network reaching 94.6% and eventually 97.7% after training with diverse images from Flickr, which enhanced the model's ability to learn device-specific features.

In Phase II, the network was tested on images from 27 different camera models within the Dresden Database, achieving over 99% accuracy for 19 models. Notably, the network maintained 100% accuracy in identifying the manufacturing company of the cameras, although some accuracy drops were observed for specific models, attributed to similarities in their interpolation methods or CFA patterns.

Phase III focused on identifying four types of image manipulations: Unaltered, JPEG Compressed, Gamma Corrected, and Resized. The model achieved an overall accuracy of 96.66% using DenseNet-201 and a patch size of 128x128, demonstrating the model's capability to generalize features across different manipulation types.

The conclusion emphasizes the effectiveness of the proposed pipeline and its potential for broader applications in image forensics, despite the noted limitations regarding the testing on the Dresden Database. The findings suggest that the learned features can be leveraged for various image forensic tasks beyond camera model identification.
