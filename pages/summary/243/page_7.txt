The paper presents a study on the detection accuracy of camera models using DenseNet-201 architecture with varying input sizes. The results indicate that larger input sizes lead to higher accuracy in distinguishing between unaltered and manipulated images. Specifically, the study evaluated input sizes of 64x64, 128x128, and 256x256, with the final pipeline achieving an overall accuracy of 98.37% using a combination of features from different input sizes.

The experimental phase utilized a test dataset of 2640 images from unseen devices, balanced between unaltered and manipulated images. The scoring metric employed was based on the accuracy of unaltered and manipulated images, weighted at 70% and 30% respectively.

Findings highlighted that smaller input sizes, particularly 64x64, resulted in reduced accuracy due to limited residual camera model information. The paper suggests that using ensemble methods could enhance performance, although the focus was on maximizing accuracy with a single network. The inclusion of EMD augmented images was noted to significantly improve accuracy.

Additionally, the paper details the detection rates for various manipulations, with high accuracy in identifying unaltered images and significant success in detecting JPEG-compressed and gamma-corrected images.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of input size and data augmentation in enhancing the performance of camera model detection systems.
