In this section, the authors discuss the formulation of a probability distribution P(v) in relation to Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) and the implications of previous theoretical results regarding cumulant generating functions. Specifically, they refer to findings by Marcinkiewicz (1939) which indicate that the cumulant generating function cannot be a finite-order polynomial of degree greater than two. This result, in conjunction with the idempotence property of the parametrization, leads to the conclusion that the exponential argument in the probability expression will either involve only single and pairwise interaction terms or all possible interactions among variables.

The authors emphasize that RBMs can represent either pure pairwise interactions or models with interactions of all orders. Notably, there is no configuration of potential that allows an RBM to exclusively represent systems with three-body interactions. However, certain choices of interaction parameters can restrict the highest order of interaction terms in the RBM. A specific instance is identified where a quadratic potential leads to a model characterized solely by pairwise interactions, aligning with a Gaussian distribution.

Furthermore, the authors aim to establish a precise mapping between the equations governing RBMs and models of interacting binary variables, leveraging the binary nature of the visible variables to express the cumulant effectively. They also reference a related study that applies Marcinkiewicz's theorem to derive similar results in the context of generalized Hopfield models.
