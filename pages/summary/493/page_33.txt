We draw connections between our work and prior studies that explore the relationships between RBMs and spin models. The expansion of log-probability distributions in RBMs parallels the Network Polynomials method in Bayesian networks, which utilizes probabilistic queries through polynomial partial derivatives. Additionally, our mapping of RBMs relates to the higher-order interaction detection techniques previously proposed.

Furthermore, the work of Martens and Sutskever, which leverages the connection between pairwise Markov Random Fields and RBMs for efficient sampling, aligns closely with our analysis. Yoshioka et al. also contribute to this discourse by transforming interacting binary model distributions into RBMs, shedding light on the advantages of such mappings in Monte Carlo simulations.

Barra et al. have examined the phase diagram of RBMs under different priors, emphasizing the retrieval phase's significance in capturing essential features from diverse datasets while mitigating overfitting. Our results underscore the intricate balance between model complexity and the ability to generalize, as indicated by the observed transitions in RBMs.
