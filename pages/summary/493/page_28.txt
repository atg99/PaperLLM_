The paper discusses the impact of different non-linear activation functions on the three-body interaction terms induced by Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) with varying sizes of hidden layers, specifically comparing configurations with M=400 and M=1200. It highlights that the magnitude of inferred weights decreases as the size of the hidden layer increases. This phenomenon is attributed to the learning algorithm's need to operate in a tunable parameter region, which facilitates effective data representation learning. Smaller weight values are beneficial in preventing excessive input currents that could saturate network dynamics and hinder training, particularly with larger M values.

The analysis reveals that while different activation functions result in varied parameters for fields and pairwise interactions, there are notable scaling and linear relationships when the hidden layer is sufficiently large. The small parameter approximation for pairwise interactions holds well, and differences across activation functions reduce to factors involving cumulants of hidden node distributions. However, the study points out that triplet interactions do not display the same relationships among activation functions, indicating a more complex interplay in this context.
