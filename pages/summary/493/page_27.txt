The paper investigates the quality of approximations for pairwise interactions in Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) using different activation functions. It focuses on comparing the weak parameter approximation to the actual values derived from trained weights on the MNIST dataset. The analysis utilizes two equations, specifically Eq. (14) for mapping interactions and Eq. (19) for the approximation, with results illustrated in scatter plots for various values of M (100, 400, and 1200).

The findings reveal that the small parameter approximation yields accurate estimates of pairwise interactions, particularly as M increases, indicating that the approximation improves with larger models. This is supported by the observation that the root mean square error between the approximated and actual interactions decreases as the root mean square of the RBM weights diminishes with increasing M. The paper also includes expressions for the second cumulant necessary for evaluating Eq. (19) in the Appendix, showcasing the relationship between activation functions and the quality of the approximations. Overall, the results highlight the effectiveness of the small parameter approximation in modeling pairwise interactions in RBMs, particularly for larger datasets.
