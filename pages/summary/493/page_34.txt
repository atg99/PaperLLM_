The paper discusses the relationship between the representational power of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) and the characteristics of their activation functions and connectivity. It references previous studies, including those by Barra et al. (2017) and Marullo and Agliari (2021), which explore the spin glass transition and its implications for optimal training set sizes. The work by Tubiana and Monasson (2017) and Tubiana et al. (2019) is also highlighted, focusing on the "compositional phase" of RBMs, where visible configurations are formed from combinations of features encoded by strongly activated hidden units. 

The paper contrasts its approach with earlier analyses that utilized statistical mechanics techniques to study ensembles of RBMs in the thermodynamic limit, aiming to understand typical case behavior. Instead, this work emphasizes the connection between the marginal distribution of observed nodes and the activation function for a single realization of the system. The authors suggest that combining statistical mechanics approaches from earlier studies with their findings could enhance the understanding of RBMs' phase diagrams, particularly in identifying the most expressive regimes with diverse activation functions.

Additionally, observations from training RBMs on the MNIST dataset indicate that nonlinear activation functions lead to a low-order interaction model for the interactive marginal distribution of observed nodes. This model exhibits a hierarchy in interaction strengths, outperforming simple pairwise models when a sufficient number of hidden nodes is utilized.
