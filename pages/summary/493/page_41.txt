The paper discusses activation functions within the context of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), focusing first on the linear activation function. It describes an RBM with a quadratic separable potential, defining it as a Bernoulli-Gaussian RBM. In this case, the conditional probability distribution is Gaussian, centered around the mean, with unit variance. The linear activation function results from the Gaussian nature of the distribution, where the mode matches the mean. The cumulant generating function for this activation is derived, leading to specific cumulants that indicate a Gaussian distribution, with the first two cumulants being non-zero.

Next, the paper introduces the ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) activation function. It defines a potential that is quadratic for non-negative values and constant for negative values. This distinction suggests a non-linear behavior, which is critical in many machine learning applications. The implications of this activation function on the behavior of the RBM are explored further, emphasizing its role in enhancing the model's capacity to learn complex patterns.
