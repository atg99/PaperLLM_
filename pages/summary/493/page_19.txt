The paper discusses the performance of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) with randomly generated weights drawn from a Gaussian distribution. The hidden biases are uniformly set to 5, while visible biases are adjusted to ensure that first order interaction terms are zero. The experiments involve sampling 512,000 data points to analyze the alignment between the observed state frequencies and the probabilities predicted by the interaction model across 20 independent samples. The results, illustrated in Figure 4, indicate a strong correlation between the two, with error bars representing the standard deviation of frequency values. Additionally, the insets provide insights into the parameter values of the interaction model in relation to interaction order, highlighting the presence of significant higher order interaction terms. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of the chosen configuration in yielding meaningful results from RBMs using non-linear activation functions.
