This paper investigates the capabilities of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) with respect to pairwise interactions and activation functions. It establishes that the Linear activation function is unique in generating models with a finite set of nonzero interactions from a generic RBM weight matrix. The authors demonstrate that RBMs employing the Linear activation function yield the best low-rank approximation for any pairwise model, specifically achieving full rank connectivity with an Ising model when the number of hidden nodes equals the number of visible nodes minus one.

Additionally, RBMs utilizing the ReLU activation function exhibit similar efficiency, suggesting a semi-Restricted Boltzmann Machine interpretation due to the resemblance of higher-order interaction terms to standard RBM structures. The presence of higher-order terms in both ReLU and Exponential cases disrupts trivial symmetries in pairwise representations, enhancing the interpretability of the latent variable representation. 

The analysis reveals that pairwise interactions are consistently characterized by a Hopfield-like term in the small parameter limit, regardless of the potential function selected. The Exponential case, in particular, presents a straightforward representation of higher-order terms that efficiently generalizes pairwise interactions.

Numerical tests validate the framework proposed in the paper, demonstrating accurate descriptions of interactions in various simple RBM architectures. The study includes an application to RBMs trained on MNIST data, revealing scaling relationships in pairwise interactions based on different hidden node configurations and activation functions. While field parameters displayed consistent patterns, triplet interactions lacked clear trends. The findings confirm that in most cases, the small parameter approximation for pairwise interaction parameters holds true, indicating that typical interaction magnitudes decrease with increasing interaction order.
