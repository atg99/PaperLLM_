This paper investigates the influence of the number of hidden nodes in Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) on pairwise interaction terms and field values generated by various activation functions. Specifically, it compares RBMs with different hidden layer sizes, focusing on ReLU and Linear activation functions, while analyzing how these pairwise interactions change with the number of hidden nodes, denoted as M.

Figure 6a illustrates the pairwise interaction terms produced by RBMs with varying hidden nodes against a benchmark RBM with M = 1200. The analysis reveals that the primary effect of increasing hidden nodes is a linear scaling, which is quantified by estimating a scaling parameter through regression. Figure 6b demonstrates that this scaling parameter grows significantly as M approaches 1200, with a rapid increase observed for the Linear activation function, reaching a value of 0.9 at M = 100, while the ReLU function exhibits a slower growth rate.

Furthermore, the study explores the relationship between the fields and pairwise interactions induced on visible units across different activation functions, as shown in Figure 7. The findings indicate that the fields generated by RBMs using Exponential and ReLU activations are quite similar, whereas other activation functions display more substantial differences, particularly the Step function compared to Linear. Notably, large negative field values remain consistent across activation functions, which aligns with expectations given their association with pixel values.

Overall, the paper highlights the complex interplay between the number of hidden nodes and the resulting interaction terms and fields in RBMs, emphasizing the distinct behaviors of various activation functions.
