The paper investigates the performance of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) with different activation functions and hidden node sizes in terms of pseudo-likelihood and likelihood. It presents findings based on experiments conducted over varying epochs and hidden node configurations (M = 50 and M = 1200). 

Figure 5 illustrates the pseudo-likelihood evaluated from a random subset of 100 data points, averaged over a moving window of 20 epochs. It reveals that RBMs with Linear activation functions yield the highest pseudo-likelihood and likelihood for smaller hidden layer sizes (M < 400). This suggests that a limited number of parameters can effectively capture pairwise correlations in the data, which the Linear activation function excels at modeling.

In contrast, non-linear activation functions demonstrate a growing ability to represent data as the number of hidden nodes increases, indicating their capacity to model more complex interactions beyond pairwise correlations. The likelihood values for the standard RBM with Step activation are comparable to those in prior studies, yet remain lower than those achieved with other configurations for M < 400. Notably, the likelihood for the RBM using ReLU activation saturates after M = 400, suggesting a limit to its effectiveness in this context. 

Overall, the results highlight the significance of activation functions and hidden node sizes in RBM performance, particularly in capturing data variability through different correlation levels.
