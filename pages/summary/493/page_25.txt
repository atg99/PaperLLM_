The paper discusses the fields and pairwise interactions generated by Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) with various activation functions, specifically focusing on the Exponential, ReLU, Step, and Linear functions. It examines the behavior of these interactions across three different hidden layer sizes: M = 50, M = 400, and M = 1200.

Key findings indicate that for M = 400, the fields induced by different activation functions show significant alignment, suggesting a consistency in how these functions influence the representation of MNIST images. Specifically, fields corresponding to pixels that are frequently off (with values around -30) are consistent across activation functions, although not displayed in the figures. 

The pairwise interactions, however, exhibit varied relationships depending on the activation function. For M = 400, the parameters derived from Exponential and ReLU activation functions align well, while those from the Step function appear to relate to the Exponential function through linear scaling. The interactions induced by the Linear RBM also show some similarities to the non-linear functions, but with greater variability.

Overall, the study highlights that fields tend to exhibit better consistency across activation functions compared to pairwise interactions, a trend that becomes even more evident when examining third-order interactions. The results underscore the importance of activation function choice in the representation capabilities of RBMs.
