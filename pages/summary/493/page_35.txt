The paper discusses the hierarchical structure in the interactions of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), attributing this to both the data structure and the characteristics of the learning algorithm. Key factors such as parameter initialization, small learning rates, and regularization encourage small parameter values. This is crucial for using unbounded activation functions like ReLU and exponential functions, as it helps prevent large inputs that could lead to training divergences.

Additionally, the paper highlights the potential of RBMs in graphical model selection for binary variable models. By adjusting the connectivity of hidden nodes and the type of activation functions, researchers can control the maximum order of interactions represented by the RBM. This allows for comparative analysis of different interaction orders, such as pairwise versus three-body interactions.

Finally, the paper suggests exploring the possibility of selecting or learning activation functions tailored to specific datasets and tasks. Recent findings indicate that the choice of activation function may significantly impact the efficiency of learning algorithms. The authors propose that by aligning activation function selection with the characteristics of the data, improvements in learning efficiency for specific tasks may be achievable.
