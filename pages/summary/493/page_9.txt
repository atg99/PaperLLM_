The paper discusses the interactions within Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) through a series of equations that outline how visible nodes interact via hidden nodes. Equation (15a) highlights that the hidden layer contributes additively to the bias term of a node if there is a corresponding hidden node with a non-zero weight. This principle extends to pairwise interactions (I(2) k1;k2) and higher-order interactions, indicating that a higher order of interaction among visible nodes is only possible if there is at least one hidden node connecting them.

The text emphasizes that while it is not feasible to restrict the potential U(z) to a finite set of interactions beyond pairwise models for every weight matrix, it is possible to limit the order of interactions in RBMs by constraining the weight matrix W. This constraint ensures that the maximum number of connections for each hidden node is kept below a specified threshold.

Furthermore, the paper asserts that RBMs can approximate any order of interaction among visible nodes, with accuracy determined by the potential choice and the number of hidden nodes, improving with an increased number of hidden nodes. The relationship between RBMs and models of interacting binary variables enables a comparative analysis of different RBMs based on their ability to parameterize interaction parameters.
