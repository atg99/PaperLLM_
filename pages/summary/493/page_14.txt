The paper discusses the limitations of representing a matrix BB| with a lower rank matrix JLin=WW| when M is less than N. Specifically, the Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) approximates BB| using a rank M+1 matrix, achieving a close representation by matching the largest M eigenvalues, thus optimizing the Frobenius norm distance. The Eckart-Young theorem supports this approach by stating that the best low-rank approximation is obtained through truncated singular value decomposition.

In the context of the ReLU activation function, the paper explains its parametrization for pairwise interactions, which includes a Hopfield-like term WW|. The threshold vector c influences the second term of the ReLU representation without affecting the Hopfield-like term. This allows for adjusting the significance of higher-order terms, making it possible to transition from a Hopfield-like representation to one that captures more complex interactions by selecting appropriate values for c. The ReLU function is characterized as a hybrid between Linear and Step activation functions, utilizing the Hopfield-like term for pairwise interactions while also incorporating higher-order terms akin to those seen in standard RBMs.
