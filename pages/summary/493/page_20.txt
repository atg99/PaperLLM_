In this section, the authors analyze the properties of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) trained on the MNIST dataset, which consists of 60000 training images and 10000 test images of handwritten digits, each represented by a 28-by-28 pixel grid. To utilize RBMs as generative models, the pixel intensity values are binarized by setting a threshold at 128, converting each pixel into a binary variable. This results in a flattened binary vector of 784 entries for each image.

The training process employs the Contrastive Divergence algorithm with mini-batches of 100 data points. The learning rate is adaptive, decreasing over time, while the number of steps in the CD-k algorithm increases with the number of epochs, specifically set as k=ceil(epoch/10). By the end of training, the process resembles a CD-50 scheme with a learning rate of 1/50. The initial learning rate is minimized to ensure smooth convergence over 500 epochs, totaling 300000 updates, regardless of the hidden layer size.

Visible biases are initialized based on the average of the training set, using a small pseudo-count to prevent null entries. Weights are initialized with random signs and a specific distribution to avoid large inputs that could cause training divergences, particularly for activation functions like ReLU and Exponential that are unbounded and strictly positive.
