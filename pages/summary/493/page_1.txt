This paper investigates the distributions that Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) can represent, focusing on the influence of different activation functions on the hidden nodes concerning the marginal distribution of observed binary nodes. The authors derive an exact expression for these marginals, representing them as a model of interacting binary variables, with the interaction forms explicitly linked to the activation functions of the hidden nodes.

The analysis delves into the properties of these interactions and examines how the accuracy of the RBM in approximating distributions over binary variables is affected by both the choice of activation function and the number of hidden nodes. Notably, the authors observe that when the inferred RBM parameters are weak, a consistent pattern emerges in the expression of interaction terms, minimizing differences across various activation functions.

The study finds that the weak parameter approximation provides a reliable model for different RBMs trained on the MNIST dataset, suggesting that the inferred models predominantly exhibit low-order interactions. This insight highlights the potential of RBMs in modeling complex variable interactions while maintaining computational efficiency.
