In this paper, we investigate the significance of the hidden layer activation function in the context of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs). We highlight how activation functions, previously overlooked, are essential in determining the performance characteristics of neural networks. The paper references key findings that demonstrate the advantages of various activation functions, particularly Rectified Linear Units (ReLU) and Threshold-Linear (TL) functions, over traditional binary units. These findings include enhanced performance in deep neural networks and smoother learning dynamics, which collectively illustrate the critical role of activation functions in shaping the capabilities of neural architectures.

The paper further elaborates on the structure of RBMs, which consist of a bipartite undirected graphical model with visible and hidden units that interact exclusively across classes. Despite their simplicity compared to Boltzmann Machines, RBMs are recognized for their effectiveness and efficiency in training. Their versatility allows them to function as generative models and tools for classification, thus making them integral to the development of deep learning technologies. Additionally, the application of RBMs in neuroscience highlights their ability to model complex relationships within data, particularly in understanding neuronal firing patterns. 

Overall, the paper aims to deepen the understanding of how the choice of activation function influences the representational capabilities of RBMs, thereby contributing to the broader discourse on neural computation and the optimization of network performance.
