The paper discusses the relationship between different activation functions in Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), particularly focusing on the ReLU and Exponential activation functions. It highlights that for a specific parameter, the relationship between the error function and the exponential function can be closely approximated for large values of x, indicating a conceptual bridge between RBMs with ReLU activation and standard semi-Restricted Boltzmann Machines.

The Exponential activation function is noted for its ability to encode higher-order terms effectively, extending the efficient pairwise representation seen in linear cases. An RBM with M hidden nodes can approximate interaction terms to their symmetric outer product decomposition, which is akin to a generalization of eigenvalue decomposition for symmetric tensors.

The paper also addresses the issue of multiple weight matrix solutions that can represent the same interaction matrix in the linear case, leading to challenges in interpreting hidden node representations due to the unidentifiability of latent representations. This problem is acknowledged in Factor Analysis as well. However, in the cases of ReLU and Exponential activation functions, the introduction of higher-order interaction terms breaks this symmetry. Consequently, the learning algorithm can more effectively choose a parameter set that not only fits pairwise interactions but also accommodates higher-order terms, enhancing the identifiability of hidden variables. This improvement is significant for model interpretation and latent variable discovery.
