The paper presents an analysis of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) with random parameters drawn from a Gaussian distribution. Specifically, the study investigates the relationship between the mean frequency of observed states in RBMs and the probabilities calculated using an interaction model. The results are based on a sample of 512,000 data points, indicating a strong alignment between the observed frequencies across 20 independent samples and the interaction model's calculated probabilities. The figures demonstrate that the strength of higher-order interaction terms diminishes rapidly with the interaction order. The findings suggest that, under certain conditions where the weights are normally distributed and kept below a magnitude of one, RBMs can be effectively represented by pairwise models. Additionally, to enhance the representation of higher-order interactions, the paper proposes increasing the weight values and hidden node biases, thereby strengthening visible-hidden connections.
