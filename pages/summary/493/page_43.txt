The paper discusses the characteristics of a distribution defined by its mode and mean, highlighting the role of the Heaviside function and the sigmoid function in these definitions. The mode of the distribution is expressed as a step function, denoted as z = H(I - c), while the mean is modeled as a sigmoid function given by hzi = exp(I - c) / (1 + exp(I - c)). The cumulant generating function is derived as K(q) = log(1 + exp(q - c)) / (1 + exp(-c)), and the second cumulant is provided by the formula (2) = exp(-c) / (1 + exp(-c))^2.

Additionally, the paper introduces an Exponential activation function characterized by a specific potential U(z), leading to a conditional Poisson distribution P(z|v) = e^(-λ) λ^z / z! for z in natural numbers, with λ = exp(I - c). The mean of this distribution is given as hzi = exp(I - c) and the mode is approximated using the floor function, z = floor(exp(I - c)). Notably, the function ρ(z) equates to P(z|v) when I = 0, confirming that it also follows a Poisson distribution with parameter ~λ = exp(-c). The cumulant generating function for this Poisson distribution is K(q) = exp(-c)(exp(q) - 1), indicating that all cumulants are equal to ~λ.
