The paper discusses the leading term of small parameter expansions in the context of interactions of order s, specifically focusing on the expression I(s) which incorporates weights wk1 to wks. It is noted that this expression reduces to a simpler form for s equal to 2 and resembles the structure of the Exponential activation function when approximated under certain conditions.

The analysis further explores the Linear activation function and its limitations in modeling pairwise interactions. It establishes that an RBM (Restricted Boltzmann Machine) with a linear activation function can only accurately represent pairwise distributions for s equal to 1 and 2. The paper argues that while any symmetric interaction matrix J can be decomposed into a product involving orthogonal matrices, the linear model can represent pairwise interactions either exactly or as closely as possible, depending on the number of hidden nodes.

The decomposition of J into its eigenvalues and the construction of the hidden-visible interaction matrix W are discussed. The matrix BB| is identified as having maximum rank under the assumption that the minimum eigenvalue is not degenerate. The approach allows the RBM to effectively capture the pairwise interactions as defined by the matrix J, integrating the smallest eigenvalue into the bias term to enhance the model's representational capacity.
