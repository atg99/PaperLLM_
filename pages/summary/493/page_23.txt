This paper examines the performance of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) utilizing different activation functions, particularly focusing on Linear, ReLU, and Exponential functions. It highlights an unexpected observation where RBMs with a Linear activation function perform comparably to those with more complex functions like ReLU, despite the latter's greater representational capacity. This could be attributed to the limitations of the learning algorithm, which may not effectively explore the parameter space, leading to biases in learning (Baldassi et al., 2020).

The study reveals that RBMs employing the Exponential activation function yield higher likelihood values than others when the number of hidden nodes exceeds 400. This efficiency is linked to the ability of Exponential RBMs to approximate the symmetric tensor decomposition of interactions across all orders, enhancing their representational power.

Further analysis is conducted on the effective interactions between visible nodes as a function of the number of hidden nodes and activation function choice. For Linear activation, the pairwise interactions stabilize after 100 hidden nodes, indicating that the representation saturates, aligning with findings of likelihood function behavior. Conversely, with non-linear activations like ReLU, the magnitude of pairwise interactions and field parameters increases as more hidden nodes are added, showcasing a distinct structural evolution in the interactions as they scale.
