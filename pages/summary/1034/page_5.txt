The paper discusses four key tensions related to the ethical implications of AI applications in society. 

1. **Data Utilization vs. Privacy**: While using machine learning and big data can enhance public services like healthcare, it raises significant concerns regarding individual privacy and autonomy, necessitating careful consideration of data protection and consent.

2. **Algorithmic Accuracy vs. Fairness**: The reliance on algorithms for decision-making can improve accuracy but may also lead to discrimination against underrepresented groups, highlighting the need for equitable treatment in predictive modeling.

3. **Personalization vs. Solidarity**: Increased personalization in digital services can enhance user experience; however, this fine-tuned differentiation risks undermining societal values of citizenship and community cohesion.

4. **Automation vs. Dignity**: Although automation can enhance convenience and efficiency in daily tasks, it may also threaten human skills and cultural diversity, raising questions about the balance between technological advancement and human dignity.

The paper emphasizes the importance of identifying further tensions by asking critical questions about the risks and benefits of AI applications, especially regarding their impact on different societal groups and values over time. 

Resolving these tensions often requires complex political processes that prioritize certain values while engaging the public to understand diverse stakeholder interests. In some cases, practical solutions may emerge through further research, allowing for the advancement of conflicting values without necessitating strict trade-offs. 

Ultimately, the paper calls for a shift from high-level ethical principles to a more grounded focus on the specific tensions that arise in real-world applications of AI, advocating for the development of standards and regulations that can better address these complexities.
