The paper reviews the evolution of principles in AI ethics over the past two years, drawing parallels with bioethics, which has a well-established tradition of using principles to govern medical practices. It highlights limitations in applying these principles to real-world cases, arguing that a deeper exploration of the tensions arising from their application is necessary. The paper identifies key tensions associated with AI's societal impact and suggests that addressing these tensions should be the next step in AI ethics discourse.

The emergence of principles in AI ethics is noted, with various organizations and initiatives developing sets of principles aimed at addressing core issues like bias and fairness. Notable examples include the Asilomar AI principles and the ACM's principles for Algorithmic Transparency and Accountability. These principles largely converge on the notion that AI technologies should be used for the common good and respect values like fairness, privacy, and autonomy. However, the authors question whether this convergence indicates a deep consensus or merely reflects superficial agreement among different groups.

While principles serve as a valuable framework for applied ethics and can guide institutional decision-making, the paper emphasizes that they are insufficient on their own. Effective application of principles requires them to be actionable and context-sensitive, as highlighted by the challenges faced in bioethics. The authors point out that principles can be interpreted differently by various groups, leading to ambiguity and masking significant moral disagreements. Therefore, they advocate for a shift in focus from relying solely on principles to understanding the complexities and tensions involved in specific AI-related cases.
