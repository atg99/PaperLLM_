The paper "The Role and Limits of Principles in AI Ethics: Towards a Focus on Tensions" by Jess Whittlestone and colleagues discusses the increasing number of principles proposed for AI ethics, which generally emphasize the importance of using AI for the common good, preventing harm, and respecting values like fairness and privacy. While these principles are a starting point, the authors argue they are often too broad to effectively guide ethical practices in AI.

Drawing parallels with bioethics, the paper highlights the limitations of these principles, particularly their tendency to conflict when applied to real-world situations. The authors advocate for a shift in focus towards understanding and addressing the tensions that arise during the implementation of these principles. By recognizing these tensions, stakeholders can make informed decisions on how to resolve conflicts and develop more practical and relevant ethical frameworks for AI.

The paper outlines various sectors involved in AI ethics, including technology companies, professional bodies, standards-setting organizations, and government regulators, all of which can benefit from clearer guidelines that address the complexities of real-world applications. The authors conclude that acknowledging and resolving these ethical tensions is crucial for advancing the field of AI ethics and ensuring its effective application in society.
