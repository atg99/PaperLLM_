The paper discusses the concept of fairness in AI ethics, highlighting the political disagreements surrounding its definition. It notes that cultural differences can influence how various groups prioritize fairness alongside other values. The authors argue that to make ethical principles more actionable, it is necessary to formalize them into standards and regulations, acknowledging that this process is complicated by the conflicts that arise when applying general principles to specific cases.

The generality of ethical principles often limits their practical applicability, as many are too broad to provide clear guidance. While phrases like "social good" are widely accepted, they lack specific commitments and can be interpreted differently across ideological lines. The paper points out that only more specific principles, such as commitments against developing autonomous weapons, can effectively guide action and accountability, though such specificity may limit their relevance.

The authors also highlight that principles often conflict in practice. For instance, the UK House of Lords AI Committee emphasizes the need for explainability in AI systems, which can clash with the goal of using these systems for social benefit. This tension illustrates the complex trade-offs involved in AI ethics, where the benefits of advanced algorithms may outweigh the need for full transparency.

The paper advocates for a focus on the tensions that exist between competing values in AI ethics. It defines tension as the conflict between important goals, which may necessitate compromises. Some tensions are strict moral trade-offs, while others are contingent on current technological and societal constraints. The authors argue that recognizing these tensions is crucial for advancing AI ethics, as it can bridge the gap between abstract principles and practical applications, acknowledge diverse values, highlight areas needing new solutions, and clarify ambiguities and knowledge gaps.

In conclusion, the paper suggests that addressing these tensions is a vital step toward developing a more practical and action-guiding framework for AI ethics.
