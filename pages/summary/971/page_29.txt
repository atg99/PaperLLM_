The optimization is formulated to maximize the expected advantage while adhering to constraints on cumulative cost and Kullback-Leibler divergence, transforming the non-convex problem into a convex one through first and second-order approximations. However, this approach introduces potential errors and computational burdens, particularly due to the necessity of inverting the Fisher information matrix.

Recent methods like Conservative Safety Critics (CSC) and First Order Constrained Optimization in Policy Space (FOCOPS) address safe exploration in reinforcement learning. CSC employs a conservative safety critic to manage environmental state functions and maintain safety constraints throughout training, demonstrating a balance between policy enhancement and safety. This method promises performance that is asymptotically no worse than standard reinforcement learning.

Overall, while current approaches aim to simplify constrained policy optimization, they face inherent difficulties associated with non-convexity and computational efficiency. Further theoretical analysis is needed to clarify the implications of convex approximations in this context.
