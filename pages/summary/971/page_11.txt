The paper presents a comprehensive overview of safe reinforcement learning (RL) methods, focusing on the immediate costs associated with optimizing performance. It emphasizes the importance of considering the entire trajectory cost in applications like robot-motion planning and resource allocation. Unlike other surveys, this work addresses the "2H3W" problems and provides an in-depth analysis of safe RL algorithms, sampling complexity, and convergence from both model-based and model-free perspectives.

The paper categorizes safe RL methods based on various criteria. Objective optimization methods incorporate cost as a safety measure, while knowledge utilization methods enhance safety during exploration using external knowledge. Safe RL approaches are further divided into policy-based and value-based methods, with specific references provided for each category.

The analysis also distinguishes between single-agent and multi-agent safe RL settings. Single-agent methods focus on balancing reward improvement with cost constraints, while multi-agent methods must consider the rewards and safety of all agents involved in a potentially unstable environment.

The section aims to provide a holistic view of safe RL methods, detailing model-based and model-free approaches, including policy optimization, formal methods, control theory, and Gaussian processes. Key findings are summarized in tables highlighting the distinctions between model-based and model-free safe RL methods.
