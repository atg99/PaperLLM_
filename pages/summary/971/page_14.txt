The paper discusses various approaches to ensure safety in reinforcement learning (RL), focusing on Lyapunov functions, Model Predictive Control (MPC), control barrier functions (CBFs), and formal methods. 

Lyapunov functions are traditionally hand-crafted, making them difficult to generalize for agent safety and performance. MPC methods are highlighted for their ability to make robust decisions in Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs) and establish a safety framework. 

CBFs are another approach to enhance learning safety, requiring a dynamics model, which complicates their deployment in RL contexts. Several studies demonstrate the efficacy of CBFs in improving sample efficiency and safety in applications like autonomous driving. However, the design of CBFs necessitates a deep understanding of the model or safety certificates, which can be challenging in complex environments.

Formal methods differ from other approaches by aiming to ensure safety without relying on unsafe probabilities. They typically require extensive model knowledge and may not outperform other methods in terms of rewards. The computational demands for verification can be high, and the curse of dimensionality presents significant challenges when applying formal methods to RL safety, especially in continuous, high-dimensional spaces.
