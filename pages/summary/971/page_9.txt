The paper discusses the concept of Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDP) as an extension of Markov Decision Processes (MDP) that incorporates safety constraints. The key components include the advantage function Aπ, which represents the difference between the action-value function Qπ and the value function Vπ. The performance objective is defined through the expected value function J(π), which is derived from the initial state distribution ρ0.

In CMDP, the constraint set C consists of cost value functions ci and their corresponding safety bounds bi, leading to the definition of expected cost functions Ci(π). The feasible policy set ΠC is established to ensure that the policies not only belong to the set of all possible policies ΠS but also satisfy the cost constraints. The primary goal of safe reinforcement learning (RL) is to maximize reward while minimizing costs to ensure safety, formalized in the optimization problem where the optimal policy π* is sought.

Additionally, the paper reviews existing surveys on safe RL, noting that many do not provide comprehensive theoretical analyses or address critical problems in safe RL, known as the "2H3W" problems. While some surveys explore safe RL from control theory and robotics perspectives, they often lack depth in sample complexity and other theoretical aspects crucial for understanding safe RL frameworks.
