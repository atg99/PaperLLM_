The paper discusses various methods for ensuring safety in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on multi-timescale approaches and control theory-based methods. 

Borkar's and Tessler et al.'s methods utilize a multi-timescale approach where cost is integrated into the reward within primal-dual frameworks. However, these methods face challenges in tuning the learning rate for real-world applications and may not guarantee safety during agent training. Yu et al. adopt a similar strategy within actor-critic settings, converting non-convex constrained problems into locally convex ones and ensuring convergence to optimal solutions. Their approach emphasizes state-action safety optimization, requiring the Lipschitz condition and policy gradient estimation.

Chow et al. propose a policy gradient method that incorporates risk optimization through CVaR and chance-constrained optimization to enhance safety, but they also acknowledge potential safety risks during training. Paternain et al. contribute duality theory for CMDP optimization, demonstrating the ability to solve primal problems through dual optimization, even in non-convex scenarios.

The paper contrasts these with control theory-based approaches, which generally offer more robust safety guarantees but may be limited in their adaptability due to strict dynamics model requirements. Berkenkamp et al. present a model-based RL algorithm that utilizes Lyapunov functions to maintain stability under Gaussian process assumptions, effectively ensuring a high-probability safe policy in continuous environments.
