The paper discusses the challenges in reconciling nominal and real systems, particularly in the presence of sensor noise from faulty sensors or adversarial attacks. It highlights the trade-off dilemmas in reinforcement learning (RL) and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), specifically between exploration and exploitation, as well as reward and cost. This multi-objective problem necessitates careful consideration in both competitive and cooperative game settings. In competitive scenarios, modeling opponents' decisions with limited information while ensuring safety is crucial. In cooperative contexts, developing policies that enhance overall team rewards while respecting individual constraints is essential. Mixed-game settings require a balance between local and global rewards under safety considerations.

The paper outlines three potential future research directions for safe RL. The first involves integrating game theory into safe MARL to address the identified challenges, particularly in varying game settings. The second suggests applying information theory to manage uncertainty in reward signals and cost estimations, particularly in large-scale MARL environments. Lastly, the third direction proposes leveraging insights from human-brain theory and biological systems to inspire the development of safe MARL algorithms, enhancing agent interactions through biologically-informed strategies.
