The paper discusses the importance of ensuring safety in the application of reinforcement learning (RL) in real-world settings. It outlines various safety policies that can be applied, including policy optimization methods, formal methods, control theory methods, and Gaussian process methods. These approaches aim to enable agents to reach safe states or perform safe actions during their operations.

The applications of safe RL span multiple domains such as autonomous driving, robotics, video compression, vehicle scheduling, recommender systems, wireless security, satellite docking, edge computing, and chemical processes. To assess the effectiveness of safety measures, the paper identifies safety benchmarks like AI Safety Gridworlds, Safety Gym, Safe MAMuJoCo, Safe MAIG, and Safe MARobosuite.

The paper highlights several challenges in achieving safety, including ensuring human-compatible safe RL, establishing industry deployment standards for safe deep reinforcement learning (DRL), and efficiently guaranteeing safety in environments with a large number of agents. It also addresses safety complexity, distinguishing between value-based and policy-based methods, and emphasizes the need for agents to interact with their environments to sample trajectories effectively.

One of the key challenges noted is balancing exploration and exploitation in RL, particularly when improving performance while adhering to safety constraints. The paper stresses the necessity of determining the appropriate number of sample trajectories required for safe RL algorithms to converge and maintain safety throughout their applications.
