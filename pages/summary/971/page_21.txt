The focus on Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) allows for the specification of safety properties during policy formation, enhancing the reliability of the agent's actions in uncertain environments. This approach contrasts with Gaussian processes by emphasizing the integration of formal specifications into the reinforcement learning framework. 

While methods leveraging Gaussian processes prioritize safe exploration and the modeling of safe states, formal methods like the one proposed by Hasanbeig et al. highlight the importance of constraining agent behavior through logical expressions. Both methodologies aim to ensure safety in reinforcement learning but do so through different mechanisms. However, the challenge remains in the rigorous definition and application of constraints to ensure consistent safety across various scenarios. The exploration of both GP and formal methods showcases the evolving landscape of safe reinforcement learning, emphasizing the need for further research into convergence guarantees and optimization across diverse objectives.
