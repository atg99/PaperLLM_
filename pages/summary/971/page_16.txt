The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of model-based safe reinforcement learning (RL) methods, focusing on various approaches to constrained Markov decision processes (CMDP). The analysis includes a review of several notable works, highlighting their methodologies and convergence properties.

1. Borkar (2005) developed an actor-critic algorithm based on Lagrange multipliers and saddle point properties for CMDP, confirming its convergence.
2. Chow et al. (2018) addressed two risk-constrained MDP problems using policy gradient and actor-critic algorithms, specifically through Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) constraints, achieving convergence.
3. Tessler et al. (2019) introduced a constrained actor-critic approach called RCPO, employing a multi-timescale strategy, also confirming convergence.
4. Yu et al. (2019) proposed a successive convex relaxation algorithm for CMDP, integrating actor-critic updates, which ensures convergence.
5. Koppel et al. (2019) utilized saddle points within a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, implementing a projected stochastic primal-dual method for CMDP, confirming convergence.
6. Polymenakos et al. (2019) focused on ensuring safety during training using a PILCO framework with policy gradient methods, but did not confirm convergence.
7. Paternain et al. (2019) explored non-convex CMDP through primal-dual algorithms, achieving convergence.
8. Bharadhwaj et al. (2021) proposed a method that avoids unsafe actions during training without requiring prior knowledge of unsafe states, utilizing conservative critics for safety exploration and confirming convergence.
9. Miryoosefi and Jin (2021) presented a reward-free approach to CMDP with general convex constraints, but did not confirm convergence.
10. Paternain et al. (2022) incorporated safety into the problem as a probabilistic version of positive invariance, applying a stochastic primal-dual method for safe policies, but also did not confirm convergence.

This summary captures the key concepts and findings of the paper, emphasizing the methodologies and their implications for safety in reinforcement learning.
