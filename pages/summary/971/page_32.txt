In this section, the paper discusses the sampling complexity and safety violation analysis of both model-based and model-free safe reinforcement learning (RL) with a focus on O(ε)-optimality. A policy π is defined as O(ε)-optimal if the difference between its utility and the optimal utility, as well as the constraint violation, is within ε. 

The authors outline three key assumptions that underpin the analysis:
1. **Feasibility**: It is assumed that there exists a feasible policy π that meets the constraints of the unknown Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), ensuring the existence of an optimal policy.
2. **Slater Condition**: This condition asserts the existence of a vector that satisfies the constraints of the CMDP, ensuring a feasible solution.
3. **Linear CMDP**: The CMDP is assumed to have a linear structure with specific feature maps and vectors that define the transition dynamics and reward functions.

The analysis provides a lower bound on the sample complexity necessary to achieve O(ε)-optimality, which is crucial for understanding the guarantees of safety in reinforcement learning algorithms. 

In model-based safe RL, techniques such as linear programming and Lagrangian approximation are commonly employed, particularly when the transition model is either known or accurately estimated. The OptDual-CMDP algorithm is highlighted for achieving sublinear regret concerning both utility and constraint violations, requiring a specific sample complexity to achieve O(ε)-optimality. Additionally, the Upper-Confidence Constrained Fixed-Horizon RL method is noted for offering a proximal optimal policy under certain probabilistic guarantees. 

Overall, this section lays the groundwork for understanding the complexities involved in achieving safe and optimal policies in reinforcement learning settings.
