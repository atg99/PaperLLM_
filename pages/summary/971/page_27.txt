The paper discusses the emerging field of safe multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), emphasizing the need for algorithms that incorporate safety constraints. It highlights the theoretical underpinnings of safe reinforcement learning (RL) and contrasts it with standard RL. 

The section begins by outlining fundamental differences between standard RL, which operates under deterministic stationary policies, and safe RL, where such policies may not always exist. It introduces two significant theoretical approaches in safe RL: primal-dual methods and constrained policy optimization. The exploration of safe RL includes aspects like sampling complexity and safety violation analysis, aiming to clarify the theoretical foundations and analytical techniques unique to safe RL.

The paper presents two theorems illustrating these differences. The first theorem indicates that in constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs), an optimal policy may be either deterministic or randomized, with randomness occurring only in specific states. The second theorem asserts that verifying the feasibility of a safe policy in CMDPs is NP-hard, underscoring the computational challenges associated with ensuring safety in RL applications. Overall, the section aims for a comprehensive understanding of safe RL theories and their implications.
