This paper discusses various methods for model-free safe reinforcement learning (RL), focusing on convergence analysis and the effectiveness of different approaches to constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs). 

The authors provide a meta algorithm aimed at solving CMDP problems through model-free methods, highlighting the challenge of reward-free methods for CMDPs. They analyze the primal-dual gap, demonstrating that it can be made arbitrarily small and can converge with a suitably small step-size. 

Several approaches are evaluated, including constrained policy optimization using trust regions, the use of logarithmic barrier functions with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and effective linear programming methods for generating Lyapunov functions. The paper also explores two-step optimization strategies, where the first step focuses on reward maximization followed by safety considerations. 

Notable contributions include a Gaussian process optimization method that expands safe regions before optimizing rewards and a constrained neural network approach for selecting safe actions in both simulation and real-world environments. 

The findings underscore the importance of safety constraints in RL and the potential of various optimization methods to achieve both feasibility and optimality in discrete and continuous systems.
