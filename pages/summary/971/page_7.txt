This paper reviews the advancements in safe reinforcement learning (RL) over the past two decades, addressing key challenges and problems associated with its application. It presents the trolley problem as a metaphor for the ethical dilemmas faced in balancing safety when implementing RL. The authors identify five fundamental problems: safety policy, safety complexity, safety applications, safety benchmarks, and safety challenges, emphasizing the need for sophisticated algorithms to ensure the safety of agents, humans, and the environment.

The paper highlights the insufficient focus on safety complexity and benchmarks, particularly in industrial contexts, while also discussing the potential insights that addressing safety applications and challenges may provide for future research. The contributions of the paper include an investigation of safe RL research progress, a detailed analysis of the five fundamental problems, and a review of safe RL algorithms and applications, such as safe model-based and model-free learning.

The structure of the paper is organized into several sections: an introduction to safe RL background and related surveys, an overview of safe RL, a detailed analysis of safe RL theory and algorithms, application analysis, an introduction to safe RL benchmarks, and a discussion of ongoing challenges and questions. The conclusion summarizes the findings and implications for future research in safe RL.
