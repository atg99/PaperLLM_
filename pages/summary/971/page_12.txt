3.1 Methods of Model-Based Safe Reinforcement Learning

Model-based deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods exhibit better learning efficiency compared to model-free approaches, despite the challenges in building accurate models. These methods are applicable in various domains such as robotics, transportation planning, and logistics. Model-based safe RL methods have been demonstrated to effectively address safe problems, such as safe robot control. This section explores model-based safe RL methods through different perspectives: policy optimization, control theory, formal methods, and Gaussian processes.

3.1.1 Policy Optimization-Based Approaches

Policy optimization methods focus on finding safe policies by analyzing cumulative cost values over trajectories. Moldovan and Abbeel utilized the Chernoff function to achieve near-optimal bounds with favorable theoretical properties. Their approach incorporates a parameter, δ, to balance reward performance and safety—setting δ to 1 ignores safety, while setting it to 0 prioritizes risk management. They tested their method in grid world environments and air travel planning, although it requires significant time to recover policies from risk areas, taking ten times longer than value iteration.

In contrast to Chernoff function methods, Borkar proposed an actor-critic RL approach for constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs) based on the envelope theorem. This method employs a three-time scale process to analyze primal-dual optimization in detail, with distinct roles for the critic, actor, and dual ascent across fast, middle, and slow timescales. Bharadhwaj et al. developed another actor-critic method to tackle safe RL challenges by introducing a conservative safety critic to estimate safety levels, employing primal-dual gradient descent to optimize reward and cost while constraining failure probabilities. This approach effectively bounds the probability of failures during policy execution.
