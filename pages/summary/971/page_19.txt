This model is designed for large-scale optimization and includes analyses of regret bounds and safety violations. While similar methodologies have been applied in tabular settings, such as in the work of Wei et al., Xiong et al. proposed a step-wise safe RL method that leverages optimistic estimations of transition kernels and cost functions to enhance safety in reward-free scenarios. Their analyses of safety violations and regret bounds extend the applicability of their method to environments like multi-robot systems. 

Despite demonstrating improved performance over baseline methods, there is an opportunity for further exploration in diverse domains, particularly in safe multi-robot control and planning. Recent advancements in primal-based methods have emerged as effective alternatives to primal-dual approaches, showcasing notable success in robotics applications with frameworks like CRPO and PCRPO. Primal-based methods have the advantage of eliminating the need for dual parameter tuning and are less sensitive to initialization. However, CRPO may face oscillations in balancing reward and safety optimization. PCRPO addresses this by incorporating a soft policy optimization technique with gradient manipulation, achieving superior performance compared to CRPO and other state-of-the-art primal-dual methods.

In addition to safe policy optimization techniques, control theory is also applied to ensure safety in RL. For instance, the use of Lyapunov functions constrains agent actions by applying control laws to remove unsafe actions from the action set. Although experiments have shown that this method can achieve safe actions, it relies on prior knowledge of a Lyapunov function, which can complicate its application in unknown environment dynamics.
