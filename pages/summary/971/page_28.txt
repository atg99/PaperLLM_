This section discusses the theoretical foundations for primal-dual approaches in constrained Markov decision processes (CMDPs), emphasizing the Lagrangian method as a standard solution technique. The formulation presented involves minimizing a function that incorporates both the objective and constraint violations, with various algorithms proposed to address this optimization problem.

Key contributions include a policy-based algorithm for CMDPs with finite states that utilizes stochastic approximation, demonstrating locally optimal policies. The Reward Constrained Policy Optimization (RCPO) method is highlighted for its asymptotic convergence properties, achieved through stochastic approximation techniques commonly used in reinforcement learning.

Theoretical support for the primal-dual method, which assures a zero duality gap, is provided through Dankin’s Theorem and Convex Analysis. Additional works explore off-line reinforcement learning frameworks, ensuring safe learning through performance guarantees and error bounds in a Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) framework.

Integration of robust Markov decision process theory with constrained CMDPs leads to the robust constrained Markov decision process (RCMDP), which underpins a robust soft-constrained Lagrange-based algorithm. The convergence of this algorithm is linked to established stochastic approximation methods.

Lastly, the transformation of cost-based constraints into state-based constraints is discussed, alongside a policy improvement algorithm that incorporates safety guarantees and utilizes backward value functions for cost estimation through policy iteration.
