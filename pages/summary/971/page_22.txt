The paper discusses the challenges and methodologies related to ensuring safety in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly in the context of multi-agent systems. It highlights the need for constraints that balance safety performance with reward acquisition. Murugesan et al. utilize satisfiability modulo theories as a safety verification tool to enhance learning safety, while Hasanbeig et al. incorporate linear temporal logic properties into the reward function using limit deterministic Büchi automata for safety assurance. Despite promising results in controlled environments, the practical application of these methods in real-world scenarios requires further exploration.

The section on Safe Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) notes the growing interest in this area, which remains less developed compared to single-agent RL. Safe MARL is conceptualized as a multi-agent constrained Markov decision process (CMDP), where both the safety of individual agents and their rewards must be considered. The problem is formulated as a tuple that includes the set of agents, state space, joint action space, transition probabilities, initial state distribution, discount factor, joint reward function, individual cost functions, and cost constraints. At each time step, agents act based on their policies, leading to a joint action that determines their collective reward. The exploration of safe MARL methodologies is still in its early stages, indicating a need for further research in this domain.
