This paper discusses the development of safe reinforcement learning (RL) methods utilizing Lyapunov functions in both discrete and continuous Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) settings. These methods aim to ensure safety during training and enhance learning stability by employing Lyapunov functions designed through a proposed Linear Programming algorithm. However, the authors acknowledge that the current methods require improvements in training stability and safety, suggesting that more efficient algorithms may need to be developed.

Additionally, the paper explores the use of Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) in model-free settings for safe RL. Recent advancements include a model-free safe RL method that learns both policies and CBFs through data-driven approaches, reducing dependency on environmental models. Notably, methods integrating CBFs into reward functions for safe policy search in power system control are highlighted. Other studies leverage model-free RL with CBFs to enhance learning safety and efficiency by constraining search spaces, while also providing convergence and stability analyses.

The paper also points out the emergence of safety layer methods, which directly ensure RL safety. These methods are categorized under control theory, functioning as control filters. Examples include the OptLayer method, which combines stochastic control policies with a safety-focused neural network layer, and the Density Constrained Reinforcement Learning (DCRL) method, which optimizes reward and cost based on state density functions rather than traditional metrics. The A-CRL method is introduced as a solution for CMDP problems where optimal policies may not be achievable.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of integrating safety mechanisms in RL frameworks, highlighting various approaches and their effectiveness in ensuring safe and stable learning processes.
