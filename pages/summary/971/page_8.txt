The paper discusses the field of safe reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on its formulation as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP). CMDPs aim to optimize agent rewards while adhering to safety constraints, a topic that has been extensively researched in both tabular and linear contexts. However, the application of deep safe RL to high-dimensional and continuous CMDP problems is a newer development, utilizing neural networks to represent safe states or actions.

The CMDP framework extends the traditional Markov decision process (MDP) by incorporating a constraint set. It is defined by a tuple that includes the state set, action set, state transition probabilities, reward function, initial state distribution, and discount factor. In this context, safe RL seeks to develop an optimal policy that maximizes rewards while minimizing costs through careful action selection.

Key components are outlined, including the state value function, state-action value function, and the advantage function, which are crucial for formulating the reward objectives in safe RL. The paper serves to provide a foundational understanding of the problem formulation in safe reinforcement learning and introduces the broader landscape of safe RL research.
