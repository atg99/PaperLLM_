In recent years, various safe reinforcement learning (RL) methods rooted in control theory have emerged, particularly for robotic applications. A notable example is the work by Perkins and Barto, who utilize Lyapunov functions to constrain the action space, thereby ensuring the safety of all policies while maintaining agent performance. Their method, which applies Lyapunov-based safe RL to pendulum tasks using a robot arm in simulated environments, represents one of the early uses of Lyapunov functions for safe RL in robotics. However, a major challenge lies in designing a suitable Lyapunov function that meets all safety requirements, which necessitates a comprehensive understanding of system dynamics and Lyapunov theory.

Additionally, other safe RL methods that depend on system models have been developed. Thomas et al. employ model-based RL to enhance agent safety by predicting trajectories and avoiding unsafe states in simulation environments such as MuJoCo. Their approach is effective for short-horizon trajectories but may struggle with longer horizons due to the need for rapid planning. Liu et al. propose a safe exploration method that utilizes robot models and manifold constraints in tangent space to ensure safety during the exploration process. Their method can adapt any model-free RL approaches for learning within constrained environments; however, it requires an accurate robot model and tracking controller, which limits its applicability in real-world scenarios.

Safe RL techniques are also being explored beyond robotics, including their application in recommender systems and video analysis.
