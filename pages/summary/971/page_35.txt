The paper discusses various algorithms and their sample complexities in the context of reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on safety and optimality. It highlights the sampling complexity of the NPG-PD algorithm, which is O(|S|^2|A|^2(1-γ)^4ε^2), and notes that this complexity is necessary for the two outer loops as established in previous work. An extension of NPG-PD introduces an online variant that increases sample complexity to O(|S|^6|A|^6(1-γ)^12ε^6). 

The paper also reviews a primal-type algorithm proposed in another study that requires O(|S||A|(1-γ)^7ε^4) for achieving ε-optimality, indicating that its inner loop necessitates a significant number of iterations. The CSPDA algorithm demands a similar sample complexity but is contingent upon an additional generative model. In contrast, the Triple-Q algorithm has a higher sample complexity of O(|S|^2.5|A|^2.5(1-γ)^18.5ε^5), particularly when addressing finite-horizon constrained Markov decision processes (CMDP). 

Further, a safe RL algorithm discussed in the paper exhibits a sample complexity of O(|S||A|(1-γ)^4ε^2), with a worst-case scenario for constraint violations reaching O(|S|^2|A|(1-γ)^4ε^2) if the number of constraints exceeds the state space. 

The paper also explores theoretical approaches to safe RL, including SAILR, which provides safety guarantees without maintaining intervention mechanisms post-learning, and SNO-MDP, which addresses exploration in unknown safety constraints. A-CRL approaches safe RL problems by augmenting states with Lagrange multipliers, while DCRL aims for near-optimal policy learning amidst bounded errors. Collectively, these methods analyze learning under various safety conditions and tasks.
