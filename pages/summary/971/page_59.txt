This collection of references discusses various aspects of safe reinforcement learning and the development of safe robotic systems. 

Mohammed Alshiekh et al. (2018) propose a method for safe reinforcement learning through a technique known as shielding, which ensures that agents operate within predefined safety constraints. Matthias Althoff et al. (2019) focus on creating safe robots by utilizing self-programming and self-verification, enabling modular robot design that enhances safety.

Eitan Altman's work on constrained Markov decision processes (1995, 1999) provides a foundational framework for decision-making under constraints, which is critical for ensuring safety in reinforcement learning scenarios. Ames et al. (2019) discuss control barrier functions, highlighting their theoretical underpinnings and practical applications in maintaining safety during control tasks.

Dario Amodei et al. (2016) identify concrete AI safety problems, emphasizing the need for robust solutions to address potential risks in AI systems. Anderson et al. (2020) explore neurosymbolic reinforcement learning, integrating formal verification into the exploration process to enhance safety.

OpenAI's research (2020) on dexterous in-hand manipulation showcases advancements in robotic control, while Aswani et al. (2013) present a framework for learning-based model predictive control that is provably safe and robust. Lastly, Atakishiyev et al. address the importance of explainable artificial intelligence in autonomous driving, underscoring the significance of transparency and safety in AI-driven systems. 

These works collectively contribute to the discourse on ensuring safety in AI and robotics, emphasizing methods and frameworks that prioritize safe interaction with the environment.
