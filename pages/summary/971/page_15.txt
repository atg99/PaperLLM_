The paper discusses various approaches to enhance safety in reinforcement learning (RL) through formal methods and Gaussian processes. Anderson et al. introduce a neurosymbolic RL method that uses formal verification within a mirror descent framework to ensure safety, though their reliance on fixed worst-case model knowledge may limit practical applicability. Beard and Baheri also explore formal methods to improve agent safety by penalizing risky behaviors during exploration, but their methods require scalability enhancements for continuous systems. Fulton and Platzer contribute by using external knowledge in offline formal verification to support agent safety.

Moreover, the paper highlights the challenges of measuring unsafe areas in formal methods, particularly through the application of Gaussian processes (GPs). Akametalu et al. develop a safe RL method utilizing reachability analysis and GPs to identify disturbances that may lead to unsafe states, iteratively computing maximal safe areas. Berkenkamp and Schoellig similarly apply GPs to ensure system stability by measuring uncertainty. Polymenakos et al. leverage the PILCO method to enhance agent safety by incorporating cost into reward functions, while Cowen et al. utilize PILCO to explore environments with risk considerations, improving safety probability through uncertainty quantification.

Overall, these safe RL approaches demonstrate a strong balance between reward and safety in complex tasks. However, the need for more rigorous investigation into safety training and the development of a unified framework for evaluating safe RL performance is emphasized.
