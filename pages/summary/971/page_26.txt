The paper discusses various approaches to safe multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), highlighting the challenges and methodologies involved. It begins with the acknowledgment of reward uncertainty and the use of randomized nature policies with Gaussian noise to enhance agent performance. However, it critiques the strong payoff and equilibrium assumptions made in some frameworks, suggesting they may not hold in real-world scenarios.

The work CMIX is reviewed as a recent safe MARL method that addresses peak and average constraints but lacks theoretical guarantees for safety in multi-agent systems. It faces similar issues to Safe Dec-PG, particularly regarding parameter sharing.

The paper then introduces Safe MARL via shielding, employing formal methods like Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) to ensure safety by defining allowed actions for agents and correcting unsafe explorations. Techniques such as central and factored shielding are evaluated using Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (MADDPG) and Coordinating Q-learning. While this approach enhances safety, it requires prior knowledge and LTL specifications.

Another method discussed involves adding a safety layer to DDPG and MADDPG for continuous action spaces, which improves safety across certain MARL tasks, though it does not eliminate all risks.

The section concludes with a summary of safe reinforcement learning methods categorized by various perspectives, acknowledging the progress made but emphasizing the need for further development to achieve practical real-world application, while also noting that early convergence guarantees for multi-agent systems were established as early as 2000.
