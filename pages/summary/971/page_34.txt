The paper discusses efficient algorithms for safe reinforcement learning (RL) with linear function approximation, specifically focusing on the ConRL algorithm. ConRL achieves a sampling complexity of O(|S|^2|A|(1-γ)^6ε^2) by analyzing two settings with strong theoretical guarantees. The first setting involves a learning maximization process with a concave reward function, leading to a convex expected value of constraints. The second setting ensures that resources do not exceed specified levels during the learning maximization process. However, despite these settings, the complexity of ConRL remains higher than previous methods by a factor of at least (1/(1-γ)^2).

The paper presents a table summarizing various model-free state-of-the-art algorithms for safe RL or constrained Markov decision processes (CMDP), including their iteration complexity and safety violation metrics. Notably, the model-free safe RL algorithms listed, such as IPO, Lyapunov-Based Safe RL, and others, lack convergence rate analysis.

The work highlights that the NPG-PD algorithm, as shown in [82], achieves a sublinear convergence rate for both reward optimality and safety constraints. NPG-PD addresses CMDP using a softmax policy, dealing with non-concave reward and non-convex cost objectives. The findings suggest that with appropriate design, policy gradient methods can also yield algorithms that converge at a sublinear rate, as established in Theorem 4 of [82].
