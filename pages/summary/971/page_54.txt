The paper discusses the theory and challenges of safe multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), particularly in the context of massive swarm robotics. It highlights several key areas for future research. The convergence problem remains unresolved for safe MARL methods that do not rely on strong assumptions. Sample complexity and stability optimization in safe MARL environments are crucial.

Key points include:

1. **Credit Assignment**: It's essential to balance costs and rewards in various game settings. Policies should aim to minimize individual agent costs while enhancing overall rewards. Additionally, the allocation of cost credits to each agent needs optimization.

2. **Nonstability**: Actions taken by one agent can adversely affect others, potentially leading to worse rewards or unsafe conditions for those agents.

3. **Scalability**: As the number of agents increases, computational challenges arise, making it difficult to ensure safety and estimate each agent's Q or V values simultaneously.

4. **Multiple Heterogeneous Constraints**: The handling of diverse constraints across agents in safe MARL remains largely unexplored. Research is needed to balance these constraints effectively to optimize agents' safety and reward policies.

5. **Complex Multi-Agent Tasks**: Current safe MARL algorithms face difficulties in executing complex tasks, such as encirclement and rescue missions, often resulting in time consumption and convergence issues.

6. **Robust MARL**: A significant challenge is achieving zero cost across different tasks without parameter tuning in the same algorithms. The transition from safe MARL simulations to real-world applications also presents ongoing concerns.

The paper emphasizes the need for further exploration and advancement in these areas to enhance the effectiveness and safety of MARL in practical applications.
