The paper discusses the critical issue of safety in Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly in real-world applications where the potential for catastrophic outcomes exists. As RL technology is increasingly utilized across various fields, including autonomous driving, robotics, and recommender systems, ensuring safety becomes paramount. The authors highlight several safety definitions to guide safe RL research. 

The first definition from the Oxford dictionary characterizes safety as the condition of being protected from danger or injury. The second definition emphasizes the management of recognized dangers to achieve an acceptable risk level. The third definition, referenced from Hans et al., states that agents are safe if they do not reach unsafe states, which underscores the need for environmental assessments of safety. 

These definitions collectively frame the discourse on safety optimization and constrained Markov decision processes, which are essential for addressing safety problems in RL applications. The paper aims to elucidate these concepts to enhance the understanding and implementation of safe RL practices.
