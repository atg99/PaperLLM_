The paper discusses the role of Explainable Reinforcement Learning (XRL) in enhancing safety in autonomous driving, noting that while XRL has potential in developing trustworthy systems, its application remains under-researched in the context of safe reinforcement learning (RL). 

In the robotics domain, various learning methods have shown promise, but they often neglect the explicit optimization of the agent's safety. The paper highlights the need for safe RL algorithms that can effectively bridge the gap between simulation experiments and real-world applications. 

One notable approach mentioned is SAFER, an offline primitive learning method proposed by Slack et al. This method aims to improve the data efficiency and safety rate in safe RL, although it lacks theoretical safety guarantees. SAFER utilizes safe trajectories collected from a scripted policy to enhance learning outcomes, demonstrating superior performance in simulation experiments compared to other safe RL demonstration methods.

Additionally, the paper introduces the OptLayer, a fully differentiable framework designed to ensure that robots only take safe actions, facilitating the transition from simulation to real-world deployment. This advancement is crucial for improving the safety and effectiveness of RL in robotic applications.
