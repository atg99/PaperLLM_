The paper discusses various approaches to safe reinforcement learning (RL) and their effectiveness in policy optimization under safety constraints. Wagener et al. introduce a method leveraging the advantage function to minimize costs and ensure safety during both training and deployment. Another approach, Shortest-Path Reinforcement Learning (SPRL), utilizes off-policy strategies to develop safe and reward policies, specifically addressing shortest path problems such as the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP). 

The SNO-MDP framework is presented, which employs a Gaussian process to optimize costs within safe regions while maximizing rewards in unknown safety-constrained areas. It posits that maximizing rewards may take precedence over safety, indicating that overly conservative strategies, like remaining stationary for safety, may hinder performance. 

A first-order policy optimization method based on interior point optimization (IPO) is also highlighted, which uses logarithmic barrier functions to adhere to safety constraints. Although this method has shown promise in empirical results, particularly in MuJoCo and grid-world environments, it still requires theoretical validation.

Additionally, a meta-algorithm is proposed to tackle safe RL problems with general convex constraints, demonstrating effectiveness in CMDP scenarios with limited samples. While theoretical outcomes are encouraging, further practical experimentation is necessary. 

The paper also explores using trajectory probabilities within a safe set to address safe RL, noting that while this method can achieve a small duality gap, it may face stability issues typical of primal-dual approaches. Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for both theoretical and practical advancements in safe reinforcement learning methodologies.
