The paper discusses the principles of safe reinforcement learning (SRL) and explores potential directions for enhancing human-robot interactions. It highlights the importance of learning safe and diverse behaviors from human feedback, akin to how geese form formations to ensure safety within their group. 

The first proposed direction emphasizes the need for robots to adapt their behaviors based on feedback received from multiple individuals, which can help establish safer exploration methods. By leveraging diverse behaviors, robots can enhance their interaction with humans and ensure safety in various tasks.

The second direction focuses on the significance of learning from non-expert users to achieve long-term SRRL. The paper suggests that successful collaboration between robots and humans hinges on mutual understanding, where robots must learn human preferences and intentions. This understanding can facilitate better adaptation to environments and improve cooperation.

In conclusion, the paper reviews two decades of safe reinforcement learning methods and aims to provide insights for future research by addressing key safety questions using the "2H3W" framework.
