The paper discusses advancements in safe reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, particularly focusing on their application in industrial manipulators and robotics. It highlights a limitation of the OptLayer method in optimizing policies for high-dimensional tasks. Garcia and Fernandez introduced a safe RL algorithm based on a smoother risk function that ensures safe exploration and a monotonic increase, allowing the algorithm to adhere to a baseline policy. Subsequent research successfully adapted this framework for real-world robotic applications.

The paper describes two types of risk functions. The binary step-risk function categorizes states based on their relationship to a case base and defines state risk with a specific equation. If a state is related to any cases, it is deemed known; otherwise, it is classified as unknown. The continuous risk function offers a smoother transition between safe and risky states, facilitating more nuanced decision-making in robotics.

Additionally, the practical implementation of this algorithm is demonstrated using a NAO robot, showcasing its effectiveness in real-world scenarios. The findings suggest that these risk functions can significantly enhance the safety and efficiency of robotic manipulations in complex environments.
