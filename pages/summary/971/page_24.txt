The paper discusses the formulation of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) with a focus on safe policy optimization under constraints. The state-action value function and state value function are defined, enabling the evaluation of policies in terms of both rewards and costs. Specifically, the Q-function and V-function are introduced to assess the contributions of subsets of agents in a multi-agent environment.

The advantage function for policy optimization is derived, which helps identify beneficial actions by comparing the Q-function and V-function. Additionally, the paper introduces a cost framework for each agent concerning specific constraints, allowing for the evaluation of the impact of actions on these costs.

The primary goal is to develop a safe MARL algorithm that maximizes the expected reward while adhering to predefined constraints. The optimization problem is formalized, aiming to maximize the agents' collective rewards while ensuring that the costs do not exceed specified limits for each constraint. This dual focus on reward maximization and constraint satisfaction is critical for the development of effective multi-agent systems.
