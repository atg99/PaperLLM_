The paper discusses the concept of global search spaces in the context of neural network architecture design, highlighting the flexibility and constraints that can influence the arrangement of operations within these architectures. It introduces architecture templates that impose limitations on the structural choices available in defining a network, which can segment the architecture graph and enforce specific operational constraints.

Figure 1 provides visual representations of different global search space configurations. The simplest form illustrated is the chain-structured search space, where architectures consist of a sequence of nodes with a single parent-child relationship between adjacent nodes. Baker et al. (2017) are noted for their exploration of this search space, which includes a variety of operations such as convolutions, pooling, and linear transformations, with considerations for hyperparameter variations.

The paper emphasizes the significance of these templates in shaping the architecture search space and sets the stage for a more detailed discussion on search spaces with predetermined repeating patterns in a subsequent section.
