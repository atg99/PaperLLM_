The paper discusses the design and optimization of neural architecture search (NAS) spaces, particularly focusing on mobile search spaces as proposed by Dong et al. (2018). It highlights the cell-based design paradigm, which is characterized by the use of cells that consist of densely connected operations, alternating between normalization and convolution layers. This design aims to meet the requirements of mobile devices, emphasizing fewer parameters and faster inference times.

The authors note that while there has been no comprehensive comparative study of different search spaces, cell-based architectures, particularly the NASNet search space, are favored due to their empirical performance. These architectures allow for easy transfer across datasets and enable flexibility in complexity by adjusting the number of filters and cells. In contrast, global search spaces, while they can also be modified by varying filters, present challenges in transferring architectures across datasets with different input shapes.

The paper references contrasting views in the literature, with some researchers, like Tan et al. (2018), advocating for global search spaces in mobile architecture design due to their potential for layer diversity, which may enhance accuracy and reduce latency. Overall, the paper emphasizes the trade-offs between global and cell-based search spaces in the context of neural architecture optimization for mobile applications.
