The paper presents a comprehensive survey on Neural Architecture Search (NAS), focusing on various constraints, multiple objective functions, and model compression techniques. It discusses alternative approaches inspired by transfer learning and methods that utilize early termination to expedite the search process. The influence of search procedures on related areas is also examined, supported by extensive illustrations and relevant experimentation.

In defining the neural architecture search space, the paper describes neural networks as functions transforming input variables to output variables through a series of operations, represented as directed acyclic graphs. Each node in the graph represents a tensor and is associated with an operation on its parent nodes. This representation is crucial as it completely defines the architecture of the network, which is referred to as a framework for unifying various NAS methods.

The survey categorizes the neural architecture search space into two main types: the global search space, which represents entire neural architectures, and the cell-based search space, which consists of repeated cells to construct the complete network. Additionally, the paper discusses search spaces for language modeling in recurrent neural networks. An objective comparison of these search spaces is provided, highlighting their nuanced aspects.
