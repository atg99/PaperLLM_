The paper presents a comprehensive survey on Neural Architecture Search (NAS), focusing on its performance across various algorithms when applied to the CIFAR-10 dataset. Table 2 summarizes the results of different search algorithms, highlighting their error rates, number of parameters in millions, and the GPU days required for training. 

Key findings include:

- Zoph and Le (2017) achieved the best error rate of 3.65% but required extensive GPU time of 22,400 days.
- The addition of Cutout in Zoph et al. (2018) improved the error rate to 2.65% with a similar parameter count and GPU time.
- Cai et al. (2018b) demonstrated a significant improvement with an error rate of 2.49% while maintaining a modest parameter count of 5.7 million and requiring 200 GPU days.
- Random searches yielded competitive results, with Liu et al. (2018b) producing an error rate of 3.91% using a non-specified parameter count over 300 GPU days.
- Human-designed architectures also performed well, with the best human-designed model achieving an error rate of 2.82%, showcasing the efficacy of human expertise in architecture design.

The survey emphasizes the trade-offs between error rates, model complexity, and computational resources in NAS, illustrating the ongoing advancements in the field.
