The work by Cai et al. (2018b) introduces new transformations to incorporate branches into network architectures, enhancing the functionality of convolutional layers and identity maps. This is accomplished by splitting or replicating inputs into multiple branches, applying the necessary transformations, and then concatenating or adding the outputs. The method employs function-preserving transformations to develop more intricate architectures. Their meta-controller, similar to Cai et al. (2018a), utilizes an encoder network to derive a low-dimensional architecture representation and generates a transformation action distribution, with learning achieved via the REINFORCE algorithm.

Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), particularly the UCT algorithm, offers an alternative approach for reinforcement learning in neural architecture search. This algorithm explores a tree-structured state-action space, utilizing upper confidence bounds for exploration. Negrinho and Gordon (2017) used the vanilla UCT algorithm for neural architecture search, while Wistuba (2018b) enhanced this with function-preserving operations and surrogate models to expedite training and predict rewards for unexplored states. Wang et al. (2019) also leveraged surrogate models in the NASNet search space, incorporating a weight-sharing paradigm.

Evolutionary Algorithms (EA) serve as population-based global optimizers for black-box functions, encompassing essential components such as initialization, parent selection, recombination, mutation, and survivor selection. The process involves generating an initial population, selecting parents for reproduction, applying recombination and mutation, evaluating fitness, and selecting survivors. The choice of mutation and recombination operators balances diversity and similarity in the population, paralleling the exploration and exploitation trade-off seen in reinforcement learning. The fitness functions reflect optimization goals, while survivor selection fosters competition among individuals.
