This paper presents a survey on Neural Architecture Search (NAS), highlighting methodologies that extend architecture search to automate various aspects of deep learning. A notable contribution by Bello et al. (2017) involves encoding a parameter optimization method using a graph structure that defines update rules similar to classical gradient-based optimization. The structure consists of blocks with two operands, unary functions applied to each operand, and a binary function that combines their outcomes. The output of each block can serve as an operand for subsequent blocks, allowing for a diverse range of operands including gradient orders, bias-corrected estimates, and optimizers like Adam and RMSProp.

In contrast, Bello et al. employ Trust Region Policy Optimization to update the controller parameters, utilizing a reinforcement controller akin to that proposed by Zoph and Le (2017). Their approach trains a simple two-layer convolutional neural network for five epochs, using validation accuracy as the reward.

Ramachandran et al. (2018) build on this framework to search for activation functions, employing Proximal Policy Optimization to guide the search. Their setup features a single operand representing the input to the activation function, with a tailored set of unary and binary operations, including various powers, sigmoid functions, and other mathematical operations. The reward for their search is based on the performance of a ResNet-20 model. This survey emphasizes the innovative use of NAS techniques to enhance deep learning processes beyond architecture design.
