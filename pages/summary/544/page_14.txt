In their paper, Martin Wistuba, Ambrish Rawat, and Tejaswini Pedapati discuss policy gradient methods as an alternative approach in reinforcement learning (RL) that focuses on directly learning policies through a set of parameters rather than relying on value functions. These methods select actions based on the agent's performance, adjusting the policy parameters using classical gradient ascent updates. Since the performance measure depends on both the actions taken and the state distributions, empirical gradient estimates are employed for the updates. A notable algorithm in this realm is REINFORCE, which estimates the gradient using returns from actions taken.

The authors also highlight the challenges posed by noisy empirical estimates, leading to the incorporation of techniques such as baselines. Other advanced methods like Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) are mentioned, both of which are on-policy methods where agents follow the policy they are trying to learn.

In exploring optimization with Q-learning, the paper references the work of Baker et al., who integrated RL-based algorithms for neural architecture search using a combination of Q-learning, ε-greedy strategies, and experience replay. Their approach allows for selecting layers in architecture design and includes the option to finalize the architecture, with the Q-function updated through experience replay.

Furthermore, the authors mention Zhong et al., who also utilized Q-learning for architecture search in a cell-based space. They then transition to discussing policy gradient methods in neural architecture search, highlighting the pioneering work by Zoph and Le, who introduced a controller modeled as a stochastic policy to generate neural architectures. This approach uses an autoregressive controller for action prediction, marking a significant development in the application of policy gradient methods in this field.
