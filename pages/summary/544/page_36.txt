The paper discusses advancements in surrogate model-based approaches for multi-objective optimization and model compression in neural architecture design. 

In the context of surrogate models, the work by Dong et al. (2018) builds on Liu et al. (2018a) by incorporating multiple objectives in the optimization process. Unlike traditional methods that focus solely on model accuracy, this approach also evaluates other objectives, ensuring that feasible candidates adhere to constraints and are not dominated by existing solutions.

The section on model compression emphasizes the importance of efficient network deployment on mobile devices, where neural architecture search methods aim to prune models while maintaining accuracy. He et al. (2018) introduce a reinforcement learning controller that prunes layers based on a desired sparsity ratio, using deep deterministic policy gradient methods to optimize controller parameters. They argue that the reward accuracy of the pruned model serves as a reliable proxy for fine-tuned accuracy.

Ashok et al. (2018) propose a two-policy reinforcement learning approach for layer removal and shrinking, employing knowledge distillation where an initial architecture acts as the teacher. Their reward function is designed to balance compression ratio and accuracy.

Cao et al. (2019) utilize Bayesian optimization with a Gaussian process to maximize the reward function while exploring the search space through random modifications of the architecture, including layer removal, shrinking, and the addition of skip connections. They emphasize the significance of skip connections for achieving compressed architectures that retain classification accuracy similar to the original model.
