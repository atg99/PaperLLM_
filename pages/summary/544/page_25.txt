Xie et al. (2019b) propose a more memory-efficient method by utilizing a fully factorizable representation of architecture, modeled with a concrete distribution, which allows for training only one path of the network at a time. This reduces memory requirements while maintaining performance. Similarly, Cai et al. (2019) implement binary gates to select parts of the network path based on learned probabilities, further alleviating memory constraints.

Brock et al. (2018) advance the concept with dynamic hypernetworks that generate weights for various architectures based on a conditioning variable, thus expediting the training of candidate architectures. Zhang et al. (2019) enhance this idea by integrating it with graph neural networks, allowing for efficient message propagation between nodes representing operations in the architecture. This approach leverages the structure of the architecture in a graph format, preserving the relationships between different operations while optimizing the overall design.

In summary, recent advancements in neural architecture search focus on optimizing memory usage and training efficiency through innovative representations and structures, enabling the exploration of more complex architectures.
