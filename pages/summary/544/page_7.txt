The paper provides an overview of Neural Architecture Search (NAS), focusing on cell-based search spaces exemplified by the NASNet architecture. Introduced by Zoph et al. in 2018, NASNet employs a structure called a cell, which is a small directed acyclic graph that represents a feature transformation. The architecture is constructed by repeating these cells in a predetermined arrangement specified by a template. While the topology of the cells remains constant, hyperparameters such as the number of filters can vary, allowing for flexibility in network design.

The paper highlights the importance of managing tensor dimensions within these networks, noting that some methods use dedicated reduction cells, while others implement pooling operations. The search process is localized to the structures within a cell, often beginning with initial convolution layers to capture low-level features. Furthermore, the authors indicate that many approaches limit hyperparameter choices during the search to enhance efficiency, ultimately training the finalized network with increased filters and repetitions of the cells to improve performance.
