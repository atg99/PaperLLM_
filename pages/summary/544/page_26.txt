In this paper, the authors explore various optimization algorithms for neural architecture search (NAS), focusing on one-shot approaches that share weights across sampled architectures. They reference studies by Bender et al. (2018) and Zhang et al. (2019), which support the hypothesis that over-parameterized networks can effectively rank architectures based on their quality, despite the complexity of training these networks and the influence of techniques like batch normalization and dropout. 

However, Sciuto et al. (2019) challenge the effectiveness of weight sharing in recurrent neural networks (RNNs), suggesting that the ranking produced by various methods does not align with actual performance, attributing success to the limited search space rather than the methods themselves. They demonstrate that random search often outperforms other optimization techniques in finding suitable RNN cells.

The authors conclude that while NASNet is a prevalent search space due to its favorable constraints for discovering high-performing architectures, the comparison of different optimization methods remains problematic due to variations in search space, duration, and data augmentation. They emphasize the need for fair baselines in architecture search, highlighting that random search has proven to be a strong baseline, outperforming established optimizers in certain contexts. The findings call for a reevaluation of weight sharing benefits in RNNs and underscore random search's efficacy in architecture optimization.
