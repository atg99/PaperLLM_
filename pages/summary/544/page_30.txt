The paper discusses automated curve prediction methods that estimate the probability of a model's performance based on its learning curve. Unlike surrogate models, learning curve prediction methods involve additional training and consider the learning curve's dynamics. Many neural architecture search approaches, such as Hyperband, utilize premature model validation accuracy as a proxy for final performance, training multiple candidates in parallel and terminating less promising ones over time.

The authors explore advanced techniques that predict final performance by analyzing architecture, data, and premature learning curves. These methods employ models to calculate the likelihood that a training job will outperform the current best network. If this likelihood falls below a set threshold, training is halted.

Domhan et al. (2015) propose a method for modeling learning curves using a weighted sum of various increasing and saturating function families. However, this approach requires long premature learning curves for accuracy. Klein et al. (2017) enhance this by employing a Bayesian neural network that learns across different learning curves, inferring function parameters based on premature data.

Chandrashekaran and Lane (2017) critique the strong assumptions made about learning curve behavior in previous works, noting that factors like noisy optimization and varying learning rates can disrupt these assumptions. They suggest using actual mature learning curves observed during training for more accurate predictions, employing an affine transformation to minimize error between mature and premature curves. Their model averages the best fits of the mature learning curves to form predictions.
