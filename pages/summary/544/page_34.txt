The paper discusses advancements in neural architecture search, particularly focusing on optimization methods for selecting hyperparameters in deep learning models. It references the work of Hsu et al. (2018) and Cai et al. (2018a), which utilize reinforcement learning to optimize the architecture of networks by learning policies for adding, removing, or modifying layers and their filter counts. Hsu et al. implemented a stricter penalty function for constraint violations, awarding a score of zero for any violations and using accuracy as a reward for compliant configurations.

The paper also delves into multi-objective optimization, which addresses the challenge of conflicting objectives in optimization problems. It explains the concept of Pareto optimal solutions—where improving one objective would detriment another—and emphasizes the necessity for users to select a model from a set of Pareto optimal solutions based on specific deployment needs.

To tackle multi-objective optimization, the authors discuss decomposition methods that convert multi-objective problems into single-objective ones through parameterized aggregation functions. However, standard methods may not capture all Pareto optimal solutions, necessitating multiple optimization runs with varying weight vectors, which can be computationally expensive. Hsu et al. (2018) suggest using a weighted sum aggregation function in conjunction with reinforcement learning to address this issue.

In summary, the paper highlights innovative strategies for optimizing neural network architectures through reinforcement learning and multi-objective optimization, thereby contributing to the efficiency and effectiveness of model selection in deep learning.
