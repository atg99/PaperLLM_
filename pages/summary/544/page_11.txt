The paper discusses Neural Architecture Search (NAS) with a focus on recurrent neural networks (RNNs). It highlights the significance of selecting initial architectures when exploring the global search space and suggests that similar performance can be achieved with well-chosen initial conditions. This insight could reinvigorate global search methods, although the criteria for selecting effective initial architectures remain vague and may depend on specific tasks.

The paper also introduces a search space for recurrent cells, initially defined by Zoph and Le in 2017. A recurrent cell is articulated through a function that incorporates trainable parameters, a hidden state, a cell state, and an input. The architecture is represented as a full binary tree where nodes correspond to binary merge operations and activation functions, such as addition, element-wise multiplication, and various activation functions like tanh and sigmoid. This structured approach allows for the parameterization of functions within the recurrent cell, facilitating the design of effective RNN architectures.
