In this paper, Martin Wistuba, Ambrish Rawat, and Tejaswini Pedapati discuss advancements in neural architecture search (NAS), particularly focusing on cell-based approaches introduced by Zoph et al. in 2018. They describe the NASNet search space, which utilizes a template of cells comprising normal and reduction cells. Normal cells maintain feature map dimensions, while reduction cells reduce the spatial dimensions by employing operations with a stride of two. Each cell contains multiple blocks, defined by their inputs and operations, where the computation flow is determined by a specific formula.

Zoph et al. found that summation operations performed better in terms of accuracy compared to concatenation when merging outputs from blocks. This architecture allows for skip connections between cells by using outputs from the previous two cells as inputs. The final output of a cell is the concatenation of outputs from blocks that do not feed into other blocks.

In parallel, Zhong et al. proposed a similar cell-based architecture, which is depicted in the paper. The primary distinctions between their approach and that of Zoph et al. lie in the specific definitions and configurations of the cells, highlighting the evolving nature of neural architecture design through these innovative search spaces.
