The paper "A Survey on Neural Architecture Search" discusses various methods to improve neural architecture search (NAS) by addressing the challenges associated with non-differentiable objective functions. One proposed solution involves using differentiable surrogate models to predict the latency of operations, thus facilitating the optimization process.

A significant approach highlighted is the NSGA-II, an elitist evolutionary algorithm that identifies non-dominated architectures and evaluates their performance. The algorithm begins with a randomly selected initial population, which is categorized into fronts based on dominance. The first front contains all non-dominated solutions, while subsequent fronts include solutions that are dominated by those in earlier fronts. Within each front, solutions are ranked according to their crowding distance, promoting diversity in the search space. Top candidates are selected for mutation and recombination.

Notable contributions to this area include the work of Kim et al. (2017) and Lu et al. (2018), both of whom apply NSGA-II to explore diverse architectures. Kim et al. focus on chain-structured networks, while Lu et al. expand to include various architectures, utilizing a two-phase sampling approach combining exploration and exploitation through mutations, crossover, and Bayesian networks.

Elsken et al. (2019) introduce a new evolutionary algorithm that emphasizes diversity without using crossover operations. Their approach incorporates function-preserving mutations and distinguishes between cheap-to-evaluate and expensive-to-evaluate objectives. They utilize kernel density estimation to select candidates from low-density regions for further evaluation.

Smithson et al. (2016) propose a surrogate model-based method for searching chain-structured architectures, leveraging Gaussian distributions around evaluated architectures to sample new candidates. A surrogate model predicts expensive objective functions while exact evaluations are conducted for cheaper objectives, determining the dominance of solutions.

Overall, the survey highlights diverse strategies in NAS, focusing on balancing exploration and exploitation, and improving efficiency through surrogate modeling and evolutionary techniques.
