The paper by Martin Wistuba, Ambrish Rawat, and Tejaswini Pedapati investigates the classification error of various neural network architectures employing different data augmentation strategies. The authors present results from their experiments alongside those reported by Cubuk et al. (2018a). The models tested include Wide-ResNet, multiple versions of Shake-Shake, and PyramidNet with ShakeDrop. 

Results indicate that the classification error varies significantly depending on the augmentation strategy employed. For instance, the Wide-ResNet achieved a classification error of 3.93 with the baseline strategy, which improved to 2.60 with the Auto-Augment + Mixup strategy. Similarly, the PyramidNet with ShakeDrop showed a reduction in error from 2.77 with the baseline to 1.33 with Auto-Augment + Mixup.

The paper also highlights the discovery of the swish activation function, which outperformed traditional activation functions in CIFAR-10 classification tasks. Cubuk et al.'s work on a controller for optimizing data augmentation policies is referenced, emphasizing the random selection of sub-policies that incorporate various augmentation techniques. 

The authors conducted their experiments using the code made available by Cubuk et al., aiming to further understand the effectiveness of established augmentation methods compared to newly discovered policies. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of data augmentation strategies in enhancing the performance of deep learning models.
