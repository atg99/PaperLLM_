The paper discusses the application of reinforcement learning (RL) in neural architecture search (NAS), presenting a general framework where an agent interacts with an environment to optimize neural network architectures. The agent's goal is to maximize long-term performance, typically measured by accuracy, through a series of actions that modify the architecture.

The RL system is framed as a Markov decision process, often simplified to finite scenarios due to the combinatorial nature of possible actions and states. The agent learns a policy that maps states to action probabilities, using value functions to guide learning. Two main approaches are highlighted: those that learn policies indirectly through state-action values and those that learn parameterized policies directly.

Temporal Difference Learning methods, such as SARSA, TD-λ, and Q-learning, are examined for their role in approximating optimal value functions, which are crucial for determining the best policy. The optimal policy is defined as the one that maximizes value across all states, with Q-learning specifically focusing on learning an action-value function that updates based on received rewards and future expected values.

The paper emphasizes the importance of finding an optimal policy in the context of NAS, as the learned architectures are derived by selecting actions that maximize the estimated action-value function, ultimately guiding the search for high-performance neural networks.
