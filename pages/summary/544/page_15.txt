The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and highlights methods for optimizing neural network design through the use of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) as controllers. Zoph and Le (2017) introduced a controller that predicts the configuration of network layers based on previous actions, employing a softmax operation to sample actions during each episode. This approach constructs a final network, which is trained for validation accuracy, with controller parameters updated using the REINFORCE method, scaling gradients by validation outcomes. They focus on chain-structured networks comprising convolutional layers with varying filter dimensions, strides, and skip connections.

Further developments include Zoph et al. (2018), who expanded the RNN-controller to define inputs and operations for blocks within the NASNet search space, updating the controller's parameters via the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) method. Cai et al. (2018) proposed a different controller action space that requires an initial architecture, allowing for layer widening or insertion through function-preserving transformations. These transformations ensure that the network's output remains unchanged initially, as new parameters are initialized to maintain the identity function. The incorporation of a bidirectional recurrent network and embedding layer helps in predicting transformation decisions efficiently, facilitating weight reuse and reducing search time for optimal architectures.
