The paper discusses advancements in neural architecture search (NAS) and highlights two prominent approaches: the search space designed by Tan et al. (2018) for mobile devices and the cell-based search space concept.

In Tan et al.'s approach, a hierarchical search space is created to find neural network models that are efficient across various dimensions, including accuracy, inference time, and parameter count. This search space consists of interconnected segments, where each segment can feature repeating operation patterns with options for skip connections and stride choices. This flexibility is crucial for addressing multi-objective design challenges.

The cell-based search space builds on the notion that many effective neural architectures utilize repetitive structures, referred to as cells, blocks, or units. These smaller graphs can be stacked to form larger architectures, enabling high performance and generalization across different datasets and tasks. Zoph et al. (2018) were pioneers in exploring this method, leading to the development of widely used architectures.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of structured search spaces in NAS, particularly the efficacy of cell-based designs that facilitate the creation of adaptable and high-performing neural networks.
