The paper discusses various evolutionary algorithms for neural architecture search (NAS), focusing on their methodologies and efficiency. Wistuba et al. propose using a (1+λ)-evolutionary strategy with a mutation process that modifies active parts of a genotype while also mutating inactive parts without affecting the phenotype. This approach allows continuous search progress, even when the parent individual is retained in subsequent generations. 

Liu et al. introduce a hierarchical search space, initializing a population of trivial genotypes and diversifying them with random mutations. Their algorithm employs tournament selection for parent selection and allows for significant modifications to the genotype, including adding and removing operations.

Real et al. extend this work by developing the AmoebaNet architectures, achieving state-of-the-art performance in image classification. Their algorithm incorporates age as a factor in the selection process to promote diversity and prevent overfitting to specific models, thereby ensuring robust architecture performance across multiple iterations.

The paper highlights that earlier methods often disregard GPU budget constraints, with search times extending to several GPU days. In contrast, Elsken et al. propose a more efficient evolutionary algorithm that combines function-preserving transformations with a greedy evolution approach, achieving comparable results in a much shorter time frame. This reflects a shift towards balancing performance and computational efficiency in NAS.
