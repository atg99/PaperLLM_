In this paper, the authors Martin Wistuba, Ambrish Rawat, and Tejaswini Pedapati discuss neural architecture search (NAS) within the context of deep learning. They define the architecture search problem by establishing the spaces of datasets, models, and architectures. The mapping from the dataset and architecture to the model is articulated as a general deep learning algorithm, which aims to minimize a loss function while incorporating regularization.

The objective of NAS is to identify the architecture that maximizes an objective function on a validation dataset, which is formalized as a maximization problem. The authors note that this objective function can be the negative loss, such as negative cross-entropy for classification tasks, where the goal is to maximize classification accuracy.

The paper emphasizes that optimizing the response function constitutes a global black-box optimization problem. To tackle this, the authors explore various optimization strategies, including reinforcement learning (RL) and evolutionary algorithms. They highlight that RL is particularly effective for sequential decision-making processes, where an agent learns to maximize its returns through interactions with the environment, receiving feedback in the form of rewards.

Overall, the authors provide a structured framework for understanding and addressing the complexities of neural architecture search, focusing on optimization methods that can be applied to this task.
