The paper "A Survey on Neural Architecture Search" by Baker et al. (2018) presents a novel approach to learning curve prediction that enhances traditional methods by incorporating additional features such as the first and second derivatives of the premature learning curve, architecture properties, and hyperparameters. They empirically find that the Support Vector Regression model outperforms other models like random forests and Bayesian linear regression. Their findings indicate that premature performance is a competitive method that often surpasses previous works, particularly emphasizing the importance of ranking learning curves over mean squared error.

Additionally, the paper explores transfer learning within neural architecture search, highlighting its efficiency in reusing knowledge across related tasks. Baker et al. (2017) showcase the applicability of architectures to datasets with similar dimensions, while Zoph et al. (2018) illustrate the successful transfer of architectures from smaller datasets to larger ones. The authors also discuss strategies such as searching on subsets or downscaled versions of data and the practice of initially designing simpler networks before scaling them up to enhance performance.

Overall, the paper underscores the potential of enriching learning curve predictions and leveraging transfer learning to improve neural architecture search methodologies, demonstrating significant advancements in both speed and effectiveness.
