The referenced works collectively highlight significant advancements in neural architecture search, optimization techniques, and deep learning methodologies. 

Wistuba et al. (2018) introduce a framework for neural architecture search utilizing Bayesian optimization and optimal transport, focusing on efficient model selection. Kim et al. (2017) present Nemo, a neuro-evolutionary approach that balances speed and accuracy through multi-objective optimization in deep neural networks. Klein et al. (2017) discuss learning curve predictions using Bayesian neural networks, contributing to better understanding of model training dynamics.

Kocsis and Szepesvári (2006) propose a bandit-based Monte Carlo planning method, enhancing decision-making processes in uncertain environments. Kokiopoulou et al. (2019) emphasize fast task-aware architecture inference, which aids in swift adaptation to varied tasks. Krizhevsky's earlier works (2009, 2012) lay foundational concepts in deep learning, particularly in feature learning and image classification using convolutional neural networks.

LeCun et al. (1998) detail the application of gradient-based learning in document recognition, pioneering techniques that are now standard in deep learning. Li and Talwalkar (2019) investigate the reproducibility of random search methods in neural architecture search, while Li et al. (2017) introduce Hyperband, a bandit-based strategy for optimizing hyperparameters efficiently.

Lastly, Lillicrap et al. (2016) focus on continuous control in deep reinforcement learning, showcasing the application of deep networks in dynamic decision-making tasks. Collectively, these contributions underscore the evolution and diversification of techniques in neural information processing and optimization strategies within machine learning.
