This paper by Martin Wistuba, Ambrish Rawat, and Tejaswini Pedapati reviews the advancements in neural architecture search (NAS) within the machine learning community. The authors frame NAS as a search problem involving decisions that define neural network components, where the search space is shaped by feasible solutions and the optimizer determines the search algorithm.

The authors reference seminal works by Zoph and Le (2017) and Baker et al. (2017), which utilized reinforcement learning to discover effective architectures. They also note that Real et al. (2017) demonstrated the potential of neuroevolution methods, although both approaches required extensive computational resources. Consequently, subsequent research has aimed at reducing this computational burden, with significant contributions from Cai et al. (2018a) and Pham et al. (2018) focusing on reusing learned model parameters.

The design of the search space is emphasized as crucial, affecting both search speed and solution quality. Initial efforts primarily targeted chain-structured architectures, but the emergence of branched architectures has prompted the development of more suitable search spaces (Zoph et al., 2018).

The paper also explores the expansion of NAS objectives beyond just optimizing search time and generalization error. Notable works have sought to minimize model parameters for efficient deployment on mobile devices (Tan et al., 2018; Kim et al., 2017). Additionally, techniques for architecture search have been adapted to automate related components in deep learning, such as activation functions (Ramachandran et al., 2018) and data augmentation (Cubuk et al., 2018a).

As NAS continues to rapidly evolve, the authors aim to provide a unifying framework to analyze existing methodologies critically, addressing misconceptions and pitfalls in current trends. The review is organized into sections discussing various architecture search spaces, a formal definition of the architecture search problem, and an overview of four main optimization methods: reinforcement learning, evolutionary algorithms, surrogate model-based optimization, and one-shot architecture search. The paper concludes by highlighting the importance of understanding the components that contribute to successful neural architecture search.
