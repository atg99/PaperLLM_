The paper discusses advancements in architecture search through weight sharing and differentiable architecture search. Weight sharing allows for the training of multiple network architectures with shared weights, where each architecture is represented as a path in a chain-structured search space. The weights of the architecture are updated while keeping the remaining parameters and the controller parameters unchanged. The controller samples architectures and evaluates them on a validation batch to update itself based on the obtained reward, following the methodology of Zoph and Le (2017). Variations such as uniform sampling and independent categorical distribution sampling are also explored.

In differentiable architecture search, the authors introduce binary variables to formulate a more flexible representation of the architecture selection process. They simplify the model by assuming that operations determining the architecture can use a single input, leading to a weighted combination of operations. The approach proposed by Liu et al. (2019b) allows for softer decisions on paths by parameterizing the selection probabilities, enabling a continuous range of values between 0 and 1 for the selection of operations. This method employs a softmax operation to ensure that the selection probabilities sum to one, allowing for more nuanced architecture decisions.
