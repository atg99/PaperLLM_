The paper discusses the importance of considering multiple objectives in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), beyond just accuracy. Practitioners may need to account for factors such as model parameters, floating point operations, and device-specific metrics like latency. Two primary approaches to incorporate these additional objectives are outlined.

The first approach involves adding constraints to the optimization problem, which allows for the enforcement of requirements like reduced parameters or faster inference times. The constraints can be tailored based on practical needs. The second approach treats the problem as a multi-objective optimization, generating a set of solutions without immediate trade-offs among objectives. This two-step process first identifies candidate solutions, which can then be evaluated against specific requirements.

In terms of constrained optimization, the paper explains that when certain constraints, such as thresholds for inference time or memory, are specified, the single-objective optimization becomes constrained. Traditional optimization methods are not suitable for this scenario, but penalty methods can be utilized. These methods reformulate the problem into an unconstrained optimization by applying a penalty for constraint violations.

Examples of penalty functions include Tan et al.'s use of a hyperparameterized penalty function that adjusts based on desired trade-offs, and Zhou et al.'s approach, which incorporates a penalty based on the maximum of zero and the normalized difference between the constraint and its threshold. The paper highlights the significance of these methods in effectively navigating the trade-offs inherent in neural architecture design.
