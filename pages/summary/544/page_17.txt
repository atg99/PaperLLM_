The paper discusses the framework of evolutionary algorithms in the context of neural architecture search (NAS). It describes a process where a population of network architectures undergoes selection, mutation, and recombination to generate novel architectures. The selection of parent architectures is typically done through tournament selection or fitness proportionate selection, which prioritizes individuals based on their fitness scores. 

Mutation is often favored over recombination due to the uncertainty of producing offspring with equal or higher fitness through the latter method. The survivor selection step aims to manage population size and enhance competition among individuals, utilizing strategies such as elitist selection.

The paper highlights the use of genetic algorithms in NAS, where individuals are represented by a fixed-length encoding called genotype. Mutations and recombination occur directly on this representation, with the resultant architecture referred to as the phenotype. The paper emphasizes that certain parts of the genotype may be conditionally active, influencing the phenotype when modified.

In conclusion, the paper outlines various approaches and components utilized in EA-based NAS, underscoring the diversity of methods available for encoding the search space and implementing mutation operators and selection functions.
