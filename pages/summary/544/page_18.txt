This paper discusses various evolutionary algorithms (EAs) used for neural architecture search (NAS), highlighting six notable works in the field. A summary of these methods is presented in Table 1, which outlines their search space, initialization, parent selection, and survivor selection strategies.

Real et al. (2017) are recognized as pioneers in applying EAs to identify competitive convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures for image classification. Their method starts with a basic architecture consisting of a global pooling layer and an output layer. During parent selection, two architectures are sampled, with the superior one being mutated and trained, while the inferior one is discarded. The mutation process includes various modifications such as adding convolutions, adjusting kernel sizes, and altering learning rates. Xie and Yuille (2017) introduced a genetic algorithm that operates within a more structured search space, focusing on segments of networks that may include parallel convolution operations. Their work emphasizes a different architectural composition compared to Real et al. (2017), showcasing the diversity in approaches to NAS.

The paper provides an overview of these evolutionary algorithms, emphasizing their methodologies and the implications of their architectural choices in the context of neural network design.
