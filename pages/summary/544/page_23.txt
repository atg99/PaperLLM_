The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), particularly focusing on the approach by Luo et al. (2018), which integrates an autoencoder with a surrogate model for architecture representation. This method utilizes the continuous encoding from the autoencoder as input for the surrogate model. The main innovation lies in the search algorithm that employs gradient-based steps to modify the architecture code based on accuracy predictions from the surrogate model. The architecture is then reconstructed using a decoder, and both the surrogate model and the autoencoder are updated iteratively through the training of new samples, optimizing for reconstruction and performance prediction losses.

Additionally, the paper introduces the concept of one-shot architecture search, characterized by training a single neural network that generates various architectures as candidate solutions. This technique relies on over-parameterized networks, allowing for efficient searching with minimal additional computational cost compared to training individual architectures. One notable method described is the weight sharing approach from Pham et al. (2018), which operates within a subspace of the NASNet search space. It employs reinforcement learning to control architecture sampling, alternating updates between the controller and the over-parameterized network through gradient descent. This approach facilitates a streamlined search process while leveraging the capabilities of existing optimization methods.
