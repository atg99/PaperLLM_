The paper discusses advancements in surrogate model-based optimization aimed at enhancing the efficiency of neural architecture search (NAS). It highlights the work of Kandasamy et al. (2018), who approach NAS as a black-box optimization problem using Bayesian optimization techniques. Their method employs a probabilistic surrogate model, specifically a Gaussian process, combined with an acquisition function that balances predicted outcomes and uncertainty. They introduce a novel kernel function for measuring similarity between network architectures, utilizing an optimal transport program for this computation, and integrate an evolutionary algorithm to optimize the acquisition function.

Additionally, Liu et al. (2018a) focus on surrogate models within the NASNet search space, progressively increasing the number of blocks in a cell as a hyperparameter. Their search strategy employs beam search, generating new candidates by adding blocks to existing cells, while simultaneously training the surrogate model to predict validation accuracy. They update the surrogate model with new architectures in their meta-dataset, exploring different models, including RNNs, LSTMs, and MLPs, to accommodate variable-sized inputs. This comprehensive approach aims to refine the search for optimal neural architectures efficiently.
