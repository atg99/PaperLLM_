The paper by Martin Wistuba, Ambrish Rawat, and Tejaswini Pedapati discusses the advancements in neural architecture search (NAS) and its implications for deep learning. It highlights the growing trend of automating architecture optimization across various network types, including autoencoders, transformers, and graph CNNs. However, the authors point out significant challenges, particularly the need for joint optimization of all configuration parameters within the deep learning workflow, as current methods often treat components independently and still require manual configuration.

The paper raises critical questions about the meaningfulness and practical applicability of NAS, emphasizing the importance of search space design in determining the performance of architectures discovered through these methods. The authors argue that many architectures derived from NASNet may not represent true innovations but rather reflect the properties of their search spaces. They reference studies indicating that random searches can yield comparable results, challenging the perceived superiority of architectures found through NAS.

Additionally, the authors suggest that if the goal is to discover genuinely innovative architectures, it may be necessary to move beyond restrictive search spaces. They question the rationale for adopting NAS, noting that many discovered architectures can be effectively transferred across datasets and tasks, which raises the issue of whether the search process is truly beneficial.

In conclusion, the paper calls for a reevaluation of the current approaches in architecture search, considering the limitations and the potential for existing architectures combined with hyperparameter optimization to be equally or more effective.
