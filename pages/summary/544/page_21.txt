The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) with a focus on two main methodologies: evolutionary algorithms and surrogate model-based optimization.

In the evolutionary algorithm proposed by Wistuba, function-preserving mutations are employed to generate offspring from an initial architecture composed of three convolution and two max pooling layers. This method allows for the creation of eight offspring, each trained for 17 epochs, which significantly reduces training time and enhances search efficiency. Additionally, the algorithm diversifies the initial population by generating fifteen children from the base network, employing tournament selection for parent selection, and ensuring that all individuals survive the evolution process to maintain diversity.

The paper also explores surrogate model-based optimization, which utilizes a surrogate model to approximate the response function of different architectures. This approach aims to bypass the time-consuming training step, thereby improving search efficiency. The surrogate model is trained on a meta-dataset containing architecture descriptions and their respective response values. The goal is to minimize the squared error for the model, although often only ranking of architectures is needed. The predictions from the surrogate model help identify promising candidates, which are then evaluated and used to update the surrogate model iteratively until convergence is achieved.

Overall, the paper highlights innovative strategies in NAS, emphasizing the importance of efficiency and diversity in the search for optimal neural architectures.
