The paper evaluates the AdaBits algorithm for image classification on the ImageNet dataset, comparing its performance against individually quantized models with varying bit-widths. The study involves quantizing models such as MobileNet V1/V2 and ResNet50 using the AdaBits approach. The methodology follows a quantization strategy similar to SAT, initializing quantized models from full-precision counterparts and maintaining consistent training hyperparameters.

Key hyperparameters include a learning rate starting at 0.05, a total of 150 epochs, and specific batch sizes for different models. The results showcase the Top-1 accuracy for various models, indicating that AdaBits achieves performance comparable to individual quantization for all tested bit-widths. Notably, the method significantly enhances performance for lower bit-widths, particularly in ResNet50, with improvements of 2.2% for both 4-bit and 3-bit configurations. The modified scheme of AdaBits allows direct adaptation from higher to lower bit-widths, facilitating reduced model sizes while maintaining accuracy.

Additionally, the paper visualizes clipping levels across different layers in the AB-MobileNet V1 model, revealing that larger bit-widths correspond to higher clipping levels. This privatization of clipping levels enhances model optimization by selecting optimal values for different bit-widths. Overall, the findings affirm that adaptive bit-width quantization offers a viable strategy for balancing efficiency and accuracy in deep neural networks.
