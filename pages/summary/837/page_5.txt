This paper investigates the impact of quantization on neural network models, specifically focusing on the variances of weights and activations across different bit-widths. The findings indicate that as the bit-width decreases, the variance of both quantized weights and activations increases, which can adversely affect model performance.

The authors conduct experiments with the ResNet50 model on the ImageNet dataset, comparing direct adaptations of models trained with different bit-widths. Results show that models trained with 4 bits and fine-tuned to lower bit-widths do not retain their performance effectively, particularly at the 2-bit level. Conversely, training initially at 2 bits and increasing to 4 bits yields better outcomes at higher bit-widths but degrades performance at lower levels. This suggests that progressive training methods may introduce unwanted perturbations that impair the model's original capabilities.

To address the limitations of sequential training, the paper proposes a joint quantization approach, akin to slimmable neural networks, where models are trained simultaneously across various bit-widths with shared weights. This method, termed Vanilla AdaBits, demonstrates comparable performance to individually trained models across all tested bit-widths. However, a slight performance gap remains at the lowest bit-width, indicating room for further enhancements.

Overall, the paper highlights the challenges of maintaining model performance across varying quantization levels and suggests joint optimization as a more effective training strategy.
