The paper discusses quantization schemes for neural networks, particularly focusing on the limitations of existing methods when adapting weights to different bit-widths. The original scheme uses a quantization function defined by Eq. (2), which lacks a practical mapping for adaptive bit-widths. This results in the need to store full-precision weights and repeat the quantization process for various bit-widths, leading to increased model size and limited application.

To address these issues, the authors propose a modified quantization function (Eq. (3)) that facilitates easier conversion between different bit-widths by allowing the direct discarding of lower bits. This modification is shown to maintain performance similar to the original DoReFa scheme while enabling efficient adaptation from higher to lower bit-widths. The paper presents Theorem 1 to support this adaptive capability.

Further experiments investigate the feasibility of using quantized models trained at one bit-width on others. Results indicate that without batch normalization (BN) calibration, models exhibit significant performance degradation when evaluated on different bit-widths. However, applying BN calibration helps preserve performance when transitioning from lower to higher bit-widths, though models trained on higher bit-widths still struggle when quantized to lower precisions.

Overall, the findings suggest that direct adaptation of quantized models across bit-widths is challenging due to variations in layer statistics, and careful training and calibration are necessary to optimize performance across different quantization settings.
