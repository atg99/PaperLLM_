This paper explores the concept of adaptive bit-width quantization for deep neural networks, specifically focusing on MobileNet V1/V2 and ResNet50 models. The authors introduce a joint training approach that optimizes bit-widths across quantized models, enhancing efficiency without significantly compromising accuracy. The study compares their adaptive bit-width method, AdaBits, with existing quantization techniques, particularly the state-of-the-art SAT approach.

Key findings include the performance of various bit-width configurations, showing that models with adaptive bit-widths can achieve similar top-1 accuracy as those quantized individually at different bit-widths. For instance, MobileNet V1 at 6 bits shows a slight accuracy improvement compared to its 8-bit counterpart, while the adaptive scheme maintains competitive performance across different configurations.

The authors argue that incorporating bit-width as a design parameter expands the flexibility of model architecture, allowing for better adaptation to hardware constraints and resource availability. They also suggest future research directions, including evaluating AdaBits against other quantization methods and utilizing adaptive models for neural architecture search.

In conclusion, the paper presents a promising approach for dynamic model design that can adjust to varying operational environments, thereby broadening the applicability of neural networks in real-world scenarios.
