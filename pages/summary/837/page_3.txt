The paper discusses the deployment of neural networks with varying bit-widths based on computational budgets, presenting two approaches for training quantized models. The left side of the figure illustrates the training of multiple quantized models tailored for specific scenarios, while the right side showcases a single model trained with adaptive bit-widths that can switch according to device conditions.

The section on Scale-Adjusted Training (SAT) highlights that quantization often leads to performance degradation due to reduced model capacity. However, recent findings suggest that a significant portion of this degradation stems from inefficient training. SAT is proposed as an effective method to mitigate overfitting by scaling down the weights of quantized models, particularly in linear layers without batch normalization. This technique, when combined with the parameterized clipping activation (PACT) approach, enhances training efficiency and can result in quantized models outperforming traditional methods, and in some cases, even full-precision models.

The paper further explores the benefits and challenges associated with quantization using adaptive bit-widths. It emphasizes the advantages such as reduced model size, latency, and energy consumption, while also noting the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency. The authors investigate direct adaptation and progressive quantization methods, which yield unsatisfactory results, leading to the proposal of a novel joint quantization approach aimed at achieving performance parity with adaptive models compared to individual models.

Overall, the research underscores the potential of SAT and adaptive bit-width quantization in enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of neural network deployments.
