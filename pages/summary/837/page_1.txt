The paper "AdaBits: Neural Network Quantization with Adaptive Bit-Widths" by Qing Jin, Linjie Yang, and Zhenyu Liao explores the potential of adaptive configurations in deep neural networks to enhance their deployment across various platforms with differing resource constraints. The authors propose a novel approach that allows for adaptive bit-widths of weights and activations during model training and inference.

The paper discusses the benefits and challenges associated with quantized models that utilize adaptive bit-widths. Through experimentation with methods such as direct adaptation, progressive training, and joint training, it is revealed that joint training can achieve performance comparable to that of individual models. A key innovation introduced in this work is the Switchable Clipping Level (S-CL) technique, which enhances the performance of quantized models at lower bit-widths.

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their proposed methods on models like MobileNet V1/V2 and ResNet50, highlighting that adjusting the bit-width of weights and activations can significantly improve the accuracy-efficiency trade-off. This adaptability facilitates immediate adjustments to meet the constraints of real-world applications, making it a valuable strategy for optimizing neural networks in scenarios with varying resource requirements.

In summary, the paper emphasizes the importance of bit-width adaptation as an underutilized aspect of model flexibility, offering a pathway for improved model compression and performance in diverse deployment contexts.
