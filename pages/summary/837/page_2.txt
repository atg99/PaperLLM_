This paper investigates the challenge of quantizing neural networks with adaptive bit-widths, a method applicable to key components such as convolutional and fully-connected layers. While adaptive bit-widths may seem straightforward, the authors argue that naive approaches can lead to significant information loss during quantization, ultimately compromising model performance. They highlight that even advanced techniques like scale-adjusted training (SAT) do not guarantee optimal outcomes when fine-tuning quantized models.

The authors introduce a novel technique called Switchable Clipping Level (S-CL), which adjusts clipping levels to accommodate varying activation values, thereby minimizing quantization errors. Through empirical analysis, they demonstrate that inappropriate clipping levels can adversely affect model accuracy, particularly at lower precisions. The paper emphasizes that their work is pioneering in addressing the production of quantized models with adaptive bit-widths.

Structured as follows, the paper first reviews relevant literature, then revisits SAT, discusses potential benefits and challenges of adaptive quantization, and proposes a joint training strategy incorporating S-CL. The results show that adaptive models can achieve accuracies comparable to individual-precision models across various bit-widths and neural network architectures, including MobileNet V1/V2 and ResNet50. 

Overall, this research contributes to the understanding of neural network quantization, particularly in the context of developing flexible models that maintain performance across different bit-width settings.
