The paper presents a framework called TCDF for temporal causal discovery, which is evaluated through experiments on two benchmarks: simulated financial market data and simulated functional magnetic resonance imaging (FMRI) data. The evaluation aims to assess the accuracy of TCDF in discovering causal relationships, predicting outcomes, and identifying delays between causes and effects. The experiments include a comparison with three existing temporal causal discovery methods, with a focus on various metrics such as causal discovery accuracy and prediction performance.

The benchmarks consist of multiple datasets, with the financial market data comprising nine datasets and the FMRI data consisting of 27 datasets. The paper details the characteristics of these datasets, including the number of variables, causal relationships, and time series lengths. Notably, the financial market data features known delays between cause and effect, while the FMRI data does not provide this information. The architecture of the AD-DSTCNs is also examined, specifically the impact of varying the number of hidden layers on the discovery of causal relationships.

Overall, the results of the experiments, which are presented in a subsequent section, aim to demonstrate the effectiveness of TCDF in uncovering causal structures in temporal data while addressing challenges such as hidden confounders.
