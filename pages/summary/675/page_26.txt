The referenced works focus on various methodologies and advancements in causal inference, particularly in the context of time series data and machine learning. Key contributions include the application of Granger causality and dynamic causal modeling for analyzing connectivity in neural data, as discussed by Friston et al. and Chen et al. Other notable works explore foundational concepts in causal inference, such as the elements presented by Peters et al. and the use of structural equation models for time series analysis.

Several studies emphasize the identification of causal relationships in non-stationary time series, with Papana et al. providing a technical report on the topic. The significance of nonlinear models in causal discovery is highlighted by Chu and Glymour, as well as by Entner and Hoyer in their exploration of the FCI algorithm. The discourse on directed information and transfer entropy is advanced by Jiao et al. and Liu and Aviyente, respectively.

Innovative approaches using deep learning, such as causal generative neural networks and interpretable LSTM models, are presented by Goudet et al. and Guo et al., showcasing the intersection of machine learning and causal inference. The challenges of learning long-term dependencies in neural networks are acknowledged, referencing the foundational work of Bengio et al.

Overall, this collection of studies illustrates the evolving landscape of causal inference methodologies, demonstrating their applicability across various domains, particularly in neuroscience and machine learning.
