For prediction performance, the mean absolute scaled error (MASE) is utilized as it remains unaffected by the scale of the time series values and is stable for near-zero values. To evaluate causal relationships, precision and recall are calculated based on the directed edges in the learnt graph (GL) compared to the ground truth graph (GG). True Positives (TP), False Positives (FP), and False Negatives (FN) are defined accordingly, with a focus on direct causes. A full ground-truth graph (GF) is constructed to include indirect causal relationships, allowing for a more comprehensive evaluation of the learnt graph.

The evaluation also includes measuring delays between causes and effects, comparing discovered delays in GL to those in GF. The effectiveness of PIVM is assessed by analyzing changes in F1-scores when PIVM is applied, aiming to identify true causal relationships. Additionally, the method's ability to detect hidden confounders is tested against a benchmark, focusing on whether the confounder has equal delays to its effects.

Overall, the evaluation framework is designed to provide a robust assessment of causal discovery methods in time series analysis, ensuring that both direct and indirect relationships are adequately considered.
