The study evaluates the performance of various causal discovery methods, specifically TCDF, PCMCI, tsFCI, and TiMINo, in analyzing time series data from two benchmarks: FINANCE and FMRI. The findings indicate that TCDF performs better on datasets with T>1000, surpassing PCMCI and significantly outpacing tsFCI and TiMINo. While PCMCI remains robust across time series lengths, TiMINo struggles, particularly in high-dimensional data, leading to numerous false positives. tsFCI is noted for being overly conservative, missing many causal relationships.

In terms of computational efficiency, PCMCI and tsFCI are faster than TCDF across both benchmarks. The study also explores delay discovery, revealing that PCMCI accurately identifies all delays in the FINANCE benchmark, with TCDF and tsFCI performing comparably. TiMINo, however, does not assess delays.

The impact of the causal validation method PIVM is assessed, showing a significant performance drop for TCDF when PIVM is omitted in the FINANCE benchmark due to the prevalence of confounders. In contrast, PIVM's effect is negligible in the FMRI datasets, which have fewer confounders. Overall, the results suggest that the effectiveness of causal validation techniques varies with the complexity of the data, typically enhancing discovery accuracy without negative repercussions.
