The paper discusses the methodology for identifying causal relationships within time series data using a temporal causal graph and a HardSoftmax function. The authors emphasize the importance of selecting appropriate gaps in the temporal data to avoid inclusion of low attention scores, with a maximum of 50% of input time series being potential causes for a target series. This selection ensures that at least two time series are considered as potential causes for each target series.

The HardSoftmax function categorizes relationships between time series based on their attention scores into four cases, addressing both direct and indirect causal relationships, as well as the influence of hidden confounders. The potential for spurious correlations is acknowledged, but the framework does not attempt to discern these correlations without expert analysis.

The paper also outlines a Permutation Importance Validation Method (PIVM) to validate the identified potential causes, distinguishing between various causal scenarios. True causes are defined as those validated through this process. The authors highlight the challenges posed by hidden confounders in accurately identifying true causes and detail how their approach maintains compliance with the principles of temporal precedence and physical influence, ensuring that cause-effect relationships adhere to established causal criteria.
