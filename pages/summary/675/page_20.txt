The evaluation involved comparing TCDF against other methods across various datasets, specifically in predicting time series and assessing causal relationships.

In terms of time series prediction, TCDF showed promising results, particularly with the L=0 configuration, where it achieved a mean absolute scaled error (MASE) of less than one, indicating better performance than naive methods. However, the performance declined for more complex configurations (L=1 and L=2) when applied to shorter FMRI datasets.

Causal discovery results highlighted TCDF's strengths, especially in the FINANCE benchmark, where it consistently outperformed other methods in both F1 and F1’ scores. The higher F1’-scores suggest that a significant portion of TCDF's identified indirect causal relationships were indeed valid, despite some false positives. In contrast, TCDF faced challenges with the FMRI benchmark due to the limited length of several datasets, which affected its overall performance relative to the FINANCE benchmark.

The findings indicate that while TCDF is effective in predicting causal relationships and identifying hidden confounders, its performance can be influenced by the complexity of the data and the length of the time series involved.
