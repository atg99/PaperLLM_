Traditional methods like Granger Causality and various extensions, such as conditional copula and constraint-based approaches, primarily focus on linear dependencies and often struggle with unmeasured confounders. These methods include PCMCI and tsFCI for temporal causal discovery, which adapt non-temporal graph algorithms, and Structural Equation Models (SEM) like TiMINo and VAR-LiNGAM, which have limitations in handling large datasets and independence testing.

Information-theoretic approaches, such as transfer entropy and shifted directed information, are model-free and can detect both linear and nonlinear dependencies but face challenges with non-stationary data. Partial Symbolic Transfer Entropy (PSTE) addresses this issue but is still less effective in purely linear contexts.

Causal Significance offers a framework for measuring causal impact while assuming linearity and full observability of causes. The authors demonstrate that even with violated assumptions, the method maintains low false discovery rates.

In contrast, the proposed deep learning approach employs neural networks to predict time series and validates findings through random permutation of variable values, allowing for a more flexible interpretation of causal relationships. However, the application of deep learning for causal discovery remains in its early stages, highlighting the need for further exploration in this area.
