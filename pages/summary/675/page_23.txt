The study evaluates the performance of various causal discovery methods, particularly focusing on the Temporal Causal Discovery Framework (TCDF), in analyzing time series data. The findings indicate that TCDF successfully identifies hidden confounders and causal relationships, outperforming alternatives like PCMCI, tsFCI, and TiMINo in accuracy, especially with longer time series data such as those from financial and fMRI sources. However, TCDF occasionally misidentifies causal relationships when delays to effects are unequal.

In contrast, PCMCI maintains accuracy regardless of time series length and is faster in computation, while tsFCI fails to discover hidden confounders and can produce incorrect causal links. The paper emphasizes the importance of interpreting the causal graphs generated by these methods as hypotheses requiring validation through domain expertise or experimentation, particularly in the presence of spurious correlations.

Additionally, the sensitivity of TCDF to hyperparameters, such as the number of hidden layers in its architecture, is discussed. The results suggest that the optimal configuration of these parameters varies depending on the dataset and its length, with certain architectures performing better with longer time series. Overall, while TCDF shows robust capabilities in causal discovery, the study acknowledges limitations and the necessity for further validation through empirical methods.
