The paper discusses the evaluation of a proposed framework, TCDF, using two benchmarks derived from fMRI data representing brain activity. The first benchmark, FMRI, includes simulated BOLD datasets from 28 brain networks, with one dataset excluded due to its 50 nodes. The remaining 27 networks feature time series affected by external noise and processed through a non-linear balloon model. A subset of this benchmark, FMRI T>1000, contains only datasets with at least 1000 time steps and is stationary.

The experimental setup compares TCDF against three other methods: PCMCI, tsFCI, and TiMINo. TCDF employs Mean Squared Error as its loss function and utilizes the Adam optimizer, training for 5000 epochs with specific parameters. The influence of hidden layers on accuracy is also examined.

PCMCI is implemented using the Tigramite module, with settings for maximum and minimum delays aligned with TCDF. It employs a linear partial correlation test for causal discovery. tsFCI similarly sets maximum delays and adjusts its conservative approach through a majority rule, focusing on direct causes. TiMINo, which assumes a linear time series model, faced challenges in yielding results across various significance levels, ultimately being configured to always produce a directed acyclic graph (DAG).

Overall, the study aims to assess the predictive performance of TCDF in relation to established methods, highlighting the experimental conditions and methodological specifics involved in the comparison.
