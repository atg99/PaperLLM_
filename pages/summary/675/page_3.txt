The paper addresses the problem of temporal causal discovery from observational data, focusing on how to identify causal relationships and the associated time delays between multiple time series. Given a dataset of N observed continuous time series of equal length T, the objective is to construct a directed causal graph where vertices represent time series and directed edges signify causal relationships, annotated with time delays.

The authors highlight several challenges inherent in causal discovery methods, particularly when dealing with complex underlying causal models. Key challenges include:

1. **Distinction Between Direct and Indirect Causes**: Traditional pairwise methods struggle to differentiate between direct and indirect causality, while multivariate methods can account for all variables to make this distinction.

2. **Instantaneous Causal Effects**: The ability to learn instantaneous effects, where the cause and effect occur at the same time, is crucial. Ignoring these effects can lead to inaccurate interpretations.

3. **Confounders**: The presence of confounders, which are common causes of multiple variables, complicates causal discovery. The authors emphasize the importance of correctly identifying relationships without misattributing causality due to confounding effects, especially when the delays differ.

4. **Hidden Confounders**: The challenge becomes more pronounced when confounders are not observed. The authors stress the necessity for causal discovery methods to hypothesize the existence of such hidden confounders to avoid deriving incorrect causal relations.

Overall, the paper underscores the complexities involved in temporal causal discovery and the need for sophisticated methods to accurately model causal relationships and their temporal aspects.
