The section discusses two key concepts in deep learning: residual connections and attention interpretation. 

Residual connections address the issue of increasing training error with deeper networks, which occurs not due to overfitting but because standard backpropagation struggles to find optimal weights in a deep architecture. By adding the input of a convolutional layer to its output after applying a transformation, the network can better learn and optimize weights, improving performance.

In the context of attention mechanisms, the initial attention scores are set to 1 and are adjusted during training through backpropagation. Two types of attention are described: soft attention, which uses the Softmax function to ensure scores are between 0 and 1, and hard attention, which produces binary decisions but is not differentiable. The paper proposes a semi-binarization function called HardSoftmax that sets scores below a defined threshold to zero after training. 

To determine this threshold, an algorithm identifies the largest gap between ranked attention scores, setting the threshold to the score just before this gap. The process ensures that the threshold is always less than or equal to 1, reflecting the initialization of scores and the network's ability to focus on relevant time series.
