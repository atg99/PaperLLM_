The paper discusses the Temporal Causal Discovery Framework (TCDF) and its capabilities in identifying causal relationships and delays between variables, particularly in the presence of hidden confounders. It illustrates how TCDF can incorrectly infer causal relationships based on the delays from a confounder to two variables, X_i and X_j. In scenarios where the delay from the confounder to X_i is less than that to X_j, TCDF may mistakenly identify a causal link from X_i to X_j. Conversely, if the delays are equal, TCDF recognizes the absence of direct causation and suggests the existence of a hidden confounder.

The advantage of TCDF is its dual ability to negate false causal relationships and identify hidden confounders. Additionally, TCDF excels in discovering the number of time steps between a cause and its effect by analyzing kernel weights associated with input time series. The architecture allows for the interpretation of these weights, revealing the significance of various time delays. The maximum delay that can be identified is contingent upon the receptive field size, which must be adequate to encompass the estimated delays for effective causal inference. This framework emphasizes the importance of understanding temporal dynamics in causal relationships and provides a robust method for delay discovery.
