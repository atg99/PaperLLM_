The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in causal discovery, particularly in the context of time series data. Traditional methods for identifying causal relationships often involve costly and impractical experiments. However, with the increase in observational data due to digital sensing, there is an opportunity to use causal discovery techniques to analyze this data effectively. Causal discovery is crucial for interpreting data, testing hypotheses, and enhancing models, and it plays an important role in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI).

The authors highlight that existing statistical methods for causal discovery from temporal data have limitations, including reliance on idealized assumptions that may not hold in real-world scenarios. To address these challenges, the paper introduces a new framework called the Temporal Causal Discovery Framework (TCDF), which utilizes Attention-based Deep Neural Networks (DNNs). This framework is designed for both time series prediction and temporal causal discovery, leveraging the ability of DNNs to learn complex patterns in data while being robust to noise.

TCDF employs multiple convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to predict time series based on their historical values. The framework incorporates attention mechanisms to identify potential causal relationships and includes a validation step to confirm these relationships through intervention. It also features a novel method for determining the time delay between causes and effects.

The paper presents the following contributions:
1. Development of TCDF for discovering causal relationships in time series data and establishing time delays.
2. Evaluation of TCDF against existing methods using financial and FMRI data benchmarks.

The structure of the paper is outlined, with sections dedicated to problem statements, a review of existing methods, detailed descriptions of TCDF, evaluation results, and discussions on hyperparameter tuning and limitations, concluding with future research directions.
