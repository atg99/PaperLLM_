The paper discusses an extension of the univariate Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) architecture to a multivariate causal discovery framework, termed TCDF. The primary limitation of traditional convolutional layers in TCNs is that they produce one-dimensional outputs, resulting in mixed input time series that complicate causal inference in deep networks. To address this, the authors introduce a one-dimensional depthwise separable architecture that maintains the separation of input time series, thereby enhancing the interpretation of relationships between inputs and target series.

The depthwise separable convolution, which gained popularity with Google's Xception architecture, applies distinct kernels to each input channel, followed by a pointwise convolution that merges outputs. This approach improves accuracy and convergence speed while preserving the integrity of the input series.

The proposed TCDF architecture comprises multiple channels, each corresponding to a specific input time series. The architecture is designed to predict target time series while incorporating an attention mechanism to enhance interpretability. The attention mechanism, labeled as Attention-based Dilated Depthwise Separable Temporal Convolutional Networks (AD-DSTCNs), utilizes a trainable attention vector that is element-wise multiplied with the input time series. This results in attention scores that indicate the degree of influence one time series may have on another, allowing for the identification of potential causal relationships after training. High attention scores suggest a strong causal link, while low scores indicate minimal influence. The framework also accommodates self-causation, enabling a more comprehensive analysis of the time series interactions.
