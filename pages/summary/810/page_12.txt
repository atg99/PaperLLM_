One such technique is dynamic pruning, which adjusts the level of sparsity during training based on the importance of weights or filters. This allows the model to adaptively determine which components to prune, potentially leading to improved accuracy and reduced computational costs.

Another notable method is gradient-based pruning, where the gradients of the weights are analyzed to identify which weights contribute the least to the loss function. This information is then used to selectively prune weights that have minimal impact on the model's performance.

Additionally, structured sparsity can be applied at levels finer than filters, such as channel or block levels. This allows for greater flexibility in the pruning process and can lead to better trade-offs between model size and accuracy.

In summary, the paper discusses various extensions to structured pruning, including the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, joint compression techniques, and special granularity approaches. These methods aim to enhance the efficiency of neural networks while preserving or even improving their performance. The iterative prune-and-fine-tune strategy serves as a foundation for exploring these advanced pruning techniques, ultimately contributing to more effective network compression strategies.
