The paper discusses various regularization techniques for structured sparsity in neural networks, focusing on filter and channel pruning methods. Structured Sparsity Learning (SSL) employs Group Lasso to remove specific channels and their corresponding filters, introducing separate regularization terms for filter-wise and channel-wise pruning. Out-In-Channel Sparsity Regularization (OICSR) also uses Group Lasso but emphasizes the interdependence of out-channel and in-channel filters, regularizing them together.

Only Train Once (OTO) proposes grouping all parameters that contribute to non-zero activations and applying mixed ℓ1/ℓ2-norm regularization to this group. Growing Regularization (GREG) utilizes a two-step approach: first, it uses ℓ1-norm for pruning masks, followed by a growing ℓ2 penalty to gradually drive unimportant filters to zero while leveraging Hessian information for effective weight separation.

The paper also covers optimization tools essential for inducing structured sparsity, including Taylor Expansion, which approximates changes in loss associated with pruning. Variational Bayesian methods, SGD-based methods, ADMM-based methods, and Bayesian Optimization are discussed as techniques to determine filter importance or to resolve redundancy in filters.

Taylor Expansion is specifically addressed as a method for evaluating the loss function when certain weights are pruned. The first-order and second-order approximations are explained, highlighting the trade-offs between computational efficiency and the amount of information retained. The first-order approximation is used in previous works to estimate the change in loss due to pruning activation maps, while the second-order expansion, though richer in information, is more computationally demanding. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of structured sparsity and optimization techniques in enhancing neural network efficiency.
