NISP proposes a framework that minimizes the reconstruction error of the Final Response Layer by propagating neuron importance scores throughout the network. This method accounts for the interdependencies among filters and layers, ensuring that pruning decisions are informed by their impact on the overall model performance.

The paper discusses several activation-based pruning methods, emphasizing their reliance on activation maps rather than filter weights for determining filter importance. These methods can be categorized based on the layer they focus on: the current layer, adjacent layers, or all layers. 

In the current layer context, methods like Channel Pruning (CP) and HRank leverage reconstruction error and activation map rank to guide pruning decisions. CP employs LASSO regression to identify which channels to prune, while HRank uses Singular Value Decomposition to assess filter importance based on average rank. Coreset-Based Compression (CBC) and Channel Independence (CHIP) further refine filter selection through statistical measures of activation norms and cross-channel correlations.

For adjacent layers, ThiNet and Approximated Oracle Filter Pruning (AOFP) utilize the activation maps of the next layer to inform pruning strategies. ThiNet approximates the next layer’s activation using subsets from the current layer, while AOFP introduces damage isolation to mitigate the impact of pruning on subsequent layers.

Lastly, methods that consider all layers, like Neuron Importance Score Propagation (NISP), aim to evaluate filter importance in a more integrated manner, ensuring that pruning decisions reflect their broader implications on model performance.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolving landscape of filter pruning techniques, detailing various methodologies that enhance model efficiency while maintaining performance.
