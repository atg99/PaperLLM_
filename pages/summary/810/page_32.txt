The table summarizes the performance of various pruning methods applied to the VGG-16 and VGG-19 models on the CIFAR-100 dataset. Key metrics include the top-1 accuracy before and after pruning, the reduction in accuracy, the baseline and pruned FLOPs (floating-point operations), and the change in parameters. 

For VGG-16, methods such as SCP, GCNP, and DLRFC show varying impacts on accuracy, with SCP achieving a baseline accuracy of 73.51% and a pruned accuracy of 73.86%, resulting in a slight decrease of 0.35%. In contrast, the GCNP method had a pruned accuracy of 72.00%, with minimal accuracy change. For VGG-19, GREG and ED methods exhibit notable drops in accuracy, with GREG showing a decrease of 6.47% from a baseline of 74.02%. Some methods like SEP and SCP demonstrate improvements or minimal decreases in accuracy while achieving substantial reductions in FLOPs and parameters.

Overall, the table illustrates a trade-off between accuracy and efficiency across different pruning techniques, with some methods maintaining performance while reducing computational complexity.
