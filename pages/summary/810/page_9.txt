This paper discusses various methods for model pruning in deep learning, focusing on both static and dynamic approaches to enhance efficiency while preserving model performance. Key techniques include:

1. **Informational Measures**: Methods such as those proposed by Louizos and Neklyudov utilize theoretical measures of redundancy and priors to model channel redundancy effectively.

2. **SGD-based Methods**: Techniques like Centripetal SGD and Regularized Modernized Dual Averaging focus on merging redundant filters or ensuring model structure remains intact while promoting sparsity through Group Lasso.

3. **ADMM-based Methods**: The Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers is employed to break down complex problems into manageable subproblems, with StructADMM addressing different sparsity types and Zhang et al. using a progressive framework for pruning.

4. **Bayesian Optimization**: This method tackles the challenge of high-dimensional spaces in pruning by employing a sequential design strategy to optimize pruning policies.

5. **Soft Thresholding**: The Soft Threshold Reparameterization technique applies a de-noising operator to establish a non-uniform sparsity budget, optimized per layer.

6. **Dynamic Pruning**: This involves adjusting pruning masks either during training or inference. Dynamic methods during training, such as Dynamic Network Surgery and Soft Filter Pruning, allow for flexible weight updates and mask changes to maintain model capacity. Dynamic inference adapts the model based on input complexity for improved resource allocation.

7. **Innovative Dynamic Techniques**: Approaches like Dynamic Pruning with Feedback and Channel Exploration use feedback mechanisms and diverse regrowing processes to enhance pruning accuracy. Dynamic Sparse Graph methods focus on activating critical neurons while maintaining computational efficiency.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of advanced pruning strategies, emphasizing the balance between model efficiency and performance.
