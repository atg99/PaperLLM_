The paper provides a comprehensive survey on structured pruning for deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs), addressing the growing need for efficient models due to the significant computational costs associated with their complex architectures. Pruning is presented as a viable model compression technique aimed at reducing parameters and computational demands without compromising performance. 

The authors distinguish between unstructured and structured pruning, highlighting that while unstructured pruning removes individual weights leading to sparsity, structured pruning eliminates entire filters, allowing for realistic acceleration on standard hardware. The survey reviews various structured pruning techniques, including filter ranking methods, regularization strategies, dynamic execution, neural architecture search, and the lottery ticket hypothesis, while also briefly contrasting these with unstructured pruning approaches.

Additionally, the paper discusses the implications of structured pruning in the context of evolving architectures like Transformers, emphasizing the importance of CNN design principles such as locality and weight sharing. The authors conclude by outlining potential research directions in structured pruning, aiming to enhance the efficiency of neural network architectures. The paper includes curated resources for further exploration of pruning methodologies.
