The references listed comprise a collection of studies focused on various methodologies for neural network pruning, a technique aimed at reducing the size and complexity of deep learning models while maintaining their performance. Key themes include early network pruning, efficient training strategies, and the exploration of lottery ticket hypotheses, which suggest that smaller subnetworks can be trained to achieve comparable performance to their larger counterparts.

Several works investigate the timing and policies for pruning, such as "When to prune? A policy towards early structural pruning" and "Winning the lottery ahead of time: Efficient early network pruning." Other studies delve into joint approaches that combine pruning with architecture search and quantization, highlighting frameworks like NPAS (a unified network pruning and architecture search) and differentiable methods that optimize pruning and quantization simultaneously.

The references also emphasize the importance of task-agnostic and meta-learning approaches for identifying trainable weights and optimizing network structure. Techniques such as dynamic probabilistic pruning and layer-wise pruning based on feature representations are discussed as strategies to improve efficiency while considering hardware constraints.

Overall, this collection reflects ongoing advancements in the field of network pruning, exploring innovative techniques that balance model compactness with operational effectiveness.
