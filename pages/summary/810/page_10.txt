This paper discusses various dynamic neural pruning methods that enhance model efficiency during inference by adapting to the complexity of input data. The methods include:

1. **Runtime Neural Pruning (RNP)**: Utilizes a CNN backbone and RNN decision network to dynamically prune channels based on the difficulty of the input image, employing reinforcement learning to evaluate filter importance.

2. **Feature Boosting and Suppression (FBS)**: Introduces a small auxiliary network to predict output channel importance by subsampling activation channels, thus reducing computational overhead.

3. **Deep Reinforcement Learning Pruning (DRLP)**: Combines runtime and static channel importance assessment through a dual RL framework, enabling tailored pruning decisions based on both individual inputs and overall dataset characteristics.

4. **Dynamic Dual Gating (DDG)**: Implements separate spatial and channel gating modules to determine channel importance during inference, enhancing feature selection through adaptive pooling and convolutional layers.

5. **Fire Together Wire Together (FTWT)**: Models dynamic pruning as a self-supervised binary classification task, generating learnable binary masks to guide pruning decisions based on input activations.

6. **Contrastive Dual Gating (CDG)**: Uses contrastive learning to derive distinct pruning masks for two branches of a model, facilitating self-supervised learning of pruning strategies.

The paper also explores NAS-based pruning, which automates the determination of pruning hyperparameters through three main approaches: reinforcement learning, gradient-based methods, and evolutionary algorithms. Notable examples include:

- **AutoML for Model Compression (AMC)**: Employs reinforcement learning to select layer-wise pruning ratios without manual analysis, using a modified reward system to ensure accuracy.
  
- **Automatic Graph Encoder-Decoder Model Compression (AGMC)**: Advances AMC by using a graph-based representation of DNNs to automatically derive input states for the RL agent, improving the model's adaptability to sparsity constraints.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolution of dynamic pruning techniques and the integration of NAS to enhance model performance while reducing computational demands.
