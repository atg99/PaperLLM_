The paper presents a comprehensive taxonomy for structured pruning in neural networks, detailing various methods categorized into weight-dependent, activation-based, regularization, optimization tools, dynamic pruning, NAS-based pruning, and extensions. 

In the weight-dependent category, methods such as Filter Norm and Filter Correlation are discussed, emphasizing their impact on filter importance ranking. Activation-based techniques include those that assess current and adjacent layers, along with all-layer approaches. Regularization methods focus on parameters within batch normalization and additional parameters, as well as filters themselves. 

The optimization tools section reviews techniques like Taylor expansion and variational Bayesian methods, while dynamic pruning is divided into strategies employed during training and inference. NAS-based pruning encompasses approaches using reinforcement learning, gradient-based methods, and evolutionary strategies.

The extensions section addresses concepts like the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis and joint compression strategies, along with special granularity methods. Future directions for pruning research are outlined, highlighting topics such as theoretical advancements, task-specific pruning, and network-specific strategies, along with targets related to hardware, energy efficiency, and robustness.

The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding structural redundancy in pruning, using methods like Structural Redundancy Reduction to evaluate filter redundancy within a layer by analyzing associated graph properties. This structured overview aims to guide future research and application in the field of neural network pruning.
