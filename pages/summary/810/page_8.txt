The paper discusses various methods for pruning neural networks to enhance efficiency while maintaining accuracy. It distinguishes between first-order and second-order approaches for calculating parameter importance, highlighting the advantages of first-order methods such as Mol-19, which offer computational efficiency with minimal accuracy loss. 

In the context of second-order pruning, it references foundational techniques like Optimal Brain Damage (OBD) and Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS), which leverage Hessian matrices for parameter importance. However, due to the complexity of deep CNNs, approximations of the Hessian are necessary for practical implementation. Collaborative Channel Pruning (CCP) and Eigen Damage (ED) exemplify recent strategies that address challenges associated with structured pruning, particularly the interdependencies between channels.

The paper also introduces Variational Bayesian methods, which provide a framework for posterior distribution estimation in the context of pruning. Variational Pruning (VP) and Recursive Bayesian Pruning (RBP) are discussed as approaches that model channel importance probabilistically, allowing for a more nuanced approach to identifying redundant channels.

In summary, the reviewed literature emphasizes the evolution of pruning techniques, from simple first-order methods to more complex Bayesian frameworks, reflecting an ongoing effort to balance computational efficiency with model performance in deep learning applications.
