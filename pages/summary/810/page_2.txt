The paper provides a detailed survey of structured pruning methods in deep neural networks, categorizing existing research into several key areas: Pruning, Quantization, Decomposition, Knowledge Distillation, and Neural Architecture Search. It emphasizes the significance of structured pruning techniques, particularly focusing on filter pruning as a means to optimize convolutional neural networks (ConvNets). The authors discuss various methodologies, including weight-dependent criteria that evaluate filter importance based on their weights, specifically through metrics such as filter norm and filter correlation.

Weight-dependent methods are highlighted for their efficiency, as they do not require input data for analysis. Two subcategories are explored: filter norm, which assesses the magnitude of weights, and filter correlation, which examines relationships among filters. The paper references notable approaches such as PFEC and SFP for filter norm, and FPGM and RED for filter correlation, illustrating the evolution of pruning strategies aimed at maintaining model performance while reducing computational load.

The taxonomy presented organizes these methods into structured categories, and the paper also outlines future research directions in the field, aiming to provide a comprehensive understanding of structured pruning's potential in enhancing neural network efficiency. The survey is designed to be a resource for researchers and practitioners looking to navigate the complex landscape of structured pruning methodologies.
