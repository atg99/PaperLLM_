The paper discusses various granularities of pruning in neural networks, categorizing them into three basic dimensions: output dimension, input dimension, and kernel dimension. It outlines different approaches, such as filter-wise and channel-wise pruning, which are established methods, and introduces additional granularities like group-wise, stripe-wise, kernel-wise, grouped kernel, pattern-based, and block-wise pruning. Each method has distinct characteristics and impacts on model performance and computational efficiency.

Group-wise pruning focuses on weights at the same position among filters, while stripe-wise pruning maintains filter independence. Kernel-wise pruning targets weights along the kernel dimension. The paper highlights that some researchers advocate for diverse pruning decisions across dimensions, as seen in grouped kernel pruning and pattern-based pruning, which allow for a more tailored approach to network compression.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the potential for further development in pruning theory, exploring concepts like synaptic flow and signal propagation, and suggests that structured pruning can benefit from insights gained from unstructured pruning. It also examines the existing training-pruning-retraining mechanism and proposes new methods, such as single-shot pruning and AC/DC training, to improve the pruning process.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of current methods and future directions in structured pruning, aiming to enhance model efficiency while maintaining performance.
