This paper provides a comprehensive review of object detection, specifically focusing on the evolution of two-stage and single-stage object detectors, with a particular emphasis on the YOLO (You Only Look Once) family of models. It discusses the transition from traditional two-stage detectors, which were historically popular for their effectiveness, to the more recent advancements in single-stage detectors that have shown superior performance due to enhanced algorithms.

The introduction of YOLO has led to significant advancements in object detection applications across various contexts, outperforming traditional two-stage models. The paper highlights the architectural innovations and optimizations in different versions of YOLO, illustrating how they have become competitive alternatives to two-stage detectors.

The review begins with an overview of deep learning and computer vision, outlining their relevance to object detection. It traces the evolution of deep learning since its early days, noting the increased interest due to the availability of large datasets and advanced computational resources. The paper also emphasizes the role of computer vision in enabling machines to interpret visual information, covering its diverse subfields, including object detection, localization, and segmentation.

The authors aim to provide insights into the challenges faced in object detection, the design details of YOLO models, and potential future research directions in the field. This review seeks to contribute to the understanding of object detection methodologies and their practical applications in computer vision.
