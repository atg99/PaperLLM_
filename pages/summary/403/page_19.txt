The paper discusses the concept of class-specific scores (CSS) associated with bounding boxes in object detection, emphasizing the importance of accurately predicting the presence and fit of objects within these boxes. Bounding boxes, which vary in size to accommodate different object shapes, are referred to as anchor boxes. These are predefined based on an analysis of the dataset to represent various classes and aspect ratios.

The challenge of multiple grid cells predicting the same object is addressed, as neighboring cells can create overlapping bounding boxes. This necessitates a method to resolve duplicate detections. Each bounding box is linked to a CSS, box coordinates, and a classification output. A total of S²×B bounding boxes are predicted, with those scoring below a certain threshold—commonly set at 0.5—being discarded. This threshold is adjustable based on dataset characteristics and reflects the confidence in object presence and class identification.

Post-thresholding, a significant number of bounding boxes remain, prompting the need for non-max suppression (NMS) to further eliminate irrelevant boxes based on Intersection over Union (IoU) metrics. NMS selects the box with the highest class score and removes other boxes that overlap significantly, iterating until only the most relevant boxes are retained. The paper illustrates these concepts with figures demonstrating the prediction process and the effects of NMS on bounding box selection.
