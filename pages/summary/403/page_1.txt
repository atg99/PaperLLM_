The paper provides an in-depth analysis of object detection, a key challenge in computer vision, particularly focusing on the YOLO (You Only Look Once) framework and its successors. It categorizes object detectors into two main types: two-stage and single-stage detectors. Two-stage detectors utilize complex architectures to generate selective region proposals, typically achieving higher detection accuracy, while single-stage detectors, like YOLO, employ simpler architectures for faster inference times.

The authors highlight the trade-off between detection accuracy and inference speed, noting that while two-stage detectors such as Fast-RCNN may achieve better accuracy—63.4 for YOLO compared to 70 for Fast-RCNN—YOLO's inference time is significantly faster, approximately 300 times quicker. This efficiency makes YOLO particularly appealing for various applications, even if the accuracy is slightly lower.

The paper reviews YOLO's regression formulation, architectural advancements, and performance metrics, providing a comprehensive overview of single-stage object detection methods. It also summarizes comparative statistics to illustrate the growing effectiveness of YOLO in the realm of object detection, underscoring its relevance in contemporary applications due to its speed.
