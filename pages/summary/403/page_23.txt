The paper discusses advancements in neural network training techniques, focusing on Direct Location Prediction, Fine-Grained Features, and Multi-Scale Training. 

Batch Normalization is highlighted as a method to address the Internal Covariate Shift that occurs due to random weight initialization, leading to varying distributions across layers. By normalizing the outputs of each hidden layer, Batch Normalization maintains consistent weight distributions and acts as a regularization technique, allowing for higher learning rates. This approach resulted in an approximate 2% increase in mean Average Precision (mAP).

The High-Resolution Classifier is another significant improvement, where the model is initially trained on 448 × 448 sized images for classification rather than starting with a lower resolution and adjusting later. This pre-training enhances the model’s ability to provide better bounding boxes during object detection, yielding an approximate 4% increase in mAP.

Overall, these techniques contribute to improved performance in neural network tasks, particularly in object detection.
