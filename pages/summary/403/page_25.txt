The paper discusses advancements in object detection through the YOLO (v3) architecture, which addresses previous limitations by focusing on improved performance, particularly for smaller objects, while acknowledging its shortcomings with medium and large objects. YOLO (v3) is built on the Darknet-53 framework, featuring a robust architecture with 53 convolutional layers and employing 3 × 3 and 1 × 1 convolutional filters along with shortcut connections. This design achieves a significant speed advantage over ReNet-152 without sacrificing performance.

The architecture integrates principles from Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN), utilizing residual blocks and up-sampling techniques. YOLO (v3) generates feature maps at three different scales by down-sampling the input, resulting in feature maps of sizes 13 × 13, 26 × 26, and 52 × 52. These feature maps facilitate the detection of large, medium, and small objects, respectively, through a series of convolutions and concatenations across different layers.

The paper also introduces YOLO (v4), developed by Alexey Bochkovskiy and colleagues, which incorporates various new methods and complex techniques, enhancing both speed and accuracy compared to its predecessors.
