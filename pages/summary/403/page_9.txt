Faster R-CNN, introduced in early 2016, builds upon its predecessor Fast R-CNN and consists of two primary modules. The first module is the Region Proposal Network (RPN), which generates region proposals by processing a single image and outputting bounding boxes along with object confidence scores. During training, the RPN is initially trained on ImageNet, and the generated proposals are later used for detection and further training of Fast R-CNN with unique dense layers. In experiments on the Pascal VOC 2007 dataset, Faster R-CNN demonstrated a significant speed advantage, being 250 times faster than traditional R-CNN while achieving a mean Average Precision (mAP) similar to that of Fast R-CNN at around 66.

While the review primarily focuses on single-stage object detectors like YOLO, it acknowledges other notable two-stage detectors, such as the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN), which effectively addresses challenges in object detection by extracting features at multiple scales.

The applications of R-CNN and its successors are varied and include real-time object detection and tracking from drone-mounted cameras, leveraging embedded hardware like Jetson TX/AGX Xavier and Intel Neural Compute Stick. An innovative OCR system named Rosetta utilizes Faster R-CNN for text detection in millions of Facebook images, while Google Lens employs RPN for character-level bounding box detection in text recognition. The performance metrics indicate a dual focus on reducing processing time and improving detection accuracy, with Faster R-CNN architectures offering faster inference rates compared to traditional R-CNN.

The paper also contrasts traditional machine learning methods, which rely on handcrafted feature extraction, with modern deep learning approaches like CNNs. This shift aims to enhance efficiency and effectiveness in feature extraction and classification tasks, marking a significant advancement in the field.
