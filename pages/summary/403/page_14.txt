The paper discusses advancements in deep neural network architectures, specifically focusing on VGG, Network in Network, and GoogLeNet. 

VGG architectures, available in two variants (VGG19 and VGG16), utilize smaller filters which lead to fewer parameters and improved identification of smaller objects. These networks contain 19 and 16 layers respectively, and incorporate 1 × 1 convolutions for depth-wise downsampling, culminating in around 138 million parameters.

The Network in Network model, introduced by Min Lin and collaborators, diverges from traditional feature extraction methods by employing multilayer perceptron convolutions (mlpconv) that slide across images. This approach, which includes three stacked mlpconv layers followed by a Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer, reduces the number of parameters and model complexity. The GAP layer condenses the image dimensions to a (1 × 1 × D) tensor, facilitating dimensionality reduction.

GoogLeNet presents a novel architecture that integrates multiple filters, allowing it to accept images of any resolution, addressing a limitation of prior models. This flexibility is a significant advancement in the field of convolutional neural networks.
