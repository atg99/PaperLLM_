The paper discusses the evolution and effectiveness of single stage object detectors, particularly focusing on the You Only Look Once (YOLO) algorithm and its successors. YOLO revolutionizes object detection by treating the localization problem as a regression task, enabling it to operate in a single stage. This approach stands in contrast to two stage detectors, which, while generally more complex and accurate, require more computational resources. Other notable single stage detectors mentioned include Single Shot Detector (SSD), Deconvolution Single Shot Detector (DSSD), RetinaNet, M2Det, and RefineDet++. YOLO has gained popularity in production settings due to its simple design, low complexity, and fast inference times, making it competitive with both single and two stage detectors.

The paper also traces the historical development of object detection methods, beginning with traditional techniques like the Viola-Jones framework and Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG), which were foundational for early face detection and object classification. The evolution of these techniques has led to the emergence of more advanced methods, including Overfeat, which harnesses spatial convolutional network features. The focus of current research has shifted towards single stage detectors, especially YOLO, due to the increasing demand for efficient models that can operate with limited computational resources. The paper concludes by highlighting the broader impact of deep learning across various domains, including healthcare.
