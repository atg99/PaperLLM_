The paper presents an object detection model tailored for production systems, detailing its structure and methodologies. The authors break down the problem statement and approaches into distinct segments, proposing various methodologies suited to available resources. The process begins with inputting an image of dimensions H×W, where H is height and W is width. 

Feature extraction follows through convolutions using powerful networks like VGG16, Darknet53, and ResNet50, collectively referred to as the backbone. A neck, which can be a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) or Path Aggregation Network (PAN), is utilized to extract features at different scales by connecting bottom-up and top-down pathways. The final stage involves predicting anchor boxes using single-stage detectors like YOLO, which feature dense layers, or two-stage detectors like Faster R-CNN, which provide sparse predictions.

On the architectural front, the authors compare CSPResNeXt50, CSPDarknet53, and EfficientNetB3 for constructing the YOLO (v4) architecture. They choose CSPDarknet53, which consists of 29 convolution layers with 3 × 3 filters and approximately 27.6 million parameters, as the backbone due to its superior performance. The CSPNet architecture facilitates rich gradient combinations while minimizing computational costs.

The authors utilize an ImageNet pretrained model for classification, leveraging the COCO dataset for experimentation. They incorporate Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) to handle varying input sizes without the need for resizing, allowing for effective object detection across diverse image dimensions. Additionally, a genetic algorithm is employed for hyperparameter tuning, and the authors prefer PAN over FPN for its bottom-up path augmentation and adaptive feature pooling capabilities.
