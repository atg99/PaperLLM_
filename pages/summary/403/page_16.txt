The paper discusses the issue of model degradation, where increasing model depth initially improves accuracy but eventually leads to diminishing returns due to the vanishing gradient problem. To address this, the concept of identity mapping is introduced, specifically through the use of skip or residual connections. These connections allow for the output of a convolutional layer to be concatenated with its input before passing it to the next layer, thus preserving original information and mitigating the vanishing gradient issue. The flexibility of the skip connection length is highlighted, as it can be adjusted based on specific application needs. The paper compares the depth of ResNet architectures to earlier models, noting that ResNet152 is significantly deeper than AlexNet and VGG, and provides details on the number of parameters in ResNet152 and ResNet110. In the following section, the paper transitions to discussing the architectural design of YOLOs, including foundational concepts, various architectures, and incremental changes across different versions, as well as the associated loss function.
