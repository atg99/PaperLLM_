The paper "Low-bit Quantization of Neural Networks for Efficient Inference" by Yoni Choukroun and colleagues addresses the challenge of deploying large deep neural networks on resource-constrained hardware, such as smartphones and IoT devices. As deep learning models become increasingly complex, their computational and storage demands grow, making real-time inference difficult. A common solution is neural network quantization, which reduces the precision of computations and model parameters.

However, aggressive quantization often leads to significant accuracy loss and may require full retraining or higher bit precision. The authors propose a novel approach that formalizes linear quantization as a Minimum Mean Squared Error (MMSE) problem for both weights and activations, enabling low-bit precision inference without the need for full network retraining.

Key contributions include optimizing the constrained MSE problem at each network layer, hardware-aware partitioning of network parameters, and employing multiple low precision quantized tensors for layers that are poorly approximated. The method allows for 4 bits integer (INT4) quantization, achieving state-of-the-art results in various network architectures with minimal loss of accuracy.

The paper highlights the benefits of low-bit precision, such as reduced storage requirements, lower power consumption, and enhanced computational efficiency. Overall, the proposed quantization technique represents a significant advancement in deploying pretrained neural networks in low-resource environments.
