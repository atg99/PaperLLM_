This paper introduces a low-bit precision linear quantization framework designed to optimize neural network (NN) parameters in a mean square error (MMSE) sense, allowing for efficient deployment on standard deep learning hardware without mixed precision operations.

The proposed method quantizes NN parameters in a kernel-wise manner, which preserves the linearity of dot product operations. The authors identify layers that are sensitive to quantization errors and employ an adaptive quantization scheme utilizing multiple low precision tensors. Additionally, a refinement procedure for scaling factors of quantized tensors is introduced, based on a small unlabeled calibration set. The quantization coefficients for NN activations are also derived offline using the MMSE criterion, ensuring more sensitive activations are accurately approximated.

Key contributions of the paper include:

1. A low-bit precision linear quantization framework that facilitates the rapid deployment of pretrained NNs on hardware limited to low precision operations.
2. Minimal task accuracy loss achieved through fine-grained partitioning of NN weights, alongside an optimal solution to the MMSE quantization problem that involves multiple tensors for key layers and fast differentiable optimization.
3. Experimental results on ImageNet demonstrate that the INT4 linear quantization method for both weights and activations results in only a 3% top-1 and 1.7% top-5 mean accuracy degradation compared to FP32 models, achieving state-of-the-art results.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews related works, while Section 3 discusses quantization challenges and the development of the proposed MMSE-based quantization approach, including experimental validations. Section 4 presents quantization results from various popular neural networks. Overall, this framework provides a promising solution for improving the efficiency of NNs without significant accuracy loss.
