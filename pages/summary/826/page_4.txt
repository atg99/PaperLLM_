The analysis indicates that the first layers of deep networks significantly influence output quality due to their recursive nature, emphasizing the importance of minimizing weight noise associated with unbounded activations, particularly in ReLU functions. Empirical studies confirm that the first layers consistently affect model accuracy the most, and the quality of weight approximation is more crucial for performance than activation quality, contrasting previous analyses.

In low precision settings, such as 4-bit quantization, global quantization can lead to poor performance due to high variance in kernel elements. However, INT8 settings are more robust under global quantization. The kernel-wise quantization approach, where each kernel has its own scaling factor, preserves the linearity of the dot product, enabling efficient tensor multiplication in dedicated hardware. This approach demonstrates significant performance improvements in various architectures, as shown in the provided results.

The paper also addresses minimum mean square error (MSE) quantization, proposing a constrained optimization problem to find the optimal tensor approximation. The optimization lacks an analytical solution, but uniform quantization is derived under certain conditions. Several methods for optimal scaling factor determination are discussed, with the Golden Section Search method yielding effective results. The proposed one-dimensional exact line-search approach allows for efficient computation, providing optimal solutions to the MSE quantization problem.

Finally, the paper highlights the potential of using mixed precision quantization for layers with varying approximation difficulty to minimize accuracy loss effectively.
