The paper presents a novel approach to quantizing neural network layers, particularly focusing on convolutional kernels with high mean square error (MSE). The authors propose a method that uses multiple low precision quantized tensors to approximate key layers more effectively, contrasting with traditional methods that apply uniform quantization across all layers.

The research includes a comparative analysis of various architectures such as AlexNet, VGG16bn, Inception v3, ResNet variants, SqueezeNet, and DenseNet, highlighting their top-1 accuracy for different quantization strategies: uniform quantization, alternating optimization, golden section search, and the proposed optimal MSE (OMSE) quantization. Results indicate varying performances across architectures, with the OMSE method generally yielding competitive accuracy.

The proposed optimization framework formulates the quantization process as an iterative nested optimization, where the goal is to minimize the L2 norm of the difference between the original tensor and the quantized tensors. The authors emphasize the efficiency of alternating optimization for convergence while ensuring minimal computational overhead.

An algorithm is introduced for the alternating optimization process, detailing the steps for quantizing high MSE layers. Special attention is given to cases with two quantized tensors, which can be optimized efficiently through dual line search.

Overall, the paper argues for a refined quantization strategy that enhances the representation of critical layers in neural networks, thereby improving inference performance on low-power devices.
