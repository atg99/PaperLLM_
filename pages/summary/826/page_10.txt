The referenced works cover significant advancements in neural network architectures and training methodologies within the field of deep learning. 

Simonyan and Zisserman (2014) introduced very deep convolutional networks, demonstrating their effectiveness for large-scale image recognition tasks. Soudry et al. (2014) proposed expectation backpropagation, a parameter-free training method for multilayer neural networks, which allows for the use of continuous or discrete weights. Sutskever et al. (2014) advanced sequence-to-sequence learning using neural networks, which has implications for natural language processing. Szegedy et al. (2016) revisited the inception architecture, enhancing its performance in computer vision tasks. Lastly, Zhou et al. (2016) developed Dorefa-Net, which focuses on training low bitwidth convolutional neural networks with low bitwidth gradients, addressing efficiency in model training and deployment.

These studies collectively contribute to the understanding and evolution of neural network design and training, emphasizing improvements in performance, efficiency, and applicability across various domains.
