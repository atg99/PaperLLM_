The paper discusses the core functionalities and supporting technologies in mobile healthcare robot systems, particularly focusing on the handling of sensor uncertainty. It distinguishes between knowledge-based and data-driven methods for activity recognition. Knowledge-based methods utilize prior knowledge to build semantic activity models, enabling better interoperability and adaptability across various applications. These methods employ formal data structures for sensor data and context representation, making them understandable for both developers and robots. Ontology-based activity recognition is highlighted as a prominent knowledge-based approach, noted for its expressiveness and reasoning capabilities. However, the effectiveness of these methods can be hindered by imperfect observations, impacting activity recognition performance.

In contrast, data-driven methods utilize supervised machine learning algorithms, such as Hidden Markov Models and Support Vector Machines, to classify sensor data into distinct human activity categories. While data-driven approaches have achieved success in various applications, the paper emphasizes the complementary nature of both methods in addressing the challenges posed by sensor uncertainty in mobile healthcare robots.
