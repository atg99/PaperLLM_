The paper by S. Wan, Z. Gu, and Q. Ni discusses the role of mobile healthcare robots in providing mental healthcare, particularly in overcoming barriers such as stigma and cost that hinder access to mental health services. The authors highlight the potential of edge computing to enhance the functionalities of these robots, allowing for efficient processing and interaction without reliance on centralized data centers.

Key functionalities of healthcare robots that are conducive to edge computing include enhanced interactivity and perception. The paper outlines how interactivity has evolved from traditional, fixed responses to more dynamic interactions facilitated by advanced AI techniques. Examples include virtual assistants like Google Siri, which utilize both online and offline algorithms to improve user engagement.

In terms of perception, the authors emphasize the importance of capturing user data through various methods such as mobile phone applications, facial recognition technology, and special sensors. Each method has its advantages and limitations, with facial recognition enabling healthcare robots to monitor medication adherence and health crises, while special sensors can track user behavior, albeit with comfort and financial concerns.

The paper also discusses the challenges of recognizing human activities through images, suggesting that advancements in deep learning, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), can significantly improve performance in this area. These technologies enable robots to make informed decisions and perform tasks more effectively, such as navigating surgical procedures and adapting to different environments.

Overall, the authors argue that integrating edge computing with advanced AI and deep learning techniques can enhance the capabilities of mobile healthcare robots, ultimately improving mental healthcare delivery and patient outcomes.
