The paper investigates the performance of the MUSE-VAE model in predicting future trajectories within various datasets, specifically PFSD, SDD, and nuScenes. The study evaluates how different sampling numbers (K=20, 50 for PFSD; K=5, 20 for SDD; K=5, 10 for nuScenes) affect the model's accuracy and distribution representation.

In PFSD, MUSE-VAE demonstrates superior performance across all metrics except for FDE at K=20, where it ranks second. The analysis reveals that competing models, Y-net and AF, struggle to accurately reflect the true trajectory distribution due to their sampling methods, which focus on diversity at the expense of accuracy. MUSE-VAE, however, maintains a low error range and effectively mirrors the ground truth trajectory distribution, leading to fewer collisions in its predictions.

For the SDD dataset, MUSE-VAE again outperforms state-of-the-art methods in average displacement error (ADE) and ties closely with the best method for final displacement error (FDE). It achieves the best KDE NLL score, although its environmental collision-free likelihood (ECFL) is slightly lower than Y-net due to incomplete scene labeling impacting its semantic mapping.

In the nuScenes dataset, MUSE-VAE consistently surpasses all other models across all metrics and sampling numbers. The model's ability to adapt to the strict navigable space of nuScenes and reflect real-world data characteristics contributes to its robust performance.

Statistical analysis indicates that MUSE-VAE ranks highest among the compared methods, with significant performance advantages confirmed through the Friedman test and Bayesian signed rank test. The paper concludes with qualitative results that illustrate the contextual effectiveness of MUSE-VAE's trajectory predictions across different environments, showcasing its strengths and trade-offs visually.
