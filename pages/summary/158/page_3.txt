The paper presents a method for trajectory prediction, focusing on a multi-scale environment-aware model called MUSE-VAE. It consists of two main stages: the Macro-stage and the Micro-stage. 

In the Macro-stage, the model generates coarse predictions of future trajectories based on a local semantic map and past trajectory data. It emphasizes the importance of understanding the surrounding environment to reduce uncertainty in predicting an individual's future heading. The model utilizes a Gaussian heatmap representation of trajectories, with a focus on local maps centered around the last observed position of the agent. This approach allows for the prediction of both long-term and short-term goals, producing heatmaps that correspond to these goals.

The Micro-stage refines the predictions made in the Macro-stage by employing recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to learn sequential features more effectively, thus addressing long-range dependencies and guiding the path to avoid obstacles.

The key contribution of the paper is the integration of a stage-wise training procedure that leverages both coarse and fine predictions to enhance trajectory forecasting. The model captures inherent uncertainties in predictions, enabling the generation of multiple plausible future trajectories while maintaining alignment with the environmental context.
