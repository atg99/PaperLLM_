The paper introduces SG-net, designed to generate waypoints that align with environmental features from the last observed step to a long-term goal. In its final stage, termed the Micro-stage, the model processes trajectories and semantic maps as one-dimensional feature vectors. This approach mitigates the risk of misalignment caused by poor spatial signal predictions when relying solely on long-term goal information.

SG-net employs U-net to produce NSG+1 heatmaps, where NSG represents the number of short-term goals and the additional one accounts for the long-term goal. The model outputs deterministic predictions based on the predicted long-term goal, addressing uncertainties in fine trajectory predictions in subsequent stages. The loss function for SG-net combines reconstruction loss and focal loss.

In the Micro-stage, the model predicts complete future trajectories, transitioning from discrete pixel coordinates to continuous world coordinates. Despite the guidance from SG-net, individual steps may vary due to the environment's influence. To manage this variability, the model utilizes Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CV AE), learning to approximate the posterior latent distribution for future trajectory predictions. The training process incorporates Teacher Forcing to refine predictions based on ground truth data.

The paper evaluates the model using three datasets: Stanford Drone Dataset, nuScenes Dataset, and a newly created Path Finding Simulation Dataset (PFSD) that presents more complex navigation challenges. Evaluation metrics include Average Displacement Error, Final Displacement Error, Kernel Density Estimate-based Negative Log Likelihood, and Environment Collision-Free Likelihood, which measures the likelihood of a collision-free path relative to environmental structures.

Statistical significance testing is applied to compare MUSE-VAE with state-of-the-art models, such as Trajectron++, Y-net, and AgentFormer, across the datasets. Experimental results indicate the model's performance across the various metrics, particularly highlighting its ability to navigate complex environments effectively. The findings underscore MUSE-VAE's potential in trajectory prediction while addressing the inherent uncertainties in dynamic environments.
