This paper presents MUSE-VAE, a probabilistic model designed to generate multimodal predictions based on environmental context through a coarse-to-fine approach. The study investigates the model’s ability to understand long-term goals in varying environments, particularly in scenarios characterized as 'fork-in-the-road.' The results demonstrate that MUSE-VAE effectively predicts long-term goals and aligns short-term goals with these predictions, showcasing a bimodal uncertainty in goal directions due to environmental structure.

The paper includes an ablation study that evaluates the contributions of different components within MUSE-VAE. The findings reveal that omitting the Micro-stage or SG-net significantly impacts performance, particularly in trajectory accuracy and distribution learning. The model's ability to process semantic maps in 2D, as opposed to a 1D representation used by other models, is highlighted as a key factor in avoiding collisions and maintaining prediction accuracy.

The authors conclude that while MUSE-VAE operates effectively for single agents, future work will explore multi-agent interactions to enhance collision avoidance in dynamic environments. The research is supported by various NSF awards.
