The MUSE-VAE architecture consists of two primary stages: the Macro-stage, which includes the Long-term Goal Conditional Variational Autoencoder (LG-CVAE) and the Short-term Goal network (SG-net), and the Micro-stage, which employs a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) based Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CV AE). 

The LG-CVAE focuses on predicting long-term goals by utilizing a combination of a U-net and CV AE to model uncertainties in semantic map and trajectory heatmap representations. It aims to maximize the conditional distribution of the long-term goal heatmap given past trajectory heatmaps and local semantic maps. The loss function for LG-CVAE is designed as a negative evidence lower bound, which incorporates both the posterior and prior distributions of the latent variables. The architecture is enhanced by using focal loss to address class imbalance in the trajectory heatmap outputs.

In the SG-net, short-term goals are predicted based on the long-term goals derived from LG-CVAE. This stage utilizes the input of concatenated local semantic maps, past trajectory heatmaps, and long-term goal heatmaps to output multiple heatmaps representing short-term goals. Implementation specifics, including pretraining strategies and techniques to prevent posterior collapse, are detailed in the supplementary materials. Overall, this architecture emphasizes the importance of accurately predicting both long-term and short-term goals for effective trajectory forecasting.
