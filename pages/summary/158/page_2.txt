The paper introduces MUSE-VAE, a multi-scale, environment-aware model designed for long-term trajectory prediction. It addresses limitations in existing models that often produce implausible trajectories and struggle with diverse forecast goals due to the uncertain, multi-modal nature of agent movement. MUSE-VAE employs a coarse-to-fine approach, predicting higher-level goals before detailing goal-conditioned trajectories, ensuring collision avoidance with obstacles while retaining spatial information.

The model operates through a three-step learning strategy involving Macro-stage and Micro-stage processes. In Macro-stage, it predicts long-term goals and sequential short-term goals. The Micro-stage subsequently generates complete trajectories. Key contributions of MUSE-VAE include: introducing a novel multi-scale learning strategy for collision-free trajectory predictions, demonstrating the ability to generalize trajectory distributions to new scenes, and enabling diverse and accurate predictions through a structured goal prediction approach.

The paper also reviews relevant literature on trajectory modeling, emphasizing sequence learning and environment learning. It highlights the limitations of traditional recurrent networks and discusses advancements such as Transformer Networks and convolutional approaches that integrate environmental context. The paper concludes that MUSE-VAE outperforms state-of-the-art methods in producing trajectory predictions that adhere to ground truth while minimizing collisions, as demonstrated through various experiments.
