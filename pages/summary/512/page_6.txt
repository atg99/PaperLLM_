The paper examines the performance of supervised and semi-supervised learning models with varying sizes and label fractions, particularly focusing on ResNet architectures. Results indicate that larger models demonstrate greater label efficiency, especially in semi-supervised contexts. For instance, increasing the model size from ResNet-50 to ResNet-152 enhances label efficiency by 10%. The study emphasizes that while larger models are generally better, specific architectures may offer improved parameter efficiency.

Additionally, the research investigates the impact of projection head depth on representation learning during fine-tuning. Findings reveal that a deeper projection head, particularly when fine-tuning from the optimal layer, yields better performance. The optimal layer for fine-tuning is often the first layer of the projection head, especially when labeled examples are limited. However, the benefits of a deeper projection head diminish with larger ResNets, suggesting a relationship between model width and the effectiveness of projection head depth. Overall, the study highlights the importance of model size, architecture, and projection head configuration in enhancing learning efficiency.
