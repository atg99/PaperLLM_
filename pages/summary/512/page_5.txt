The paper presents an analysis of fine-tuning SimCLRv2 models and training linear classifiers on their representations, focusing on the impact of model architecture on label efficiency. It reports Top-1 accuracy across various label fractions (1%, 10%, and 100%) and compares self-supervised learning with supervised learning using ResNet models of different depths and widths, including the use of selective kernels (SK).

Key findings indicate that larger models (greater depth and width) enhance performance significantly in self-supervised contexts, with accuracy improvements of up to 17% for fine-tuning on 1% labeled data. In contrast, the performance gains for supervised learning are more modest, with only up to 4% differences observed between the smallest and largest models. The results suggest that while increasing model size leads to greater label efficiency in self-supervised learning, the benefits may plateau at certain sizes, as seen with the marginal improvement of ResNet-152 over a slightly smaller variant.

The methodology includes fine-tuning strategies, learning rate adjustments, and the use of various data augmentations. The paper highlights the importance of model architecture in achieving better performance, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data.
