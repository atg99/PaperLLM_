The paper discusses the effectiveness of large self-supervised models as semi-supervised learners, particularly in the context of computer vision tasks using ImageNet. The authors propose a three-step semi-supervised learning algorithm: first, unsupervised pretraining of a large ResNet model using SimCLRv2; second, supervised fine-tuning with a limited number of labeled examples; and third, distillation of the model using unlabeled examples to refine and transfer task-specific knowledge.

The key finding is that larger networks significantly enhance performance when trained with fewer labeled examples. The results demonstrate that with only 1% of the labels, the proposed method achieves 73.9% top-1 accuracy using ResNet-50, marking a substantial improvement in label efficiency compared to previous state-of-the-art methods. With 10% of the labels, this method surpasses the performance of fully supervised training with all labels, reaching 77.5% top-1 accuracy.

Overall, the study emphasizes the advantages of leveraging large models and a task-agnostic approach to utilize unlabeled data effectively in semi-supervised learning scenarios.
