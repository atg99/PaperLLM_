This paper provides a comprehensive review of the evolution of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) models, from YOLOv1 to the latest YOLOv11, highlighting significant advancements in detection speed, accuracy, and computational efficiency. The discussion emphasizes the potential of deploying YOLO on edge devices, which could lead to improvements in efficiency and accuracy in constrained environments through techniques like model pruning and quantization. The integration of YOLO with advanced communication protocols and edge computing frameworks is proposed to enhance collaboration between edge devices and cloud services.

The paper also explores the role of YOLO in Embodied Artificial Intelligence (EAI), where it enhances sensory capabilities, allowing for more natural interactions in applications like autonomous vehicles and robotic surgical assistants. Furthermore, it addresses the environmental impact of training YOLO models, citing concerns over energy consumption and carbon emissions, thus highlighting the need for more sustainable AI practices.

In conclusion, the review not only chronicles the advancements in YOLO models but also identifies gaps in current capabilities and suggests future research directions, such as improving the balance between detection speed and accuracy and facilitating multimodal data processing through the integration of large language models. The authors express optimism about YOLO's future applications and its potential to revolutionize AI interactions through Generative AI and multimodal approaches. The paper acknowledges support from various institutions and outlines the contributions of each author in the research and manuscript preparation.
