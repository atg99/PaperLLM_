Real-time object detection is essential for applications in various fields such as agriculture, transportation, education, and healthcare. It plays a vital role in the success of autonomous vehicles and robotic systems, enabling them to identify and track objects like vehicles, pedestrians, and obstacles, which enhances navigational safety. The technology is also crucial for action recognition in video sequences, benefiting areas like digital surveillance, sports analysis, and human-machine interactions.

However, object detection faces several challenges, including the complexity of real-world environments, where objects can vary in orientation, scale, and lighting. Illumination factors significantly impact detection performance, as variations in lighting can obscure object features. Additionally, occlusions and clutter in scenes can hinder accurate analysis, while the need for speed and efficiency requires algorithms to balance accuracy with low latency for timely decision-making.

Initially, object detection relied on hand-crafted features and classical machine learning methods. Traditional approaches included correlation filters, the sliding window method, and the Viola-Jones detector, which utilized Haar-like features. Various hand-crafted feature extraction techniques, such as Gabor features, Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG), Local Binary Patterns (LBP), Haar-like features, Deformable Part Models (DPM), Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT), and Speeded-Up Robust Features (SURF), supported these methods.

As the field evolved, a shift towards automated feature learning and end-to-end deep learning methods emerged, addressing the limitations of traditional approaches and enhancing the robustness of object detection in real-time applications.
