The paper discusses the evolution and applications of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) object detection models, highlighting their significance across various fields such as defect detection, structural health monitoring, security, public health, agriculture, and remote sensing. YOLO's integration into surveillance systems enhances real-time monitoring and threat identification, contributing to public safety. The models have also played a vital role in enforcing health regulations during pandemics by detecting compliance with measures like social distancing and face mask wearing. 

In agriculture, YOLO assists in crop and pest detection, enabling precision farming and increased productivity. In remote sensing, it aids in object recognition from satellite and aerial imagery, supporting urban planning and environmental monitoring, thus addressing global challenges.

The study aims to critically summarize YOLO's progress over the past decade, beginning with YOLOv1, which introduced a single-stage network architecture for object detection. Subsequent versions, including YOLOv2, YOLOv3, YOLOv4, and YOLOv5, introduced improvements in resolution, multi-scale predictions, and variants tailored for different performance needs. YOLOv6 and later versions continue this trend, focusing on scalability, computational efficiency, and real-time performance.

The paper is organized into sections tracing the trajectory of YOLO models, reviewing their unique features, applications, challenges, and future directions. Each section is further divided into sub-sections to detail specific relevant topics. The comprehensive analysis aims to reflect the significant influence of YOLO in the computer vision community, as evidenced by its extensive citation and search results.
