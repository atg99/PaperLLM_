YOLOv11, the latest iteration, achieves a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 54.4% and a latency of 10.70 ms, showcasing improvements in both accuracy and inference speed. The model features an advanced backbone and neck architecture for precise feature extraction and includes optimized designs for enhanced efficiency and performance. YOLOv11 offers a balance between precision and computational efficiency, making it versatile for applications ranging from embedded systems to large-scale implementations.

The metrics of inference time and mAP are crucial for evaluating the performance of YOLO models. Inference time measures how quickly the model processes images, while mAP assesses accuracy by averaging precision across classes. Both metrics are influenced by the hardware used for testing, highlighting the need for standardized benchmarks. Additionally, the review emphasizes the importance of True Positives, True Negatives, False Positives, and False Negatives in assessing model reliability.

The paper also discusses the evolution of single-stage detection models, particularly the influence of the Single Shot MultiBox Detector (SSD) on YOLO. This shift to a single-stage approach enables faster and more efficient object detection, essential for real-time applications. YOLO models have adapted SSD's architecture to improve detection capabilities through enhanced feature extraction. 

The review identifies a gap in the existing literature regarding the latest YOLO versions, especially YOLOv9, YOLOv10, and YOLOv11, and aims to systematically document and analyze these models across various applications. This comprehensive evaluation seeks to contribute to the ongoing development and understanding of the YOLO algorithm in the context of modern object detection challenges.
