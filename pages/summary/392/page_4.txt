The paper discusses the evolution of object detection paradigms, highlighting a timeline that spans from traditional methods to advanced deep learning approaches. It begins with early techniques such as the Viola-Jones (VJ) Detector and HOG, progressing through various R-CNN models including Fast R-CNN and Faster R-CNN, and culminating in the YOLO series, which has been pivotal in real-time object detection.

The timeline illustrates the transition from two-stage detection methods to one-stage models like YOLO, emphasizing advancements in both CNN and transformer-based architectures, including DETR and ViTDet. These developments have significantly enhanced performance in object detection tasks.

The paper also explores the practical applications of YOLO in various fields. In aviation, it aids in hazard analysis to improve safety and operational efficiency. In healthcare, YOLO supports diagnostic processes, with applications in cancer detection, skin segmentation, and pill identification, showcasing its adaptability to diverse needs. In industrial contexts, YOLO is utilized for surface inspection, demonstrating its versatility across multiple domains.
