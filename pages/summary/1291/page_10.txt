This paper offers a theoretical examination of the overfitting issue in linear autoencoders when trained on data, particularly in relation to the identity function, and discusses strategies for mitigation. The insights gained aim to enhance the performance of autoencoders across various practical applications, potentially yielding positive societal impacts. The authors highlight the relevance of autoencoders in collaborative filtering and recommender systems, acknowledging the associated ethical concerns such as filter bubbles and fairness. These issues may be alleviated since autoencoders are often part of more extensive systems designed to address negative ethical consequences.

The author expresses gratitude to colleagues and reviewers for their constructive feedback and acknowledges the support received in developing the experimental code. Financially, the research was conducted under the author's employment at Netflix without additional funding. 

The references provided include significant works in neural networks, machine learning, and collaborative filtering, underscoring the foundational research that informs this study.
