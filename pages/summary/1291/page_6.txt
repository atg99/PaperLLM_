The section on low-rank EDLAE discusses the limitations and capabilities of low-rank models in learning identity mappings. Despite their inability to perfectly learn the identity, these models can overfit, particularly when the model rank is large (e.g., rank 100). The authors focus on the low-rank factorization of the model represented as B(EDLAE) = UV^T, where U and V are rank-k matrices. They highlight that in this low-rank setup, the diagonal elements of the factorization cannot generally be assumed to be zero, unlike in full-rank models.

The optimization problem is presented, emphasizing that with an appropriately large rank, the low-rank model can closely approximate the full-rank solution, allowing the diagonal to be effectively zero. The authors propose a modified optimization problem that enforces this condition and can be efficiently solved using Alternating Least Squares. A key insight is that when training with full emphasis, the latent embeddings in the encoder and decoder become approximately orthogonal for each feature, which contradicts the intuitive notion that similar features should have similar embeddings.

This (near-)orthogonality constraint leads to reduced similarity among embeddings of different features, which is supported by experimental results showing lower cosine similarities for similar features when using this constraint compared to without it. The section concludes by referencing related work that employs regularization techniques with effects similar to the (near-)orthogonality constraint discussed in EDLAE.
