In the paper "Autoencoders that don’t overfit towards the Identity," Harald Steck discusses the tendency of autoencoders (AEs) to overfit by learning the identity function, where the output is a direct reproduction of the input. This behavior is particularly problematic in prediction tasks involving noisy data, as it fails to capture the necessary interactions among features. The author explores linear autoencoders to derive analytical solutions and demonstrates that while denoising techniques can mitigate this overfitting, they do so only as much as they are penalized by L2-norm regularization.

The main contribution of the paper is the assertion that a specific denoising autoencoder can entirely eliminate the overfitting to the identity function. The author presents new theoretical insights, including a closed-form solution for the full-rank model and a near-orthogonality constraint in the low-rank model. Although this new constraint differs conceptually from recent regularizers, the effects on learned embeddings are empirically similar. Experimental results on three well-known datasets support the theoretical findings, reinforcing the importance of designing AEs that prioritize feature interdependencies over simple identity mappings for improved prediction accuracy in noisy contexts.
