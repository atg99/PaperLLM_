The paper discusses the implications of different settings in a model training context, particularly focusing on the role of the diagonal in the training process of EDLAE (Enhanced Deep Linear Autoencoder). It establishes that when no elements are subtracted from the diagonal (b=0), the model simplifies to DLAE, which is expected. In contrast, with full emphasis (b=0), the diagonal is completely removed during training, which prevents overfitting to the diagonal values. This setup compels the model to rely on other features for reconstruction, thus enhancing generalization.

The paper highlights the distinct roles of the diagonal during training versus testing: it is partially removed during training but included in the learned model for prediction. The analysis continues by deriving insights for both full-rank and low-rank models under full emphasis. For full-rank EDLAE, the diagonal values become undetermined, leading to an optimization problem with a constraint that enforces a zero diagonal, which is solvable via Lagrangian multipliers.

The closed-form solution indicates that the learned off-diagonal entries are optimized such that each feature is predicted by all other features, thereby mitigating overfitting. A comparison between DLAE and EDLAE reveals that despite differences in regularization approaches, the solutions exhibit structural similarities, particularly in how they handle the diagonal elements. The findings underscore the importance of diagonal manipulation in improving model robustness during training.
