In this section, the paper explores the tradeoff between accuracy and search cost for various retraining techniques, particularly focusing on rewinding methods versus fine-tuning at different compression ratios. The findings indicate that both rewinding techniques consistently achieve higher accuracy compared to fine-tuning across a range of retraining times, suggesting that they can effectively replace fine-tuning in many scenarios.

The methodology includes analysis through figures that depict the accuracy of retraining techniques against varying retraining times, categorized by specific compression ratios. The study defines two key metrics: "No Accuracy Drop," which identifies the highest compression ratio where any retraining method retains original accuracy, and "1%/1 BLEU Accuracy Drop," denoting the highest compression ratio where accuracy remains within 1% or 1 BLEU of the original model.

Results from unstructured pruning reveal that rewinding techniques generally match or exceed the performance of fine-tuning, except when weights are rewound too close to initialization, which negatively impacts accuracy. The paper introduces a "rewinding safe zone," a range of retraining time (25% to 90% of the original training time) where rewinding methods outperform fine-tuning.

For structured pruning, the trends mirror those of unstructured pruning, but it is noted that weight rewinding does not lead to accuracy drops when retraining for the full original training time. This aligns with prior work suggesting that initialization is less critical for structured pruning retraining.

The conclusion emphasizes that both weight and learning rate rewinding outperform fine-tuning in terms of accuracy within the rewinding safe zone. To maximize accuracy, retraining with learning rate rewinding for the full original training duration is recommended, although improvements tend to plateau after approximately half of the training time.
