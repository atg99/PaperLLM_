The paper evaluates pruned neural networks based on three key metrics: accuracy, efficiency, and search cost. Accuracy is assessed by the network's performance on unseen data, aiming to match the accuracy of the unpruned network. Efficiency focuses on parameter efficiency, defined by the compression ratio of the pruned network relative to the full network, with higher ratios indicating better efficiency. Search cost is measured by the retraining time, where fewer epochs suggest lower costs.

The analysis explores the tradeoff between accuracy and parameter efficiency, highlighting that weight rewinding generally achieves higher accuracy than fine-tuning across various compression ratios. Learning rate rewinding consistently matches or exceeds the performance of weight rewinding. The methodology involves selecting optimal retraining settings for each technique and plotting the corresponding test accuracies.

Results from one-shot pruning indicate minimal differentiation at low compression ratios, with learning rate rewinding often surpassing the unpruned network's accuracy. At higher ratios, learning rate rewinding outperforms other techniques, particularly fine-tuning, which shows greater accuracy loss. For iterative pruning, learning rate rewinding again leads in performance, matching state-of-the-art accuracy and efficiency tradeoffs. Notably, it produces a ResNet-50 that achieves a compression ratio of 5.96 without accuracy loss, establishing a new benchmark in this context. Weight rewinding closely follows, particularly in scenarios with lower compression ratios.
