The paper discusses techniques for pruning neural networks to achieve a balance between accuracy and efficiency. It explores both unstructured and structured pruning methods, focusing on magnitude-based heuristics. Unstructured pruning involves removing the lowest magnitude weights globally, while structured pruning removes convolutional filters based on their L1 norms, using specific pruning rates previously established.

The authors evaluate retraining methods post-pruning, including fine-tuning, weight rewinding, and a novel approach called learning rate rewinding. Fine-tuning adjusts the unpruned weights for a set number of epochs at a fixed learning rate. Weight rewinding reverts the unpruned weights to values from earlier epochs before retraining, while learning rate rewinding combines aspects of both methods by using the final weights but applying the learning rate schedule from previous epochs.

The paper also compares one-shot pruning, where the network is pruned to a target sparsity level in a single step, with iterative pruning, which involves multiple cycles of pruning and retraining to reach the desired sparsity. The authors find that iterative pruning often allows for greater weight removal while maintaining accuracy, a crucial insight for optimizing neural network performance.
