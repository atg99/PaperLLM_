This paper, presented at ICLR 2020, investigates various retraining techniques following the pruning of neural networks, focusing on weight rewinding and a new approach called learning rate rewinding. The authors seek to validate the existence of trainable subnetworks early in training by comparing the accuracy of pruned networks retrained using these methods against traditional fine-tuning.

The paper outlines three primary evaluation criteria: accuracy, efficiency (specifically parameter efficiency), and search cost (the retraining effort required). The authors emphasize the goal of neural network pruning: to enhance efficiency while preserving accuracy.

Key contributions include:

1. Demonstrating that weight rewinding outperforms fine-tuning across various networks and datasets, achieving higher accuracy for equivalent search costs.
2. Introducing learning rate rewinding, which adjusts the learning rate schedule without rewinding weights, matching or exceeding the performance of weight rewinding in all tested scenarios.
3. Proposing a simplified pruning algorithm based on learning rate rewinding, characterized by network-agnostic hyperparameters. This algorithm involves training, globally pruning the lowest 20% of weights, and retraining with learning rate rewinding, iteratively refining until the desired sparsity is achieved.
4. Showing that weight rewinding can nearly match the accuracy of the proposed pruning algorithm, establishing that lottery tickets identified through pruning and rewinding are highly effective.

Overall, the findings suggest that learning rate rewinding is a superior alternative to fine-tuning, offering a straightforward and efficient approach to pruning that could serve as a valuable baseline for future research and practical applications. The methodology focuses on retraining techniques without delving into regularization or gradual pruning, except for comparisons with state-of-the-art methods.
