This paper presents a thorough analysis of iterative pruning techniques applied to various ResNet architectures, specifically ResNet-56, ResNet-110, and ResNet-50. The study evaluates the speedup achieved in terms of floating point operations (FLOPs) over the original networks and fine-tuning methods. Results indicate significant speedup across different compression ratios, with values reaching up to 9.31 times for the ResNet models.

The authors emphasize the importance of considering both compression ratio and FLOPs to assess the efficiency of neural networks. They note that while a higher compression ratio is generally desirable, it does not necessarily correlate with a reduction in FLOPs, as the impact of pruning varies depending on the layer from which weights are removed. For example, removing weights from the initial layers of a ResNet results in a more substantial decrease in FLOPs compared to pruning in the final layers.

The methodology employed involves a comparative analysis of different retraining techniques, including learning rate rewinding, weight rewinding, and fine-tuning. The paper highlights that iterative pruning leads to different FLOPs due to the divergence of networks post-pruning, particularly when global pruning strategies are used. The findings underscore the nuanced relationship between pruning strategies and their effects on network efficiency, indicating that careful consideration of both pruning location and technique is crucial for optimizing neural network performance.
