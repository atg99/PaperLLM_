The paper presents a comparative analysis of two structured pruning approaches, Structured-A and Structured-B, applied to the ResNet-34 architecture. The experiments evaluate the impact of retraining epochs on model accuracy across varying levels of pruning. The results indicate that both Structured-A and Structured-B exhibit variations in accuracy changes, ranging from -4% to 0% across different retraining epochs and pruning ratios.

The evaluation highlights that as the pruning ratio increases, the accuracy tends to decline, though the extent of this decline varies between the two structured approaches. The paper emphasizes the importance of retraining strategies, such as learning rate rewinding and weight rewinding, in mitigating accuracy loss during the pruning process. Overall, the findings suggest that careful consideration of pruning methods and retraining techniques is essential for maintaining model performance post-pruning.
