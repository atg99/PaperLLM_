The paper "Comparing Rewinding and Fine-Tuning in Neural Network Pruning" by Alex Renda, Jonathan Frankle, and Michael Carbin investigates the effectiveness of different retraining techniques following neural network pruning. The traditional approach, fine-tuning, involves retraining the unpruned weights from their final values using a small fixed learning rate. The authors compare this method with two alternative techniques: weight rewinding, which resets unpruned weights to earlier values during training and retrains them using the original schedule, and learning rate rewinding, which applies the same learning rate schedule as weight rewinding but starts from the final values of the weights.

The study highlights that both rewinding techniques significantly outperform fine-tuning, enabling the development of a network-agnostic pruning algorithm that achieves accuracy and compression ratios comparable to several state-of-the-art methods tailored to specific networks. The authors categorize pruning techniques into two types: those that prune during the training process and those that do so afterward, followed by retraining to recover accuracy. The paper underscores that iterative pruning and retraining can lead to better outcomes than a single pruning iteration.

In conclusion, the findings suggest that incorporating rewinding techniques in neural network pruning can enhance performance, offering a promising direction for future research in model compression.
