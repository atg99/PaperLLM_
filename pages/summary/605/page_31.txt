The paper discusses the results of applying different pruning techniques to neural networks, specifically focusing on the efficiency of these methods in terms of floating-point operations per second (FLOPs) and inference efficiency. It highlights that the networks selected for analysis are those with the highest validation accuracy for various compression ratios, emphasizing that the reported FLOPs correspond to these optimal networks rather than the minimum required for each compression ratio.

Figures 11 and 12 illustrate the theoretical speedup achieved through pruning methods compared to the original network and fine-tuning, respectively. The findings reveal that the rewinding techniques yield networks that demand fewer FLOPs than those produced by standard fine-tuning through iterative pruning. This improvement in accuracy from rewinding leads to a significant enhancement in inference efficiency. For example, a ResNet-50 pruned to retain the same accuracy as the original network shows a theoretical speedup of 4.8 times with the rewinding technique, while a similarly accurate network achieved via fine-tuning only realizes a speedup of 1.7 times.
