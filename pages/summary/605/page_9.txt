The paper presents a novel pruning algorithm aimed at optimizing the tradeoff between accuracy and parameter efficiency in neural networks. The authors propose an iterative process where 20% of the lowest-magnitude weights are pruned globally after training, followed by retraining using a method called learning rate rewinding. This process is repeated until the desired compression ratio is achieved. 

Figure 5 illustrates the performance of the proposed algorithm against state-of-the-art baselines, showcasing its ability to maintain competitive accuracy without the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning typically required by other methods. The authors emphasize that their approach achieves similar results to more complex algorithms that utilize reinforcement learning or additional hyperparameters.

The discussion section highlights two key retraining techniques: weight rewinding and learning rate rewinding. Weight rewinding reverts weights to their values from early training, demonstrating that extensive retraining is unnecessary after pruning. In contrast, learning rate rewinding is shown to provide superior accuracy and efficiency, although the paper does not delve into the reasons behind its effectiveness. Overall, the proposed pruning algorithm stands out for its simplicity and effectiveness in achieving high accuracy while significantly reducing model size.
