This paper, presented at ICLR 2020, explores neural network pruning through a systematic approach involving three key steps: Train, Prune, and Retrain. The authors detail their experimental setup, including the choice of networks, datasets, and hyperparameters.

In the training phase, the authors utilize various architectures for image classification and machine translation, specifically ResNet-56 for CIFAR-10, ResNet-34 and ResNet-50 for ImageNet, and GNMT for WMT16 EN-DE. The training setups are based on standard implementations, with modifications made to the GNMT model to achieve improved BLEU scores through an extended training schedule.

The pruning strategy is divided into two main types: unstructured and structured pruning. Unstructured pruning targets individual weights without regard for their tensor locations, while structured pruning focuses on groups of weights, such as neurons or filters. The paper argues that although unstructured pruning reduces parameter count, it may not enhance performance until a significant portion of weights is pruned. In contrast, structured pruning maintains dense computation, potentially resulting in immediate performance gains.

The authors employ magnitude pruning, which involves removing weights with the lowest magnitudes, as their pruning heuristic. This method is widely recognized in the literature and serves as a foundation for their experiments.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive investigation into neural network pruning, providing insights into methodologies and their implications for performance.
