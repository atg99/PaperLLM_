The paper discusses the tradeoff between accuracy and parameter efficiency in neural network compression, specifically addressing iterative pruning methods applied to various architectures and datasets. The experiments conducted on CIFAR-10 using ResNet architectures (20, 56, and 110 layers) and ImageNet with ResNet-50 show how different compression ratios affect model accuracy. 

Results indicate that as the compression ratio increases, there is a corresponding change in accuracy, which can vary from -3% to +1%. The paper also references prior works, such as those by Carreira-Perpiñán and Idelbayev, and He et al., illustrating the iterative approach's effectiveness in maintaining performance while reducing model size. 

The findings emphasize the potential of iterative pruning as a viable method for achieving high compression without significantly compromising accuracy, showcasing the balance between model efficiency and performance in deep learning applications.
