The paper presents a study on the tradeoff between accuracy and search cost in the context of structured pruning for neural networks, specifically ResNet-56. It explores various configurations, labeled as Structured-A and Structured-B, across multiple retraining epochs. The results indicate fluctuations in accuracy, with some configurations showing a slight improvement or decline compared to the baseline, highlighting the sensitivity of the model's performance to the pruning strategy employed. Key techniques discussed include learning rate rewinding, weight rewinding, fine-tuning, and reinitializing, which are essential for optimizing the balance between model accuracy and computational efficiency. The findings underscore the importance of carefully selecting pruning methods to maintain performance while reducing resource requirements.
