The paper presented at ICLR 2020 explores the tradeoff between unstructured and structured pruning techniques in neural networks, specifically focusing on accuracy and search cost. It examines how different pruning ratios impact the accuracy of models such as ResNet-56 and ResNet-34 across datasets like CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and WMT16.

Key findings indicate that unstructured pruning allows for a range of compression ratios with minimal accuracy drops, demonstrated through accuracy curves that show performance stability across various retraining epochs. For instance, ResNet-56 maintains a slight accuracy decline when subjected to different unstructured pruning levels, while structured pruning methods like Structured-B for ResNet-56 and Structured-A for ResNet-34 also reveal similar trends.

The paper emphasizes the importance of learning rate and weight rewinding, as well as the concept of a "rewinding safe zone," which helps maintain accuracy during the pruning process. Overall, the results suggest that careful management of pruning strategies can lead to efficient models without significant losses in performance.
