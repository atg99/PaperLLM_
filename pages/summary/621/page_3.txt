The study investigates the prediction of conversion from Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) to Alzheimer's Disease (AD) using a classifier trained on both cross-sectional and longitudinal data. The classifier was tested on MCI patients at various time intervals (6, 12, 18, and 24 months) from baseline. Due to the nature of the data, the sample size available for training varied across these intervals, with fewer samples available if AD developed early.

The researchers employed a 5-fold cross-validation method, ensuring that each fold maintained a consistent ratio of MCI converters (MCI-C) and non-converters (MCI-NC). They compared two prediction schemes: one using only baseline data and another using longitudinal data. The analysis demonstrated that the longitudinal data provided superior predictive performance, particularly in sensitivity, which is crucial for accurately identifying patients who may benefit from timely interventions.

The results indicated that the model leveraging temporal changes in cognitive performance and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers outperformed the baseline-only model, highlighting the importance of incorporating multiple time points for more effective MCI conversion predictions. The study provides key statistics on the features used in the model, including cognitive performance, demographic information, CSF, and MRI data.
