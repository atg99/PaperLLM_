The paper evaluates the effectiveness of multimodal data integration in predicting the conversion from Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) to Alzheimer's Disease (AD). It compares the performance of a proposed model against single modality models using different types of data. The findings indicate that the model utilizing cognitive performance data achieved the highest accuracy among single modality models, despite the larger sample size of neuroimaging data. This is attributed to cognitive performance being longitudinal, providing data closer to the MCI conversion point. However, the cognitive performance model exhibited significant variability in sensitivity, particularly for predicting conversions at 18 and 24 months, indicating it is not a stable long-term predictor. The integration of other biomarkers in the proposed model helps mitigate this variance. The paper includes figures depicting predictive performances and ROC curves for various prediction intervals, highlighting the advantages of the multimodal approach.
