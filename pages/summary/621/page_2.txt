The study investigates the prediction of Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) conversion to Alzheimer's Disease (AD) using a multimodal recurrent neural network approach. It integrates various data types, including demographic information, longitudinal cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarkers, cognitive performance metrics, and neuroimaging biomarkers from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) cohort. The study sample comprised 1,618 participants, including cognitively normal older adults, MCI individuals (both converters and non-converters), and AD patients.

The research highlights the limitations of previous methodologies, such as linear discriminant analysis and single-modality approaches, and emphasizes the improved performance achieved by combining multiple data modalities. The proposed deep learning model effectively captures temporal features from longitudinal data, allowing for a more robust prediction of MCI to AD conversion.

Experimental evaluations compared three data schemes: using baseline data only, a single modality focusing on cognitive performance, and the proposed multimodal approach. The results indicate that the multimodal method outperforms the other schemes in predicting MCI conversion, thereby demonstrating the value of integrating diverse data sources in Alzheimer's research. The findings underscore the potential of deep learning techniques in enhancing diagnostic accuracy and understanding of AD progression.
