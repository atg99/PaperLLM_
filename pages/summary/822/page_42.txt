The paper investigates the effects of pruning neural networks beyond mere test accuracy, focusing on the trade-offs involved in model performance and robustness. It highlights the pruning potential of various architectures, such as ResNet110 and VGG16, when applied to the CIFAR10 dataset, including its out-of-distribution variations.

Key findings include:

1. **Pruning Techniques**: The study compares different pruning methods, including weight pruning (WT), structured pruning (SiPP), and fine-tuning (FT) approaches, showcasing their varying impacts on training and testing performance across diverse datasets.

2. **Prune Potential Measurements**: The analysis presents prune potential metrics, indicating that even with a low prune ratio of 0.5%, significant variations in accuracy can occur, particularly when evaluating against different types of distortions and noise.

3. **Effect on Accuracy**: The results demonstrate that pruning can lead to notable changes in top-1 test accuracy, with specific prune-test curves illustrating how different models respond to various pruning ratios. For example, both ResNet110 and VGG16 show distinct patterns of accuracy decline with increased pruning.

4. **Robustness Against Distortions**: The paper emphasizes the importance of evaluating model robustness beyond standard accuracy metrics, as pruning can influence resilience against out-of-distribution data, which is critical for real-world applications.

Overall, the research underscores the necessity of a more nuanced understanding of pruning's effects on neural networks, advocating for a balance between model efficiency and performance across diverse scenarios.
