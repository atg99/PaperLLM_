The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" explores the implications of pruning neural networks beyond just test accuracy. It focuses on the DenseNet22 and WRN16-8 models applied to the CIFAR10 dataset, analyzing how different pruning rates (PR) affect the performance and functional similarities of pruned models compared to their unpruned counterparts.

The findings indicate that as the pruning rate increases, the models exhibit varying degrees of robustness against uniform noise levels and matching labels. The analysis includes metrics such as the Softmax 2-norm difference, illustrating how pruning impacts the model's ability to maintain functional integrity despite reduced parameters.

The results are presented through detailed figures that depict the relationship between pruning rates and performance across different configurations (SiPP, PFP, and WT). The study concludes that pruning can significantly alter the characteristics of neural networks, emphasizing the need for careful consideration beyond mere accuracy metrics when evaluating pruned models.
