The paper investigates the impact of pruning neural networks on their performance, particularly focusing on the relationship between the amount of pruning and test accuracy. It analyzes several architectures, including ResNet56, ResNet110, VGG16, and DenseNet22, all trained on the CIFAR10 dataset.

Key findings reveal that as the prune ratio increases, the excess error also changes, indicating that aggressive pruning can lead to a loss in model performance. Different pruning strategies, such as weight pruning (WT), structured pruning (SiPP), fine-tuning (FT), and progressive pruning (PFP), were evaluated to understand their effects on excess error across varying prune ratios.

The results suggest that while pruning can reduce model size and improve efficiency, it also risks degrading accuracy if not managed carefully. The paper emphasizes the need for a balanced approach to pruning, where the benefits of reduced complexity do not outweigh the potential losses in performance. This highlights the importance of understanding the trade-offs involved in neural network pruning strategies.
