The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the impact of pruning various convolutional neural network architectures, such as VGG16, ResNet (20/56/110), DenseNet22, and WRN-16-8, on their performance as measured by test accuracy. The authors provide a detailed breakdown of the hyperparameters used during the training, pruning, and retraining phases across different models on the CIFAR-10 dataset. Key parameters include learning rate, batch size, momentum, weight decay, and pruning ratios.

Table 4 presents the pruning performance of different algorithms applied to the various architectures, highlighting the prune ratio (PR) and the ratio of floating point operations reduction (FR). The results show that, in many cases, the pruned networks maintain competitive accuracy compared to their original versions, achieving high levels of sparsity without significant accuracy degradation. For instance, ResNet20 achieved a pruning ratio of 85% with minimal change in test error.

The experimental setup extends to ImageNet, where similar hyperparameters and training procedures are adopted for ResNet18 and ResNet101. The paper emphasizes that the performance of the pruned networks is competitive with state-of-the-art results in neural network pruning, suggesting that effective pruning strategies can significantly enhance model efficiency while preserving accuracy.
