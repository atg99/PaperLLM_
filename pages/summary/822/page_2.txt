The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the impact of pruning neural networks using advanced prune-retrain techniques on the functional representation of these networks. The authors introduce new classification-based functional distance metrics to assess the similarities and differences between pruned and unpruned networks. They define "prune potential," which indicates the maximum prune ratio at which a pruned network can maintain its performance, providing insights into the degree of overparameterization.

The findings reveal that while pruned models often retain functional similarity to their uncompressed counterparts, their prune potential can vary significantly across different tasks. Although a pruned model may achieve comparable test accuracy, it may exhibit brittleness when exposed to out-of-distribution data, raising concerns about relying solely on accuracy for deployment in safety-critical applications.

The authors emphasize the necessity of considering task-specific evaluation metrics during pruning and propose guidelines for practitioners, including cautions against excessive pruning in light of potential data distribution shifts. They also establish a framework to measure genuine overparameterization and present experimental results across various datasets and architectures to support their observations.

In summary, the paper contributes novel metrics for assessing functional similarity, introduces the concept of prune potential as a measure of overparameterization, and provides practical guidelines for effective pruning in neural networks.
