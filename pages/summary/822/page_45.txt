The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" explores the implications of pruning neural networks, particularly focusing on how excessive pruning can affect performance beyond just test accuracy metrics. The research presents several experiments involving different neural network architectures, such as ResNet110, VGG16, DenseNet22, and WRN16-8, trained on the CIFAR10 dataset.

Key findings indicate that while pruning can reduce model size and potentially improve efficiency, it also introduces a risk of increased excess error when the pruning ratio exceeds certain thresholds. The analysis showcases the differences in excess error across various pruning ratios, illustrating that aggressive pruning can lead to significant performance degradation.

The study emphasizes that practitioners should be cautious when applying pruning techniques, as the relationship between pruning ratio and model performance is not straightforward. The results suggest that maintaining a balance between model efficiency and accuracy is crucial, and that blindly pursuing lower model complexity without considering the impact on performance may lead to suboptimal outcomes.
