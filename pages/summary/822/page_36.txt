The paper investigates the effects of pruning neural networks, specifically focusing on ResNet20 trained on the CIFAR10 dataset. It evaluates various "slack" values, denoted as epsilon, which represents the allowable difference in test accuracy between pruned and unpruned networks. The findings indicate that while higher values of epsilon lead to increased prune potential, the significant variation in prune potential across different tasks and distributions remains consistent regardless of epsilon's value.

Additionally, the paper examines excess error, defined as the additional error on the test distribution compared to the training distribution. The analysis employs linear regression to predict the relationship between the prune ratio and the difference in excess error. The results reveal a notable increase in excess error with higher prune ratios for most networks, suggesting that pruned networks struggle with out-of-distribution data. However, exceptions like WRN16-8 and ResNet101 show minimal correlation between prune ratio and excess error, indicating potential overparameterization.

The paper concludes that while pruning can reduce network complexity, it must be approached cautiously, especially in the context of out-of-distribution performance, as excessive pruning can lead to significant performance degradation.
