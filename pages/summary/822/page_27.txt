The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the impacts of pruning on neural networks, specifically ResNet110 and VGG16 models trained on CIFAR10. The study emphasizes that while pruning can reduce the size of neural networks, it may also lead to unexpected consequences regarding their functional similarity to unpruned counterparts.

The authors present data demonstrating how different pruning rates (PR) affect the models' performance under varying levels of uniform noise. They analyze the models through two key metrics: matching labels and the Softmax 2-norm difference. The findings reveal that as the pruning rate increases, the models' ability to maintain functional similarities to their unpruned versions diminishes, even when test accuracy remains high.

The results highlight the complex relationship between pruning and model robustness, indicating that a model's accuracy does not necessarily correlate with its structural integrity or functional behavior post-pruning. Through figures illustrating these relationships, the paper underscores the need for careful evaluation of pruned models beyond simple accuracy metrics to fully understand their operational capabilities and limitations.
