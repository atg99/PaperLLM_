In "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy," the authors explore the functional similarity between pruned neural networks and their unpruned counterparts despite maintaining similar test accuracy. The study focuses on two key metrics: informative features and noise resilience.

To assess informative features, the authors employ a methodology that identifies which pixels are most critical for the decision-making of each model. They utilize a greedy backward selection algorithm to mask the least informative pixels, enabling a comparative analysis of informative subsets across various networks. Their findings indicate that pruned networks retain a high degree of functional similarity to the original unpruned network, more so than a separately trained unpruned network.

The study also examines noise resilience by injecting random noise into test data and analyzing the predictions of both pruned and unpruned networks. The authors measure the expected number of matching label predictions and the norm difference in outputs under noise conditions. Results demonstrate that pruned networks exhibit a comparable level of performance in the presence of noise to their unpruned counterparts, reinforcing the notion that pruning does not significantly compromise the functional integrity of the model.

Overall, the research underscores that pruned networks can maintain essential functional characteristics while achieving model compression, thereby contributing to the understanding of network pruning beyond mere test accuracy. The appendix provides additional details on the comprehensive results obtained throughout the study.
