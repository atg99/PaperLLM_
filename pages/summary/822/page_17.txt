The paper discusses the effects of pruning neural networks, particularly focusing on the performance outcomes when applied to the ImageNet dataset and the Pascal VOC segmentation task. In the ImageNet experiments, the authors present results that demonstrate the achievable test accuracy for various target prune ratios. Notably, they halted experiments when further pruning led to a significant drop in accuracy, instead of exploring extreme pruning ratios. The results are competitive with state-of-the-art pruning methods, showcasing the pruning performance of different algorithms such as SiPP, PFPWT, and FT on architectures like ResNet18 and ResNet101, as summarized in Figure 10 and Table 6.

For the Pascal VOC segmentation task, the authors utilized a DeeplabV3 architecture with a ResNet50 backbone and applied a comprehensive data augmentation strategy during training. The results, detailed in Figure 11 and Table 8, similarly report the achievable accuracy for various prune ratios, emphasizing the importance of maintaining accuracy within a 0.5% margin of the unpruned networks. Overall, the findings highlight the balance between pruning efficiency and maintaining model performance across different datasets and architectures.
