The paper examines the effects of pruning neural networks on test accuracy, particularly focusing on various convolutional architectures trained on CIFAR10 and ImageNet datasets. It presents empirical results demonstrating how different pruning strategies impact the performance of models like ResNet, VGG, DenseNet, and WRN. 

Figures illustrate the relationship between the percentage of pruned parameters and the resulting change in test accuracy across various models. The data suggests that excessive pruning can lead to significant degradation in performance, indicating that optimal pruning thresholds exist. The paper also details the hyperparameters used for training and retraining the models, including specifics like learning rates, momentum, and weight decay, which are crucial for replicating the results.

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of carefully balancing the extent of pruning to maintain model efficacy, suggesting that pruning beyond certain limits can be detrimental to accuracy.
