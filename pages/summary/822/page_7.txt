The study aims to explore the prune potential and excess error of neural networks, particularly focusing on how these factors vary with shifts in data distribution. It evaluates the performance of pruned networks against nominal and out-of-distribution (o.o.d.) test data, using various corruption techniques on datasets like CIFAR10 and ImageNet.

The findings indicate that the prune potential significantly diminishes when networks are exposed to increased noise levels, with performance not deteriorating for human classifiers. The results suggest that while pruning can maintain nominal accuracy, it may not be effective under slightly more challenging conditions. The study also highlights that the prune-accuracy relationship can vary greatly with different types of corruptions, leading to unpredictable performance outcomes.

The analysis reveals that certain corruptions can lead to nearly zero prune potential, suggesting that pruning could harm network performance. Additionally, the choice of parameter influences the prune potential, but the general trends observed remain consistent regardless of this choice. The difference in excess error is crucial for understanding how pruning affects performance in varying data distributions, emphasizing the trade-off between maintaining accuracy and enabling pruning.

Overall, the research underscores the complexity of pruning neural networks and the necessity to consider how pruning affects performance under different conditions, particularly when aiming for generalization in real-world applications.
