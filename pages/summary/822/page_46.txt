The paper discusses the impact of pruning neural networks on their performance, focusing on the concept of overparameterization and its relationship with pruning potential. The results highlight that the average and minimum prune potentials for various networks and pruning methods (weight and filter) differ between training and testing distributions, particularly due to the presence of multiple corruptions in the training data. Robust training techniques appear to stabilize the average prune potential despite distribution changes, suggesting that overparameterization is influenced by both the dataset and the training approach.

The findings indicate that even pruned networks maintain a nonzero minimum prune potential on test distributions, which emphasizes the importance of robust training. The authors also argue that regularizing pruned networks during retraining can help recover significant portions of the prune potential, indicating that the training procedure plays a critical role. However, the generalizability of these results may be limited by the similarities between the training and testing distributions, as significant differences could lead to pruned networks experiencing more drastic performance declines compared to their unpruned counterparts. 

Tables included in the section provide detailed statistics on the prune potential for different models across various pruning methods, illustrating the effectiveness and variability of pruning across network architectures.
