The section discusses the evaluation of prune potential in neural networks as a measure of overparameterization. It presents findings from experiments on various networks using both weight and filter pruning methods. Average and minimum prune potentials were calculated for training and test distributions, with the training distribution containing only nominal data and the test distribution including various corruptions. Results indicate a significant drop (around 20%) in average prune potential from training to testing, while many networks displayed a minimum prune potential of 0% on test data.

Notably, robustly overparameterized networks like WRN16-8 and ResNet101 maintained stable prune potentials across both distributions. Tables summarize the prune potentials for different models under weight and filter pruning methods, showing varying performance based on the network architecture.

Additionally, the paper explores the impact of incorporating data augmentation techniques derived from CIFAR10-C corruptions into the training process. The goal is to assess whether such augmentations can enhance or stabilize the prune potential of the networks, suggesting further experiments and setup details will be provided in subsequent sections.
