The section presents additional experimental results on the prune potential of neural networks when exposed to noisy inputs and corrupted images. The findings reaffirm the main paper's conclusions that prune potential diminishes significantly with increased noise levels. Various architectures such as ResNet20, ResNet56, ResNet110, VGG16, DenseNet22, and WideResNet16-8 were tested on the CIFAR10 dataset, revealing that filter-pruned networks generally exhibit lower prune potential compared to weight-pruned networks. Notably, WideResNets displayed resilience to noise, likely due to their wider architecture, which allows for better noise absorption and reduced depth that minimizes positive noise amplification.

The section also discusses the prune potential in relation to out-of-distribution (o.o.d.) data sets, including CIFAR10-C, ImageNet-C, and VOC-C. The results indicate significant variability in prune potential across different architectures and pruning methods, with some networks performing better with o.o.d. data than others. The experiments demonstrated that the sensitivity of prune potential varies considerably with task, emphasizing the complexity of predicting outcomes.

Finally, evaluations on larger networks like ResNet18 and ResNet101 for ImageNet, as well as DeeplabV3 for VOC, confirmed that the observed trends extend beyond smaller datasets like CIFAR10. The authors suggest that higher nominal prune ratios could reveal even greater disparities in prune potential between in-distribution and out-of-distribution test data.
