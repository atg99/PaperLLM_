The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the implications of pruning neural networks, specifically focusing on the VGG16 and DenseNet22 architectures applied to the CIFAR10 dataset. It examines the performance of pruned models in terms of their functional similarities to their unpruned counterparts, emphasizing the trade-offs between model efficiency and accuracy.

The results are illustrated through various figures that present matching labels and Softmax 2-norm differences across different pruning rates (PR). The analysis indicates that while pruning can lead to reduced model size and potentially faster inference times, it may also impact the functional integrity of the model. The findings suggest that beyond a certain level of pruning, there may be diminishing returns in terms of test accuracy, raising questions about the optimal pruning strategies.

The paper highlights the importance of understanding how pruning affects not only accuracy but also the underlying functionality of neural networks, prompting further exploration into balanced pruning techniques that maintain performance while achieving efficiency.
