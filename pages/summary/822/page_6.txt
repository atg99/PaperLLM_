The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the implications of pruning neural networks, specifically focusing on the effects of weight pruning on model performance and decision-making processes. The study utilizes a ResNet20 model trained on the CIFAR-10 dataset to compare the performance of pruned networks against both the original unpruned network and a separately trained unpruned network.

Key findings reveal that weight-pruned networks retain higher confidence in features derived from their parent unpruned network compared to filter-pruned networks. Notably, when networks are pruned beyond a certain accuracy threshold, the informative features cease to be predictive across all models. The analysis of noise similarities indicates that pruned networks maintain a closer correlation in predictions to their unpruned counterparts than to independently trained unpruned networks, suggesting that pruning preserves the decision boundaries of the original model.

The paper further explores the behavior of pruned networks under distribution shifts, such as noise and other data corruptions, addressing concerns about their robustness in practical applications, particularly in safety-critical contexts. The authors introduce the concept of "prune potential," which quantifies the maximum pruning ratio that maintains performance within acceptable loss margins. Additionally, they define "excess loss" to measure the performance drop when encountering new data distributions.

Through a methodical approach, the study assesses the prune potential and excess loss across different distributions, revealing that while pruned networks show functional similarities to their parent models, they may experience varying performance under distributional changes. The findings emphasize the importance of understanding the trade-offs of pruning, particularly in terms of maintaining network performance and robustness in dynamic environments.
