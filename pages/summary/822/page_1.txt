The authors argue that while pruned networks can closely approximate the performance of their unpruned counterparts, the prune ratio at which this occurs varies significantly across different tasks. This variability raises critical questions about the true extent of overparameterization in deep learning and suggests that relying on test accuracy alone may not provide a comprehensive assessment of a model's performance, particularly in safety-critical applications.

The study highlights the need for a more nuanced evaluation of pruned networks, emphasizing metrics beyond test accuracy, such as generalization to out-of-distribution data and resilience to noise. The authors conducted experiments across various architectures and datasets, demonstrating that while pruned networks can achieve comparable test accuracy, their performance in other important areas may not be reliable. This finding underscores the importance of thorough evaluations before deploying pruned models in practical scenarios.

Overall, the paper calls for a reassessment of pruning practices in deep learning, urging researchers and practitioners to consider a broader range of performance metrics to ensure the safety and reliability of pruned networks in real-world applications.
