The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the implications of pruning neural networks beyond just their test accuracy. The research analyzes various models, including WRN16-8, ResNet18, ResNet101, and DeepLabV3, across different datasets like CIFAR10, ImageNet, and VOC2011. 

Key findings show that as the pruning ratio increases, there are notable changes in excess error rates, indicating that aggressive pruning can lead to performance degradation beyond what is suggested by test accuracy metrics. The study employs different training methods, including Weight Training (WT), Structured Importance-based Pruning (SiPP), Fine-Tuning (FT), and Pruning with Fine-Tuning (PFP), to evaluate their effects on excess error as the pruning ratio varies.

The results highlight the need for a careful balance in pruning strategies, as excessive pruning can result in a loss of model performance that may not be immediately reflected in standard accuracy metrics. This suggests that researchers and practitioners should consider additional metrics and methodologies when pruning neural networks to avoid detrimental effects on model effectiveness.
