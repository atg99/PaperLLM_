The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" explores the implications of pruning neural networks, particularly focusing on how excessive pruning can impact performance beyond just test accuracy metrics. The study examines various architectures, including ResNet and DenseNet, using datasets such as CIFAR10.

Key findings indicate that as the level of uniform noise injected into the network increases, the potential for effective pruning diminishes. The results are illustrated through graphical representations, where different pruning techniques (PFP, SiPP, WT, FT) are evaluated across multiple ResNet variants and other architectures like VGG16 and WRN. The graphs demonstrate that the achievable prune potential is significantly affected by the noise levels, suggesting that pruning strategies must consider not only accuracy but also the robustness of the network against input perturbations.

Overall, the research highlights the need for a balanced approach to pruning, taking into account the broader effects on model performance and resilience, rather than solely focusing on improving test accuracy.
