The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the implications of pruning neural networks, specifically examining how excessive pruning affects model performance beyond traditional test accuracy metrics. The study utilizes Wide Residual Networks (WRN16-8) and ResNet20 architectures on CIFAR10 datasets, analyzing various pruning rates (PR) ranging from 15% to 95%.

Key findings include:

1. **Functional Similarities**: The analysis highlights that pruned models maintain functional similarities to their unpruned counterparts, even at high pruning rates. This is assessed through metrics such as matching labels and Softmax 2-norm differences.

2. **Impact of Pruning on Performance**: The results indicate that while pruning can enhance efficiency, it may also lead to a degradation in performance metrics that are not solely based on accuracy. This suggests that reliance on accuracy alone as a measure of model effectiveness could be misleading.

3. **Different Pruning Techniques**: The study contrasts various pruning methodologies, including weight pruning (WT), structured pruning (SiPP), and progressive pruning (PFP), revealing differing impacts on model robustness and functional retention.

The paper emphasizes the need for a nuanced understanding of pruning effects, advocating for comprehensive evaluations that extend beyond mere accuracy to include functional performance metrics. This approach could lead to more informed decisions in model optimization and deployment.
