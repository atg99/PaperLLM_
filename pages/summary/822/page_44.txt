The section discusses the evaluation of excess error differences between pruned and unpruned neural networks, specifically focusing on various architectures like ResNet20, ResNet56, ResNet110, VGG16, DenseNet22, and WRN16-8. The results indicate that for most networks, especially larger ones, there is little correlation between the pruning ratio and the difference in excess error, suggesting that robust pruning methods are essential for maintaining reliable performance in pruned networks. It is important to note that the excess error is averaged over all corruptions in the training and testing datasets, which may not accurately reflect how specific corruptions influence pruning potential. Overall, the findings emphasize the need for careful consideration of pruning techniques to ensure effective network performance post-pruning.
