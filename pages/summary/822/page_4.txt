The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the impact of various pruning techniques on neural network performance, especially focusing on their effects beyond mere test accuracy. It categorizes pruning methods into unstructured and structured approaches, detailing specific techniques such as Weight Thresholding (WT), Sensitivity-informed Pruning (SiPP), Filter Thresholding (FT), and Provable Filter Pruning (PFP).

WT is a heuristic method that assesses weight sensitivity based on weight magnitude, while SiPP leverages data to compute weight sensitivities with guarantees. The structured pruning methods, FT and PFP, operate by allocating prune ratios per layer and removing filters with the lowest sensitivity. PFP extends SiPP by evaluating filter sensitivity based on the next layer’s channel sensitivity.

The paper introduces a "PRUNE RETRAIN" algorithm that iteratively prunes and retrains the network, aiming for smaller and more efficient models while maintaining performance. The authors present empirical results, particularly for Resnet20 on the CIFAR10 dataset, showing the accuracy variations corresponding to different pruning ratios.

Furthermore, the research includes experiments to understand the functional similarities of pruned networks and their robustness against noise and out-of-distribution data. The results indicate that pruned networks can outperform their unpruned counterparts in certain scenarios, suggesting that pruning can lead to beneficial outcomes beyond just maintaining accuracy. Overall, the findings highlight the nuanced effects of pruning on neural network performance, emphasizing the importance of understanding functionality and generalization in pruned models.
