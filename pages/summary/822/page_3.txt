The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" explores the implications of pruning neural networks, particularly focusing on their robustness and generalization capabilities. It builds on previous research that has produced mixed results regarding the adversarial robustness of pruned networks. While some studies suggest that pruning may improve or maintain adversarial robustness, others indicate a decline in this aspect. The authors argue for a comprehensive evaluation of pruned networks across diverse tasks to understand their prune potential and safe deployment.

The paper also discusses the integration of robust training with pruning, highlighting that while pruned networks can be trained to be robust, they are more susceptible to distributional changes in input data. This suggests that pruning reduces the network's implicit regularization, which is critical for generalization. The authors emphasize that pruning leads to a compromise between compactness and robustness, as pruned networks exhibit diminished resilience to noise and data corruption.

Furthermore, the methodology section details the experimental setup, which includes various network architectures and datasets, such as CIFAR10 and ImageNet. The authors implement a straightforward iterative pruning and retraining pipeline, using both unstructured and structured pruning methods to analyze the effects on network performance.

Overall, the findings underscore the need for careful consideration of the trade-offs between network compactness and robustness when applying pruning techniques, advocating for robust training as a means to mitigate the negative impacts of pruning.
