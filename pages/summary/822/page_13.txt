The paper discusses the implications of excessive pruning in neural networks, particularly how it affects generalization beyond mere test accuracy. The authors explore the balance between reducing model size and maintaining performance, emphasizing that aggressive pruning can lead to unexpected degradation in generalization capabilities. They reference various studies to illustrate the challenges and methodologies associated with neural network pruning, including comparisons between fine-tuning and rewinding techniques, and the impact of pruning on adversarial robustness. The paper argues that while pruning is a valuable tool for model compression, it necessitates careful consideration of its effects on the network's ability to generalize to unseen data. The authors conclude that more research is needed to understand the complex interactions between pruning, model performance, and generalization.
