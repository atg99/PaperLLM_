The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the impact of pruning on neural network performance, specifically focusing on the DeeplabV3-ResNet50 architecture. The study reports various hyperparameters used during training, including a learning rate of 0.02, SGD optimizer, and weight decay of 1.0e-4, across 45 epochs with a batch size of 32.

Key findings include the evaluation of pruning techniques on the Pascal VOC 2011 dataset, where the original model achieved a test error of 34.78% for TrainIoU and 7.94% for top-1 accuracy. The results indicate that performance varies significantly with different pruning strategies, as shown in the provided tables. For instance, the effectiveness of different algorithms like Weight-based (WT), SiPP, and PFP are compared, highlighting the trade-offs between prune ratios and error rates.

The analysis further includes additional empirical evidence regarding function distance in pruned networks, exploring the effects of informative input features and noise on prediction matching. Overall, the paper emphasizes the complexities and potential pitfalls of pruning neural networks beyond mere test accuracy, suggesting that careful consideration is required to avoid performance degradation.
