The section discusses the impact of pruning on neural networks, particularly examining the functional similarities between pruned networks and their unpruned counterparts. Two primary metrics are utilized: the percentage of matching predictions and the norm-based difference in outputs under perturbed inputs. The analysis reveals that pruned networks tend to approximate their unpruned parent networks more closely than a separately trained network with the same architecture.

Empirical results across various neural architectures, including ResNet20, ResNet56, ResNet110, VGG16, DenseNet22, and WideResNet16-8 trained on CIFAR10, demonstrate consistent functional similarities when applying the same pruning strategies. Figures illustrate the findings, showing the relationship between noise levels and matching labels for weight-pruned and filter-pruned networks. Overall, the evidence suggests that pruning maintains significant functional integrity, allowing pruned networks to retain performance characteristics similar to their unpruned versions.
