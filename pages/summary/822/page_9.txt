The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the impact of pruning neural networks on their performance, particularly in relation to robustness against various types of image corruptions. The study examines several network architectures, including ResNet and VGG, assessing their prune potential, which reflects the percentage of weights that can be removed while maintaining acceptable accuracy on both training and test datasets.

The authors find that while pruning can lead to significant reductions in model size, it also tends to degrade robustness, particularly against out-of-distribution (o.o.d.) data. They propose that incorporating data augmentation during retraining can help mitigate some of these adverse effects. Specifically, the study employs a methodology where a fixed subset of corruptions is randomly applied to training images, effectively altering the training distribution.

Results show that robust retraining stabilizes prune-accuracy curves and enhances the predictive ability of nominal test data. However, it is noted that even with these adjustments, prune potential can still vary significantly across different corruptions. The paper also highlights the relationship between prune ratio and excess error, indicating that while robust training reduces this correlation, it cannot completely eliminate it.

Furthermore, the authors discuss the concept of implicit regularization in overparameterized networks, suggesting that unpruned models exhibit greater robustness due to more effective stochastic optimization. By integrating explicit regularization techniques like data augmentation, pruned networks can recover some of this robustness.

The findings emphasize the importance of selecting appropriate training and test distributions, as the nature of the corruptions can influence the observed trade-offs between model size and performance. Overall, the research underscores the potential benefits of robust training methods in enhancing the resilience of pruned neural networks against various data corruptions.
