The paper titled "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the impact of pruning neural networks, specifically focusing on the functional similarities between pruned models and their unpruned counterparts. The study utilizes ResNet56 and ResNet110 architectures on the CIFAR10 dataset, analyzing both weight (WT) and fine-tuning (FT) scenarios.

Key findings include the evaluation of matching labels and softmax 2-norm differences at various pruning rates (PR). The results indicate that even at high pruning levels, significant functional similarities persist between pruned and unpruned models. For instance, the study presents graphs illustrating how performance metrics change with increasing uniform noise levels and different pruning rates, revealing that pruned models maintain comparable performance in terms of label matching and softmax differences.

Overall, the analysis suggests that while pruning can reduce model complexity, it does not necessarily lead to a loss in functional capability, challenging the notion that higher test accuracy is the sole indicator of a model's effectiveness post-pruning. This work emphasizes the need to consider additional performance metrics beyond just accuracy when evaluating pruned neural networks.
