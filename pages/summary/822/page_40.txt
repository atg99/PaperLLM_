The paper investigates the effects of pruning neural networks beyond just evaluating test accuracy, specifically focusing on the impact of various corruptions in the CIFAR10 dataset. It outlines an experimental setup where neural networks are trained, pruned, and then retrained using a subset of CIFAR10-C corruptions. The training data is corrupted with these techniques uniformly at random, ensuring that the training and test distributions are mutually exclusive while spanning similar corruption categories.

The results indicate that pruning can maintain the prune potential for corruptions included in the training phase, allowing for accurate predictions of network performance. However, the prune potential tends to be lower for corruptions present in the test distribution, leading to higher variance and unpredictability. Figures presented in the paper illustrate the nominal prune-accuracy curves for various network architectures, including ResNet20, ResNet56, ResNet110, VGG16, DenseNet22, and WRN16-8, highlighting the differences in test accuracy relative to uncompressed networks.

In conclusion, while pruning can be effective when training with specific corruptions, its efficacy diminishes when faced with unseen corruptions during testing, underscoring the need for comprehensive training strategies to enhance robustness against diverse data distributions.
