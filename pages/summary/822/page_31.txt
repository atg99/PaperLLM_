The paper "Lost in Pruning: The Effects of Pruning Neural Networks beyond Test Accuracy" investigates the implications of pruning neural networks, particularly focusing on ResNet architectures applied to the CIFAR10 dataset. It explores how pruning affects neural network performance not only on the standard test accuracy but also on various out-of-distribution datasets.

The authors present data indicating the pruning potential of ResNet20 and ResNet56 models. The results show that while pruning can lead to reduced model size and computational requirements, it can also adversely affect accuracy across different types of distortions, such as JPEG compression and Gaussian noise. The analysis includes comparisons between weight training (WT) and fine-tuning (FT) approaches, revealing distinct prune-test curves that demonstrate varying degrees of accuracy decline as the prune ratio increases.

Key findings indicate that pruning beyond a certain threshold can lead to significant drops in performance, emphasizing the need for careful consideration of pruning strategies. The results suggest that maintaining accuracy post-pruning requires a balance between the extent of pruning and the robustness of the model against diverse input variations. This research highlights the complexities involved in optimizing neural networks through pruning and calls for further exploration into effective pruning methodologies that preserve model efficacy.
