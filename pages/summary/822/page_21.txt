The paper investigates the impact of pruning neural networks on their performance, specifically focusing on ResNet20 and VGG16 architectures. It presents heatmaps that illustrate the confidence levels in correctly classifying informative pixels from pruned models when evaluated on CIFAR10-C corrupted test images. 

The heatmaps display data for two pruning methods: Weight Thresholding (WT) and Filter Thresholding (FT). The Y-axis represents the model used to generate 10% pixel subsets, while the X-axis shows the models being evaluated. Each cell in the heatmap indicates the mean confidence towards the true class for the evaluated model based on the tested data from the generating model.

The findings suggest that pruning beyond a certain point can lead to a decline in model performance, emphasizing the need for careful consideration of pruning strategies in neural network optimization.
