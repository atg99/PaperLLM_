The authors argue that the observed performance drops raise questions about the reliability of current pruning methods and emphasize the need for more robust approaches. They advocate for a deeper understanding of the brittleness and potential biases of pruned networks, especially as deep learning applications expand to smaller devices.

Additionally, the appendix provides extensive experimental details, including hyperparameters for pruning, measurements of functional similarities between pruned and unpruned networks, and investigations into prune potential and excess error. The experiments encompass a variety of architectures and datasets, underscoring the comprehensive nature of the study. The authors also make their code available for reproducibility, facilitating further exploration of their findings. 

In conclusion, this paper sheds light on the nuanced effects of pruning neural networks, urging caution in their deployment and calling for improved methodologies to ensure both efficiency and accuracy in real-world applications.
