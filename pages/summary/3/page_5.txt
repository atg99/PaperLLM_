The paper discusses the challenges in modeling plausible continuations for complex data like video, highlighting the limitations of traditional supervised and reinforcement learning methods that require extensive human-labeled data or interactions. It introduces contrastive learning as a potential solution, where an energy function E(X, Y) is used to determine the compatibility of an input video X with a proposed continuation Y. This energy function is computed using deep neural networks trained to assign low energy to compatible pairs and high energy to incompatible ones, allowing for the modeling of diverse continuations.

A significant challenge in contrastive learning is selecting effective negative samples—examples that should have high energy but currently do not. The paper notes that while early methods relied on Monte Carlo techniques, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have advanced this by generating contrastive samples in a differentiable manner, enabling end-to-end training.

The paper emphasizes the importance of unsupervised and self-supervised learning, as these approaches can reduce reliance on human-labeled data and improve robustness across different tasks. It draws parallels between human learning and AI, questioning how humans acquire extensive background knowledge through observation, which enables quick learning of complex tasks. This leads to the exploration of self-supervised learning, which leverages high-bandwidth data modalities like audio and video to predict or reconstruct missing information, thus enhancing the model's understanding of the world.
