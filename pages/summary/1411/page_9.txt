The paper evaluates the performance of a proposed object detection method across different devices, specifically a CPU and a Raspberry Pi. The CPU used is an Intel Xeon E5-2678 v3, while the Raspberry Pi is a 3B model with a BC219M2835 processor. The study employs the MS COCO dataset, utilizing images sized 416x416 with a batch size of 16. For the CPU, the entire dataset is tested, whereas only 40 images are randomly selected for testing on the Raspberry Pi due to storage limitations.

Results indicate that the proposed method outperforms existing models, YOLOv3-tiny and YOLOv4-tiny, in terms of processing speed measured in frames per second (FPS). On the CPU, the FPS for YOLOv3-tiny, YOLOv4-tiny, and the proposed method are 32, 25, and 37, respectively, showing increases of 15% and 48% over the other two methods. On the Raspberry Pi, the FPS values are 0.18 for YOLOv3-tiny, 0.19 for YOLOv4-tiny, and 0.31 for the proposed method, representing increases of 72% and 63%, respectively. 

The analysis concludes that the FPS improvements for the proposed method are more pronounced on the Raspberry Pi than on the CPU, suggesting that this method is particularly well-suited for embedded devices.
