This paper quantitatively evaluates the performance of various object detection methods, focusing on metrics such as mean Average Precision (mAP), Frames Per Second (FPS), and GPU utilization. The proposed method is compared against YOLO v3, YOLO v4, YOLO v3-tiny, and YOLO v4-tiny. Results indicate that while YOLO v4 and YOLO v3 have higher mAP values, they suffer from lower FPS due to their complex network structures, making them less suitable for mobile and embedded devices. In contrast, lightweight models like YOLO v3-tiny, YOLO v4-tiny, and the proposed method offer better FPS but lower mAP.

The proposed method demonstrates the highest FPS at 294, compared to YOLO v4-tiny's 270 and YOLO v3-tiny's 277, with only a minor decrease in mAP (38% for the proposed method versus 38.1% for YOLO v4-tiny). Additionally, the proposed method exhibits the lowest GPU utilization at 1003MB, which further supports its suitability for mobile applications.

Visual analysis of object detection results on the MS COCO dataset shows that the proposed method often yields higher confidence scores than YOLO v4-tiny for detected objects, indicating superior performance, especially for larger objects. Overall, the proposed method balances speed and efficiency, making it a promising option for real-time detection on resource-constrained devices.
