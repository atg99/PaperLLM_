The paper discusses an improvement to the Yolov4-tiny object detection method by proposing a new module called ResBlock-D to enhance detection speed with minimal impact on accuracy. The ResBlock-D module replaces two CSPBlock modules traditionally used in Yolov4-tiny, resulting in reduced computational complexity. 

Key components of the ResBlock-D module include two paths for processing input feature maps. Path A features three layers comprising 1x1 convolutions, 3x3 convolutions with a stride of 2, and additional 1x1 convolutions. Path B includes two layers of 2x2 average pooling with stride 2, followed by a 1x1 convolution. This design allows for a reduction in the overall computational load compared to the CSPBlock, as the ResBlock-D eliminates one 3x3 convolution layer.

The paper quantifies the computational efficiency using floating point operations (FLOPs) to compare the two modules. The FLOPs for CSPBlock are calculated to be approximately 7.421 x 10^10, while the FLOPs for ResBlock-D are around 6.438 x 10^10, indicating a significant reduction in computational complexity, with a ratio of about 10:1 favoring ResBlock-D.

To mitigate the potential decrease in accuracy resulting from the speed optimization, the authors propose adding two auxiliary residual network blocks to the ResBlock-D module. This adjustment aims to maintain a balance between detection speed and accuracy, ensuring that the improved method retains effectiveness in object detection tasks. The proposed backbone network is illustrated in the paper.
