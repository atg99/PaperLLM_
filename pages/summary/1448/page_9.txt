In this paper, the authors present a series of ablation studies to evaluate the design choices made in Squeezeformer, a novel hybrid attention-convolution architecture for automatic speech recognition (ASR). The studies focus on various components, including the Temporal U-Net structure, LayerNorm configurations, and activation functions in the convolution module.

The results, summarized in Table 4, reveal that Squeezeformer-M, the base model, achieves a word error rate (WER) of 2.43 on dev-clean and 6.51 on dev-other. The absence of the skip connection in the Temporal U-Net leads to a notable increase in WER, indicating the importance of high-resolution information for decoding. Moreover, the model fails to converge without the upsampling layer, highlighting its critical role in training stability.

LayerNorm experiments demonstrate a significant WER increase when using a PostLN-only scheme without the learned scaling layer, as well as degradation with a PreLN-only scheme. These findings underscore the necessity of the learned scaling layer for effective training. Additionally, the removal of the GLU activation from the convolution modules results in worse WER, further emphasizing the impact of architectural choices on performance.

The authors conclude that Squeezeformer consistently outperforms existing ASR models, including Efficient-Conformer, while maintaining smaller model sizes and lower computational requirements. The architecture's key innovations, such as the Temporal U-Net and the simplified macro and micro structures, contribute to its superior performance across various computational budgets. Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of Squeezeformer in advancing ASR technology.
