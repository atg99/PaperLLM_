The paper presents a redesign of the Conformer architecture, leading to the development of the Squeezeformer model, which aims to improve performance in automatic speech recognition (ASR) while maintaining computational efficiency. The authors systematically analyze the limitations of the Conformer, particularly its quadratic complexity in attention mechanisms and its intricate design, which complicates deployment on hardware platforms.

Key contributions of the Squeezeformer model include the incorporation of a temporal U-Net structure to reduce computational overhead by addressing temporal redundancy in feature representations. The architecture also simplifies the hybrid attention-convolution design by replacing the complex back-to-back multi-head attention and convolution modules with a more efficient structure that includes a single feed-forward module following each component. Additionally, the micro-architecture is optimized through modifications such as unifying activation functions and streamlining layer normalization.

The results demonstrate that Squeezeformer consistently outperforms the Conformer architecture and other state-of-the-art ASR models on the LibriSpeech test dataset, achieving a 1.40% improvement in word-error-rate (WER) without increasing the number of floating-point operations (FLOPs). This research highlights the potential for simplifying ASR architectures while enhancing performance, addressing both efficiency and accuracy in speech processing tasks.
