The paper discusses architectural improvements to the Conformer model, focusing on enhancing its efficiency and performance while simplifying its structure. It introduces the FMCF structure, which incorporates feed-forward, multi-head attention, convolution, and another feed-forward module. The authors argue that large convolutional kernel sizes in ASR models allow for global information mixing, contrasting with smaller kernels in computer vision. They propose an MF/CF structure instead of the back-to-back MC substructure to avoid redundancy, leading to a slight improvement in word error rate (WER) without increasing computational load.

In the micro-architecture design, the paper critiques the use of heterogeneous activation functions, specifically the switch from Swish to Gated Linear Unit (GLU) in convolution modules. To simplify implementation, the authors recommend unifying the activation function to Swish across the model, which does not significantly affect WER or FLOPs.

The authors also identify redundancies in Layer Normalization within the Conformer model, noting the coexistence of pre-LayerNorm and post-LayerNorm that complicates the architecture and is computationally intensive. They highlight the importance of maintaining stability in training when modifying these layers, suggesting the use of scaling layers to balance the effects of removing either normalization type. This approach aligns with strategies from other domains to stabilize training without normalization. Overall, the proposed changes aim to streamline the Conformer architecture while maintaining or improving its performance metrics.
