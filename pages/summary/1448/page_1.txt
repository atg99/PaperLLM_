The paper introduces Squeezeformer, a new architecture for automatic speech recognition (ASR) that addresses the limitations of the Conformer model, which has become widely used due to its ability to capture both local and global features through a hybrid attention-convolution framework. The authors conducted systematic studies that revealed suboptimal design choices in the Conformer architecture, leading to the development of Squeezeformer.

Squeezeformer enhances the macro-architecture by implementing a Temporal U-Net structure to reduce the computational cost of multi-head attention on long sequences and simplifying the block structure of attention and convolution modules followed by a feed-forward module, as opposed to the more complex Macaron structure in Conformer. In terms of micro-architecture, Squeezeformer streamlines the activations in the convolutional block, eliminates unnecessary Layer Normalization operations, and includes an efficient depthwise down-sampling layer for better input signal subsampling.

The results demonstrate that Squeezeformer achieves state-of-the-art performance with word-error rates of 7.5%, 6.5%, and 6.0% on the LibriSpeech test-other dataset, outperforming Conformer-CTC by significant margins while maintaining the same number of floating-point operations (FLOPs). The authors have made their code publicly available, contributing to the ongoing advancements in ASR models.
