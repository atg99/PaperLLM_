The paper discusses the architectures of Conformer and Squeezeformer models in the context of end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR). The Conformer architecture combines convolutional layers with Transformer models to effectively capture both local and global dependencies in speech data. However, the paper highlights the computational inefficiency of the quadratic complexity associated with multi-head attention (MHA) layers, particularly with longer sequences.

To address these inefficiencies, the Squeezeformer architecture introduces several innovations: it simplifies the model by replacing redundant pre-layer normalization layers with a scaled post-layer normalization approach, which allows for a more efficient residual path merging during inference. Additionally, it incorporates a depthwise separable convolution for the initial subsampling layer, significantly reducing floating point operations (FLOPs).

The Squeezeformer architecture is shown to scale effectively across model sizes and consistently outperforms state-of-the-art ASR models under the same training conditions. The paper further supports its design choices through a reverse ablation study, demonstrating the effectiveness of the architectural modifications.

The paper categorizes recent advancements in ASR into two main areas: model architecture and training methods. It emphasizes the importance of a strong encoder architecture for effective acoustic feature extraction and discusses the evolution of ASR models from convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to hybrid architectures like Conformer, ultimately leading to the development of Squeezeformer for enhanced performance and efficiency.
