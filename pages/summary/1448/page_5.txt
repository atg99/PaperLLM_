The paper discusses improvements made to the Conformer model for speech processing, focusing on the incorporation of temporal downsampling and the Temporal U-Net structure. Through analysis of 100 audio signals from the LibriSpeech dataset, the authors measured the cosine similarity between neighboring embedding vectors processed through Conformer blocks. Results indicated a high average similarity of 95% for adjacent frames, suggesting significant temporal redundancy as the network depth increases. To address this redundancy and reduce computational overhead, the authors introduced subsampling after the 7th Conformer block, maintaining a sample rate of 40ms initially and then increasing it to 80ms using a depthwise separable convolution pooling layer.

However, this temporal downsampling led to unstable training due to insufficient resolution for the decoder. To counter this, the authors integrated a Temporal U-Net structure, which upsampled the embeddings to restore resolution for accurate decoding. This modification resulted in a 20% reduction in total FLOPs compared to the original Conformer model and improved the word error rate (WER) from 7.90% to 7.28%. Additionally, the Temporal U-Net architecture effectively mitigated the cosine similarity issues observed with neighboring embeddings, leading to enhanced model performance. Overall, the paper emphasizes the balance between computational efficiency and model accuracy in speech recognition tasks.
