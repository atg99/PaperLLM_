This paper presents the architecture configurations and improvements of the Squeezeformer model in comparison to the Conformer-CTC baseline. Key configurations are detailed in Table 2, showcasing the number of layers, dimensions, heads, parameters, and floating point operations per second (FLOPs) for various model sizes. The Squeezeformer models generally exhibit a smaller FLOPs count while maintaining or improving performance metrics.

The authors propose replacing the pre-layer normalization (preLN) with a learnable scaling layer, which enhances model performance without incurring additional inference costs. This change contributes to a reduction in word error rate (WER) from 7.09% to 6.89%. 

Additionally, the paper discusses the importance of the subsampling block, which accounts for a significant portion of the overall FLOPs. By implementing depthwise separable convolutions in place of one of the convolution operations, they achieve a 22% reduction in FLOPs and a slight improvement in the test-clean WER.

The final model, Squeezeformer-SM, demonstrates a WER improvement of 1.01% over the Conformer-CTC-M baseline with a 40% reduction in FLOPs. Further scaling of the model leads to the Squeezeformer-M architecture, which achieves an additional WER improvement of 0.39%.

Overall, the paper establishes the effectiveness of Squeezeformer variants, detailing their architecture and performance metrics, and highlights the advantages of the proposed modifications in reducing computational overhead while enhancing model accuracy.
