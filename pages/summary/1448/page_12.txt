The references listed encompass a range of significant contributions in the fields of natural language processing, automatic speech recognition, and deep learning architectures. 

1. Kudo and Richardson introduce SentencePiece, a versatile subword tokenizer that operates independently of language, enhancing neural text processing.
2. Li et al. present Jasper, an end-to-end convolutional neural acoustic model designed for improved performance in speech recognition tasks.
3. Li et al. also discuss advancements in multiscale vision transformers, focusing on their application in classification and detection.
4. Likhomanenko et al. reevaluate evaluation methodologies in automatic speech recognition (ASR) to determine model robustness.
5. Liu et al. enhance RNN Transducer-based ASR through the integration of auxiliary tasks, showcasing the importance of auxiliary learning.
6. Loshchilov and Hutter propose a decoupled weight decay regularization method, which offers improved generalization in neural networks.
7. Lu et al. explore the Transformer architecture through the lens of multi-particle dynamics, aiming to enhance understanding and performance.
8. Lüscher et al. compare hybrid and attention-based ASR systems, particularly in the context of the LibriSpeech dataset, without data augmentation.
9. Majumdar et al. introduce Citrinet, bridging the performance gap between non-autoregressive and autoregressive end-to-end ASR models.
10. Ng et al. push the boundaries of non-autoregressive speech recognition, demonstrating its potential in practical applications.
11. The NVIDIA Nemo framework and NVDLA Primer provide resources for deep learning model development and deployment.
12. Pan et al. develop ASAPP-ASR, which utilizes multistream CNNs and self-attentive SRUs to achieve state-of-the-art results in speech recognition.
13. Panayotov et al. created the Librispeech corpus, a significant dataset for ASR based on public domain audiobooks.
14. Park et al. introduce SpecAugment, a straightforward data augmentation technique that enhances ASR performance.
15. Perslev et al. present U-Time, a fully convolutional network for time series segmentation, applied to sleep staging.
16. Radford et al. discuss generative pre-training methods to enhance language understanding capabilities.
17. Ronneberger et al. propose the U-Net architecture, which has become a standard in biomedical image segmentation tasks.

These works collectively advance our understanding and capabilities in machine learning, speech recognition, and text processing.
