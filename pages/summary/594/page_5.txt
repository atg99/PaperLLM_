The methods discussed primarily focus on latent space transformation through discrepancy-based and adversarial approaches. Discrepancy-based methods measure the differences between latent spaces using specific loss functions such as maximum mean discrepancy (MMD), Rényi-divergence, L2 distance, and moment distance. These methods aim to optimize the alignment of features without introducing additional parameters.

Conversely, adversarial methods utilize a discriminator to ensure that features from multiple sources appear indistinguishable. Key loss functions in this category include GAN loss, H-divergence, and Wasserstein distance, with the latter providing stable gradients and effective alignment even when source and target distributions do not overlap. 

The paper highlights various modular implementations, including alignment techniques between target and source domains, weight sharing for feature extractors, and classifier alignment strategies to enhance prediction accuracy. While shared feature extractors are common, unshared extractors may enhance the alignment but increase model complexity. Classifier alignment is also emphasized to mitigate prediction discrepancies near domain boundaries.

Overall, the comparison of methods presented in the paper underscores the importance of selecting appropriate alignment strategies and loss functions to optimize performance across different domains in multi-domain adaptation (MDA).
