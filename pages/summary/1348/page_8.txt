The paper discusses the critical role of socio-eco-technological systems in driving transformations towards sustainable deltas and addresses the necessary interdisciplinary approaches to achieve this. Wesselink et al. emphasize the need for earth system governance to facilitate these transformations, suggesting that research can provide valuable insights into effective strategies. Geels et al. focus on socio-technical transitions as a pathway to deep decarbonization, highlighting the interconnected nature of societal and technological changes needed for sustainability. McPhearson et al. argue for radical changes to achieve a desirable Anthropocene, pointing to the urgency of addressing urban sustainability challenges. Grabowski et al. outline five considerations for viewing infrastructures as socio-eco-technical systems, advocating for interdisciplinary dialogue to enhance understanding and collaboration in addressing these complex issues. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of integrated approaches in tackling sustainability challenges within socio-eco-technological frameworks.
