The paper discusses the advancements in autonomous robotic systems for soil sampling and plant breeding, emphasizing their potential to enhance precision agriculture and support farmer decision-making. Robotic phenotyping accelerates the breeding of new crop varieties by enabling precise measurement of traits, which can lead to increased yields and reduced energy and water inputs. The deployment of diverse robotic platforms, including ground and aerial systems, has shown promise in capturing environmental responses of key traits, especially in controlled environments. However, challenges remain in characterizing complex plant structures and developing mobile robotic systems capable of functioning effectively in harsh field conditions.

The paper also highlights the role of robotics in regenerative agriculture, which aims to restore soil health and enhance carbon sequestration. Key technologies such as controlled traffic farming (CTF) utilize real-time kinematics for precise vehicle guidance, reducing soil compaction and potentially decreasing N2O emissions significantly. Regenerative practices, particularly no-till farming, have been shown to increase soil organic carbon, with reported gains of approximately 350 kg C per hectare per year. Overall, the integration of robotic technologies into agriculture presents a significant opportunity for sustainable farming practices and improved productivity.
