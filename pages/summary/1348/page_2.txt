The challenge of decarbonizing the food system is critical for humanity to meet the Paris Agreement's temperature goals. Achieving reductions in CO2 equivalent emissions from the food sector is essential, yet these efforts must not compromise the availability and affordability of healthy food for a growing global population. There is no singular solution to this complex issue; instead, it requires a comprehensive reassessment of food production technologies and multiple interventions.

This article explores how robotic and artificial intelligence technologies can facilitate the decarbonization of agricultural crop production, while also acknowledging their potential in animal production systems. The development of agri-robotic technologies has garnered significant global research attention, with applications ranging from selective harvesting and pest control to environmental monitoring and autonomous farm logistics.

Key opportunities identified for deploying robotic technologies to reduce emissions in agriculture include:

1. Optimizing nitrogen use to reduce N2O emissions.
2. Accelerating the breeding of low carbon crops.
3. Utilizing lightweight robotic machines to regenerate soils and minimize compaction.
4. Implementing electrified robotic vehicles.
5. Employing AI and machine learning to decrease farm waste, including losses from pests and diseases.

Nitrogen is a crucial nutrient for crops but also a significant source of cost and emissions, with global nitrogen prices having doubled recently. Current nitrogen use efficiency (NUE) remains low, indicating a substantial opportunity for precision agriculture and robotic technologies to enhance NUE through improved application methods. Variable rate application approaches, using sensors for spatial assessment of nitrogen needs, have shown potential for increasing NUE.

However, the relationship between crop nitrogen requirements and canopy color, which is often used for assessments, can be inconsistent due to various influencing factors. Therefore, the integration of machine learning tools in next-generation precision agriculture is necessary to optimize NUE by analyzing nitrogen status and predicting crop needs for precise fertilization.

Autonomous robotic platforms can significantly enhance decision-making in variable soil environments by assessing factors like soil fertility and moisture. Innovations in robotic technologies could also draw inspiration from extraterrestrial research, further advancing agricultural practices on Earth.
