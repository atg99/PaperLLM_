The paper reviews the role of robotics and autonomous systems (RAS) in decarbonizing agricultural production to address the climate emergency, which significantly contributes to greenhouse gas emissions. It highlights the need for substantial reductions in emissions within the agri-food sector, which accounts for 21 to 37% of global GHG emissions. The authors identify five key areas where robotics can facilitate the transition to net zero agriculture: increasing nitrogen use efficiency, accelerating plant breeding, promoting regenerative agriculture, electrifying robotic vehicles, and reducing food waste. 

The paper emphasizes that RAS technologies can optimize the use of agricultural inputs, lessen environmental impacts, enhance efficiency and precision in farming processes, and inform better decision-making for crop care and waste reduction. The authors call for further research and development to fully realize these opportunities and improve sustainability in food production systems. 

Overall, the paper underscores the transformational potential of robotics in achieving net zero agriculture while addressing critical environmental challenges associated with current agricultural practices.
