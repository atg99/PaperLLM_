The paper discusses a novel approach to motion planning in autonomous driving using a large language model (LLM), specifically GPT-3.5. The authors argue that while LLMs excel in language tasks, their capability in precise numerical reasoning, particularly for predicting high-precision waypoint coordinates, remains unproven. To address this, they reformulate motion planning as a language modeling problem by converting heterogeneous planner inputs into unified language tokens. The GPT-3.5 model is then instructed to generate future driving trajectory coordinates through natural language descriptions.

The authors introduce a prompting-reasoning-finetuning strategy to enhance the model's numerical reasoning. Initially, GPT-3.5 is prompted within the autonomous driving context, followed by chain-of-thought reasoning to produce coherent outputs, and finally fine-tuned with actual human driving trajectories to align its predictions with human behavior. This method enables the model to forecast waypoint coordinates with centimeter-level accuracy and enhances the interpretability of its decision-making process.

Key contributions of the paper include:
1. The introduction of GPT-Driver, which transforms motion planning into a language modeling task and offers an intuitive interpretation through the GPT tokenizer.
2. The development of a novel prompting-reasoning-finetuning strategy that facilitates precise numerical reasoning and transparent decision-making.
3. Demonstrating that GPT-Driver outperforms existing state-of-the-art motion planners in terms of performance, generalization, and interpretability using the nuScenes dataset.

The paper also reviews related works on motion planning, categorizing them into rule-based, optimization-based, and learning-based methods. It highlights the limitations of traditional approaches and emphasizes the advantages of leveraging GPT-3.5's reasoning capabilities for complex driving scenarios, ultimately presenting GPT-Driver as a more interpretable and effective alternative in the field of autonomous driving.
