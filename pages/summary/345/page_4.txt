The paper discusses a novel approach to motion planning for vehicles by transforming the problem into a language modeling task. It begins by defining a planned trajectory as a series of waypoints over time, with the ego-states representing the vehicle's historical trajectory and current status. Traditional learning-based motion planners often use L1 regression to imitate human driving trajectories, but this method struggles with precision for distant waypoints due to the varying scales of coordinate values.

To address this issue, the authors propose using language modeling to represent motion planning. They suggest that driving trajectories can be expressed as sequences of words through a language tokenizer, such as the GPT-3.5 tokenizer, which converts numerical coordinates into a series of tokens. For example, the coordinate 23.17 would be tokenized into "23", ".", and "17". This transformation allows the motion planning problem to be reformulated as maximizing the probability of word occurrence in the planned trajectory, thereby generating human-like driving behaviors.

The authors argue that this method simplifies the motion planning process by eliminating the need for specially designed tokens used in previous works, which often complicate generalization to other tasks. Instead, they leverage existing language tokenizers to achieve precise numerical estimations in a more intuitive manner, enhancing the approach's generalizability and compatibility with natural language.
