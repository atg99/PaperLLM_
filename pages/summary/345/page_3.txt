The paper discusses the integration of foundation models, specifically large language models (LLMs), into the domain of motion planning for autonomous driving, introducing a novel framework called GPT-Driver. The authors argue that while LLMs have shown significant advancements in language understanding and reasoning, their application to autonomous driving presents unique challenges due to the numerical nature of motion planning inputs and outputs.

In the proposed GPT-Driver framework, motion planning is reformulated as a language modeling problem, allowing LLMs to process observations and ego-states as language prompts. This innovative approach transforms the traditional motion planning problem into a format that LLMs can handle effectively. The authors detail a prompting-reasoning-finetuning strategy that enhances the reasoning capabilities of LLMs specifically for low-level waypoint-based trajectory planning, distinguishing it from existing methodologies that focus on high-level task planning.

The main objective of motion planning is to derive a safe and comfortable driving trajectory based on real-time observations and vehicle states. The framework aims to bridge the gap between language processing and the numerical requirements of autonomous driving, with initial results indicating that GPT-3.5 can perform detailed numerical reasoning necessary for effective motion planning.

Overall, this research presents a significant step towards leveraging LLMs in autonomous driving applications, proposing a method that could enhance decision-making processes in real-world driving scenarios.
