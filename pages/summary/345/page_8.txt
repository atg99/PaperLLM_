The paper discusses the performance of the GPT-Driver model in the context of motion planning, particularly when training data is limited. The results indicate that GPT-Driver outperforms UniAD in few-shot settings, demonstrating superior generalization capabilities. Specifically, GPT-Driver achieves better average metrics in both average L2 distance and collision rates across various training data percentages.

The comparison between in-context learning and fine-tuning reveals that fine-tuning is significantly more effective for motion planning tasks. While in-context learning relies on a limited context window, fine-tuning allows the model to leverage more comprehensive training data, leading to improved performance. 

Interpretability is another key aspect highlighted in the paper, where the GPT-Driver not only generates planned trajectories but also provides reasoning behind its decisions. This includes identifying relevant obstacles and formulating coherent driving actions based on these observations.

However, the paper acknowledges limitations regarding the inference time of the GPT-Driver model due to constraints of the OpenAI APIs, raising questions about its applicability in real-time commercial driving scenarios. Overall, the findings suggest that GPT-Driver is a promising approach for motion planning with significant advantages in few-shot learning and interpretability.
