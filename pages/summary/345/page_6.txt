The paper discusses the development of a novel approach to motion planning for autonomous vehicles using large language models (LLMs) within the framework of the Foundation Models for Decision Making Workshop at NeurIPS 2023. The key elements include the integration of perception and prediction data into a structured reasoning process that enhances interpretability and decision-making.

The authors highlight notable objects detected by the ego vehicle, such as animals and adults, and their potential effects on vehicle safety within a specific timeframe. The proposed meta action involves turning right at a constant speed, with a detailed trajectory provided.

A major challenge addressed is the disparity in data types between motion planners and LLMs, which traditionally handle language inputs. To mitigate this, the authors convert observations and ego-states into language descriptions, creating perception and prediction prompts that encapsulate critical information about detected objects and the ego vehicle's status.

The reasoning framework is structured into three steps: identifying critical objects affecting driving dynamics, analyzing their future motions, and formulating a high-level driving decision that translates into a planned trajectory. This structured approach aims to improve transparency in motion planning.

To align LLM outputs with human driving behaviors, the authors employ a fine-tuning strategy using real driving data. They compute hypothetical ego-trajectories and identify critical objects that may interfere with these paths to generate ground truth guidance for the reasoning process. This method reduces the need for manual annotation and facilitates the fine-tuning of LLM outputs.

The paper concludes with a description of extensive experiments conducted on the nuScenes dataset, showcasing the effectiveness, generalization ability, and interpretability of the proposed GPT-Driver approach compared to state-of-the-art motion planning methods.
