Our findings indicate that the GPT-Driver exhibits robust performance even with minimal training data, demonstrating its potential for few-shot learning scenarios. This adaptability is critical for real-world applications where data may be limited or costly to obtain. The results suggest that the model can effectively generalize from a small sample of training scenarios, maintaining a balance between accuracy in trajectory planning and safety in collision avoidance.

In conclusion, the GPT-Driver outperforms existing methods in L2 error metrics while showing competitive results in collision rates. Its simplicity in design, relying on language-based inputs rather than complex sensor data, positions it as a promising solution for autonomous driving motion planning tasks. The few-shot experiment further underscores the model's versatility and capability to function effectively with limited data, highlighting its applicability in diverse operational contexts.
