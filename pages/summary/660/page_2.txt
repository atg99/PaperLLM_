The paper discusses the development and performance evaluation of GraphACT, an FPGA-based accelerator for training Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). The key contributions include online pre-processing of operations between common node neighbors, which reduces on-chip operations and BRAM accesses, and parallelization of training steps using optimized computation modules, achieving load-balancing across various FPGA devices. 

A performance model for GraphACT is introduced, identifying key architectural and algorithmic parameters that affect performance. The evaluation of GraphACT on a Xilinx Alveo U200 board, hosted by a 40-core Xeon server, reveals a significant training time speedup—an order of magnitude—while maintaining accuracy compared to state-of-the-art multi-core implementations.

The paper provides foundational concepts of GCNs, explaining their structure, feature aggregation, and weight transformation processes. It highlights the challenges of training GCNs on large graphs, particularly in constructing minibatches due to the interconnected nature of graph nodes. The paper also reviews existing FPGA-based accelerators for training deep learning models, emphasizing the modular design approach to enhance performance.

In summary, GraphACT presents a promising advancement in efficiently training GCNs on FPGA platforms, showcasing improvements in speed without compromising accuracy.
