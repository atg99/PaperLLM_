The paper "GraphACT: Accelerating GCN Training on CPU-FPGA Heterogeneous Platforms" by Hanqing Zeng and Viktor Prasanna addresses the challenges of training Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), which are essential for representation learning on graphs. The authors highlight two primary challenges: substantial and irregular data communication required for information propagation within graphs, and the intensive computation needed for processing through neural network layers.

To tackle these issues, the authors propose a novel accelerator tailored for CPU-FPGA heterogeneous systems, integrating multiple algorithm-architecture co-optimizations. They analyze the computation and communication characteristics of various GCN training algorithms and choose a subgraph-based approach that is particularly suited for hardware execution. The paper introduces a lightweight pre-processing step utilizing graph theory, which effectively reduces memory access needs and computation on the FPGA.

Furthermore, the authors design a systolic array for efficient parallelization of weight updates in GCN layers. They create a comprehensive hardware pipeline, assessing its load-balance and resource utilization through precise performance modeling. Evaluations conducted on a Xilinx Alveo U200 board with a 40-core Xeon server demonstrate that GraphACT achieves significant training speedup—by an order of magnitude—on large graphs, with minimal accuracy loss compared to existing implementations on multi-core platforms.

In summary, GraphACT represents a significant advancement in the acceleration of GCN training, addressing memory access, computation, and load-balance challenges through innovative algorithm selection and hardware design strategies.
