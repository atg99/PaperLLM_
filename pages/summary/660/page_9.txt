In Section 5.3, it is noted that for most FPGA platforms, the utilization of DSP resources is close to 1, with specific examples showing over 93% utilization on the Xilinx Alveo U200 board. The experiments are conducted on this board, which is hosted by a powerful 40-core Xeon server, utilizing PCIe 3.0 x16 for communication, achieving a peak bandwidth of 15.8GB/s. The FPGA contains significant resources, including 5867 DSP slices and various types of RAM, allowing for substantial on-chip storage and processing capabilities.

The evaluation employs three large benchmark graphs to assess training speed and accuracy. The datasets include PPI, Reddit, and Yelp, each characterized by a specific number of nodes, edges, attributes, classes, and training/validation/testing splits. The Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) architecture used consists of two convolutional layers and one MLP layer, with a hidden dimension set to 256. The performance of the proposed GraphACT method is compared against a baseline method, with results indicating that GraphACT achieves higher accuracy on PPI and comparable results on Reddit and Yelp.

The study highlights the importance of redundancy reduction in computation and communication, which is achieved at the cost of additional on-chip buffer storage. The results demonstrate that redundancy can be significantly reduced while maintaining efficiency, particularly depending on graph topology.

A comparison with state-of-the-art implementations reveals that the proposed design achieves a substantial speedup over CPU implementations, ranging from 12 to 15 times faster, while also slightly outperforming GPU implementations in convergence speed. The efficiency gains are attributed to the optimized FPGA architecture, which minimizes the overhead associated with the CPU and leverages the FPGA's capabilities to handle the computational demands effectively. Overall, the paper emphasizes the advantages of using FPGA for deep learning tasks, particularly in terms of speed and resource utilization.
