This paper discusses advancements in Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) training on CPU-FPGA heterogeneous platforms, focusing on hardware-software optimizations to enhance performance. Key findings from the comparisons of convergence and test set accuracy across different methods, including GraphSAGE, Para-GCN, and GraphACT, indicate varying levels of accuracy and resource utilization.

The discussion highlights design challenges in optimizing memory access and load balancing. For FPGAs, the paper emphasizes the importance of optimizing both on-chip and off-chip memory access, reducing bank access conflicts, and enhancing data reuse. In contrast, CPU and GPU optimizations focus on improving data locality through software techniques like node reordering.

The paper also addresses redundancy reduction as an algorithm-level optimization that may not universally enhance CPU performance due to potential increases in cache misses. However, it asserts that this technique does not negatively impact FPGA performance as long as data fits into BRAM.

The conclusion highlights the success of the proposed optimizations in achieving efficient GCN training and outlines future plans to extend these methods to accelerate inference on large, un-sampled graphs. Overall, the findings suggest that FPGA-based solutions can achieve higher efficiency than traditional CPU or GPU approaches in GCN training by leveraging customizable computation and fine-grained memory control.
