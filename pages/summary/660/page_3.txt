The paper discusses advancements in accelerating Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and related algorithms through various optimization techniques. It highlights the challenges faced in GCN training, particularly the need for both sparse and dense matrix operations, irregular memory access, and load balancing. The paper compares existing methods for accelerating Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) with those required for GCNs, noting significant differences in their operational requirements.

The authors review prior works that focus on traditional graph analytics, such as PageRank and shortest path algorithms, and emphasize that the memory optimization techniques developed for these tasks do not directly apply to GCNs due to differing data propagation methods. The GCN training algorithm involves three types of matrix products, each with unique computational demands, necessitating different optimization strategies for efficient execution.

To enhance training efficiency on large graphs, the paper suggests using minibatch sampling to minimize external memory communication. It categorizes various sampling algorithms and their hardware implications, analyzing their effectiveness in selecting appropriate minibatches for GCN training. The paper concludes with considerations for implementing these algorithms on FPGA systems, addressing the challenges posed by the size of input data and the need for efficient memory access.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of the state of GCN acceleration techniques and the complexities involved in optimizing their training processes.
