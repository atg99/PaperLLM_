The paper discusses optimizing the computation and communication costs involved in training Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) using a minibatch approach, particularly focusing on reducing redundancy in feature aggregation. The authors assume equal feature sizes across layers and define the costs associated with transferring feature vectors and aggregating node features. They present a comparison of various training algorithms based on their computation-communication ratios, highlighting that algorithms with lower ratios hinder the development of efficient hardware accelerators. 

The selected algorithm for acceleration is based on a previous work (denoted as [34]), which demonstrates lower complexity and higher training accuracy compared to others. The paper emphasizes the importance of redundancy reduction during minibatch training, illustrating this with examples of how certain pairs of nodes frequently occur in neighbor lists. By constructing an aggregation graph (Ga) from the subgraph (Gs), the authors propose an efficient method to identify common pairs of neighbors for feature aggregation, which minimizes both computation and communication costs.

The authors introduce an algorithm to build the aggregation graph, where edges represent potential vector sum operations between nodes. Weights on edges indicate the frequency of node pairs appearing together in neighbor lists. The paper concludes that by finding a maximum weight matching in the aggregation graph, significant reductions in the number of vector reads and additions can be achieved, enhancing the efficiency of GCN training on hardware accelerators such as FPGAs.
