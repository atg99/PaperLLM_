This paper discusses an approach to optimize graph convolutional networks (GCNs) by reducing redundancy in feature aggregation through the use of matchings in aggregation graphs. The authors first present a proof highlighting the reduction in read operations achieved by accessing combined feature vectors instead of individual ones. They propose a greedy algorithm to compute a good matching, which minimizes the number of edges while maintaining disjointness among them.

Following the matching computation, the original graph is updated to create a new directed subgraph that incorporates pre-computed feature sums, leading to fewer edges but more nodes. The complexity of the algorithms is analyzed, revealing that they operate efficiently compared to traditional feature aggregation methods. The authors emphasize that this transformation not only reduces redundancy but also minimizes floating point arithmetic in further operations.

The paper introduces iterative redundancy reduction, where the process of applying the matching algorithms can continue until minimal redundancy remains. This iterative approach is feasible even when dealing with directed graphs.

In the section on accelerator design, the authors outline a partitioning of tasks between the FPGA and CPU to enhance training throughput. The FPGA is designated for computation-intensive tasks while the CPU handles communication-heavy operations. They stress the importance of optimizing memory access and resource utilization, suggesting adjustments to minibatch parameters to fit the graph size within on-chip memory constraints. Overall, the paper presents a systematic method for improving the efficiency of GCNs through algorithmic innovation and hardware optimization.
