The paper discusses various techniques employed in deep neural networks (DNNs) to enhance performance and mitigate issues such as overfitting. 

Global average pooling is highlighted as a method for extreme dimensionality reduction, transforming the entire input size to one dimension, which increases robustness to spatial translations. Batch normalization is introduced as a solution to the problem of changing data distributions during training, as it ensures that inputs maintain zero mean and unit variance, thereby enhancing accuracy and reducing training time. 

Dropout is presented as a strategy to curb overfitting by randomly nullifying weights of certain units during training, allowing for faster training and improved model generalization. Fully connected layers are described as standard neural network layers where each neuron is connected to every output from the previous layer, allowing for task interchangeability with convolutional layers.

Loss layers serve to penalize deviations from expected outputs, with different types available for various prediction tasks, such as sigmoid for independent probabilities and Softmax for class predictions. Lastly, the paper addresses the challenge of insufficient training data in deep convolutional networks (CNNs) and suggests transfer learning as a viable solution, leveraging knowledge from previously trained models to improve performance on new tasks.
