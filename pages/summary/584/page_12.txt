The paper presents a hybrid model designed by integrating elements from previous state-of-the-art architectures, comprising traditional convolutions, parallel convolutions, and residual connections. The model consists of 91 layers, as detailed in accompanying tables and figures.

Initially, two traditional convolutional layers with filter sizes of 3x3 and 5x5 are employed to minimize the input image size while preserving small and large features. This choice of filter sizes is critical to avoid bottlenecks that could impede the recognition of significant features or neglect small details that may lead to misclassifications. Each convolutional layer is followed by batch normalization, which accelerates training, and a ReLU activation function to mitigate the vanishing gradient problem.

Following the traditional layers, five blocks of parallel convolutional layers are introduced, each containing four layers with distinct filter sizes (1x1, 3x3, 5x5, and 7x7). The outputs of these layers are concatenated, enabling the model to capture a variety of feature levels and enhance gradient propagation through multiple pathways.

Atop these convolutional blocks, a global average pooling layer is utilized, followed by three fully connected layers interspersed with two dropout layers to combat overfitting. The final output is generated using a Softmax function.

Additionally, the paper outlines different training scenarios, including one where the proposed model is trained from scratch using original images from target datasets.
