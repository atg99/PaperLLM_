This paper presents the results of a proposed model evaluated across three scenarios using two target datasets, A and B. In Scenario 1, Dataset A outperformed Dataset B with precision of 84.8%, recall of 88.6%, and F1-score of 86.6%, while Dataset B achieved 82.9% precision, 87.5% recall, and 85.1% F1-score, indicating similar overall performance.

In Scenario 2, transfer learning (TL) from medical sources was applied, leading to significant improvements for Dataset A with precision of 96.8%, recall of 98.6%, and F1-score of 97.6%. However, performance for Dataset B declined to 81.8% precision, 86.7% recall, and 84.1% F1-score, suggesting that TL from a medical source was inappropriate due to feature discrepancies.

Scenario 3 utilized TL from the same domain as Dataset B, resulting in improved metrics: 91.6% precision, 96.9% recall, and 94.1% F1-score for Dataset B. Dataset A also showed slight improvement with 86.5% precision, 92.7% recall, and 89.4% F1-score, although still lower than in Scenario 2.

Finally, when comparing with state-of-the-art models trained on the ImageNet dataset, results for Dataset B were higher than for Dataset A. Using VGG19, Dataset B scored 89.3% precision, 95.2% recall, and 92.1% F1-score, while ResNet50 achieved the best results at 93.7% precision, 98.9% recall, and 96.2% F1-score. These findings underscore the importance of appropriate transfer learning sources for optimal model performance.
