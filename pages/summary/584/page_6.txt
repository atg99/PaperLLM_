The paper addresses the challenge of limited training data for diabetic foot ulcer (DFU) classification and investigates the impact of image types on transfer learning (TL) performance. The primary aims include enhancing DFU classification, employing deep convolutional neural networks (DCNN), and exploring same versus different domain TL.

Key contributions of the study are as follows: 

1. A new dataset comprising 1200 manually labeled images of feet, categorized as normal or abnormal by a DFU expert, has been created.
2. A hybrid deep learning model that integrates traditional and parallel convolutional layers with residual connections has been designed.
3. Multiple training scenarios have been executed using the proposed model.
4. Two pre-trained models, VGG19 and ResNet50, have been adapted to the target datasets.
5. Empirical evidence shows that TL from the same domain significantly enhances performance.
6. An F1-score of 97.6% has been achieved in DFU classification.

The methodology section outlines the use of four datasets, distinguishing between two target datasets from different domains for comparative analysis. The DFU dataset (Dataset A), obtained with ethical approval from a medical center in Iraq, consists of 1200 images, with a focus on a specific region of interest cropped to 224 by 224 pixels. This dataset includes 742 normal and 735 abnormal images, facilitating a robust evaluation of the proposed methods.
