Diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs) have become increasingly prevalent over recent decades, affecting approximately 15% of diabetic patients throughout their lives. The incidence of DFUs varies between 4% and 27%, making it a significant cause of hospitalization and morbidity among diabetic individuals. Once a patient develops a DFU, the risk of complications, including amputation, rises, leading to treatment costs around $17,500 per ulcer. In Europe and North America, DFUs account for 7% to 20% of diabetes-related healthcare expenditures.

Effective evaluation of DFUs involves assessing medical history, conducting physical examinations by specialists, and employing imaging techniques like X-rays and MRIs to inform treatment plans. Computer vision (CV) algorithms can aid in analyzing visual characteristics such as texture and color. The application of telemedicine for DFU detection is still developing; previous studies have attempted to utilize 3D imaging and machine learning techniques for classification and segmentation of DFUs, though these efforts often faced limitations regarding dataset size and practical implementation in healthcare settings.

Current classification methods rely on traditional machine learning, which extracts features from wound images before categorizing them. However, variations in skin color and lighting conditions can affect the accuracy of these systems. Deep learning (DL) approaches, particularly deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs), have shown promise in classifying DFUs but require extensive training data, which is scarce. The main challenge for DL models in DFU classification is the limited availability of labeled images, underscoring a broader issue in medical imaging classification tasks.
