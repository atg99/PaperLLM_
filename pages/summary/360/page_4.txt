This paper presents Speech-XLNet, a method for unsupervised speech representation learning using self-attention networks (SANs). The authors evaluate its effectiveness on hybrid SAN/HMM and transformer-based end-to-end (E2E) systems, demonstrating that permutation-based pretraining allows SANs to focus on global information and bi-directional context, leading to faster convergence.

The study compares various training methods, including a baseline E2E model with randomly initialized weights and models pretrained using Speech-XLNet. Results indicate that finetuning with Speech-XLNet consistently outperforms models trained from scratch, particularly when limited labeled data is available. Furthermore, models trained with more unsupervised data show improved performance, and the permutation procedure enhances the ability to capture context.

The analysis of loss landscapes reveals that models pretrained with Speech-XLNet exhibit flatter and wider optima, correlating with better generalization capabilities on unseen test sets. The authors conclude that their method not only enhances performance in ASR tasks but also holds potential for other applications, especially in scenarios with limited labeled data.

Future work will involve testing Speech-XLNet on large vocabulary continuous speech recognition (LVCSR) tasks and exploring its applicability in code-switching ASR using extensive mono-lingual data for pretraining.
