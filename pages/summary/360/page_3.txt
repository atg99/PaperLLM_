The results indicate that the performance significantly improves with pretraining, demonstrating the effectiveness of the Speech-XLNet model. The model shows a marked reduction in word error rates (WERs), particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data, which highlights the benefits of leveraging pretrained models for automatic speech recognition (ASR) tasks. 

The paper illustrates that pretrained SANs not only achieve faster convergence but also yield lower phoneme error rates (PERs) compared to those initialized randomly. The attention mechanisms in the pretrained model exhibit a broader context understanding, enhancing classification efficacy. Comparisons with existing methods indicate that the proposed approach outperforms several prior techniques, confirming its competitive edge in the field. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of pretraining in improving ASR performance, particularly when dealing with constrained datasets.
