The paper introduces "Speech-XLNet," an unsupervised pretraining scheme for learning speech representations using self-attention networks (SANs). This approach builds upon the success of existing models like BERT and XLNet in natural language processing. The authors highlight two main contributions: first, by shuffling the order of speech frames, Speech-XLNet acts as a regularizer that promotes the SAN's focus on global structures through attention mechanisms. Second, it allows the model to leverage bi-directional context while maintaining an autoregressive training framework.

The authors argue that traditional autoregressive (AR) methods have limitations, including difficulties in capturing global structures and a lack of bi-directional context. In contrast, Speech-XLNet employs a permutation-based approach to maximize the expected log likelihood of a speech feature sequence, thus integrating the benefits of both autoregressive and autoencoding models.

Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in performance on the TIMIT dataset, with a relative reduction of 15.2% in phone error rate. Additionally, when applied to an end-to-end SAN model on the WSJ dataset, the word error rate decreased by up to 68% with limited labeled data. The authors also provide visualizations indicating enhanced generalization capabilities of their method compared to conventional approaches.

In summary, Speech-XLNet represents a novel advancement in unsupervised acoustic model pretraining, effectively combining autoregressive and bi-directional learning strategies to enhance speech recognition performance.
