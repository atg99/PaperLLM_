The proposed Speech-XLNet introduces several key modifications to the original XLNet framework to enhance its performance in ASR. Firstly, it shifts from a classification to a regression task by focusing on masking and reconstructing speech frames rather than word tokens. This adaptation allows for a more nuanced understanding of acoustic features. Secondly, a dynamic permutation strategy is employed during training, where the permutation order of speech sequences is regenerated for each instance, effectively increasing the diversity of training data and improving model robustness.

Speech-XLNet captures bi-directional context information more effectively than previous approaches, such as the LSTM-based pretraining scheme, by jointly learning forward and backward contexts within a single model. This integration results in a deeper representation of acoustic frames.

The pretraining objective function of Speech-XLNet is designed to predict the next acoustic frame based on previous frames in a permutation order. The model utilizes a smooth Mean Absolute Error loss to facilitate convergence during training. Furthermore, a two-stream self-attention mechanism is implemented to enhance target-aware representations, addressing ambiguities in target prediction.

Experiments on the TIMIT dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed model, highlighting its architecture and training strategies. The encoder consists of six self-attention blocks, optimized with specific configurations and hyperparameters. Pretraining utilized a variety of corpora, and the model was subjected to extensive training steps to ensure robust performance before fine-tuning for specific ASR tasks. Overall, Speech-XLNet represents a significant advancement in leveraging deep learning for acoustic modeling in speech recognition applications.
