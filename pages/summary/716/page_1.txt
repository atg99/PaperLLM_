The paper presents an enhanced analysis of policy gradient (PG) methods, natural policy gradient (NPG) methods, and their variance-reduced variants, focusing on their convergence properties under general smooth policy parametrizations. The authors establish that when the Fisher information matrix is positive definite, a leading variance-reduced PG method not only reaches stationary points but also converges to the global optimal value, accounting for inherent function approximation errors tied to policy parametrization. They demonstrate that NPG exhibits lower sample complexity compared to traditional methods. 

Additionally, the authors introduce a new approach called SRVR-NPG, which integrates variance reduction into the NPG framework, enhancing both global convergence and efficiency in finite sample complexity. The analysis reveals a beneficial interplay between PG and NPG convergence: insights from stationary convergence in PG can enhance NPG convergence, while global convergence results from NPG can inform variance-reduced PG methods.

The introduction underscores the importance of PG methods in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly their role in optimizing policies in Markov decision processes (MDPs) and their empirical successes fueled by advancements in deep learning. Despite these successes, the paper points out a gap in theoretical convergence guarantees for PG methods, particularly regarding non-asymptotic global convergence. The authors aim to address this gap through their improved analyses and methodologies.
