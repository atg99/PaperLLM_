The paper discusses convergence guarantees for various policy gradient methods used in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). It compares the sample complexities of several algorithms, including Natural Policy Gradient (NPG), Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO), and others, in terms of their efficiency to achieve global optimality while accounting for function approximation errors. The results indicate that different methods have varying complexities, quantified by parameters such as the variance of gradient estimators and importance weights.

The paper begins by defining the components of an MDP, including state and action spaces, transition probabilities, and reward functions. The agent's objective is to maximize the expected discounted return by finding the optimal policy, which is typically parameterized using deep neural networks due to the large state and action spaces.

The authors emphasize the distinction between finding stationary points in the optimization landscape and achieving significant improvements in the expected return. They aim to find parameters that not only satisfy the stationary condition but also lead to a return close to the optimal value, with a focus on the inherent errors due to the policy's expressive limitations. The paper thus highlights the challenges in achieving global optimality in policy optimization within the context of MDPs.
