The paper discusses the global convergence rates of various stochastic optimization algorithms, specifically focusing on Policy Gradient (PG), Natural Policy Gradient (NPG), and their variants, including Stochastic Variance Reduced Policy Gradient (SRVR-PG) and SRVR-NPG. 

Key results include:

1. **Low Variance Estimation**: When the parameters N and H are sufficiently large, the estimator wk becomes a low-variance estimator of the expected return, rJH(θk), leading to improved convergence properties.

2. **Convergence Rates**: Theorems 4.6, 4.9, 4.11, and 4.13 provide specific convergence rates under different conditions and parameter choices, highlighting the number of trajectories required for convergence based on the variance of the gradient estimator and the target accuracy.

3. **Sample Complexity Improvements**: The results show that the proposed methods reduce sample complexity compared to previous works. For instance, Theorem 4.9 improves the sample complexity of NPG by allowing a constant step size, contrasting with prior work that used a diminishing step size.

4. **SRVR-PG and SRVR-NPG**: The theorems establish the global convergence of these algorithms, demonstrating their advantages in terms of sample complexity and convergence speed, particularly in comparison to standard stochastic PG methods.

Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed algorithms are more efficient and provide better convergence guarantees than earlier methodologies, with detailed proofs and parameter settings provided in the appendix.
