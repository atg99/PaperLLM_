In this paper, the authors present a detailed analysis of a newly introduced algorithm, SRVR-NPG, which enhances the performance of the existing SRVR-PG method by demonstrating improved dependence on the parameter W. However, they note that the current sample complexity of SRVR-NPG does not surpass that of the improved natural policy gradient (NPG) due to the trade-off between the benefits of variance reduction and the computational cost of solving subproblems.

The authors conduct numerical experiments to compare the performance of several algorithms: stochastic PG, NPG, SRVR-PG, and SRVR-NPG, using benchmark reinforcement learning tasks such as Cartpole and Mountain Car. They implement a Gaussian policy with neural networks to model the mean, and the results indicate that SRVR-NPG performs best in the Cartpole task, while NPG outperforms others in the Mountain Car task.

In the concluding remarks, the authors emphasize their contribution to the understanding of global convergence in policy gradient methods and their variance-reduced variants, establishing sample complexity for stochastic PG and SRVR-PG, while improving that of NPG. The introduction of SRVR-NPG is highlighted as a significant advancement, providing both convergence guarantees and efficient sample complexity, and the analysis is positioned as a foundation for future developments in faster variance-reduced NPG methods.
