The paper discusses the concept of transferred compatible function approximation error in reinforcement learning, particularly within the context of natural policy gradient (NPG) methods. Assumption 4.4 establishes that the error in approximating the advantage function can be quantified by a bias term, which varies based on the parametrization used for the policy. Specifically, when using softmax parametrization, the bias is zero, while restricted parametrizations often lead to a positive bias due to their inability to represent all stochastic policies. 

Section 4.1 introduces a general framework for analyzing the global convergence of NPG algorithms. Proposition 4.5 outlines how the convergence rates of these algorithms are related to their stationary convergence rates and the discrepancies between their update directions and the exact NPG update directions. The proposition derives an inequality connecting the expected performance of the optimal policy with the average performance of the iterates generated by the algorithm. 

Key components of the analysis include the smoothness of the score function, the Performance Difference Lemma, and the implications of Assumption 4.4 on the advantage term. The results indicate that the overall error in the convergence process can be broken down into contributions from function approximation error, stationary convergence, and the accuracy of the update directions. The paper concludes by emphasizing the importance of these factors in ensuring effective convergence in NPG methods, with specific examples provided to illustrate the theoretical findings.
