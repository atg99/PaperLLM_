In this section, the paper discusses natural policy gradient (NPG) methods as a solution to the optimization problem outlined in a previous section. The policy gradient (PG) method is introduced, where data from a sampled trajectory under a policy is utilized to perform stochastic PG ascent updates. The update formula is presented, highlighting the use of a step size and the number of trajectories to estimate the policy gradient.

The paper details common unbiased estimators of the policy gradient, including REINFORCE and the GPOMDP method. The GPOMDP estimator is expressed mathematically, emphasizing its unbiasedness when the expectation of an infinite sum exists. A truncated version of GPOMDP is also discussed, which serves as a biased estimate of the policy gradient, with the bias diminishing as the trajectory length increases.

The paper further introduces the NPG method, which integrates a preconditioning matrix, leading to a modified update rule that incorporates the Fisher information matrix. This update is defined in terms of a state visitation measure induced by the policy and initial distribution. Additionally, the NPG update can be reformulated to minimize a specific error related to function approximation, providing a deeper insight into the method's mechanics. The discussion of state-action visitation measures is included to clarify the behavior of the policy under consideration.
