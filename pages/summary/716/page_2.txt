The paper discusses recent advancements in the global convergence of policy gradient (PG) methods in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on non-asymptotic global convergence from any initialization. It highlights the significance of converging to globally optimal policies, a concept that extends beyond the traditional first-order stationary-point convergence. While recent research has shown that certain nonconvex landscapes can lead to global optimality, the global convergence properties of variance-reduced PG methods have not been adequately established.

The authors present a framework aimed at enhancing the convergence of both PG and natural PG (NPG) methods, including their variance-reduced variants. The main contributions of the paper are threefold: 
1. Establishing global convergence for the variance-reduced PG method SRVR-PG, accounting for function approximation errors.
2. Improving the global convergence rate of NPG methods from O(ε^4) to O(ε^3).
3. Introducing a new variance-reduced algorithm based on NPG with demonstrated global convergence and efficient sample complexity.

These improvements rely on an assumption regarding the positive definiteness of the Fisher information matrix associated with the policy parametrization. The paper also reviews related work on global convergence in PG methods, noting various studies that have addressed the convergence to globally optimal policies and the incorporation of variance reduction techniques to enhance sample efficiency.

In summary, the paper provides significant insights into improving the convergence properties of PG methods, addressing gaps in existing literature, and establishing a clearer understanding of the effectiveness of variance-reduced approaches in achieving global optimality in RL.
