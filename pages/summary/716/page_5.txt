The paper discusses the Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) update direction denoted as wk, which is derived from a stochastic optimization problem using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD). It introduces a standing assumption about the Fisher information matrix, asserting that it behaves well as a preconditioner in the NPG update. This assumption is critical for the convergence of preconditioned algorithms, applicable in both convex and nonconvex optimization contexts.

The paper highlights that certain policy parameterizations, particularly Gaussian policies, can satisfy the assumption regarding the Fisher information matrix. The positive definiteness of this matrix is a common requirement in the literature, contributing to the establishment of Riemannian metrics in the parameter space and supporting the convergence properties of natural gradient methods.

Moreover, the paper compares sample complexities, indicating that under the stated assumption, NPG achieves a better sample complexity of O(ε^(-3)) for global convergence than the existing O(ε^(-4)) complexity. It also acknowledges that other methods, like Policy Gradient (PG) and its variance-reduced variant, SRVR-PG, exhibit global convergence without explicit reliance on the Fisher information matrix.

The paper introduces SRVR-PG, which enhances the sample complexity to O(ε^(-1.5)), and provides a positive affirmation of its global convergence. Additionally, it presents a new algorithm, SRVR-NPG, which integrates variance reduction into the NPG framework, promising comparable global convergence results to the improved NPG findings. The paper details the construction of a weighted gradient estimator used in SRVR-NPG, emphasizing the methodology to reduce variance effectively.
