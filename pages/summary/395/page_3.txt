The paper discusses the architecture of the YOLO Nano network, which is designed for efficient object detection in edge and mobile scenarios. It introduces a constrained optimization problem aimed at generating networks that maximize a universal performance function while adhering to specific requirements. The generator G is optimized iteratively, starting from an initial solution and progressively improving it to meet constraints defined by an indicator function.

Key constraints include a mean average precision of at least 65% on the VOC 2007 dataset, a computational cost of no more than 5 billion operations, and the use of 8-bit weight precision. The computational cost is specifically set to ensure that YOLO Nano remains more efficient than Tiny YOLOv3, a widely used compact network.

A distinctive feature of YOLO Nano is its use of a residual projection-expansion-projection (PEP) macroarchitecture, differing from previous YOLO models. The PEP architecture includes a projection layer to reduce dimensionality, an expansion layer to increase channel numbers, a depth-wise convolution for spatial processing, and another projection layer to lower dimensionality again. This design aims to enhance both efficiency and object detection performance, ensuring the network is compact yet effective for embedded applications.
