This paper investigates the effectiveness of data augmentation techniques in enhancing the robust accuracy of neural networks against adversarial attacks, particularly on the CIFAR-10 dataset. The authors present their findings in the context of previous research, highlighting the advancements in robust test accuracy achieved through their method, which improves upon existing state-of-the-art techniques.

The study reveals that their approach, which incorporates data augmentations without external data, surpasses the robust accuracy of prior models. Specifically, they report a robust accuracy of 60.07%, a 2.93% improvement over the previous best performance by Gowal et al. The authors identify CutMix as the most effective augmentation method. Unlike previous studies that explored data augmentation without success, the authors demonstrate that their techniques effectively enhance robustness across various architectures and datasets.

The paper also delves into the concepts of robust overfitting and underfitting, explaining why certain techniques, such as MixUp, perform poorly compared to spatial composition methods. Additionally, it discusses the role of weight averaging in leveraging data augmentation by ensembling model snapshots.

In the context of adversarial attacks, the paper reviews the evolution of methods designed to craft adversarial examples and the significance of adversarial training as a defense mechanism. It critiques prior research on data augmentation, noting that while traditional methods improve generalization in standard training, they have been less effective in adversarially robust training.

Overall, the authors provide empirical evidence supporting the effectiveness of their approach while situating their contributions within the broader landscape of adversarial robustness research.
