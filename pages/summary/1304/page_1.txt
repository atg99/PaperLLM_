In the paper "Data Augmentation Can Improve Robustness," the authors address the issue of robust overfitting in adversarial training, where the robust test accuracy declines during training. They propose that common data augmentation techniques, when combined with model weight averaging, can significantly enhance robust accuracy, countering previous findings that suggested otherwise. The study compares various data augmentation methods and finds that spatial composition techniques yield the best results for adversarial training.

The authors conduct evaluations on the CIFAR-10 dataset against `1 and `2 norm-bounded perturbations, achieving notable improvements in robust accuracy—+2.93% and +2.16%, respectively—compared to state-of-the-art methods. Specifically, their model achieves 60.07% robust accuracy against `1 norm-bounded perturbations without utilizing external data. Additionally, the approach demonstrates significant performance gains across other architectures and datasets, including CIFAR-100, SVHN, and TinyImageNet.

The introduction highlights the inherent fragility of neural networks to adversarial perturbations and discusses the evolution of defenses, particularly robust optimization techniques that aim to enhance model robustness. The authors emphasize the importance of addressing the dichotomy in existing literature regarding the effectiveness of data augmentation for improving adversarial robustness, ultimately advocating for a revised training procedure that incorporates both data augmentation and model weight averaging to mitigate robust overfitting.
