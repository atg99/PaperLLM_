In this section, the manuscript outlines the foundational concepts relevant to adversarial training, robust overfitting, and model weight averaging. 

Adversarial training, as formulated by Madry et al., seeks to minimize adversarial risk by solving a saddle point problem. This involves finding model parameters that minimize expected losses over adversarially perturbed inputs. The Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) method is utilized to compute adversarial perturbations, which helps in enhancing model robustness against attacks.

The concept of robust overfitting is introduced, highlighting a key difference from standard training. While typical training may not exhibit overfitting, adversarial training often leads to robust overfitting, where robust accuracy on the test set declines even as it improves on the training set. To mitigate this, Rice et al. propose early stopping, which has been shown to yield more robust models compared to other techniques like data augmentation or increased regularization. Interestingly, introducing external data can eliminate robust overfitting entirely.

Model weight averaging (WA) is presented as a technique that utilizes an exponential moving average of model parameters to enhance robustness during evaluation. Research by Chen et al. and Gowal et al. indicates that WA can lead to a flatter adversarial loss landscape, thereby reducing the generalization gap. However, it is noted that WA can still be susceptible to robust overfitting, as older model parameters are progressively forgotten during training, which may adversely affect robust accuracy over time. 

Overall, this section sets the stage for further exploration of these concepts in subsequent sections, particularly in terms of experimental validation.
