The paper presents an analysis of the robust accuracy of various neural network architectures on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and TINY-IMAGE NET using different augmentation techniques, specifically Pad & Crop and CutMix. The results indicate that CutMix consistently outperforms Pad & Crop across various architectures in terms of both clean and robust accuracy. For instance, the WRN-70-16 architecture achieves a robust accuracy of 60.07% with CutMix compared to 57.17% with Pad & Crop.

In the context of model ensembling, the study demonstrates that ensembling early-stopped models can enhance robust accuracy significantly. For example, ensembling two WRN-28-10 models trained with CutMix yields a robust accuracy of 56.35%, a notable improvement over individual models. The paper posits that the diversity introduced by augmentation techniques like CutMix contributes to better ensemble performance.

Furthermore, the authors discuss the efficiency of model weight averaging as an alternative to traditional ensembling methods. By leveraging the linearity of models trained with adversarial techniques, weight averaging can effectively combine model iterations from a single training run, thus optimizing computational resources while still enhancing robustness.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of data augmentation and model ensembling techniques in improving the robustness of neural networks against adversarial attacks.
