The paper investigates the effects of various data augmentation techniques on robust test accuracy against adversarial attacks in the context of CIFAR-10, using a WideResNet-28-10 model. Specifically, it examines the performances of MixUp, Cutout, and CutMix under different configurations. The findings reveal that while MixUp's robust accuracy decreases with increased mixing rates, Cutout and CutMix show improved performance with larger window lengths, peaking at a length of 20. This suggests that spatial composition techniques are more effective than blending techniques like MixUp in adversarial training, as they better preserve low-level features crucial for robustness.

Further analysis demonstrates that CutMix consistently outperforms the Pad & Crop baseline across various model architectures, showing an increase in robust accuracy by at least 2.90% for WideResNet and 1.76% for ResNet models. The evaluation extends to different threat models and datasets, where CutMix also shows significant improvements in robust accuracy against both `1-norm and `2-norm bounded perturbations. Notably, on CIFAR-100 and TINYIMAGENET, the proposed methods achieve state-of-the-art results, underscoring the generalizability and effectiveness of the augmentation techniques in enhancing adversarial robustness. The paper concludes that augmentations designed for robustness should focus on preserving low-level features to mitigate underfitting in adversarial contexts.
