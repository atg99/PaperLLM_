The referenced works cover a range of topics related to the robustness of deep neural networks against adversarial attacks. Papernot et al. discuss the use of distillation as a defense mechanism, while Polyak introduces methods to enhance convergence in iterative algorithms. Qin et al. explore local linearization for adversarial robustness, and Rice et al. examine overfitting in adversarially robust deep learning. 

The Imagenet challenge, reported by Russakovsky et al., highlights large-scale visual recognition, which is foundational for understanding model performance. Santurkar et al. focus on image synthesis using robust classifiers, and Song et al. address privacy risks linked to securing machine learning models. Ensemble methods as a defense strategy are discussed by Strauss et al., while Szegedy et al. reveal intriguing properties of neural networks. 

Takahashi et al. present random image cropping and patching for data augmentation, and Torralba et al. provide a large dataset for non-parametric recognition. Tramèr et al. investigate ensemble adversarial training, emphasizing the interplay between attacks and defenses. Uesato and colleagues raise concerns about evaluating against weak attacks and question the necessity of labels for improving robustness. 

Wu et al. highlight the benefits of adversarial weight perturbation for generalization, while Xie et al. propose feature denoising techniques. Yun et al. introduce CutMix as a regularization strategy, and Zagoruyko and Komodakis present wide residual networks. Finally, Zhai et al. assert that robust generalization requires more unlabeled data, and Zhang et al. stress the need to rethink generalization in deep learning. Collectively, these works contribute to advancing the understanding and development of defenses against adversarial perturbations in deep learning.
