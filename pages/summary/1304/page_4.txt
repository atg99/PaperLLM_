The paper investigates the impact of model weight averaging (WA) on the robust accuracy of adversarially trained Wide ResNet (WRN)-28-10 models, specifically on the CIFAR-10 dataset. The findings are presented in three panels, each illustrating different scenarios of training with and without external data and the effects of WA.

In panel (a), the authors highlight the issue of robust overfitting when using additional external data from the 80M-T I dataset. Panel (b) demonstrates the benefits of WA, showing that it mitigates robust overfitting and enhances robust test accuracy. Panel (c) indicates that WA remains effective even when robust overfitting is not present, suggesting that WA contributes positively to robustness.

The authors hypothesize that WA fosters robustness to a greater extent when the robust accuracy remains stable across iterations. This is supported by the observation that WA functions as a temporal ensemble, similar to Fast Geometric Ensembling, which aggregates parameters from multiple checkpoints during training. The discussion acknowledges the limited success of ensembling in adversarial training, referencing prior work that shows ensembling can help reduce gradient obfuscation.

The paper also addresses data augmentations, specifically focusing on limiting robust overfitting without external data. It cites research by Rice et al. that indicates combining augmentations like Cutout or MixUp with early stopping does not yield better robustness compared to early stopping alone. However, these methods can slow down robust overfitting compared to traditional adversarial training techniques.

To test their hypothesis, the authors compare the effects of WA on robustness when using MixUp versus a standard method (Pad & Crop). The results show that MixUp with WA consistently outperforms Pad & Crop, indicating that WA enhances robust accuracy even when the non-averaged MixUp model initially shows lower "best" robust accuracy. This suggests that maintaining performance across iterations is crucial for improving robustness in adversarial training.
