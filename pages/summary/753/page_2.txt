This paper presents a novel model architecture for translating Spanish speech into English while also enabling voice transfer. The architecture consists of three main components: a sequence-to-sequence network that generates target spectrograms, a vocoder that converts these spectrograms into time-domain waveforms, and an optional speaker encoder that conditions the decoder on the source speaker's identity. 

The encoder is built with eight layers of bidirectional LSTMs that process 80-channel log-mel spectrogram features. An attention mechanism aligns these hidden states to guide an autoregressive decoder, which predicts log spectrogram frames for translated speech. Auxiliary decoders predict phoneme sequences for both source and target languages, enhancing the model's ability to learn attention during training. 

Key modifications to the Tacotron 2 TTS model are made, including the use of multi-head attention and a narrower pre-net bottleneck to improve performance in this speech-to-speech translation task. The model employs multitask training, integrating auxiliary decoders to aid in phoneme prediction, although these are not used during inference.

Experiments utilize two Spanish-to-English datasets: a large conversational corpus and the more challenging Spanish Fisher corpus. The performance is evaluated using BLEU scores to measure translation quality and intelligibility, alongside listening tests for assessing speech naturalness and speaker similarity. The results indicate that the proposed model effectively translates speech while maintaining speaker identity, demonstrating its potential for cross-language voice conversion.
