This paper evaluates the performance of a speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) model using various auxiliary losses and different datasets. The experiments focus on the conversational and Fisher Spanish-to-English tasks, utilizing a Tacotron 2 text-to-speech (TTS) model and Parallel WaveNet for synthesizing speech. 

In the conversational test set, different combinations of auxiliary losses were analyzed, revealing that training without auxiliary losses resulted in poor performance, with the model only able to translate simple phrases. The integration of auxiliary phoneme recognition tasks significantly improved the model's attention mechanism and overall performance, with a peak BLEU score of 42.7 when using both source and target auxiliary losses. However, a gap of 6 BLEU points remained compared to the baseline, indicating further potential for improvement.

For the Fisher Spanish-to-English dataset, the model again demonstrated enhanced performance with the use of auxiliary losses, achieving a BLEU score of 30.1 when combining source and target losses and leveraging encoder pre-training. The results highlighted the challenges of alignment learning without robust supervision.

Subjective evaluations of the synthesis quality were conducted using Mean Opinion Score (MOS) ratings, showing that models utilizing WavernN vocoders achieved significantly higher ratings compared to those using the Griffin-Lim vocoder. This suggests that the choice of vocoder plays a crucial role in the perceived naturalness of the synthesized speech.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of auxiliary losses in enhancing the performance of end-to-end S2ST models while revealing areas that require further development to close the performance gap with traditional methods.
