The paper presents Translatotron, an attention-based sequence-to-sequence neural network designed for direct speech-to-speech translation (S2ST). Unlike conventional systems that rely on an intermediate text representation through automatic speech recognition, machine translation, and text-to-speech synthesis, Translatotron aims to translate speech from one language to another in an end-to-end manner. This approach mitigates the compounding errors associated with cascaded systems and reduces computational demands and inference latency.

The model is trained to map speech spectrograms from a source language to target language spectrograms, enabling the synthesis of translated speech that retains the original speaker's voice. Despite the challenges posed by the need for extensive input-output speech pairs and alignment difficulties, the model demonstrates the feasibility of direct S2ST. Although it slightly underperforms compared to a baseline cascaded system, it serves as a proof of concept for further research.

The architecture includes multitask training with speech-to-text tasks to facilitate training without predefined alignments, although no text representation is used during inference. The paper also highlights previous research on integrating various components in cascaded systems and contrasts the complexities of S2ST with simpler voice conversion tasks. Ultimately, the study emphasizes the potential of direct S2ST models while acknowledging the need for further advancements in the field.
