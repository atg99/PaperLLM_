This paper presents a direct speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) model that is trained end-to-end, emphasizing the importance of using speech transcripts during training while eliminating the need for intermediate transcription during inference. The model performs well on two Spanish-to-English datasets but does not outperform a baseline cascade of speech translation (ST) and text-to-speech (TTS) models. 

The paper discusses the performance of Translatotron, highlighting its tendency to mispronounce words and retain source language features, which can detract from naturalness in the target language. The authors compare various voice transfer methods, noting that conditioning on the source speaker's voice yields lower similarity scores compared to using ground truth target embeddings. The challenges of cross-language voice transfer are acknowledged, and potential improvements are suggested, such as enhancing the speaker encoder and exploring multitask learning.

The results indicate that while the model achieves high translation quality, further research is needed to improve performance in voice transfer and to explore alternative training techniques. Future work could involve using weak supervision, cycle-consistency terms, and transferring prosodic elements from source to translated speech.
