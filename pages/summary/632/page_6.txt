The paper outlines an experimental setup for a study utilizing deep learning techniques on multi-modal 3D medical images. The experiments were conducted using MATLAB's Deep Learning Toolbox and Image Processing Toolbox on a Hewlett-Packard Z6 G4 Workstation. The data used was sourced from The Retrospective Image Registration Evaluation (RIRE) dataset, specifically utilizing T1 weighted MRI images as fixed images and corresponding CT images as moving images. 

A critical pre-processing step involved manually removing artefacts to reduce error in automatic methods, followed by resizing images to 256x256x26. The dataset was split into training and test sets, with 60% allocated for training and 40% for testing. Data augmentation was performed through random rigid body transformations, generating synthetic images to enhance the dataset while maintaining the original training/test allocation.

The performance of the regression model was evaluated using regression plots and R2 values, indicating the variance explained by the model. Results suggested that model accuracy stabilized at around 94 synthetic images, leading to the decision to generate 99 synthetic images for each original dataset image. The paper effectively details the methodology and reasoning behind the experimental design, ensuring a robust framework for the deep learning model applied to medical imaging.
