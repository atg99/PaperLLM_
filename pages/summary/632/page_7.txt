The paper discusses the generation of ground truth data for evaluating image registration processes, specifically focusing on the accuracy of a proposed ground truth generation method. A manual assessment was conducted by a radiologist who evaluated 200 randomly selected image pairs from an augmented dataset of 1600 pairs. The assessment compared two methods of registration, revealing that 39.5% of the time the processes were similar, while the mathematical optimization with symmetry alignment performed better in 32.5% of cases, particularly when the orientations of images differed significantly.

The study also explores multi-modal image registration by selecting the best regression model for predicting registration parameters. Several models were compared, including a full 3D DCNN and others such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). The ANN was ultimately chosen due to its superior R2 value of 0.998.

Furthermore, the performance of the proposed registration method was compared against existing methods using various metrics, including the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC), Jaccard Similarity Coefficient (JSC), and Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM). These metrics, along with the elapsed processing time, were utilized to assess the accuracy and efficiency of the registration outcomes. Visual results illustrated the differences in registration with and without symmetry alignment, emphasizing the effectiveness of the proposed method in specific scenarios.
