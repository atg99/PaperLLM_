The paper discusses a method for processing MRI and CT images using a combination of feature extraction through a 3D Deep Convolutional Neural Network (DCNN) and regression analysis via an Artificial Neural Network (ANN). A total of 128 features were extracted from each image, resulting in 256 input neurons for the ANN. The ANN consisted of one hidden layer with 10 neurons and aimed to predict six transformation parameters. The architecture utilized hyperbolic tangent sigmoid transfer functions in the hidden layer and linear transfer functions in the output layer, with Bayesian regularization backpropagation selected as the training algorithm based on performance comparisons with other methods.

Additionally, to automate the process of identifying image modalities, the authors adapted the 3D DCNN architecture by replacing the fully connected layer with a softmax layer and modifying the regression layer to a classification output layer, implementing a cross-entropy loss function for training. The paper illustrates the overall architecture and methodology for effective image modality identification and parameter prediction.
