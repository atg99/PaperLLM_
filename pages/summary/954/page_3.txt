The research demonstrates that models exhibiting superior in-distribution calibration also perform well in out-of-distribution benchmarks. This suggests that focusing on in-distribution performance can enhance out-of-distribution outcomes. Various strategies for improving model calibration have been explored, including post-hoc rescaling of predictions, averaging multiple predictions, and data augmentation. However, this paper specifically examines the intrinsic calibration properties of advanced model families, rather than additional enhancement methods.

Temperature scaling is highlighted as a baseline method for intrinsic calibration, as it effectively improves calibration with minimal cost. It adjusts a model's logits through a single parameter to optimize likelihood on a held-out training dataset, without affecting classification accuracy. Miscalibration often stems from average over- or underconfidence, which temperature scaling helps to normalize, thus revealing trends in calibration across models.

The paper addresses the multi-class classification problem, defining a well-calibrated model as one that accurately quantifies predictive uncertainty. The authors focus on top-label calibration, which requires the model's predicted probabilities to reflect the true likelihood of the most likely label. The Expected Calibration Error (ECE) serves as the primary measure of miscalibration, assessing the discrepancy between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes. Due to its estimation challenges, ECE is typically calculated using a bucket-based approach, where predictions are binned, and the accuracy and confidence are averaged within each bucket. The paper also discusses the statistical properties of this estimator and potential pitfalls.
