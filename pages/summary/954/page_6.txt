The paper investigates the relationship between pretraining data size, model architecture, and the calibration of various neural network families, specifically focusing on BiT models pretrained on different datasets: IMAGE NET, IMAGE NET-21K, and JFT-300. The findings reveal that while increased pretraining data correlates with higher accuracy, it does not consistently improve calibration, as evidenced by similar expected calibration error (ECE) values across these pretraining datasets. 

The results suggest that the differences in calibration between model families cannot be solely attributed to the amount of pretraining. Instead, model architecture, particularly spatial inductive bias, appears to play a significant role. MLP-Mixer and ViT models demonstrated superior calibration compared to others, remaining more stable under distribution shifts, such as those tested on the IMAGE NET-C dataset. 

Moreover, the paper notes that larger models generally exhibit worse calibration in-distribution, but this trend reverses out-of-distribution, indicating that larger models might be more robust to distribution shifts. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of model architecture in achieving both high accuracy and effective calibration, particularly in safety-critical applications where uncertainty estimates are crucial.
