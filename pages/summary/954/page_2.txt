This paper systematically compares recent image classification models, focusing on their accuracy, calibration, and design features. Key findings include:

1. Current top models like MLP-Mixer and Vision Transformers demonstrate better calibration compared to earlier models and show robustness to distribution shifts.
2. In-distribution calibration slightly declines with increased model size, but this is outweighed by gains in accuracy.
3. Calibration improves with model size under distribution shift, contrasting with in-distribution trends.
4. A correlation exists between accuracy and calibration under distribution shift, indicating that optimizing for accuracy can also enhance calibration.
5. Differences in calibration properties among model families cannot be solely attributed to model size, pretraining duration, or dataset size.

The results imply that advancements in model accuracy will likely continue to enhance calibration, emphasizing architecture's role in calibration outcomes. The study provides code and a comprehensive dataset of calibration measurements from 180 distinct models across 16 families, evaluated on 79 ImageNet-scale datasets with 28 metric variants.

The paper also discusses model calibration measures, primarily focusing on Expected Calibration Error (ECE), which quantifies the difference between predictive confidence and accuracy. It highlights the challenges in accurately estimating ECE and reviews various alternatives and visual representations of model calibration. 

Empirical studies referenced indicate that while larger models have historically been associated with worse calibration, recent findings suggest that this trend is diminishing in current models, particularly under distribution shifts. The paper underscores the importance of understanding calibration, especially as model sizes and complexities grow.
