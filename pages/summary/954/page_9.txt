The paper discusses various calibration metrics for evaluating machine learning models, specifically focusing on negative log-likelihood (NLL) and Brier score, alongside expected calibration error (ECE). It highlights the strong correlation between these metrics and classification error, suggesting that the calibration performance of models can be more nuanced than simple accuracy measures. 

The analysis includes residuals of NLL and Brier score after adjusting for classification error, revealing models that perform better or worse in calibration than expected based on their accuracy. The paper notes that biases in calibration estimates can lead to inconsistent rankings of models, influenced by the choice of ECE variant and bin size. This inconsistency is particularly pronounced for models with varying levels of accuracy, potentially leading to contradictory conclusions regarding the relationship between model accuracy and calibration. 

Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of careful selection of calibration metrics and methods in model evaluation to avoid misleading interpretations of model performance.
