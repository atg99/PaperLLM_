The paper presents an empirical evaluation of various state-of-the-art image classification models, focusing on their accuracy and calibration performance on the ImageNet dataset. It includes a comparison of models such as MLP-Mixer, ViT, BiT, ResNeXt WSL, SimCLR, CLIP, and AlexNet, highlighting their respective architectures and training methodologies. 

The left side of Figure 1 illustrates the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) against classification error for these models, with marker sizes indicating relative model sizes. The right side shows confidence distributions and reliability diagrams for selected models, suggesting that some modern architectures achieve both high accuracy and good calibration.

The study encompasses both convolutional and non-convolutional models, including those trained through various paradigms like supervised, weakly supervised, unsupervised, and zero-shot learning. Datasets used for evaluation include the ImageNet validation set and several out-of-distribution benchmarks, such as ImageNetV2, ImageNet-C, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-A, which test robustness and generalization capabilities.

For calibration, the authors implement post-hoc recalibration techniques, using a subset of the validation set to fit temperature scaling parameters, while ensuring that reported metrics are based on an independent dataset portion. The findings underscore the importance of model calibration in addition to accuracy in the context of modern neural networks.
