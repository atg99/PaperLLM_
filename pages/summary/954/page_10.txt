The paper investigates the relationship between model calibration and classification accuracy in modern image models, particularly focusing on the impact of binning on expected calibration error (ECE). It highlights a near-linear relationship between ECE and classification error after temperature scaling, but notes that this relationship varies with the number of bins used for ECE estimation. Specifically, a larger number of bins can lead to higher variance and positive bias in ECE estimates, especially for models with average accuracies around 0.5. 

The authors provide a formal analysis showing that the bias in accuracy estimation is inversely proportional to the sample size in each bin and depends on the variance and covariance of accuracy and confidence. Higher accuracy models tend to exhibit lower bias, not only due to their accuracy but also because their outputs correlate more closely with the correct labels.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of jointly considering accuracy and calibration when evaluating model performance, as the rankings based solely on ECE can change while the Pareto-optimal models remain consistent. Alternative metrics like Brier score and negative log likelihood (NLL) are suggested for assessing model calibration without relying on ECE.

In conclusion, the study finds that modern image models are generally well-calibrated, irrespective of their focus on accuracy. It identifies architectural design as a significant factor influencing calibration, with MLP-Mixer and Vision Transformers emerging as notably well-calibrated models. This suggests potential calibration advantages of non-convolutional architectures, warranting further exploration of architectural influences on calibration and robustness to distribution shifts. The findings may indicate a shift away from the convolutional paradigm in computer vision.
