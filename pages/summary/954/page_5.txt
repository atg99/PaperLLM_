The paper focuses on the calibration of modern neural network models, specifically assessing Expected Calibration Error (ECE) using equal-mass binning across 100 bins. The authors explore in-distribution calibration on clean ImageNet images, revealing that recent models such as MLP-Mixer, ViT, and BiT exhibit improved accuracy and calibration compared to older models like AlexNet. This challenges previous assumptions that high accuracy correlates with poor calibration. The paper highlights that even zero-shot models like CLIP maintain good calibration alongside their accuracy.

The study further examines the effects of temperature scaling, which improves calibration across various model families. After applying temperature scaling, a clearer distinction in calibration properties emerges, suggesting that newer architectures remain better calibrated than older ones regardless of scaling. Interestingly, models pretrained without additional labels or with noisy labels generally show worse calibration.

The authors also investigate the impact of model size and pretraining on calibration. While larger models often display higher calibration error, they also achieve lower classification errors, indicating a tradeoff between accuracy and calibration. The findings suggest that model size alone cannot account for the calibration differences among families. Additionally, the study notes that transfer learning influences calibration, with implications for future research directions.
