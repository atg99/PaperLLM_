The paper examines the calibration and accuracy of various deep learning models on the ImageNet-C dataset, both before and after applying temperature scaling. The findings indicate that newer model architectures, such as MLP-Mixer and ViT, exhibit more robust calibration under distribution shifts compared to older models. The analysis reveals a consistent relationship between model performance on ImageNet-C and natural out-of-distribution datasets, where models that are Pareto-optimal on ImageNet-C maintain their optimal status on out-of-distribution datasets.

As corruption severity increases, larger models tend to experience slower increases in classification error, while changes in expected calibration error (ECE) are less pronounced. This suggests that models like MLP-Mixer and ViT can sustain their in-distribution calibration even when faced with significant distribution shifts. The paper posits that strong calibration on in-distribution data may correlate with good calibration on unanticipated out-of-distribution data.

Additionally, the authors explore the trade-offs between accuracy and calibration within model families, highlighting that different families occupy distinct Pareto sets in the accuracy/calibration space. This raises important considerations for practitioners in safety-critical applications when choosing between models that may differ in their calibration and accuracy performance.
