The paper discusses Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Twin Support Vector Machines (TWSVM) as methods for classification, particularly in scenarios where data samples are not linearly separable. 

In the SVM section, the optimization problem is formulated to minimize a quadratic function subject to constraints that ensure correct classification of the training data. The complexity of this optimization is noted to be O(l^3), which arises from the need to solve a large quadratic programming problem involving all data samples.

The TWSVM introduces a binary classification model that employs kernel functions to transform data into a higher-dimensional space for better separation. Two kernel-generated surfaces are defined, and the optimization problems for TWSVM are presented, focusing on minimizing specific quadratic forms while adhering to constraints that involve slack variables. The use of Lagrange multipliers and the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions is mentioned as a method to derive the dual forms of these optimization problems.

Overall, the paper highlights the complexity and formulation of both SVM and TWSVM, emphasizing their utility in handling non-linear classification tasks.
