The paper by M. Tanveer et al. discusses advancements in support vector machine (SVM) algorithms aimed at addressing challenges related to imbalanced datasets. The authors introduce a hybrid re-sampling technique that combines the one side selection (OSS) algorithm with the synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE) to enhance training data balance, integrated with the twin support vector machine (TWSVM) for classification. They highlight potential overfitting issues with the SMOTE algorithm and present robust rescaled hinge loss TWSVM and density weighted TWSVM as solutions that improve performance.

Xu et al. in 2016 proposed the universum-TWSVM (U-TWSVM), which utilizes prior knowledge from universum data to enhance generalization capability through two hinge loss functions. This method demonstrated superior accuracy and reduced running time compared to other algorithms. In 2017, Xu introduced the maximum margin twin spheres SVM (MMTSSVM) to address TWSVM limitations by determining two concentric spheres that effectively capture positive samples while repelling negative ones.

In 2018, Richhariya et al. enhanced universum TWSVM by incorporating a regularization term to improve generalization and training efficiency. Their improved universum TWSVM outperformed both USVM and UTWSVM. The following year, Richhariya and Tanveer developed a fuzzy universum SVM (FUSVM), which integrates information entropy to enhance efficiency, especially in noise-free conditions, and proposed fuzzy universum TWSVM (FUTWSVM) with similar advantages.

More recently, they introduced the reduced universum twin support vector machine for class imbalance learning (RUTWSVM-CIL), which leverages prior distribution information and uses a reduced kernel matrix for large imbalanced datasets. Their numerical experiments validated its applicability. In 2020, they extended the universum based twin support vector machine to Alzheimer's disease classification. Additionally, they proposed fuzzy universum least squares TWSVM and robust TBSVM, which solve linear equations for faster optimization and enhance robustness against noise in imbalanced datasets.
