This section discusses various advancements in fuzzy twin support vector machines (TWSVM) aimed at improving classification performance, particularly in imbalanced datasets. In 2017, Chen and Wu introduced the novel fuzzy TWSVM (NFTWSVM), which utilizes fuzzy membership to address class imbalance, integrating fuzzy neural networks for more generalized outcomes. Richhariya and Tanveer, in 2019, presented a fuzzy universum SVM (FUSVM) based on information entropy, and subsequently proposed the robust fuzzy LS-TWSVM (RFLS-TWSVM-CIL), enhancing TWSVM's performance on imbalanced data by employing a fuzzy membership function that considers sample weights and imbalance ratios. While RFLS-TWSVM-CIL is computationally efficient, it relies on the positive semi-definiteness of matrix inverses.

To improve upon this, Ganaie et al. introduced the regularized robust fuzzy least squares TWSVM (RRFLSTWSVM), which eliminates the need for the positive semi-definiteness assumption, yielding better performance. Other contributions include Khemchandani et al.'s fuzzy LS version of TWSVC in 2018, which assigns fuzzy membership values to data points across clusters, and Chen et al.'s entropy-based fuzzy least squares TWSVM in 2020, which incorporates fuzzy membership based on entropy. Rezvani and Wang's intuitionistic fuzzy TWSVM (IFTWSVM) adapts fuzzy TWSVM by factoring in data position in feature space, enhancing noise resistance. Zhang and Li's approach assigns fuzzy membership based on intra-class hyperplanes, achieving superior accuracy compared to traditional methods.

In 2019, Gupta et al. developed the entropy-based fuzzy TWSVM (EFTWSVM-CIL) for imbalanced datasets, which utilizes sample entropy for fuzzy membership assignment. Chen et al. further refined this in 2020 with the entropy-based fuzzy least squares TWSVM (EFLSTWSVM), which formulates quadratic programming problems in a least squares context, demonstrating improved performance over existing fuzzy TWSVM algorithms.
