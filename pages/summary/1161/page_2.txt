The paper provides a comprehensive overview of Twin Support Vector Machines (TWSVM), an advanced classification technique derived from traditional Support Vector Machines (SVM). SVM, introduced by Cortes and Vapnik in 1995, is known for its capability to find optimal decision hyperplanes that classify data points into two distinct classes. It employs a quadratic programming problem to maximize the distance between support hyperplanes, thereby enhancing generalization and reducing training error.

Despite its effectiveness, SVM has limitations, particularly in handling complex datasets due to high computational costs and dependency on kernel functions. To address these challenges, TWSVM was developed as an improvement over Generalized Eigenvalue Proximal SVM (GEPSVM), which itself aimed to define two nonparallel hyperplanes through generalized eigenvalue problems. TWSVM offers a significant reduction in computational cost—approximately one-fourth that of SVM—by solving two smaller quadratic programming problems instead of one complex problem. This results in faster processing times and better generalization performance, especially beneficial for large datasets.

The paper highlights that while TWSVM has shown promising results, particularly in nonlinear classification scenarios, its performance remains contingent on the selection of appropriate kernel functions and their parameters. The authors note ongoing research and enhancements in TWSVM, indicating its potential for broader applications in various fields, as previously reviewed by Huang et al. in 2017.
