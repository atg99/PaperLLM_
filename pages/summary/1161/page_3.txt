The paper discusses Support Vector Regression (SVR), a variant of Support Vector Machine (SVM) tailored for regression tasks. SVR differs from SVM by establishing a margin of tolerance ε, allowing for acceptable errors within this deviation, thereby minimizing the overall error. However, SVR has a low learning speed due to the need to solve a large Quadratic Programming Problem (QPP). Various adaptations of SVR have been proposed to enhance its performance, including ε-SVR, Fuzzy SVM, and robust SVR, among others.

To address the high computational cost of SVR, Twin Support Vector Regression (TSVR) was introduced, which determines two non-parallel hyperplanes to optimize the regression function while solving a pair of smaller QPPs, thus improving speed. An improved version, TWSVR, was later developed to further enhance performance while aligning more closely with Twin Support Vector Machine (TWSVM) principles.

The paper highlights the rapid development and application of twin support vector classification and regression algorithms in real-world scenarios, yet notes the limited literature on TSVR, indicating a need for further exploration and refinement in this area. The paper aims to compile recent advancements in TWSVM and TSVR, assess their limitations and advantages, and encourage future research.

The structure of the paper includes an introduction to SVM and TWSVM, recent advancements and applications of TWSVM and TSVR, and concludes with a synopsis and future research directions.
