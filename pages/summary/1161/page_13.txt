The paper discusses various advancements in twin support vector machine (TWSVM) algorithms that utilize pinball loss to improve classification performance, particularly in noisy and imbalanced datasets. M. Tanveer et al. highlight the strengths of Pin-TWSVM, which demonstrates noise insensitivity and re-sampling stability, although it struggles with sparsity and high testing times. Xu et al. introduce Pin-M3HM, which enhances generalization by using maximum margin and minimum volume hyper-spheres, achieving faster and more accurate results compared to TWSVM. Sharma and Rastogi propose two models, ε-Pin-TWSVM and Flex-Pin-TWSVM, focusing on noise reduction and adaptive insensitivity zones.

Yang et al. present a method for TPMSVM that simplifies parameter tuning by solving one instance at a time analytically. The paper also addresses smooth pinball loss TWSVM to overcome differentiability issues and introduces a maximum margin twin spheres algorithm that balances complexity with performance. Sharma et al. propose a Stochastic Quasi-Newton method to scale TPSVM for large datasets while managing noise.

The authors emphasize that the discussed pinball loss TWSVM algorithms build upon TPMSVM rather than the original TWSVM framework. Tanveer et al. later introduce Pin-GTSVM, which integrates pinball loss into the original TWSVM, offering improved stability and reduced sensitivity to outliers. To maintain sparsity, a novel sparse pinball TWSVM is proposed. Overall, the advancements aim to enhance classification accuracy while addressing the challenges of noise and data imbalance.
