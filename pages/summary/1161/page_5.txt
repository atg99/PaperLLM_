The paper discusses the Twin Support Vector Machines (TWSVM) framework, presenting its optimization problems and highlighting its efficiency compared to traditional Support Vector Machines (SVM). The optimization problems involve constraints that focus on one class at a time, leading to a significant reduction in the number of constraints, which makes TWSVM approximately four times faster than SVM. The complexity of TWSVM is noted as 2 O((l/2)³), while its generalization performance is comparable to that of SVM.

The paper also introduces variants of TWSVM, particularly the Least Squares Twin Support Vector Machines (LSTSVM), developed by Kumar and Gopal to further reduce training time. The proximal hyperplanes are derived using regularization terms, allowing for effective classification based on the proximity of kernel-generated surfaces. The classification of a new sample is determined by a signum function that evaluates the distance to the hyperplanes.

Overall, the research highlights the advancements in TWSVM and its effectiveness in classification tasks, emphasizing its computational advantages and performance metrics.
