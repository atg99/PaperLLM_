The paper discusses various advancements in the Twin Support Vector Machine (TWSVM) methodology, highlighting improvements aimed at enhancing accuracy, efficiency, and scalability. Key developments include the introduction of a probability-based TWSVM model (P-TWSVM) by Shao et al. in 2012, which demonstrated higher accuracy than the original TWSVM. Shao and Deng also proposed a coordinate descent TWSVM to improve efficiency through a regularization term, enabling faster training on large datasets by processing single data points.

Ye et al. introduced the Weighted TWSVM with Local Information (WL-TWSVM), focusing on underlying correlations, although it faced limitations with large-scale problems. In 2013, Peng and Xu developed the Twin-Hypersphere SVM (TH-SVM), which avoids matrix inversions in dual formulations. Qi et al. formulated structural-TWSVM, enhancing training time and accuracy but failing to account for the importance of samples within clusters.

To address this, Pan et al. proposed a K-nearest neighbor based structural TWSVM in 2015, which introduced varied penalties for different classes but struggled with overfitting. Subsequently, an efficient KNN weighted TWSVM was introduced, incorporating a regularization term to mitigate overfitting and enhance generalization.

Further innovations included a non-parallel hyperplane SVM by Shao et al. in 2014, which clustered samples for better accuracy and lower computational time. Ding et al. in 2016 utilized the Fruit Fly Optimization Algorithm for optimal parameter selection in TWSVM. In 2017, Pan et al. introduced safe screening rules for large-scale classification, significantly reducing computational time without sacrificing accuracy.

Yang and Xu proposed a safe sample screening rule for Laplacian twin parametric-margin SVM (LTPSVM), while Pang and Xu applied a similar rule to WL-TWSVM, demonstrating improved performance over traditional SVM and TWSVM with reduced computational demands. The paper concludes with recent contributions from Zhao et al. focusing on efficient non-parallel approaches, indicating ongoing advancements in TWSVM methodologies.
