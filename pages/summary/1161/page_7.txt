Tanveer et al. introduced robust ELS-TWSVM (RELS-TSVM) to address the limitations of existing techniques such as TWSVM, LS-TWSVM, and ELS-TWSVM, which adhere to the empirical risk minimization principle but struggle with noise and outliers due to their positive semi-definite matrices. RELS-TSVM incorporates the structural risk minimization principle to ensure positive definiteness of matrices and employs an energy parameter for enhanced robustness against noise. This approach demonstrated promising generalization performance and reduced computational time compared to baseline algorithms, ranking as the best classifier in a comprehensive evaluation of 187 classifiers across 90 datasets.

Khemchandani and Sharma proposed robust least squares TWSVM (RLS-TWSVM), which is noted for its speed and improved generalization performance while effectively handling noise. They also introduced Incremental RLS-TWSVM to enhance training speed while managing noise and outliers. Tomar and Agarwal focused on feature selection in LS-TWSVM for heart disease diagnosis, utilizing F-score to improve model accuracy. A novel weighted LS-TWSVM was developed to address imbalanced datasets, achieving better accuracy and geometric mean than traditional SVM and TWSVM. To further tackle imbalanced data, Hybrid Feature Selection Based WLS-TWSVM was proposed for diagnosing diseases like Breast Cancer, Diabetes, and Hepatitis.

Xu et al. proposed structural LS-TWSVM (SLS-TWSVM), which incorporates data distribution information and performs clustering before classification, leading to better generalization and noise insensitivity compared to other models. Mei and Xu presented a multi-task LS-TWSVM algorithm, focusing on multitask learning and optimizing computational efficiency by solving linear equations instead of quadratic programming problems.

Finally, the least square twin support vector hypersphere (LSTSVH) was introduced as an enhancement of twin support vector hypersphere (TSVH), achieving comparable accuracy to several established models while balancing computational efficiency. In 2020, Tanveer et al. further explored efficient large-scale methods in this domain.
