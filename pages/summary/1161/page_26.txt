The paper discusses the applications of Twin Support Vector Classification (TWSVM) in various fields, particularly highlighting its effectiveness in real-life classification challenges.

In the biomedical sector, TWSVM has been utilized for early breast cancer detection through digital mammograms and has incorporated hybrid feature selection methods for diseases like Hepatitis and Diabetes. Additionally, it has been combined with dual-tree complex wavelet transform for pathological brain detection and has shown promise in cardiac disease detection, with variations such as Swarm-TWSVM improving accuracy. Other applications include predicting arrhythmia heart disease using LSTWSVM and DAG LS-TWSVM classifiers, and semi-supervised TWSVM for detecting Acute-on-chronic liver failure. TWSVM has also been applied in the early detection of Alzheimer's disease, demonstrating its versatility in medical diagnostics.

Specifically for Alzheimer's disease prediction, several advanced methodologies have been proposed. The angle-based universum least squares TWSVM achieved 95% accuracy in detecting Alzheimer's. Other methods, including universum support vector machine-based recursive feature elimination and structural MRI data processing, have enhanced classification accuracy among different cognitive states, such as mild cognitive impairment and normal controls. Additionally, fuzzy LS-TWSVM has been integrated into deep learning networks for Alzheimer's prognosis.

In the realm of speaker recognition, TWSVM has been adapted for multi-class classification, utilizing Gaussian mixture models for feature extraction, which outperformed traditional SVM methods. Another approach, multi-TWSVM, finds hyperplanes for each class while considering constraints from others, resulting in superior performance compared to various algorithms.

Overall, TWSVM has proven to be a robust tool across multiple domains, particularly in healthcare and voice recognition, highlighting its potential for addressing complex classification tasks.
