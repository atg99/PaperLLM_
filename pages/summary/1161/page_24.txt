Wang et al. first introduced TSVC, which clusters data samples around k center points by leveraging inter- and intra-cluster information and solving a series of Quadratic Programming Problems (QPP). They also enhanced model stability with a nearest neighbor graph-based initialization.

Khemchandani et al. later proposed a fuzzy least squares version of TSVC, allowing data points to have fuzzy membership values across different clusters. This approach solves primal problems instead of dual ones, resulting in comparable accuracy to TSVC but with reduced training time. Further modifications by Khemchandani and Pal involved replacing the hinge loss function with a weighted linear loss, leading to improved accuracy through the introduction of a regularization term.

In subsequent work, the weighted linear loss function was modified for continuity, with implementations in semi-supervised frameworks and a fuzzy extension that enhanced robustness against noise. Tree-TSVC emerged as a novel clustering algorithm that utilizes a binary tree structure, improving training time and accuracy by solving linear equations at each node.

To address TSVC's sensitivity to noise and computational complexity, Ye et al. proposed an L1-norm distance-based robust TSVC, which simplifies the problem to linear equations. They introduced RTSVC and Fast RTSVC for improved computational speed in nonlinear cases. Finally, Wang et al. introduced ramp-based TSVC, which employs a ramp cost function and solves a non-convex programming problem through an alternating iteration algorithm, further enhancing clustering capabilities.
