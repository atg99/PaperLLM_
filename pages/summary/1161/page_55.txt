This paper references a series of studies centered around twin support vector clustering methods and their applications in various fields. The works discussed include distance minimization techniques, ramp-based clustering approaches, and least squares methodologies. Notable contributions include the development of the least squares projection twin support vector clustering, pinball loss twin support vector clustering, and sparse twin support vector clustering models. These models are aimed at enhancing clustering efficiency and robustness in different contexts, including biomedical applications. The research also highlights the integration of twin support vector machines in mass detection for digital mammograms and other complex data scenarios. Overall, the studies collectively advance the understanding and application of twin support vector techniques in clustering and classification tasks.
