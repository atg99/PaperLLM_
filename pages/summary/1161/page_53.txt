This paper discusses various advancements in multi-class classification methods, particularly focusing on least squares twin support vector machines (TWSVM). The authors reference multiple studies that contribute to the development and improvement of TWSVM techniques. Key contributions include the comparison of different multi-class classification methods (Tomar and Agarwal, 2015), the introduction of recursive projection techniques for enhancing classification accuracy (Yang et al., 2016; Li et al., 2016), and reviews summarizing the state of multi-class TWSVM (Ding et al., 2017).

Additionally, the paper highlights innovations such as the incorporation of local density information in weighted least squares TWSVM (Ai et al., 2018) and methods for scaling KNN multi-class TWSVM through safe instance reduction (Pang et al., 2018). Further advancements include improvements on least squares TWSVM (de Lima et al., 2018) and robust methods for large-scale classification (Qiang et al., 2020). The authors also discuss the integration of K-nearest neighbor approaches with TWSVM (Tanveer et al., 2021; Xu and Wang, 2014) and propose novel kernel-free methods for efficient multi-class classification (Gao et al., 2021). Overall, the paper emphasizes the ongoing evolution and optimization of TWSVM methodologies in addressing multi-class classification challenges.
