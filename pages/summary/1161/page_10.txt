The paper discusses several models, including a robust rescaled hinge loss based projection Twin Support Vector Machine (TWSVM) which effectively manages outliers and enhances performance. 

The classification accuracies of different models on various datasets are presented in a table, illustrating the performance of models like TWSVM, WLTSVM, P-TWSVM, LSP-TWSVM, and LIWLSP-TWSVM across multiple datasets, with notable results in the Australian dataset achieving an accuracy of 88.13%.

The concept of Twin Parametric Margin Support Vector Machine (TPMSVM) is introduced, which seeks to define two parametric margin hyperplanes for classification, proving effective in handling heteroscedastic noise. TPMSVM is noted for its lower training time compared to other models, despite its computational complexity due to the need to solve two Quadratic Programming Problems (QPPs). 

Subsequent developments include the Smooth Twin Parametric Margin SVM (STPMSVM) which incorporates a quadratic function for improved training speed and utilizes a genetic algorithm for optimizing parameters. The Least Squares TPMSVM (LSTPMSVM) further reduces training costs and complexity by solving primal problems, yielding competitive classification accuracy with significantly reduced computational time. LSTPMSVM shows better generalization performance compared to its predecessors.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution of TWSVM and its variants, showcasing the continuous effort to enhance classification accuracy, reduce computational complexity, and effectively manage noise in datasets.
