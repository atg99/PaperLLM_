P-TWSVM, introduced by Chen et al. in 2011, utilizes a multiple-surface classification approach that minimizes within-class variance by finding multiple projection axes rather than relying solely on hyperplanes. This method is particularly effective for datasets with intricate distributions, such as XOR problems, leading to improved classification accuracy. 

The paper also discusses the Least Squares Twin Support Vector Machine (LS-LSTWSVM), which enhances the performance of TWSVM on large datasets by employing different Lagrangian functions that eliminate the need for calculating inverse matrices. A comparative analysis of classification accuracies across various datasets demonstrates the efficacy of LS-TWSVM against other models, highlighting its advantages in specific cases such as the Ionosphere and Votes datasets. Overall, the advancements in TWSVM variants signify progress in machine learning methodologies for handling complex classification tasks.
