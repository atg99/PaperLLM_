The paper presents a series of studies and methodologies focused on the classification and analysis of electroencephalogram (EEG) signals, particularly in the context of detecting epileptic seizures and motor imagery recognition. Key contributions include the use of entropy-based features within the Fast Adaptive Wavelet Transform (FAWT) framework for automated seizure detection, as well as the application of Hjorth parameters for distinguishing between seizure and seizure-free EEG signals. The research also explores advanced techniques such as the self-adaptive frequency selection common spatial pattern combined with least squares twin support vector machine for motor imagery EEG classification. Furthermore, the paper discusses Universum-based approaches and fuzzy Lagrangian twin parametric-margin support vector machines for enhanced biomedical data analysis. Additional studies presented include the investigation of EEG biometric identification and the development of automated systems for focal EEG signal identification using fractal dimensions. Overall, the work emphasizes innovative computational intelligence techniques to improve EEG signal classification and analysis, contributing to advancements in biomedical applications.
