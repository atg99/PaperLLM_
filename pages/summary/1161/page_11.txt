The paper discusses advancements in Twin Support Vector Machine (TWSVM) methodologies, highlighting various enhancements aimed at improving classification performance and computational efficiency. Peng et al. introduced the structural twin parametric-margin SVM (STPMSVM) in 2013, which incorporates class data structures based on cluster granularity, achieving good generalization and fast learning compared to traditional TWSVM (TPMSVM). The centroid-based TPSVM (CTPSVM) was later proposed to address the sparsity issue inherent in TPMSVM by optimizing centroid projection values for a sparse optimal hyperplane.

In 2017, Rastogi et al. introduced the robust parametric TWSVM (RP-TWSVM), which seeks to find two margin hyperplanes capable of adjusting to heteroscedastic noise. They also developed angle-based TPSVM (ATPSVM), maximizing the angle between twin hyperplanes to efficiently handle noise. A recent contribution by Richhariya and Tanveer, the angle-based universum LS-TWSVM (AULSTWSVM), contrasts with ATPSVM by minimizing the angle between the universum hyperplane and the classifying hyperplane. This approach incorporates prior distribution information using linear loss, demonstrating promising generalization performance and reduced computational time, particularly in Alzheimer's disease diagnosis.

Additionally, the paper notes that while TWSVM offers improved accuracy and speed over conventional SVM, it compromises sparsity due to the l2-norm consideration in its objective function. To enhance sparsity, Peng proposed a primal space TWSVM that maintains better generalization ability. Further improvements were made with the robust minimum class variance TWSVM (RMCV-TWSVM), which adds a regularization term to boost robustness and generalization without significant increases in computational costs. Overall, these developments reflect ongoing efforts to refine TWSVM methodologies for better performance in various applications.
