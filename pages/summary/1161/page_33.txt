The paper discusses various improvements made to Twin Support Vector Regression (TSVR) to enhance its generalization performance and reduce computational time. While TSVR is known for its better generalization results, it traditionally requires solving two Quadratic Programming Problems (QPPs), which can be time-consuming. 

To address this, Peng proposed a primal version of TSVR (P-TSVR) that focuses solely on linear equations, thus speeding up the learning process while maintaining comparable generalization. Additionally, a back-fitting strategy was introduced to enhance sparsity in TSVR. 

Singh et al. introduced rectangular kernels into the TSVR formulation, resulting in reduced TSVR that significantly cuts down computational time, making it more suitable for large datasets. Zhao et al. developed a Least Squares version of TSVR (TLSSVR) that simplifies the problem to linear equations, further improving efficiency. 

Huang and Ding proposed LS-TWSVM in primal space, which also resolves two linear equations and achieves accuracy similar to TSVR. For handling heteroscedastic noise, Peng's twin parametric insensitive SVR (TPISVR) effectively addresses noise that varies with input values, despite requiring two SVM-type problems. 

Shao et al. introduced the ϵ-TSVR, which utilizes the SRM principle in the primal space and employs the successive over-relaxation technique to enhance generalization and training speed. Balasundaram and Tanveer developed the Lagrangian TSVR (LTSVR), demonstrating improved generalization and reduced computation time. 

Inspired by LTSVR, Balasundaram and Gupta proposed a Lagrangian dual of the 2-norm TSVR, achieving better learning speed and accuracy. Lastly, Tanveer et al. introduced a regularization term to the TSVR objective function, leading to the regularized Lagrangian TSVR (IRLTSVR), which optimizes linear systems instead of QPPs, resulting in superior accuracy and efficiency compared to conventional TSVR.
