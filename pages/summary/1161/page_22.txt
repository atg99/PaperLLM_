The paper discusses various advancements in multi-class classification algorithms, particularly focusing on Twin Support Vector Machines (TWSVM) and their modifications. Key developments include the introduction of multiple recursive projection TWSVM (Multi-P-TWSVM) by Li et al. in 2016, which addresses the complexity of generating multiple orthogonal projection axes while enhancing generalization through a regularization term.

Ding et al. in 2017 reviewed various multi-class structures such as one-versus-rest and one-versus-one, noting that one-versus-one TWSVMs generally offer superior performance. Ai et al. in 2018 improved the local sensitivity of least squares TWSVM by integrating local density information, while Pang et al. proposed K-nearest neighbor-based weighted multi-class TWSVM (KMTWSVM) that utilized a "one-versus-one-versus-rest" strategy, although it posed computational challenges for large datasets. 

Lima et al. presented improvements on least squares twin multi-class SVM using a similar strategy which yielded better classification accuracy than previous models. Qiang et al. enhanced LSTKSVC by developing a robust weighted linear loss twin multi-class SVM (WLT-KSVC) to mitigate issues related to outliers and misclassification, despite a decrease in training accuracy with an increased number of classes.

Li et al. introduced a nonparallel support vector machine (NSVM) demonstrating advantages on benchmark datasets. In 2020, Tanveer et al. proposed LS-KWMTWSVM, a faster version of KWMTWSVM, which showcased high accuracy and efficiency on KEEL imbalance datasets. Additionally, a multiclass nonparallel parametric margin SVM and a kernel-free least squares TWSVM were noted for their improved performance in multiclass classification tasks.
