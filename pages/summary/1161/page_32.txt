The paper discusses advancements in Twin Support Vector Regression (TSVR) aimed at improving robustness and generalization while addressing issues related to outliers and noise in datasets. The authors highlight that traditional models utilize a uniform weighting approach, which may not effectively manage the influence of outliers. To mitigate this, wavelet weighted projection TWSVM was introduced, employing wavelet-based weights.

Zhong et al. enhanced TSVR by substituting the 2-norm with the 1-norm in its Quadratic Programming Problem (QPP), resulting in reduced training time and improved generalization. Chen et al. developed a smooth version of TSVR (STSVR) that ensured positive definiteness in the QPP through a smooth function, transforming the QPPs into unconstrained minimization problems solvable by the Newton method.

Despite these improvements, high computational costs persisted due to the quadratic and linear programming challenges. Zhao et al. addressed this by formulating a least squares version of TSVR (TLSSVR), which streamlined computations to linear equations and introduced a sparse variant. 

Further, Chen et al. incorporated regularization into TSVR using an l1-norm loss function, enhancing robustness and sparsity. Huang et al. followed with a sparse least squares TSVR that shifted the primal problems to linear programming, achieving significant computational efficiency. Tanveer’s 1-norm TSVR variant also demonstrated enhanced accuracy and reduced computational time compared to the original TSVR.

In 2020, Singla et al. introduced Res-TSVR, notable for its robustness against noise, by converting a non-convex optimization problem into a convex one. Gu et al. proposed fast clustering-based weighted TSVR (FCWTSVR), which categorizes samples based on similarity, assigning varying weights to enhance classification performance. Finally, the paper mentions robust Huber loss based twin SVR, which linearly penalizes large deviations while squaring smaller errors, further increasing robustness against noise and outliers.
