 Unsupervised Learning
When people reference systems that can learn for themselves, they 
are actually referencing unsupervised learning (see Figure  3-5). In 
unsupervised learning, the learning algorithm doesn’t receive labels for 
data, leaving the algorithm to find structure from the input. As data is 
unlabeled, there is no evaluation of the accuracy of the structure that is 
output by the algorithm. Data may be missing both classifications and 
labels. As a result, the model is developed through interpretation: through 
finding hidden structures and inferences in the input data. This may be 
through the extraction of rules, reducing data redundancy, or as the result 
of organizing data.
