39 Veracity
Worthless data, no matter how plentiful, is always going to be worthless. 
Veracity refers to data truthfulness and whether data is of optimal quality 
and suitability in the context of its use relating to the biases, noise, and 
abnormality in data.
Various factors can affect the veracity of data, including the following:
• Data entry. Was data entered correctly? Have there been 
any errors or events? Is there an audit trail of data entry?
• Data management. What is the integrity of the data 
moving through the system?
• Integration quality. Is data appropriately referenced, 
commented on, and unique?
• Staleness. Is data appropriate for use? Is it timely and 
relevant?
• Usage. Is the data available in a way that is actionable? 
Will the data be useful toward achieving business 
goals? Is it ethical to use this data?
The six Cs of trusted data can help assess whether the data you are 
using for your projects has veracity. It is essential to ensure your datasets 
are clean, complete, current, consistent, and compliant.
• Clean data.  This is the result of good data quality 
procedures such as deduplication, standardization, 
verification, matching, and processing. Clean data 
enables robust decisions to be made on quality,  
non-  tainted data. Clean data is the biggest challenge
