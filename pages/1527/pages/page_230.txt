From supermarket checkouts to airport check-ins and digital healthcare to 
Internet banking, the use of data and AI for decision-making is ubiquitous. 
There has been an astronomic growth in data availability over the last two 
decades, fueled by, first, connectivity, and now the Internet of Things. 
Traditional data science teams focus on the use of data for the creation, 
implementation, validation, and evaluation of machine learning models 
that can be for predictive analytics.
In the past decade, there has been a wealth of data-driven AI and 
technological advance in healthcare, including the following:
• Noninvasive blood glucose levels: There have been many 
advances in noninvasive blood glucose management. 
Google developed a contact lens that determines the 
user’s blood glucose levels in 2016. French company 
PKvitality developed a small wristwatch that determines 
the level of glucose in the blood, and there have been 
advances in determining blood glucose levels through 
using far-infrared signals.[67]
