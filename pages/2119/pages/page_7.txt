the two domains: water body extraction is usually undertaken across large spatial scales,
whereas the water quality monitoring literature is still focused on smaller, often individual,
bodies of water. This points to a future research direction in the water quality literature
that we touch on in our review paper: we need to scale up water quality estimation using
RS imagery by matching it with ground-truth water quality measurements.
Tables 2 and 3 provide a brief summary of the methods used for water body detection
and water quality monitoring, elaborated in Sections 3.2 and 3.3, respectively. The general
characteristics summarized by machines (i.e., the word clouds in Figure 2) align with the
literature; convolutional neural network (CNN) models are indeed applied much more
frequently for water body detection, and long short-term memory (LSTM) models are often
used for water quality monitoring. The evaluation metrics used in the reviewed articles
were also summarized and are provided in Tables 2 and 3 (a brief explanation of each
metric is in Appendix C).
